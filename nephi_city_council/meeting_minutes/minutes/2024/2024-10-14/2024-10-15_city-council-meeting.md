PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE: Allen Sperry led the pledge of allegiance. Mr. Sperry reported to the council
that he has written a book about the history of baseball in Nephi. Jan 1, 2025 will be the 150 year
anniversary of the first game of baseball played west of the Missouri River and the game was played in
Nephi. He would like to do a commemorative sign at Pioneer Park marking the historical event. He
presented to the council a written history of the first baseball game played in Nephi. He asked the
council for permission to place the sign in the park. A temporary sign will be displayed with a QR code
that people can scan to read the entire history that he has written. Eventually a permanent marker
would be placed in the park. The council agreed to allow the sign and thanked Mr. Sperry for his efforts
and commitment to serving the community.

THE REFUGE UTAH PRESENTATION:

  Vanessa Tuttle, Lori Jenkins and Brynn Peterson representing the Refuge Utah, formerly known as the
Center for Women and Children in Crisis reported to the council about the services and resources that
their organization has for people that have experienced domestic violence or sexual assault. Ms. Tuttle
explained that they have services that help with recovery and prevention resources. They work hand in
hand with law enforcement and with the county’s victim’s advocacy director Amy Taylor. They offer
support classes, individual and group therapy and a 24-hour hotline. The prevention resources include
healthy relationships classes. Ms. Tuttle asked the council for recommendations of how to establish
their services in our community and how to best get the hotline number out to those in need.

ORDINANCE 10-15-2024- SHORT TERM RENTALS DRAFT REVIEWED AND TABLED:

   Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council a draft of an ordinance governing short-term rentals. He
explained that it is currently illegal to have a short-term rental in Nephi City but a few do exist. The
proposed ordinance would allow for a short-term rental and set the terms of how one may operate.
   A short-term rental is a residential unit that is rented for less than 30 consecutive days to the same
person or entity. The ordinance does not allow them in internal accessory dwelling units, accessory
structures, or temporary living structures. The rental unit must receive a fire inspection, be licensed,
pay the city and county transient room tax, and is also required to have an off-street parking space for
each guest bedroom with a minimum of 2 parking stalls.
  There is no current maximum unit allowed set in the ordinance. Mr. Atkinson recommended that the
city wait to see how many units are licensed and determine whether the housing supply is affected for
residential homeowners. If there is an impact, a maximum limit could then be set.
   Councilor Parady had some questions about the amount of parking that the ordinance permitted. He
expressed that he thought the ordinance required too many spaces. Councilor Parady moved to table
the ordinance so that the council could have more discussion on parking requirements. Councilor Skip
Worwood seconded the motion. The motion carried on a unanimous vote.

LOVELESS ESTATES PLAT AMENDMENT AND LOVELESS APARTMENTS SUBDIVISION FINAL PLAT
APPROVED:

Mr. Atkinson updated the council that Priority Homes was looking to build two more four-flexes on the
northeast corner of the intersection of 200 W and 700 N. In order to have the opportunity to do so the
city had previously approved:
     1.    A rezone of parcel XA00-0775- (.53 acres) from R1-8 to R3-8 to allow for multi-family
          housing.
     2. A partial street vacation on 200 W from approximately 700 to 760 N, abandoning the right-
          of-way to 60 feet.
     3. A vicinity plat for the Loveless Apartments, and an Amendment to the Common Area Lot of
          the Loveless Estates Plat A Subdivision.
 Mr. Atkinson explained that Priority Homes has now returned with their combined preliminary and
 final plat and the amended Loveless Estates Plat A common area. The proposed subdivision includes
 the following:
     1. Two existing 4-plexes on parcel #: XA00-0776-11 (1.03 acres).
     2. An amendment to Loveless Estates Plat A Common Area Lot (XA00-0822-12-COMM) that
          would give additional acreage to the project to meet the required lot size for the density
          requested.
     3. Two proposed 4-plexes on parcel #XA00-0775- (.53 acres).

 Mr. Atkinson assured the council that the application has met all the minimum code requirements for
 preliminary and final plat. Councilor Travis Worwood asked if the home on the corner was going to
 be torn down. Representatives from the Priority Homes, Garrison and Brett Ludlow answered yes,
 the home would be torn down.

 Councilor Skip Worwood moved to approve the amended Loveless Estates Plat A common area.
 Councilor Travis Worwood seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

 Councilor Skip Worwood moved to approve the combined preliminary and final plat of the Loveless
 Apartments Subdivision. Councilor Parady seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

    Mr. Atkinson updated the council that the Rollin’ Home RV Park has been on one large parcel and
now the owner is wanting to divide it into three parcels. This subdivision project property is located at
approximately 2888 S Four Mile Road and includes approximately 57.29 total project acres with around
12.58 acres in phase 1 (Lot 1). The development is in the HC1 zone. The first phase of the development
is a one (1) lot subdivision with two remaining parcels left behind for future phases of subdivision. The
lot that will be created already has an established RV business on it with some installed infrastructure.
    The developer and landowner have entered into a development agreement to allow them to split the
property into 3 parcels, that are deed restricted, until the full subdivision process has been completed
for each future respective phase. Phase 1 must happen within an agreed upon time frame. This has
allowed the new landowner (Chad Ledy) to purchase the property, and work towards finalizing the
subdivision process. The agreement includes the developer paying either a fee in lieu or supplying a
non-expiring bond to cover any missing infrastructure, particularly along Four-mile Road.

    The remainder parcels, and the future Lot 1 parcel have been deed restricted in accordance with
USC § 10-9a-103(68)(c)(v) to state that the division:
 a) is in anticipation of future land use approvals on the parcel or parcels;
 b) does not confer any land use approvals; and
 c) has not been approved by the land use authority.

Councilor Parady asked the developer Kalli Bowers, who was in attendance, if she had any idea what the
use of the subdivision would be. Ms. Bower answered that she does not know the use yet.

 ADMINISTRATIVE UPDATES:

 REQUIRING A CONCEPT PLAN FOR REQUESTED PROPERTY TO BE ANNEXED DISCUSSED:

  Mr. Atkinson asked the council for some direction on requests from two landowners who want to
 annex two large parcels of land into the city. Both property owners have not submitted a concept
 plan for the desired property to be annexed. Mr. Atkinson said it is difficult to do an analysis on
 property when he doesn’t know the plan for the property. Mayor Seely said that he really would like
 a concept plan before making decisions on annexation. Councilor Travis Worwood said he thought
 that each request should be looked at on a case-by-case basis and to ask the landowners to have a
 general idea of what is going on the property. Skip said that the one individual wanted it annexed
 into the city and then sell the property immediately after annexation.
 Councilor Travis Worwood asked if another entity could encroach on the city’s growth zone and form
 A new city inside our designated growth zone. Atkinson said he would need to research that
 question.
     A lengthy discussion ensued about why we do or don’t want to require a concept plan.
 Corey Thomson who is a member of the Planning Commission said the Planning Commission had
 made a concept zoning map that covered much more area than the annexation area map. The
 council directed Mr. Atkinson to require a concept plan for any property owners interested in
 annexing property.
 Councilor Travis Worwood left the meeting.
