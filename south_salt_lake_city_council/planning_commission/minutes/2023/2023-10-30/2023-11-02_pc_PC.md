1.     PUBLIC HEARING - A Petition for an Extension for the Final Plat to Consolidate Seven
       Existing Parcels into a One-Lot Plat, for a Multi-Family Dwelling Building Located at
       2354 South State Street.

Action Item

Applicant:     Parallel Acquisitions, LLC
Address:       2354 South State Street

City Planner, Dawn Emery, reported that the applicant, Parallel Acquisitions, LLC, is seeking an
extension of the subdivision amendment. She shared an aerial view of the seven parcels the applicant
wants to consolidate. Images of the existing conditions were shared as well as the proposed Site Plan.
There is one entrance and one exit to the courtyard where the parking is located inside the Site Plan.
The exit and entrance are on Truman Avenue. She reviewed the east and north elevations followed
by the west and south elevations. For the south elevation to the left, there is a loading dock that goes
onto Burton Avenue. The ALTA survey was shared as well as the Preliminary Plat, both of which
were included in the Staff Report.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission table the decision on the application by Ryan
McCulloch, on behalf of Parallel Acquisitions, LLC, for an extension of 360 days from the date of
the request, for the petition for a Final Subdivision Plat Amendment to consolidate seven existing
parcels on property located at 2354 South State Street to a date certain, to allow the applicant and
Staff time to respond to specific inquiries or concerns raised by the Planning Commission, or to allow
the Planning Commission more time to consider the proposal. Ms. Emery shared information about
the timeline. On October 5, 2023, the applicant submitted an application to petition the Planning
Commission for a one-year extension of the Final Subdivision Plat that was reviewed and
unanimously approved by the Planning Commission on October 20, 2022.

The proposed project included the redevelopment of seven existing parcels located around 2354 South
State Street. The redevelopment will include an apartment building with 319 rental units. The
applicant received a Conditional Use Permit and Final Plat approval on October 20, 2022. Parallel
Acquisitions, LLC first applied for a Design Review, Subdivision, and Conditional Use Permit on
May 27, 2022. The Design Review was held on August 24, 2022, at which time there were no major
concerns. On September 15, 2022, after a public hearing was held, the Preliminary Plat was approved
by the Planning Commission. On October 20, 2022, after a public hearing was held, the Final Plat
and Conditional Use Permit were approved by the Planning Commission.

Ms. Emery shared the South Salt Lake City Code 17.10.100, which allowed the applicant to apply
for, and potentially be granted, an extension of the Preliminary Plat and Conditional Use Permit.
Notice of the extension request was submitted in writing to Staff before the expiration date. Through
their writings and application, the applicant demonstrated that there had not been any major changes
that would prohibit the plat proposal. The extension was requested due to concerns about increased
construction costs and higher interest rates. Parallel Acquisition, LLC stated that there was a
commitment to move forward with the project, but an extension was needed.

Ms. Emery reported that the Planning Commission is the Land Use Authority on all Subdivision Plat
applications. The three options for the Planning Commission to consider included approval, denial,
or a continuance of the request made by Parallel Acquisition, LLC for an extension.

Chair Pechmann opened the public hearing. There were no comments. The public hearing was
closed.

The Applicant’s Representative, Ryan McCulloch, participated virtually. Also on the phone was
Parallel Acquisition, LLC Founder, David Pierce. Mr. McCulloch reported that the project was
approved last year but there are industry-wide issues such as higher construction costs and increased
interest costs. The project was postponed as a result. Parallel Acquisition, LLC likes South Salt Lake
and wants more time to complete the project. The request was for a 360-day extension.

Chair Pechmann asked if there had been supply chain issues. Mr. McCulloch clarified that it was not
necessarily related to the supply chain but there have been increased construction costs as well as
elevated interest rates. Commissioner Carter noted that a request was made for a 360-day extension.
He asked about the rationale for that number. Mr. McCulloch explained that the number is referenced
in the Code for the extension timeline. Commissioner Slifka asked what would need to happen in the
next year for the project to continue. Mr. McCulloch reported that Parallel Acquisitions, LLC is in
the process of getting the work repriced by local general contractors. There will also be continuous
monitoring of interest rates. If there are positive developments they will reengage on the design,
continue with the construction documents, and make a submission for a Building Permit with the City
next year.

Chair Pechmann asked how many projects Parallel Acquisitions, LLC currently has in development.
Mr. McCulloch reported that two projects were recently opened in Texas and Ohio. There are also
other properties under contract with Letters of Intent for other markets in Arizona and Florida.
Commissioner Bellina asked for additional details about the timeline. Mr. McCulloch reiterated that
it will depend on market conditions and when the construction price is adequate to begin moving the
project forward. Currently, it is too expensive to justify the investment. Parallel Acquisitions, LLC
is under contract on the property and has extensions through next year. A variety of economic
conditions will determine when the work will begin. 10 to 25 percent of the construction documents
have been completed. Once there is comfort moving forward further, it would take a few months to
submit for a Building Permit.

Commissioner Bellina asked if the applicant would be able to abide by all of the commitments made
during previous approvals. Mr. McCulloch confirmed this. Nothing had changed in terms of the
design, amenity package, or anything that was approved during the Design Review. Commissioner
Bellina asked about affordable units in the development. Mr. McCulloch reported that no affordable
units had been incorporated into the current design but work was being done with South Salt Lake
City to determine what opportunities exist to add affordable units.

Chair Pechmann asked Staff if the applicant would come back with a request for a Conditional Use
Permit. Ms. Emery confirmed that they will. Chair Pechmann stressed the importance of affordable
housing. Commissioner Carter asked if the applicant was aware of the high water table in the City.

if there is no Building Permit or no active work done. The Conditional Use Permit technically expired
on October 20, 2023, as a result. Mr. McCulloch noted that there had been work with Staff throughout
this process so he was not sure why this had not been communicated. Ms. Ungricht explained that it
was overlooked in the Code. Typically, most Conditional Use Permits are active. There was not
necessarily a violation but there would need to be a reapplication for a Conditional Use Permit to
operate a multi-family development. Mr. McCulloch did not want to be penalized on the plat due to
something overlooked by Staff.

Commissioner Carter raised a question regarding when a Conditional Use Permit is approved by the
Planning Commission and if the applicant was given the information needed to start the project to
prevent the Conditional Use Permit from expiring. Mr. Collins stated that everyone is presumed to
know what is in the Code. That is the legal standard. Commissioner Holscher suggested that the item
be continued so the applicant can work with Staff on outstanding issues.

2.      PUBLIC HEARING - A Petition for Preliminary Plat Approval to Consolidate Two
        Existing Properties into One Lot, for a Restaurant, Located at 28 East 2100 South.

Action Item

Applicant:     Cassie Ruiz
Address:       28 East 2100 South and 50 East 2100 South, In-N-Out Burger

Planning Consultant, Alexandra White Halleck, informed those present that the applicant was unable
to attend tonight’s meeting; however, there was a local representative in the audience by the name of
Spencer Hymas. Ms. White Halleck reported that this was the third application from In-N-Out Burger
for 28 East 2100 South and 50 East 2100 South. In early October, the Planning Commission reviewed
a Petition to Rezone and a Petition for an Ordinance Amendment. The property was back before the
Commission for Preliminary Subdivision Plat approval. The Final Plat would come to the Planning
Commission on November 16, 2023. This was the final step with the Planning Commission. Once
completed, the applicant would move toward Building Permits.

Ms. White Halleck shared background information about the properties. The two parcels are on the
corner of Main Street and 2100 South. 28 East 2100 South is the vacant lot and 50 East 2100 South
is the vacant former Mad Greek building. She shared the existing conditions looking from 2100 South
and looking southbound. Chair Pechmann asked if the billboard would remain. Ms. White Halleck
reported that it will not. Additional details will be shared soon.

On October 25, 2023, the South Salt Lake City Council approved the rezone of 50 East 2100 South,
which was the former Mad Greek parcel. It was rezoned from Commercial Corridor to Crossing

MPMU. As part of that approval, one of the conditions was that the applicant come back and
consolidate the two parcels via plat. The original request was made because it is not possible to
consolidate via plat two parcels in two different zones. Now that the rezone has been done, it is
possible to consider the consolidation of the two parcels.

Ms. White Halleck shared information about Lot 5 in the Crossing Subdivision, which is located at
28 East 2100 South. In 2008, the South Salt Lake City Council vacated a portion of Major Street and
a street that no longer exists as well as the public alleyways that historically run through that
neighborhood. As part of the vacation process and the original plat, all of the existing water and
sewer lines running through the larger area were relocated as part of the WinCo development but the
easements were left behind. That was an intentional move by the City Council at that time. The
easements were shown on the map with the red hashmarks. There are existing pipes in the ground
for water and sewer but those were discontinued and had not been used at all since 2008. In
discussions with the South Salt Lake Engineering and Public Works Departments, there was no
purpose for those pipes as there was a relocation to the south. The applicant, as part of their petition,
would be dedicating new 10-foot PUEs, which were the minimum standard required under the South
Salt Lake Code. That language was still being worked through but it was something the City Attorney
would have and that would be approved on the Final Plat if the Planning Commission chose to move
forward with the easement relocation.

All other utilities on site were compliant. Ms. White Halleck identified overhead power on the ALTA
survey. Overhead power was shown on the materials in yellow. It went around and through the
parcel. South Salt Lake City Code requires as part of any Subdivision Plat process that the applicant
underground the power, so that was one of the requirements in the Conditions of Approval listed in
the Staff Report. Another element that was considered as part of the petition had to do with right-of-
way dedications. South Salt Lake City Code contains standard cross-section widths for all public
rights-of-way. It was quickly identified on Main Street that the right-of-way width was substandard
to what was identified in Section 17.10 of the South Salt Lake City Municipal Code. As a result, the
applicant would dedicate two feet of additional right-of-way along Main Street. Ms. White Halleck
noted that the curb and gutter, as well as the existing stormwater drainage system, will remain. The
above-ground infrastructure will be shifted as part of the dedication.

On Main Street, the applicant will install 10.5-foot public sidewalks that are flush with curb and
gutter, trees, and tree grates. The intention was to create a more urban feel along the streetscape. Ms.
White Halleck reported that 2100 South is a Utah Department of Transportation (“UDOT”) road and
there is no right-of-way dedication required along that frontage. However, the applicant would
upgrade the public infrastructure above ground so the existing park strip will be removed and the
wider sidewalk installed. She noted that there are no trees along the right-of-way back of the curb.
The UDOT right-of-way is slightly smaller and does not allow for the trees. As a result, the trees
were pulled back behind the sidewalk. Americans with Disabilities Act (“ADA”) clearance needs to
be maintained along that sidewalk, which was the reason the trees will be pulled back. On Major
Street, the right-of-way meets the cross-section width, so no dedication was required. As a result,
there will be a 10.5-foot sidewalk with trees and tree grates along the frontage.

Ms. White Halleck reported that the applicant was in the process of completing a Photometric Study
for the rights-of-way. There are existing streetlights there but the applicant was trying to determine

if modifications need to be made. Depending on the outcomes, there might be Conditions of Approval

District projects relating to sewer infrastructure. Half of the parcel was already annexed into that.

The billboard was discussed. Ms. White Halleck explained that there would be a separate application
not made by In-N-Out Burger. The application would be made by Reagan Outdoor Advertising,

existing three-panel billboard in the northwest corner of 50 East 2100 South. Negotiations were
underway to shift the billboard approximately 100 feet to the east. That would align with the In-N-
Out Burger application but there would be a separate petition.

Ms. White Halleck presented the Conceptual Site Plan and explained that it was color-coded to make
the landscaping and access stand out. The only two points of vehicle access to the site were from
Main Street and Major Street. There is no access from 2100 South. There are 63 total on-site parking
stalls as well as a 34-vehicle queue for the drive-thru. She noted that this will be the largest drive-
thru In-N-Out Burger has done in Utah. For context, the average queue accommodates approximately
12 vehicles. Ms. White Halleck informed the Commission that a resident reached out with questions
about the site and the larger context of 2100 South and Main Street.

2100 South is not a South Salt Lake City road but is a UDOT road that is managed by UDOT. State
Street is also a UDOT road. Main Street, on the other hand, is a South Salt Lake City road from 2100
South going south. Everything north of 2100 South is a Salt Lake City road that is managed by Salt
Lake City and is completely separate from South Salt Lake. Ms. White Halleck explained that a
resident asked about traffic, speed limits, and access to and from the site. UDOT has a standard
classification system that matches up to the U.S. Department of Transportation and Federal Highway
Administration requirements. As part of the South Salt Lake General Plan and UDOT planning, each
road in the county needs to be identified in a roadway classification. In this case, State Street has the
highest classification with the purpose being to serve vehicles to and from interstates. 2100 South
has a similar classification from UDOT. It was considered another principal arterial (“OPA”). Main
Street had a similar classification but it was a South Salt Lake classification and classified as a minor
arterial.

One of the concerns from the residents related to the speeds on the roads. Ms. White Halleck
explained that the posted speeds range from 30 to 35 miles per hour. That is within the U.S.
Department of Transportation and Federal Highway Administration classification average.
Anywhere from 35 to 55 MPH is recommended and on the lower end of the recommendation. At the
current time, there is no proposal to reduce the speed limits further. There could be coordination with
the Police Department to address speeding and non-compliant issues in the area, but the traffic issues
were not necessarily related to the actual design of the road.

Ms. White Halleck noted that another concern from the resident related to bicyclists and bicycle
access to and from the site. Main Street was not identified as the bicycle corridor in South Salt Lake

due to the classification of the road. That was something the South Salt Lake City Engineer would
continue to work through as redevelopment occurred. There was a desire to look at safer bicycle
lanes through the downtown area. Ms. White Halleck shared information about traffic studies. As
part of this process, the applicant completed a traffic study for the proposal. The study looked at AM
and PM peak hours. For AM, there would be no impact, because In-N-Out Burger would not be open

Burger development. The study anticipated 131 new weekday PM hour vehicle trips and net new
weekday daily trips of approximately 1,800 vehicles.
