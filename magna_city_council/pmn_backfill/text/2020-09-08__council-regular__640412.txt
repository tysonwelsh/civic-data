Electronic Board Meeting Determination

                 Greg Schulz read the following statement regarding the electronic meeting
determination:

               Consistent with provisions of the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act, specifically
Utah Code Ann. § 52-4-207(4), and acting in my capacity as the Mayor of the Magna Metro
Township (“Magna”), I hereby make the following written determinations in support of my decision
to hold and convene electronic meetings of the Magna Metro Township Council without a physical
anchor location:

1.     Conducting Council Meetings with an anchor location that is physically accessible for
members of the public to attend in person presents a substantial risk to the health and safety of
those who may be present at the anchor location.

2.     This determination is based upon the following:

       a.      Pursuant to the Public Health Orders issued by the State of Utah and Salt Lake
       County, the Magna Metro Township remains under a state of public health emergency
       related to the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, and significant person-to-person
       transmission of the virus continues to occur in Magna; and

       b.      All other facts noted in the August 7, 2020 Letter of Determination are still in
       effect.

               These written determinations shall be read into the minutes at the beginning of the
Council meeting held in Magna on September 8, 2020, and future Council meetings as
appropriate. Council staff are instructed to include in the public notice of each Council meeting
the information on how a member of the public may view and make comments during the Council’s
Electronic meetings.

              This written determination is effective until midnight on October 10, 2020 and may
be re-issued by future written determinations of the Mayor at that or any other appropriate time.

Reports from other Municipal Service Providers

Unified Police Department (UPD)

               Chief Jason Ackerman stated a week ago, the Magna Precinct got a new
Lieutenant, Zach Van Emmerik who has a lot of experience in SWAT and the Metro Gang Unit.
The precinct is also getting a new Detective Sergeant to help with drug investigations. He is
coming from Midvale, with a lot of experience.

He updated the Council on what has been going on in Magna:

   •   Today, there was a SWAT incident, which took place in close proximity to the junior high
       school. Two people were taken into custody, but the primary suspect is at large.
   •   The Magna Precinct received state funding to target problem crosswalk areas. About 10
       officers were dedicated to that operation.
   •   The Magna Precinct has been fielding a lot of speed and traffic complaints, and recently,
       added a speed trailer, since its last one got hit. Those cost about $15,000, but the precinct
       received a state funded grant for that. It has speed data recording and can track speeds
       and narrow down the times of day speeding is happening. Then, officers can be deployed
       during those times. Currently, two officers are dedicated to speed enforcement and traffic
       problems and are trying to make a difference, but they have to be there at the right time.
   •   The Magna Precinct is trying to leave a bigger footprint on social media, and be more
       available to the community, so the community can keep it in the loop with issues and areas
       that need focusing on.

               Greg Schulz asked if the speed changes along 8400 had made a difference in
speed limits and driving patterns.

                Chief Ackerman stated that is an ongoing process. Some of the engineering will
help for the long term, but he still has officers there regularly fighting violators.

With not having in person meetings, the Magna Precinct loses out on networking and the ability
to address other issues. He suggested having a work session with the Council to discuss law
enforcement practices, policies, and some upcoming legislation to help the precinct understand
what the Council expected of its police officers and to make sure what they were doing was in

Council Reports

Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (GSLMSD)

               Council Member Peay stated the GSLMSD is in the middle of budget and is
asking everyone to cut their budget by 5 percent, or even see if they can cut it by 10 percent. His
personal opinion is that the Magna Metro Township should not cut its budget. Based on the
money it put into that fund, it is entitled to the money for its requests.

                Council Member Prokopis asked what the reasoning was for the request, and if
it was projecting a decrease in revenues.

                Council Member Peay stated there is going to be a revenue shortfall.

             Council Member Hull stated with all the growth and everything else happening in
the community, the Magna Metro Township needs the money that is in its budget.

                Council Member Peel asked if the GSLMSD was cutting its administrative costs
by 5 percent.

                Council Member Peay stated it was going to try to do that.

                 Greg Schulz stated across the board cuts is lazy budgeting. Departments and
agencies that were lazy in budgeting beforehand will get a huge benefit. It is fairly easy for an
agency or department to cut 5 percent when it is getting more money than it put into the pot, but
it is challenging for other agencies or departments. The Magna Metro Township’s current budget
does not even touch 10 percent of its total revenues, and it has been working in a construct that
is consistently lower than what a normal municipality of its size would operate an administration
under.

                                                6
DATE           TUESDAY                               SEPTEMBER                               8, 2020

              Council Member Prokopis stated he had not seen that revenues were going
down. Every time the Council gets a tax revenue notice, its sales tax revenue is up. He did not
want to make a decision to cut the budget until seeing how short the revenues were.

               Mr. Schulz stated Magna is a growth community and will probably see sales tax
revenue increases for the next five to seven years. Revenue is not as rosy for other entities.
Some entities do not make any revenue to speak of. For example, the Copperton Metro Township
only generates about $90,000 a year in sales tax revenue.

            Council Member Peel stated the Council should take a hard look at some
numbers sooner than later because it might be able to get its own services for a good dollar value.

              Council Member Hull stated it is time to look seriously at that. The Magna Metro
township could take care of itself now.

               Council Member Prokopis stated he was not advocating for that. The townships
were stronger together, and the Magna Metro Township should be conscientious about what other
townships were going through. The Magna Metro Township could afford to pay for its own
services, but he did not think it would get the quality it had now.

               Council Member Peel stated it is unreasonable to ask for a 5 percent cut. The
GSLMSD talking 5 percent off a $10,000 administrative budget is way different than what the
Magna Metro Township would have to cut. He was willing to look at doing some cutting, but not
a straight across the board 5 percent cut.

             Council Member Hull stated Mayor Peay should let the GSLMSD that the Magna
Metro Township was not in favor of that. The Magna Metro Township is contributing so much and
needs its administrative fees to handle the growth out here, where other municipalities do not
need that. She was tired of the Magna Metro Township’s needs not being met when it had the
money coming in to take care of those needs. The GSLMSD needed to look at something else.

Resolution No. 20-09-01

               Paul Ashton reviewed the following Resolution regarding Magna’s membership
and participation in the “Urban County” CDBG program and the conduct of the Community
Development Block Grant Program, Emergency Solutions Grant Program, and the Home
Investment Partnership Program:

RESOLUTION NO. 20-09-02                                                 Date: September 8, 2020

       A RESOLUTION OF THE MAGNA METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL APPROVING
       AND AUTHORIZING THE EXECUTION OF AN INTERLOCAL COOPERATION
       AGREEMENT WITH SALT LAKE COUNTY, ET AL, RELATING TO MAGNA’S
       MEMBERSHIP AND PARTICIPATION IN THE “URBAN COUNTY” CDBG
       PROGRAM AND THE CONDUCT OF THE COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT
       BLOCK GRANT PROGRAM, EMERGENCY SOLUTIONS GRANT PROGRAM
       AND THE HOME INVESTMENT PARTNERSHIP PROGRAM

                                           RECITALS

 The Legislative Body of the Magna Metro Township (“Magna”) resolves as follows:

         WHEREAS, the County operates an “urban county program,” as defined by federal
 regulation, in the Community Development Block Grant (“CDBG”), Emergency Solutions Grant
 (“ESG”), and the HOME Investment Partnership (“HOME”) programs administered by the U.S.
 Department of Housing and Urban Development (“HUD”); and


                                                8
DATE           TUESDAY                              SEPTEMBER                              8, 2020

       WHEREAS, the Magna Metro Township has participated as a member of the Salt Lake
County “Urban County” program as Magna does not qualify pursuant to HUD standards to receive
funds directly from HUD and to operate its own CDBG program; and

       WHEREAS, the County has previously entered into three distinct interlocal cooperation
agreements with participating municipalities within Salt Lake County that did not receive separate
CDBG, ESG, and HOME program entitlement grants governing the Parties participation in the
CDBG, ESG and HOME programs which fell short of strictly complying with all HUD-imposed
requirements; and

        WHEREAS, the County now desires to proceed with a single interlocal agreement with all
participating municipalities which does strictly comply with all HUD-imposed requirements and
which supersedes and terminates effective upon all CDBG, ESG, and HOME funds and income
received in the three-year period ending June 30, 2021 are expended and the funded activities
completed, the following interlocal agreements between the County and the above referenced
cities which pertain to participating in the urban county for CDBG, ESG, and HOME Investment
Partnership Programs: Salt Lake County Contract No. BV9303C, Salt Lake County Contract No.
BV03192C, and Salt Lake County Contract No. BV043108; and

        WHEREAS, an Interlocal Cooperation Agreement (“Agreement”) has been prepared for
approval and execution by and between the County and participating municipalities, which states
the purposes thereof, and the extent of the required participation of the parties and the rights,
duties, responsibilities, and obligations of the parties in the conduct and administration of the
CDBG, ESG, and HOME programs as specified therein; and

         WHEREAS, under the Utah Interlocal Cooperation Act, Utah Code Annotated, 11-13-101
et seq. (2020) any two or more public agencies may enter into agreements with one another for
joint or cooperative action and may also contract with each other to perform any governmental
service activity or taking which each public agency entering into the contract is authorized by law
to perform; and

      WHEREAS, Magna desires to continue its membership and participation in the Urban
County Program.

                                         RESOLUTION

       NOW, THEREFORE, be it resolved by the Magna Metro Township Council that the
attached County Resolution and Interlocal Cooperation Agreement between Salt Lake County
and Magna, et. al, hereinafter known as ATTACHMENT A, relating to the conduct of the CDBG,
ESG, and HOME Programs is hereby approved by the Council and the Mayor is hereby authorized
to execute the same on behalf of the Municipality.

      APPROVED AND ADOPTED THIS 8TH DAY OF SEPTEMBER, 2020 in the Magna Metro
Township, Salt Lake County, Utah.

Interlocal Cooperation Agreement with the Unified Fire Authority and to pay UFA $40,954.00 from
Magna’s CARES Act funding for UFA’s COVID-19 related expenses:

       A RESOLUTION OF THE MAGNA METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL APPROVING
       AND AUTHORIZING THE EXECUTION OF AN INTERLOCAL COOPERATION
       AGREEMENT WITH THE UNIFIED FIRE AUTHORITY AND TO PAY UFA
       $40,954.00 FROM MAGNA’S CARES ACT FUNDING FOR UFA’S COVID-
       RELATED EXPENSES

       WHEREAS, the Magna Metro Township (“Magna” or the “Municipality”) recognizes our
need for municipal-types services; and

       WHEREAS, the Unified Fire Authority (“UFA”) is an Interlocal Cooperation Agency that
provides fire protection services to Magna; and

        WHEREAS, Salt Lake County is a County pursuant to Utah Code Title 17: COUNTIES
and is the County where Magna is located; and

        WHEREAS, due to the impacts to state and local governments nationwide, the United
States Government passed the CARES Act which, through Salt Lake County provided Magna
with certain funding that could be used to cover unbudgeted COVID-related costs for the delivery
of municipal-type services; and

       WHEREAS, as Magna’s municipal services are provided by local districts and interlocal
agencies, it is prudent for Magna to assist them with their unbudgeted COVID -related costs; and

agreement) with an invoice and purchase information to request $40,954.00 from Magna’s
CARES Act funds to cover UFA’s unbudgeted COVID-related costs, hereinafter known as
Attachment A ; and

      WHEREAS, providing this financial assistance benefits Magna and softens the impacts
caused to a service provider due to COVID-19; therefore be it

their unbudgeted COVID-related costs.
