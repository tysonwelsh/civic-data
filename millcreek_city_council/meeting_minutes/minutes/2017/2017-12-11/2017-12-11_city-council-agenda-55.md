1. Presentation on Transportation Master Plan and Storm Drain Master Plan project;
John Miller, Associate Director, Public Works Engineering

The Mayor introduced John Miller. Miller explained an overview of the master plans for
transportation and storm drains. The transportation master plan is part of the General Plan,
and it will give an outline of modes of transport throughout the City; vehicles, walking, bus
and bike. The plan isn’t just about roads, it gives an overview of what the City wants in the
future so as development comes, the appropriate right-of-way’s can be preserved. Miller has
been working with J-U-B Engineers on a scope of work for service. Miller showed the
Council the City’s best storm drain plan, which only contained a few lines. He wants a plan

that shows the whole system, and getting a master plan will help to get an inventory of the
whole system, identify where the problem areas are, and come up with solutions for them.
The plan will prioritize the projects as funding becomes available.

The Mayor reiterated that the plan will identify problems with project solutions and will
prioritize those with included costs. Miller showed Lehi City’s storm drain map as an
example of what J-U-B will provide for Millcreek. Mayor Silvestrini asked if having this
plan is part of the MS4 compliance. Miller said the City can be compliant without it, but the
City can’t get any better without it. The plan helps to make decisions with facts.

Michael Lasko with J-U-B said they want to create integration between the General Plan, the
transportation master plan, and the storm drain master plan. The plans will predict where the
immediate needs are so there is tremendous value in integrating these plans together. He said
that creating the plans will take 6 months. The information can be put into the General Plan
and vice versa. The Mayor asked if there is a public engagement component. Millcreek now
has a focused look on a transportation network model because it is independent of the County
and they then can develop a five-year plan for investments. Every five years, updates will be
made to the capital improvements plan for transportation and storm drains. Council Member
Marchant asked what other cities J-U-B has performed this work for; Herriman and Clinton.

Siobhan Locke, J-U-B, said there is public engagement involved in the process. She said they
will talk to the stakeholders and share information with Logan Simpson (General Plan
consultant). The Mayor expressed worry about General Plan fatigue with the public. Council
Member Uipi asked if there are practices used from other cities to model public engagement
towards. Locke said yes, they do targeted stakeholder identification. She wants to know at
what level to engage with the public in relation to the General Plan. Mayor Silvestrini asked
if this plan addresses growth capacity for pavement management in the transportation plan.
Lasko mentioned that they are currently working with Cottonwood Heights and coming up
with a plan for prioritization on deteriorating roads. Miller said he hopes to optimize what the
City has.budgeted for these plans. The budget is for $250,000 for both plans; $100,000 for
transportation and $150,000 for storm drain. J-U-B’s bid for the plans is $240,000. The
monies come from the capital improvements budget line item. Mayor Silvestrini asked if the
storm drain plan would also map the irrigation ditches, yes.

2. Discussion on Storm Water Maintenance Fee

The Mayor said the City storm water system is inadequate. He asked Miller if the townships
are implementing a fee levied through the Municipal Services District (MSD). Miller
affirmed and said the MSD Board will finalize that this week.

John Geilmann asked the Mayor if Rita Lund could introduce her new hires. The Mayor
allowed the pause from the current discussion. Lund stated that Nathan Haynes is the
Emergency Manager and Skyler Stratford will be assisting. They will be working part-
time under the Community Relations Department. They started work today and attended
a meeting with the County today. Lund asked the Council to send any relevant contact
information their way. The Mayor is pleased the City has these roles filled and would like

Millcreek City Council Meeting Minutes 11 December 2017 Page 3 of 12

them to reach out to the community councils next month. Council Member Marchant
asked if they have other related employment. Nathan said no.

Council Member Marchant asked if the plans would include the whole city, because the
displayed map only showed part. Miller said yes. Council Member Jackson asked if the storm
drain system is similar on opposite sides of the City. Miller didn’t know.

Millers said most cities have implemented this fee. The storm drain utility fee allows
earmarked funding for storm drains whereas funding is currently coming from general sales
tax dollars. The intent of it is to have the people who use it to pay their fair share. Miller
explained what an Equivalent Residential Unit (ERU) is. An ERU is based on how much a
residence uses/owes, but using the measurement to evaluate the use and size of other
property helps calculate how much each entity owes. This way businesses, nonprofits, etc.
are paying for clean water too. The Mayor said this is a mechanism to equalize how entities
pay because they impact the system. If the Council moves forward, this funding will pay for
the storm drain projects. Council Member Jackson asked how the ERU gets determined.
Miller said the City has to go through a truth in taxation process, a study is required to set the
amount.

Mayor Silvestrini asked how the transportation plan figures into it. Miller said all of the
storm drains typically follow a road, so the City would want to coordinate projects with street
projects so that new roads aren’t being cut into. Mayor asked if the storm water master plan
will be used to establish the rate; yes. The Mayor asked if another study would be required in
addition. John Brem asked if the fee for the study is included in the contract amount for the
master plan creation. Miller said he took out any reference to a storm drain utility fee in the
agreement with J-U-B for the storm water master plan, he wanted them to be separate, but it
could be added. The information used for the plan will be used for the storm drain utility fee.
Mayor Silvestrini asked how MSD is doing it. Miller said they have hired a consultant to
determine the ERU and will go to commercial developments to determine impervious surface
runoff amount. The consultant will bring the information to the MSD Board for a
determination.

Geilmann said the component of the storm drain fee was removed from the contract because
the Council hadn’t yet addressed it, but it can be added back in. The fee is $10,000. Brem
said the City would attach the utility fee to the resident’s utility bill. Council Member
Jackson asked how the bill would look. There will be an individual line item for storm drain
utility that will come to the City. Council Member Uipi asked how the Council will educate
the residents about it. The Mayor said there first needs to be a study to determine the rate.
The Mayor gave direction to staff to insert the fee study back into the contract. The Council
was in agreeance. Council Member Uipi wants to be transparent to inform the community the
Council is heading down this path. Locke said they will talk to the stakeholders about it and
mentioned that there is a lot of existing information from other cities who have implemented
the fee.

3. Discussion on Splash Pad; Wayne Johnson, Parks and Recreation

Mayor Silvestrini gave background context to the discussion in that he and Council Member
Jackson have been having meetings on the topic. He said residents in the Canyon Rim
community, specifically Nichole Allen, made application and was awarded a Tourism,
Recreation, Cultural, Convention, and Airport Facilities Grant (TRCC) from the County. They
were awarded a $250,000 grant for a splash pad in Canyon Rim Park. He said there are 3 ways
that a splash pad can operate: some are like a swimming pool in that someone must monitor and
treat the recycled water, another is having a reservoir so that the water used by the splash pad
goes into a tank and gets pumped out at night to irrigate the park, and the last, is to pay for the
water and when it’s used that it goes into the sewer and not used again. The Mayor said that the
Council knows that Canyon Rim Park needs a new irrigation system at some point, and the
splash pad reservoir option could be a potential partial solution to that problem. The Mayor
wanted the Council and public to be aware of the splash pad discussions.

Wayne Johnson with Salt Lake County Park and Recreation said the County has 2 splash pads,
Wardle Fields Regional Park at 2700 W. 14450 S. in Bluffdale and Western Springs Park in
Riverton. The Wardle Fields Park splash pad is very large, and the Western Springs Park is
comparable or maybe larger than what is proposed for Canyon Rim Park. Mayor Silvestrini said
the grant application requires a City match. It was expressed that Midvale has a small splash pad
and Cottonwood Heights has one too. Council Member Marchant asked about the maintenance
cost. Johnson said it depends on the size and what type of system is used; treating water is
expensive and there is a cost to pump water back on to the grass too. He said that an additional
parking stall cost is roughly $5000 to install.

Johnson’s recommendation to the City is to engage someone to work with for the site, to
determine the size and design. Then based on that design, the number of parking stalls and
operational cost can be determined. He said it costs about $3.59 per 1000 gallons of water and
the one at Wardle Fields Park costs $1,300 a day to run. He said if there is standing water in the
pad, the health department will issue citations. Council Member Marchant asked how long the
grant would be good for. Johnson replied that it is typically 1 year, but it could possibly be
extended. Council Member Uipi said if the money was repurposed, it would be a budget
adjustment. Johnson said the Council would have to get approval of the TRCC Board for
approval to move the money to a higher priority project. Mayor Silvestrini and Johnson said the
City would need a consultant to help design, plan, and come up with a system. Mayor Silvestrini
thinks that it may make sense to phase the park improvements. Council Member Uipi inquired as
to the liabilities if there would be more people going to the park, because there are more
improvements needed than just a new irrigation system. Council Member Jackson clarified about
having a reservoir tank to water the grass, that it would be two systems. Johnson confirmed that
the tank would not be enough to irrigate the whole park.

Mayor Silvestrini asked if there are people at Salt Lake County that could be used as a consultant
or if a third party would be needed. Johnson recommended an outside consultant to work with
their staff. The Mayor asked what the cost of the study might be. Johnson guessed about $20-
$40,000 and he believes the grant money could be used to pay for the study. Council Member
Marchant asked why the east side of the valley wasn’t considered when installing the existing
county splash pads. Johnsons said Wardle is a regional park and was built from bond money
from 2012. Council Member Marchant asked if the regional park in Millcreek would be
considered in the future. Johnson said the County could, but would need to wait for money.
Council Member Uipi asked why the splash pad couldn’t be at the Millcreek Community Center.

background history on the issue. He pointed out that there are 97 houses on the south side
