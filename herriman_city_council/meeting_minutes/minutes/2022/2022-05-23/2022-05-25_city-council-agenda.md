   2.2    Discussion of potential right-of-way vacation of property located at approximately 5700
          West 13000 South - Blake Thomas, Community Development Director
Community Development Director Blake Thomas explained right-of-way for 13000 South was
preserved to allow for future roadway prior to the incorporation of Herriman. He continued with the
subdivision application submitted which proposed to provide roadway connection through the
subdivision to both 5800 West and Herriman Main Street which does not follow the preserved right-
of-way. He stated staff would like to have the alignment determined as subdivisions are incorporated

east and would meet engineering requirements.

Director Thomas offered scenarios of how the development would be altered if the Council determined
to maintain the existing right-of-way compared to the updated proposal. Council consensus
determined to move forward with the vacation process and relayed their desire to preserve
connectivity.

       7.2 Arts Facility Feasibility Study Update Wendy Thomas, Assistant City Manager
Assistant City Manager Thomas recalled the anonymous donation received to fund the feasibility study
for an Arts Facility. Victus Advisors representative Brian Connolly and Method Studio representative
Todd Kelsey were in attendance to present the findings and market survey for a performing and visual
arts center. The project goals included:
    • Estimate market demand for a new venue that would be beneficial to the local community and
        the greater Sale Lake market
    • Identify existing gaps in arts/events service areas in Herriman and identify potential
        opportunities which could be provided by a new venue
    • Recommend a market-supported building type and develop a sample building program and
        development cost estimate
    • Analyze potential development sites and conduct building program test fits
    • Forecast usage and event days for proposed venue
    • Analyze the potential economic and fiscal impacts of a performing and visual arts venue
The study process was completed in five key phases:
    1. Market feasibility analysis
    2. Market research finding and program recommendations
    3. Site analysis and cost estimate
    4. Operational, financial and economic analysis
    5. Final deliverables
The group discussed the demographic and socioeconomical analysis for Herriman City residents and
engaged with local arts groups, Herriman Arts Council, Herriman High School dance and music
programs, theater, Jordan School District, life house, Mountain Ridge High School Theater Program,
SLCC, Salt Lake Symphonic Winds, and others where it was projected 27 annual performances were
needed for all groups. It was recommended an arts facility should be at least 70,000 square feet with
at least a 600-seat proscenium theater for the community and regional use as well as a 150-to-200
seat black box theater for more diverse event activity. A large lobby which could be used for receptions,
weddings and banquets was also on the list of recommendations.

Annual operations of a new performing arts venue estimated to operate at 46-percent cost recovery.
This pro forma was developed based on the unique market characteristics of Herriman and the
operations of comparable public arts facilities. It assumes the venue would be operated by a public
entity and most of the public arts venues regionally and nationally require annual operating subsidies.
Representative Connolly reminded the incorporation of a performing arts center was to increase the
quality of life for residents in the community and noted youth have better reading, writing, and math
skills and would be less likely to engage in delinquent behavior when associated with the performing
arts.

After viewing seven potential sites, Victus and Method identified Herriman Towne Center as the
primary recommended site for purpose of test fit and cost estimation. The site was closest to
compatible public functions, in addition of being in the heart of the City. There was significant
opportunity to integrate the arts venue with other surrounding public assets to create a dynamic
campus feel.

Councilmember Hodges agreed it would be a great addition to the community; however, could take
several years for adequate funding. Councilmember Shields compared this proposal with the midvalley
center and highlighted the cost increase. It would take a combination of public and private interests to
build and operate the facility and be viable on a long-term basis. The facility could support itself, but
the use would exclude local community use. He stated additional sources of financial support was
necessary and needed to work with partners to bring an arts center to the southwest Salt Lake valley.

Mayor Palmer relayed his desire to see a facility brought to the community and realized it was not
something the City could afford. Councilmember Ohrn agreed it was a great idea along with a
substantial cost.
