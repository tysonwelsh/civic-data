b. ZM-19-001, Billboard Code Updates; Francis Lilly, Planning & Zoning Director
Mayor Silvestrini disclosed that he had a conflict with the matter because he had done legal
work for Reagan Outdoor Advertising, his wife still worked for them, and they contributed to
his campaign for Mayor. He said there were some technical corrections in the original
ordinance that he wanted fixed. He said in paragraph 11 there were references to the County
which were no longer applicable and that the term “chairman” in reference to the community
councils should be “chair.” He felt there were inconsistencies between the proposed sign
ordinance and the proposed billboard ordinance with respect to lighting. Francis Lilly said the
Planning Commission recommended adoption of an updated billboard ordinance. The staff
report presented for the meeting included an analysis of other cities’ ordinances, public
comments received, and comments from the sign companies.

Lilly explained the current billboard ordinance. Billboards are a permitted use in the C-2, C-3,
and M zones and a conditional use in the C-1 zone. They can be up to 35 feet tall in the C-1
zone and 45 feet tall in all other zones. Freeway oriented billboards can be up to 35 feet above
freeway grade level, or 50 feet overall, whichever is greater. Billboards can be no more than
672 square feet in the C-2, C-3, and M zones and can be no more than 300 square feet in the
C-1 zone. Signs must be setback 18 inches from property lines or the future right-of-way line
and 150 feet from any residential zone boundary. They must follow a separation requirement
from each other; signs 300 square feet or less must be 300 lineal feet from each other on the
same side of the street, signs greater than 300 square feet must be 500 lineal feet from each
other on the same side of the street, and all signs must be separated 150 radial feet from each
other. Millcreek imposes a cap on the number and square footage of billboards in the City.
Square footage for signs that are removed can be banked for future use and those square
footage credits expire after 36 months. There are 51 signs in Millcreek, totaling approximately
31,000 square feet in sign area; 17 signs have faces that are in excess of 300 square feet, and
17 have other nonconforming circumstances, such as being a two-decked sign. Lilly
highlighted a map of billboard locations in Millcreek with a concentration on 3300 S., 3900
S., 4500 S., State Street, and along I-15. He said a concern that signs in some areas were too
big near single-family residential prompted the concern for an updated billboard ordinance.
He showed the Council an image of a billboard in the view from a residential zone near 3300
S.

Lilly went over the proposed billboard ordinance. Billboards would no longer be allowed in
the C-1 zone. The sign area would be limited to 300 square feet except along State Street or
along interstate travel when it would be limited to 600 square feet (industry standard for
freeway-oriented signs is 672 square feet). Heights would be limited to 32 feet or 40 feet in
certain circumstances. The exception is whether the sign is relocated to accommodate

redevelopment or not visible within 150 feet of a residential zone boundary. Lilly explained
that 40 feet was the tallest any building can be in Millcreek outside of the City Center and or
the M and MD zones. There would be a 500 lineal foot separation between billboards and 250
radial feet. Billboards would be setback 150 feet from residential zones except if it was not
visible from any point on a property within 150 feet of the residential zone boundary and
would be setback 5 feet from the property line. If the sign was being relocated out of a
community reinvestment area or a redevelopment of an acre or larger, a minor increase in
height or a reduction in separation requirements would be provided in order to facilitate
redevelopment. Billboards would be illuminated so that the direct light source could not be
seen from ground level. There would not be any changes to existing code on electronic
message centers. Each sign company would be required to obtain a single business license
regardless of the number of signs they operated in the City. The proposed ordinance
recommended that the City notify affected property owners within 500 feet of the site to
which a sign was to be relocated under provisions of state law. Billboards would also be
prohibited from the following locations (within 500 feet of):

* Main Street and east of I-215

* 2000 East and 3300 South

* 1100 East and 3900 South

° 1300 East and 3900 South

° 2300 East and 3900 South

¢ 2300 East and Claybourne Avenue

* Highland Drive and Murray-Holladay Boulevard
Lilly said there were no signs at those locations currently and billboards could be difficult to
move. Those intersections were listed because the City had imagined future town centers

Lilly said that all four community councils unanimously recommended approval of the
proposed ordinance. The East Mill Creek Community Council asked that the City have
additional input for where signs were to be located. He brought up the Great Connections
theme from the General Plan; Goal GC-3: Improve the safety and visual image of the
community by enhancing the look and feel of major corridors and protecting our scenic
viewsheds; and Strategy 3.9: Reduce the number, size, and height of billboards along all city
streets. He said there was not an ordinance solution that was good, fast, and cheap. The
community had high expectations to not allow more billboards in the City. He went over other
considerations for billboards in Millcreek.
* The total square footage of billboards in Millcreek is capped.
¢ Many areas in Millcreek are at or near saturation in terms of billboards, in terms of what
the current code will allow and what the market will effectively bear, given existing
limits.
* The combination of State Statute and the billboard bank provision give the City and the
billboard owners an incentive to work out solutions for specific signs over time.
* The cost associated with eminent domain make it impractical for a City to reduce the
inventory of billboards using the method LUDMA provides.

Lilly rhetorically asked the Council for their preference and what tradeoffs they would be
willing to consider. If the preference was for more, smaller billboards, then the height and area
limits as proposed by staff may be appropriate. However, if the preference was to consolidate
the billboard inventory into fewer, bigger signs, then additional height or sign area allowances

c. ZM-18-012, Sign Code Updates; Erin O’ Kelley, Planner

2. Reports from Millcreek Staff
Mayor Silvestrini said the first meeting of municipal jurisdictions on H.B. 411 Community
Renewable Energy Act (2019) was held. He showed the Council a diagram on the program
process timeline. He said the Public Service Commission had adopted the rules without
changes. The jurisdictions would form two committees with city employees and staff;
governance agreement subcommittee and technical subcommittee. The governance
subcommittee would draft an interlocal agreement for the 23 participating entities and may
look at a weighted scale based on population or power usage. The technical subcommittee
would work on rate design and cost projection. Each entity must design their own low-income
plan though there would be collaboration. The entities would come up with a uniform opt out
form and model language for the associated ordinance to adopt. Mayor Silvestrini went
through the program timeline in the handout: governance agreement, utility agreement, low
income plans, opt out form, ordinance language, and rate design/projection in 2020;
application submittal, application filing cost, consultant cost, application approved by the
Public Service Commission, and municipalities adopt ordinance in 2021; and the next phase is
that mailers are sent out, the program is implemented, and residents can cancel. The Mayor
mentioned that Park City and Summit County had hired a consultant to advise them on rates
and hired an energy attorney. Council Member Uipi asked about the consultant cost in 2021.
Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek would share the cost for that consultant but that would be
addressed in the governance agreement. If the Council did not like the agreements, Millcreek
did not have to sign them or participate.
