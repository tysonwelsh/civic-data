Public Hearing

            THIS BEING THE TIME heretofore set for a public hearing to receive public
comment regarding the following requests:

Application #31120 – Wei Li requesting approval of a zone change from R-1-6 (Residential) to R-
1-5 (Residential) on 3.0 acres of property, located at 3054 South 8850 West.

                Ms. Julia Friedman, planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District,
reviewed the application stating the property is located in a neighborhood of existing R-1-6 zoning
located to the west of Cyprus High School. The property is located in a stable area on the General
Plan map, an area that has limited potential for the absorption of growth and unlikely to experience
only minor changes in overall character over time. One of the objectives to the goals of the
General Plan is to encourage residential development that establishes a variety of lot sizes,
dwelling types, densities, and price points, as well as an appropriate balance of owner occupied
and rental units. There were no issues or concerns with this request. The planning staff and
Magna Metro Township Planning Commission recommended approval.

               Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to open the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

                 No one spoke in favor of or in opposition to the rezone request.

               Council Member Hull, seconded by Council Member Prokopis, moved to close the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

              Council Member Hull, seconded by Council Member Peel, moved to approve
Application #31120. The motion passed unanimously.

Application #31117 – Miguel Munevar requesting approval of a zone change from R-2-6.5
(Medium Density Residential) to R-M (High Density Residential) for four parcels on 0.64 acres of
property, located at 2943 S. Dora Street.

                Mr. Travis Hair, planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, reviewed
the application stating the applicant wants to combine the existing four-lot subdivision on the other
side with these four lots to be able to build two five-plexes of town homes, so he would be going
from eight units to ten units. The lots are located to the south of the Oquirrh Hills Apartments, so
there is some high density near these buildings, and the property is located in an area of moderate
change on the General Plan. There is also some commercial area just to the west. Areas around
it are zoned R-2-6.5 and some R-1-6. The R-1-M allows for six stories if they are stacked units.
However, zoning conditions were recommended that would limit the applicant’s ability to do that,
and the applicant has agreed to limit the building height to 35 feet to be in line with residential
standards. The Planning staff recommended the Council review a list of permitted and conditional
uses currently allowed under the Zoning Ordinance, to determine if other uses should be
prohibited on this property. They also recommended an additional condition to limit the number
of units on the combined properties to 10. During the Planning Commission meeting, neighbors
on the four lots to the west were against the rezone because it would add too much density.

               Council Member Prokopis asked what the concerns of the residents were.

             Mr. Hair stated those residents spoke in opposition to the additional density there
because they were concerned about the traffic and the cars.

              Council Member Hull asked where the road would come in and where parking
would be, since parking has been a problem there.

                Mr. Hair stated the road would come from the south, but loop around and dead-
end just to the north. It would not connect to the apartments to the north. Each unit would have
its own driveway and garage

               Greg Schulz stated the ingress/egress would be from Robin Street.

               Council Member Hull, seconded by Council Member Peel, moved to open the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

             Mr. Todd Richards, Magna Metro Township Planning Commission, stated the
concern was Robin Street being a narrow street and not enough capacity for 10 units there. The
Planning Commission voted 4 to 1 to approve it. He was the opposing vote.

                  Ms. Clare Collard, Magna Metro Township Planning Commission, stated this was
a difficult vote. The Planning Commission was looking at it from the aspect of what it was required
to do for zoning.

Ordinance 20-05-01

Ordinances, which repeals Chapter 3.52, entitled “Subdivision and Zoning Fees,” and replaces it
with a new Chapter 3.52 that references the Planning and Development Services’ fee schedule
approved by the Council pursuant to the Magna Municipal Code of Ordinances 3.42.040. The
ordinance also amends Sections 3.48.020, 3.48.025, and 3.48.030 of the Engineering Fees

Chapter to likewise incorporate Planning and Development Services’ fee schedule. The benefit
of doing this is it will bring the fee schedule into better compliance with Title 10, and also lay out

Reports from Municipal Service Providers

Unified Police Department (UPD)

               Chief Jason Ackerman stated one of the Magna Precinct members came down
with COVID six weeks ago, and just came back yesterday. The UPD has been active with COVID,
as well as with what is going on downtown. On the first day of the protest, the UPD got a call for
assistance and sent some officers to help. Then, when everything escalated and Salt Lake City
sent out a valley-wide call for help, the UPD activated everybody. There was some vehicle
damage, but no injuries. A couple weeks ago, there were a couple shootings in Magna. One
victim who got shot will be paralyzed, and the involved shooters have been taken into custody.
The other happened in old Magna and detectives are still following leads on that.

              Council Member Hull stated there was a home invasion in Magna too, and the
guy who did that was caught today in West Valley.

                Chief Ackerman stated West Valley police officers pulled over a car with the
suspect in it and took him into custody. They located some items in the car that led back to the
home in Magna. Officers from Magna then went to check on the home, and found an elderly
female victim in bad shape, and transported her to the hospital.

              Council Member Peel stated he had a citizen reach out to him and commend the
Magna District on the way it handled a call at the Copper Club, and that citizen has not always
been supportive of the police.

            Chief Ackerman stated a lot of community members have reached out to the
UPD. Those are the kinds of things officers love to hear. It makes them feel appreciated.

             Council Member Hull asked what the Chief’s take was on the protests, and how
the UPD was positioned.

                 Chief Ackerman stated the biggest thing is to communicate and tear down
barriers. The UPD will be looking for opportunities to get to know the members in the community.
Several years back, the UPD implemented the use of body cameras and it tracks all uses of force.
In the vast majority of cases, there are no arrests and no use of force. About 5 percent of people
are taken into custody, and the UPD has to use force on less than 1 percent of those people. It
is frustrating for officers who are doing a good job and trying to change perceptions when all it
takes is one bad apple to undo the work they have done.

Council Reports

Unified Police Department (UPD)

                 Council Member Prokopis stated the UPD Board meets next Thursday where it
will entertain the idea of a 4 percent market adjustment for UPD employees. Last month, it was
looking into a 2 percent market salary increase for UPD employees, but today, in a budget and
finance committee meeting, there was a proposal to add another 2 percent for officers. The hope
is to close the gap between the UPD and what other entities pay. If the board approves that, the
request will come before the Council.

               Chief Jason Ackerman stated increasing police officers’ salaries is quite
unpopular, but the reality is, the UPD is down 15 officers. The Magna Precinct is down 3 officers.
People are going elsewhere for employment or they are retiring. The UPD is behind in its hiring
practices due to COVID, and it takes an average of a year for someone to get through the
academy and training.

               Council Member Hull asked how the UPD could recruit amongst the diverse
populations.

               Chief Ackerman stated Sheriff Rosie Rivera has been very proactive about that.
There has been an uptick in female officers applying, as well as Latinos. Recruiting costs money
and the UPD has been through some big budget crunchers lately, but it is doing presentations in
its police academies, so as people near graduation, they are given information on how to apply.

                                      RECITALS

A.    The Magna Metro Township (“Magna”) is a municipality pursuant to Utah Code Annotated
(“UCA”) §§ 10-2a-401 et seq.

B.    The Magna Metro Township Council (the “Council”) is the municipal governing body for
Magna pursuant to UCA § 10-3b-501.

C.     The Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (the “MSD”) is a Local District that
provides certain municipal-type services to Manga which include accounting and financial
management of Magna’s accounts.

D.     Pursuant to State Law, the MSD’s Planning and Development Services Department
(“Planning”) has been providing Magna with Building Permits and Inspections, Business
Licensing, Code Enforcement, and other Planning and Development services.

E.       Due to the transition of Planning to an MSD-Provided Service, Magna’s Municipal Code
needed to be amended to clarify Planning’s ability to impose and collect certain fees as a part of
their office’s work for Magna.

F.       Earlier in this meeting the Council approved and adopted Ordinance 20-05-01 making
those necessary amendments to the Magna Municipal Code allowing Planning to impose and
collect those fees.

G.     This resolution hereby designates Planning as the Planning Department for Magna.

                                         RESOLUTION

             THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED by the Magna Metro Township, the Mayor and
Council hereby designates the MSD’s Planning and Development Services Department as
Magna’s Planning and Development Services Department to fulfill the “Planning and
Development Services Department, or designee” definition form the amendments passed in
Ordinance 20-05-01.
