 4.    PUBLIC COMMENT
 Chair McConnehey opened the public comment period at 6:07 p.m.
 Public Comments:
 Ann Marie Barrett, Bingham Creek Library Manager, spoke of upcoming events at the library and
 was grateful for the opportunity to work in the West Jordan community.
 Lanese Hendrickson, West Jordan resident, spoke of the master plan in place for roads in her area
 when she purchased her home, and said development subsequently allowed by the City had
 caused the plan to change. Ms. Hendrickson would never have purchased her home if she had
 known it would end up between two major roadways. She did not believe all possible alternatives
 had been vetted and asked that the City put a hold on expansion of 9000 South between Mountain
 View Corridor and U-111 until further studies were completed.

 Amelia George, West Jordan resident, said she crossed 9000 South when she walked with friends
 to school every day and walked her dog to the park. She said getting to all the places she needed
 to go would be more dangerous if there was a busy street blocking the way and commented that
 she did not want to live in a noisy neighborhood. Amelia asked that the Council make sure she
 could stay safe in her neighborhood.

 Nathan George, West Jordan resident, said he and his friends liked to ride bikes and play night
 games, and all of his friends lived north of 9000 South. He and his friends crossed 9000 South
 many times each week without an adult and noted that his mom said she would not let him cross
 alone if the planned road went through, especially if crosswalks did not have pedestrian-
 controlled signals. He believed he would be cut off from church, school, and friends. Nathan asked
 that the Council make sure there were safe ways for kids to cross if the planned road went
 through.
 Melissa Hiller, West Jordan resident, said she echoed sentiments against 9000 South going
 through. Ms. Hiller noted that she is PTA President at Antelope Canyon Elementary and expressed
 a safety concern with current plans for a particular business entrance to be 20 feet from the
 school entrance. She asked City representatives to contact the business and request that their
 entrance be moved further from the school for the safety of the children.
 Teresa McEntire, West Jordan resident, expressed the opinion that expanding 9000 South as
 proposed would do the opposite of the goal stated on the City website for the project for the
 families currently living in the area. She asked if anyone present agreed with her sentiment, and
 15-20 hands were raised. Ms. McEntire asked that the City put a hold on the project until all
 options had been considered.
 Laurie Sheen, West Jordan resident, shared the concerns of her neighbors that had been
 expressed. It had been disconcerting to hear from City officials that their neighborhood should
 never have been approved or built. She realized the location of the neighborhood brought
 challenges. Ms. Sheen asked Council Members to use City influence with UDOT on the residents’
 behalf. Their concerns were brought to City representatives many times over the years, and the
 comments went unheeded. She rejected the idea that it was too late to reconsider the plans.

 Jackie Gale, West Jordan resident, provided a packet to Council Members and said her
 neighborhood needed help. Ms. Gale said her son was already nervous to cross 9000 South. For
 her it was heartbreaking that the Mayor told their neighborhood nothing could be done at that
 point, because it meant her son would be cut off from his friends. Ms. Gale suggested there were
 other options available, and asked the Council to put a hold on the project until further
 communication and studies could take place.

 Casey Barns, West Jordan resident, did not understand how the 9000 South project would help
 his neighborhood. He agreed with everything that had been shared during the public comment
 period.
 Chad Lamb, West Jordan resident, appreciated the many citizens who were sharing comments on
 the matter. He believed the City had a chance not to make another mistake. Mr. Lamb asked the
 Council to listen to the residents and not just UDOT.
 Barbara Brindle, West Jordan resident, said she cared about her community. She asked that the
 Council come look at her community and see the families, kids, and homes, and have the
 conscience to make it right.
 Ken McConaughey, West Jordan resident, echoed comments about trucks. His family crossed
 between the two neighborhoods several times a week and did not want to have to “shoot the
 gap”. He believed the neighborhood was special because it was so quiet. Mr. McConaughey
 expressed the opinion that opening it up for traffic was not worth the safety risk.
 Logan Gregory, West Jordan resident, did not understand the drive to punch 9000 South through,
 nor did he understand what the benefits would be. He echoed other comments made and asked
 how many of the Council Members would sincerely want the project in their backyard. Mr.
 Gregory suggested the Council needed to better understand the issue before taking a vote.

 Gale McConaughey, West Jordan resident, said she and her sisters frequently walked around their
 neighborhood. She believed crossing would be unsafe if proper precautions were not put in place.
 She asked if the road must go through, please have proper safety measures in place.

 Brett Seegmiller, West Jordan resident, echoed all sentiments that had been shared that evening,
 and expressed the opinion the project needed more discussion.
 Andrea Schovers, West Jordan resident, agreed with all the comments of her neighbors that
 evening.
 Robyn Moody, West Jordan resident, said the 9000 South project would put people in danger
 because drivers coming off Mountain View Corridor did not slow down. She believed it was a
 safety issue and asked Council to stall the project and come up with a different plan.
 Jeff Newman, West Jordan resident, said he was a neighbor of everyone else that had spoken that
 evening and agreed with all of the comments.
 Amy Hoil, West Jordan resident, lived in the same neighborhood as others who had commented.
 She noted that her main plea was to the City Council and Mayor to please listen to their
 constituents and give them an honest shot at trying to engage in conversations to find an
 agreement or middle ground. She believed in the elected officials.

 5. REPORTS TO COUNCIL
       a. City Council Reports
          Council Member Green mentioned enjoying the recent annual guns and hoses softball
          game and looked forward to the event again next year.

           Council Member Jacob was grateful for the invitation to play with the Police Department
           at the recent softball game.

           Council Member Jacob left the dais at 6:52 pm

           Council Member Pack spoke of attending a recent monster truck event, a recent ribbon
           cutting, and neighborhood meetings.

           Chair McConnehey spoke of attending a recent community event and a neighborhood
           meeting. He mentioned two separate projects: completion of 9000 South between 6400
           West and U-111, and realignment of the intersection of Duck Ridge Way (9000 South) and
           New Bingham Highway. He encouraged Council Members to read the information
           provided to the Council.

       b. Council Office Report
          Council Office Director Alan Anderson and Warren Hallmark provided an update
          regarding the West Jordan Youth Council, and answered questions from the Council.
          Council Members Whitelock and Green had additional feedback they planned to share
          with Council Office staff.

       c. Mayor’s Report
          Mayor Dirk Burton spoke of recent events in the community, reported a donation to the
          Police Department from the Autism Council of Utah, and spoke of upcoming events. Chair
          McConnehey commented that a ribbon cutting for Home Depot was scheduled to occur on
          October 25th.

       d. City Administrator’s Report
          City Administrator Korban Lee reported renovation of the Salt Lake Valley Emergency
          Communications Center (SLVECC) was complete, and SLVECC would celebrate its 35th
          anniversary with an open house on November 15th. Mr. Lee provided an update on the
          wheels park project.
