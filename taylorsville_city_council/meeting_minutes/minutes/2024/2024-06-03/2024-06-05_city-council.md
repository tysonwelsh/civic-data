          1. 2   Mayor' s Report

 ID      Mayor Overson explained that the Taylorsville High Softball team had cancelled their
appearance that evening and would instead be recognized during the June 19th meeting. She then
presented a comprehensive report, highlighting the remarkable success of the recent city-wide
cleanup event. She also mentioned her participation in the Salt Lake County Library's summer
reading challenge and attendance at Arcadia Elementary School's 5th-grade graduation.
Furthermore, the Mayor spoke positively about the free lunch programs provided by Granite
School District as well as Salt Lake County' s My County Rec Pass program. This program
allowed free access to 17 recreation centers in the County for school kids aged 5- 18. Mayor
Overson spoke about how great it was to ensure kids had free access to these amenities.

Mountain America Credit Union held a flag raising ceremony recently at their Sandy
headquarters. Mayor Overson had been in attendance as had other local dignitaries as Mountain
America honored their employees who had served in the military.

The Council of Mayors subcommittee was analyzing potential sites for a winter overflow shelter.
They worked with Salt Lake County, the Salt Lake Valley Coalition to End Homelessness, the
State Office     of   Homeless      Services,   and other cities to review/tour the various sites and
recommend one for the coming winter season.

The Plaza+ Art selection committee had met to select new art to be displayed at Centennial Plaza
beginning in September. The current collection of sculptures could be viewed until that time.
The Taylorsville Arts Council had a crosswalk musical the previous weekend wherein they
would dance in a crosswalk until the light turned green for vehicular traffic. Everyone was safe
and had fun as they promoted the Disaster! musical which would be presented July 8- 13th at the
Midvalley Performing Arts Center.

 The Taylorsville Temple dedication had been held the previous Sunday and it was now open. It
 was hoped that traffic patterns in the area would normalize.


 Taylorsville Park was currently undergoing renovations. A site plan could be found in the lobby
 of city hall for those interested in reviewing it. Mayor Overson pointed out that the City had
 saved up for this project and had planned for it for several years.

         6. 2      Resolution No. 24-12 A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville Adopting a Final
                   Budget for the Fiscal Year Beginning July 1, 2024 and Ending June 30, 2025 —
                   Scott Harrington


                Mr. Harrington explained there had been no updates or changes to this budget since
the Council reviewed it two weeks before.


Council Member Knudsen was grateful that some items had been cut from the budget in light of
the proposed increase to property taxes. He asked if there were any specifics as to what the
increased tax revenue would pay for.

Mr. Harrington responded that it would most likely be for roads, parks, or some combination of
the two. There was then a thorough discussion regarding the proposed tax increase and
allocation for infrastructure, parks, roads, economic development, and police needs.
Taylorsville City Council Minutes

Mr. Taylor added that certain capital projects had been planned and saved for over the past ten
years. An increase was necessary to fund projects for the next decade. This was largely because
Taylorsville was a" pay as we go" city which was preferrable to carrying a large amount of debt.
Council Member Knudsen agreed that it was best not to bond for these things. Still, he wished to
ensure that taxes were not going to be increased more than was necessary.

Council Member Harker pointed out that it was important to remember that decisions were not
just being made for today but also for the City' sfuture needs.
Chair Cochran and Mr. Harrington discussed the fact that costs for many projects were steadily
increasing, so delaying them meant paying higher prices.
Mayor Overson explained that the proposed budget was based on priorities that the Council had
shared with the Administration and also with citizen surveys which identified services and
projects that residents indicated a desire to see. She pointed out that the reason that Salt Lake
County' s Valley Regional Park took so long to complete was a lack of funding. The proposed
budget would make it possible to complete projects rather than leave them half-finished for an
extended amount of time. While there were a variety of services or activities that could be cut
from the budget, there would be a cost to making those cuts( such as a reduction in services). She
stressed that there was nothing extravagant in the budget.

Council Member Knudsen understood the importance of keeping up on infrastructure
maintenance. Still, he wished to satisfy himself as well as his constituents, that the additional
funds were absolutely necessary.

 Chair Cochran noted that several members of the budget committee were present. He asked if
 any of them wished to address the Council, but they declined. Lynn Handy pointed out that he
 had shared his thoughts on the proposed budget at the previous public hearing.

 Council Member Burgess asked if every department' s budget had been examined for potential
 cost savings.   Mr. Harrington indicated that was correct. For example, more than $ 110, 000 had

 already been removed from the police budget.

 Council Member Burgess wished to understand if the City had enough officers for its population
 since it was his understanding that 1, 000 residents per officer was sufficient, and Taylorsville
 currently had one for ever 900 residents. Mr. Taylor explained that the 1: 1000 ratio was
 approximately fifteen years old and no longer held true. Today' s problems and crime were very
 different than they were fifteen years ago.

Council Member Barbieri appreciated everyone' s comments. She felt this was a good budget that
had been well thought out. The Administration seemed to be very cognizant of where each dollar
would go and that was in line with how they traditionally functioned.

Council Member Burgess expressed concern that the City could be over staffing the police
department and was not sure how to investigate that possibility. Mr. Taylor did not believe that
TVPD was overstaffed. He believed that it was on the lower end of average staffing levels but
would have to double- check that. He did know that Taylorsville' s per-officer costs were lower
than     that    of   most   other    nearby   departments.   Council Member Burgess asked for some
documentation that he could review.


Mr. Taylor wished to ensure that the council members understood that the City was currently
building Phase I of the parks improvements which had previously been funded. Phase II was
what the proposed tax increase would go towards.


Council Member Burgess preferred a 10% increase each year rather than one as large as the

currently       proposed     25%.    He was under the impression that the 1: 1000 was ideal, so if we
currently had 900 residents per officer, he did not understand why another officer was being
requested in the budget.


Chief Brady Cottam interjected that South Salt Lake with a population of about 26,000 had 76
sworn officers. Murray had approximately 50,000 residents and he believed they had 70 officers.
Therefore, by comparison, TVPD' s staffing was quite lean, particularly considering the number
of calls for service it handled.


Council Member Burgess found that information helpful.
