business to be located at 5595 North 1070 West in Winchester Hills. Parcel WHS-2-109-SW. The
property is zoned RE-40 (Residential Estates 40,000 sq. ft. minimum lot size). The applicant is
Mel Jensen.

Mr. Messel provided the site location and map of the property. He reported Washington County
Code requires a business license for any person who, whether directly or indirectly, transacts,

engages in, or operates a business within the unincorporated areas of the County. Mr. Mel Jensen
is requesting approval of a business license to operate an auto repair shop called Red Desert Repair.
The business will be located in the detached accessory building north of primary dwelling on the
applicant’s property located at 5595 North 1070 West in Winchester Hills. The accessory structure
is approximately 2400 square feet in size. Mr. Jensen purchased the property in 2019. The home
is owner occupied. Home occupations are permitted in residential zones. Home occupations are
divided into two (2) categories; minor home occupation and major home occupation. Auto repair
is listed as a major home occupation in Washington County Code 10-4:1. The code further defines
a major home occupation as follows:

 Major Home Occupation:
    1. A major home occupation is:
      a. Medium impact in nature in that it may introduce minimal increases in noise, fumes, dust,
lighting, and traffic into the neighborhood but allows the residential character of the
neighborhood to be maintained;
      b. Performed by the homeowner, the family of the homeowner (e.g., children, parents,
siblings, grandchildren, grandparents, nieces, nephews, aunts, or uncles), and:
               (1) Generally, up to one other employee on the property at a time; however,
               (2) If the nature of the home occupation does not bring clientele to the premises,
        then up to three (3) other employees may be on the property at a time; additionally,
               (3) There is no limit on the number of employees permitted for daycares and
               preschools; and
      c. Meets the criteria in section 10-26-2 of this title.

Title 10-26-2 reads as follows:

10-26-2: MAJOR HOME OCCUPATIONS:

The following are requirements of major home occupations:
  A. No storage or display of goods shall be visible from outside the dwelling, attached garage,
or accessory building, except vehicles in running order. If any storage, except vehicles in
running order, occurs outside of an enclosed building, the yard or the area where storage occurs
shall be surrounded by a sight obscuring fence or wall of not less than six feet (6') in height.
Nothing shall be stored to a height greater than the sight obscuring fence or wall.
  B. The home occupation shall provide an additional off street parking area reasonably
adequate to accommodate needs created by the home occupation.
  C. The business owner must provide an accurate description of estimated traffic, on and off
street parking, and deliveries from commercial suppliers.
  D. When a business license application involving a major home occupation is initially sought
(for the first time) or is sought for renewal after a significant change to the home occupation
(typically growth of the business activity), then the application must be reviewed by the County
Planning Commission to ensure compliance with this title. Otherwise, business license renewals
for major home occupations shall be reviewed by the Community Development Department.
(Ord. 2018-1120-O, 8-21-2018)

The above referenced code states that the home occupation shall not detract from the residential
character of the neighborhood. Washington County Code 10-4: Definitions defines residential
character as follows:

“RESIDENTIAL CHARACTER: The characteristics of a neighborhood that make it residential,
i.e., having the feel of being a neighborhood, as opposed to an area that has a commercial,
industrial, or manufacturing feel. These characteristics include, but are not limited to, the intent
of the zone, with careful consideration being given to the permitted and conditional uses allowed
within the zone; the population density of the surrounding area; safety, with particular weight
being given to the safety of children residing nearby; peacefulness that is consistent with a
residential area; whether the home occupation is one generally conducted within a residential
neighborhood; low traffic density; and any other factor that may impact the quality of life that is
customary within the neighborhood.”

In his application, the applicant has stated that he will have only one car at a time and he will be
the only employee. The business will not have any signage. The Planning Commission needs to
mitigate any concerns or issues with this proposed home occupation. If the concerns cannot be
reasonably mitigated, then the Planning Commission may deny the major home occupation.

Recommendation:
Staff has reviewed the application for a major home occupation for auto repair and believes that
certain conditions may be necessary to mitigate the detrimental effects o f a home occupation auto
repair business, and believes the following conditions apply:

 1. Auto repair is a major home occupation.
 2. Major home occupations must be secondary in nature to the primary residential use.
 3. Vehicles, auto parts, junk, rubbish or various other material and goods must be stored in the
    accessory structure or behind a sight obscuring fence or wall around the perimeter of the
    property, or within a fully fenced storage area. All vehicles for repair and all materials shall
    be stored in the fully enclosed accessory structure. No vehicles or materials shall be stored
    outdoors unless fully enclosed behind a site obscuring fence or wall that complies with
    Washington County ordinances. If outside storage is utilized, applicant shall present a site
    plan with a solid fence or wall which shall be reviewed and approved by the Community
    Development Director as a condition of continuing with the major home occupation. Failure
    to comply is grounds for revoking the business license.
 4. No inoperable vehicles, or customer vehicles are permitted to be parked on the street.
 5. The business must be registered with the state prior to receiving the County business license.
 6. That the home occupation must maintain the residential character of the residential
    neighborhood as defined in County Code. No other home occupations are approved at this
    time.
 7. The hours of operation are 8:00 am to 7:00 pm.
 8. Noise such as air wrench, compressor and running of cars shall not exceed levels generally
    accepted in a residential neighborhood.
 9. Any junk, inoperable vehicles, and any materials or vehicles on site for the home occupation
    business must also be stored in a building or behind a site obscuring fence or wall approved
    by the Community Development Director.

 10. No more than one customer at a time.
 11. All oil, antifreeze and other fluids and hazardous materials must be disposed of correctly.

The applicant was not in attendance of the meeting.

The commissioners discussed at length the proposed major home occupation, and concerns that a
car repair shop, if it became busy or if there were too many cars on site, it could negatively impact
the residential nature of the area. The neighbors, the surrounding residential homes, and maps and
pictures of the area were reviewed. The old aerial pictures of the site at issue showing multiple
cars parked on site were discussed, and their negative visual impact to the neighbors and the
neighborhood. The lack of fencing was discussed, the code requirements for fencing, and the types
of adequate fencing were discussed. The recent pictures of the site showing that some of the cars
from the earlier site pictures had been cleaned up, and how it would be important to have conditions
in place to assure that the negative impacts from the business use did not change the residential
nature of the area.
