   a. Fraud Assessment – Utah State Auditor Alert 20-21

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, explained that the Office of the State Auditor
developed a Fraud Risk Assessment to help local government board members understand best
practices in fraud detection and prevention. He said the Assessment assigned an overall risk
level of undetected fraud based upon points earned for the implementation of the identified best
practices. He said the best practices identified in the Assessment were not required, but were
highly recommended, all of the best practices identified in the Assessment may be implemented
at a low or no cost, and the Assessment was not a replacement or duplication of the internal
control assessment performed by an independent auditor as part of a financial statement audit.
While they were both risk assessments, their methodologies and purposes are distinct. Mr.
Hicks said that requirements beginning in 2020 were that all local governments would complete
the Assessment internally, the Assessment must be completed and presented to the governing
board before the end of the 2020 fiscal year, the Chief Administrative Officer and Chief Financial

Officer must certify the results of the Assessment, and the Assessment must be submitted to
the Office within six months after the end of the fiscal year.

Department. He said that occurred during a month when the total volume of traffic stops was
significantly less than other months. He attributed the success to the officers developing their
skills to identify the signs of an impaired driver based on the driving pattern they were observing
on the roadways. He said it was a testament to the officer’s commitment to discourage impaired
driving within the City and save lives on the streets. Chief Hutson then reviewed the Riverton
Police Department Activity Report for the month of May.


Chief Hutson said they were excited to launch a Citizen’s Police Academy, which would be an
opportunity to introduce community members to the operations of the police department. He
said they were actively preparing a curriculum for dates in the fall. He said the academy would
last for six weeks and it would provide opportunities for hands-on demonstration and active

a. Resolution No. 20-48 - To Confirm Riverton City Council Shall Not Provide the Services
   Proposed to be provided by the Riverton Fire Service Area

   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained the following information:

            “The Riverton City Council has expressed the desire to create a new service area
            that would replace Unified Fire Service Area ("UFSA"). To this end, On April 21,
            2020 the City Council adopted resolution No. 20-31 which formally commenced the
            process to begin the creation of the Riverton Fire Service Area, (“RFSA”).
             The next step in the process, scheduled for the June 16, 2020 meeting of the
            Riverton City Council, is to conduct a public hearing which allows the public to ask
            questions and obtain further information regarding the issues contained in the
Page | 9 Minutes – City Council Meeting
June 16, 2020



         resolution No. 20-31. [See: Utah Code Ann. §17B-1-210(2)(d)(ii)]. At beginning and
         the conclusion of the public hearing, the City Council is required to announce the
         deadline for filing protests and generally explain the procedure and requirements to
         create RFSA, or protest the same. [See: Utah Code Ann. §17B-1-210]

         Protests may be filed within 60 days of the last public hearing required under Utah
         Code Ann. §17B-1-210. Because we are scheduled to convene only one public
         hearing, the hearing conducted on June 16, 2020 constitutes the last hearing
         required before the time begins to run for filing protests. The 60-day deadline to file
         protests lands on August 15, 2020; however, this date happens to land on a
         Saturday. This means the proper deadline will carry forward to the following
         Monday, August 17, 2020. To be valid, a protest must be filed with the Riverton City
         Recorder’s office, and signed by the owners of private real property that is located
         within the proposed local district (in the present case, RFSA), or by registered voters
         residing within the proposed boundaries of the district.

         In the event that the land held by persons filing a protest: (a) is located within the
         proposed local district; (b) covers at least 25% of the total private land area within the
         applicable area; and (c) is equal in value to at least 15% of the value of all private
         real property within the applicable area; then adequate protests will have been filed
         to bar further action by the Riverton City Council to create RFSA. Alternatively, if
         protests are filed by registered voters residing within the proposed boundaries of
         RFSA which equal in number to at least 25% of the number of votes cast in the
         applicable area for the office of president of the United States at the most recent
         election prior to the adoption of the resolution, then further action to create RFSA is
         also barred. [See: Utah Code Ann. §17B-1-213].

         A final point is that after opening and closing the public hearing described herein, the
         next step in the process is for the Riverton City Council to adopt a resolution
         indicating whether Riverton City “will provide to the area of the proposed local district
         within its boundaries the service proposed to be provided by the proposed local
         district.” Utah Code Ann. §17B-1-212. Utah law further requires said resolution to be
         adopted within 60 days after the last hearing to consider adoption of RFSA pursuant
         to Utah Code §17B-1-210. Thus, after the June 16 hearing is closed, the Council
         may adopt the enclosed Resolution No. 20-48 but is not required to. Adopting said
         resolution will not affect future steps in the process to create a district, so long as it is
         adopted within 60 days of the June 16, 2020 hearing.”

declared the Public Hearing closed.

    b. Resolution No. 20-41 - Authorizing the City to Elect to Pay the Employee
       Contributions on behalf of the Employee for Employees in the Tier 2 Public Safety
       and Firefighter Retirement System

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, explained that enhancements to Tier 2 Public
Safety & Firefighter retirement benefits would take effect beginning July 1, 2020. He said they
apply to new and current employees in the Tier 2 Public Safety & Firefighter Retirement System
for benefits earned on or after July 1, 2020. Due to the cost of the plan enhancements,
participants in the Tier 2 Public Safety & Firefighter Hybrid plan would be required to make
employee contributions in addition the employer contributions. According to IRS law, an
employer may "pick-up" the employee's portion of the retirement contribution and treat it as an
employer contribution; however, in order to do so, formal action must be taken.

Mr. Hicks further explained that current Riverton City policy stated that Tier 2 Public Safety
employees have an additional retirement contribution amount equal to the difference between
the required employer contributions for Tier 1 employees less the required employer
contribution for Tier 2 employees less 2%. This resolution would only 'pick-up' the employee
contributions to the extent the Tier 2 required contributions, plus the employer 'pick-up' were
less than or equal to the required Tier 1 Public Safety contributions.

Mr. Hicks said that, as a way of following City Strategic Priority #7 - Establish Operational
Excellence, Objective 7.2 - attract, motivate, develop, and retain a high-quality, engaged, and
productive workforce, staff recommended that the City Council approve the proposed retirement
'pick-up'.

Council Member Stewart MOVED that the City Council approve Resolution No. 20-41 -
Authorizing the City to Elect to Pay the Employee Contributions on behalf of the
Employee for Employees in the Tier 2 Public Safety and Firefighter Retirement System.
Council Member McCay SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the
motion; there being none, he called for a roll call vote and the vote was as follows: Council
Members Buroker-Yes, McCay-Yes, McDougal-Yes, Stewart-Yes, and Wells-Yes. The motion

    c. Resolution No. 20-49 - Adopting the Salt Lake County Multi-Jurisdictional Hazard
       Mitigation Plan

Emergency Manager Scott Chatwin presented the following information:

    “Multi-Jurisdictional Hazard Mitigation plans are given a five year duration. The last
    approved plan was in 2015. During 2019 SLCo hired a contractor to develop and
    updating a hazard mitigation plan for SLCo as a whole and all the jurisdictions with-in the
    boundaries. By using a contractor they ensured compliance with all State and Federal
    reporting requirements. This plan has two volumes, the first volume is an overview of the
    county situation, capabilities, gaps and plans for mitigation. Volume 2 has all the local
    jurisdictions including Riverton City.

    The Riverton Annex includes hazard risk ranking, hazard profiles for threats to Riverton
    City. Three new mitigation actions have been added for the 2019 plan, to go with the 17
    on-going mitigation actions from 2014 and 2009 actions that are on-going. By identifying
    specific hazards and threats to Riverton City other businesses and residents can use
    this list to plan mitigation and response plans for their specific locations. The federal
    center for medical & medicaid services (CMS) requires medical businesses to build
    response plans using local jurisdictions hazard threats.

    This plan was also developed to make Salt Lake County and participating jurisdictions
    eligible for certain federal disaster assistance, specifically, the Federal Emergency
    Management Agency’s (FEMA) Hazard Mitigation Grant Program and Pre-Disaster
    Mitigation program, and to earn points for the National Flood Insurance Program’s
    Community Rating System (CRS), which could lower flood insurance premiums in CRS
    communities.”

Mr. Chatwin addressed questions from Council Members.

Council Member Stewart MOVED that the City Council approve Resolution No. 20-49 -
Adopting the Salt Lake County Multi-Jurisdictional Hazard Mitigation Plan. Council
Member Wells SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion; there
