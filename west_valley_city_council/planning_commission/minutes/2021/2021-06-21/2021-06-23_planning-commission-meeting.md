designated as business park in the West Valley City General Plan.

Surrounding zones include A to the south, east and a portion of the north and BRP to the west and

vacant.

building. Also included is a letter from the applicant. Since the proposed building will include a
light industrial use, the City’s commercial design standards will apply. Curb, gutter and sidewalk

GPZ-12-2021
Marco Diaz
4612 West 3500 South
General plan change from General Commercial to Medium Density Residential and a zone
change from C-2 to RM
1.0 acre

Marco Diaz has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for a 1.0 acre parcel located at
4612 West 3500 South. The property is currently zoned C-2 (General Commercial) with a General
Plan designation of General Commercial. The proposed zone is RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling
Residential) and the proposed General Plan designation is Medium Density Residential (7 to 12
units/acre).

The zoning and General Plan designation on this property were changed back in August of 2019.
The previous zoning was R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 SF) and
the previous General Plan designation was Low Density Residential (3 to 4 units/acre). At the time,
the proposed land use was a dermatologist office. The dermatologist that was going to build here is
now moving to a new office building at 4133 W Pioneer Parkway.
Surrounding zones include R-1-8 to the north, east, and west and C-2 to the south. Surrounding land
uses include homes to the north and west, a City park to the east and commercial to the south. The
subject property is currently vacant; however, in 2014 a home that was previously on the property
was demolished.

The applicant is proposing to develop the property into a 10-unit, 3-story townhome development.
Each unit would be 1,876 sq. ft. and include 3-bedrooms. While the property is 1 acre in area, the
south portion of the property will need to be dedicated as it falls within the existing road
improvements along 3500 South. The resulting density after dedication would be about 12
units/acre. The attached concept plan and application materials from the applicant provide details on
and support for the proposed project.

In reviewing this application, staff suggests that there are two primary questions to answer:

   1. Is this the right location for a townhome development?

   2. Assuming the location is appropriate, does the quality of the project further City goals?

These two questions are explored below.

Is this the right location for a townhome development?

According to the City’s zoning ordinance, zone changes to the RM zone can only be considered by
the City if the following four conditions are met:

   1. For properties without existing Structures which are proposed to be developed, the property
      must be a minimum of one acre and the Density must not exceed 12 units per acre. For
      properties with existing Structures proposed to be redeveloped, the property must be a
      minimum of 20,000 square feet and the Density must not exceed 20 units per acre. For
      properties that are west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West Valley
      Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station or the Redwood Junction light rail
      station, the Density may exceed 20 units per acre;

   2. The property shall meet any one of the following four requirements: (i) have access and
      Frontage on a Street with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet (for properties
      under two acres) or 100 feet (for properties two acres or larger) as indicated on the Major
      Street Plan; (ii) be adjacent to existing multifamily residential development on two sides; or
      (iii) be located west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West Valley Central
      light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station, or the Redwood Junction light rail
      station; or (iv) if the property is vacant, was vacant as of January 19, 2021, will be an
      exempt housing development under 24 CFR 100.304 or successor regulation with a density
      no greater than seven units per acre, and have access and Frontage on a Street with a
      planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet;

   3. A Development Agreement must be proposed with the zone change application that
      addresses Dwelling Unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, Landscaping, and project
      Amenities; and

   4. A traffic impact study prepared by a registered traffic engineer is required for projects with
      100 or more Dwelling Units.
On the first condition, the property is one acre in area and the density is 12 units/acre. For the
second condition, the subject property has access and frontage on 3500 South, which has a planned
right-of-way width of 115’. On the third condition, the applicant’s development agreement
standards are addressed later in this report. The fourth condition does not apply because the project
is under 100 units.

While the subject property is eligible to be considered for RM zoning, the City can still deny the
zone change request. Potential reasons for denial include the existing homes on two sides, the tight
setbacks proposed for the 3-story buildings, the relatively small size of the property, or the fact that
the property has been zoned C-2 for less than 2 years.

Does the quality of the project further City goals?

If the Commission believes that townhomes are appropriate here, the quality of the proposal should
then be considered.

Dwelling Unit Sizes
The proposed unit size is 1,876 square feet. Each unit will have three bedrooms. The ordinance
requires a minimum area of 1,400 square feet for a three-bedroom townhome.

Exterior Materials
The ordinance states the following concerning exterior materials: “All multi-unit residential
Building exteriors shall be brick, stucco, stone, tile, metal panels or fiber cement siding. With the
exception of brick or stone which may be used on the entire Building exterior, no single material
shall comprise more than 60 percent of a Building’s exterior. Where stucco, tile, metal panels or
fiber cement siding are used, at least 20 percent of the Building’s exterior shall be brick or stone.
Wood and vinyl may be used as trim, soffits and/or accent materials only.” The proposed materials
are brick and fiber cement siding.

Architecture
The renderings provided don’t match the floor plans and don’t provide enough detail for staff to
evaluate whether they comply with the City’s design standards. Staff is not comfortable
recommending a development agreement with a rendering that doesn’t reflect what is actually
proposed and would prefer more detail on the proposed product.

Landscaping/Recreation Space
Townhome developments require at least 50% of the site as landscaping and recreation space. The
latest concept plan does not include a landscaping and recreation space percentage so this will not
be addressed in the development agreement and the project, if approved, will be required to meet
the minimum percentage.

Project Amenities
For a project of this size, the ordinance requires one amenity. The proposed project includes one
amenity from the ordinance – a covered pavilion.

Other Considerations

For the RM zone, the Zoning Ordinance states: “For properties adjacent to residential uses with
buildings with two stories or less, the maximum building height at the minimum side and rear yard
setback shall be 25 feet. For each foot of height over 25 feet, buildings shall be set back an
additional foot from the minimum setback, with a maximum total building height of 75 feet.”
This increased setback standard would apply to the north and west property lines. Assuming the
buildings are 30’ tall, the required rear setback for buildings would be 25’. Along the west side, the
concept plan shows a setback of 15’. As an alternative, staff prepared a rough concept with two 5-
unit buildings that exceed the setback requirements.

A significant amount of fill has been brought to the site. Near the north end of the property, the
grade of the property is nearly as tall as the neighboring fences. According to the applicant, he plans
to bring the grade down to native soil before excavation of the foundations.

There is an existing 6’ tall vinyl fence along the north side of the property that is in good repair.
However, the wood fencing along the west side is, in staff’s view, in need of replacement.

Staff conveyed to the applicant the concerns on the setbacks and architecture. The applicant
indicated that he would work on revisions to be submitted prior to the Planning Commission public
hearing. Without revisions, staff would not recommend approval.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. This General Plan/zone change application should be approved subject to a
      development agreement that incorporates the standards proposed by the applicant with the
      following additions recommended by staff:
          a. All standards in the City’s Multi-Unit Residential Design Standards shall be met.
          b. Setback requirements of the RM zone shall be met.
          c. Fill dirt brought to the site shall be removed so that the grade is restored to the native
             soil before excavation of the foundations.
          d. A 6’ tall vinyl or masonry fence shall be installed along the west property line.
          e. The developer shall dedicate right-of-way along 3500 South in accordance with the
             Major Street Plan, which shows the future right-of-way on 3500 South at 115’.
          f. Upon completion of all of the on-site improvements, the developer shall provide a
             letter to the City from an independent engineering firm which certifies that all of the
             on-site improvements are completed as shown on the plans approved by the City. For
             the purposes of this provision, on-site improvements shall include landscaping,
             irrigation, private streets, sidewalks, parking, lighting, entrance features, fencing, and
             any other outdoor recreational amenity.

   2. Continuance.

   3. Denial. This property should remain commercial.


Discussion:    Steve Pastorik presented the application. Commissioner Woodruff asked if the side
               of the subject property viewed from 3500 South will be something other than a blank
               wall. Steve said that the ordinance requires design relief and other design features

               present to the Commission today so, this item could be continued to allow them to
               view the side and rear elevations.
               Marco Diaz, the applicant, explained that they had changed their original concept
               plan per Steve's recommendations and neighbor's concerns while still meeting the
               goal of this project. He said the most recent concept plan depicts a good project
              allowing ample space between the property in question and the neighboring
              properties. In addition, Mr. Diaz stated they want to meet all the ordinances outlined
              by the City.
              Colt Piper, an adjacent property owner, expressed that he is against this proposal.
              He explained that they purchased this property because there was not someone living
              directly behind their home. Mr. Piper said he would prefer a two to three-story
              commercial building over the proposed townhome development.
              Mr. Piper asked Mr. Diaz if he was inquiring about purchasing more land. Mr. Diaz
              explained they had been looking to buy more land. However, that is no longer in
              their plans. He stated the first set of plans we submitted did not leave much room
              between properties, so they changed their plans to increase the setback between the
              subject property and all neighboring properties. Mr. Diaz explained that he wants to
              minimize the impact on the neighbors.
              Mr. Piper stated that he is also concerned about the possible increase in traffic and

              Commissioner Wood said that she feels this property is too small for a townhome

              Commissioner Lovato said there had been conversations before about maintenance

CA-4-2021
SLC4 – Aligned Technologies Data Center
6368 West Beagley Rd
M zone, 5.65 acres

The applicant, Aligned Data Centers, is requesting a conditional use amendment for a data center
and equipment yard at 6368 West Beagley Rd in Building 1 of the 201 Mountain View Park
(MVP - Hines). The zoning for this property is M, Manufacturing and the General Plan
designation is Light Manufacturing. The surrounding zones are Manufacturing (M). The property
to the northwest is zoned A, Agricultural with general plan designation of light industrial. To the
south across Beagly Rd. (2540 South) is a canal trail system and the area is zoned A-1,
Agricultural with general plan designation of large lot residential. The surrounding uses are
office/warehouse (ARA) to the west and north, the other 201 MVP buildings to the east and vacant
undeveloped land to the south.

Aligned Technologies Center has recently purchased this site to develop a data center and
equipment yard. The use will be located in the south end of building 1. The overall square footage
of the building is 97,563 sqft. There is currently a tenant leasing the north half (14,262 sqft) so
Aligned Technologies Center will occupy the remaining 77,627 sqft with plans to expand at some
point in the future to occupy the entire building.

As outlined in the attached use description, the building itself will be used for the data center
servers (Data Halls) and equipment (Power Rooms) as well as the office functions (Front of
House).

This project also includes an equipment yard on the outside of the east side of the building. This
area will be developed in phases with the overall build-out being approximately 520’ x 90’ and
46,800 sqft in area. This yard will be used to house the cooling equipment, power transformers
and utility meters, electrical containers and backup generators. This area will be screened by a 19’
tall, perforated metal screen system that sits on a 4’ tall concrete stem wall. Staff has worked
closely with the applicant to ensure the proposed screening is appropriate for this use and location.
More specific design details of that screen system have also been provided for review.

The exterior of the east side of the building will be modified to remove the overhead doors. There
may be some slight modifications to the landscaping to accommodate the screening, but the
majority of the landscaping will remain as originally designed and installed. There will also be an
8’ black wrought iron fence installed on the west and south side of the building for security
purposes.

Lastly, since this use does require a substantial amount of power, Aligned Data Centers is working
directly with Rocky Mountain Power (RMP) on a Duct Bank System that will be connected to the
Lake Park Substation. This work is all being coordinated with RMP, UDOT and WVC Public
Works to ensure the proper easements and construction permits are in place.

Staff Alternatives

Approval subject to any issues raised at the public hearing as well as the following conditions:

    1. The site and equipment screening shall be constructed as presented to the Planning
       Commission and as outlined in the approved plans for Aligned Technologies Data Center.

    2. Must meet the requirements of all affected departments and agencies including but not
       limited to Rocky Mountain Power, UDOT and West Valley City Public Works.


Continuance, to allow for the resolutions of any issues raised at the public hearing.

C-14-2021
Winco Pad E
2616 S. 5600 W.
C-2 zone, 1.26 acres
The applicant, Petersen Staggs Architects, is requesting conditional use approval for a retail center
with a drive-thru at 2616 S. 5600 W. The zoning for this property is C-2, General Commercial and
the General Plan designation is General Commercial. The surrounding zone is C-2 on all sides. The
surrounding uses include the WinCo shopping center to the north and west, a multi-tenant retail
building and a Maverik gas station to the south.

This property is located within the WinCo Shopping Center (C-8-2008, West Valley Pavilion). This
parcel was initially improved as a parking area and part of a larger lot. It has since been divided to
create a parcel for this retail tenant (Lot 9). The building is proposed to be approximately 1900
square feet and will contain a drive-thru. The building will be constructed of stucco with a stone
wainscot and columns with steel canopies as accent features. The building must comply with the
Commercial Design Standards as well as the Development Agreement for the overall project which
requires that the east and west sides of the buildings be considered a primary façade for the
requirements of the Design Guidelines for Commercial Buildings.
Therefore, additional design elements will be necessary to the building façade as well as the roof
treatments as discussed with the Planning Commission in the study session.

This property is located within the 5600 Overlay Zone. The building does have a drive-thru and is
therefore subject to the use restrictions as outlined in 7-14-404. Staff will continue to work with the
applicant to ensure the proposed tenant mix is in compliance with this section of the City Code.

The overall shopping center has adequate parking to accommodate this use and the development of
this lot. Parking requirements will be based off the use so staff will continue to work with the
applicant to ensure the parking provided can accommodate the proposed uses. Staff will also review
traffic circulation, pedestrian connections and landscaping requirements to ensure all requirements
in the City Code are met. The landscaping along 5600 West, which is a High Image Arterial, is in
place as required and will remain. There will be a dumpster provided for this building that will be
located within a 6’ masonry enclosure that is constructed to match the building.

There was an overall sign package that was approved for this shopping center when it was
originally developed in 2008. No additional, ground signs are proposed with this building
development and only wall signs will be allowed.

Staff Alternatives

Approval subject to any issues raised at the public hearing as well as the following conditions:

    1. The building must be completed per the approved plans and be designed in accordance
       with the Commercial Design Standards and the Development Agreement.

    2. The site shall be landscaped per the approved plans and all existing landscaping must be
       maintained and/or replaced if it is in poor condition.

    3. This use is subject to section 7-14-404 and must comply with the use restrictions set forth
       in the City Code.

    4. The dumpster shall be completely screened and secured with a 6’ masonry enclosure that
       matches the building.

    5. There shall be additional ground signs (Monument or Pole) and all wall signage must
        comply with the West Valley City Sign Ordinance.

    6. Must meet requirements of all affected departments and agencies.

    7. Subject to review upon valid complaint.
