Presentations

       Process for Selection of Food Trucks at Riverton Town Days and Friday Fun
       Nights

Craig Calvert, Contract Administrator and Purchasing Manager, said that Councilmembers had
requested a summary of the vendor selection process, particularly in relation to interactions with
local business owners. The City sent out a Request for Proposal (RFP) for vendor participation,
which had a submission deadline of February 8. The City limits participation in events like the
rodeo and Riverton Town Days due to space constraints and to avoid having duplicate vendors.
Multiple vendors selling the same items can result in revenue loss and participant
dissatisfaction.

He explained that the RFP was distributed to a vendor list maintained by the recreation
department throughout the year. Approximately 95 email notices were sent out, and the RFP
was made available for about 10 days. Once the responses were received, they were evaluated
based on specific criteria outlined in the RFP. The criteria for evaluation included favorable
participation and performance in Riverton City events or similar government-type events, booth

appearance, space requirements, compliance with health department regulations (for food
vendors), and selling prices of items. Vendors were scored based on their responses to these
criteria.

Sheril Garn, Events and Operations Director, displayed a map of the City Park and explained
that both vendors and food truck spaces were full, leaving no available space. She said last
year the Mayor had received two complaints, one from a vendor who felt there was insufficient
parking for vendors and another from a resident who expressed concerns about vendors
occupying all the parking spaces.

She said that due to safety considerations, a second row of vendors was not feasible as it would
interfere with the fireworks' firing line.

Councilmember McCay suggested adding a bullet point to the Strategic Priorities to encourage
Riverton business participation in Riverton events, including the rodeo, town days, and fun
nights. She emphasized the importance of offering opportunities to Riverton businesses and
suggested making it the highest criterion.

Councilmember Pierucci suggested broadening Councilmember McCay’s suggestion to include
a line about promoting Riverton businesses in general and exploring opportunities to do so
through City policies.


Discussion Items

         Budget and Roles Discussion

Mayor Staggs explained his proposal regarding the appointment of a city manager and the
potential name change to "city administrator." He clarified that the proposal was not aimed at
changing the form of government but rather establishing a better process for the Mayor, as the
Chief Executive Officer, to appoint a city manager who would be confirmed by the Council. He
thought this process would enhance efficiency and avoid potential issues and inefficiencies that
could arise when multiple elected officials appoint the same individual. He emphasized that the
Council would still have ultimate control over budget and policy decisions, while the Mayor
would focus on executive and administrative responsibilities.

Councilmember Wells stated that he did not want to see the Mayor position reduced to a
figurehead and highlighted the accountability that comes with being the face of the City.

Councilmember McCay discussed a budget item related to the request from Jordan School
District to contribute to the busing expenses for students from Western Springs to South Hills
Middle School. She acknowledged her reluctance to spend additional funds but recognized the
importance of providing safe walking routes for students. She supports the proposal to allocate
up to $40,000 per year, covering half of the cost, to support the busing until a safe walking route
is established.

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained that the bridge installation would eliminate the need for
crossing guards or busing and mentioned that the responsibility for providing safe pedestrian
routes falls on the cities according to the statute. He acknowledged that assessing the bridge's
structural integrity and retrofitting it for aesthetic purposes might take some time, possibly up to
Page | 3 Minutes – City Council Meeting
June 6, 2023




a year. He also mentioned ongoing communication efforts with the school district and promised
to provide email updates as more information becomes available.

Councilmembers were supportive of the $40,000 bus expense and the suggestion that this
come out of the general fund.

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, referred to page 27 of the budget and highlighted
line item 10-51-630, which currently has $5,000 allocated for abatement expenditures. He said
staff's recommendation is to increase the abatement line item by $5,000 to a total of $10,000,
and increase the litigation line item, specifically 10-52-315, by $20,000. This would allocate
more resources to code enforcement, weed control, and property cleanup. However, he noted
that if the Council believes a higher amount is necessary, they can recommend a larger
increase.

Mr. Carter said the objective is not to hire a crew of full-time employees for property cleanup.
Instead, the goal is to have funding available to hire a landscaping company as needed for
abatement tasks. This approach is more efficient as it allows the City to contract out the work to
experienced professionals who can handle landscaping needs, such as mowing and weed
control, for both public and private properties.

Councilmember McDougal expressed frustration regarding certain incidents where the City had
taken legal action against property owners but observed that the properties in question
remained in a state of disrepair. He emphasized the need for a stronger approach, specifically
financial resources, to address situations where property owners repeatedly ignore their
responsibilities.

In response, Mr. Carter said a tactical decision was made to settle with a property owner rather
than pursue the matter in court. He clarified that he was not involved in directing that decision.
He said that negotiating settlement deals with property owners who have a history of neglecting
their properties may not be an effective solution.

Councilmember Buroker asked if the suggested budget amounts would allow for litigation and
clean-up of properties. Mr. Carter responded that in previous years, the City has never had to
spend more than $5,000 out of pocket for property cleanups. He emphasized that while some
seed money is needed in the budget, it is likely that most of it will be recovered from the
property owners, and he was confident that a budget amendment requesting additional funds
would not be necessary.

Mr. Hicks said that bids for three playground pavilion replacements were received over a month
ago, but it was determined that the work cannot be completed before June 30. He asked the
Council whether they were comfortable rolling over the project into the next budget year. All
Councilmembers were supportive of rolling over this expense.

Chief Blair Barfuss, Riverton Police Department, addressed the Council and emphasized that a
significant portion, approximately 60 to 70%, of the police department's dedicated time is spent
on traffic accidents and related issues. The Chief further explained that approximately 80% of
community complaints received by the department are also traffic-related, including concerns
about safe streets and walkways for students. The Chief noted that motorcycles were
historically discouraged for deployment, but the department currently possesses three
motorcycles that were inherited in poor condition from Unified Police after the split. With officers
recently completing motor certification courses, the Chief proposed utilizing motorcycles for
Page | 4 Minutes – City Council Meeting
June 6, 2023




traffic enforcement at key intersections, construction sites, and school crossings, citing data that
indicates 70% of accidents occur at two specific intersections. He explained that by investing in
two motorcycles per year for a two-year lease or similar arrangement, the upfront cost would
amount to $60,000 per year. The Chief highlighted the lack of proactive enforcement at present,
as the department primarily responds to traffic accidents rather than actively working to prevent
them. Considering the community's repeated concerns about traffic issues and the ongoing road
construction, the Chief asked the Council to consider funding for the motorcycles.

Councilmembers were supportive of Chief Barfuss’ request for additional motorcycles.

Councilmember McCay expressed a personal preference for having a city filled with trees and
questioned the policy that was considered at the previous Council meeting of not replacing
trees. She cited an example of a tree in her neighborhood that was damaged by a young
individual, and she hoped that such trees would be replaced. In response, Councilmember
Buroker said the Council trusts the department's expertise in choosing which trees to replace
and in choosing the appropriate trees for the City.

Councilmember McDougal informed residents about an upcoming change from TransJordan
Landfill regarding the disposal of green waste. Starting on December 1 of this year, the landfill
will no longer accept any green waste into the recycle program. The last opportunity to obtain
mulch or recycle green waste will be in the spring of 2024, and it will be on a first-come, first-
serve basis. This change is occurring for two reasons: the land that was being used for the
green waste station is being encroached upon by South Jordan, and the lease for that land has
been lost. Additionally, this change is also in anticipation of the dump being completely full
within the next seven years. Plans are already underway to transition and relocate the facility to
a more southern location.

Councilmember McCay raised two points. Firstly, she noticed that the strategic priorities
document indicated an adoption date of April 5, 2022, but she recalled making updates during
work sessions and wanted the communication department to review the notes for accuracy.
Secondly, she expressed concern about the removal of the project manager position. She
emphasized the importance of having a project manager to oversee various projects, especially
considering the number of grants the City had received and the need for effective
communication with elected officials.

Mayor Staggs stated that he viewed project management as a principal role of the city manager.
He believes that the city manager, with a project management background and general
business experience, should be responsible for overseeing projects. He mentioned the
possibility of considering a shorter-term contract for project management assistance or
incorporating project management requirements in vendor contracts for specific projects like
trails and broadband.

Mr. Necaise suggested using consulting firms or external companies to handle project
management tasks and inspections for specific projects similar to what was done on the
secondary water meters project. He said that for the secondary water metering project, a
separate contract was issued for the installation, and then a separate contract was also issued
for project management and inspections. The consultant or consulting firm worked with the
awarded contractor to determine the scope of work and provided their proposal for handling the
project management aspect.

Mr. Necaise also said that for the city park project, a specific person or consultant was hired to
oversee the project management responsibilities. This approach allows for effective
coordination and management of the project, even if it may incur additional costs.

Mr. Necaise said that for future projects, the City can assess the scale and complexity of each
project to determine whether it can be managed internally or if it would be beneficial to hire a
consultant or project manager separate from the contractor. This decision can be made based
on the specific needs and requirements of each project.

Councilmember McDougal agreed with Mr. Necaise and believes this approach allows for
flexibility and cost-effectiveness, and the City can tailor the resources to the specific needs of
each project. As long as there is a reporting component in place to ensure transparency and
effective communication, he supports the recommendation made by the staff.

Councilmember Wells said when the city manager position is posted and interviews are
conducted, they may be able to find someone with innovative ideas on how to handle project
management. He emphasized the importance of accountability and regular updates in
managing projects effectively.

Councilmember Pierucci asked how the process has been for hiring a project manager given
the labor market. In response, Mr. Necaise said it has been extremely hard to find a project
manager, very few applicants and not enough qualifications.
