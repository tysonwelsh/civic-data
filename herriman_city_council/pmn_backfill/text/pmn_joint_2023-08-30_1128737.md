 2. Discussion Items

       2.1. Discussion regarding a proposed Housing and Transit Reinvestment Overlay
            Zone (HTROZ) – Michael Maloy, Planning Director

              Planning Director Maloy disclosed discussion has been on going with Olympia’s
              development partner and city administration about Housing and Transit
              Reinvestment Overlay Zone (HTROZ). The project would potentially modify the
              land use map for Olympia by adding a new HTROZ. It would include an overlay
              tool which the city would create and adopt. If the plan was applied the city would
              need to discuss how it would affect the land use, the design standards, and the
              MDA itself for Olympia. There are multiple steps involved in creating this project.
              The project potential amends the number of dwelling units allowed in Olympia. H
              TROZ requires an average minimum of 50 units per acre within an 1/3 mile of a
              transit hub. The city doesn’t have a transit hub yet so we would need to work with
              the State to create a future transit hub. There are a lot of hurdles to get there.
              Planning Director Maloy asked the Commission and Council to discuss the options
              for moving forward with the project. Discussion needs to include amending the
              MDA to align, reviewing the state code requirements, the benefits to the city and
              the developer, and are there any risks.

              City Manager Nathan Cherpeski divulged there is a limited number of Bus Rapid
              Transit (BRT) lines in state law. The development team has already met with staff
              and talked to the council a few times. A consultant would need to be hired to
              proceed with the project. If we are interested in doing this, we need to get a plan
              set in the near future. The State only considers HTROZ’s a few times a year and the
              zone must be established before you can apply for the HTROZ.

              Planning Director Maloy pointed out creating the overlay tool is only one step.
              Applying the overlay would be the second step. The city only needs to create the
              overlay tool to apply for the HTROZ.
              City Manager Cherpeski directed attention to the point that the city currently
              doesn’t have a bus rapid transit. UTA requires the city to show the ridership before
              they will put it in place, but the city must zone the area like it already exists.

           Councilmember Ohrn queried for the ¼ mile perimeter from a BRT from station, If
           the units are not within the ¼ miles, how do you get within this, how far out are the
           numbers and does this delay any commercial development going in?
           City Manager Cherpeski provided the developer is proposing to UTA that they put
           in a BRT and dedicate property to this area.

           Commissioner Fenn reiterated since there is not an existing station, Is this a
           prerequisite to getting one approved? Mayor Palmer noted the city doesn’t want to
           approve a bunch of housing for a transit that may never come to this area. There
           would be a contingency to approving the housing only if the BRT is approved.
           This will also augment the commercial area, they (Olympia) will be able to go
           above and beyond their minimum amounts.

           Councilmember Henderson stressed UTA doesn’t have to wait for the city. The city
           has already donated land to UTA for fixed rail, but UTA has come to meetings
           saying they don’t have any plans to build in this direction. For anyone wondering
           why you would do this, the answer would be state tax money. The state has taken
           away the sales/property tax discounts as an incentive. This new bill gives back
           some incentive. It provides a way to do it “if” you fit all the criteria. There will be
           a mass amount of density but also a mass amount of commercial. There could be
           some shifting and moving of density in the area. The first step is to have this tool
           in motion to get to the next step. But the city doesn’t have to follow through
           completely with approval. All we are looking at right now is to create this overlay
           tool.

           Commissioner Fenn inquired somebody has investigated this and said this is a good
           idea, listing pros and cons, correct? Councilmember Hodges expressed this was
           originally brought to the city by the developer. Councilmember Ohrn questioned
           where South Jordan’s zone has been approved? Mayor Palmer offered South
           Jordan was approved by the new ballpark, they are adding a new Trax stop.

           Councilmember Henderson reiterated the city hasn’t committed to any of this. We
           are just open to the idea. But we need to do a few of these things now to see if it is
           a possibility down the road.

           Mayor Palmer offered at the last Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC) meeting
           the discussion of extension of the UTA red line was mentioned for the first time in
           a few years. If the red line does extend years down the line this would be a tool to
           help in this matter.
           Councilmember Shields emphasized during the annexation of Olympia the city
           wanted to know how much commercial would need to be created. The U111 and
           12600 s connection was a big decision on how much they could bring versus what
           South Jordan might do. With this mapped out now there is a large area for
           commercial with 3 sides this intersection inside of Herriman. The conversation has
           now changed to whether a mass transit center could bring in more commercial, but
           for this to happen this tool must be in place for these things to happen.

           Councilmember Henderson stated in his opinion this is worth it if it brings in the
           needed infrastructure and commercial for the state to help. If the state tax incentive
           increases, this is our opportunity to build commercial and get this revenue.
           Councilmember Shields affirmed South Jordan has said they are only building
           housing in the area across from this area more than double the density than
           Olympia. WFRC wants to create west side “town centers” with employment,
           services, and transportation so people are not traveling across the valley and stay in
           the southwest region. There is a potential tax rate decrease with something like
           Farmington Station size commercial area with large housing density across the
           street.

           City Planner Maloy stipulated the development partners need an incentive to
           preserve these town centers that become the draws. He believes this is the right
           incentive for this growth. This tool would bring in a commitment for a transit hub.
           If you can’t add the infrastructure, then the growth needs to go back where it
           already exists.

           Councilmember Shields specified this isn’t just about Olympia. There will be a ton
           of traffic when it’s built out and even more with South Jordan’s build. They are
           predicting 12600 south to look like University Parkway. This could bring 70,000
           people between those two areas. If it brings a transit hub to these people, allowing
           them to connect to the larger public transportation infrastructure the city has to
           investigate the possibility. Councilmember Henderson stressed usually with a hub
           approval the road funding will follow. Councilmember Hodges predicted PID’s
           will play a big role in this, get the money upfront, bond for it and get out in front of
           the infrastructure and when government money comes through it can get right to
           use. Councilmember Shields reiterated if this includes a PID, the tax dollars would
           be immense. Commissioner Oberg inquired if the city gets a hub to get this
           incentive for commercial would the hub turn into a rail line in the best-case
           scenario. Mayor Palmer specified no; this is only rapid transit bus. It sounds like
           the red line rail wants to go down the point.

           Commissioner Rypien stipulated his concerns is most of us don’t use mass transit,
           he thinks what UTA is doing isn’t working. He likes the other benefits but doesn’t
           believe this would help traffic. Commissioner Sickles noted the amount of acreage
           in this overlay would be minimal. This is not the entire Olympia area.

           City Manager Cherpeski refined the density units per acre only applies per
           residential units or mixed units. Commercial doesn’t count for residential density.
           There is a min of 40 units with a max of 49 units.
           Commissioner Sickles requested the difference between what we have established
           and what we would need to add. City Manager Cherpeski specified in the hundred
           acres it’s 19-20 units per acre or an additional 1400-1800 more units. They (the
           developer) still need to park it. The plans had some structure parking to
           accommodate this.

           practice or shopping center. Commissioner Rypien believes with the culture of

           housing costs and then add owning/driving a car the cost of living is high. If there
           is a way to take out the car from the equation your housing cost goes down. It can

           attention to the southwest part of the valley having the highest cost of living due to
           cost of commuting, higher water rates and other things we have talked about.

           a chance to get in on these opportunities. To bring the attention of the right people
           into this area and show the ballpark, the Riverton campus, and Olympia are a
