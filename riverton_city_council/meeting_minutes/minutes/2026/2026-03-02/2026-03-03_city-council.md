       Budget Study Session on Operational Budget and Debt

Nick Geer, Administrative Services Director, introduced a discussion of the City’s debt and
operational budget, outlining existing bond obligations, vehicle leases, and other reportable debt
items before explaining that departments build operational budgets from the ground up each year
and highlighting structural changes such as moving animal control to police operations, shifting
street lighting costs to road funding, reorganizing debt funds, and directly charging internal utility
costs rather than using accounting offsets.

Mr. Geer clarified that the City previously adopted the “non-reciprocal” accounting method for
internal utility but now recommends directly charging City departments for water and sanitation
services to simplify reporting, eliminate required public notices and hearings, and better reflect
actual usage. He noted that this approach is common among other cities, aligns with accounting
standards, and will improve transparency and accountability as the City continues reviewing
operational budgets, including culinary water costs and projected increases such as the anticipated
rise in Jordan Valley Water purchases.

Kevin Hicks, City Manager, discussed the implications of House Bill 501, explaining that earlier
versions of the bill would have required cities to set water rates at a level tied to 1.5% of local
median income, which initially suggested Riverton’s total water revenue would need to increase
dramatically. After revisions, the bill no longer mandates those rates but instead makes that
threshold a requirement for cities that want to qualify for state funding for water infrastructure
projects. Based on updated calculations using per-capita income, Riverton would still need to
significantly increase its water revenue, potentially approaching about $15 million annually
compared to the current roughly $7 million, to qualify for such funding by the 2031 timeframe. This
could affect future councils’ ability to access state assistance unless the City focuses on long-term
rate planning.

Mr. Geer reviewed several budget areas and proposed adjustments across multiple areas of the
general fund, including council expenses, city management, utilities, public works, recreation, fire,
code enforcement and parks. Increases were noted for necessary litigation, insurance, utility, and IT
expenses, while some areas saw minor decreases due to reduced activity or reallocation.

Mr. Geer discussed the City’s newsletter and recreation calendar distribution methods.
Councilmembers expressed the importance of maintaining physical copies for residents who do not
use digital platforms, particularly older residents. Council also requested a cost breakdown of
printing versus postage for the next meeting to determine a recommended approach.


         General Plan Discussion

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, provided an update on the next steps for the
City’s general plan priorities. He explained that instead of focusing on individual properties or
designations, the Council and Planning Commission will review broader strategic priorities. The
goal is to develop a clear, public-facing version of the priorities while maintaining a more detailed
working document for internal planning, budgeting, and CIP alignment.

         Ordinance No. 26-04 – “Priority Builders Rezone,” General Plan Amendment and
         Rezone of Approximately 0.71-acres located at 1758 and 1742 West 11800 South.
         Changing the General Plan Designation to High Density Residential and the zoning
         to RM-8-“SD, a multi-family residential zone up to 8 dwelling units per acre

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained the rezone was proposed for two
lots at the northwest corner of 11800 South and Redwood Road. The request is for RM-8 SD
zoning, matching the surrounding RM-8 multifamily development, with the SD designation limiting
it to townhomes only. The property is part of a larger area controlled by Priority Builders, who plan
to assemble the remaining lots along 11800 South to complete the development. The General
Plan would be updated to medium-high density residential for these lots, and the Planning
Commission has recommended approval.

Scott Yermish, Applicant, explained that he has spent over a year improving and unifying the
area, with the current rezone focused on the Baird property to move the overall development
forward. He noted a nearby UDOT-owned parcel that may be acquired later to prevent
commercial use. The proposed development preserves the character of the existing neighborhood
while offering updated designs and slightly larger homes (around 2,220 sq. ft.).

Mayor Buroker called for public comments; there being none, she closed the public hearing.

Councilmember Pierucci MOVED to adopt Ordinance No. 26-04, “the “Priority Builders
Rezone,” amending the General Plan Designation to High Density Residential and rezoning
the subject properties to RM-8-SD for the approximately 0.71-acres located at 1758 and 1742
West 11800 South, with the following Specific Development (SD) Designations: 1. The
development shall be limited to “townhomes” only. Councilmember Haymond SECONDED the
motion. Mayor Buroker called for a vote; the vote was as follows: Haymond-Yes, Johnson-Yes,
Pierucci-Yes, and Smith-Yes. The motion passed unanimously.


         Ordinance No. 26-03 – “Quinn Rezone,” General Plan Amendment and Rezone of
         approximately 0.97-acres located at 1980 West 13400 South. Changing the General
         Plan Designation to Medium Density Residential and the zoning to R-4, a single-
         family residential zone with up to 4 dwelling units per acre.

Mr. Lethbridge explained that the proposed rezone concerns the property at 1980 West, 13400
South, currently under 1 acre and containing an existing home and outbuildings. The parcel is
uniquely split between R3 and R4 zoning, a remnant of prior actions when multiple lots were
combined, resulting in a mix that doesn’t practically allow additional subdivision. The surrounding
area includes a variety of zoning types, including quarter-acre lots created prior to City
regulations. The applicant requests R4 zoning across the entire parcel to align with the southern
portion and provide consistency along the block. However, the Planning Commission reviewed the
request and recommended denial, noting the unique circumstances and limited practical effect of
the existing R4 portion. Options presented to the Council include extending R4 for consistency or
leaving the rear as R3, with staff providing sample motion language for either approach.

Jeff Quinn, Applicant, explained that the intent was to subdivide the property into quarter-acre lots,
retain the existing home on the front portion, and allow their son, Dallas, to build on the rear lot
accessed via a private lane. He clarified that only one additional home was planned, that one

Greg Knight addressed the Council to clarify that his concern at the prior hearing had been

Craig Bird expressed opposition to the proposed private lane development. He raised concerns
about potential overdevelopment, comparing it to other areas where private lanes led to cars
parking on streets and traffic congestion. He emphasized that a private lane would place utility and
maintenance responsibilities on future residents and suggested that if additional homes were built,
a standard 24-foot street with curb, gutter, and a cul-de-sac should be required.

Randy Christensen expressed opposition to the proposed rezone and private lane. He clarified that
he did not park on sidewalks and described the area’s existing traffic congestion, particularly during
morning and afternoon peak hours, which affects his daily routines. He noted that many lots in the
area were originally half-acre, reduced over time due to rezoning and road widening, and
referenced prior Planning Commission minutes recommending no additional private lanes,
emphasizing that any new development should include a standard street rather than a private lane.

Cindy Lloyd spoke regarding concerns about the proposed private lane, noting that her driveway is
just south of the planned access and is already difficult to exit, especially when turning west. She
emphasized safety concerns as a cyclist, explaining that additional private lanes increase traffic
near the bike lane, making it harder to see and safely access.

Mayor Buroker called for additional public comments; there being none, she closed the public
hearing.

Mr. Lethbridge addressed a question regarding noticing, clarifying that all notices are generated
through the Salt Lake County recorder’s office system. He explained that the property in question
is listed as a protected, private lot in the County records, which prevents an address from being
produced for mailing. He emphasized that this designation is beyond the City’s control, and while
the City has a legal obligation to provide notice, in this situation the County-generated mailing list
could not deliver a notice to that property.

Councilmember Smith expressed a preference for consistency with the surrounding R3 lots and
suggested a possible compromise: rezoning the front portion to R4 while keeping the back portion
as R3, which would limit development to no more than two or three homes on the 0.97-acre
parcel.
