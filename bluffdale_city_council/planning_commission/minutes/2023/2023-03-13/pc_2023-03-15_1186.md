4.     CONSIDERATION AND VOTE on a Proposed Site Flan for the City of Bluffdale Fubhc
       Works Complex located on 7»42 Acres located at 3301 West 13800 South. City of
       Bluffdale. Applicant (Application 2023-01)- Staff Presenter. Grant Crowell»

Chair Cragun reported that the above agenda item would not include a public hearing. The Planning
Commission would be making the final decision on this application.

Community and Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, presented the staff report and
stated that it is an amazing project being proposed by the City ofBluffdale. Currently, Bluffdale City
has a small Public Works facility on Redwood Road north of the cemetery. The City outgrew that
facility many years ago and a significant amount of time has been spent identifying a location and
saving up for a new Public Works Complex. The proposed location is on the edge of the City
boundary. It is in the R-1-43 residential zone, where public uses are allowed as a permitted use. The
site plan specifies that 13800 South, a minor collector road, is north of the subject property, and
Asherwoods Cove,a private road, is to the east ofthe subject property. That road will not be utilized
as an access point for the subject property to minimize any impact on that road.

Mr. Crowell next presented the site layout. There is a large L-shaped main building, along with some
accessory structures for salt storage, a vac truck, bulk materials, and places for vehicles. There is also
a retention basin, parking area, fencing surrounding the 7.42-acre site, and open and secured areas.
The main building will be 34,737 square feet in size and the auxiliary buildings will be up to 11,324
square feet in size. There will be a lot of open yard area, concrete, and road base. The maximum
allowable coverage in the zone is 30% with the current site plan being well under that. There will be
parking for staff, the public, and training participants. There will also be an operations area and stalls
for large vehicles on the site.

Mr. Crowell reviewed the architecture for the main building and noted that the architect was present
to answer any questions. The main building will primarily consist of CMU block construction,
architectural metal panels and accents, glazing, and windows focused on the public-facing areas. The
design is intended to be durable and have the needed longevity for such an important municipal
facility. No stucco or Exterior Insulation and Finish System ("EIFS") was planned as part of the
proposed design. There were also elements included that were reminiscent of City Hall.

The facility will be very functional with bay doors to allow for large trucks, skylights, and aesthetical
appeal firom the street. He reviewed the elevations for the areas facing the public on the west, east,
and south. He reviewed the design proposed for the building that would be used for salt storage. It
will be on the southwest portion of the site against the canal. Some of the buildings will not be
completely enclosed such as the facility for the vac truck.

Most of the site will be protected by an eight-foot concrete wall for security. The public side will
have open steel fencing to provide visibility without compromising security. Landscaping is focused
on the parking lot, frontage area, street trees, and low water use. The middle ofthe site will not have

landscaping because it would be too impractical with large trucks coming and going from the site.
The Lifting Plan was designed to minimize light pollution in the surrounding areas.
Mr. Crowell reported that the Engineering Department did a lot ofthe civil work,including a review
ofthe Traffic Study prepared by Hales Engineering, which was included in the meeting packet. Hales
Engineering's judgment was that there would not be any significant traffic impacts as a result ofthe
proposed facility. The drainage plan was found to meet City Code.

In response to Commissioner Kraupp's question regarding funding ofthe facility, Mr. Reid reported
that the proposed facility will house two of the three departments in Public Works consisting of the
Water and Streets Departments. The Parks Department will remain in the current Parks Building on
the Rodeo grounds. The Water Department will pay for halfofthe building, which is a non-General
Fund or Enterprise Fund. The City will bond for the rest ofthe building. The City is looking to make
a down payment of$5 million to reduce the amount to be financed. Mr. Reid reported that when the
Utah Department of Transportation("UDOT")took over Porter Rockwell Boulevard they indicated
that they would give Bluffdale the difference between what the City paid for a local road and what
the City actually paid for the road that was built to UDOT's standard. The refund amounted to
approximately $7 million. About $5 million of the refund will be committed to this project. The
project itself will cost $15 to $16 million. The bond that was submitted will not exceed $16 million.
Consequently, the anticipated bond will be for approximately $11 million.

In response to Commissioner Kraupp's question regarding the project timing, Mr. Reid indicated that
work would begin as soon as possible. Mr. Reid reported that there had been some debate about
whether City Hall or the Public Works Building should be built first. It was ultimately decided that
City Hall would be constructed first. He explained that a Public Works facility should be built on at
least seven acres and the current Public Works facility is on just under two acres. Public hearings for
the facility were conducted two years ago. The design shows the canal on one side, Bangerter
Highway on the north, and a road on the east. One home touches the subject property in the rear.
Two years ago, those residents were amenable to the proposed facility. The goal of the design was
to have a minimal impact on the area.

The Project Architect, Scott Holmes, gave his business address as 577 South 200 East in Salt Lake
City. Mr. Holmes reiterated that the proposed design included shared design themes with the City
Hall and Fire Station 92 on the east side ofthe City. A particular focus ofthe design was to ensure a
minimal impact on the surrounding area. He confirmed that public hearings have taken place with
particular emphasis on the neighbors nearby to gamer their support and address concems. Frontage,
beautification, and setbacks were designed to soften the transition from the residential to the proposed
project. Mr. Holmes showed what the view of the site would be from Asherwoods Cove to confirm
their effort to soften the transition from the adjacent neighborhood to the proposed project. The
surrounding landscaping will buffer the noise and light emanating from the site. The appearance of
the buildings will be similar to City Hall and the Fire Station. Mr. Holmes presented a rendering of
fleet maintenance bays, which will be placed behind a secured fence.

In response to a question raised by Chair Cragun,it was confirmed that 13800 South will be the only
access to the subject property. It was noted that certain parts ofthe site will be secured.

Mr. Holmes confirmed that the surrounding walls will be consistent with what was shown presented.
Mr. Crowell added that the R-1-43 Zone requires 10-foot setbacks from the accessory building to the
property line. Because the proposed site is in the R-1-43 Zone,the same buffer requirements imposed
in commercial zones do not apply. However,it is typical for an eight-foot wall to separate commercial
from residential sites.


Public Works Director, Shane Paddock reported that an Open House took place about two years ago
to share the vision of the project with the public. The subject property has been the first choice for
this facility. Fliers were delivered to every home in Asherwood and Palisades with 25 to 30 residents
attending the Open House. He specifically met with residents who live on the south side ofthe subject
property. At that time, they were amenable to the City's plans for the property. The eight-foot wall
on the property line would buffer the facility from the adjacent neighborhood. There are also areas
on the site plan that describes the anticipated paving that will occur. Bulk materials to be stored on
the site would include sand and gravel, salt, road base mix, cold mulch material, and other materials
commonly used by the Public Works Department.

Mr. Paddock clarified that truck traffic to bring bulk materials will be sporadic and infrequent. The
most frequent delivery would be for the salt storage beginning in late October and continuing for as
long as dictated by the weather. Those deliveries will typically take place during the day. The current
work hours are from 7:00 a.m. to 3:30 p.m., and there are no weekend work hours. The Division of
Water Quality has shut down maintenance in the City. Currently, the City is not allowed to use the
vac truck for storm drain maintenance or mainline breaks. They are also not allowed to sweep the
roads or clean storm drains because the City does not have the facility to handle the materials. The
current salt storage site is inadequate and will likely be shut down. It was stressed that the City is in
great need ofthe new facility.

The timing ofthe construction ofthe facility would be about 1.5 years. In the meantime,the City will
be able to function but not to the degree it needs to because there is the potential for fines to be
imposed for the City not complying with the regulations imposed by the Division of Water Quality.
Mr. Paddock reported that to date the Parks Department has been able to meet its needs at its current
location. The Public Works Department is approximately 25 employees short of where it should be
to meet the City's maintenance needs. He felt that the new facility would make it easier to resolve
that employee shortage.

Commissioner Swanson thanked. Paddock for his due diligence in addressing the concerns of the
neighbors.

Chair Cragun commented that although there was no public hearing conducted tonight, she received
an email from someone asking if the developer plans to fence offthe existing homes that will be torn
down. Mr. Paddock responded that the area will be fenced before construction begins and the homes
will be razed before starting the project.

Mr. Holmes reported that the vehicular flow design was intended to minimize any backups. It will
consequently reduce the noise created by trucks backing in and out. There will be security perimeter

       Text Amendments to the Bluffdale City Land Use Ordinances Regarding Accessory
       Dwelling Units.Bluffdale City Code("BCC")Chapter 11.340 and Associated Definitions
       in the BCC 11.20, City of Bluffdale., Applicant. (Application 2022-491- Staff Presenter,
       Grant Crowell.


Chair Cragun reported that the public hearing for the above item remained open from the previous
meeting. The Planning Commission was asked to forward a recommendation to the City Council.

Mr. Crowell thanked the Planning Commission for their feedback from the previous meeting. He
made changes to the proposed text amendments based on that feedback. Mayor Hall expressed deep
appreciation for the thoughtful consideration the Planning Commission gave to the proposed text
amendments. Pending concerns were design standards and the maximum and minimum size of
Detached Accessory Dwelling Units(DADU). Other concerns pertained to whether paved parking
should be required.

Mr. Crowell reported that he asked the City Attorney to review the draft, as well. Mr. Crowell
provided a brief overview of the changes made to the document. One question that was addressed
with the City Attorney was whether a homeowner could have both an Internal Accessory Dwelling
Unit(lADU)and a DADU. The legal opinion was that a homeowner could have both provided they
have adequate parking designated for the residents of both.

Chair Cragun asked that the document be reviewed from beginning to end.

      Mr. Crowell presented the Definitions Section and reported that there are two types of Accessory
      Dwelling Units ("ADU") consisting of lADUs and DADUs. Mr. Crowell clarified that if an
      individual wants to have a legal ADU the homeowner must occupy one of the units. Chair Cragun
      asked why the ADU has to be a long-term rental unit. She gave an example of a person building a
      DADU and allowing a family member to live in it rent-free. Mr. Crowell stated that he could allay
      that concern by specifying rental or occupancy.

      Commissioner Luker asked why the 30-day requirement was included in both the IADU and DADU
      definitions and not just consolidated by specifying the requirement for all ADUs. Mr. Crowell
      indicated that that would be an option. He did not object to redundancies when rules are being
      articulated. The 30-day requirement could be applied to ADUs,lADUs,and DADUs.

      Mr. Crowell next reviewed the Scope Section. Commissioner Kraupp asked Mr. Crowell to explain
      the thought behind setting the square footage and the percentage level requirements. Mr. Crowell
      stated that the current City Ordinance does not have a 6,000-square-foot limitation for lADUs. The
      requirement stipulates anywhere a parking space can be placed. State Code specifies that ADUs do
      not have to be allowed on lots smaller than 6,000 square feet. Beyond that minimum lot size
      requirement, ADUs must be allowed. Mr. Crowell opted to emulate the State Code. There are parts
      ofIndependence that could have lot sizes that are smaller than 6,000 square feet. The main concern
      at the State and City levels is the provision of an off-street parking space for an ADU. The 10,000-
      square-foot minimum requirement for DADUs was based on a discussion he had with the City
      Council.


      Commissioner Flynn considered the minimum requirements to be overly permissive, especially for
      Independence, where the lots are small and cars are abundant, which makes parking challenging. Mr.
      Crowell explained that the requirement cannot be more stringent than State Code. As a result, the
      lADU requirement cannot change the minimum lot size requirement to anything greater than 6,000
      square feet. Therefore, the proposed text amendment would be less permissive than what the City is
      currently allowing for lADUs. The State is currently not addressing DADUs. Mr. Crowell noted that
      paragraph A would be less permissive than the current norm. Paragraph B has no guidance fi'om
      State Code.


      Chair Cragun asked if it was still the intent to use the R-1-43 Zone as the starting point for DADUs,
      as discussed at the previous meeting. Mr. Crowell explained that that requirement would eliminate
      Spring View Farms and Parry Farms, which are the primary neighborhoods with 10,000-square-foot
      lots and other areas that are zoned R-1-10. Chair Cragun noted that Spring View Farms and Parry
      Farms have Homeowners Associations ("HOA"), so they may have Codes, Covenants, and
      Restrictions ("CC&Rs") in place that prohibit DADUs. Mr. Crowell stated that the Planning
      Commission will forward a recommendation to the City Council who will make the final decision.
      The consensus of the Commission was to support Paragraph A, as proposed. Paragraph B could be
      higher. Commissioner Luker felt that 10,000 square feet would be an appropriate lot size for a
      DADU. Commissioner Flynn felt that R-1-43 should still be the starting point.

      Chair Cragun remarked that State mandates should not be the only consideration. She felt that
      Bluffdale needs to do its part to address the affordable housing problem. She also thought a lot about

property rights ifthe parking stipulation can be met. Commissioner Flynn did not want to exacerbate

Mr. Crowell stated that since there is a lack of consensus on certain aspects of the proposed text

Mr. Crowell next addressed the Approval Process, which is an administrative matter. He explained
that to make an ADU legal, an application must be submitted to the Zoning Administrator and a
permit issued. During that process, a Building Permit must be obtained to ensure compliance with
the Building Code. The applicant must also confirm that they occupy one of the units. With regard
to Paragraph B, Mr. Crowell explained that a Land Use Permit runs with the land and the notice is
posted on the property record that is maintained by the City. The City Council does not want to
require an annual permitting process although some cities do have that requirement. A similar
requirement would be onerous and require more staffthan the City has currently.

In response to Chair Cragun's question about current non-conforming individuals as it pertains to
Paragraph E. Mr. Crowell stated that that issue is addressed later in the document. Some non-
conforming instances will have to be grandfathered into the new Code. With regard to the last
sentence in Paragraph B, Mr. Crowell stated that it was the applicable Building Code when the ADU
was first created and not necessarily the current Building Code. Mr. Crowell added that in the case
of non-conforming ADUs,the main concern would be the assurance that the ADU is a safe, habitable
place.

Mr. Crowell next addressed General Accessory Dwelling Unit Standards. In particular, only one
lADU is allowed in a home. An ADU cannot be in a mobile home or where there is an inadequate
septic system. ADUs cannot have separate utility meters to prevent the splitting oflots. A property
owner can allow a family member to stay in an ADU for free but it must not be used as an Airbnb.

Chair Cragun asked what other cities do with their percentage allowance ADUs. Mr. Crowell offered
to research that question. He noted that some lots in Bluffdale are very large.

Commissioner Luker asked ifthe suggested minimum of400 square feet would fit in the size category
of the document. It was noted that there is no minimum size requirement for an lADU because it
might make it impossible for a homeowner to create a basement apartment as an lADU.

Mr. Crowell explained that Paragraph I specifies that the homeowner must live on the property where
an ADU is located. Paragraph I includes exceptions to that requirement. The homeowner can live in
the larger or smaller dwelling unit. Commissioner Kraupp asked how compliance would be enforced,
particularly in a scenario where the property owner has sold the property and vacated the primary

residence but the renter still lives in the ADU. Mr. Crowell stated that Code Enforcement has to wait
until there is a complaint before verifying non-compliance. Prosecution ofcode violation takes place
in a criminal court, so the City must have clearly defensible evidence to present in court. The City is

Commissioner Kraupp said that the language needs to be changed to accommodate the fact that a

Mr. Crowell explained that although Paragraph K does not allow a property owner to have both an

their property, they may not rent out their current lADU. It was clarified that this is a zoning issue.
Mr. Crowell indicated that he would need to revise Paragraph K which states that property cannot
contain both a DADU and an lADU. Ifthe parking requirement is met, that should be sufficient.

Paragraph L addresses parking. The parking provided for residents of ADUs must be an all-weather
surface rolled with compacted road base, asphalt, or concrete. Chair Cragun considered these options
to be reasonable. Mr. Crowell emphasized that ADU parking must be off-street. Commissioner
Flynn addressed the number of parking places that might be needed to allow for off-street parking.
There was discussion regarding the merits of basing the number of parking stalls on the number of
bedrooms. The current requirement for lADUs is one parking place.

Paragraph Q regarding addressing was an issue because it might give the impression of splitting a lot
and make it confusing to sort out the mail for the residents of the primary dwelling and the ADU.
The consensus was to allow the use ofunit numbers. The Post Office would also likely be supportive
of the use ofseparate mailboxes.

Paragraph R was not seen as a problem in terms ofallowing an ADU to run with the land.

Mr. Crowell reported a meeting is scheduled for April 5 to continue the discussion on the text

findings.

authority for subdivision plats. He assumed the Governor would sign the bill into law. Previously,
the City Council was the approval authority.

Changes were also made to some noticing requirements. Some laws passed that deal with the number
of submittal reviews can take place on a proposal.

Mr. Crowell reported that the Utah League of Cities and Towns("ULCT") has some open training
for Legislative Updates on April 5 and a Housing Training on March 29. The training is free and
can be accessed via Zoom.


Mr. Crowell reported that the Grand Opening ofthe new restaurant went well. It took two years and
three months from the first phone call to the City to the Grand Opening.

In response to Commissioner Flynn's question regarding the Holiday Oil status, Mr. Crowell reported
that it is coming along. The owners are determined to get it done in spite of the hurdles that have
impeded progress. In addition, a site plan for a 7-Eleven on Porter Rockwell and Redwood Road has
been approved.

Mr. Crowell reported that there are a lot ofprojects in the queue but financing needs to be determined.
