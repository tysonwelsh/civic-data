 (Tabled from December 9, 2025) Proposed Ordinance 2025-40 rezoning
property located at 211 Patterson Street from Multiple-family Residential
Zone (R-4) to Multiple-family Residential Zone (R-5) to allow development
of a 25-unit multi-family project that will provide long-term housing options
for young adults who have aged out of foster care, managed by the Ogden
Housing Authority
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed rezone of 211 Patterson
Street came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Housing Management and Development Corporation,
affiliated with the Ogden Housing Authority, has submitted a petition to rezone 0.54 acres from R-4 (multiple-family residential)
to R-5 (multiple-family residential with higher density). The petitioner requests to establish zoning on the property to allow a 25-
unit apartment building as long-term housing for young adults aging out of foster care. The Planning Commission reviewed and
recommended approval of the proposal on October 1, 2025. The main difference between the R-4 and R-5 zones is the level of
residential density they allow. Both zones permit multiple family housing, but R-5 allows more units on the same amount of land
by requiring less lot area per dwelling unit and by allowing larger projects by right. For example, R-4 only allows up to eight units
as a permitted use, while R-5 allows up to 49 units before a conditional use permit is required. On December 9, 2025, the City
Council tabled action on this item until January 20, 2026. The proposal was most recently discussed at a work session on January
13, 2026. Information requested by the Council, including a map of nearby bus stops and a heat map illustrating the location of
other low-income housing projects in the area, has been provided.
          An additional memo from Council staff also summarized the actions available to the Council this evening, including
approval or denial of the application, tabling until a date certain, or extending the matter for additional time without a date certain.
          Assistant Planning Director Simpson summarized the memo and requested that the item be continued to allow for further
community engagement. He explained that City Administration was working with neighborhood residents on potential mitigation
measures that would address the concerns that have been raised.
          Council member Lopez asked to postpone the final decision on the rezoning until February and direct Administration to
collaborate with neighborhood residents to develop a neighborhood improvement plan. She emphasized the importance of taking
into consideration the voices of the neighborhood residents.

                    “An ordinance of Ogden City Utah, amending the Zoning Map of Ogden City, as adopted by Section 15-3-3 of

Public Comments
        Public comments centered around three main topics: the Mount Ogden Golf Course access issue, concerns about ICE
(Immigration and Customs Enforcement) activities, and various City management issues.
        Golf Course Access Comments:
            • Multiple citizens, including golf course employees, marshals, and regular golfers, expressed support for Mayor
                 Nadolski's decision to restrict public access across the Mount Ogden Golf Course. Speakers included Kirk
                 Smith, Richard Bruckner, Butch Zampedri, Craig Nichols, Marisa Hermsen, Edward Hilbert, Roger Reiner,
                Chris Richins, Thor Goresh, and Laura Lewis.
             •  They cited safety concerns, potential liability issues, damage to the course, interference with play, and
                emphasized that no other golf course allows pedestrian traffic during active play.
             • Several speakers mentioned that the Gibbs Loop trail was designed to go around the golf course, not through it,
                and that there are plenty of alternative trails available.
             • Speakers noted that as a fee-based park, the golf course serves a specific purpose, and that mixing golf with
                other recreational activities presents serious safety hazards.
         ICE/Immigration Comments:
             • Multiple residents, including Sam (no last name given), Oliver Ross, David (no last name given), Kim Haslam,
                James Humphreys, Chris Barrera, Malik Dayo, Julie Ray, Thor Goresh, Eva Barnett, Linda Sanchez, Jerida
                Bansack, Cassandra Quendel, and Heath Satow expressed concerns about potential collaboration between
                Ogden Police Department and ICE.
             • Speakers highlighted constitutional violations by ICE agents across the country, incidents of excessive force,
                and the fear these operations create in the community, particularly among people of color.
             • Several speakers requested the Council take a firm stance against any 287(g) agreements with ICE and establish
                clear written policies.
             • Residents shared personal stories about family members being afraid to leave their homes due to fear of
                profiling or detention.
             • One speaker specifically mentioned reviewing police body camera footage of an incident where OPD contacted
                ICE during a traffic stop, raising concerns about policies versus practices.
         City Management Comments:
             • Damian (no last name given) criticized water rate increases, the Wonder Block parking structure debt, and other
                financial decisions as burdens on Ogden residents.
             • Linda Sanchez raised concerns about policing practices and surveillance cameras, questioning whether these
                measures balance rights appropriately.
             • Laura Lewis raised concerns regarding the Planning Commission discussions on rezoning. She drew attention
                to the inconsistency in restrictions for home occupations, limited by the number of people allowed in the home
                and number of employees, while redefining single-family homes permits up to 15 people on-site frequently
                without facility restrictions. She questioned the impact on neighborhoods, considering more significant effects

             • Teresa Bramwell expressed appreciation for the Ogden City Police for maintaining safety and order in the City.
                She mentioned past years under the Biden administration, during which there appeared to be a higher crime rate

Council member Comments
          Council members responded to the topics raised during the public comment period; Council member Lopez addressed the
concerns brought up during public comments, particularly relating to fears around ICE and immigration enforcement. She
reassured attendees, emphasizing that the Ogden Police Department (OPD) is committed to protecting the community. As a Latina
woman herself, she expressed understanding and empathy for those who fear discrimination, offering to assist anyone who feels
apprehensive by working with the OPD. Lopez encouraged those living in fear to reach out directly to her via email or engage
with the OPD, highlighting their openness and dedication to community safety.
          Council member Lundell acknowledged the golf course issue and clarified that the survey mentioned was for an alternate
trail route, not about trail access through the course. Regarding ICE concerns, he shared his recent experience on a police ride-
along where he witnessed professional and empathetic policing. He referenced his attendance at the Martin Luther King Day
breakfast and quoted Bishop David McKinley's statement that "the hottest place in hell is reserved for those who remain neutral in
times of great moral conflict." Council member Lundell expressed concern about Sheriff Arbon's public support for ICE and
proposed developing a joint resolution, with Council member Washington, to formalize the City's position against 287(g)
agreements with ICE.
          Vice Chair Graf expressed sympathy for residents' fears regarding immigration enforcement, noting his own family in
Minneapolis and the disturbing situations occurring there. He emphasized the importance of ongoing community conversation
around these issues.
          Council member Washington affirmed her work with Council member Lundell on language for a formal statement on
ICE and referenced the town hall conversations on race she had organized in 2016. She stressed the need for actionable results
beyond conversation.
          Council member Myers also referenced the Martin Luther King Day celebration and drew parallels between civil rights
struggles of the past and present immigration issues. He expressed support for protecting community members regardless of their
status and acknowledged the value of the diverse perspectives on the Council.
          Council member Richey thanked the trail builders and users for their comments regarding the golf course access issues;
he also expressed support for Chief Sube and the Ogden Police Department, noting his positive experiences with them through
ride-alongs and participation in the Citizens Police Academy.
         Chair Hyer shared his experience with police ride-alongs and the Citizens Police Academy as well, emphasizing the
professionalism of the officers and recommending the Academy to interested citizens. He expressed empathy for community fears
while reassuring residents about the professionalism of local police.

Mayor Comments
         Mayor Nadolski addressed multiple topics from the meeting; regarding 211 Patterson, he thanked Council member Lopez
for her leadership in neighborhood engagement and emphasized the Administration's commitment to working with the
neighborhood. On the golf course issue, he thanked those who came to support his decision, acknowledging the difficult balance
of competing interests. He addressed the utility transfer and fees at Marshall White Center and provided context on the financial
reasoning behind these policies, including:
             • The utility transfer has been transparent and consistent with other cities' practices
             • Raising taxes instead would put more financial burden on residents
             • Marshall White Center fees were established to be consistent with other recreation centers while encouraging
                  regular use through affordable monthly and annual rates
             • Free and reduced scholarships are available through a simple process
     On ICE and immigration enforcement, the Mayor emphasized:
             • He has sworn an oath to uphold the Constitution, which he takes seriously
             • He has been clear that OPD will not participate in a 287(g) agreement
             • OPD has no authority over immigration enforcement
             • The City has communicated its position through meetings with community and faith leaders, social media
                  posts, and media releases in both English and Spanish
     Mayor Nadolski invited Chief Sube to speak regarding the ICE concerns that have been raised; he reinforced that:
             • OPD is not part of the 287(g) program and will not be
             • OPD does not participate in ICE operations, raids or sweeps
             • OPD has no authority to enforce federal immigration law
             • The two incidents that raised community concerns involved specific circumstances where immigration was not
                  the focus of OPD's actions
             • OPD's policies and procedures are distinct from the Weber County Sheriff's Office
             • The Department prioritizes accountability and protecting community safety
         Council member Lundell inquired about procedures when ICE is not available, and Chief Sube explained the
complexities officers face in identifying individuals providing false information. He outlined the importance of probable cause to
proceed with arrests and detailed how challenging it can be without ICE's involvement.
         Regarding Council member Lundell's suggestion for a formal resolution, Mayor Nadolski stated that entering into
agreements is within his purview as Mayor, but he would be open to discussions about a joint resolution to formalize a policy
regarding 287(g) agreements.
