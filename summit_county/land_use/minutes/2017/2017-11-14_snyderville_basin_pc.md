1. Discussion regarding potential regulations for the operation of Food Trucks in 
the Snyderville Basin Planning Area- Ray Milliner, Principal Planner   
 
Planner Milliner said last year the State legislature made changes to laws regarding 
food trucks.  One of the changes was if a food truck received health and business 
licenses in a municipality, such as Salt Lake City, the food truck could come to another 
municipality without requiring the same permits from the second municipality.  Before 
the Commission, is a draft ordinance regarding food trucks operating within Summit 
County.   
 
Planner Milliner said since that ruling, Summit County has received a number of 
requests.  These were turned away because the use isn’t found within the Use Chart.  
Planner Milliner said this item was brought up a few months ago with the Planning 
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission

Commission.  The ordinance being presented at this meeting is a result of that 
discussion.  Two categories have been created.  The first is a mobile food truck parked 
somewhere within the County.  The second category is a mobile food truck court.   
 
Planner Milliner said the rules for mobile food trucks are that they must be stationed on 
private property.  It is being proposed that these are allowed in the Commercial Zone 
only.  Certain uses are not allowed under this ordinance, such as: vending carts, ice 
cream vendors, seasonal farm stands or other temporary merchants.    
 
Planner Milliner said it is proposed that a mobile food court would consist of 2-10 
vehicles dedicated to the sale of food products only.  This would be a conditional use.  
Pictures were shown of food courts within the Salt Lake Valley.  Picnic tables and 
several parking stalls are available.  The applicant will present a site plan for review.  
Planner Milliner said Staff would like feedback from the Commission.

COMMISSION COMMENTS AND QUESTIONS    
 Attorney Thomas stated if the Commission is uncomfortable with approving 42 rooms, 
they can deny the application.  He said there are two fundamental hurtles.  Before they 
get to the mitigation, they have to look at the vested uses.  The Commission should 
determine if the proposal fits within those uses.   
  
Attorney Thomas said the Commission needs to define what a small hotel is.  It isn’t a 
case of mitigating more rooms.  The question about the restaurant isn’t if it has to be in 
the hotel, because the original approval did not specify that.  The question is if they are 
proposing to enlarge the use.   
 
Attorney Thomas explained that neither Utah nor Summit County has defined 
enlargement.  The law only states that a legal non-conforming use cannot be enlarged.  
There are two schools of thought on what it means.  The first view is that whatever was 
approved is allowed, with nothing beyond.  In this case, they would look at the square 
footage of the Snowed Inn in 1987 including the kitchen and dining area.  The 
application would be held to that square footage.  The other school of thought is that 
businesses are allowed the opportunity to expand as they grow.  Natural expansion 
would not enlarge the use.       
 
Attorney Thomas said the Commission needs to decide which school of thought they are 
most comfortable with.  With the first scenario, the applicants will be allowed to put the 
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission 
November 14, 2017 
Page 14 of 36 
 
square footage within a separate building.  With the second, the mitigation of natural 
growth comes into play.     
Attorney Thomas said the accessory uses were not permitted in 1985 or 1987; 
however, accessory uses are available to any property owner under the current 
Development Code.  In order to be an accessory use, it has to be subordinate to the main 
use.  In this case, that is a small hotel.  The Commission has to make this determination.  
These are the preliminary steps that have to be made before they get to mitigation. 
 
Attorney Thomas suggested the Commission first discuss if they are going to change 
their position on the definition of a small hotel.  Then, they need to decide which school 
of thought applies to the restaurant.  They also need to look at the two accessory uses.  
Are these subordinate to a small hotel in terms of scope?  These items need to be 
decided upon before they move to mitigation.   
 
Chair Harte asked Attorney Thomas if the Commission should consider that a small
