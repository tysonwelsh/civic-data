Proposed Ordinance 2022-7 approving the request to rezone 2530-2544
Porter Avenue from Multiple Family Residential East Central Zone (R-3EC)
to Mixed Use (MU); AND
Proposed Ordinance 2022-8 approving the request to amend the Mixed-
Use Zone (MU) ordinance for rezoning the southeast corner of 25th Street
and Porter Avenue

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed rezone at 25" Street and
Porter Avenue came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated this rezone request is the first of a two-part process,
the second part being an amendment to the Mixed-Use (MU) zone. Additionally, developments within the MU zone must have
development agreements, so a development agreement will be considered with this rezone petition. This rezone petition was
originally considered by the Planning Commission in 2019, with the Commission recommending approval of the rezone and
development agreement. Several Commission meetings were held, and the original petition changed throughout the process. The
original petitioner, Mike Vowles, proposed building a mixed-use project with townhomes, apartments, and a mixed-use building.
In order to do so, it would be necessary to rezone from R-3EC to MU. Mr. Vowles, however, did not move forward with the
project, and the property was sold to Heritage Land Development. The new property owner changed the site plan and would like
to construct 21 townhomes on the property. The development would also include live-work units within some of the townhomes.
In order to allow 21 units on the property it will be necessary to rezone to MU. This item was tabled during the meeting on
February 15, 2022 due to issues with the rezone ordinance, and to give time to Council staff to look into allegations of the City not
following notification requirements. These issues have been resolved, and it has been found that there were no issues with the
City’s notification process.

An additional memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed zone text
amendment pertaining to the Mixed-Use Zone (MU) came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the purpose of

Minutes of Regular Meeting of Council of Ogden City, Utah, March 15, 2022

this zone text amendment is to create a section within the MU zone text that establishes specific requirements for the rezone at 25"
Street and Porter Avenue. Creating specific standards for the MU zone in this area may help protect surrounding properties from
unwanted uses that do not match their zoning designation. This item was tabled on February 15, 2022, in part because there was
an issue with some of the language in the ordinance. The ordinance stated that structures along 25" Street could reach up to 55” in
height. This is incorrect and has been removed. The maximum height for buildings along 25" Street is 40’. The ordinance also
incorrectly stated the amount of parking stalls per dwelling. It has been changed from 1-1.5 per dwelling to 1.75-2.2 stalls per
dwelling.

Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to orient the
Council to the location of the property subject to the rezoning application and development proposal. He also summarized the
history of this petition dating back to 2019, after which he presented an amended site plan and project design since the Council’s
last review of the petition. Proposed revisions include hipped end roofs rather than a gable, lowering the building height by two
feet, and using carriage doors facing the homes on Jefferson Avenue. The design respects the visibility of the existing church on
the corner parcel; will mirror the scale of the Jefferson District and Porter Avenue; includes quality materials; respects existing
architectural elements of the area; and provides art or creative spaces as part of the project. He referenced neighborhood concerns
regarding building heights and overall density and indicated that the proposed building heights match the existing scale in the
area. Additionally, the proposed density of the project is 22 units per acre, which reflects or is lower than density in projects in
close proximity to the subject property. The applicant has also addressed traffic circulation and provided a second access to the
development on 25" Street; on Porter Avenue, there should be local traffic only. The road is 24 feet wide and traffic on the street
travels fairly slow. With or without the development, it may be appropriate to install “no parking” signs on the street to maintain
fire access requirements if increased traffic becomes a problem. It is also important to recognize that with or without the zone
change, the parcel and others in the area will eventually be developed. The proposed development would include 45 new stalls,
which equates to 2.1 stalls per unit. Mr. Montgomery then noted that residents also expressed concerns regarding the project’s
non-compliance with the Nine Rails Plan; he noted that one of the directives of the Plan was to create a creative district zone to
allow higher density and mixed uses in the specific creative district intensive area. Finally, the residents expressed concern that the
Jefferson Historic District will be damaged by this project. He noted that a development agreement has been negotiated to address
these concerns. Existing access rights must be maintained and there may be a mutual agreement to adjust the location of existing
access points. The developer has also agreed to pave the area along the development. Mr. Montgomery concluded that the
Planning Commission found the rezone petition to be consistent with the East Central Community Plan and they recommended
approval of the rezone. In addition to recommending approval of the rezone, the Planning Commission voted to recommend
approval of a development agreement. Significant requirements of that agreement include:

13. Townhomes must be built in a modern version of the Arts and Crafts architectural style.

14. Townhomes are limited to a maximum height of 30’ for townhomes with road frontage, and 40’ for townhomes
adjacent to existing homes.

15. The existing right-of-way along the east property line shall be maintained and access allowed for those who
have recorded rights to the right-of-way.

16. Murals shall be placed in certain locations within the development.

17. A 6’ cedar fence shall be placed along the southeast property lines with gates for those with legally recorded
rights-of-way.

18. Any live-work space shall maintain quiet hour from 9:00 p.m. to 7:00 a.m.

Planning Manager Montgomery then referenced the companion application to amend the MU zoning ordinance, noting
the Planning Commission found this application to be consistent with the General Plan, the East Central Community Plan, the
Nine Rails District Plan, and the BRT Ogden On Board Plan. The Planning Commission recommended approval of the
amendment.

Proposed Joint Resolution-2022-3 Ordinance 2022-9 revising the four
municipal districts and adopting the official municipal district boundary map
of Ogden City; AND Proposed Joint Resolution 2022-4 establishing voter
participation areas

A memo from Council staff came before the Council to consider an ordinance revising municipal districts and adopting
the official municipal boundary map of Ogden City. The memo stated every 10 years, after each census, the City Council is
responsible for adopting new municipal district boundaries. New municipal district boundaries must follow new precinct
boundaries—which were established by the county in December—and various minor changes were made accordingly. After
reviewing a few map options and taking public input, it has been determined that Map #1 from the Fact-Finding Work Session
will be adopted as the new Municipal District Map. This map most closely resembles the existing boundaries. Additionally, an
identical map for Voter Participation Areas will be adopted in order to maintain consistency between Municipal Districts and
VPAs.

Council Policy Analyst Watkins summarized the memo and reviewed the history of this matter. Though a vetting
process, which included an opportunity for public input, the Council has decided upon the most appropriate municipal district map
for the City. This map most closely resembles the City’s current district map. The district map may also serve as the City’s Voter
Participation Area Map.

Mayor’s Comments

Mayor Caldwell thanked everyone for their participation tonight. He stated that the annual budget development process
will begin soon and there will be many difficult decisions for Administration and the Council to make, and he appreciates the
Council’s willingness to engage in that process.

Council member Comments

Council member White stated that she is very disheartened by activity occurring in other states, such as Florida and
Texas, that is harmful to gay people. She wants people to know that Ogden is a welcoming City; she will listen to anyone that
needs help at this time of great divisiveness. She stated that the teen suicide rate in the LGBTQ+ community is very high, and she
hopes that any resident of Ogden City would be willing to lend a listening ear to any youth that needs help.

Vice Chair Lopez offered his voice of support to Council member White’s comments. He stated the LGBTQ+
community has his full support.

Council member Hyer stated that there have been a few times that he has been called out for representing his
constituents; he is not ashamed of doing what he feels is right and he will continue to do so.

Chair Nadolski also expressed his support to Council member White and the LGBTQ+ community and indicated his
willingness to always listen to and support anyone going through a difficult time. He then stated that he had a conversation with a
constituent at the Airport recently and he does not know what to do to improve the situation and resolve differences but answering
concerns in a legislative session is not the answer. He is exhausted by the issue and hopes for a resolution soon. He also addressed
the concerns expressed by Marriott-Slaterville residents and asked Mayor Caldwell to provide information about a recent
engineering review of the BDO project. He then asked City Attorney Williams if there is anything that Mr. Montgomery said
tonight that misled the Council regarding any development procedure. Mr. Williams answered no and indicated that the BZA has
no authority whatsoever over a recommendation from the Planning Commission for a legislative act. He stated he is sure Mr.
Montgomery would have spoken to Mr. Stratford about the matter and was relaying the legal advice he received.

There being no further business to come before the Council, COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED THE MEETING
ADJOURN AT 7:53 P.M. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER BLAIR, ALL VOTING AYE.

MICHELLE MORTENSEN
DEPUTY CITY RECORDER
