Review and Approve City Council Protocols:
Mayor Worel reviewed the protocols were set up so Council could operate from the
same perspective. Council Member Rubell asked when Council members would act
representing the Council versus acting as a community member. Margaret Plane stated
Council members retained first amendment rights but it didn't mean comments wouldn't
be taken in the role of an elected official. She advised Council to be aware that people
might not distinguish actions as individuals. Mayor Worel gave the example that there
were times when she would clarify she was not speaking representing the Council.
Plane also cautioned that when there was a land use item that would most likely be
appealed, the Council would sit on the appeal board and should be cognizant of their
comments and opinions on the matter.

Council Member Toly asked if family members could speak out on issues. Plane stated
immediate family members came into play if there was a significant financial interest in
something that was regulated by the City. From the disclosure forms Council filled out, it
didn’t appear any Council members had those conflicts.

Council Member Dickey asked about Protocols 3B and F, regarding email responses.

Utah Open Public Meetings Act (OPMA) and Government Records Access and
Management Act (GRAMA) Training:
Luke Henry, Assistant City Attorney, presented this item and noted Council members
were required to have this training annually. He reviewed a quorum was necessary to
have a meeting and it had to be open to the public unless it met certain requirements to
be a closed meeting. Council Member Toly asked if City Tour was considered a
meeting. Margaret Plane explained no City business was being conducted and the
public would not be able to attend so it wasn't a convened meeting. Henry reviewed the
meetings where notices had to be published so the public would know what would be
discussed. Council Member Doilney asked if notices were published during the fires last
summer when Council met at the ice arena. Plane stated decisions weren't being made
by one public body, so the City didn't notice those.

Henry reviewed an audio recording of the meeting and written minutes were required.
Plane reviewed the reasons to have a closed meeting. Henry explained the
requirements for having an electronic meeting with an anchor location or without an
anchor location. Plane reviewed GRAMA laws, including the definition of a record and
types of records.

Council Member Gerber asked if she had to keep all her emails on City issues. Plane
stated transient emails did not need to be retained. Council Member Toly asked if she
had to forward emails that had been sent to her personal email to her Council email.
Plane stated if a record was public, it didn’t matter where it was stored; it could be
admissible as a public record.

Plane stated if the City received a records request involving records from Council, the
City Recorder would contact Council with the information requested and the timeline for
getting those records to her. She also noted if someone believed there was an
unfounded denial for records, they could appeal the decision.

Council Member Doilney stated there was active discussion in the State Legislature on
Garrity statements and asked what that was and the impacts. Plane explained the
Garrity statement being debated at the Legislature, and gave the example that if an
officer was brought in to be interviewed under Garrity, their statement would not be
used against them in a criminal investigation.

2. Park City Mountain Resort Mid-Season Recap by Mike Goar, PCMR General
Manager:
Mike Goar, PCMR General Manager, updated Council on the challenges during the
holidays. He stated he was grateful for all the employees working in all fields in Park
City. He heard the feedback from the community and his team was responsive. Most of
the feedback was positive. There had been a reduction in the food and beverage
offering, and he realized it was tough for skiers when a restaurant or lift was closed. His
team was committed to getting enough staff to get all the lifts open. The good news was
that there were more lifts and trails open at PCMR than any other resort in North
America.

Goar indicated the resort visitor numbers were similar to other years. Traffic had
increased and he was working with City staff to mitigate traffic impacts. They were
testing solutions and finding improvements. He indicated there was a 47% increase in
season pass sales. Most of the growth had been from upgrading day passes to Epic
passes. He noted much of the negative feedback was about the lack of parking and
acknowledged the peak days were full and the parking lots filled up earlier in the day.
Areas for improvement included better communication and how to get cars diverted
earlier, as well as having a transit system that could get skiers to the resort in a timely
manner. Looking forward, Goar stated the resort had capital project plans, including
new and larger lifts.

Council Member Doilney sympathized with the challenges Goar mentioned. He stated
there was a discrepancy between the report that capacity had not been exceeded and
the congested and diminished skiing experience. It felt like there were more skiers and it
did not feel as safe to be there. Many asked why the City was providing the
transportation and infrastructure to get skiers to the resorts. There also hadn't been
efforts to house the resort employees. He hoped PCMR would make a commitment to
be a partner. He knew there was not a simple solution to the problems. There was a
capacity limit and the City was bursting. He asked that Vail take responsibility for
communicating full lots.

Council Member Gerber indicated it was unfortunate that the City and resort were in
conflict. It was evident there were more people than usual in town and they were going
to the resort. Traffic problems started outside of town, but they impacted the community.
Residents couldn't get to community services on the weekends. Solutions needed to be
discussed, like transportation and communication plans for guests. She stated in the
future, the City couldn't support the mitigation efforts.

Council Member Rubell stated there was a lot of public relations efforts from Vail on
