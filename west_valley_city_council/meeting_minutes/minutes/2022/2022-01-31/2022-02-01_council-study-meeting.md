     FILED BY WEST VALLEY CITY, REQUESTING A ZONE TEXT
     CHANGE TO DEFINE AND ESTABISH PARKING STANDARDS FOR
     TOWNHOMES
     Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council
     Meeting scheduled February 8, 2022, in order for the City Council to hear and
     consider public comments regarding application ZT-7-2021, Filed by West Valley
     City, Requesting a Zone Text Change to Define and Establish Parking Standards for
     Townhomes

       Proposed Ordinance 22-04 related to the proposal to be considered by the City
       Council subsequent to the public hearing, was discussed as follows:

       ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 22-04, AMEND SECTIONS 7-1-103, 7-9-104,
       AND 7-11-404 OF THE WEST VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO
       AMEND CERTAIN REQUIREMENTS CONCERNING PARKING FOR
       MULTIUNIT HOUSING DEVELOPMENTS
       Steve Pastorik, CED Director, discussed proposed Ordinance 22-04 that would
       amend Sections 7-1-103, 7-9-104, and 7-11-404 of the West Valley City Municipal
       Code to Amend Certain Requirements Concerning Parking for Multiunit Housing
       Developments.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

              At the request of the City Council, City staff is proposing an ordinance
              amendment to define and establish parking standards for townhomes. As
              both the Planning Commission and City Council have reviewed proposals
              for townhome developments, parking is often discussed and addressed by
MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – FEBRUARY 1, 2022
                       -3-


         development agreement. In all recent development agreements for
         townhome projects, the amount of parking required has exceeded the
         current ordinance requirement of 2 spaces per unit. For this reason, the
         Council asked that the ordinance be updated to reflect the higher
         standards.

         The proposed ordinance defines a townhome as “a Dwelling Unit in a row
         of at least three such units in which each unit has its own front and rear
         access to the outside, no unit is located over another unit, and each unit is
         separated from any other unit by one or more vertical walls.” In addition
         to defining the term townhome, the proposed ordinance adds the following
         standards:

                requires single unit dwellings, townhomes, and twin homes to
                 provide a driveway with minimum dimensions of 18 feet by 18
                 feet,
                requires single unit dwellings, townhomes, and twin homes that are
                 not adjacent to a public street with no more than two lanes to
                 provide an additional 0.75 spaces per unit,
                sets a minimum interior dimension of 20 feet by 20 feet for
                 townhome garages, and
                prohibits things like stairs and water heaters from encroaching into
                 the 20 feet by 20 feet garage space.

  Councilmember Harmon verified that this is for townhomes, twin-homes, and
  single-family homes. Steve replied yes. Mayor Lang stated she would rather not
  have on-street parking regardless of what type of street it’s on. She indicated she
  feels all developments should be self-contained for parking and streets should be
  free of on-street parking. Councilmember Nordfelt stated that he would like to
  eliminate the option of private roads as a future agenda item. The Mayor and
  Council discussed private versus public streets. Mayor Lang stated that she would
  prefer the language not to address streets and 2.75 spaces be required for every unit
  regardless. Steve stated that “adjacent” would mean any unit that is next to a public
  street. Mayor Lang asked if staff is aware of any townhome development that
  charges for interior parking stalls. Steve replied no. Councilmember Harmon asked
  if there is a minimum road width that would still allow vehicles to park on streets.
  Steve replied the minimum requirement for public roads is 29 feet of asphalt.

  The City Council will consider Ordinance 22-04 at the Regular Council Meeting
  scheduled February 8, 2022 at 6:30 P.M.

       This resolution authorizes the agreement with Zions Bank to provide funding for

       Pierce engine- E76. This replacement aerial ladder truck will be used for our
       southwest ladder truck and extrication unit. Financing this equipment through a

       competitive leasing proposal for the purchase of this equipment. It is expected that
       revenue from emergency operations in the fire department and ambulance services
       will be sufficient to make the debt service payment.

RESOLUTION 22-30: AUTHORIZE THE CITY TO EXECUTE THREE
QUITCLAIM DEEDS IN FAVOR OF STEPHEN J. MINER, ZACARIAS
GREGORIO FLORES, AND LACEM CONSTRUCTION, INC.
Dan Johnson, Public Works, presented proposed Resolution 22-30 that would authorize the
City to Execute Three QuitClaim Deeds in Favor of Stephen J. Miner, Zacarias Gregorio
Flores, and Lacem Construction, Inc.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       In 2005, certain property was conveyed to the City at the locations listed above.
       At the time it was believed the property was located within the right-of-way of
       2300 South Street. The Haynes Addition Plat E subdivision was recorded May 9,
       1889. The plat shows all roads on the plat as being 65 feet wide, with the
       exception of the south road which is shown as 30 feet wide. After further
       research and a survey was conducted, it was discovered that Blocks 1, 6, 7 and 8
       as they exist on the ground are shifted 35 feet north of the locations shown on the
       subdivision plat. It appears previous land owners (or their surveyors) assumed the
       road on the south to be 65 feet wide instead of the 30 feet shown on the plat.
       Parcels on the ground have been occupied for decades based upon this 35 foot
       shift to the north. After applying the 35 foot shift on the ground for Blocks 1, 6, 7
       and 8, the lines of occupation, fences, street improvements, etc. are in harmony
       with the recorded subdivision plat. The 2005 effort to clean up the title issues
       now leaves encroachments onto the property previously deeded to West Valley
       City.

       The Quit Claim Deed to Stephen J. Miner describes the entire property to be
       owned by Miner, including the portion previously conveyed to the City, less a
       portion conveyed to UDOT at the southeast corner of the property. With the Quit
       Claim Deed, West Valley City is only conveying that portion of property it
       currently owns. The Quit Claim Deed to Zacarias Gregorio Flores will convey
       the portion previously conveyed to the city for road right-of-way back to the
       current owner of the adjacent remainder of Lot 2. The Quit Claim Deed to Lacem
       Construction, Inc. will convey the portion previously conveyed to the city for road
       right-of-way back to the current owner of the adjacent remainder of Lot 47.

The City Council will consider Resolution 22-30 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
February 8, 2022 at 6:30 P.M.

     VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO UPDATE ADMISSIONS FEES
     FOR THE WEST VALLEY CITY FAMILY FITNESS CENTER
     Nancy Day, Parks and Recreation Director, discussed proposed Ordinance 22-02
     that would amend Section 1-2-107(3) of the West Valley City Municipal Code to
     Update Admissions Fees for the West Valley City Family Fitness Center.

      Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
      information as follows:

             The WVC Family Fitness Center has seen a significant increase in expenses
             since its last fee increase 10 years ago. The proposed membership and drop-
             in admission fee increases outlined below will help cover upsurges in
             maintenance costs, personnel and benefits, property insurance and overall
             cost of services. Staff compared membership fees with other similar
             facilities and found that the proposed increases remain comparable to most
             nearby recreation facilities. With these changes, it is recommended that the
             Centennial Outdoor Pool be included in the memberships.
MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – FEBRUARY 1, 2022
                       -14-


         It is proposed that all membership rates be increased by 15% apart from
         senior membership rates which will be raised 5%. This reflects a monthly
         membership fee increase of:
               Family (up to six members): $4.58 per month
               Adult Couple: $4.17 per month
               Adult: $2.58 per month
               Senior Couple: $0.83 per month
               Senior: $0.67 per month
               Student: $2.08 per month
