Item 2: A biannual report regarding Victim Services grant funding (25-018) 0:05:34

Leigh Ferre, Victim Services Coordinator, presented a biannual report on victim services grant
funding. She introduced her team members: Kim Thayne, Dennys Hernandez, and Estrella Farias
Sanchez. Ferre explained that their program is partially funded by the VOCA grant, which
requires reporting to the City Council twice in each grant cycle. The Victim Services team
consists of five members: one part-time advocate funded by the CDBG grant and assigned to the
court, and four full-time members, with two funded by the City and two funded by VOCA. Their
office is housed within the police department as part of the Criminal Investigation Division.

Kim Thayne discussed the types of victimizations they address, including domestic violence,
sexual assault, stalking and harassment, crimes against children, and unexpected emergency
situations. Estrella Farias Sanchez described their typical workday, which includes reviewing
cases, contacting victims, assisting with protective orders, collaborating with police and
detectives, and working with community outreach partners. Dennys Hernandez detailed their
community service efforts, including writing protective orders, educating victims about the court
process, accompanying victims to court, and connecting them with resources. The team also
participates in community outreach events.

Estrella Farias Sanchez presented statistics for 2024, including 618 domestic and family violence
victims, 227 adult sexual assault victims, 110 child sexual assault victims, and 148 child abuse
victims. She noted that these figures represent only reported cases. Ferre addressed concerns
about decreased federal funding for the VOCA grant over the past several years. She explained
that if funding is reduced again this year, it will impact staffing levels. The team utilizes the VS
tracking system to monitor victims and services provided.

Council members inquired about the impact of immigration status on reporting, the team's
proactive outreach efforts, and the potential effects of federal funding cuts on their services. The
mayor and administration expressed their support for the program and acknowledged the critical
importance of the services provided.

Item 4: A resolution to place a 14,038 sq. ft. parcel of ground located generally at 3100
West 240 North on the surplus property list and authorize the mayor to dispose of the
property (25-019) 0:26:09

Tara Riddle, Property Administrator, presented information regarding a 14,038 square foot
parcel of ground located at 3100 West 240 North. A developer to the west of the subject property
approached the city with the intent of incorporating this parcel into their subdivision plat. The
parcel has historically been used for access to the Provo River for maintenance purposes and was
acquired by the city in 2005.

Riddle explained that a departmental review was conducted, and all departments agreed to the
disposal of the property, provided that continued access to the Provo River is maintained through
another portion of the subdivision plat. The property was appraised at $36,000, which is the
amount the developer will pay for it.

The developer will ensure continued access for city maintenance of the Provo River through a
dedicated street in the subdivision plat. Riddle presented the plat and highlighted the designated
area for access.

Councilor Whipple inquired whether the city needed to purchase an easement for access. Riddle
clarified that the city already possesses the easement, and it will be officially recorded on the
subdivision plat.

Riddle requested that this item be moved to the next council meeting on February 25th for a
resolution to surplus the property and authorize the mayor to proceed with its disposal.

Item 10: An ordinance amending water fees on the Provo City consolidated fee schedule
(25-013) 1:58:45

Public Works Director Gordon Haight and Water Consultant Keith Larson presented information
regarding proposed amendments to water fees on the Provo City consolidated fee schedule. They
explained that the proposed changes address two key issues: the implementation of tiered rates
and a rate increase.

Haight emphasized that the tiered rates proposal is revenue-neutral and intended to create more
equitable billing for customers. He noted that water costs are higher in the summer due to peak
demand, and the tiered structure would better reflect this seasonal variation.

Larson provided details on the proposed tiered structure for both residential and non-residential
customers. He explained that the tiers were determined based on meter size and historical water
usage data. The proposed structure includes three tiers for residential customers and a
comparable system for non-residential customers, with slight variations in allowances.

Councilmembers raised questions regarding the fairness of the tiered system, its potential impact
on various customer types, and how Provo’s rates compare with those of other municipalities.
They also discussed concerns about the financial burden of the proposed rate increase on
residents, particularly in the context of rising living costs.

Haight and Larson underscored the necessity of proactive infrastructure maintenance, warning
that deferring upgrades could lead to increased costs and service disruptions in the future.

Item 11: An ordinance amending Provo City Code Title 14 regarding multifamily amenities
(PLOTA20240211) 2:58:50

Planner Hannah Salzl presented information regarding proposed amendments to Provo City
Code Title 14, focusing on multifamily amenities. The proposal aims to align city priorities, such
as the inclusion of EV charging infrastructure, with a point-based amenity system for
multifamily developments.

Salzl explained that the proposed system establishes a list of amenities with assigned scores.
Developers must meet a minimum score requirement in addition to existing square footage
requirements for amenities. The system allows developers to select from a menu of amenities as
long as they achieve the required minimum score.

Key elements of the proposal include:

      A minimum score of 60 points for amenities.
      High point values assigned to EV charging stations and infrastructure.
      Flexibility for substitutions with approval from the director.
      A prohibition on charging non-residents for permission to use amenity.
      Maintenance requirements for amenities to ensure long-term usability.

Salzl noted that the proposal had been reviewed by the Utah Valley Home Builders Association
and individual developers, both of whom provided generally positive feedback. She also reported
that the Planning Commission unanimously recommended maintaining the minimum score at 60
points.

Council members inquired about the scoring system, the potential impact on affordable housing,
and how the proposal compares to similar initiatives in other municipalities. They also discussed
the importance of long-term maintenance for amenities and explored the possibility of opening
select amenities to non-residents for a fee.

Overall, council members expressed support for the proposal and commended the efforts put into
its development. They agreed to proceed with considering the ordinance at the evening council
meeting.

Item 12: A discussion regarding housing and public finance tools (25-017) 3:20:11

Brian Baker, vice-President of Zions Public Finance, presented information on various housing
and public finance tools available to the city. He covered the following topics:


Provo City Municipal Council Work Meeting – February 11, 2025                          Page 8 of 10
      Public Infrastructure Districts (PIDs)
      Housing and Transit Reinvestment Zones (HTRZs)
      Low-income housing tax credits
      Other financing tools and incentives

Baker explained that PIDs are increasingly being used in Utah to finance infrastructure for new
developments. He emphasized that while the city, as the creating entity of a PID, has significant
control over its governing document, it relinquishes control once the PID is established.

Regarding HTRZs, Baker noted that these zones are designed to promote economic growth
around transit hubs and can help address housing affordability issues. He highlighted that HTRZs
enable tax increment financing without requiring approval from all taxing entities.

Council members inquired about the potential benefits and risks of these tools, as well as their
impact on affordability and city finances. They discussed the importance of carefully evaluating
the use of these tools to ensure alignment with the city’s goals and priorities.

Baker also provided an overview of low-income housing tax credits and other financial options
that developers might utilize to create affordable housing. He stressed the necessity of
incentivizing developers to pursue projects that align with the city's vision while remaining
financially viable.

No formal action was taken on this item, but council members expressed interest in further
exploring these tools and developing policies to guide their implementation in Provo.
