8.     PUBLIC HEARING. CONSIDERATION. AND RECOMMENDATION on Proposed
       Ordinance Text Amendments to Bluffdale Citv Land Use Code Sections 11.20
       (Definitions^. 11.160 (Supplementarv Provisions), and 11.80 (Residential Zones) to
       Update the Standards Related to Allowed Locations for Small Accessorv Buildings and
       Structures. Citv Initiated (Application 2022-381- Staff Presenter. Grant Crowell.

Mr. Crowell reported that the above agenda item was a continuation from the Planning Commission
meeting ofOctober 5,2022, He reviewed the request from the City Council to come up with a means
ofadding flexibility for small accessory buildings. In 2017,the City Council created a Code that used
the term "portable buildings" of400 square feet or less, and they had to be on skids. With that policy,
side and rear setbacks were set at two feet but they had to be behind the home in the backyard. The
proposed text amendment suggested a zero-setback requirement. Other changes, such as removing
the skids, were also considered.

Since the previous meeting, Mr. Crowell had visited with the Fire Department about the zero-foot
setback,either in the side yard behind the front line ofthe dwelling,or the backyard. The Intemational
Fire Code does not necessarily prohibit buildings from being built close together because that
condition is common in urban settings. Firewalls and openings are essential. Having a setback is
valuable for enhancing mobility and maintenance around the structure. Sheds are storage areas for
flammable liquids.

Commissioner Flynn stated that in his discussions with Council Member Kallas, there was a desire to
stop having small accessory buildings that people just stick together as opposed to a structure that is
a bit larger and better constructed. Mr. Crowell received the same type of feedback. He stated that
at his home in Riverton, he has a 10' x 12' shed, and in that city, people can stack as many structures
as they want ifthe structure is smaller than 200 square feet. The setback requirement isjust one foot
from the property line and five feet apart from each other. Once structures exceed 200 square feet,
more restrictions are imposed.

The proposed text amendments were an attempt to address a wide variety ofissues and concerns. The
proposed document operates off the assumption that the structure is less than 400 square feet, with
two-foot setbacks from the property side and back property lines and ten-foot separations between
structures. He noted that in the R-1-10 zone, where lot sizes can be as small as 10,000 square feet,
the placement of a small structure might require more careful planning. If structures are attached to
a home, then the setback requirement associated with a house would also apply to the auxiliary
structure. Setbacks are a ubiquitous issue for aesthetics and safety. Setback and lot coverage
requirements are impacted significantly by the lot size requirement of the zone. Mr. Crowell noted
that if a building is over 200 square feet, a building permit must be obtained.

Chair Cragun liked the two-foot setback and the 10-foot distance between a structure and a house. In
response to Commissioner Flynn's question regarding the required distance between two auxiliary
structures, Mr. Crowell did not think the Code addressed that detail. Commissioner Loumis stated
that he would never support a proposal that does not require a two-foot setback. The property owner
needs to be responsible for the water that will run offtheir structures, so a two-foot setback would be
vital to meeting that obligation. The other Planning Commissioners agreed.

Chair Cragun added that keeping the area between buildings clean requires at least a two-foot
separation. Commissioner Luker stressed the importance of preserving the aesthetics of a
neighborhood to ensure that structures are not allowed in the same plane as the front of the home. It
should be at least 10 feet behind the front plane even though it is on the side ofthe house.

There was discussion regarding potential motion language. Conunissioner Flynn suggested that
auxiliary buildings be at least two feet apart if there is more than one.

Chair Cragun reopened the public hearing. There were no new public comments. Chair Cragun
closed the public hearing.

       1.      That a two-foot separation be required between accessory buildings.
