        3.2      Prosecutors and Indigent Defense Reports – Michael Green and Doug Stowell

      Lead Prosecutor Michael Green made a presentation to the council regarding prosecutorial
activities for the city. There were four prosecutors in his office as well as 4.5 support staffers. The
philosophy of his office was to:

     o Never lose credibility or compromise ethics

     o   Get the right outcome
     o   Escalate with repeat offenders
     o   Treat everyone fairly
     o   Understand city resources

Mr. Green explained that although COVID protocols had been reduced, defendants still had the
option of appearing in court virtually. Continuing to make that option available had decreased the
number of defendants who failed to appear in court.

A fairly new law had been enacted which required prosecutors to certify that there was no
restitution owed to crime victims. If the prosecutor was unable to do so, a Restitution Hearing
needed to be set. These hearings seriously increased the case load when the court was already
handling as many as 180 cases per week. It was slowing the entire judicial process down. There
was a working group that was attempting to collaborate work with the state legislature to address
this problem, particularly for lower offenses. While he agreed that victims should be contacted
about restitution needs, he did not feel it was helpful to require a special hearing if the victim has
failed to respond to the prosecutor’s inquiry.

Mr. Green concluded his comments by saying the justice court was working well and that
Taylorsville Police Department was great to work with.

Indigent defendants in the justice court were typically provided council by Stowell & Crayk,
PLLC. Associate attorney Danny Heaps was present on behalf of Doug Stowell. He distributed
his firm’s quarterly report outlining the number and type of cases handled. He pointed out there
was a larger volume of cases than in many other cities. He felt that an efficient judicial process
had been established through a collaboration between the court, the prosecution and his office.

5.      PLANNING MATTERS

        5.1   Ordinance No. 22-02 An Ordinance of the City of Taylorsville approving a
        Text Amendment to Taylorsville Municipal Code Section 13.04.090, related to
        Minimum Land Area Required to Establish a Zoning District – Wayne Harper

     Wayne Harper explained that the proposed text amendment addressed the size and acreages
of commercial developments in Taylorsville. Roughly half the cities in Utah had a minimum
acreage requirement. Staff had analyzed the idea and determined the following:
    • Very few lots and parcels met the minimum area thresholds
    • In some cases, a zone change was appropriate to align the property’s zoning designation
       with the City’s General Plan.
    • The minimum area thresholds would act as a barrier to infill and redevelopment
    • Staff recommended the minimum area thresholds be eliminated so the City Council could
       determine on a case-by-case basis the merits of a rezone request
    • All other underlying development and zoning standards would apply to development
       requests
    • Staff did not anticipate any detrimental impact or effect associated with this amendment

A public hearing had been held before the Planning Commission on February 22, 2022 and had
voted unanimously to forward a positive recommendation to the City Council. Staff also
recommended approval.

MOTION:          Councilmember Burgess moved to adopt Ordinance No. 22-02, approving a
                 Text Amendment to Taylorsville Municipal Code Section 13.04.090 related to
                 Minimum Land Area Required to Establish a Zoning District as presented.

      Mr. Harper explained that he would present information on both a General Plan Map
Amendment and a Zoning map amendment for the property at 2720 West 6200 South. He would
present information on both items and then the council could vote separately on each ordinance.

Applicant Mark Stephenson of Red Mountain Builders had submitted an application to rezone and
change the general plan at the address given. Mr. Harper described the properties which surrounded
the lot in question. He indicated that the applicant was proposing an approximate 4,000 square
foot building allowing for both residential and nonresidential uses. This would expand the city’s
tax base and economic sustainability. The application allowed the potential for sorely needed
additional housing and promoted an efficient use of land, allowing multiple uses on a relatively
small and currently undeveloped property. The proposed designation would allow for an attractive
development that incorporated pedestrian-oriented design to enhance the quality of life of
Taylorsville residents

Regarding the Zoning Map, staff was supportive of the rezone request as it would provide a viable
use of the property. Due to its location and size, the lot had proven to be challenging to develop.
The proposed concept plan allowed both commercial and residential uses on a site with minimal
impacts to adjacent properties. There would be three office units on the ground floor with three
two-bedroom apartments on the second floor. A public hearing had been held on February 22,
2022. The Planning Commission had made a positive recommendation with a vote of 4-2. Those
voting against the application had expressed a desire for the property to remain undeveloped.

Councilmember Burgess asked about the public comments made at the public hearing. Mr. Harper
responded that his notes indicated no members of the public spoke during the hearing.

Mr. Harper then invited the applicant to step forward and address the council. Mark Stephenson
expressed appreciation to Mr. Harper for his presentation of all pertinent information to the
Council. He addressed some of the constraints on the property, particularly with public utility
easements on the north, south and east of the property.

The council, staff and the applicant discussed various details related to the property.
