Proposal 
The applicant requests a plat amendment among Lots 39, 40, and 41, Pineridge Subdivision 
(located in the Pinebrook area), to divide Lot 40 in half, giving each half to the adjacent Lots 
(Lots 39 and 41) and to slightly reconfigure a platted building pad on Lot 41 to accommodate 
the existing as-built residence (Exhibit A). 
 
Background  
The Pineridge Subdivision was recorded on September 1, 1995 (Exhibit B). The subdivision 
includes 130 lots, and each lot contains a building pad. Building pads were placed on the lots to 
preserve sensitive areas, such as ridgelines and steep slopes.  
 
Lots 39 and 41 contain existing residences. Lot 40 is vacant. When the house on Lot 41 was 
constructed, approximately 3’ of the corner of the house was constructed outside of the 
building pad. The proposed amendment to the building pad will fix this (see red area outlined 
on Lot 41 below). 
 
 
3
3 
 
Analysis and Findings 
Per Section 10-3-18. B of the Code, the Snyderville Basin Planning Commission is the land use 
authority for plat amendments that combine lots and adjust building pads. The Planning 
Commission may approve, approve with conditions, or deny an amendment to a recorded 
subdivision plat based upon written findings of fact, according to each of the following 
standards.  
  
Standard 1: If the land use authority is satisfied that neither the public nor any person will be 
materially injured by the proposed vacation, alteration or amendment, and that there is good 
cause for the vacation, alteration or amendment, the county manager or land use authority 
may vacate, alter or amend the plat, any portion of the plat, or any road or lot. COMPLIES 
  
Analysis: Splitting Lot 40 between Lots 39 and 41 results in a decrease of density. 
Additionally, adjusting the building pad on Lot 41 to include the as-built residence will 
ensure that the structure is compliant with the plat.  
 
Standard 2: No plat amendment shall be approved which results in an increase in density. 
COMPLIES  
 
Analysis: No increase of density, in terms of residential lots or units, is proposed.

BACKGROUND/PROPOSAL 
 
The applicant currently possesses a Conditional Use Pe rmit for  an Indoor Riding Arena and 
Horse Boarding Facility , known as the Whileaway Ranc h, a p rivate dressage horse training 
facility. in the Rural Residential (RR) Zoning District .  The property is located  at 7664 Nor th 
Whileaway Road in the Silver Creek Subdivision, parcel SL-D-206.  
 
On March 9, 2021 , the Snyderville Basin Planning Commission denied the Conditional Use 
Permit. The applicant appealed the SBPC decision to the County Council.  Council granted the 
10
2 
 
appeal approving the Conditional Use Permit with specific revisions to the building location. The 
Private Dressage Horse Training Facility was relocated to the rear of the subject property to 
minimize potential impacts on neighboring residents.  The Conditional U se Permit  site plan, 
building design, and materials are set forth in County Council Findings of Facts dated April 21, 
2021, “EXHIBIT A”. A building permit has been issued and the Private Dressage Horse Training 
Facility is under construction.  
 
The applicant has submitted a Low Impact Permit request seeking to chan ge the exterior siding 
material.  The exterior materials illustrated in EXHIBIT A  include a stone wainscot and dark 
brown stucco. The applicant requests to use white metal siding in lieu of the dark brown stucco. 
The rest of the originally approved exterior materials, in cluding the stone wainscot, are to  be 
applied to the building.   The dark brown stucco was an exterior building material proposed by 
the applicant. It was not a specific condition of  approval. Minor Amendments  to Conditional 
Use Permits are processed through a Low Impact Permit review. 
 
ANALYSIS:  Metal siding is not a prohibited exterior siding material under the Snyderville Basin 
Development Code, Section 10 -4-19.B: Architectural Re gulations for All Buildings: Siding

residential homes, negatively impacts both aesthetics of the surrounding neighborhood, the 
character of the area as principally a residential communi ty, and the privacy of the adjoining 
neighbor homes.    
9. We are also mindful of Utah Code §17-27a-507 which directs us to “approve a 
conditional use if reasonable conditions are proposed, or can be imposed, to mitigate the 
reasonably anticipated detrimental effects of the proposed use.”

10. We believe that the adverse impacts of the Barn can be mitigated by moving and siting
the Barn within the eastern half of the Property, and thereby siting the home within the western 
half of the Property, to front E. Whileaway Road.  Thus, this new site plan configuration will be 
more in line with the neighborhood character and provide a setback of the commercial horse 
operation from the adjacent residential homes. 
11. For the above reasons, while we agree with the Commission that “human scale” within
the context of compatibility with the neighborhood applies to the Project, we disagree that no 
conditions could be imposed which would mitigate the negative impact.  Thus, we conclude that 
the Commission erred in its Denial of the Project.   
12. The Council approves a conditional use permit for the Project with the following
conditions of approval:

presented to the Director of Community Development for his approval consistent with these

minimizes or eliminates discharges to gutters, storm drains, and
watercourses resulting from on-site road paving, surfacing, and asphalt

prevent contaminants from contacting stormwater runoff.

seal coat, tack seal, slurry seal, fog seal, or similar materials

materials, since they tend to drip continuously.

bags, or other controls which will divert or trap and filter runoff.
