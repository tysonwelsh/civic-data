BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Subdivision Plat Amendment for
approximately 26.94 acres located on the west side of Pony Express Road and south of
Golden Harvest Drive, at approximately 13004 S. Pony Express Road (Exhibit B & C). The
property is currently zoned M1. The applicant is requesting that a Subdivision Plat
Amendment be approved to allow for the existing single lot subdivision to be divided into 2
Lots.

The original Greenfield Farms Subdivision, recorded April 13, 1976, was recently amended
and the 22 lots within that subdivision were consolidated into a single lot within the
Summit Commerce Center Subdivision Plat, recorded April 22, 2021. The property is also
the subject of an approved Development Agreement from 2020, file number DA-955-2020.
A Site Plan, file number SPR-0014-2021, for the construction of an industrial building on the

Subdivision Layout. The proposed subdivision layout (Exhibit F) seeks to divide each building
currently under construction onto its own lot. Lot 1 will contain the commercial building
and Lot 2 will contain the industrial building. All existing public utility easements as shown
on the Summit Commerce Center Subdivision Plat remain, and a new public utility
easement will be added along the new property line in compliance with applicable
ordinance requirements. New easements for fire apparatus and emergency vehicle access
across both Lots are included on main access drives through the property. While vehicular
connection points will exist between the two proposed properties, the applicant’s proposed
subdivision plat does not fully include the required cross access or reciprocal easements
between parking areas for nonresidential development. The proposed plat limits vehicular
access easements to that of access across Lot 1 for the benefit of Lot 2. The applicant has
expressed verbally to staff that the owner does not wish to provide the required reciprocal
cross access across to lot 2 due to potential future manufacturing uses of the building on
lot 2 and their desire for potential increased security. Under the ordinance provisions
governing cross access that were in effect at the time the development agreement was
entered into and the application was first submitted, the Planning Commission may
approve a plan that does not provide the required reciprocal cross access upon finding the
following:

(Code prior to May 3, 2022) - DCMC Subsection 9-25-080(E)(2)
   2. Nonresidential developments may be approved without providing cross access when the
       planning commission finds that:

        a. The cross access would result in a slope greater than twelve percent (12%);

        b. Requirements of this title prevent the provision of cross access by creating a
           nonconformity in those requirements; or

        c. The uses for which the cross access would link are incompatible; and

        d. The site cannot be otherwise designed to accommodate the cross access and avoid
           the conditions of subsections E2a through E2c of this section.

Staff has reviewed the application against the four criteria listed above. Providing reciprocal
cross access as required between parking areas would not result in slopes greater than
twelve percent (12%) or create a nonconformity in other zoning requirements. The building
on the proposed Lot 2 that is under construction was speculatively approved as a mix of
fifty percent (50%) warehousing and fifty percent (50%) manufacturing to meet parking
calculations (Exhibit G). The building on the proposed Lot 1 is a commercial building,
currently being developed and constructed as a retail furniture store. Another current



Summit Commerce Center Amended                                       App. #SUBD-0204-2021
Subdivision Plat Amendment Request               4
application, file number DA-0120-2022 to amend the Development Agreement for the site,
is under consideration to allow for the inclusion of a fast food restaurant into the design of
the retail furniture store. The Planning Commission will be required to determine if the
current proposed and potential future uses within the two buildings are indeed
incompatible with one another and sufficient to warrant a waiver of the reciprocal cross
access requirements of ordinance. Should the Planning Commission find that the uses are
compatible the Subdivision Plat Amendment request should be denied as the plat as
proposed does not comply with applicable ordinances. Alternatively, the Plat could be
approved if a condition were added requiring that reciprocal cross access easements for
the benefit of Lot 1 are added to the plat between the access drive to Lot 2 on Golden
Harvest Drive, extending through the parking areas on Lot 2, and connecting to the access
points with Lot 1.

Criteria For Approval.
The grounds for review and potential approval of a subdivision plat amendment request is
found in Section 17-9-040 of the DCMC. This section depicts the standard of review for such
requests as:

The Planning Commission may approve the vacation or amendment of a plat by signing an
amended plat showing the vacation or amendment if the Land Use Authority finds that there is
good cause for the vacation or amendment; and no public street, right-of-way, or easement has
been vacated or amended.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommends that the Planning Commission review the request, receive public
comment, and make a decision based on the findings listed below and the criteria for
approval, or denial, as listed within the staff report.

If the Planning Commission decides to approve the request, staff recommends they include
the following conditions of approval:

        1. That all requirements of the Draper City Engineering, Public Works, Building,
           Planning, and Fire Divisions are satisfied throughout the development of the site
           and the construction of all buildings on the site, including permitting.
        2. That all requirements of the geotechnical report are satisfied throughout the
           development of the site and the construction of all buildings on the site.

The findings for approval as are follows:

        1. That there is good cause for the Subdivision Plat Amendment.
        2. No public street, right-of-way, or easement is vacated or amended by the plat.
        3. The uses for which cross access would be required are incompatible and the site
           cannot be otherwise designed to accommodate the cross access and avoid the
           conditions of such incompatible use.

The findings for denial as are follows:

        1. Good cause does not exist for the Subdivision Plat Amendment.
        2. The plat does not provide for required cross access, the uses for which cross
           access is required are not incompatible.
