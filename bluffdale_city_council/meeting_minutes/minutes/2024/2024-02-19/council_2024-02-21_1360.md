The City has a Redevelopment Agency (“RDA”) with money that is collected through Tax
Increment, which are the taxes collected above the base level when the RDA was originally
formed. Money is collected in those areas with the bulk to be spent in its own area or on something
that could benefit that particular area. It is also restricted in terms of what it can be used for. It
must be used for infrastructure or incentives. If a particular business is desired, it can be used as
an incentive but must be a type of business that the RDA desires, which does not include a business
that is sales tax-generating.
There is a 20% fund that can be used in a variety of ways such as for moderate-income housing
projects. This past budget the City Council approved donating money to the County to start a
project. The next are impact fees, which consist of multiple funds. Each is restricted to the impact
that it is related to. Each fund is designed to cover the cost or impact on the City of growth. Ifa
project has to be upsized because of growth, impact fee money can be used but is restricted to
projects that are on the list of approved projects. Even though a project could come along that
requires an upsize, it may not be able to be done from impact fees if it is not on the Impact Fee
Schedule. He reiterated that impact fees are limited to infrastructure.
The Roads Fund has historically been fuel taxes for which the City receives a percentage and is
based on the number of road miles in the City. Over the last eight years, the City has improved its
measurement of roads and tracked them more closely. The City has received more funding as a
result. In addition, a few years ago the State presented a new concept to provide a shortfall because
cars are more efficient and not necessarily paying the fuel tax. A sales tax component is also
collected that can be used to maintain roads. It can be used for major equipment, road construction,
reconstruction, maintenance, and resurfacing. It cannot be used for salaries.

The next area was the Capital Projects Fund. The list of capital projects has not decreased for
years and was not expected to do so. The City’s large equipment is frequently purchased through
Capital Projects. Grants are also received. Council Member Wilding asked where the money
comes from that is tied to the State’s housing requirement. Mr. Kartchner explained that those
would generally be specific transportation projects. For example, the City is completing the trestle
widening project. Currently, the Capital Projects Fund has already received $8 million from the
State for projects related to that. An additional $20 million will be received from the State over
the next few years. They also anticipate that the project will cost $30 million more than planned.
Mr. Kartchner reported that the City pledged $5 million in the Eastern Bluffdale RDA. Mayor
Hall pointed out that if they do not meet their requirements for housing, they will lose future
funding opportunities. Mr. Kartchner explained that because of the RDA money, there are funds
that can be used for housing.
The money that goes into capital projects primarily comes from grants or transfers from the
General Fund. Those transfers may be for specific reasons such as American Rescue Plan Act
(“ARPA”) money that came into the General Fund and was transferred into the Capital Projects
Fund knowing that is where it would be spent for large structure projects. They include long-term
expenditures. Capital Projects are never short-term and are one-time projects.
The newly created Fire and Police Protection Fund is segregated specifically and has its own fund
balance. Any money that is not spent remains in the fund to be used in the future. The Police and
Fire Protection Fund also has the ability to tax and raise its own taxes. Any tax increment included
in the fund must stain in that fund as well.
The Water Operating Fund is the one fund that is run like a business. It depreciates its assets and
looks to make a profit to cover the cost of ongoing maintenance, big projects, and paying debts.
The City is encouraging people to cut back on water usage. As a result, revenues drop which has
to be dealt with. Sometimes that requires a rate increase since there are fixed costs.

Mr. Kartchner explained that if the City buys a dump truck in the Parks Department, they raise the
funds for it that are spent immediately. It is no longer shown on the balance sheet and is just an
expenditure. There are times when leasing is recommended as a result. That was not his preference
because it is not the least expensive financing mechanism but is sometimes the most efficient from
a governmental standpoint. In the Water Department, such an item would be purchased with
revenue sources and depreciated over time. There are times when it will be depreciated to zero.
When it has no more value to the Water Department it is transferred to the Parks Department.
Mr. Kartchner reported that the New Public Works Building is split 50/50 between the General
Fund and the Water Fund. The Water Department will pay the lease payment which will cover
half of the cost. There are also some out-of-pocket costs that it will help pay for that are above
and beyond the debt. City Manager, Mark Reid, stated that they plan to house the Parks
Department in the Rodeo Arena at the Parks Building. The New Public Works Building will house
the Water and Streets Departments.
Mr. Kartchner reported that the General Fund is the City’s Operating Fund. The City of Bluffdale
is ultimately a service organization as such, people are hired by the City to provide services. The
bulk is paid from the General Fund. The cost of equipment and maintenance is part of the operating
costs and providing that service. The General Fund has several different revenue sources and costs.
The City Council was asked to think about what they want the City to look like. Cities across the
nation have personalities that are based on what has been important to their city councils over the
years and what they have decided to invest in and maintain. Mr. Kartchner commented that the
City has been accused of not prioritizing and gave an example of prioritization that has taken place
in the past. The different departments identify their needs as part of the budget process. He urged
the Council to keep in mind that not all costs are equal and the least essential costs are the first to
be eliminated. The various costs are also different in nature. Some are primary services, others
are discretionary, some are recurring, and others are non-recurring or limited recurring. Some are
stable while others are volatile.

Mr. Kartchner explained that in reviewing the budget there is a continuum that has to be addressed
daily by staff. He also wanted to make the City Council aware of the thought process that staff
goes through in terms of prioritization and what is important. He explained that the only cost that
must be paid every year is the debt payment. The most stable revenue recurring revenue that the
City has is property taxes. He added to that the RDA and EDA management fees because they are
a percentage of the property taxes being collected by the RDA and EDA. The general Fund receives
a management fee for managing those funds, which was included in the budget. When those funds
go away there will be a tax increment that comes back to the City that will replace those accounts
plus some.
Other important expenditures include the Fire Department, Police Department, and Ambulance.
Those are the two most critical costs but the City’s most stable revenue does not cover them.
Franchise Fees were next discussed. They were described as somewhat consistent but not as
consistent as property taxes. Franchise fees vary and largely come from energy telecom taxes.
People tend to cut back on certain economic conditions. With franchise fees, the City still does
not have enough to pay for critical costs. The next group is Government Service Fees, which are
pretty stable and consist of garbage collection fees. Citizens are required to pay to take garbage
away. The downside to including that is because it is quite stable but it also has a required stable
cost that offsets it. The next step is to add the costs that include those stable sanitation costs that
are part of that revenue source along with the somewhat volatile sales taxes, business licenses, and
court fines. That is revenue that is not overly stable. He explained that the estimate of what the
City’s sales taxes would be for any given month or year has a very broad range at a 95% confidence
level.
Development-related costs were next addressed. Mr. Kartchner reported that the City has a number
of costs that are development-related such as the Planning, Engineering, and Building
Departments. There are, however, several projects that have nothing to do with development but
growth of the City, which requires engineers and planners. Even though very volatile,
development revenues may go away and the City still has to cover many of those costs.

Police Department
Police Chief, Andrew Burton acknowledged that the priority in the City is Public Safety. He
appreciated the support. He explained that the current budget is about $2.5 million for the contract.
About $2 million of that is salaries and benefits. Of the remaining $500,000, about half goes to
contract services for various police services including dispatch that support their operations.
Capital expenses are not included in the $2.5 million. Vehicles and facilities are a separate item
that Mr. Reid manages. The Police Department in Bluffdale consists of 15 full-time sworn, two
part-time sworn, and two part-time support staff. Many of the support functions are completed in
Saratoga Springs at the Police Department Headquarters. The two support staffin Bluffdale focus
mostly on walk-in customers and records types of support. Evidence, investigations, technician
operations, forensics, and other support functions take place at the headquarters.
Of the two reserve or part-time officers, one is a patrol officer and the other is in investigations
and works five to 10 hours per week. Of the 15 full-time sworn officers, 10 are assigned to patrol.
One is assigned as a Traffic Enforcement Officer who handles patrol calls as necessary. There has
been a lot of praise from people about his presence, especially around school zones. There is a
Liaison, who is a Sergeant, who is also the Supervisor of the Investigations Unit and includes a
detective, a corporal, a detective, and a school resource officer at Hidden Valley Middle School.
This is the makeup of what is referred to as the Bluffdale Precinct in-house. It is part of the
Bluffdale Police Department for all intents and purposes when they are in Bluffdale and they
operate the precinct as though it is a Police Department. They have tried to create its own its own
identity with markings on the cars and uniforms that are very similar to Saratoga Springs with both
having different patches.

Projected cost increases were described. Chief Burton stated that $105,000 is approximately what
they would expect in terms of pay increases due to normal raises. The officers are all.on a step
plan and get a step every year, which becomes automatic. The increase in overtime pay is an

development have been seen, which adds to the police workload. They were happy to see that
because they understand the sales tax implications there.

Council Member Crockett asked about the type of increase in calls received from a Costco,
Smith’s, or an extended stay hotel opening in the City. Chief Burton stated that shoplifting, theft,
and fraud are seen at commercial retail outlets. There are also individuals trying to pass bad checks
or use stolen credit cards. There are also disputes and fights outside of those businesses. There
are alcohol sales that attract people who are already intoxicated. With convenience stores that sell
gas, there are sometimes situations involving gas runoff. In terms of an extended stay hotel or an
affordable hotel near a freeway entrance, it becomes a place for drug dealing and activities that are
typically against the law. Extended-stay hotels are potentially a good revenue source for the City

but will increase the workload of law enforcement workload to some degree.

Chief Burton reported that response times are impacted by increased traffic and businesses create
increased traffic not just from locals but others coming to the area. They also have experienced
additional cross-city traffic over the last couple of years.

Officer safety and efficiency were discussed. Chief Burton stated that they are seeing an increase
in the kinds of calls that require more than one officer, which increases staffing and workload
requirements. An accident is a typical call that requires more than one officer. There was a fairly
significant increase in traffic accidents last year, much of which was due to an increase in traffic.

Council Member Austin asked for a brief description of all the different types of pavement
treatments the City typically uses. Mr. Mitton explained that there are two main types of
pavement treatments, preventative and restoration. Chip seal, while often less attractive to the
public than the other methods, does an excellent job sealing cracks and providing an all-season
durable wearing surface, especially for the price. This can also include an additional layer of tar
sprayed on the top to help lock down the majority of loose rocks, similar to what was done last
summer on 2700 West. Slurry seals are the most common preventative maintenance surface
treatment currently used in the City, and also cost-effective. It is a pre-mixture of sand/small
gravel and tar that sets up quickly and allows traffic to reopen same-day. Crack sealing fills
pavement cracks with tar to prevent further water intrusion and is done before slurry seals. High
density mineral bonds are a thin surface treatment of sand and a modified tar mixture that stays
nice and black for years, but is mostly suitable for lower-volume roads. Pothole repairs are a
costly restoration treatment that require cutting out the asphalt and replacing the asphalt and
sometimes the underlying base course for a pavement that is failing structurally. Rotomill and
overlay is also a restoration treatment that grinds off all or part of the asphalt layer, followed by
paving a replacement layer of asphalt to the same thickness as before.
