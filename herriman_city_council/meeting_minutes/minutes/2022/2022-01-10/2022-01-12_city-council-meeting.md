    2.4. Discussion regarding a proposed amendment to Title 10 pertaining to accessory structure
         regulations (File No. Z2021-054) - Clinton Spencer, Planning Manager
Planning Manager Clinton Spencer detailed the accessory structure regulations found in various chapters of City
Code which needed consolidating. Planning Manager Spencer noted there have been unintended conflicts
between these standards.

He began by pointing out the various applicable sections of City Code are often difficult for residents and
builders to locate, understand, and apply. Planning Manager Spencer noted staff had also observed the difficulty
of applying the strict standards of City Code on properties that have an unusual shape, location, or relationship
with surrounding development. In the past, he said staff had unsuccessfully attempted to draft standards which
would accommodate these situations. He went through a draft ordinance attempting to consolidate accessory
structure regulations into a single chapter. He said the draft also provided a special exception process similar to
a Conditional Use process for reviewing requests which have specific unusual conditions.

Planning Manager Spencer noted the updates offered more flexibility and expand upon their development
standards and special exception rules. He noted three large changes which included property sizes, setback rules,
and classifications, specifically with cul-de-sacs. He felt the more detail the better. First, he said small building
setbacks would be limited to sixteen feet with adjacent lines being two feet. He added this would include
accessory structures around the home. He noted medium and large setbacks would be six feet from accessory
structures with five feet parameters along yards. He said the main difference was these sizes require three feet
of distance from the street. Councilmember Ohrn asked about parking rules for large vehicles. Planning
Manager Spencer said they could be closer to the street but must stay in driveways.

Planning Manager Spencer discussed uniform regulations for accessory structures more in detail. He said staff
would include rear and side yards when assessing distance rules. He then reviewed regulations for lots. He
continued with the proposed amendment relating to height with small buildings (16 foot tall maximum) less than
200 square feet, medium buildings 201-400 feet with a sixteen foot max on height. He said with lots less than
10,000 square feet and on lots larger than 10,000 square feet, they would allow 25 feet of additional side and rear
setbacks adjacent to the street.

Councilmember Henderson asked about how usable side yards would be with these rules. Planning Manager
Spencer said the rules were still flexible and would vary depending on which direction the house faces.
Councilmember Henderson felt they needed to create a special exception process as some of these lots and yards
were oddly shaped.

Councilmember Hodges said the heights could also become daunting. Councilmember Ohrn asked if this could
be changed. Planning Manager Spencer said he was open to addressing this concern.

Councilmember Shields also had concerns with the language for building heights associated with setbacks. He
said there was not a lot of language considering the height of the building with the square footage of the house.
Councilmember Henderson felt similarly as the language could have setbacks inhibiting someone else’s property

    2.6. Discussion pertaining to the Herriman City Council districting options - Jackie Nostrom,
          City Recorder
City Recorder Nostrom noted the legislature realigned district boundaries at the state level has the ability to
district however they so choose. She explained that each district must be approximately the same population
distribution. She stressed that Utah code requires adjusting district boundaries to meet population requirements
within six months of redistricting. She noted staff received 2020 Census Data for the redistricting process. City
Recorder Nostrom indicated that Herriman City’s population at the census conclusion totaled 55,144. With this
data, she stated the ideal district population will be around 13,786. She presented a draft map to the Council
which indicated a population deviation of 1.5 percent: a deviation which met the definition of “substantially”
equal. Lastly, she noted that these boundaries tried not to divide neighborhoods, have equal populations, and
maintained easily identifiable boundaries.

    10.6. * Discussion and consideration pertaining to the Salt Lake County mask mandate –
        Nathan Cherpeski, City Manager
City Manager Cherpeski noted last Friday, the County Health Officer mandated mask usage for Salt Lake County;
however, he said it is unclear if it would be revoked.

Councilmember Henderson felt it was necessary to determine what the City could do legally. City Attorney
Andrizzi noted the main issue was with enforcement. Councilmember Henderson felt the police department
did not have the ability to enforce the mask mandate. Councilmember Hodges said responsible people should
set an example, but this could be an HR issue. Councilmember Shields felt this was not a binary choice, and
they could create new rules to accommodate others.
