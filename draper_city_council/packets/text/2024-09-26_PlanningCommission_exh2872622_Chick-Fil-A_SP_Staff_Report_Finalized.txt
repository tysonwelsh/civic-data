BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a site plan application with associated deviations
for approximately 1.68 acres located south of Bangerter Highway, at approximately 13673 S.
100 E. (Exhibits B & C). The property is currently zoned CR. The applicant is requesting that a
site plan be approved to allow for the development of the currently vacant site as a fast-food
restaurant and deviations from requirements for building orientation and the number of
parking spaces.

A Master Development Agreement (MDA) was previously approved in 2022 for construction
of an office building on the subject property, however, that application was not further
pursued. Earlier this year in February 2024, a rezone was approved to place the entire site in
the CR zone. The proposed fast-food restaurant is approximately 6,239 ft2 in size and
features a double-lane drive-through.

Architectural Design Standards Modification. DCMC Section 9-22-030 states that the Planning
Commission may approve architectural plans associated with development applications that
deviate from the requirements of the DCMC based on making at least three findings listed
in DCMC Section 9-22-030(b):

         1. The proposed development includes additional amenities or pedestrian and bike
            connectivity in excess of what is otherwise required.
         2. The overall landscape and open space area provided within the proposed
            development exceeds required amounts by more than ten percent (10%).
         3. The proposed development is a transit oriented development (TOD) and
            provides direct connectivity for active transportation methods to a
            transportation facility.




Chick-Fil-A                                                                 App. No. 2024-0073-SP,
Site Plan and Deviation Request                         7                            2024-0249-VAR
         4. The traffic impacts of the proposed development will be mitigated through
            creative design, traffic calming measures, companywide carpool or transit pass
            programs, or other similar measures.
         5. The proposed development is designed to meet LEED certification standards.
         6. The proposed development involves redeveloped buildings, additions to existing
            buildings, minor structures being added to a site, or is a new building within a
            previously approved phased development and the deviation is necessary to
            create a consistency with other existing buildings.
         7. Vegetative landscaping (excluding lawn, turf, and groundcovers) exceeds the
            amounts required in this Title by at least ten percent (10%).
         8. The building is designed by a licensed architect in a specific architectural style,
            that utilizes facade materials differently than prescribed in subsection 9-22-
            040(F) of this chapter.
         9. The amount of primary materials used for each building facade exceeds the
            required amount by more than ten percent (10%).

The applicant has indicated in their deviation request letter (Exhibit J) how they meet at least
three (3) of these criteria. The letter specifically identified Criteria 1, 7 and 9 from the list
above. Staff has reviewed the deviation request and provides the following analysis:

    •    Criteria 1. Staff agrees that the amenities proposed are in excess of what is required.
         Per DCMC Section 9-22-050(C)(1), no amenities are required, but as shown on the site
         plan and landscape plans (Exhibits F and G), outdoor seating areas are being provided
         to the north of the restaurant building. The amenities include four (4) tables that will
         seat sixteen (16) and one (1) bench near the main entrance.
    •    Criteria 7. Staff agrees that the proposed vegetative landscaping exceeds the amount
         that is required in ordinance by at least ten percent (10%). Per DCMC Section 9-23-
         050(G) the required plant coverage is 75% at maturity. The landscape plan (Exhibit G)
         shows that the plant coverage will be 85.2% at maturity.
    •    Criteria 9. Staff agrees that the amount of primary materials used for each building
         facade exceeds the required amount by more than 10%. As shown in Table 7, the
         proposed restaurant is designed to be 100% brick, exceeding the requirement of 75%
         found in DCMC Subsection 9-22-040(F)(3)(a).

Lighting. The lighting plan (Exhibit I) shows how new lighting fixtures will illuminate the site
during business hours. After hours, per the submitted plans, all lights will be turned off. Most
of the proposed lighting meets the standards of DCMC Section 9-20. However, the proposed
plans exceed the maximum permissible illumination for restaurant use and for the canopy,
the maximum average illumination for the canopy, and the maximum permissible
max/average illumination ratio per DCMC Table 9-20-1. Staff recommends that as a condition
of approval a revised lighting plan, showing compliance with DCMC Section 9-20 for outdoor
lighting design and illumination standards, be provided to staff for review and approval prior
to receiving land use approval for a building permit.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION

Staff recommends that the Planning Commission review the requests based on the
suggested findings and the criteria for approval, or denial as listed within the staff report.
The Planning Commission should also, hear from the applicant, and receive public comment
prior to making a decision.

If the Planning Commission decides to approve the site plan request, staff recommends they
include the following conditions of approval:

    1. That all requirements of the Draper City Engineering, Public Works, Building,
       Planning, and Fire Divisions are satisfied throughout the development of the site
       and the construction of all buildings on the site, including permitting.
    2. That all requirements of the geotechnical report are satisfied throughout the
       development of the site and the construction of all buildings on the site.
    3. That a revised lighting plan that complies with the outdoor lighting design and
       illumination standards of DCMC Section 9-20 be submitted for review and approval
       by staff prior to approval of a building permit.
    4. That a revised drainage report that complies with the LID requirements and retains
       the 80% storm events onsite be submitted for review and approval by staff prior to
       approval of a building permit.

Request for Deviation from Parking Counts Required in 9-25-060
Per Draper City Code Section 9-25-060 and Table 9-25-1, a fast-food establishment is required to
have 10 parking stalls per 1,000 square feet of gross floor area. Per Draper City Code Section 9-25-
060-B, the required parking calculation includes both a maximum and a minimum number of
required stalls. Calculated parking stall requirements for the proposed site are as follows:

• Proposed Site (6,239 square feet)

o (6,239 /1,000) * 10 = 63

Section 9-25-090 of Draper municipal code gives information on required parking deviations.
Required parking can deviate 10% higher or lower than calculated values without requesting a
formal deviation. This would result in providing parking in a range of 57 to 69 stalls. Because
proposed parking is greater than 69 stalls a formal deviation will be required for the site.
Section 9-25-090(B) states, In cases where parking in amounts other than required herein may be
appropriate, the planning commission may approve a request for a modification, by way of a
parking study, to increase or reduce parking requirements based on findings found in subsection C
of this section.
The Chick-fil-A development proposes 75 parking spaces and Kimley-Horn has prepared a parking
study, that is included in this submittal, which supports Chick-fil-A’s need for 75 spaces to provide
adequate parking for the anticipated demand from customers and employees.

The applicant is seeking a deviation from Draper City Code parking requirements and seeking an
increase in parking for the proposed Chick-fil-A development.

Chick-fil-A is expecting the proposed development location to be a busy location given the location
directly off I-15. The proposed restaurant is larger than others in the area in order to accommodate
more customers. The larger restaurant is also expected to require more employees at any given
time than a typical store. Additionally, the expected parking demand at the proposed location is
anticipated to be greater than the observed data from other local locations due to location, store
size, and the increased number of employees required (up to 30 employees need to be on site
during peak periods and will need to park on-site).
The expected parking demand based on ITE (anticipates 63-104 stalls required) and from local
collected data (anticipates 80 stalls required) is higher than the Draper City code parking
requirement. If Draper City Code spaces were the maximum allowed, over 50% of available parking
stalls during peak periods would be occupied by restaurant employees only.

A parking deviation in excess of the allowed standard parking stalls would allow the site to provide
adequate parking for the anticipated demand from customers and employees.



Request for Deviation from Building Placement Required in 9-22-050(A)(1)
Per Draper Municipal Code section 9-22-050(A)(1), all buildings shall be located with the main
facade and main entrance facing the principal street to which it has frontage. For the proposed
Chick-fil-A restaurant, the principal street to which it has frontage is the cul-de-sac of S 100 E to the
south of the development site. For this site to accommodate the size of the restaurant building to
provide adequate drive-through queueing to accommodate anticipated demand the restaurant
needs to be located to the west of the site and oriented to the east, toward Harmon’s Shopping
Center. Relocating the building to the north of the site and orienting it to the south would require a
significant reduction in the size of the building as well as a significant reduction in the length of the
drive-through. A reduction in the length of the drive-through has the potential to create traffic
conflicts if the drive-through queue spills out beyond the drive-through stack.
The applicant is requesting a deviation from municipal code standards to allow the building to be
oriented to the east rather than to the south towards the primary street frontage.

Per Draper Municipal Code section 9-22-030(A), an applicant may request deviations from the
design standards of section 9-22-040 and 9-22-050 of this chapter with the exception of the height

 both a weekday and weekend. A survey of the site parking and parking demand data were collected at two

     • Chick-fil-A at 3470 Digital Drive, Lehi, Utah
             o Estimated square feet: 4,700
             o On-site parking provided: 48

             o Estimated square feet: 4,700
             o On-site parking provided: 62

OPERATIONAL DATA
Chick-fil-A provided information regarding numbers of employees at each of the restaurants observed.
Lunch time peak periods of the day requires up to 30 employees to be on site. Information provided
indicates 90% or more of employees drive to/from work at these locations. For the proposed Chick-fil-A
development, 30 vehicles would occupy nearly half the total parking spaces allowed to the development
per City Code.

At the two locations observed, employees at the restaurants parked outside official Chick-fil-A restaurant
parking lot boundaries. In Lehi, it was observed that employees and sometimes patrons parked in
neighboring restaurant spaces, on the adjacent street, or in a nearby vacated cul-de-sac. In Draper, it was
observed that employees or patrons parked in neighboring business spaces (McDonald’s) or on the vacant
street to the north.

Conclusions
The development applicant is seeking a deviation from Draper City Code parking requirements and seeking
an increase in parking for the proposed Chick-fil-A development.

Chick-fil-A is expecting the proposed development location to be a busy location given the location directly
off I-15. The proposed restaurant is larger than others in the area in order to accommodate more customers.
The larger restaurant is also expected to require more employees at any given time than a typical store.
