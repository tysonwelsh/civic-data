Electronic Board Meeting Determination

                 Greg Schulz read the following statement regarding the electronic meeting
determination:

               Consistent with provisions of the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act, specifically
Utah Code Ann. § 52-4-207(4), and acting in my capacity as the Mayor of the Magna Metro
Township (“Magna”), I hereby make the following written determinations in support of my decision
to hold and convene electronic meetings of the Magna Metro Township Council without a physical
anchor location:

1.     Conducting Council Meetings with an anchor location that is physically accessible for
members of the public to attend in person presents a substantial risk to the health and safety of
those who may be present at the anchor location.

2.     This determination is based upon the following:

       a.     Pursuant to the Public Health Orders issued by the State of Utah and Salt Lake
       County, the Magna Metro Township remains under a state of public health emergency
       related to the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, and significant person-to-person
       transmission of the virus continues to occur in Magna; and

       b.        All other facts noted in the August 7, 2020 Letter of Determination are still in effect.

DATE             TUESDAY                             JANUARY                              26, 2021

                These written determinations shall be read into the minutes at the beginning of the
Council meeting held in Magna on January 12, 2021, and future Council meetings as appropriate.
Council staff are instructed to include in the public notice of each Council meeting the information
on how a member of the public may view and make comments during the Council’s Electronic
meetings.

              This written determination is effective until midnight on February 28, 2021, and
may be re-issued by future written determinations of the Mayor at that or any other appropriate
time.

Reports from Community Stakeholders

Magna in Motion

               Keri Duckworth thanked the Council for allowing it to apply to be recognized as a
healthy state. Magna in Motion is meeting virtually. It will be doing an online four-week challenge
in February called a Happy Heart challenge. Each week, it will focus on a different area – strong
heart, happy heart, nourish heart, and calm heart. People are being encouraged to participate at
least once or twice during the week to be entered into a drawing for prizes.

Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District

              Mayor Peay stated one of the biggest things the GSLMSD is doing is bonding,
which he had some concerns about.

               Greg Schulz stated one item that came up during the last GSLMSD meeting was
weed abatement. Salt Lake County used to give the metro townships updates on those
abatements on a regular basis, so the townships knew whether there was going to be a cost
involved and to get the necessary paperwork done to put a lien on the property tax assessment
so they could get their money back for the cost of doing that weed abatement. This year, that did
not happen. Right now, the Magna Metro Township has about a $10,000 bill that it is going to
have to work through in arrears on to try to get paid back. The Magna Metro Township is working
with the County and the GSLMSD so that does not happen again in 2021.

               Mayor Peay stated the GSLMSD is looking into attaching that to property taxes
rather than the title of the property, so it can get money back faster.

                                  −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Unified Police Department (UPD)/Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area

                Council Member Prokopis stated the UPD is in the middle of a wage and benefit
study. It looks like some ranks will get 5-6 percent increases, and others will get less. The UPD’s
target is to get to the top three in the market. Sheriff Rosie Rivera has also been trying to right
size the organization with the departure of Taylorsville. The UPD is going to have to absorb that
loss, which involves cuts of $4 million. That precinct was one of the biggest precincts with 47
officers. Taylorsville has hired a police chief, and that person was a UPD employee. The UPD
is hoping several of its officers will get picked up by Taylorsville, so it does not have to do a
reduction in forces (RIF). Currently, it has a hiring freeze in place to try to absorb the loss.
