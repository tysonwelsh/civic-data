    c. Secondary Water Meter Installation Project

Dan Woodbury, Water Engineer, explained that Riverton City began work on May 4, 2020 to
install secondary water meters throughout the City. He said the new meters were part of an
effort to meet the State of Utah requirements announced in 2019 to meter all commercial and
residential properties in the state. He explained that installing secondary water meters would
support water conservation efforts and would inform residents of their secondary water use. He
said this would allow the city to test the effectiveness of the conservation programs and work
toward reducing the strain on the secondary water system. He said that crews would be working
throughout the southwestern portion of the city in order to complete the meter installation at
approximately 3500 properties by fall 2020. He made a PowerPoint presentation and then
addressed question from the Council Members.

    d. Emergency Operation Plan

Scott Chatwin, Emergency Manager, presented and reviewed an Emergency Operation Plan
and explained that the Emergency Operations Plan (EOP) would be activated and implemented
when the Riverton City Mayor City Council or designee declared an emergency or when the
Mayor or designee determined that an emergency was considered imminent or probable and
the implementation of the plan and the activation of the Riverton City Emergency Operations
Center was considered a prudent, proactive response.
Mr. Chatwin said that saving lives would take precedence over protecting property for decisions
involving resource allocation and prioritization. He explained that, under the direction of the City
Manager/EO Director, the Riverton City Emergency Management Coordinator was responsible
for correlating the emergency activities of Riverton City when dealing with regional, state, and
private partners. Coordination would be accomplished through established liaison roles within
the incident or unified command structure as outlined in the National Incident Management
System (NIMS).

    a. Ordinance No. 20-12 - Approving a Text Amendment, Adding Chapter 2.150,
       Administrative Interpretations, to the Riverton City Code, Proposed by Riverton
       City

Development Services Direct Jason Lethbridge explained that the public hearing was for an
amendment to the Riverton City Code proposed by Riverton City. He said the proposed
amendment adds a Chapter 2.150, Administrative Interpretations to Title 2 of the Riverton City
Code, which formalizes a process that already occurs. He explained that City Staff frequently
makes interpretations of the Riverton City Code, which most typically occurs through the
Planning Department. He said that for example, the Table of Commercial Uses Chart includes a
category for “Beauty and Barber Services” but not a category specific to nail salons. When a nail
salon contacts the City seeking a business license for a particular location, the Department
makes an interpretation as to whether a nail salon would be considered “beauty and barber
services”, rather than forward this action to the Planning Commission and City Council. The
authority to make such administrative interpretations is already established in the Riverton City
Code; however, a formal procedure for issuing an interpretation, especially when requested by
an applicant, does not. He said the proposed amendment adds the Administrative
Interpretations section to City Code, and formalizes the process.

Mayor Staggs opened a public hearing and called for public comment; there being none, Mayor
Staggs declared the public hearing closed.

Council Member Troy McDougal MOVED that the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 20-12 -
Adding Chapter 2.150, Administrative Interpretations, to the Riverton City Code,
Proposed by Riverton City. Council Member Buroker SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs
called for discussion on the motion; there being none, he called for a roll call vote and the vote
was as follows: Council Members Buroker-Yes, McCay-Yes, McDougal-Yes, Stewart-Yes, and
Wells-Yes. The motion passed unanimously.

    b. Discussion of Proposed Rezone, RD Development Located at 11845 South
       Redwood Rd, Proposed Rezone to RM-6-SD, Residential Multi-family with a
       Maximum of 6 Units per Acre, with Specific Development Designations Limiting
       Density and Configuration

Jason Lethbridge, Community Development Director, explained that a public hearing was held
for a rezone of several properties totaling about 12.8 acres located on the east side of Redwood
Road at approximately 11845 South. He said the subject properties were currently a mix of
zoning with single family residential (RR-22 and R-3), Professional Office and Neighborhood
Commercial Zones. He said the current General Plan was a mix of Medium Density Residential
and Professional Office. He said that nearby zoning included R-4 and R-3 to the south and R-3
across the canal to the east, and to the north is RR-22 zoning with larger lots. Across Redwood
Road to the west is RM-8 south of 11800 South and RR-22 north of 11800 South.

Mr. Lethbridge said the applicant requested RM-6 SD, which is residential multifamily, 6 units
per acre. The “SD” stands for Specific Development and is a means to apply special rules and
Page | 10 Minutes – City Council Meeting
May 5, 2020



regulations for Report by: TJP 4/28/2020 a development. In this application the SD would assign
areas within the overall development specific lot sizes and unit types.

Mr. Lethbridge further explained that following discussion with the Commission at a work
session, the applicant further modified the proposed density and layout for the project, and the
proposed SD designations were based on that layout as follows: Essentially, the project area
would be divided into three sections as follows:

         Area 1 would consist of the southern portion of the properties. There would be a
         maximum of 14 lots in that area, with a minimum lot size of 10,000 square feet. Other
         than lot size, all setbacks and other standards for this area would be consistent with the
         R-4 zone.
         Area 2, which is the northern portion, would be developed consistent with the detached
         single-family requirements of the RM-6 zone. This includes a minimum lot size of 5,000
         square feet. The lots adjacent to the north and east boundaries of this area will be a
         minimum of 6,000 square feet. There will be a maximum of 29 lots in that area.
         Area 3 is the smaller parcel just to the south of the existing commercial site on Redwood
         Road. This property may be developed consistent with the attached unit standards in the
         RM-6 zone, with the exception that up to 5 units may be attached. A maximum of 10
         units will be allowed in this area. This is reflected on the included diagram.

The RM-6 zone includes a requirement that perimeter fencing comply with Section 18.155.080
of the Riverton City Code where development is adjacent to a single-family zone. That section
requires a six foot solid masonry fence, and so for this project area the fencing would be six foot
solid masonry for the entire project boundary. The RM-6 zone also includes a requirement for
open space. Section 18.45.180(11) specifies that where individual lots are utilized in a project
over 10 acres, which is the case in this proposed project, a minimum of one (1) acre of open
space. The ordinance requires that open space to be privately maintained, unless otherwise
approved. The conceptual plans show open space that complies with the minimum area
requirement, and specific treatment and configuration would be approved as part of a site plan
application.

Mr. Lethbridge said the question of road connections has been a significant issue with the
application. Laurel Chase Drive currently stubs to the southern boundary of this project area,
and the northwest corner of the project area is the intersection of Redwood Road and 11800
South. Much has been discussed on the potential connection of Laurel Chase Drive to that
signalized intersection, and the possible impacts of that connection on the surrounding road
network. The applicant has presented several conceptual layouts showing alternative road
connections. Traffic impacts are typically reviewed with a rezone, and are an important
consideration in this case. But the road connection is not specific to this application or to a
particular density. He said the City would need to address the road connection irrespective of
the eventual density of this site. Final technical design of a roadway system is typically made
with the site plan/subdivision design for a project. He said the Council may consider adding
language such as the following, or other similar direction:

    •    Laurel Chase Drive shall be connected to 11800 South as a full public right-of-way.
    •    Laurel Chase Drive shall terminate in a cul-de-sac in Area 1 on the south side of the
         open space.
    •    Laurel Chase Drive shall not connect to the intersection of 11800 South and Redwood
         Road.
