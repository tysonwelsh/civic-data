GPZ-7-2020
Holiday Oil
6443 West 3500 South
General plan change from low density residential to neighborhood commercial and zone
change from R-1-8
0.34 acre

This application was continued during the Planning Commission meeting on September 23. The
Commission wanted to grant the applicant additional time to see if more landscaping could be
added along 3500 South.

Holiday Oil has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for a 0.34 acre parcel located
at 6443 West 3500 South. The property is currently zoned R-1-8 (single unit dwelling residential,
minimum lot size 8,000 SF) with a General Plan designation of low density residential (3 to 4
units/acre). The proposed zone is C-1 (neighborhood commercial) and the proposed General Plan
designation is neighborhood commercial.

Surrounding zones include R-1-8 to the west and south, A (agriculture, minimum lot size ½ acre)
to the north and C-1 to the east. Surrounding land uses include homes to the west and south, a
church to the north and a convenience store to the east. The subject property includes a home that
was built in 2002.

The applicant is proposing to demolish the existing home and neighboring convenience store and
car wash to make way for a larger convenience store, car wash and fuel canopy. If this General
Plan/zone change is approved, a conditional use permit will be required for the planned
redevelopment.

The City’s Major Street Plan shows the future right-of-way width for this portion of 3500 South
as 106’. The existing right-of-way width is 80’. Section 7-2-111 states:
       “Wherever a Front Yard is required for a Lot facing on a Street for which a Subdivision
       plat has been recorded in the office of the Salt Lake County Recorder, the depth of such
       Front Yard shall be measured from the mapped Street line provided by the Subdivision
       plat. Where a Subdivision plat has not been recorded and no widening is anticipated in
       the Major Street Plan, measurements shall be made from the existing Right-of-way line.
       Where widening is anticipated in the Major Street Plan, measurements shall be made
       from the proposed Right-of-way line.”

According to the applicant, requiring a minimum landscaped setback of 33’ as measured from
the existing right-of-way line along 3500 South would make the site unusable for a convenience
store. If the Commission believes that the proposed General Plan/zone change is appropriate and
that a landscaping exception should be granted by development agreement, staff suggests that the
development agreement require 13’ of right-of-way dedication.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. This application should be approved subject to a development agreement
      requiring 13’ of right-of-way dedication as described in this report and allowing an
      exception to the additional landscaping required.

   2. Continuance. This application should be continued for reasons determined during the
      public hearing.

   3. Denial. This application should be denied as the parcel should remain residential.



Discussion:    Steve Pastorik presented the application. Commissioner Winters explained
               looking at the map and seeing one business near the subject location with a
               similar setback as the subject property. Therefore, the two businesses would look
               alike when they widen 3500 South. He feels that the 33-foot setback is not
               needed in this case.

ZT-7-2020
West Valley City
A zone text change to create the Chesterfield Overlay Zone

At the request of the Planning Commission and City Council, City staff is requesting a zone text
change to create the Chesterfield Overlay Zone. Related to this application is application Z-8-
2020, which applies the proposed overlay zone to the Chesterfield neighborhood.

The Chesterfield neighborhood is unique in that homes within the neighborhood are generally
older and smaller than most of the rest of the City and many of the neighborhood streets do not
include curb, gutter and sidewalk. The attached PowerPoint presentation includes information
that highlights ways that the Chesterfield neighborhood is unique. The proposed Chesterfield
Overlay Zone includes standards for new development that will allow new homes that are more
compatible with existing homes. Below is a brief explanation for the standards included in the
proposed overlay zone:

   1. Minimum Area for Homes – As mentioned above, many of the homes within the
        Chesterfield area are older and smaller than most other homes within the City. The
        average year built of homes in Chesterfield is 1965. The Citywide average is 1980.
        Within the R-1-6 (single unit dwelling residential, minimum lot size 6,000 sq. ft.) zone,
        77% of Chesterfield homes are smaller than 1,400 sq. ft., which is the proposed minimum
        size for ramblers within the overlay zone that are zoned R-1-6. Within the A (agriculture,
        minimum lot size ½ acre) zone, 73% of Chesterfield homes are smaller than 1,600 sq. ft.,
        which is the proposed minimum size for ramblers within the overlay zone that are zoned
        A. In addition, a large portion of Chesterfield falls within a flood zone where basements
        are not allowed.
   2.   Maximum Building Height – 95% of homes within Chesterfield have only one story.
   3.   Exterior Materials – The exterior of most homes in Chesterfield is some form of siding or
        stucco.
   4.   Minimum Garage Size – 43% of homes do not have a garage.
   5.   Garages within the R-1-6 Zone – Most of the lots within the R-1-6 zone are 50’ wide.
        After accounting for side yard setbacks, there is 34’ of width remaining for a home. With
        a two-car garage being at least 20’ wide, only 14’ would remain for the non-garage
        portion of the home. For homes with attached garages, City Code requires the width of
        the front of the house excluding the garage to be at least 18’ for ramblers and 15’ for
        multi-levels. Also, for those Chesterfield homes within the R-1-6 zone that do have a
        garage, 76% of the garages are detached from the home.
   6.   Flag Lots Prohibited – The City Council does not support flag lots in Chesterfield.
   7.   Improvement of Right-of-Way – Many streets within Chesterfield do not have curb,
        gutter and sidewalk. Those streets where improvements are still required within the
        overlay zone are streets that provide access to community uses like the Tongan United
        Methodist Church, American Preparatory Academy, Khadeeja Islamic Center and
        Redwood Elementary.
   8.   Design Treatments – Since the overlay zone allows smaller homes and does not require
        brick or stone, the list of design treatments needed to be amended for the overlay zone
        accordingly.
   9.   Design Variation – Given the variety of homes within Chesterfield, the City Council
        wanted to prevent “cookie-cutter” type houses in the neighborhood.

Included with this report is a table that compares the proposed overlay zone standards with the
current ordinance requirements.

Staff Alternatives:

   4. Approval.

   5. Continuance.

   6. Denial.

Z-8-2020
West Valley City
Between 2420 South and 2920 South and between 1680 West and the Jordan River
Add the Chesterfield Overlay Zone
202 acres

West Valley City planning staff has requested a zone change to add the proposed Chesterfield
Overlay Zone to properties located between 2420 South and 2920 South and between 1680 West
and the Jordan River. Associated with this application is application ZT-7-2020 which creates
the proposed Chesterfield Overlay Zone within the Zoning Ordinance.

If approved, the proposed Overlay Zone would apply to 493 parcels. The underlying zoning on
all of these parcels is either R-1-6 (single unit dwelling residential, minimum lot size 6,000 sq.
ft.) or A (agriculture, minimum lot size ½ acre). Those properties with A zoning will retain their
animal rights if the Overlay Zone is adopted.

The proposed Overlay Zone will apply to all new homes and additions to existing homes. The
Overlay Zone standards address housing design and right-of-way improvements. It does not
change allowed uses, setbacks or density regulations.

Woodhollow Estates PUD Phase 4 - Preliminary Plat

BACKGROUND:

Ken Olson is requesting preliminary approval for the Woodhollow Estates PUD Phase 4
Subdivision. The subdivision is bordered on the north by property zoned C-2 and the west by the
Woodhollow Estates PUD Phase 2 Subdivision. U-111 is located immediately to the east and two
communication facilities are located to the south.

As mentioned in the study session, this phase was not part of the original Woodhollow Estates
PUD development and was therefore not part of the development agreement. The developer
recently petitioned the City to include this property within the overall project and suggested several
enhancements to the development agreement, not only for this property, but for the remainder of
the Woodhollow Estates PUD Subdivision. However, the amended agreement would not affect
phases 1 or 2 which were developed by Century Homes.

       any other modification to existing ditches or irrigation systems.

   ▪   Soils report will be required.

   ▪   Masonry wall required along U-111

   ▪   Revisions to plat required.

ISSUES:

The subdivision will consist of 9 lots on 2.7 acres. This equates to an overall density of 3.3 units per
acre. Lots range in size from 7,500 square feet to 16,700 square feet. The average lot size has
been calculated at just over 10,000 square feet. All lots meet the area and frontage requirements
of the development agreement.

Access to the subdivision will be gained from a stub street in phase 2 of the Woodhollow
Subdivision to the west. As part of this subdivision, a new road and cul-de-sac will be constructed.
The cul-de-sac will be constructed to provide a turnaround for residents and emergency vehicles.
It will also provide access to the southern properties for when they develop. While there are no
plans to develop this property at the present time, at some point in the future this could change.
Access and infrastructure would be available if the communication properties were to develop.

The proposed subdivision has a substantial grade change from the south to north and east to west.
The preliminary plat identifies these grades throughout the subdivision. The City Engineering
Division will review the proposed grading plan. Should there be problems or concerns with
regards to existing or proposed grades, those will need to be resolved prior to City Council review.
The developer will need to illustrate how the site will be contoured and what retaining methods if
any, will be used to ensure slope stability and to eliminate potential grading problems between
lots.

The developer is proposing to install a decorative masonry wall along the east side of the
development being adjacent to U-111. All other fencing requirements will be per the development
agreement. More specifically, the sides of the development adjacent to the communication tower
and commercial property will be fenced with black vinyl coated chain link. Residents could install
a vinyl fence at these locations if desired. The masonry wall will be required to match the masonry
wall being installed along 6200 South.
