WASHINGTON COUNTY LAND USE APPEAL HEARING
MINUTES
APRIL 15, 2019

The Appeal Hearing of the Board of the Washington County Commission was called to
order by Commissioner Iverson at 3:00 p.m. on April 15, 2019, in the Washington County
Commission Chambers 197 East Tabernacle, St. George, Utah. Those in attendance were:
Commissioner Victor Iverson, Commission Gil Almquist, Deputy County Attorney Eric
Clarke, Commission Administrator Nicholle Felshaw and Deputy Clerk Cheyenne
Bentley.

Others in attendance: Dean Holliday, representing himself (appellant); Attorney Heath
Snow, representing Slade Hughes and John Leavitt (applicants); and Attorney Ron Hill
representing fifty-seven (57) citizens of Veyo and Brookside (appellants).

Commissioner Cox was not in attendance because he recused himself from considering the
appeal.

APPEAL OF A CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT APPROVAL FOR PINE VALLEY
RECREATION LLC

Washington County Deputy Attorney Eric Clarke explained that this appeal was in regards
to a conditional use permit for a Rural Recreation Ground Facility. The permit was granted
on March 12, 2019, to the Pine Valley Meadow Recreation, LLC., by the Washington
County Planning Commission. The appeal hearing today is de novo and no deference will
be given to the Planning Commission. The appellants bear the burden of proof, and if a
split decision is reached, the planning commission’s decision will be upheld. He also
explained that a decision on this appeal hearing will not be given today. A written decision
will be formalized within fifteen (15) business days, as the County Ordinance allows.

Dean Holliday owns a parcel of property directly adjacent to the proposed recreation area.
He expressed concerns with the following issues: inconsistent plans; lighting; animals; and
potential commercial use.

Commissioner Iverson asked Washington County Community Development Director Scott
Messel for clarification as to why there were multiple changes to the application.

Mr. Messel explained that the term Rural Recreation Grounds and Facilities was recently
created. As complaints were received, the applicant was notified and given the opportunity
to make changes to the site plan.

Attorney Ron Hill, explained that the water to the area is supplied by a culinary water
association and the Planning Commission has not determined if water supplied to the area
is sufficient and sustainable. The Veyo Culinary Water Association owns 299.4 acre feet
of water, and is using 445.5 acre feet of water. Of the appellants, twenty-three (23) are
users of the culinary water.

Mr. Hill also mentioned that the development did not fit the OST-20 (Open Space
Transition 20-acre minimum lot size) zone as the parcel was only 19.93 acres, rather than
20 acres. It was suggested that the recreation area would be more appropriate in the C-2
(Commercial) zone, where the main purpose is to convenience the travelling public.

Mr. Hill stated that the code requires a site plan to be submitted which includes: the
consideration of elevations, auto circulation, fencing or screening, drainage plans,
landscaping, and garbage removal. Other items to be included with the site plan include:
traffic plan, and a demonstration that utility capacity is adequate, as well as adequate
emergency access and parking. Because those items were not included in the site plan, the
Planning Commission could not make determinations about the area,.

Mr. Hill concluded by stating that the applicant must demonstrate that the health and safety
of the residents will not be affected by the proposed recreation area. Currently, it is
detrimental through being on the same water supply as the proposed project.

Attorney Heath Snow explained that the applicants applied for a conditional use permit for
a rural recreation grounds and facility. He then read aloud the definition of the use area.

Mr. Snow argued that the County does not regulate water use and the Planning Commission
makes decisions based on facts presented.

Mr. Snow stated that a Conditional Use Permit is an administrative permit that does not
necessarily approve a structure, but rather approves a use of the land. The site plan
requirements are guidelines based upon the type of use permit being sought. It would be
considered appropriate to approve a Conditional Use Permit subject to state and federal

requirements being met.

Mr. Snow concluded by stating that the Planning Commission was correct in their decision
to award the applicant the Conditional Use Permit.

Mr. Holliday rebutted by stating that the newly completed plans now showed the sewage
leach field directly adjacent to his property, and a portion of the recreation area encroaching
on his driveway. He stated that a fence or wall should be constructed in order to separate

the properties.

Mr. Hill rebutted by stating that there was no discretion about what does and does not need
to be submitted as part of a site plan as outlined in the County’s code. If the plan is not
complete and accurate the Planning Commission cannot take action on it.

Mr. Snow concluded by reminding the Commission that the appeal was not regarding the
water regulation in the proposed area, rather, it was about land use. He also stated that the
water report mentioned earlier was not presented in full.

Mr. Hill concluded by stating that although, there are discretionary items in a site plan
requirement, the required items were not entirely met by the applicants. He also reiterated
that the 20-acre minimum lot size is important.

Mr. Clarke thanked the parties for being prepared and presenting their arguments in a clear
and calm manner.

Commissioner Iverson thanked all parties for their civility during the hearing and stated
that the arguments will be taken under advisement.

There being nothing further to come before the Commission, the appeal was adjourned at
5:30 p.m.
