    GOLF PROFESSIONAL AT THE RIDGE GOLF CLUB

              their lives, but my own as well. My new reason for being a PGA

RESOLUTION 20-21: APPROVE AN INTERLOCAL COOPERATION
AGREEMENT BETWEEN WEST VALLEY CITY AND SALT LAKE COUNTY
FOR ACQUISITION OF THE SWIMMING POOL AT CENTENNIAL PARK
Nancy Day, Parks and Recreation Director, presented proposed Resolution 20-21 that
would approve an Interlocal Cooperation Agreement between West Valley City and Salt
Lake County for acquisition of the swimming pool at Centennial Park.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The Centennial Outdoor Swimming Pool has been owned and operated by Salt
       Lake County. Discussion has been initiated by West Valley City staff in regard to
       what implications may incur by acquiring and operating this amenity. After careful
       consideration, staff feel that this acquisition would have positive effects on the West
       Valley City community for several reasons.

       The Family Fitness Center, also located in Centennial Park, has full-time aquatics
       staff and a fully functioning division who could successfully operate and maintain
       this pool. The Family Fitness Center also has support staff to aid in the operation
       such as maintenance, concession and desk clerk staff. Adding this amenity to the
       operational structure of the Family Fitness Center would be a seamless endeavor.
       Acquiring this facility will assist with programming and management options that
       will be beneficial to West Valley City residents.

       A financial plan, based off of records obtained from Salt Lake County along with
       West Valley City staff calculations, has been designed. This shows that revenue
       produced (including admission fees, memberships, programming and events) will
       offset the expenses needed to operate this facility.

Councilmember Buhler asked if this will be run as part of the Fitness Center where current
membership will grant residents access. Nancy replied there will be an additional $5 a
month fee for individuals, $8 a month fee for families, annual memberships for just the
outdoor pool, and daily passes. Councilmember Lang asked how old the pool is and what
condition it is in. Nancy replied it is 19 years old and is in fairly good condition.

RESOLUTION 20-22: AUTHORIZE THE CITY TO PURCHASE FOUR OVERT
ACTIVITY SUPPRESSION POLE CAMERA KITS
Nicole Cottle, Assistant City Manager, presented proposed Resolution 20-22 that would
authorize the City to purchase four overt activity suppression pole camera kits.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The West Valley City Police Department is requesting to purchase four Overt
       Activity Suppression Pole Camera Kits at a cost of $43,940.00. These four cameras
       will be placed in permanent locations along the parkway trail system with West
       Valley City. The cameras will be used to alert the police department to crimes in
       progress, obtain evidence, and identify offenders. It will allow us to monitor issues
       such as drinking and gambling on the trail systems, gang loitering, and narcotic
       usage. The cameras will have alert notifications for afterhours violations and
       enable officers to speak to the person in violation of the law directly and increase
       both the safety of the public and the efficiency of public safety personnel in the
       area.

       Specialty Vehicle Concepts, Inc is a sole source manufacturer.

Councilmember Lang asked if the cameras are mobile. Nicole replied these details are still
being discussed but two of them may be ordered as mobile units. Councilmember
Christensen asked how the City will protect the cameras from vandalism or theft. Nicole
replied the cameras are placed very high up but there is a risk, as there is with anything.
Mayor Bigelow asked if footage is recorded and stored. Nicole replied yes. Councilmember
Buhler asked if someone will be monitoring the cameras every day. Nicole replied the
cameras can be viewed in the Intel room of the Police Department or can be routed to a
cell phone of a particular officer. She indicated that staff can determine the most effective
way of utilizing the cameras, but they will be monitored frequently.

Steve Lehman, CED, presented application SD-1-2019, filed by West Valley City,
requesting Final Plat Approval for the Beagley Road Dedication Plat for property located
at 2540 South 6755-7000 West.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The West Valley City Engineering Division is requesting final plat approval for the
       dedication of Beagley Road from 6755 West to approximately 7000 West.

       In 2015 the City began processing the first phases of the ARA Industrial Center
       Subdivision at approximately 6755 West SR-201 South Frontage Road. Phase 2 of
       the ARA Industrial Center was reviewed and approved in 2016. Phase 2 is located
       immediately to the east of the Beagley Estates Subdivision and north of the Riter
       Canal.

       During the review process for phase 2 a recommendation was made that to provide
       for a more efficient access to the Industrial Park, a connection could be made with
       Beagley Lane. Beagley Lane intersects 7200 West at approximately 2590 South.
       The intersection of Beagley Lane and 7200 West is better situated than the
       intersection at the SR-201 South Frontage Road, especially for the vehicles coming
       out of the ARA Industrial Center. The distance from SR-201 and the South
       Frontage Road is insufficient for stacking and maneuverability for larger vehicles.

       Over the last few years, the City Engineering Division has been coordinating this

       Jeff Jackson, CED, presented proposed Resolution 20-03 that would approve a Real
       Estate Purchase Agreement with DRH Development, LLC, to purchase property
       located at 2574 South Redwood Road.

       Written documentation previously provided to the Redevelopment Agency
       included information as follows:

              The RDA has been studying the Redwood corridor and the evolution that
              has occurred over the last decade. This area has long been in need of
              redevelopment. As with other redevelopment areas, the City and the
              Redevelopment Agency have monitored this area in order to assess the right
MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – FEBRUARY 18, 2020
                       -14-


         time for redevelopment. With the addition of the opportunity zone
         designation, this area has become an area of focus. As with other areas,
         when key pieces of property are put to market, the RDA will often purchase
         the pieces and assemble them for future redevelopment. This piece of
         property is vacant, located right on Redwood Road and backs onto the area
         known as the Lake Park Four Plex neighborhood. This property is a small
         but essential piece of the potential larger development. Therefore, the
         Redevelopment Agency desires to get this under contract in order to
         assemble for a larger development. In the event that the area is not
         redeveloped, this piece remains viable for resale and would not represent a
         loss the RDA.

  COUNTY FOR THE PURCHASE AND SALE OF CERTAIN REAL

  Jeff Jackson, CED, presented proposed Resolution 20-01 that would approve a
  Purchase and Sale Agreement with the Housing Authority of Salt Lake County for
  the purchase and sale of certain real property.

  Written documentation previously provided to the Redevelopment Agency
  included information as follows:

         The Salt Lake County Housing Authority has made a determination that it
         must divest itself of properties all across the county. Two of the properties
         the City has been watching are these four plex properties in Lake Park.
         These properties are part of a larger area of four plex units. They are
         representative of an area of high usage of public services and they sit
         directly in the middle of the potential redevelopment area. When the
         County Housing Authority put them on the market the RDA sought to get
         them under contract for the purpose of controlling an important piece of any
