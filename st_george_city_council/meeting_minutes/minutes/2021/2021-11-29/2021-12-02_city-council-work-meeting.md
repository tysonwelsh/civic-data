OPENING:
     Mayor Randall called the meeting to order and welcomed all in attendance.

DISCUSSION REGARDING PROPOSED CHANGES TO TITLE 10, CHAPTER 13A OF
CITY CODE AS IT PERTAINS TO CERTAIN PUBLIC STREETS IN THE HILLSIDE
OVERLAY ZONE:
      Community Development Director John Willis remined the Council that they
      requested this item be discussed at a work meeting. He showed the proposed
      language changes to the code.

      Public Works Director Cameron Cutler provided a history on the proposed changes.
      He showed images depicting alternatives for traffic on Bluff Street and St. George
      Boulevard from when Bluff Street was widened; Continuous flow intersection; and
      Median U-turn. He then spoke about the environmental assessment from January,
      2012 and showed a map outlining a conceptual plan for a proposed traffic pattern at
      St. George Boulevard and Bluff Street. This proposed pattern violates the City’s
      hillside ordinance which is why the ordinance is being proposed as it is.

      Councilmember Larkin noted those with Tech Ridge and for those living on the hill
      already have never been able to safely ride a bike from the Boulevard to the top of
      the ridge; it is not a comfortable pedestrian or cycling space. She feels the proposal
      will be great for Tech Ridge as well as residents on the Black Hill if it ties into 100
      South as there are already bike lanes and sidewalks.

      Public Works Director Cameron Cutler explained moving the intersection coming
      down from the hill from St. George Boulevard to 100 South will make the
      intersection at St. George Boulevard and Bluff much better. He commented there
      already is an intersection at 100 South. There is a proposed concept plan that
      includes a pedestrian bridge across Bluff Street to Tabernacle.

      Community Development Director John Willis commented there is no active
      transportation access currently on the road to Tech Ridge. Tying the road into 100
      South provides some benefits as there is active transportation access already on 100
      South. After being asked by Councilmember Curtis, Mr. Willis explained part of the
      ordinance requires the section that is abandoned to be mitigated.

      Public Works Director Cameron Cutler noted walls would be required much like the
      walls along Bluff Street.

      Resident Dan McArthur commented these items concern him. Past Councils put
      density on the hill to accommodate these types of problems. He is concerned as the
      hillside ordinance was put in to protect the hillsides and that the ordinance is being
      changed to accommodate Tech Ridge and nowhere else in the City. HE asked the
      Council to think this through and get the input form the public, not discuss the item
      in a work meeting as there are a number of residents who are concerned with the
      development on this property.

      After being asked by Mayor Randall, Community Development Director John Willis
      stated there was a public hearing at the Planning Commission meeting. He then
      explained there are no other areas that fit the criteria. If Tech Ridge were to be
      developed as single-family homes, he asked if it would require the alignment of the
      road.

      Public Works Director Cameron Cutler explained the alignment of the road eliminates
      the requirement of having a through turn situation; there would need to be a
      through turn or mitigation with single-family homes. It did not take much traffic on
      Tech Ridge Drive to require the changes. The traffic engineer looked only at what
      has been approved on the property with Dixie Applied Technical College, Zonos, and
      Vaison, the intersection starts failing with these buildings.

      Mayor Randall noted Rick Torgersen with UDOT has said the intersection will fail.

      Public Works Director Cameron Cutler explained even with trying to mitigate to
      change the cycle lane, the north bound failed. After being asked by Councilmember
      Hughes Public Works Director Cameron Cutler indicated the roadway alignment is
      needed now, no matter what.

      Councilmember Larkin commented to Mr. McArthur’s point, there has to be a failing
      intersection to drive this change in the ordinance. All of the Councilmembers want to
      protect the hillsides.

      Councilmember McArthur commented his biggest concern is putting the ordinance in
      place for hillsides the Council does not want to be disturbed.

      Community Development Director John Willis noted the benefit is that this is the
      hillside ordinance therefore, each request would need to be reviewed and approved
      by the Council on a case-by-case ordinance. After being asked by Councilmember
      McArthur, Mr. Willis stated the proposed changes do not open the door for others to
      ask for special concessions for other areas.

      City Attorney Tani Pack Downing agreed with Community Development Director John
      Willis.

      Public Works Director Cameron Cutler explained the speed will need to be reduced to
      accommodate the proposed turn. There was a concept that had pillars going into the
      hillside to elevate the road, he was concerned with the pillars. After being asked by
      Councilmember Hughes, Public Works Director Cameron Cutler outlined who would
      pay for the proposed roadway improvements.

      Economic Vitality and Housing Director Shirlayne Quayle noted the developer plans
      to cover the costs.

      Community Development Director John Willis commented staff will need to make
      sure the cost for the improvements is added to the development agreement. He
      explained the Hillside Review Board would still review these types of requests.

      The meeting adjourned at approximately 4:35 p.m.
