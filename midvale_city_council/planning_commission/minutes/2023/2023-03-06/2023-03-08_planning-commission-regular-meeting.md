PUBLIC HEARINGS

1.   Jay Deher requests an amendment to Midvale Municipal Code 17-7-1.4 regarding
     Development Standards in the SF-1 Zone.

       The applicant proposes to amend the side and rear yard requirements in the SF-1 Zone in
       the following ways:

              1. Expand the “Stairs and Landing” paragraphs to also include balconies and
                 porches.
              2. Allow stairs, balconies, porches, or landings to project into the 8-foot side and
                 15-foot rear setback by 6.5 feet.

       The proposal results in an effective side setback of 1.5 feet and an effective rear setback
       of 8.5 feet when stairs, balconies, porches, or landings are present. The petitioner
       provided a letter detailing the reasoning for the request, which is attached.

       Public notice has been sent to affected entities as required in Section 17-3-9 of the
       Midvale Municipal Code. At the time of this writing no public comment has been
       received.

       Staff does not recommend approval.

APPLICANT:

Jared Bramwell, representing Jay Deher. Mr. Bramwell has been working with City Attorney,
Garrett Wilcox, to gain more insight and approach this text amendment in an amenable way.

Mr. Bramwell has investigated the MMC Section 17-7-1.4 that was referenced by Midvale City
Staff to recommend the denial of the proposed text amendment. Mr. Bramwell would argue that
the section in the code can stand to defend the reasoning behind the proposal. The section refers
to landowners having the freedom and protection of private property. He also states that this
section discusses the encouragement of redevelopment if in the public’s interest. Mr. Bramwell
would like to point out that the setback change would bring consistency to the code, references
the SF-2 Zone setbacks and intrusion allowance are more flexible than the SF-1 Zone.

Mr. Bramwell does not believe that the privacy issues mentioned by Midvale City Staff are
reason for denial.

DISCUSSION:

Commissioner Liedtke raises concern with a setback change based on the election of SF-1 zone
by Midvale families. The limited redevelopment in this zone is intentional. If the zoning
requirements are changed, it could cause a loss of control and spike in construction. Many
homeowners choose to reside in this zone for the sake of more privacy and space between
neighbors.

Commissioner Tippetts agrees that this could cause a lot of upset within the neighborhoods with
SF-1 zoning requirements.

Commissioner Tippetts and Vice Chair Anderson mention concern for residents’ privacy. The
setbacks would allow for second story patios and any construction of that sort would be very
intrusive.

Vice Chair Anderson mentions that it may cause more harm than good opening up this option for
many different neighborhoods within the growing city.

Aaron McKnight, Deputy City Attorney, states that this is a legislative decision. The Commission
will make a recommendation for or against and pass it on to City Council for a final decision.

Commissioner Erickson explains the differences between the SF-1 and SF-2 Zones and the
reasoning for those differences. The separate designations are intentional and exist to protect
residents’ land and privacy based on their zone. The change could cause not only homeowners to
take advantage of the situation but developers to overcrowd the SF-1 zone. Already facing scrutiny
for recent land development, Midvale could face more negativity and possible land development
abuse to follow.
