3.        REPORTS


          3. 1    Unified Fire Service Area Regarding Discussion of a Proposed Tax Increase
                   ChiefBurchett/Rachel Anderson

                 UFSA District Administrator Rachel Anderson gave a brief overview of the UFSA,
explaining that it was the taxing entity that most Unified Fire Authority member cities belonged
to. She     explained   that it   was   the   funding mechanism for fire and EMS             services   in those
Taylorsville City Council Minutes
October 4, 2023
Page 5




communities. The UFSA anticipated the need for a tax increase for each of the next two years
and state statute required that they provide a report regarding that increase to the legislative
bodies of those member cities. Ms. Anderson explained that a tax increase was needed to achieve
the following:
         Maintain recommended 4- handed crews in all UFA stations
         Upgrade fire stations as needed
         Meet the general cost of service issues
         Maintain the board- approved minimum fund balance of 15%


Ms. Anderson explained that UFSA had increased taxes previously in 2008 and again ten years
later in 2018. An increase was necessary once again and she anticipated a 12% increase in 2024
with potentially a smaller one the following year.

Chief Dominic Burchett explained some of the cost increases that inflation had brought to the
firefighting and EMS industry. The cost of a full set of turnout gear had gone up by 61% since
2018, fuel costs were up 40% since then, and the cost of medical supplies had increased by 15%.
Unified Fire sought to recruit and retain the best firefighters and paramedics, and their entry-
level wage had been increased 11. 37%             to maintain that level of staff quality. Chief Burchett
noted that it was challenging for his employees who made entry-level wages to afford a home in
the communities they served.

The   costs     of   fleet vehicles had increased as well. A fire engine now cost 32%              more,    while

ambulances were            up 27%, and ladder trucks were up a phenomenal 69%. Most of these vehicles
typically needed to be replaced every nine years so there was a replacement plan in effect.

Five fire       stations    had recently been   retrofitted   seismologically   while   an   additional    twenty
stations had received non- structural retrofits. The cost of building a fire station had almost
doubled since 2018, going from $290/ sq foot to $554/ sq foot.

Chief Burchett turned the time over to Tony Hill, the Chief Financial Officer of Unified Fire. Mr.
Hill explained that UFA' s call volume had increased by 28% and indicated the proposed tax
increase would mean approximately $ 49.67 more per year for the average homeowner and an
additional $      90. 30 for a commercial property. There would be a public hearing held so that
residents of the effected communities could weigh in on the proposed              increase. It would be held
on December 5th at 6pm at the UFA boardroom which was located at 3380 South 900 West.

7.       OTHER MATTERS


         7. 1     Discussion Regarding the Fair Housing Act— Tracy Cowdell/Jim Spung


         M     Senior Planner Jim Spung explained the importance of the Fair Housing Act and
provided a brief history of various federal fair housing laws that had been passed to prohibit
discrimination of various types when it came to housing opportunities. Specifically, the Fair
Housing Amendments Act of 1988 made it illegal to create housing-related policies and practices
which discriminated against someone based on his/ her disability or familial status. Mr. Spung
stated that drug and alcohol addiction were considered disabilities.

Mr. Spung then turned the time over to City Attorney Tracy Cowdell. Mr. Cowdell went into
greater detail regarding the stance that the Department of Justice and the Department of Housing
and Urban Development( HUD) had taken regarding fair housing legislation.
