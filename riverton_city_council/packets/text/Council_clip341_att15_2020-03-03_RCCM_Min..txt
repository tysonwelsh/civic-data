31         a.     Glass Recycling - Momentum Glass.
32
33   Jason Utgaard from Momentum Glass gave a presentation on glass recycling. He
34   recognized individuals present in the meeting who had visited the facility and welcomed
35   any desiring to take a tour. He summarized the history and mission of Momentum Glass
36   and stated that they are the only facility in Utah and the Rocky Mountain Region. They
37   maintain another facility in Colorado.
38
39   Mr. Utgaard reviewed the process glass goes through to be recycled into fiberglass
40   insulation. They currently have 700 commercial customers and are approaching 9,000
41   residential customers, who span the Salt Lake Valley as well as Park City. He explained
42   that glass assists in creating jobs. He gave an example of the benefit of recycled glass
43   with the Owens Corning facility in Nephi.
44
45   Mr. Utgaard reviewed the Curbside Glass Recycling Program, stating that it is a monthly
46   opt-in service where residents can sign up on the Momentum Recycling website. The
     Page | 2 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     March 3, 2020


 1   program also includes their municipal partners, who have sign-up forms on their websites
 2   as well. This is a month-to-month program and does not require a contract. Pick-up
 3   reminders for the glass recycling bins are sent by text, phone, email, and through a mobile
 4   app. After sign-up, the bin is delivered within a week and the glass collection service is
 5   provided.
 6
 7   Mr. Jeppesen highlighted the mobile app, which consists of the Waste Wizard that
 8   educates residents as they learn how to recycle. The app was launched in June and
 9   currently, 1,200 residents have downloaded it. It was estimated that 10,000 notifications
10   will be sent out each month through the app.
11
12   Mr. Jeppesen stated that Momentum Glass’s program is transparent in terms of their
13   subscriber information. This means that the City will have administrative access to their
14   residents’ subscriber data on the website. Monthly reports are also provided on the
15   website.
16
17   Mr. Jeppesen reviewed the history of the program consisting of growth and cost since it
18   first launched in 2012. The current cost for the program is $8.00 per month with a $25
19   activation fee. The cost is consistent with all the municipalities they work with. Each
20   municipality is required to meet a threshold of 125 subscribers before the service can be
21   launched.
22
23   Mr. Jeppesen stated that the drop-off locations generally increase in use once curbside
24   pick-up is provided due to the general awareness of the glass recycling program.
25
26   In response to a question raised, Mr. Jeppesen reviewed the billing process and
27   breakdown of payment to the City. He explained that the most effective ways of raising
28   awareness for the program are through the City newsletter, website, and social media
29   channels, along with a task force of college students hired by Momentum Recycling to
30   advertise for the company.

40   Emergency Management Coordinator, Scott Chapman introduced Emergency
41   Management Director, Bob Jeppesen from the Salt Lake County Health Department who
42   discussed the current coronavirus situation. Mr. Jeppesen reviewed the history of
43   COVID-19 (Coronavirus) and the details of how it is spread. He reviewed the symptoms
44   as well as the incubation period of the disease. He compared the Coronavirus to similar
45   diseases and their fatality rates. The case fatality rate for the Coronavirus is currently
46   estimated at 0.7% which is slightly higher than seasonal influenza.

 1
 2   There is currently no vaccine, however, it is under development. Generally, vaccines take
 3   up to one year to 18 months to produce. Mr. Jeppesen suggested individuals carry out
 4   the same practices as during the influenza season to prevent illness. To date, there is no
 5   anti-viral treatment for this disease, however, there is a new product that is being tested
 6   on patients in Nebraska. Currently, patients can only be treated with supportive care for
 7   this disease.
 8
 9   Mr. Jeppesen advised the residents of Riverton to wash their hands frequently, refrain
10   from touching faces, having physical contact with others, disinfect surfaces, stay home if
11   symptoms become worse, contact health care providers, and remain home for 24 hours
12   after sickness to prevent the contraction and spread of this disease.
13
14   Public health interventions began in China and travelers arriving in the United States after
15   February 23, 2020, were closely screened. The individuals were closely monitored for 14
16   days, required to take their temperature every day, and report to those monitoring them.
17   The Health Department urges communities to be sensitive to those individuals.
18
19   The Health Department has developed an Infectious Disease Emergency Response Plan.
20   Mr. Jeppesen reviewed the plans for various diseases and situations. Both Infectious
21   Disease Epidemiologists and Environmental Health professionals have a 24/7 emergency
22   call number. Mr. Jeppesen reviewed the process if an individual were to call that number
23   in the event of an emergency.
24
25   Mr. Jeppesen defined the terms quarantine and isolation and described the distinction
26   between them. He explained that all local public health officers in the State have the
27   statutory authority to order both quarantine and isolation as well as close mass
28   gatherings.
29
30   Mr. Jeppesen stated that the Council should consider how best to prepare Riverton
31   residents as well as determine what the City would need to do to maintain daily
32   operations.
33
34   Coronavirus is not currently in the State of Utah. The individual who is under quarantine
35   is asymptomatic and has not had the sickness. The Council should plan for possible
36   increased numbers in staff absences due to illness, possibly up to 30% of employees.
37   Mr. Jeppesen suggested the City monitor the absenteeism in the workplace and
38   presented ideas of how the City could accomplish this.
39
40   Mr. Jeppesen reviewed the vectors from the Middle East Respiratory Syndrome in a slide.
41   It is currently uncertain how the COVID-19 virus originated, but researchers speculate it
42   could have originated from bats. The Centers for Disease Control widened their case
43   definition of this disease recently, which results in a broader scale of individuals who may
44   currently have the disease. Currently, there are 105 cases of individuals with the disease
45   in the United States, however, the risk of contracting the disease remains low. The Health
46   Department has screened individuals who have been cooperative, which was a process

11   The second would address how information will be communicated to and from the
12   Riverton Police Department. The goal was for staff to make the City Council aware of
13   issues in the City as soon as possible. The concern was with how much information
14   should be disclosed to an elected official during an investigation. Staff wants to refrain
15   from giving sensitive information to elected officials, including names of victims,
16   witnesses, and suspects. However, if there is an incident of a certain nature at a certain

18   to allow police to provide pertinent information to Council Members, but not all
