Public hearing to allow input regarding transfers from the Water, Sanitary
Sewer, Storm Sewer, and Refuse Funds to the City’s General Fund ( )

A memo from Council staff regarding the proposed annual enterprise fund transfer came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated as required by Utah State Code §10-6-135.5, a city that is proposing to transfer funds from an
enterprise fund to another fund for a purpose not directly related to the goods or services provided by the enterprise fund must
provide notice of the transfers to the users of the enterprise fund. The city must also hold a public hearing on the transfers prior to
adoption or the amendment of the city’s budget. In Ogden, a budgetary transfer from the city’s Water, Sanitary Sewer, Storm
Sewer, and Refuse Utility enterprise funds to the general fund has been done for many years; however, the requirement to provide
notice of the transfers and to hold a separate public hearing is a recent change to State law. These utility funds are accounted for as
enterprise funds in Ogden and are operated with user fees charged for goods and services. In the proposed Fiscal Year 2024
budget, the City is proposing a transfer from these utility funds into the City’s General Fund to act as the collection of franchise
and property tax.

Council Deputy Director Symes summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation expound on the
purpose of the enterprise fund transfer. Other utilities like Dominion and Rocky Mountain Power pay franchise tax and property
tax to the City. The intent is to operate City utilities as separate utilities and the transfer is intended to act as the City’s mechanism
to collect those franchise and property taxes. The amount collected equates to six percent for franchise tax and six percent for
property tax. He discussed the impact of the transfer as follows:

e Proposed transfer for the FY2024 budget is $6,360,725 million (maximum, actual may be less)

e If the transfer is not made, utility rates may be lowered; however, revenues would have to be made up
elsewhere to remain fiscally solvent.

e Anticipated property tax revenue in FY24 is approximately $20.5 million.

e If these utilities were independent, the city would receive revenue from the collection of franchise and property
taxes.

Mr. Symes concluded State law requires that the legislative body hold a separate public hearing, which has been
scheduled for tonight; there is no action to be taken at the public hearing. The transfers are incorporated into the proposed budget;
the approval/denial of the transfers happens with the adoption of the FY2024 budget. The City will send a follow-up notice once
the budget is adopted.

Stuart Taylor stated that the main argument from the City in support of this transfer is that it is routine and is done every
year, but he wished to urge the Council to reject the transfer and be transparent in their decision-making process. Before
proceeding, it is important to recognize that enterprise funds serve a distinct purpose in the City’s financial framework, operates as
a self-sustaining entity, and to cover operational costs. The purpose of enterprise funds is not to generate a surplus of revenue.
Diverting a substantial fund of money from the Enterprise funds to the General Fund, the City is undermining the very principles
upon which this financial mechanism is built. Enterprise funds are intended to support and enhance the specific services provided
by the enterprise, rather than serving as a means to alleviate budgetary constraints in other areas. This practice flies in the face of
transparency and prior to the adoption of House Bill (HB) 164 in 2017, the serious lack of transparency regarding these transfers
had become all too common, and that is why the HB was passed. It is incumbent on all elected officials to ensure that hard earned
dollars are being used in a responsible and accountable manner. Ogden City should not rely on a routine surplus in the Enterprise
funds to transfer to a ‘slush fund’, especially given that there is no specific accounting of how the $6.3 million will be used. The
enterprise fund transfer is wrong and should not be approved; if the City needs the $6.3 in funding, a property tax increase should
be considered, but such a proposal will be viewed very negatively by the taxpayers and that is why Administration and the City
Council have chosen to support the enterprise fund transfer.

Proposed Ordinance 2023-32 approving the Capital Improvement Plan for
Ogden City for the Fiscal Years 2024-2028

Amemo from Council staffregarding the proposed Fiscal Year FY) 2024-2028 Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) came
before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the CIP is reviewed and adopted each year as a planning tool for major
capital investments in the city’s physical plant over a rolling five-year period. Each year’s plan is updated based on the changing
needs ofthe city, funding opportunities, projects completed and other similar factors. During this year’s budget process, proposed
capital projects will be reviewed alongside each department’s proposed budget and in context with the overall needs and resources
available to the city through the budget process.

Council Deputy Director Symes summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide Council
staff's analysis of the FY24-28 CIP. CIP projects include projects exceeding $50,000 that add to the City’s physical plant,
including design and/or construction and public buildings, streets, utility lines, parks, and other public facilities. Projects
exceeding $50,000 for major rehabilitation or reconstruction of the physical plant, projects resulting in the acquisition or long-
term lease of land, and certain feasibility studies and investigations relating to CIP projects are also examples of CIP projects. He
summarized funding sources for CIP projects, as well as the annual process for reviewing the proposed CIP at the same time as the
annual budget to allow for better alignment of the two documents. The Council has reviewed sections of the CIP along with the
budgets of various Departments of the City for the past two months and also received a recommendation from the Planning
Commission to approve ethe CIP with the following recommendations:

e Post an updated map showing the location of planned downtown parking structures on the city’s website.
e Extend the parking management plan south to 28th Street and east to Adams Avenue.
e Add park space as the community grows.

Mr. Symes concluded by summarizing the proposed funding for CIP projects in FY24, and highlighted major CIP
projects, including the Marshall White Center project, the 36-inch Ogden Canyon waterline project, and the Downtown Parking
Lot project. The final steps of the CIP process are to hold the public hearing, consider final adoption of the Plan, and revisit |
project ranking in September or October of this year.

Public Utility Manager Herd explained the Water Utility Department is proposing changes to water hodkup fees and
proposing to establish a fee for thawing frozen water lines. As part of the utility rates ordinance adopted in 2012, each of the four
utilities included in the study was to have an annual increase based on a set consumer price index. When the initial ordinance was
adopted in 2012, the CPI was calculated using a Wells Fargo CPI calculation. In 2015, a change was made to use the U.S. Bureau

a of Labor Statistics — Western Region calculation. While the CPI calculations used since 2012 have helped to capture a general
} consumer price index increase, in 2021 the Administration proposed what it considered a more accurate method. The annual
-’ increase would be a combination of two different indices. For operations and maintenance, the western region CPI produced by i

the US Bureau of Labor Statistics would be used, and for capital expenses, a Construction Cost Index (CCI) would be used. The 7
two indices would then be blended to produce the increase for that fiscal year. The two rates together would constitute the
Blended Inflation Adjustment (BIA). The proposed split between the CPI and CCI impacts the Water Utility budget most
significantly. While the other utilities have a different ratio of operations and maintenance expenses (reflected with the CPI) to
capital costs (reflected with the CCI), the Public Utility is the ratio used to calculate the annual BIA increase. The BIA applies to
Water, Sanitary Sewer and Storm Sewer. Because the Refuse utility does not generally have capital expenditures, the CPI is used
for the annual increase for that utility. The CCI index is the American City and County January year over year index. The CPI
remains as the western region BLS January year over year index. The proposed fee amendments include an annual inflationary
increase based on the operations CPI (BLS western region calculation). Since these fees have not been included in the annual ,
increases, Administration is proposing a large increase now to catch up to what the fees would have been if the CPI had been
applied since 2012 along with the charges for services increases. The proposed one-time increase is 35.7 percent. The fees will be
increased by the CPI annually beginning July 1 each subsequent year. The CPI applied to the Utilities’ charges for services

Public Comments

Kerry Wayne stated he has written to the Mayor about problems the residents on 1200 West in Marriott-Slaterville are
experiencing with Business Depot Ogden (BDO) and he asked for Council assistance. He then expressed his concern about the
eviction of the Northern Utah Wildlife Rehabilitation Center and urged the Council to address the issue by extending the lease for
the Center. It is an important use in the community and there is room for both the Center and Dinosaur Park, or there may be
another location that the City could relocate the Center to.

Steven Van Wagoner addressed discussion during the work session tonight about the idea of removing benches from
parks to prevent them from being used by the unhoused population of the community. He noted that the taxpaying residents of the
community are being punished for the actions of others and he urged the City to replace the benches that have already been
removed.

Angel Castillo echoed Mr. Van Wagoner’s suggestion to replace park benches. She then addressed the topic of employee
wages; she noted that money is not the only thing that keeps an employee in their current position, and she acknowledged that the
City offers good benefits, but money is the largest factor, and the Council needs to improve salaries offered to employees,

especially first responders. She then noted the Council has received an email from O2 Utah, anonprofit entity addressing clean air
, issues in the State of Utah; they are pledging to help find money to supplement low-income residents to take advantage of clean
_” — energy solutions.

Travis Pate highlighted the importance of the Airport Advisory Committee and suggested that the Council utilize them in
evaluating matters at the Airport before taking permanent actions. He then noted that the Council should expect accurate and
complete information from City staff relative to the applications the Council is considering before taking action on any matter. He
Eee
noted that the information presented to the Council regarding the boundary adjustment with South Ogden is not accurate and the
Council needs to reexamine the issue to determine that the City is taking full advantage of important opportunities. ( }
Mayor Comments

Mayor Caldwell thanked the Council for acknowledging Chief Administrative Officer Johnson and wishing him well in
his retirement. He then thanked City Comptroller Stout and her staff for their work on the Fiscal Year (FY) 24 budget; they have
worked closely with Department heads and staff to carefully craft a budget. He also appreciates the partnership with the City
Council to develop and review an annual budget and he feels good about the priorities that have been identified by the Council; he
also feels the City is very transparent in its budgeting process. Budgeting is the most important thing the City does and the check

, and balance system that has been employed in Ogden is effective.
Council member Comments

Council member White offered kudos to the Public Services and Fire Departments for their preparation for flooding in
the recent months; the past year has been remarkable in terms of weather conditions, but City staff was prepared and helped to
avoid more extensive damage.

Council member Hyer addressed the enterprise fund transfer that was acted upon tonight; he has some of the same
questions as many residents, but his concerns have been resolved by the explanation that the mechanism the City has chosen helps
to ensure that all users in the community are paying into the system, rather than private taxpayers being responsible to carry the
load for non-profit, public, institutional, and governmental organizations. He commented on the value that Business Depot Ogden
(BDO) has contributed to the City, and he is grateful for the lease revenue funding source in the City.
