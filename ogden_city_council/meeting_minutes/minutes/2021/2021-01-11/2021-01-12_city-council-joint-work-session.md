Proposed Annexation of 4753 Glasmann Way
         Curtis Galvez has submitted a petition to annex the western 0.09 acres of property at 4 7 53 Glasmann Way. Mr. Galvez's
property is split by the City's municipal boundary with the eastern portion inside the City and the western portion in
unincorporated Weber County. The property is vacant and Mr. Galvez wishes to construct a new single-family home on the
property. To do that, Mr. Galvez must first annex the rear portion and combine the two parcels. The parcel petitioned for
annexation is in Area 5 of the City's Annexation Plan. The property is proposed to be zoned R-1-8 as all the properties adjacent
are zoned as such.
         Planning Manager Montgomery made a presentation to provide additional information regarding the annexation petition.
         Council member Stephens asked if it is appropriate to consider annexing a single property or if it would be better if all
property owners on the block agreed to pursue annexation of their properties at the same time. Mr. Montgomery replied that the
City has spoken with all property owners surrounding the subject property about annexing the entire block at once, but the other
owners were opposed to having their properties included in the City's Annexation Plan. Due to that feedback, the City agreed to
consider annexation of properties on the street on a case-by-case basis. The City has committed to only annex a property if the
property owner initiates the process.
         Council member Hyer asked ifthe property being annexed is ready to be developed. Mr. Montgomery answered yes, the
applicant desires to build a home on the property and to receive City services.
         Council Deputy Director Symes noted the public comment/protest period for the annexation petition will end next
Tuesday. An action item is included on the January 19, 2021 agenda to set a public hearing for February 2, 2021 to consider a
fmal action on the petition.

                When the City receives Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) funds and HOME grant funds, a Five-Year
      Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) and an Annual Action Plan (AAP) that outline the work to be done with the funds are required. As
      part of the review process required by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), a Consolidated Annual
    . Performance and Evaluation Report (CAPER) is required to evaluate the progress that the City has made in using the funds and
      implementing the goals set forth in the ConPlan and the AAP. As part of the process, the City's Community Development
      Division provides a review of the CAPER that is submitted to HUD.
                Project Coordinator Fuentes made a presentation regarding the CAPER for Fiscal Year (FY) 2020. The CAPER satisfies
      the annual HUD reporting requirement and includes an outline ofCDBG and HOME expenditures, a summary offederally funded
      accomplishments, and addresses key interest items and required narratives. She summarized BUD funding sources: in FY2020 the
      City had $3.3 million in CDBG funds and $1.6 million in HOME funds. The total funding available for the year was just over $5
      million. She provided a snapshot of funding allocations in the FY2020 AAP. Total expenditures were $3.2 million spread across
      various AAP programs. She summarized HUD accomplishments in FY2020, beginning with Community Development. The City
      issued 74 Own in Ogden down payment assistance loans, constructed and sold seven new homes, purchased, rehabbed and sold
      eight Quality Neighborhoods homes, and facilitated seven emergency home repair loans. She presented photographs of several of
      the housing projects completed in the City.
                Council member Choberka asked how the City determines pricing for homes that are constructed or rehabbed. She noted
      she was aware of a home in the Stone Hill project area that was listed for sale in the $400,000 range and asked how that price was
      set. Community Development Manager Ogden noted that home was sold at appraised value, which is the case for all homes
      purchased or constructed and then resold by the City. He added that the City does offer programs that make it possible for buyers
      to buy a home at appraised value, including offering down payment assistance or second mortgages that help lower payments.
                Ms. Fuentes noted in Business Development, 276 people received assistance at the Business Information Center (BIC),
      17 full time equivalent (FTE) jobs were created with the small business loan program, four microenterprise business owners were
      assisted, and one special economic development project is underway. She presented photos and graphics related to the businesses
      who received assistance in FY2020.
                Council member White stated that she is asked regularly whether the City provides CDBG funding to non-profit entities.
      Ms. Fuentes answered the City does not provide grants to non-profits. Grant funding is reserved for low to moderate income
      people in housing and job creation areas. Mr. Ogden and Community and Economic Development Director Christopulos
      explained that Administration has concentrated funding in areas that will provide the greatest social and economic impact.

     Council Business
               Council Leadership Meetings: Council member Stephens mentioned the schedule for joint leadership meetings and
     asked how effective the meetings between leadership and Administration are. He noted that he would like to hear the ideas of
     other Council members and suggested discontinuing the practice of joint leadership meetings and, instead, offer each Council
     member the opportunity to sit in on a leadership meeting on a rotating basis. He feels that would be more productive than meeting
     with City Administration. Council member Hyer stated he is not sure it is necessary to choose one over the other. He feels that any
     Council member could meet with leadership and Council staff in addition to the monthly joint leadership meetings. Chair Blair
     said he does not want to discontinue joint leadership meetings, but he is willing to entertain other Council members participating
     in leadership meetings when they desire to.
               Short Term Rentals: Council member Stephens stated a property at 205 North Polk Avenue is being used as a vacation
     rental property and there have been problems associated with that use. He asked for a report from Administration regarding code
     enforcement actions for that property. Mr. Christopulos replied he is not aware of any previous complaints about this property;
     however, Code Enforcement is responding to this new complaint and will report back to him once they have more information.
               Speeding on Washington Boulevard: Council member Stephens noted the Council received correspondence from a
     resident who indicated he was nearly struck by a vehicle on Washington Boulevard and complained about speeding along that
     road. Chief Administrative Officer Johnson stated the Police Department has met with the complaining party and has also asked
     the patrol division to watch more closely for speeding. The complainant has been told that Washington Boulevard is a state road
     over which the City does not have jurisdiction relating to speed limits. The individual has indicated that he will follow up with the
     Utah Department of Transportation.

     as well. Ms. Eller-Smith said that recommendation can be forwarded to Administration and they may be willing to include it in a
     CIP project. If that is not the case, the Council could suggest a CIP amendment during the budget process. Council member
     Stephens stated he would be willing to meet with Council leadership to explore the idea further. Vice Chair White commented she
