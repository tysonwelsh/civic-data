SR 224 Roadway Improvements Discussion:
Dave Thacker, Building Official, presented this item and stated the City hoped to
improve from the accidents that had occurred on Marsac Avenue. He coordinated with
the Police Department and UDOT on continued safety measures.

Chief Wade Carpenter reviewed the runaway truck accident that occurred in August and
he indicated the City had dealt with brake problems for several years. Captain Phil Kirk
indicated his team had been working on dump truck enforcement on the road for the
past few months and had achieved almost 100% compliance with brake checks. Chief
Carpenter stated dump truck drivers were the main violators and it was good to see the
education and progress. Lieutenant McKinney remarked SR 224 was a high priority for
monitoring these vehicles and noted his appreciation to the Highway Patrol. Officer
Powers, who was in charge of brake inspections, indicated there were many violations
that were critical such as the vehicle being over its weight limit. He gave examples of
violations that officers could use to put the trucks out of service until those things were
fixed and noted most out-of-service violations were not serious. He stated most trucks
being stopped were from around town.

Chief Carpenter stated that after monitoring the offenses on Marsac Avenue, only five
percent were from trucks. He indicated officers were not allowed to stop trucks
randomly, so an inspection schedule needed to be set up. It was indicated most
violators were not the local vehicles.

John Robertson, City Engineer, stressed it was important to have brake check areas.
After the accident in August, flashing beacons were installed on top of the brake check
station signs. The speed limit was also reduced for trucks from 45 mph to 30 mph.
Signs would also be installed encouraging truck drivers to use their engine brakes. He
noted a pavement rehabilitation project would commence on Marsac Avenue in 2023.
Thacker stated the Flagstaff Master Plan Development (MPD) included language that
stated primary access was Mine Road and trucks should be using that road. Staff

Short Range Transit Plan (SRTP) Update:
Alex Roy, Transportation Planner, and Jason Miller and Paul Jewel, Fehr & Peers,
presented this item. Miller reviewed the goals for the SRTP: improve connectivity,
engage the community, ensure a top rated transit system, and incorporate tactical,
relevant innovation. This would be a five-year plan to help understand where to go from
here. He stated the recent separation of Transit with the County and COVID gave the
team incentive to have a clean slate approach in thinking what transit would look like in
the future and how it would fit into the mobility plan. Roy stated staff wanted to include
the community as much as possible. A survey was online, and there would be open
houses and pop-up open houses to help get feedback on transportation. He reviewed
the many forms of outreach used to make the community aware of the survey.

Miller indicated he completed an existing conditions analysis and had initial findings. He
thought Park City was making the right transit connections. He presented findings on
ridership and stated that although there was a drop, it was better than most transit
areas.

Council Member Doilney asked that the Chamber of Commerce and local businesses
were made aware of the surveys so they could communicate it to their employees.
Council Member Worel asked who the target group was for open houses, to which Roy
stated the general public. Roy indicated the SRTP survey link was on the Transportation
Planning page of the City website.

Council Member Gerber was happy to see additional transit lines coming back. Mayor
Beerman stated Deer Valley requested an express line to the resorts and asked if they
were happy with the proposed lines. Sarah Pearce, Deputy City Manager, stated Kim
Fjeldsted, Transit Manager, could answer that in the Communications section of the
meeting. Miller noted that Deer Valley was part of the stakeholder group that was
included in the analysis process.

declarations were issued to hold electronic meetings during the pandemic. This code
amendment would eliminate the need for Council and all City public bodies to meet
without the requirement of having an anchor location. She noted the Open and Public
Meetings Act was amended by the State Legislature as well. This amendment stated
Council would not have to be present at meetings, but staff would have to be at the
anchor location.

Council meeting in person. Council Member Gerber asked for clarification on when an
electronic meeting could be held. Plane stated an electronic meeting could be
scheduled, but it had to be noticed and staff would have to be at the anchor location,
unless there was a health/safety risk. Council Member Worel asked how other boards
would authorize electronic meetings. Plane indicated boards could put that provision in
their bylaws.

Willow Ranch Subdivision

The WRS is a unique development in Park City and environs. At the time it came
up for annexation and approval in 1992, the Developer, the P lanning Commission
and City Council all agreed that these sensitive, beautiful lands replete with
wildlife and open space needed protections substantially greater than the Estate
District protections under the LMC.

The annexation and approval of Willow Ranch was quite a contentious issue at the
time it was proposed. The Developer resides in the Park City area and can confirm
the events of the time. The original, approximately seventy acre parcel was wholly
undeveloped, extremely wet (characterized as swampy on an early map and
designated as wetlands subject to Army Corps of Engineers j urisdiction),
encompassed a historic railroad grade and straddled SR 224, and, importantly, was

                                                                                    2
situated adjacent to several existing subdivisions - Ridgeview, McCloud Creek,
and the Park Meadows subdivisions -- all with common property boundaries. It
also lay outs ide the city limits of Park City. It was abundantly clear then (and now)
that any development would impact each of these neighbors. At the time, there was
a fair amount of opposition from many quarters to any kind of traditional single
family residential development whatsoever on the site.

The City and the Developer recognized that there were critical issues to be
resolved before the subdivision could be approved. Some of the land would have
to be withdrawn from potential development and thus a conservation easement was
agreed to. WRS was apparently the first subdivision in Park City to impose/accept
a conservation easement on the property. As a result, the City took over
stewardship of a significant portion of Willow Ranch. In further negotiations, the
City conditioned residential development approval upon maintaining as much of
the property as possible as open space in its "Natural" condition. The City and the
Developer also recognized that additional protections were required to be
memorialized with binding effect in some fashion beyond the Estate zoning
prov1s10ns.

In lieu of a development agreement or the creation of a new "Super-Estate
District", these much-needed restrictions were incorporated into and noted on the
Plat and further defined in the WRS CC&Rs (CC&Rs). They include substantially
increased setback restrictions and maximum square footage limitations which
supersede and moot the Estate District restrictions and exceptions which would
otherwise permit additional Improvements and a variety of types of land uses. The
threat of this occurring is precisely what City and Developer wanted to eliminate.
Further development was strictly prohibited in certain designated protected
"Natural Areas" as defined by specific setbacks. The limitations noted in the Plat
incorporate the restrictions in the contemporaneously negotiated CC&Rs and are
difficult to be understood without reference to them. The Plat and the CC&Rs, read
together, make it crystal clear that further development beyond the Plat restrictions
requires a Plat amendment, with active Council participation and approval.

Based on my review of the 1992/3 negotiation documents and minutes of public
hearings, the CC&Rs serve, in my judgment, as part of a quasi-development
agreement between the City, Developer/Declarant and current Lot owners. The
Developer/Declarant and the City essentially incorporated the CC&Rs into the
Plat. This is because the CC&Rs define certain elements of the Plat, which the City
and other agencies (wildlife, water and Army Corps) had significant input into.
Yes, the City had direct input to the CC&Rs. They provide definitions and under

                                                                                     3
Article 3.3 specifically require, accept and obligate the City (acting through the
Council) to participate on certain critical matters and constraints as noted on the
Plat. The original Plat Notes, subsequent amendments and Conditions of Approval
of amendments refer in several instances to the CC&Rs for definition and required
compliance. Accordingly, the combined CC&Rs and Plat are more than just a
private agreement among homeowners.

Without knowledge of this Park City history and/or participation in previous
Willow Ranch Plat amendments, it is difficult to appreciate the Plat restriction
setbacks and limits of disturbance without looking at the inter-related provisions of
the CC&Rs and Plat.

All of the foregoing becomes very relevant when analyzing the process City
Planning and Building appears to have followed and be following for both the
improper building permit granted on August 9 th for the indoor sauna building on
Lot 4 and the proposed pickleball court on Lot 3. The specifics of the sauna permit
are addressed first.

The Sauna Permit

In a follow up meeting, on October 81\ I met with the Planning Director, as well as
the Building Director and Resident Advocate at City Hall to discuss these Willow
Ranch issues. Both City Manager and City Attorney were noticed but did not
attend or respond. While the meeting was congenial, my overall sense of the
meeting is that the new Planning Director (and staff) misreads the implications of
the Willow Ranch subdivision approval process and the Plat and thus the City
erred in granting the sauna permit and, if not corrected, will err again in respect of
the pickleball application.

Among the matters we discussed and the questions giving rise to my concerns
about the sauna process are:

   1. Staff erroneously applied the LMC Estate District setback exceptions (15-
      2.10-3 E 6 and F 9) to the sauna approval process. I believe this was
      incorrect for reasons stated above and because the Planning Department has
      no authority to disregard the legally binding setbacks adopted by the Plat.
      This misguided notion usurps the responsibility and authority of City
      Council.



                                                                                     4
2. In my view, the setback exceptions for an Accessory Building are simply not
   available on Lot 4 or anywhere in WRS, because the stricter setback
   restrictions on the Plat supersede the Estate District requirements and
   exceptions. If the owner wanted an exception, he should have sought (and
   the City should have told him to seek) a Plat Amendment for any
   modifications to the Plat or Notes, including Note #1 Setbacks (Limits of
   Disturbance Area). Simple research of City/County records indicates that
   plat amendments have always been required for changes in Willow Ranch.
   See Ordinances: 95-44, 97-40, 99-25 and 2021-20.

3. Of particular relevance, the owner of Lot 2 in the WRS recently sought a
   Plat Amendment (Ordinance 2021-20) even though the changes sought
   involved relatively few, if any, real impacts, only requiring simple Note
   changes to the Plat to achieve a flexible allocation between home and barn
   square footage---with no increase to total square footage and without
   expanding beyond the Limits of Disturbance Area and encroaching into the
   "Natural Area." Yet, this issue was scrutinized by Planning Commission
   with a Plat Amendment ultimately presented to and granted by Council. The
   Staff Reports and audio recordings of the proceedings are telling as to the
   concerns and level of detail of discussions for this "simple" matter. The
   Conditions of Approval for this application reference and require
   compliance with the CC&Rs. Why the sauna application, with significant
   potential impacts and modifications required on the Plat, was not forwarded
   and subjected to the same scrutiny by Planning Commission and Council, is
   befuddling to me.

4. The Plat Note # 9 sets forth Maximum Building square footage. Neither the
   Planning Director nor Building Director has been able to explain to me why
   staff accepted the sauna Applicant's apparently untrue claim that his
   building square footage is less than the maximum allowed 4 ,400 sq. ft.
   Public records show that the existing residence already exceeds the
   maximum square footage by at least 102 square feet (not including excess
   garage square footage) and the sauna plan pushes the excess to
   approximately 775 sq. ft., in violation of the Plat. The public information
   relating to existing square footage was and is readily available to City
   staffers. I assume the original building plans were approved by the City and,
   as I understand it, actually provided by the City to the County for tax
   purposes. The Director could not clearly explain to me how the sauna
   application, which substantially exceeds the maximum square footage limits
   could have been approved. There are three serious issues to be questioned

                                                                               5
   here: 1) the applicant's apparent misleading claim to City authorities; 2) the
   HOA's absence of oversight and disclosure to the City; and 3) the planning
   technician's acceptance of the applicant's claim without diligent verification.
   This is notwithstanding the fact that the issue and debate regarding the size
   of the buildings becomes moot when it is recognized that without plat
   amendment, only one building/structure of any type is allowed on Lots 3, 4,
   and 5 .... the home. No such amendment has been sought or given.

5. I have recently spoken to the WRS Developer/Declarant, as well as to
   several fonner planning commissioners and two Council members in
   1992/3, who all say that the purpose of the CC&Rs and Plat restrictions
   when imposed was in effect to supersede Estate District exception
   provisions. There are additional 1993 Council and Planning Commission
   members that reside in the Park City area who I believe will also support this
   perspective. The Building Director disagrees, claiming that the restrictions
   imposed by the CC&Rs and incorporated into the Plat in 1993 are not
   binding on the City because they are private matters. This disagreement
   needs to be resolved. In addition to the reasoning described above, my
   position is the Plat was approved by Ordinance and the City Council is
   obligated to review any proposed modifications.

6. The Building Director has told me that one reason the CC&Rs are not
   relevant to consideration is because they were not registered with the City or
   locatable in City offices. I respectfully suggest that he is wrong. The
   documentation regarding the negotiations of approval of WRS and minutes
   of public hearings held by Planning Commission and Council is readily
   available. The CC&Rs were dutifully recorded as suggested/required by the
   1993 LMC 15.4.1 (b) - Self-Imposed Restrictions. The 2021 version of the
   provision is LMC 15-7.3-1. It would seem that the Director(s) should
   recognize the need to, and would know how to, research and obtain these
   documents on-line from the County Recorder's Office, if at all interested.

7. Based on my analysis of the GRAMA responses (although to date
   incomplete), it appears that the Building Director executed the sauna Permit
   based upon a "Planning Technician's" (incomplete) review and
   recommendation with little or no oversight. I cannot locate on the Permit or
   Plan Review Report any acknowledgement or sign-off from a senior planner,
   the Planning Director, or the Assistant Director.



                                                                                 6
The Pickleball Application

While I am concerned that Planning may have wrongly decided the sauna permit
for the reasons mentioned, I am even more concerned that Planning may apply
elements of the same illogical, inconsistent and illegal permitting process to its
future evaluation of the pickleball court and other Willow Ranch issues.

Quite simply, the pickleball court application on Lot 3 simply cannot and should
not be approved.

As discussed extensively in the slide presentation, if the pickleball application is
approved, it will violate the setbacks required under the Code and the Plat. If the
court is built it will inevitably be a noise nuisance which simply cannot be abated.
A recognized acoustic expert has told us that courts in such close proximity to
neighbors would require a literal enclosure, a building, to abate the noise to
acceptable levels. The court, if permitted to proceed, would both severely impact
the quiet enjoyment of our homes and also devalue homes collectively in the
$millions.

In mid-August Planning did deny the Owner of Lot 3 a permit to build a pickleball
court as an Allowed Use and told the Owner that if he wishes to proceed he will
have to apply for a CUP. I agree that a pickleball court cannot be approved as an
Allowed Use. However, I am concerned that Planning apparently did not advise
him that a permit to build a pickleball court inside the Plat imposed setbacks
("Natural Area") on Lot 3 (as Owner intends) would absolutely require a variance
or plat amendment. It would seem that an applicant would want to know the
complete process and what was required before spending the funds necessary for
just a CUP approval. The City apparently did not tell him. The planning and
building departments are doing a dis-service to the applicant by not providing full
disclosure of the required process. I believe a plat amendment or variance process
is unavoidable. In saying this, however, in no way do I believe a plat amendment
or variance should ultimately be granted. There is no basis here, no hardship
whatsoever, which could possibly justify a plat amendment or variance. I am
happy to discuss with any interested person the numerous reasons why no
conceivable hardship exists or can be found, and why a plat amendment or
variance is, in this situation, practically impossible.

While we wait to find out whether the Owner will file an application for a CUP,
we, the severely impacted neighbors, must also live through the unsightly and

                                                                                       7
incomplete work which the Owner, (who undoubtedly should have known better
by common sense and who was provided constructive notice of the LMC
requirements) began last June without a permit for, according to the Building
Director, up to 180 days -- in order to give the Owner the opportunity to
"perhaps" file an acceptable application to determine if he can proceed with the
court. The Building Director insists to me that the 180-day grace period extended
to the Owner/applicant is required by law and absolute. I can find no such
requirement and wonder if the director is not erroneously confusing this
requirement with the requirement that an applicant be given 180 days to commence
construction AFTER a permit is issued. In any event the director has yet to
provide me with any document or code reference substantiating his claim.

Meanwhile, it is me and my neighbors who are being damaged now. What if I were
considering or were forced to sell my home in the near term? According to real
estate agents (of which I am one), I would be relegated to accept perhaps 20% or
more in reduced selling price solely because of the violations, indecision of the
Owner, and buyer uncertainty of the possibilities. What if I needed an appraisal for
refinancing? This is not fair. I believe the Owner should be informed of the
complete process (CUP and Plat Amendment), be required to immediately (10
days) apply for such, or promptly restore his property and ... he should be penalized
as the law provides for commencing construction without a permit.

There is one additional concern that relates to these and any future development
proposals submitted by Willow Ranch owners. The Owner/Applicant of both the
Lot 4 sauna and the Lot 3 pickleball proposals, as well as all other homeowners,
knew, or at least had constructive notice of, the effect of the restrictions illustrated
on the Plat and more fully detailed in their own CC&Rs. Owners, presumably,
have reviewed and voted on, and have experienced the process of, previous Plat
Amendments submissions. Aren't HOA presidents obligated to know the process
and procedures applicable to additional development and diligently and accurately
assist the members with proposals? What were his suggestions and/or directives?

According to the Willow Ranch HOA president, the owners voted unanimously to
approve both the sauna and the pickleball court, implying that they believed the
project was in full compliance with CC&RS and Plat. Yet, the plans presented to
City staff for both permits omitted the operative setbacks as well as conflicting and
unverified square footage claims. No evidence of any HOA analysis, site visit, vote
or approval was provided to the City staff or has been provided to us through a
GRAMA request. The HOA's participation or absence thereof and the submissions
made by the applicants is very concerning to me and very suspicious at best.

                                                                                           8
Apparently, everyone in the WRS, in unanimous consent, simply ignored the
binding Plat restrictions and supposedly approved the projects. Any future
submissions from members of this HOA should be thoroughly vetted with all
pertinent facts substantiated with audits and surveys

City Council Review

We have been waiting for the Planning Director to notify us of a meeting she
intended to arrange with her and the legal department. However, I believe that
these and other issues relating to recent development activity in WRS clearly
justify an immediate call-up and further review by Council. These issues are under
the direct purview of the Council, not planning staff or department heads who, by
approving the sauna, are also ignoring the purpose and requirements of the Plat
restrictions approved by the City Council in 1993. The current Council may not
have a choice whether to call-up and participate. Abdicating or allowing any
improper decisions to stand regarding modifications to the Code or Plat restrictions
may be contrary to law. I assume Council has or will consult with its legal
department.

Conclusion

The Planning and Building Department personnel may be outstanding on other
matters and issues. But, in this case, I believe they have fallen short. I assure you
that I understand that the shoes of the previous Planning Director and people that
were in his department are difficult to fill, if not impossible. I do acknowledge and
appreciate that the Planning Department is operating understaffed and current staff
may be newer to town and more recently employed. Staff may not be aware of the
historical facts of 30 years ago. (Although a proper Willow Ranch amendment
process did take place last April.) Any criticisms in this matter are not meant to be
personal. ..any face behind the job and responsibility would be questioned and
addressed in the same manner. And, I know that the City Council and all of the
various departments are trying really hard to do their best given all the
circumstances, including the Covid impacts.

My efforts are not intended to criticize but, hopefully, to constructively inform the
City and help improve the City planning operation. Citizens should not be
subjected to spending significant time and very significant dollars arguing with
City staff that, in my, and many others' informed opinion, have not properly
administered the permitting process allowing for the proper Planning Commission
and Council review and enforcement of the Code and Willow Ranch Plat.
                                                                                        9
These issues are time-sensitive for numerous reasons and we have been attempting
to discuss the matter with the City for months, with little response until very
recently. I hope this effort can result in reaching some important decisions in these
important matters very soon.

If you believe I am incorrect on any point, please notify me. Otherwise, as a
previous or current participant in Willow Ranch issues your input would be
important and welcome. We are available to discuss these issues w ith anyone,
including City Council at the next meeting on October 28 th .

It is important that not only applicants but neighbors, too, as well as the
community at large can rely upon a logical, coherent, consistent, diligent and fair
approval process which takes into account all of the relevant facts and legal
restrictions.

We await a response from the City.

only to "hardship", with unanimous consent by the HOA Committee and City
approval. The City Council is the only entity authorized to do so!

An application to build a pickleball court on the already excavated site on Lot 3
in the Willow Ranch Subdivision cannot be approved under the Land
Management Code (Code) as an Allowed Use or a Conditional Use.
• Development of Improvements in the Estate District is permitted for certain
  enumerated Allowed Uses, including Accessory Uses. Section 15-2.10-2.A.
    and 15-2.10-2.A.11.

• There is no Allowed Use which covers a pickleball court in the Estate District.
    Section 15-2.10-2.A.

• Accessory Uses are permitted Allowed Uses in the Estate District, but a
  pickleball court is not an Accessory Use. Section 15-2.10-2.A.11.




~    e--   ~                   ..... .--.,....~       ~                  ~   ~      ~
• A pickleball court is a Private Recreation Facility which is a Conditional
  Use in the Estate District. Section 15-2.10-2.B.19.

• Under the Code, the City shall not issue a Conditional Use permit if the
  application violates any requirement of the Code. Section 15-1-10 .D .1.

• The excavated pickleball site violates the 30 foot required side and rear
  setbacks in the Estate District. Section 15-2.10-3 .C., E., and F.

• There is no exception to the 30 foot required side and rear setbacks for a
  Private Recreation Facility (including a pickleball court) in the Estate District.
  Section 15-2.10-3. E., and F.
• The excavated pickleball site violates the self-imposed, City-approved, 40-
  foot required side and rear setbacks in the Willow Ranch Subdivision Plat and
  the Willow Ranch CC&Rs.

• A Use which is not an Allowed Use or a permitted Conditional Use is a
  Prohibited Use. Section 15-2.10-2.C.

• Therefore, the application cannot be approved.

Decibel levels exceeding limits imposed by local noise ordinances are regularly found in
con.n ection with pickleball play within 350 feet of homes. Noise abatement and mitigation
measures should be adopted by developers and planning authorities for all courts located
within this distance
Courts located within 75 feet of homes have been measured at decibel levels exceeding 80
dBA, which far exceed local noise ordinances, representing a high level of noise impact for the
occupants of receiving properties. At this distance, courts will have a significant impact on
quality of life for adjacent residents and will regularly produce noise complaints to facility
operators and public officials. Experts have found t hat paddle impacts at this distance easily
penetrate the interior of~ home and prevent the quiet enjoyment of the residents' use of
their living rooms, dining rooms, bedrooms, balconies, decks, patios, and other spaces used
for dining, work, rest, sleep and other family activities requiring concentration, such as
conversation and reading.
Accordingly, experts conclude that some potential backyard sites may simply be deemed so
close to residences that noise concerns are impractical or impossible to abate or mitigate
and construction at these sites should simply be prohibited.
