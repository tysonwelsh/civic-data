Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area

               Frank Nakamura, District Administrator, Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement
Service Area (SLVLESA), stated SLVLESA would like the support and input from the Kearns
Metro Township Council as it works to modify the existing legislation relating to the property tax
cap. SLVLESA is not asking for a property tax increase for this year, it would just like the ability
to do so when it is needed. The existing cap was established 14 years ago. SLVLESA is now up
against that cap. Property tax revenue is the only sort of revenue for law enforcement services
in the townships.

               Council Member Snow asked what the increased cap would be.

              Mr. Nakamura stated an independent consultant was hired to run the calculations
based on future merit increases, centrally assessed valuations, the number of members that have
left SLVLESA, and the assumptions used to establish the original cap. As soon as the calculations
are done, he would present that number to the Council.

                Captain Levi Hughes, Unified Police Department, stated the increased cap would
allow SLVLESA to decide for itself if it wanted to increase taxes for police services. It would give
them the ability to do so without having to wait; the increased cap would already be in place.

COVID-19 Update

              Mayor Kelly Bush updated the Council on the following:

    The number of cases has held steady, staying below double digits. There have been nine
     deaths in the Kearns area related to the virus.

    The use of CARES Act Funding deadline has been automatically extended by the County.
     However, on the contract between Kearns and Salt Lake County, Kearns Metro Township
     Council needs to fill in a date as to when the unused CARES Act funds will be returned.

              Council Member Snow suggested December 10, 2020. All Council Members
were in agreeance.

    Thermometers

              Mayor Kelly Bush stated the Council needed to decide on its next purchase using
CARES Act Funds. She suggested the Council purchase thermometers to be distributed
throughout the community.

              Council Member Snow asked if the thermometers were ordered tomorrow, if they
would be here by the 26th.

              Mayor Bush stated the thermometers can be delivered within seven days.

              All Council Members agreed with the proposed purchased of thermometers.

                Council Member Schaeffer asked if they would have a slogan on them, if so what
the price difference would be.

              Mayor Bush stated there was a price difference of $1,000 between those with the
slogan and those without.

              Council Member Snow asked if the slogan would be “We are Kearns” or “Kearns
Strong”. She suggested the Council go with “We are Kearns.”

              All Council Members agreed.

    Return of CARES Act Funds

               Mayor Kelly Bush stated the Council has spent close to $100,000 of CARES Act
Funds and did not think it would be able to spend all of it. She suggested that some of the funds
be returned to the County early, so the funds can be used by other entities. The funds would be
earmarked for Kearns, so if needed the Council could get some of the funds back.

Utah League of Cities and Towns Letter

                Mayor Kelly Bush referred to a letter written by Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel,
Kearns Metro Township Council, in which he is requesting the League of Cities and Towns (the
League) to support the development of legislation that would repeal or amend Utah Code to allow
metros to impose franchise taxes. Currently only cities can impose this tax and since the
townships are independent of Salt Lake County and act as cities for their respective areas, it is
only right that they should have the ability to impose this tax. The League is hoping the larger
townships (Kearns and Magna) would become cities and is pushing back on this legislation. She
did not feel that Kearns was ready to become a city at this time. The letter also asked the League
to allow the townships to become members. This would require an amendment to the League’s
constitution.

               Council Member Snow stated she agreed that Kearns was not ready to become
a city.

               Mayor Bush stated if the League does not support this legislation, then the
townships will not get it passed. This could cost the Kearns Metro Township millions of dollars.
The township has the same responsibilities as a city but is unable to impose this tax.

                Council Member Snow, seconded by Council Member Butterfield, moved to
support the letter and to move forward with this. The motion passed unanimously.
