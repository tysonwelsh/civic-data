ZONE TEXT CHANGE APPLICATION

Application #:        ZT-9-2024
Applicant:            Wasatch Commercial Developers, LLC
Request:              A zone text change to Sections 7-1-103 and 7-6-301 of the zoning ordinance to
                      add a definition for the term “data center” and to allow data centers as a permitted
                      use in the LI (Light Industrial) and M (Manufacturing) zones and as a permitted
                      or conditional use in the BRP (Business/Research Park) zone.

Craig Martin with Wasatch Commercial Developers, LLC is proposing an ordinance amendment to
change Sections 7-1-103 and 7-6-301 to add a definition for the term “data center” and to allow data
centers as a permitted use in the LI (Light Industrial) and M (Manufacturing) zones and as a permitted or
conditional use in the BRP (Business/Research Park) zone. Since data centers aren’t currently addressed
in the City’s zoning ordinance, staff has been considering them as warehouses. No more than 60 percent
of the square footage of buildings on a BRP zoned lot can be used as a warehouse or light industrial.

The applicant, Wasatch Commercial Developers, is looking to build a data center in Lake Park
Corporate Center, which is zoned BRP. According to the applicant, the 60% restriction that staff
currently applies to data centers makes the investment in a data center non-viable.
                                                                                              Page 1 of 4
The applicant’s proposed data center definition for Section 7-1-103 is: “An establishment engaging in
the storage, management, processing, and/or transmission of digital data, and housing computer and/or
network equipment, systems, servers, appliances and other associated components related to digital data
operations.”

As pointed out earlier, the applicant is also proposing that data centers would be a permitted use in the
LI and M zones and a permitted or conditional use in the BRP zone. For the BRP zone, staff would
recommend that data centers be a conditional use as they tend to have backup generators that may need
screening.

The applicant’s justification for the proposed changes is as follows:

       “Data centers are not a ‘typical warehouse’ type. In order to make them financially successful,
       the majority of the square footage use needs to be dedicated to the core business. Limiting to the
       60% rule would not allow us to attract users, nor allow Wasatch to make an investment.

       Data Centers are a growing requirement across multiple business sectors worldwide. Lake Park
       is a very attractive location for this type of business, especially with Lake Park being in a
       Qualified Opportunity Zone.

       Wasatch would work closely with WVC to assure that this Data Center would be aesthetically
       pleasing, not looking like a typical ‘industrial’ Building. Like WVC, Wasatch does not want our
       development to look like, or to be confused for a distribution / heavy industrial development.

       Another thing to note is that Data Centers on the whole are very low impact to the surrounding
       area. There are very few employees (5-10), thus very low traffic and impact to the local area, as
       well to WVC services (Fire, Police, Medical, etc.).”

The Institute of Transportation Engineers’ (ITE) trip generation data shows that data centers generate
0.99 trips per 1,000 square feet of building. By comparison, warehouses generate 1.71 trips per 1,000
square feet and general light industrial generates 4.87 trips per 1,000 square feet.

The 60% restriction on warehouse and light industrial space in the BRP zone was put in place in 2022 at
the same time the City rezoned property within Lake Park Corporate Center from M to BRP. The stated
reasons for this restriction were:

      The two main areas of the City with BRP zoning – Lake Park Corporate Centre and Metro
       Business Park – are predominantly office uses. The proposed use restrictions will help prevent
       these areas from becoming predominantly industrial.

      Given the large amount of property zoned to allow industrial uses, there are plenty of
       opportunities for exclusively industrial use. Adding more use restrictions to the BRP zone will
       further differentiate it from other zones.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. The ordinance change should be approved with data centers being allowed as a

   2. Continuance. This application should be continued for reasons determined during the public

Discussion: Steve Pastorik presented.

Craig Martin, representing Wasatch, located at 299 S. Main St., Salt Lake City, was present. He added
that Wasatch owns a significant amount of property in the Lake Park area, one of which is just to the
south of the lake. That parcel of the land is slated to be a Research and Development Park with 5
buildings roughly 88,000 to 95,000 square feet per building, one of which they are looking to build a
data center in because businesses that are attracted to that area have a significant need for cloud storage
or data storage. Some of those entities have facilities that are older, traditional type data centers that
don't have the processing power and speed that they need. A lot of the entities that they are looking at
putting into this area are medical in nature and when the companies are medicine and bioscience based
there needs to be a lot of processing power to help determine cures the companies develop for various
diseases. The issue that Wasatch had with the 60/40 warehouse is that when you lease this amount of
space in a building of that size, 40% of the building doesn't generate any revenue. So, trying to build a
building of that size and only be able to generate revenue from 60% of it just doesn't make financial
business sense. That's the main reason why they are trying to make that facility upwards of 90 to 95%
data center. It's very minimal impact to the to the city and will generate a significant amount of property
tax in terms of the amount the municipality can generate versus the county because of the value that will
be placed on that building.

Craig goes on to say that Wasatch has been proven in the past to be a very conservationist type entity.
They utilize a lot of solar and want to conserve water. There are new technologies with data centers that
use very high-pressure, high-targeted air to help cool the servers in terms of using less water. There's
also immersive cooling where servers are placed in a waterproof box and submerged in water. Ambient
air and cooling have different properties in terms of holding on to heat and level of chill. There is waste
heat that needs to be removed, so there will be a certain amount of water that will have to be involved,
but a lot less as opposed to traditional data centers.

Craig feels Wasatch being able to generate more revenue over the course of the entire building is much
more attractive to them in terms of bringing in high-end clients and lessees into Lake Park.

Steve Pastorik asked Craig if Wasatch was planning on subdividing with each building be its own
parcel. Craig answered that they talked about subdividing them and plat them individually or having the
entire development be its own parcel, but they haven't decided yet. They are leaning toward the former
instead of the latter and would take any advice or any direction from Steve on how he’d like to see that
work out.

Motion: Commissioner Wood motioned to approve ZT-9-2024. Commissioner Porter seconded.

Vote: Motion passed unanimously.
