                                      MEMORANDUM

TO:           The Sandy City Council
FROM:         Zach Robinson
DATE:         August 2, 2024
RE:           Short-Term Rentals



BACKGROUND

       For the past several months, I have been working to improve the ordinance concerning
Short-Term Rentals (“STRs”). After presenting my initial ideas to the Council, I consulted with
James and his team to discuss the proposal. Based on the feedback from you and the
professional staff, I have made several revisions. The updated and streamlined proposal aims to
reduce disturbances, ensure more compliance with established laws, and improve protections for
community members, thereby allowing them to enjoy their neighborhoods more peacefully.
Additionally, the proposal seeks to bolster the safety of guests residing in STRs. Following the
second reading of these proposed amendments, I anticipate that James and his team as well as
Darien from the City Attorney’s Office will review the revisions, make further improvements,
and seek additional feedback from the Planning Commission who will provide a
recommendation back to the Council.

PROPOSED AMENDMENTS TO THE CURRENT CODE

         To enhance the regulatory framework for STRs, several key updates are
proposed. Applicants will be required to complete class or video before applying, ensuring they
are informed of City policies and standards. Safety improvements include mandatory checks for
smoke and carbon monoxide detectors during STR inspections, with functional detectors
required in all sleeping areas and on each floor. Noticing and posting requirements will be
refined to improve clarity and effectiveness, including better communication of winter parking
restrictions and other key issues. Compliance certification will be mandatory, requiring owners
to sign a certification with their STR application, affirming adherence to all ordinance terms.
Additionally, STR listings will be required to display the City-issued license number to ensure
transparency and compliance. Noise and nuisance Control measures will mandate that STR
owners enforce adherence to local noise regulations and health department standards. Tax
compliance will be reinforced by requiring proof of a registered Utah Tax Commission account
and fulfillment of all relevant tax obligations. Finally, administrative enforcement will be
strengthened with the appointment of an administrative law judge who will handle STR
violations, including fine, revoking licenses, enforcing compliance, and reviewing appeals.

CONCLUSION

        In recent years, the revenue generated from STRs has shown an increase, with amounts
totaling $163,000 and $207,000 for the last two years respectively. From my perspective, a
designated portion of these funds should be allocated toward educational and enforcement
