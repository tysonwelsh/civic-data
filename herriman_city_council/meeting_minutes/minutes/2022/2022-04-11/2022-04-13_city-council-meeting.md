    2.2. Discussion of amendments to City Code proposed by Herriman Traffic Committee relating
          to Title 10: Visual Obstructions - Bryce Terry, Assistant City Engineer
Assistant City Engineer Terry explained the visual obstruction ordinance, and said the current ordinance banned
the placement of anything over three feet in certain designated intersections as to not obstruct the view of the
road. He elaborated mostly, it was intended to not allow for property owners to have landscaping or fences
which would inhibit a driver from seeing down the road when stopped at an intersection. He read the wording
of the current ordinance, and explained some of the language was unclear in the description of the “clear view
triangle.” He said in general application, the park strip was considered in the clear view triangle, since trees or
parked cars along the park strip could easily block the view of the road for drivers. He said the anticipated
proposal would clarify the vague language, and change the distance from 30-feet to 60-feet in the code. He
looked to the Council for feedback. Councilmember Shields asked if the tree canopy had to be higher than a
certain height as so not to obstruct the view, and Assistant City Engineer Terry replied tree trunks could not be
in the clear view triangle, but the canopy could reach over into the triangle, as long as it reached a height which
would not block the view. Councilmember Shields asked if that height was defined, and Assistant City Engineer
Terry said it had to be higher than at least seven feet. Assistant City Engineer Terry added all roads were
different, and not every intersection was a perfect grid. He explained there was a formula which could be used
in situations where the intersection was located on a curve, although developers often did not take this into
account when designing intersections. He noted this could be reviewed this complaints were received, but the
biggest issue was with new development applications, and developers could modify their plans in order to be in
line with the ordinance.

    2.3. Discussion of amendments to City Code proposed by Herriman Traffic Committee relating
         to Title 6: Obstructing Traffic - Bryce Terry, Assistant City Engineer
Assistant City Engineer Terry explained this proposal was similar to the item just presented, but this dealt
specifically with enforcement. He said the intent of the ordinance was to discourage the obstruction of traffic,
and was not limited to just intersections, but extended to all passing lanes. He said the ordinance did not allow
any car to be parked in a manner which left less than ten feet of space in the road for cars and pedestrians to go
around the car. He shared an example at Miller Crossing, and indicated many minor collector roads had a three
foot shoulder, which left less than ten feet of space when cars were parked, and partially blocked the lane. He
said it was an issue because the shoulders were on both sides, which made the road too narrow for cars to pass.
April 13, 2022 – City Council Minutes                                                           Page 4 of 14
He elaborated intersections complicated the issue even more, and noted Miller Crossing had a lot of
intersections. He explained the need to clarify language in the ordinance in order to make enforcement easier
and more straightforward. He said the issue would be continual, unless it was addressed, or it was decided it
was not an issue. He said staff had determined a “passing lane” would be defined by roadway striping or
markings, unless there were ten feet available. He added minor collector lanes often had bike lanes as well, so
staff had also added that cars should not block the entire width of a bike lane. He shared some concerns from
the residents, and said concerns had been received from residents who only had street parking, and left their
recreational vehicles in the street over the winter.

     7.1. Camp Williams Update - Brigadier General Joseph Green, Lieutenant Colonel Shane Day, and
           Paul Raymond, West Traverse Sentinel Landscape Manager
Brigadier General Joseph Green thanked the Council for the opportunity to speak, and said Herriman had been
a great partner to them in the past. He spoke briefly about the history of their partnership, and said he wanted
to discuss the compatible use buffer program. He offered some context, and explained the National Guard was
an artillery unit whose main training area was at Camp Williams. He said artillery had been a main component
of the Cold War until the 1990s, and the role of artillery had been reduced as national security matters turned to
anti-terrorism efforts, so they had reduced the training which took place at Camp Williams since the early 2000s.
He discussed the role of artillery was once again integral to the security of the country, as they needed to develop
technology which could match the technology of Russia and China. He said going forward, there would be
more research, weapon development, and training which would take place at Camp Williams as the role of
artillery increased.

Lieutenant Colonel Shane Day explained the Utah National Guard at Camp Williams was an arterial training
institute, and noted they trained more artillerymen annually than anywhere else in the country. He said they had
several training exercises each year which focused on key functions in the US Military and brought in thousands
of artillerymen each year from all over the country. He stated they were very busy holding training sessions, and
said the focus was to build up the same technology as their adversaries. He explained he had been tasked with
facilitating the training, and he wanted to make sure they had the time and space at Camp Williams to hold those
trainings. He said they wanted to be good neighbors to those in Herriman as well as all communities around

natural spaces around Camp Williams so they could be recreated upon while also continuing the military mission

said at that time Herriman had joined as a cooperative agreement partner. He noted Herriman was the only city
in the country to be a cooperative agreement partner, and said he enjoyed working with Herriman City officials.

    9.2. Discussion and consideration of an amendment to Title 10 of City Code to allow internal
         accessory dwelling units as a permitted use in R-2-10 and R-2-15 Residential Zones. (City
         File No. Z2022-025) - Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Maloy stated this was a project that they had worked on last year, in response to amendments to
the State Code. He said the State mandated accessory dwelling units be allowed in areas that were primarily
intended for single family residential use, and it had been his recommendation to the Planning Commission that
they introduce that in R-1 zones and agricultural zones. He said after the ordinance had been adopted, they had
received a call from a resident in an R-2 zone, who wanted to change the ordinance. He clarified the request
would allow the units only in single family homes, not attached units. He said the initial request had been to
allow it in an R-2-10 zone, but the Planning Commission had suggested to expand upon the original request
from the applicant. He said this text amendment would allow for accessory dwelling units in the R-2-10 and R-
2-15 zones, and he noted this was in line with the City’s overall plan. He commented the Planning Commission
had recommended approval 6-0, and that the applicant was in the audience that evening and would be happy to
answer any questions.

Councilmember Hodges moved to approve Ordinance No 2022-14 amending Title 10 of the to allow internal accessory dwelling
units as a permitted use in R-2-10 and R-2-15 Residential Zones. Councilmember Ohrn seconded the motion.

    9.3. Discussion and consideration of a proposed amendment to Title 10 of City Code for digital
          sign regulations (File No. Z2020-059) - Michael Maloy, City Planner
Planner Sheldon Howa presented the existing Code, which limited the possibility for business owners to place
digital signs on their property. He said the setback rules for electronic signage was 500 feet, and said this
impacted many business owners in the City. He proposed to reduce the setbacks, particularly towards signs
which were no taller than six feet above district grade, and make the setback 150 feet. He stated this would
allow greater flexibility on dwell and transition times, and recommended the transition be fade-in/fade-out. He
noted there would be exceptions on the Mountain View Corridor, and said the exceptions would be consistent
with advertising corridors. He shared an example of a sign with a 150 foot setback.

Councilmember Ohrn moved to approve Ordinance No 2022-15 amending Title 10 of the Herriman City Code regarding Electronic
Signs. Councilmember Hodges seconded the motion.
