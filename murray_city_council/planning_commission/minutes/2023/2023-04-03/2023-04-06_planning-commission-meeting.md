Rhino Property Management - 97 West Winchester Street -- Project #23-041

Ms. Nixon presented the application for Paul Henderson, requesting to convert the existing
dwelling into an office for a property management business. The subject property is located on
the south side of Winchester Street, on the curve of Winchester Street onto Malstrom Lane. It’s
just under a half-acre site located in the R-N-B zone. It recently went through a zone change
from R-1-8, with the intent that the property owner would come back and convert existing
dwelling into an office use, for property management. The applicant has provided a site plan.
The existing home would be converted to an office. One of the policies in the R-N-B zone is that
homes should be preserved and converted to appropriate uses and should have residential
character. This conversion will meet the criteria.
Planning Commission

This zone is limited to certain hours and lighting requirements. It will serve as a buffer from a
busy arterial street (Winchester Street). This property is surrounded by residential zoning and
uses. The property received variances from the hearing officer last month, in order to meet
parking and access requirements. With the conversion of the home, it has certain constraints.
The first variance is not to install landscaping along the east property line in order to meet the
minimum access requirement per the fire department, which is twenty feet. The second variance
was for the southwest corner, adjacent to the ninety-degree parking. They chose to do a three-
and-half foot strip of landscaping to facilitate a better circulation of traffic. There is a six-foot solid
wall required around all sides.

The office has two floors. The first floor will have a breakroom and a reception area. The second
floor has office areas. The total square footage is just over 3,000 square feet. With the attached
and detached garage, there is about 2,000 square feet of storage. There will be fifteen (15)
parking stalls, based on the total number of square feet. This zone requires a minimum of fifteen
percent total landscaping and the applicant is close to twenty-three percent, exceeding the
requirement. Photos were presented to demonstrate that the structure does have a residential
look.

Thirty-one (31) notices were sent to property owners within 300 feet of the subject property. Staff
has not received any comments prior to this meeting. During the variance process with the
hearing officer, two citizens attended the meeting and shared that they are in favor of this
application.

Based on the analysis and application, the findings for this approval for the conditional use are
as follows:
1. The proposed change of use and conversion of the existing dwelling to office use is
    encouraged in the R-N-B zoning district.
2. With conditions outlined in the staff report, the proposed use and property will comply with
    the standards of the Murray City Land Use Ordinance.
3. The proposed use is in harmony with the goals and objectives of the Murray City General
    Plan in this area.

Ms. Nixon is recommending that a conditional use permit be granted for the requested
conversion, subject to the twelve conditions listed in the staff report.

The applicant, Paul Henderson, stated their address as 497 West Winchester Street, Murray. He
indicated he reviewed and could comply with all the conditions. He had no other information to
share.

Commissioner Hacker asked about the height of the lighting poles and the requirements for
height and light shielding. Mr. Henderson will not be putting poles in. The detached garage is
where lights will be mounted at a height of fifteen (15’) feet high with lights at a 30-degree angle.
Commissioner Hacker was pleased with this, as it will not impact the residents.

Commissioner Milkavich asked about lighting as well. Her concern is having the lights on at
fifteen feet away at 11 o’clock at night and would be disturbing to the neighbors. Mr. Henderson
said the lights on the garage will be motion sensor only. The housing structure will only have
regular residential lights. Commissioner Milkavich was satisfied with this response.

The Ridges at Murray - 5322 South Allendale Drive & 662 West 5300 South - Project #23-027

Ms. Nixon presented the application for Brad Reynolds, requesting final subdivision approval of
The Ridges at Murray Subdivision. The subject property is made up of two parcels located on
the north side of 5300 South and the west side of Allendale Drive. It’s about five-and-a-half
acres in size. The property went through a zone change and general plan amendment about a
Planning Commission
April 6, 2023
Page 6

year ago. The Planning Commission granted conditional use and preliminary subdivision
approval about a month ago. The properties are currently zoned R-M-15, which does allow for
planned unit developments with projects of more than two acres. The project is for 66
townhomes with common space. There will be two accesses off Allendale drive. The PUD does
have private drives that are going to be twenty-six feet of hard surface and twenty feet of right-of-
way. The application does meet the Fire Department approval for the width of the driveways.

Ms. Nixon stated that there are no significant changes from the preliminary review. Per the
planning commission’s request, they did add benches in the landscape area, which also serves
as a detention area. The project has thirty-three percent coverage in landscaping. The R-M-15
zone requires two-and-a-half parking spaces per unit. Each of the units have a two-car garage,
plus parking in front of the garages. There are two exceptions with units 153 through 164. They
will still have two-car garages, but to make up the other half-stall they have visitor parking. The
project exceeds parking requirements for this zone.

The zone allows for a base density of twelve units per acre. This project averages about eleven-
and-a-half. The applicant has proposed that the front setbacks will range from twenty to thirty
feet. The rear setbacks will range from fifteen to twenty-seven feet. The interior side yards will
range from five to thirty-nine. The corner side yards will range from five-and-a-half to twenty-
three feet.

The R-M-15 zone allows up to a height of forty feet. The applicant is proposing a height of up
thirty-four-and-a-half feet (34’6”). Staff feels this is an excellent proposal as it provides for a
good transitionary buffer between the residential and commercial. There is a billboard on the
corner adjacent to 5300 South. That access will have to be maintained. That does require an
agreement between the city and UDOT, and then the city, again, and the developer. The
agreement was drafted by the city attorney and given to the applicant. The applicant will need to
have their legal team review and sign it. There was an update on the CC&R’s regarding
specification for who will maintain emergency access off 5300 South and the paved area.

With this subdivision, Allendale will become much busier. The traffic level of service is poor in
this area. The city engineer said there would be a traffic calming feature installed to assist
(electronic feedback sign). The unit sizes will be between duplexes to six-plexes on the property.
Allendale is a very busy street. The street of 5300 South is very busy. To serve as a buffer, the
applicant will put a wall to serve that purpose and be attractive. It will be an eight-foot solid
masonry wall with wrought iron and jut-
outs.

Based on the information presented in this report, application materials submitted, and a site
review, Staff recommends that the Planning Commission grant Final Subdivision approval for the
Ridges at Murray on the property addressed 5322 South Allendale Drive & 662 West 5300 South
subject to the 11 conditions.

Ms. Nixon did want to point out that Ms. Milkavich stated in the conditions that the fence was
vinyl, but it is actually a combination of wood and vinyl. Ms. Nixon asked that the condition be
changed to reflect that.

Murray Station Townhomes - 184 West Berger Lane - Project #23-040

Mr. Smallwood presented the application for Simon Rucinski to request Design Review approval
to allow the construction of six (6) new townhouses. The subject property is located on the north
side of Berger Lane, east of Commerce Drive. This property is currently a single-family
residence. This property is located in the MCMU East subdistrict the following review is based
upon the requirements of the MCMU East subdistrict. The applicants are allowed up to forty
units per acre. They will be tearing down the existing single-family home and putting in a
driveway with two buildings, containing three units each. They will have the required entrances
in the front. They are including two benches, two sets of bike racks, and landscaping that
matches what’s required in this zone. The setbacks for this property are between fifteen and
twenty-five feet from the back of the existing curb. This is to be used for an eight-foot
landscaping strip and a seven-foot sidewalk. Though the applicant could go up to forty units per
acre, this proposal is at about twenty per acre. They are required to have nine parking stalls –
twelve are provided. In the code, it says that side yard areas need to be used for parking or
open space. Each unit will have its own private yard. Originally the city engineer was concerned
about the lack of a dumpster. Each unit will have their own garbage cans, picked up by a private
service on the street. The containers will be kept inside the garage for each unit. Each unit has
a small area at ground level. The living and dining area is on the second floor. The third floor
has bedrooms and then there is a rooftop patio.

Fifteen (15) notices were mailed to property owners within a 300’ radius and to affected entities.
One email was been received and was included with the staff report, the email expresses that
the six townhomes are not appropriate for the area and the street could not handle the additional

Berger Lane subject to the seven conditions.
