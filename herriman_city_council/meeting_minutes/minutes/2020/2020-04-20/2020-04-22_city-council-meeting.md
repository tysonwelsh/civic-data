    2.1. Water Efficiency Standards Discussion - Justun Edwards, Public Works Director
Public Works Director Justun Edwards stated the growth in the Herriman area and the southwest corner of
the valley had been rapid over the past several years. To address some of those concerns, Jordan Valley Water
Conservancy District (Jordan Valley) had adopted some efficiency standards to help maintain water resources
as the growth continues to move forward.

Assistant General Manager with Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District Matt Olsen said Jordan Valley was
one of the largest water providers in the State of Utah. They service over 700,000 people in the southwest
corner of Salt Lake County. They are primarily a wholesale water provider, but they also service some retail
customers from unincorporated areas of the county. Jordan Valley has been governed by a Board of nine
trustees representing eight geographical divisions. Division Seven, which encompasses Bluffdale, Herriman,
and Riverton, is represented by Sherrie Ohrn. Jordan Valley recognized the importance of having a reliable
and sustainable water supply for decades into the future, and their current planning horizon was about 50 years.
Plans were continually being updated based upon the latest data and research available. Three key services
included delivering water, managing demand, and developing additional water supply. One of the critical
factors that influence water supply and demand is the trend of increasing temperatures in the area. The higher
the temperatures are, the more water is required to irrigate landscaping. Mr. Olsen presented a graph depicting
water use and noted that 60% of water use was outdoors. The water supply and delivery structure must be
designed to handle the peak demand levels, which were always in July.

In 2019, Jordan Valley conducted a study to see if the current water supply portfolio was sufficient to meet the
demands of its existing service boundaries. The study found there was enough water to meet the demands of
the existing service areas, as long as new construction conforms to a series of water efficiency standards. As
the Board adopted these efficiency standards, a concern was raised regarding the potential for large
developments in lands outside of the district boundaries eventually petitioning to join the district. If these
areas have good water conservation measures in place before they annex in, they would be less impactful on
the existing member agencies.

Mr. Olsen then addressed landscaping. While they maintain their appropriate landscape, they want it to be
used in areas where it would be the most functional and successful. They have begun a new movement called
Localscapes, which they believe was a comprehensive landscaping approach providing a moderate shift in style,
but would be a large benefit for homeowners and water providers alike. In Herriman City, there was a series
of water conservation education incentives and regulations that he hoped the residents were taking advantage
of. Mr. Olsen thanked Herriman City staff for providing input regarding the water efficiency standards adopted
by the Board. Based on feedback received, they were able to refine the standards, and the revised standards
would be presented to the Board for adoption in June. He briefly described the revised standards and incentive
programs. At the request of Councilmember Smith, Mr. Olsen described the Localscapes rewards program in
more detail and confirmed the incentive was available to new and old properties alike.

     2.2. Utility Billing Enforcement Discussion during Pandemic - Justun Edwards, Public Works
          Director
Public Works Director Justun Edwards explained some residents and customers may have trouble paying their
utility bills as a result of the current pandemic. For the months of March and April, staff decided not to
perform water shutoffs, which staff could do if Council requested the continuation of the practice. There were
other potential solutions staff had considered, and they wanted feedback from the Council. They could go
back to performing shutoffs with modifications, and/or reduce or waive reconnection fees. Director Edwards
provided information regarding shutoffs and said that they would have had 97 shutoffs in April. There were
just over 11,000 accounts in Herriman City.

Director Edwards briefly described how an account became delinquent and said that payment options were
available to those who contact the City before becoming delinquent.

Councilmember Ohrn said they need to be sure there was proper communication between the City and the
potentially delinquent account holder, and to have a payment plan in place. She was concerned if someone
didn’t make any payments for several months, they would never be able to pay off their bill in the future.
Director Edwards said they could look into adopting a temporary policy that allows for a payment plan after
an account is delinquent; however, currently the Ordinance does not allow for payment plans. They could also
look at temporarily holding off the non-payment program policy.

Finance Director Alan Rae explained that under the current policy, an individual or company has to sign up
for the equal-pay program. Staff monitors the situation monthly to ensure customers remain current, or they
will drop out of the program. If the Council desired to pursue the equal pay program, they could adjust the
terms to allow people into the program that might already be behind in payments.

Councilmember Ohrn commented this was still a cost to the City. The water was still being used and needed
to be paid. She reiterated she didn’t want people to fall so far behind that they couldn’t pay off their bill, and
she stressed the importance of having some sort of payment plan. Councilmember Smith suggested they
consider waiving the 1.5-percent delinquent fee for a few months. It wasn’t a large amount, but it wouldn’t
add to the burden. Mayor Watts was willing to consider a reduced reconnection fee while the emergency
declaration was in place. It was a good precedent to set. Councilmember Shields worried about encouraging
“bad behavior” by not requiring any payments. He was open to options that reduced their obligation, but not
elimination.

Director Edwards said staff would look at waiving the late fee and reducing the reconnection fee. Staff would
bring this back before the Council in May for consideration.

    2.4. Herriman City Events during Pandemic Policy - Anthony Teuscher, Deputy Director of Parks,
         Recreation and Events
Deputy Director of Parks, Recreation and Events Anthony Teuscher said the department was in a state of
limbo, but they were considering upcoming events, and what those would look like if they were to proceed.
The department was currently scheduling virtual events and doing their best to engage the community from
home. They were now seeking the Council’s input regarding the larger City events, including the PRCA Rodeo,
Herriman Towne Days, and the Herriman Enduro Challenge. Staff recommended they make final decisions
on these events six weeks prior to the scheduled date. This would allow staff to contact vendors, contractors,
and other staff to either accommodate the event, modify the event, or cancel.

The Council asked if they could look into postponing the Rodeo to the end of the summer. Assistant City
Manager Wendy Thomas said the likelihood was slim. The Rodeo was contingent upon PRCA approval, and
the only other date that would work was in mid-July. This was not an ideal time for a rodeo because it was hot
and dusty. Deputy Director Teuscher said the stock contractor was willing to move forward with the Rodeo
if the City gives the go-ahead.

Councilmember Smith said it was unlikely that they would be permitted to have mass gatherings for several
months. He didn’t anticipate large gatherings until late August or September. Councilmember Ohrn asked if
they could hold off a little longer before they have to notify the stock contractor. Things were changing almost
weekly. They needed to remember they needed to consider the economy, and people’s livelihoods were at
stake. City Manager Brett Wood said he didn’t want to move ahead of the County Health Department
directives. He was in favor of making decisions six weeks in advance. This would allow them time to advertise
the event if they decide to proceed.

Councilmember Shields said they should remain optimistic until they could not. He didn’t want to cancel
events too early, and he was in favor of pushing back the decision as far as possible. If there were things that
required a non-refundable deposit, they should discontinue those things now. Deputy Director Teuscher said
a decision needed to be made for the Rodeo within the next few days. The Council also needed to make a
decision on the Memorial Day breakfast as soon as possible. Council Member Ohrn expressed concern about
how these cancelled events affected the economy and individuals. Councilmember Henderson also felt they
shouldn’t be premature in cancelling events, and they should be considerate of the organizers. However, it
wouldn’t be fair to the citizens to give false hope in the upcoming summer events.

Assistant City Manager Thomas commented they could reschedule some of these events for later in the year,
but they needed to remember that the events would go into the next fiscal year. The budget from this year
would roll over, but it was still something they should anticipate.

Councilmember Smith said there would still be risk in moving forward with an event. It was possible they
didn’t have enough attendance to recover their costs. Although it was a hard decision to make, he thought
they needed to look into cancelling the rodeo.

    2.6. Review of the Herriman City Public Improvement District (PID) Policy - Alan Rae, Finance
          Director
Finance Director Alan Rae briefly highlighted the process for creating and approving a Public Improvement
District (PID). The City should approve their own PID policy, which could be more restrictive than the State’s
PID policy. The City Council would approve all governing documents for the PID. Director Rae addressed
the following key components of the policy:

      Community development committee serves to review the letter of intention
      Application fee $100 for staff time
      Governing document fee of 1,000 for legal and other consultants
      Costs that exceed application and governing document fee will be paid by the district
      Eligible projects include commercial, retail, consumer services and civic amenities.
      Maximum mill rate is 10 mills (10 per 1,000 value) state statute allows 15
      Public Infrastructure Districts will be required to adhere to City Procurement Policy.

Director Rae noted the City could charge additional funds to cover actual fees incurred.

Mayor Watts requested they include language prohibiting residential development with PID funds, and
Director Rae said they could include those provisions.

Councilmember Shields asked if the State statute would take precedence if the City did not have their own
policy in place, and Director Rae answered affirmatively. Councilmember Shields requested they limit the PID
area to the Auto Mall property, as previously discussed. He wasn’t ready to open this up across the City.

Council Member Ohrn still harbored her original concern of taxation without representation. Once a new
