PC 25-055 YABC LLC Rezone [Zone Change] Jonathan Shill/YABC LLC, authorized agent/owner,

is requesting the zone change of a 2.52-acre property located at 920 North 200 West from Mixed
Residential Low (MR-12) to Commercial (COM); TIN 05-047-0036 (Bridger Neighborhood).

Staff: Aimee Egbert, Logan City Planner, reviewed the request to rezone a 2.52-acre property
located at 920 North 200 West from Mixed Residential Low (MR-12) to Commercial (COM). The
applicant is requesting the rezone to allow installation of an 8-foot perimeter fence and to
accommodate a future expansion of the existing building. Under current MR-12 zoning, the maximum
permitted fence height is 6 feet.

The property has supported various commercial uses since the existing building was constructed in
the 1960s. In 1997, a Conditional Use Permit (CUP) was issued, legally establishing commercial use
within the residential zone. Because the building has continued to operate commercially, the CUP
remains valid.

Rezoning the property to Commercial (COM) would allow a broad range of commercial activities,
including office, retail, and service-oriented uses intended for businesses serving city-wide or
regional populations along high-capacity roads. COM zoning has approximately 45 permitted uses,
including gas stations, pawn shops, drive-throughs, and automobile sales and service—uses that
would be incompatible with the surrounding multi-family residential development.

Staff recommended denial of the request for Commercial zoning and suggested the Commission
consider a rezone to Community Commercial (CC) instead. The CC zone would be more consistent
with surrounding land uses and better reflect the intended character of the subject property. The CC
zone also requires CUPs for many higher-impact uses, giving the Planning Commission greater
discretion to review and condition commercial activities to ensure compatibility with the area.


Commissioner Doutre asked whether the Community Commercial zone allows 8-foot fence height.
A. Egbert said that any commercial zoning permits 8-foot fences.

Commissioner Peterson asked what uses are allowed under the existing CUP. A. Egbert responded
that historically the CUP has allowed a sports and fitness club, medical office, day spa, restaurant,
and daycare.

Proponent: The proponent was not present.
Public: No public comments were made.

Commission: Commissioner Heare stated he recommends denial, noting that Commercial zoning
appears too intense for this property and that the proponent was not present to request Community
Commercial instead of full Commercial zoning.

MOTION: Commissioner Heare made a motion to forward a recommendation of denial to the
Municipal Council for the zone change requested on PC 25-055 with the findings for approval as
listed below. Commissioner Duncan seconded the motion. The motion was approved unanimously.

FINDINGS FOR DENIAL

The Planning Commission bases its decision on the following findings:

1. LDC 17.10.080 states that the COM zone is intended for retail, service, and hospitality uses that
serve citywide or regional populations and located on high-capacity roads.

2. LDC 17.11.030 for the COM zone allows a wide range of high-intensity commercial uses that are
incompatible with adjacent multifamily and institutional uses.

3. The surrounding collector streets are not designed to accommodate the traffic volumes or access
demands associated with higher-intensity commercial uses.

4. The current FLUP calls this property out as Mixed Residential (MR).

PC 25-056 Andor Fields [Design Review] Brice Sadler/Romeo Rentals LLC, authorized
agent/owner, is requesting a design review permit to build three, three-unit, two-story townhome
buildings for a total of 9 new units located at 675 % North 500 West in the Mixed Residential
Traditional (MR-9) Zone; TIN 05-044-0019 (Bridger Neighborhood).

Staff: Aimee Egbert, Logan Citty Planner reviewed the request for a design review permit to construct
three buildings, each containing three, two-story townhome units, for a total of nine residential units
on a 1.01-acre property at 675 % North 500 West in the MR-9 Zone. The project site is accessible
on the east side via a private road owned and maintained by the Palatial Living Mobile Home
Community. The applicant has secured a written access agreement with Palatial Living.

As presented, the project does not meet the required 15-foot front setback, with the east side of the
project considered the front yard as that is where the site is accessed from. The applicant is
proposing to designate the north side as the front, which is allowed; however, the four parking stalls
on the north side would not meet the required 10-foot parking setback and would need to be
relocated.


A. Egbert noted that the existing storage units on the property were originally intended for use by
residents of Palatial Living and, because the site is zoned MR-9, cannot be rented commercially.
Rental signs currently posted at the storage units suggest they are being rented commercially,
placing the site out of compliance with zoning.

Commissioner Lucero asked how the Commission could approve the project when the storage units
are out of compliance.

Mohammed Abdullahi, Logan City Attorney, stated that the Commission may approve the design
review permit with a condition that the building permit will be withheld until the storage sheds are
brought into compliance.

Commissioner Lucero requested additional background on the storage units. A. Egbert explained
that the storage sheds were constructed for Palatial Living Residents. When the mobile home park
was subdivided and sold, zoning did not allow commercial storage, so the storage sheds were
required to remain for the use of the mobile home park residents only.

Proponent: Brice Sadler stated that the plan is to convert the existing storage units into parking
garages for the townhomes. Mr. Sadler also discussed designating the east side as the side yard
and replacing the north parking stalls with garage parking.

Commissioner Heare asked whether the storage sheds need to be part of the design review. R.
Holley, Logan City Planner, clarified that conversion of existing storage sheds to garages does not
require a new design review permit.

Public: Jeannie Simmonds asked whether there is a binding agreement restricting the use of the
storage sheds to palatial living residents only.

Merrill Bird, a nearby resident, asked why some of the existing pavement is not being removed to
allow the townhomes to be better situated on the property.

Commission: Commissioner Peterson asked M. Abdullahi to clarify the needed condition regarding
the storage sheds. M. Abdullahi stated that a condition may be added requiring that the storage
sheds be brought into compliance.

Commissioner Doutre stated that it makes sense for the front of the property to be designated where
the property is accessed. She added that the project should be able to meet setback requirements
given the size of the site.

The commission discussed the front setback on the east side of the property and determined that a
10% reduction in the required setback would be allowed.

PC 25-057 Highway 89 Plaza [Design Review] AWA Engineering/Highway 89 Plaza LLC, authorized
agent/owner, is requesting a design review permit for a new 1,025 square foot Dutch Bros with a
drive-thru located at the corner of 1200 South and Highway 89 in the Commercial (COM) Zone; TIN
02-087-0002 (Woodruff Neighborhood).

Staff: Russ Holley, Logan City Planner reviewed the request for a design review permit to construct
a 1,025 square foot Dutch Bros coffee shop with a drive-thru window at the corner of 1200 South and


Highway 89-91. Access to the building will be from 1200 South via a new shared driveway, which
will also serve future development areas to the north. The proposed building and site improvements
will occupy a 0.88-acre area located at the southwest corner of the overall 2.91-acre parcel.

A wetland delineation submitted in August 2021 determined that no jurisdictional wetlands are
present on the site, as confirmed by the U.S Army Corps of Engineers.

R. Holley noted that while the proposed design complies with most standards in the Land
Development Code (LDC), there are two issues for Planning Commission discussion.

1. Frontage Requirement: The LDC requires a minimum lot frontage of 50%. The project site is
approximately 180 feet wide while the proposed building is 50 feet wide resulting in approximately
28% frontage. The applicant is proposing a three-foot tall stone wall along the south side of the
building to increase perceived frontage mass. If counted toward frontage, the wall would provide
approximately 60% frontage, thereby meeting the LDC frontage standard. The wall would also
serve as a safety feature by blocking drive-thru headlights from shining toward northbound
highway traffic.

2. Fenestration (Transparency) Requirement: The LDC requires 30% transparency on ground floor
street facing facades. The proposed west fagade, which faces Highway 89-91, does not meet the
30% requirement due to interior electrical panels located along that wall. The applicant is
requesting alternative design options to satisfy the intent of the code.

Proponent: Jason Thompson, representing the landowner and Dutch Bros, asked the commission
if they had any questions.

Commissioner Peterson asked whether options exist to meet the fenestration requirement on the
west facade. Mr. Thompson said relocating the electrical panels is not feasible.

Commissioner Lewis suggested adding a faux window using spandrel glass.

Commissioner Doutre recommended adding an awning on the west side similar to the canopy on the
north facade.

Public: No public comments were made.

Commission: The Commission discussed options for requiring additional design features on the
west fagade to meet or satisfy the fenestration requirement.
