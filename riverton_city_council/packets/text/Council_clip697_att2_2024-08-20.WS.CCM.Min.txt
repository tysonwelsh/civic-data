10            Transit Fresh Look Study Presentation
11
12   Ted Knowlton, Deputy Director of Wasatch Front Regional, Carlton Christensen, Chair of Utah
13   Transit Authority, and Lauren Victor, WFRC Transit Fresh Look Project Manager attended the
14   work session to present the “Transit Fresh Look” Study.
15
16   Mr. Knowlton provided an overview of the "Transit Fresh Look" initiative and discussed its focus
17   on regionally significant transit connections, such as bus rapid transit and potential rail
18   improvements. This project is part of a broader effort to address both long-term and short-term
19   transit needs in the region.
20
21   The "Transit Fresh Look" initiative is distinct from other transit planning efforts, as it emphasizes
22   regional connections. However, Mr. Knowlton explained that this is only one aspect of the transit
23   planning process. UTA is also working on a five-year service plan, which includes more
24   immediate improvements to local bus routes, specifically in Riverton, with plans for
25   implementation around 2025. Two specific routes, Route 126 and Route 218, were discussed
26   as being in development. These routes are expected to connect to key areas and provide bi-
27   directional service with the possibility of increasing service frequency based on demand.
28
29   Mr. Knowlton noted that these local transit services are not included in the regional
30   transportation plan but are essential for addressing current transit needs. UTA has made
31   progress in staffing, which allows for the expansion of services that were previously suspended
32   due to shortages. He emphasized that public input is still being sought, with further opportunities
33   for feedback in the coming months before final decisions are made by the local advisory council
34   and UTA board later in the year.

39            Traffic Calming Discussion
40
41   Mayor Staggs explained that in 2018, a resolution was passed prohibiting speed bumps in the
42   City, but there's been a significant influx of requests from residents asking either to bring them
43   back or to explore alternative solutions. One such alternative is the implementation of speed
44   dips, which might not require a change to the existing resolution or ordinance. City staff has
45   been gathering information on the costs associated with these measures, and it has been
46   estimated that installing a speed dip would cost around $5,000, while a properly constructed
47   concrete speed bump would cost about $8,000.
48
49   Given the challenges associated with snow plows and the maintenance of speed bumps, the
50   staff's preference seems to be leaning towards speed dips as a more practical solution. This
51   topic is expected to be further discussed in upcoming meetings, especially as the City continues

 2   generated considerable feedback from residents.

37            Resolution No. 24-79 – Approving Amendments to the Municipal Fee Schedule for
38            the 2024-2025 Fiscal Year
39
40   Nick Geer, Administrative Services Director, discussed the proposed increase in the event
41   security fee to help cover the rising costs of having police officers provide security at events.
42   Additionally, he mentioned that revisions were made to the dog license section, not only to
43   address necessary changes but also to streamline and condense outdated language.
44
45   Councilmember Pierucci asked for clarification on the vaccine fee. In response, Mr. Geer
46   explained that the new vaccine requirement fee was designed to simplify the payment process
47   and cover the costs of vaccinations. Previously, there were multiple incremental fees for
48   different vaccination stages, which have now been consolidated into a single flat fee. This flat
49   fee covers approximately $10 more than the original contract price for vaccinations. The cost to
50   the City varies depending on the type of animal and is based on a contract with a third-party
51   provider. This fee structure aims to account for the range of costs associated with different
     Page | 6 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     August 20, 2024




 1   animals, including both high and low ends. However, it does not cover all administrative costs
 2   related to animal control; it primarily reflects the charges from the contract with the third-party
 3   provider.
