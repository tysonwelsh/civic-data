PUBLIC HEARINGS
A.   ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING THE BUDGET OF WEST
     VALLEY CITY FOR THE FISCAL YEAR 2020-2021
     Mayor Bigelow informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular
     Council Meeting scheduled August 11, 2020, in order for the City Council to hear
     and consider public comments regarding the budget of West Valley City for the Fiscal
     Year 2020-2021.

        Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
        information as follows:

               West Valley City will adopt a final budget that will be made available for
               public inspection during regular office hours in the City’s Recorder’s office
               and gave notice of a hearing to receive public comment, before the final
               adoption of this tentative budget for FY 2020-2021.

        Jim Welch provided the annual budget presentation.

        Mayor Bigelow opened the Public Hearing.

        Matt MacPherson stated that he knows the Police Department makes up the largest
        hunk of the City’s budget. He indicated that it is very important for Cities to have
        a strong Police Force and noted that West Valley City has greatly reformed its’
        department into a successful organization. Mr. MacPherson stated that he is proud
MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING – AUGUST 11, 2020

   of the West Valley City Police Department and stated that the City needs to get
   serious about fighting crime. He indicated that he feels more officers are needed,
   more patrol units should be active at all times, and more officers should be on duty
   for the number of citizens in West Valley City.

   Jim Vesock stated that he agrees completely with the previous comment and stated
   that he does not support defunding the Police Department. He noted that he would
   caution the City against dipping into its “rainy day” funds.

   Cliff Chase stated that he has been a resident of West Valley City for 26 years and
   was a Police Officer with the City for 20 years. He indicated that he is here to voice
   his support for the Police Department and to express his concern and opposition to
   protests and requests calling to defund the Police Department. Mr. Chase expressed
   concern about interviews he has seen from protestors and indicated that many of
   them do not live or work in West Valley City and have alternative agendas and
   political tactics. He indicated that the City needs more funding and more officers
   and stated that the Department is one of the busiest in the State. Mr. Chase discussed
   crime rates in West Valley City as they compare to other communities. He indicated
   that he has seen progress the City has made and noted that he has confidence in the
   City to do what is best for the citizens of the community and leave the Police Budget
   intact.

   Mayor Bigelow closed the Public Hearing.

   ACTION: RESOLUTION NO. 20-123, ADOPT A FINAL BUDGET; MAKE
   APPROPRIATIONS FOR THE SUPPORT OF WEST VALLEY CITY FOR
   THE FISCAL YEAR BEGINNING JULY 1, 2020 AND ENDING JUNE 30,
   2021; AND DETERMINE THE RATE OF TAX AND LEVYING TAXES
   UPON ALL REAL AND PERSONAL PROPERTY WITHIN WEST
   VALLEY CITY, UTAH, TAXABLE BY LAW FOR THE YEAR 2020
   The City Council previously held a public hearing regarding proposed Resolution
   20-123 that would adopt a final budget; make appropriations for the support of West
   Valley City for the Fiscal Year beginning July 1, 2020 and ending June 30, 2021;
   and determine the rate of tax and levying taxes upon all real and personal property
   within West Valley City, Utah, taxable by law for the year 2020.

   Councilmember Fitisemanu stated that members of City staff and the City Council
   have been getting many emails and phone calls regarding the budget and how it
   applies to the Police Department. He stated that the West Valley City Council is
   not increasing the budget for the Police Department and is not looking to militarize
   or increase the number of personnel. He noted that no more new officers are being
   added at this time, even though the population continues to grow in West Valley

   City. Councilmember Fitisemanu stated that the City has many programs that work
   to better the community and often work hand in hand with the Police Department.
   He indicated that there are CDBG grants offered, Opportunity Scholarship
   programs to help first generation college students, Mental Health partnerships and
   court systems, Operation Hometown, etc. He stated that these types of programs
   are not reactionary to recent concerns expressed by the greater population but have
   been City initiated, long lasting, and long acting efforts to establish a healthy, safe
   City. Councilmember Fitisemanu stated that he supports the right of all residents
   to express their opinion and he agrees that he would like to continue increasing
   funding and supporting these types of programs. He thanked all residents who have
   contacted him with concerns or support and those that spoke during tonight’s Public
   Comment. He indicated that he would like to see COP expand, more complimentary
   programs, and a continued improvement in West Valley City.

   Councilmember Buhler thanked Councilmember Fitisemanu for his comments. He
   noted that there are many older programs and some that are new. He stated that
   these programs work to increase and improve a sense of community and stop crime
   before it happens. Councilmember Buhler stated that the City has a well-trained,
   accredited Police Department and he is proud of the work that is done. He added
   that he is thankful there is no tax increase this year despite the economic scare that
   2020 has brought to the Country.

   Councilmember Huynh stated he supports the budget but doesn’t want to raise taxes
   to pay for the judgment levy. He indicated that he feels this is wrong to do in the
   middle of a pandemic.

   Mayor Bigelow stated that the Police Department operates in a constantly changing
   environment. He stated that providing victim and mental health services are now
   important and they may not have been as needed 50 years ago. He indicated that
   Police Officers may look more “military” because they must wear bullet proof vests
   today and they have bigger weapons because criminals do too. Mayor Bigelow
   stated that we place a great responsibility on our Police Officers as a society and
   ask them to deal with people who are violent and who may, on any given day, take
   their lives. He noted that sometimes action may result in loss. Mayor Bigelow stated
   that training grows and evolves as society changes. He indicated that this training
   costs money. He indicated that not everyone will be honest, obey the law, and be a
   good member of society. Mayor Bigelow stated that we cannot judge a Police
   Officer for the action of another just as we cannot judge a protestor for the action
   of another.

   Councilmember Huynh agreed but stated that his concern is raising taxes He
   indicated that he feels the judgment levy should be paid from the general fund.

       Wayne thanked the Council for their support regarding the budget and their support
       of the Police Department. He indicated that there is no tax increase this year, just a
       judgment levy. He indicated that the City is prepared for the long months ahead and
       is ready to take on challenges that may come.

       Mayor Bigelow explained that a judgment levy is simply restoring the tax rate to
       its original level after the County’s automatic built in decrease. He indicated that
       the City will not collect any more money from citizens on taxes with the exception
       of new homes or businesses. Mayor Bigelow stated that this should be seen as an
       adjustment, not a tax increase. Councilmember Huynh stated that he doesn’t feel
       this is a good time. Mayor Bigelow stated that cutting funding would mean cutting
       services. He agreed it is a challenging situation.

       Upon inquiry by Mayor Bigelow there were no further questions from members of
       the City Council, and he called for a motion.

       Councilmember Buhler moved to approve Resolution 20-123.

       Councilmember Christensen seconded the motion.

RESOLUTION 20-124: APPROVE THE AMENDMENT NO. 1 TO THE
AMPHITHEATER EVENT MANAGEMENT AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE
CITY AND PRISTINE ALPINE ENTERTAINMENT
Mayor Bigelow discussed proposed Resolution 20-124 that would approve an Interlocal
Cooperation Agreement between West Valley City and Salt Lake County for acquisition
of the swimming pool at Centennial Park.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:
  MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING – AUGUST 11, 2020

       Under the Amphitheater Event Management Agreement, PAE is required to make
       an annual payment of $75,000 to the City for services performed by the City in
       connection with events at the USANA Amphitheater. Due to the COVID-19
       pandemic and associated closures and cancellations, PAE has been unable to hold
       events at the USANA Amphitheater, resulting in a large decrease in revenue and a
       reduction in PAE’s need for City services. This amendment would cancel PAE’s
       annual $75,000 payment for 2020.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Bigelow there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and he called for a motion.

       Councilmember Christensen moved to approve Resolution 20-124.

       Councilmember Lang seconded the motion.

       Unanimous.

RESOLUTION 20-125: APPROVE THE 2019 SALT LAKE COUNTY
MULTIJURISDICTIONAL HAZARD MITIGATION PLAN
Mayor Bigelow discussed proposed Resolution 20-125 that would approve the 2019 Salt
Lake County Multijurisdictional Hazard Mitigation Plan.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The Hazard Mitigation Plan identifies mitigation goals and actions to reduce or
       eliminate long term risk to people and property in West Valley City from impacts
       of future hazards and disasters.

       The Hazard Mitigation Plan is a requirement of the Federal Emergency
       Management Agency for all State, County and Local communities. The plan which
