F. PRESENTATION - SPIKE 150 (JOINING OF THE RAILS CELEBRATION).
   John Bond, County Treasurer, and Spike 150 executive committee member, described what happened on March
   8, 1869 in Ogden relating to this monumental rail project connecting the Atlantic and the Pacific, opening
   commerce to the nations, shortening the trade routes, and uniting our nation after the Civil War. He depicted the
   military brass band, the “Welcome to Utah” banners along the street, the three cheers, the 21-gun salute with its
   artillery thunder and deafening echoes vibrating through the mountains and the shrill whistle of the steam engines
   in celebration of the joining of the rails. He quoted from John Hall, through the Salt Lake Telegraph, that the
   Union Pacific Railroad tracklayers (coming through Echo and down through Weber Canyon) were rapidly
   marching into Ogden at half past five and laying the rails. There was much excitement from the truckloads of
   lively crowds with the sight and sound of the anxiously awaited “fiery steed.” Mr. Bond’s great-great grandfather,
   Major Seth Blair, was one of the five who spoke that day. He grew up near Davy Crockett’s children, was a Texas
   Ranger with Sam Houston, and Mr. Bond’s father also retired from the Union Pacific Railroad, after 47 years.
   Reminiscent of that earlier time, those present participated in giving three cheers and Mr. Bond’s staff then blew
   train-sounding whistles reminiscent of that earlier celebration.

    4. ACTION ON A REQUEST TO AMEND THE CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT FOR VILLAGE AT WOLF CREEK PRUD.
         Steve Burton, of the County Planning Division, stated that in this case there is an amended site plan of a PRUD
         which is required to go before the Commission. This item is for the multi-family lots only. The proposed
         changes are fairly minor—the applicants would like to narrow the roads slightly in order to get their multi-
         family buildings in a little easier, changing the location of public utility easements and the landscaping plan.
         On 4/29/2019 the Ogden Valley Planning Commission recommended approval by a 4-1 vote of the proposed
         amended PRUD (CUP #2019-04). The recommendation is subject to all review agency requirements and
         based on the following conditions: 1) Any proposed outdoor lighting will need to comply with the Valley’s
         outdoor lighting requirements; 2) The applicant will be required to provide escrow for the improvements; 3)
         Screening for all outdoor dumpsters will be required as listed in the Land Use Code; 4) The applicant will be
         required to submit a landscape maintenance plan prior to approval from the County Commission (this has been
         completed); and 5) The developer needs to work with county staff to deal with pathway/trail access to meet
         the intent of the General Plan and existing pathways to provide pedestrian connectivity (this has been
         completed). Chair Jenkins invited comments from Wolf Creek representatives and none were offered.
         Commissioner Harvey moved to approve the amended conditional use permit for the Village at Wolf Creek
         PRUD; Commissioner Froerer seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Froerer – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye
    5. F INAL APPROVAL OF C AMERON VILLAGE C LUSTER S UBDIVISION , 27 LOTS , INCLUDING A S UBDIVISION
       I MPROVEMENT A GREEMENT WITH F INANCIAL G UARANTEE WITH H AMBLIN I NVESTMENTS .
         Felix Lleverino, of the County Planning Division, stated that this cluster subdivision is in the A-1 Zone. The
         developers, Jason Hamblin and Doug Hamblin, were present. This item is to request approval for the escrow
         and the remaining improvements. Mr. Lleverino showed area maps. The dedication plat lays out the lots, the
         area being dedicated to the county, and three open space parcels, which when combined roughly equates to
         30% open space. Within open space parcels A and B there are some detention basins, fulfilling the
         requirements to manage storm water. The lots range from 15,000 to 20,000 square feet. The developer has
         provided a public trail to be maintained by the HOA along with the open space. Mr. Lleverino addressed
         Commissioner Froerer’s question stating that a fair amount of the open space will be maintained as a detention
         basin. Commissioner Froerer expressed concern that in some cases the open space is not maintained. Mr.
         Lleverino showed the detention basins and how they will operate and the path that runs along the Hooper
         Irrigation Canal that was buried. There are some inlets, through grading, and a concrete channel through the
         larger detention basin to divert the water to an irrigation ditch. He said that this was designed with the direction
         of the County Engineer. Doug Hamblin, developer, clarified that they had the detention basin installed for
         over a year and it has never had any water, it has mild slope, and will be a very usable park. The water that
         goes out of that detention basin goes into 4065 West, then heads to 2200 South, and does not go into Hooper
         Irrigation, but to the drainage that is on 2200 S., then heads west. He added that the HOA will be professionally
         managed and the project is designed as something the community will be proud of.
         Commissioner Froerer moved to grant final approval of Cameron Village Cluster Subdivision, a 27-lot
         subdivision, including a Subdivision Improvement Agreement with a Financial Guarantee with Hamblin
         Investments; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Froerer – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye
