**Ordinance: Community Clean Energy Program**   ~ 4:00 p.m.
  20 min.
The Council will receive a briefing about a proposal that would enact Title 9, Chapter 50 to the *Salt Lake City Code*, establishing participation in the Community Clean Energy program, formally known as Utah Renewable Communities. The proposal would provide a new opportunity for nearly all homes and businesses within the City to choose clean energy through their Rocky Mountain Power (RMP) bill. Customers will be automatically enrolled in the program, with the option to opt out. If customers choose to stay enrolled in the program, a new fee will appear on their RMP bill.

**FYI – Project Timeline:** (subject to change per Chair direction or Council discussion)
Briefing - Tuesday, April 14, 2026
Set Public Hearing Date -  Tuesday, April 7, 2026
Hold hearing to accept public comment - Tuesday, April 21, 2026 at 7 p.m.
TENTATIVE Council Action - Tuesday, May 5, 2026

**Summary:**

Lehua Weaver introduced the item, including an overview of the Community Clean Energy Program, estimated participation rates, and the City’s upcoming decision regarding participation in the program.

Sophia Nicolas and Glade Sowards presented the item, including the program’s goal of providing Rocky Mountain Power customers the option to choose clean energy, proposed customer rates and opt-out provisions, low-income participation benefits, implementation timelines, and a proposed Budget Amendment No. 5 request related to startup costs.

Council Members and staff discussed:

* Enthusiasm and support for the program as a major clean energy milestone for the City
* Clarification that the $30 termination fee would go to Rocky Mountain Power to help stabilize program rates
* Opt-out provisions for customers moving into or out of participating communities, including a 60-day notice period before termination fees would apply
* Outreach and education efforts for low-income households eligible for waived fees and participation benefits through Rocky Mountain Power’s existing financial assistance resources
* Appreciation for Council Member Dugan’s involvement and leadership throughout the program development process

**Ordinance: Camping and Obstructing Public Property and Park Rules Text Amendment**   ~ 4:20 p.m.
  30 min.
The Council will receive a briefing about a proposal that would amend various sections of the *Salt Lake City Code* relating to camping on public grounds, streets, parks, and playgrounds, park hours, disposing of unclaimed property, and obstructing sidewalks. The proposal would introduce an updated definition of what constitutes camping and consolidate and clarify the use and obstruction of public spaces and rights of way to reduce confusion and ensure proper enforcement.

**FYI – Project Timeline:** (subject to change per Chair direction or Council discussion)
Briefing - Tuesday, April 14, 2026
Set Public Hearing Date -  Tuesday, April 21, 2026
Hold hearing to accept public comment - Tuesday, May 5, 2026 at 7 p.m.
TENTATIVE Council Action - Tuesday, May 19, 2026

**Summary:**

Nick Tarbet introduced the item. Andrew Johnston presented the proposed ordinance updates related to camping and obstruction on public property, including revised definitions of camping intended to reflect current conditions and improve consistency in enforcement. The presentation included discussion of prohibited camping activities, overnight vehicle camping, unattended personal property, enforcement criteria, permitted exceptions, encampment removal practices, available shelter and outreach resources, and coordination with surrounding jurisdictions. Commander Jared Gilbert was available for questions.

Council Members and staff discussed:

* Enforcement considerations related to parade routes and temporary exceptions
* Deployment of alternative response teams and coordination with social workers and outreach providers
* Balancing public safety, public space use, and the needs of individuals experiencing homelessness
* Concerns that enforcement may relocate encampments rather than resolve underlying homelessness issues
* The ordinance’s role in addressing vehicle camping and creating more consistent enforcement standards
* Creation of success measures, including decreases in visible camping, response times, and connections to services
* Efforts to connect unsheltered individuals with shelters, transitional housing, detox services, and other resources
* Communication and public education plans related to ordinance changes and enforcement expectations
* Opportunities to use data related to vehicle camping and enforcement to support future shelter and micro-housing funding requests through State funding
* Concerns regarding the potential criminalization of homelessness and the importance of compassionate, consistent enforcement practices

**Council Requests:**

Council Member Young requested a future discussion regarding how success measures related to the ordinance would be communicated to constituents.

**Ordinance: Master Plan and Zoning Map Amendment at Approximately 346, 350, 354, and 370 South 800 East, and 775 East 400 South**   ~ 4:50 p.m.
  20 min.
The Council will receive a briefing about a proposal that would amend the zoning of the properties at approximately 346, 350, 354 South 800 East from RMF-35 (Moderate Density Residential Multi-Family) to MU-5 (Mixed Use 5). The proposal would also amend the Central Community Plan Future Land Use Map of the properties at approximately 346, 350, 354, 370 South 800 East and 775 East 400 South from Medium Density Residential (15-30 dwelling units/acre) and Medium Density Transit Oriented Development (10-50 dwelling units/acre) to High Density Transit Oriented Development (50 or more dwelling units/acre). The proposed amendments would allow the construction of a mixed-use building encompassing 109 total residential units and ground-floor commercial space, which includes 2-,3- and 4- bedroom units. The properties currently contain three existing residential buildings, including seven apartment units. Consideration may be given to rezoning the property to another zoning district with similar characteristics. The Planning Commission forwarded a positive recommendation to amend the Zoning Map, and recommended that the Council deny the amendment to the Central City Community Future Land Use Map. In addition to the requested amendments, the applicant is seeking design review approval to exceed the maximum building façade length of 200 feet, which the Planning Commission will reconsider after the Council takes action on this proposal. The project is within Council District 4. Petitioner: Sean Thompson, representing the property owner Hardage Hospitality.

**Fiscal Year 2026-27 Funding Allocations for Federal Housing and Community Development Grants and City Funding Our Future Housing Programs Follow-Up**   ~ 6:00 p.m.
  20 min.
The Council will receive a follow-up briefing on recommendations for allocating grant funding provided through four Federal Housing and Urban Development Department (HUD) programs and the City's Funding Our Future (FOF) housing programs. The funding is allocated by the Council on a competitive basis to organizations which are mostly local non-profits that specialize in providing services to the City’s most economically vulnerable residents. For Fiscal Year 2026-27, approximately $11.3 million dollars is expected to flow through the Division of Housing Stability to the organizations eligible to receive grant funding. The HUD programs that provide this funding and oversee activities of grant recipients are: Community Development Block Grants (CDBG), Emergency Solutions Grants (ESG), the HOME Investment Partnership Program, and Housing Opportunities for Persons with AIDS (HOPWA). City housing programs are funded from the local sales tax increment known as Funding Our Future.

For more information visit [www.tinyurl.com/annualhudgrants](https://www.tinyurl.com/annualhudgrants).
