STAFF PRESENTATION
The Staff Report to the Planning Commission provides details of the facts of the case and the Staff's analysis, conclusions,
and recommendations.

NEIGHBORHOOD MEETING DATE
• Citywide Application; all Neighborhood District Chairs received notification.

NEIGHBORHOOD AND PUBLIC COMMENT
• This item was Citywide or affected multiple neighborhoods.

CONCERNS RAISED BY PUBLIC
Any comments received prior to completion of the Staff Report are addressed in the Staff Report to the Planning
Commission. There were no issues raised in written comments received subsequent to the Staff Report or public comment
during the public hearing.

PLANNING COMMISSION DISCUSSION
Key points discussed by the Planning Commission included the following:
• Commissioner DeSoto noted that pet spaces for multifamily residential developments makes sense and encouraged
   pet spaces in public spaces as well.


                                                        Page 1 of 7
•   Commissioner Jensen raised the question of whether requiring this standard of amenities would raise costs for
    developers and residents. Commissioner South explained that there are several low-cost combinations of amenities
    that could easily reach the minimum score of 60 points.
•   Commissioner Shin expressed concern that the decision about what residents need and what they are willing to pay
    for should be left to developers and residents. He would not want to see the number of units reduced to accommodate
    amenities requirements.
•   Commissioner DeSoto reasoned that low-income residents still deserve a good quality of living space, and quality
    spaces might encourage people to stay and invest in their neighborhoods. Costs need to be balanced, but the proposed
    amendment does not necessarily increase price for most developments.
•   Commissioner Jensen liked the flexibility built in for developers and that items not on the list can be approved by the
    Development Services Director. If a certain combination or amenities does not work for a developer, the developer
    can simply pick other amenities. She also debated the City’s role but also appreciated a minimum standard of quality.

FINDINGS / BASIS OF PLANNING COMMISSION DETERMINATION
The Planning Commission identified the following findings as the basis of this decision or recommendation:
• Most of the Commissioners determined that a minimum standard of quality would be beneficial for residents as long
   as it did not increase costs. Although they did debate whether this was an issue for the free market or for City policy,
   they determined that this was an area where it was appropriate for the City to lead out and communicate clear, flexible
   expectations, provided there was enough flexibility not to significantly increase costs.
• Commissioner Shin determined that the decision about what residents need and what they are willing to pay for should
   be left to developers and residents in order to prevent adding costs for developers and residents.

14.34.330 Multifamily Amenities.

(1) Each multifamily zone requires that any new project with a specified number of units must allocate a certain
percentage of the residential gross floor area for the development of recreational amenities. Landscaping in front and
streetside yards, minimum setbacks, and other required areas (such as distance provisions required by the
International Building Code) do not count towards meeting the requirements of this Section. Only up to fifty percent
(50%) of the required recreational amenity space may be located outdoors at the ground floor level. The open space must
be massed or accumulated to be functional as a recreational amenity or gathering space. Amenities that are enclosed may
count towards meeting the requirements of this Section.

(2) Amenities may be selected in any combination from the table below so long as they amount to a total score of 60.
Multiple instances of the same amenity receive points for each instance.

        (a) If a developer would like to pursue an amenity that is not on this list, the Department Director (or the
        Director’s designee) may give authorization to assign that amenity the score of the most similar amenity on the
        given list and adjust the score accordingly.

        (b) All amenities must be maintained for the entire duration of occupancy. If a developer, HOA, or other
        responsible party removes an amenity, it must be replaced with one or more amenities that achieve a combined
        score of at least 60 to meet the required total.

        (c) The developer may elect in writing at the time of application whether parking spaces with electric vehicle
        charging stations or electric vehicle-ready infrastructure will be counted toward the zone’s amenity square footage
        requirements or parking minimums, but those spaces may not be counted toward both requirements. If no written
        election is made, such spaces will count toward amenity space and not toward parking minimums.

        (d) Outdoor features include, but are not limited to, fire pits, pavilions, outdoor cooking equipment, dining areas,
        and other similar comforts for outdoor spaces. They do not include seating, meaning that seating provided without
        an accompanying feature does not count as an outdoor feature and seating that is provided with an accompanying
        feature does not count as an additional feature, but is instead considered part of the feature it accompanies.

CONCERNS RAISED BY PUBLIC
Any comments received prior to completion of the Staff Report are addressed in the Staff Report to the Planning

the public hearing included the following: No public comment was made at the meeting; the district chair expressed her

APPLICANT RESPONSE
Key points addressed in the applicant's presentation to the Planning Commission included the following:
The applicant highlighted that other homes in the neighborhood have subdivided to create flag lots. He explained that
leaving the lot as a large backyard would not be the most effective use of the property and mentioned that he had discussed
with the City the idea of putting 9 townhomes at the rear of the property. To close he stated that approving the rezone to
R1.6 would allow him to create a second lot and add one single-family dwelling to the community.

                                                        Page 1 of 4
PLANNING COMMISSION DISCUSSION
Key points discussed by the Planning Commission included the following:
   • Commissioner DeSoto asked the applicant to explain why the concept plan only shows one lot instead of the
       possible 9 he mentioned. Staff explained that there had been many meetings with the applicant and that a rezone
       to R1.6 with one flag lot would be the best option based on the technical restrictions at the property.
   • Commissioner Jensen asked why staff was going through a rezone process as opposed to a variance. Staff explained
       that this property likely would not meet the requirements for a variance to be approved.
   • Commissioner Jensen asked why there would be just one lot in the rear of the property when there could possibly
       be room for two. The applicant explained that based on the flag lot restrictions and lot width requirements the
       property could not meet the lot width requirements and the flag lot requirements. Staff confirmed the statement.
   • Commissioner Shin asked if this flag lot width requirement was related to the flag lot text amendments that the
       Planning Commission had recently reviewed. Staff confirmed that it was.
   • Commissioner Jensen asked if he demolished the existing house could he create more lots? Staff explained that
       the lot width would not allow any additional flag lots to be created.
   • Commissioner Jensen stated although her preference is not for small rezones, this is a creative solution to allow
       for an additional single-family dwelling and a better use of the land. She noted that the proposed rezone is not to
       a density that would impact the neighborhood, and the rezone is in line with General Plan goals.
   • Commissioner Jensen asked if the home to be built on the proposed lot would be for sale or if the applicant was
       planning to rent it. The applicant confirmed it would be for sale.
   • Commissioner South stated that he was in favor of the proposed rezone and subdivision but wished that there could
       be a rezone for something that allowed more density.
   • Commissioners Whitlock. Gonzales, and DeSoto agreed with Commissioner South.
   • Commission Whitlock consulted staff on why rezoning to R1.6 was chosen over other options. Staff explained that
       they had gone over other possible zones, but lot width and flagstaff width were the constraining factors.
   • Commissioner Jensen stated that the discussion about having more density at this site is not the item at hand and
       asked if the Commission felt comfortable making a motion about the item at hand.
   • Commissioner South made the motion to recommend approval to the Municipal Council. The motion was seconded
       by Commissioner Shin.
