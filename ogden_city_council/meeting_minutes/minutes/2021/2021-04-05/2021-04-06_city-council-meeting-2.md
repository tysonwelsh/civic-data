Recognizing the late John Ulibarri for his invaluable contributions to the
Ogden community
          A memo from Council staff regarding recognition of the late John Ulibarri for his invaluable contributions to the Ogden
community came before the Council for consideration. Council member Lopez read the certificate of recognition issued by the
Ogden City Council and Mayor Caldwell and presented John Ulibarri's son, John, with the certificate.
          John Ulibarri thanked the Mayor and Council for recognizing his father; his father loved this community, his students,
and his family. He stated it was an honor to be his son and he hopes that his father's spirit will be carried with others who are
performing community service. He stated his father's biggest goal was to make things better in his community, not just one group
or class of people, but he loved everyone, and he hoped for everyone in the community to strive to do the same.
          Chair Blair stated Mr. Ulibarri was his principal at Taylor Elementary School; he told a story of an interaction that he had
with Mr. Ulibarri, which was a valuable learning lesson. Mr. Ulibarri taught him the importance of getting all he could out of his
educational opportunities and to also try to make things better for others around him. He stated he will never forget his
interactions with Mr. Ulibarri and stated he was one of the most influential people he met in his youth. His passing is a great loss
for the community and for his family.
          Council member Lopez also relayed a story about his personal experiences with Mr. Ulibarri, who was his supervisor
when he was performing his studentteaching at Mound Fort Middle School. He has great memories ofhim and is grateful for the
influence he had on his professional career.

               A memo from the Management Services Department regarding proposed wage and travel appropriations adjustments
     came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Council will consider setting a public hearing on a proposed
     budget amendment that transfers $1,973,650 from respective fund balances to payroll accounts in the General Fund, Enterprise
     Funds and Internal Service Funds to cover a four-percent wage adjustment for full time employees ($1,830,650). The wage
     adjustments are proposed to become effective July 1, 2020. The amendment also appropriates funds to travel accounts ($143 ,000)
     for costs associated with staff training. Salary increases and travel costs were not funded in the adopted FY21 budget due to
     budget uncertainties arising from the Covid-19 pandemic. A separate, related proposal amends the FY21 Salary Schedule to add a
     four-percent increase to each salary range.
               Comptroller Stout summarized the memo and stated the amendment recognizes money from various fund balance
     accounts and appropriates those funds to payroll accounts in the General Fund, Enterprise Funds and Internal Service Funds to
     provide a four percent wage adjustment to full-time City employees. The proposal also uses Fund Balance to increase non-
     departmental and departmental travel accounts used for costs associated with employee training. Salary increases and travel costs
     were not funded in the adopted FY21 budget due to budget uncertainties arising from the Covid-19 pandemic and this action is
     intended to correct that issue. The Mayor and Chief Administrative Officer have opted to decline the four percent wage
     adjustment and the money that would have been used for those adjustments will be redirected to administrative employees in the
     Mayor's office. The fiscal impact of both the wage adjustments and travel allocation is $1,973,650.
               An additional memo from the Management Services Department regarding proposed salary schedule adjustments came
     before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Council will consider setting a public hearing on proposed
     amendments to the FY21 Salary Schedules. Administration is proposing a 4% wage adjustment to the pay ranges adopted in the
     FY21 Salary Schedules that were approved with the FY21 Budget. Exceptions are the Mayor and the Justice Court Judge pay
     ranges which will not change under the proposal. As proposed, the new salary schedules would be effective July 1, 2020, and
     implemented with the April3, 2021, pay period.
               Human Resources Manager Judkins summarized the memo and stated Administration is proposing a four percent
     adjustment to the pay ranges adopted in the Fiscal Year (FY) 21 Salary Schedules. This proposal is the first step in implementing
     the Classification, Compensation and Benefit Study. The new salary schedules are proposed to be effective July 1, 2020, with
     implementation beginning April3, 2021; if approved, a stipend will be provided to full time employees adjusted retroactively to
     July 1, 2020. In order to receive the stipend, an employee must be a currently active full-time employee as of April16; temporary
     full-time employees are not eligible. The total impact for the wage adjustments across all Funds is $1,830,651. She then provided
     examples of proposed salary schedules to illustrate the manner in which certain scales will be adjusted if the four percent increase
     is approved.

    Proposed Ordinance 2021-17 to rezone three residential properties at
    approximately 1752 Pacific Avenue from R-1-5 to M-1 to allow for the
    development of a light manufacturing shop
              A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed zone change for property
    located at 17 52 Pacific A venue came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Sam Hunter has submitted a petition
    to rezone three parcels at approximately 1752 Pacific Avenue from R-1-5 to M-1 for the construction of a small manufacturing
    shop. The properties were included in the 2016 Gibson Community Plan update and were rezoned from M-1 to R-1-5 in 2019 in
    accordance with that plan. The Planning Commission has forwarded a recommendation of denial of the proposed rezone with a
    vote of six to two. The Council reviewed the proposal at its March 9 and March 23 work sessions. At the March 9 work session
    meeting, Council members requested additional information on the development proposals in the area and expressed interest in
    having an ordinance drafted. The information Council members had requested was discussed at the work session of March 23. The
    direction from the Council at that meeting was to proceed with the proposed rezone by placing it on a future agenda for fmal
    consideration.
              Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide detailed
    information regarding the petition and the Planning Commission's recommendation. The applicant seeks to rezone three parcels to
    allow for the construction of a light manufacturing shop. This would include the demolition of the existing single-family dwelling,
    combining all three parcels into one, and developing a 50 foot by 135-foot building that will be divided into two to four shop
    spaces. He discussed the history of the property dating back to 2016 when the Gibson Community Plan was adopted; in 2019, the
    Planning Commission reviewed and approved a request to rezone the properties from M-1 to R-1-5 and in January 2020 a four-lot
    subdivision was approved. He then discussed the Planning Commission's consideration of the application; they considered
    whether the proposed rezone creates a defmite edge for the development and providers buffering between types of uses. The
    subject property is along the boundary ofanM-2 zone to the west, anM-1 zone to the south, and aR-1-5 zone to the east. It fronts
    Pacific Avenue to the west and provides a defmite edge and buffer between the two zones. There are a mix of single-family
    dwellings and manufacturing uses to the south. In 2019, the Commission found Pacific A venue could act as a transition area with
    manufacturing zones on the west and residential to the east. The proposed M-1 zone would connect with properties to the south
    and no buffer was provided for the R -1-5 zone to the east. Further Commission considerations for the current application include
    whether the proposed use reflects the character of the overall district; districts determined to be in transition may be given special
    considerations. The area has historically been zoned for manufacturing uses, but residential uses are scattered throughout the
    manufacturing areas. The Gibson Community Plan spurred the rezone of existing single-family homes from manufacturing to R-1-
    5 and many of the properties on the east side of Pacific Avenue are single family homes. The area is part of a larger future
    redevelopment area, the primary objective of which is to establish a residential and mixed-use community. Rezoning any
    properties back to a manufacturing use is contrary to the direction of the Gibson Community Plan. The Commission considered
    whether the Community Plan supports the current application; the Plan identifies development objectives for the area, including
    strengthening the residential character of the area south of 17th Street near the river. He reviewed the zoning map to orient the
    Council to zoning changes; there is light manufacturing called for to the west of Pacific A venue, adjacent to the railroad, with
    residential uses to the east. Pacific A venue can provide a separation or buffer between zones. He then presented photographs taken
    from the subject property to illustrate the property's surroundings, after which he discussed the development possibilities for the
    property; under the requested zoning designation, the applicant could build a 50 foot by 135-foot building. This would require a
    dedication of16 feet of property for street widening, a lot depth of115 feet with a 20-foot rear and front setback. An additional42
    feet would be set aside for parking area. The Commission considered the fact that the area is in transition as well as the
    appropriate boundary line for the property. They ultimately voted six to two to recommend denial of the application to rezone the
    property from R-1-5 to M-1, based on the fmding that the proposed rezone does not provide a defmite edge to the development
    and buffering between types of uses; it also does not reflect the prevailing character of the overall area and does not comply with
    the intent of the Gibson Community Plan. The two Commissioners that vote in support felt that a manufacturing use of the
    property would better reflect the character of the area given that manufacturing uses exist on both sides of the subject property.

Public Comments
          Angel Castillo stated she wished to speak about a matter discussed in tonight's work session relating to housing in the
community; she is very disappointed that the person in charge of development in the City is not familiar with Housing and Urban
Development (HUD) standards. There is a specific standard that was updated in October of20 19 relating to housing affordability
and Community and Economic Development Director Christopulos is unfamiliar with that standard. The answer to housing
affordability issues, which other communities are aware of, is to allow condominiums, townhomes, and tiny homes; these types of
housing units are typically a smaller footprint and, therefore, the cost is lower. HUD changed their standards to make home
ownership easier because this is the fastest way for someone to achieve fmancial independence and security; they expanded their
allowances to new or existing condominiums. HUD standard 4,000.1 provides information on how to get HUD approval of
existing condos or the construction of new condos. She stated that the City needs to start doing things differently and more
creatively to address housing affordability issues, which will only worsen. The City Council, especially Vice Chair White, have
repeatedly stressed the need for a housing plan and that still has not come to fruition. The City needs housing of all types: high-
end, moderate, and low-income homes and she encouraged them to develop a plan that will provide solutions for developing an
inventory of all housing types and something other than the standard single-family home. This will help residents to pursue
opportunities for building equity and credit through home ownership.
          Heath Satow thanked Council member Lopez for the questions he asked during tonight's work session regarding
housing; he hopes that the Community and Economic Development Director will be able to update his own philosophy regarding
housing in the near future. He then noted that he has been asking the Council and Administration questions about the Brown's Ice
Cream Building development for the past several weeks; those questions remain unanswered, but it is his guess that the Council is
now aware that they have been misled about the details of the project, such as how the developer was chosen and where the
property was listed and marketed. The value of the property was underestimated, and he wondered how the Redevelopment
Agency (RDA) could, in good conscience, not allow other qualified and interested developers to bid on the project after the
original contract expired. Most important is why there is not a formalized bid and selection process for RDA owned properties. He
first began asking these questions two months ago and since that time the City Council has learned that the broker undervalued the
building by nearly half and it is very hard to believe this was an accident, especially since no one can show how and where the
building was marketed for sale publicly. The optics regarding this transaction are not good and they give the appearance of a
conflict of interest on the part of the broker and that he worked for the builder, rather than the City, as initially communicated to
the Council. He hopes the Council is disturbed by these matters as he is and if he is wrong, he would like someone to provide
evidence of such. It is unacceptable for residents to be told to blindly trust their government; the ongoing lack of answers from
City Administration does not leave residents with many options. If mistakes were made, he would like to hear an explanation for
those mistakes and see processes tightened and improved. A few Council members have spoken with him about these questions,
and he appreciates that; however, they have not been able to provide legitimate answers to his questions and that only frustrates
him more. He is left with no other conclusion than that City Administration is not being entirely forthcoming with the Council. If
the Council cannot get more complete answers to these questions for the public, it is at least their duty as the City Council and
RDA Board to formalize the bid and procurement process. The Council makes the laws of the City and provides checks and
balances for the City's budget; the Council's own budget statement states: "it has always been a Council priority that fmancial
information is transparent and readily available to residents and City employees." All he is asking is that the Council live up to
that motto as citizens of Ogden deserve as much. The Administration's development process is not transparent, and they should
not be keeping information private because doing so is not in the best interest of the City. He concluded the process is broken and
asked that the Council fix it.
