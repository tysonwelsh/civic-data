Discussion Items

       Little Library Policy

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, said the City currently has two Little Libraries on City property and
the City needs to have a policy to manage the contents placed in the libraries. He will email a
rough draft of the Little Library policy to the Council so it can be adopted at the next City Council
meeting.

Councilmember McCay said the Little Library that was installed in the parking lot of City Hall
was created for STEM materials only.

Councilmember McDougal asked who would be responsible if the library was damaged. Mayor
Staggs said he thought it would be the responsibility of the person who installed the library. Mr.
Carter agreed and noted that the City Council could choose to budget for any expenses incurred

from damage.

Councilmember Stewart asked for clarification on whether items would be removed from the
Little Library at City Hall if the items are not STEM materials. Mr. Carter said if the Council
would like to designate the Little Library at City Hall as a STEM library only, then the policy will
need to be written differently.

Mr. Carter also explained it is challenging to craft a policy for two different types of libraries and
it is important to make sure the City is not engaging in any discriminatory behavior when
determining what stays in the library and what does not.

Councilmember Buroker said the Little Library down by the river had graffiti sprayed on it and
someone repainted it. She believes this sends the message that the library has value and is a
nice resource.

Mayor Staggs said he would like the Council to see the policy and asked Mr. Carter to email it to
everyone for comments. He said the policy is important to have so the City does not face a first
amendment issue.

Councilmember McCay said the Little Library in City Hall was labeled as a STEM box. Pam
VanWagoner with 4H will be refilling this library and checking the contents of the library.


Manager/Staff/Council Reports

Mayor Staggs met with Casey Saxton earlier in the day and went through the schedule of
events for the month. On November 17, the Council will meet with legislators at the Old Dome
Meeting Hall. Mayor Staggs will communicate the top priorities including pushing for
transportation equity and the completion of Mountain View Corridor.

Another item that will be discussed is the intersection at 13400 South and Bangerter. In order to
have the intersection fully submerged, UDOT will require the City to pay $4 million. He said
some property owners have loosely committed to contributing money for this project. He would
also like to work with Senator Dan McCay and the lobbyists to utilize quarter-for-quarter monies.
This would leave about $500,000 to $1 million the City would contribute, most likely from RDIF
funds. He said the County also has $20 million for transportation choice money and $2 million
needs to be allocated. He would like to work with the lobbyist to secure some of this money for
this intersection.

Councilmember Buroker asked what the plan was for intersection at 2700 West and Bangerter.
Mayor Staggs said it would be the standard overpass and would not require any money from the
City. Mayor Staggs said that there would be large sound walls installed.

Councilmember McCay said she would like both intersections to be modified. Cary Necaise,
Public Works Director, said UDOT is already moving forward with the plans to have 13400
South fully submerged and 2700 West standard overpass. They have already done
environmental studies for both of these projects.

Councilmember McCay said she would also like to talk to legislators about an issue she heard
from an affordable housing meeting. Legislators at this meeting expressed concern about cities
charging too much for impact fees. She would like to educate legislators about what the impact

fees are and how impact fees are used. Mayor Staggs agreed and said they could also share
the infographic on housing units showing that over 50% of the housing in most cities are multi-
family.

Councilmember McCay would also like to express the importance of connectivity of roads.

Mayor Staggs said he thought that Councilmembers and property owners expressed support for
13400 South to be fully submerged. UDOT did not require South Jordan to pay anything extra
for the intersection at 11400 South to be fully submerged. The intersection at 10400 South cost
$6 million in betterment fees and South Jordan was able to get quarter-for-quarter monies to
cover this cost. The intersection at 9800 South will also be fully submerged and all three
intersections have been largely paid for by other sources, not the city itself.

He said the original betterment cost for 13400 South to be fully submerged was $20 million.
UDOT agreed to share this cost and said they would pay $10 million and Riverton would be
responsible for the other $10 million. Now, the fee has gone to $4 million and the City will hold
them firm to these figures. He would like to have the Councilmembers all on the same page
because UDOT is well into the design on both of these intersections.

Councilmember Stewart asked if the letter regarding the land exchange was factored into the $4
million that UDOT is requiring for the betterment. He also expressed support for 13400 South
being fully submerged because line of sight is key to business.

Mayor Staggs agreed and said that Gary Whatcott from South Jordan feels the same way. He
believes there is also a psychological aspect when the intersection is not submerged, causing
the residents on the east and west to feel separated.

Mayor Staggs explained he communicated in writing to UDOT that the City gave them 21 acres
of property years ago and that is how the betterment was lowered from $10 million to $4 million.

Councilmember McDougal said that he would like to have 13400 South fully submerged but he
is concerned that the City may have to pay $4 million and does not want to do that. Mayor
Staggs said he has verbal commitments from landowners in the area and really thinks the City
will only need to come up with $500,000 to $1 million.

Councilmember McDougal asked whether the County could provide funds. Mayor Staggs said
he is not counting on money from the County.

Councilmember Buroker clarified that the City requested that 13400 South be fully submerged.
Mayor Staggs said it was the City that requested it.

Councilmember McCay asked if a hybrid could be considered for the 2700 West intersection.

overpasses along this area of Bangerter consistent and they would all go over. Bluffdale was
also in agreement with this plan.

Mayor Staggs said the relocation of Rose Creek would be cost prohibitive. Councilmember
Stewart was very supportive of fully submerged as well as Suburban Land Development.

Public Hearings

         Ordinance No. 22-24 – Funaro Rezone and General Plan Amendment, Proposed
         Amendment of the General Plan to Medium Density Residential and the Zoning
         from RR-22 to R-4 for 0.5 acres at 1794 West 11800 South

Mr. Lethbridge explained this was a public hearing for a rezone application brought by the
applicant for 0.5 acres at 1794 West 11800 South. The subject property is a residential ½ acre
lot with about 125 feet of frontage on 11800 South at 1794 West. Neighboring properties to the
east and west are all zoned RR-22 but the property sizes range from 0.3 to 0.4 acre lots, less
than the 0.5-acres required in the RR-22 Zone. The property to the north is a 1.4-acre lot
zoned RR-22. Properties to the south across 11800 South are zoned in a mix of zones
including one lot of RR-22, and several lots of R-4 and RM-8.

Mr. Lethbridge said the property is currently zoned “RR-22” (Residential ½ acre). The applicant
is requesting this rezone to R-4 (Residential ¼ acre). There is a small home on the west front of
the lot, and the lot has two accesses onto 11800 South. The lot size is approximately 21,780
square feet and would be appropriate for a private lane and a subdivision. The final result would
be two lots of 11,000 square feet and a private lane.

Councilmembers asked clarifying questions of staff and the applicant.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.

Councilmember Buroker MOVED that the Riverton City Council adopt Ordinance No. 22-24
– Funaro Rezone and General Plan Amendment, Proposed Amendment of the General
Plan to Medium Density Residential and the Zoning from RR-22 to R-4 for 0.5 acres at
1794 West 11800 South. Councilmember Wells SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs
called for discussion on the motion. There being none, he called for a roll-call vote. The vote
was as follows: Buroker-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-no vote (technical difficulties), Stewart-yes
and Wells-yes. The motion passed unanimously.
