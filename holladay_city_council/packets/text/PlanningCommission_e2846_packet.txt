A Planned Unit Development (PUD) is a conditional use and all amendments require a site plan review
and approval by the Land Use Authority (Planning Commission). Decisions and approval must be made
during public meeting.

SUMMARY
The previously reviewed and approved PUD designated open space on the site plan with the statement
that only trees within the road and building footprints would be allowed for removal. The project was
delayed for just over a year with extensions for recordation being granted to the applicant. As the
applicant began a permit for site work to remove the trees previously designated for removal, it became
clear that other trees on the site may need to be removed due to their health or close proximity to the
roadway, structures, and required storm water retention areas.

The commission is encouraged to visit the site in order to see the trees which are originating from within
the property and their associated canopy.

The site plan provided by the applicant and assessment by an arborist proposes the following tree
removals of trees outside of the previous approval:
    • Multiple trees along the North property line
            a. Concerns due to both current tree health and the impact of the construction of the
                road due to proximity of the improvements.

BACKGROUND
Tree canopy sustainability has been an important aspect of the City of Holladay code when new projects
are started. The City’s tree canopy sustainability requirements help to ensure that when properties are
redeveloped that only the necessary trees are removed and that the mature canopy that is being
removed is replenished over time by requiring the mature canopy to be replaced, sustaining Holladay’s
tree canopy for future generations. Tree replacements also help to replenish trees that have health
issues due to poor planting locations and care, age, effects of drought, and effects of insects, wind
damage, or rot.

When sites are redeveloped, there are significant challenges, especially when an under-utilized property
is developed under zoning that is already in place which allows more of a property to be covered with
paved surfaces and more structures. In this case, a property that had retained an old, smaller home and
minimal pavement had zoning that would allow more residential units and allowed lot coverage than
was being used. Developers face a significant challenge trying to balance the development rights
associated with the property and other requirements for fire access and on-site storm water retention
with existing trees and fitting in open space. Properties that are zoned as R-M allow for a higher
percentage of lot coverage due to having multiple dwelling units and associated parking and access and
thus have less open space and more pavement than would be seen in single family zones.

TECHNICAL REVIEW COMMITTE ANALYSIS
PLANNED UNIT DEVELOPMENT - CUP
The TRC has received a detailed tree assessment by an arborist from the applicant detailing the need for
the proposed tree removals that were outside of the original approval. Many of the trees are in poor
health and/or would be impacted by either the roadway improvements or building footprints. The
provided landscaping plan shows the location of replacement trees and provides an assessment of their
total mature canopy size.

RECOMMENDATION
The TRC recommends a dialog with the applicant regarding the distances from the improvements and
the assumed impact on trees that may have existing health issues.

The TRC recommends the Commission approve the amendments to the HIGHLAND PARK PLANNED
UNIT DEVELOPMENT SITE PLAN.
