Discussion Items

       Sycamore Glen Pond Discussion

Matt Cassel, City Engineer, presented three alternatives for the Sycamore Glen detention pond,
also known as Walker Pond, seeking direction from the Council. Alternative one, the "Do
Nothing" option, involves minimal intervention, but poses the risk of overflow into the
neighborhood during storm events due to reduced capacity. Alternative two entails maintaining
the existing design, which lacks landscaping and aesthetics, while still posing overflow risks.
Alternative three, the recommended option, involves utilizing the north half of the existing pond,
deepening it, and landscaping to enhance aesthetics. This alternative closely aligns with the
original design, offering improved capacity and reduced overflow risk.

Mr. Cassel emphasized the need to address concerns raised by neighbors and outlined the
budget estimate for implementing the recommended alternative. The Council was invited to

Councilmember Buroker highlighted historical concerns raised by residents during the initial
development phase of the area around the detention pond. Residents expressed concerns
about preserving the natural landscape and wildlife habitat, including a stream and deer
population, in the area before the development. She emphasized the importance of considering
residents' perspectives and reaching out to them to gather feedback on the proposed changes
to the pond.

Mayor Staggs inquired whether the plan includes plans for a buildable lot or if it is intended as
open space to be maintained by the Toll Brothers community. In response, Mr. Cassel said
there is potential for the front piece of the area to be utilized as a benefit to the community and
expressed a need for further consideration regarding its management, including the possibility
of fencing for safety reasons.

Councilmember Pierucci expressed a preference for not putting up a fence around the pond if
efforts are made to beautify the property. He suggested engaging the community to gather
feedback and help them understand the necessity of the project. Additionally, he expressed
support for option three but emphasized the importance of considering the costs and comparing
them to the initial budget investments.

Councilmember McDougal asked about vegetation surrounding the pond. Mr. Cassel said it will
be low-lying vegetation and could incorporate a meandering stream-like feature.
Councilmember McDougal highlighted the importance of considering the durability of vegetation
around the pond, citing an example with Midas Creek where flooding caused damage to grass
and landscaping. He emphasized the need to factor in the costs of potential replacement due to
occasional flooding to avoid ongoing expenses.

Councilmember McCay expressed a preference for not having a fence around the pond and
suggested instead putting up signs to remind people to watch their children. She also mentioned
the possibility of creating a path around the pond, which aligns with requests from residents for
more paths in the City. She expressed support for option three but suggested further discussion
and site visitation before making a final decision.

Mayor Staggs discussed potential traffic improvements along 12600 South between Bangerter
Highway and Mountain View Corridor. The proposed plan includes expanding the road to three
lanes from Bangerter Highway to 4150 West to alleviate traffic congestion during peak hours.
There was also mention of seeking funding through the transportation omnibus bill to further
address traffic issues in the area.

Mayor/Council Reports

Councilmember Buroker expressed gratitude for the completion of the river stabilization project
along the Jordan River, noting that it was completed on time and under budget. She also
highlighted the opening of the new Primary Children's Hospital in Lehi, which will feature the

maintaining fire stations in their area.

Councilmember Pierucci provided updates on two key issues. Firstly, he shared positive news
about Utah Lake levels being high, leading to plans to open gates in a couple of weeks, which

highlighted this as a win for conservation efforts and other planning projects.

Secondly, Councilmember Pierucci discussed his recent meeting with UTA (Utah Transit
Authority) and UDOT (Utah Department of Transportation) at the Capitol regarding the lack of
service and investment by UTA in the region. He expressed cautious optimism about potential
improvements following the meeting and mentioned plans for UTA planners to reach out to local
officials to discuss mapping out preferred bus routes and potentially implementing a pilot
program to prove ridership.

Councilmember McCay reported on the successful effort to secure excused absences for the
Youth Council members from Jordan School District when they participate in activities such as
visiting the Capitol and attending training sessions. She expressed appreciation to Broc Stowe
from Riverton City for his role in advocating for this approval, highlighting it as a positive

and highlighted the need for Riverton City to adjust its debris management program. With the
designated site at Trans Jordan being unavailable by June of 2024, he emphasized the
importance of finding an alternative location for storing debris in the event of a natural disaster.

Councilmember Haymond expressed gratitude towards the Riverton Youth Council program,

Public Hearings

         Ordinance No. 24-01 – Adopting Section 17.35, Review and Approval Process in
         Compliance with Utah State Code, regulating processing, review and approval of
         subdivisions in compliance with adopted Utah State Code, Ordinance proposed
         by Riverton City

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, discussed an amendment to the subdivision
ordinance, which aligns with changes in the Utah State Code mandating a standardized process
for reviewing residential subdivisions. The changes include setting maximum review cycles for
staff, limiting the time for each review cycle, and specifying the land use authority (Planning
Commission in Riverton's case). The ordinance also outlines the preliminary and final approval
processes for residential developments. While the adoption brings the City's code in line with
state requirements, there may be additional changes pending based on ongoing legislative
sessions. The recommended motion was for the adoption of the revised subdivision ordinance
as dictated by Utah State Code.

Councilmember McDougal asked if the only decision making power the City Council would have
is the original development decision or preliminary zoning. Mr. Lethbridge said this was correct.
The Council's involvement in development decisions would primarily pertain to zoning changes,
while the Planning Commission and City staff would handle the review and approval process for
subdivisions according to the standards set forth in the ordinance. The Council would still have
authority over any changes to the code itself, but the day-to-day approval process for
development packages would be managed by the Planning Commission and staff.

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained that according to the development agreement act, any
amendments to land use within a development agreement would require approval from Riverton
City through the same administrative or legislative process as other changes. He clarified that
this process would involve a subdivision process and a public hearing before both the Planning
Commission and the Council prior to execution.

Mr. Carter said this legislation is not cause for celebration. He believes it doesn't significantly
benefit the Council's interactions with developers. He highlighted that the legislation affects the
staff's ability to address various issues that arise during land development processes, such as
ensuring community safety and welfare. However, he clarified that the legislation doesn't alter
the Council's handling of matters when brought before them appropriately.

Councilmember McDougal expressed frustration with the state's involvement in local-level
decisions, particularly regarding land development. He emphasized the importance of decisions
being made by those closest to the affected residents, and expressed disappointment that
current regulations appear to favor developers over residents.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.

         Resolution No. 24-19 – Conveying Real Property from Riverton City to the Riverton
         Redevelopment Agency

Mr. Carter provided an overview of a transaction involving the Council’s adopting a resolution in
April 2022 which established the Community Reinvestment project area for Redwood Road. He
explained that Riverton City currently holds title to certain properties outlined in red in the
designated area. To facilitate development in the region, he proposed transferring title of the
properties to the Redevelopment Agency (RDA). This transfer would enable the RDA to better
collaborate with the development community and potentially initiate development projects. If no
project materializes before the Community Reinvestment project area expires, the title can
revert to Riverton City.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.

Councilmember Pierucci MOVED that the City Council approve Resolution No. 24-19 –
Conveying Real Property from Riverton City to the Riverton Redevelopment Agency.
Councilmember Buroker SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the
motion. Mayor Staggs called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: Buroker-yes,
Haymond-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes, and Pierucci-yes. The motion passed unanimously.


         Resolution No. 24-17 – Budget Amendment

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, provided a summary of the budget adjustments
included in the Council's packet, noting that the majority involved cleanup as the chart of
accounts was transitioned. Some budgets needed to be moved to the correct locations within
the new chart of accounts. Additionally, there were new items, notably the vehicle leases
discussed during the strategic planning meeting. The proposal involves having Fund 45 pay for
the vehicles instead of securing outside financing, with the police department leasing the
vehicles back from Fund 45.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.

Councilmember McDougal MOVED that the City Council approve Resolution No. 24-17 –
Budget Amendment. Councilmember McCay SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called
for discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows:
Buroker-yes, Haymond-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes, and Pierucci-yes. The motion passed
unanimously.
