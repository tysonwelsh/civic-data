Public Comment No comments TransPlan40 (RTP) Modification - Shawn Seager The proposed modifications to the TransPlan40, is to move 3 projects that are funded in Phase 2 to unfunded in Phase 1. Projects are being moved in anticipation of getting construction funding from the legislature.  RTP #51 I-15/Orem 800 South Interchange: the project is designed to be built with the BRT and could include a new pedestrian bridge and off ramp improvements to University Parkway.  RTP #56 I-15/Springville 1600 South/Spanish Fork 2700 North Interchange: proposed interchange creates better connectivity to high growth areas of Mapleton, Spanish Fork, Springville, and Spanish Fork commercial area.  RTP #60 Springville 1600 South/Spanish Fork 2700 North: the project widen a 2-lane road between Spanish Fork Main Street and SR-51 to 5-lanes. It extends the corridor between SR-51 and US-89 creating a direct I-15 access for Mapleton. Roads needs to done at the same time and RTP #56 to complete the road. Additionally, a proposed new unfunded project, Provo Sub Railroad Realignment, is a new freight project that needs to be added to Phase 1 with anticipation that full funding will be available in the near future. This project realigns and consolidates two railroad tracks into one in Springville, Mapleton, and Spanish Fork area. The two corridors are no longer needed, due to modern locomotives. It will also eliminate multiple at grade highway railroad crossing and reduces the need for a new bridge that is part of RTP #60. The TransPlan40 will be amendment when funding is secured for an unfunded project in Phase 1 and an air quality determination has happen. The TIP priority list will remain in its current order. At the August 1st TAC Committee meeting it was recommended that Regional Planning approve the modifications to TransPlan40. Mayor John Curtis moved that the MPO Regional Planning Committee approve the modifications to TransPlan40 as presented. These include moving 3 projects (RTP #51 I-15/Orem 800 South Interchange, RTP #56 I-15/Springville 1600 South/Spanish Fork 2700 North Interchange, and RTP#60 Springville 1600 South/Spanish Fork 2700 North) from Phase 2 funded to Phase 1 unfunded and add one new project (Provo Sub Railroad Realignment) to Phase 1 unfunded in the plan. If funding is identified for any unfunded project, a full air quality amendment will be required. Mayor Bert Wilson seconded the motion, the carried unanimously. Unified Planning Work Program Amendment (UPWP) - Shawn Seager The Work Program is Regional Planning annual budget. It illustrates to our members, Federal Highway Administration, Federal Transit Administration, and the general public where our planning resources are allocated and describes the planned work, budget, and funding sources. Funds combined into the Consolidate Planning Grant (CPG) which is administrated by UDOT who has the responsibility of managing and monitoring how the funds are used. This year UDOT Accounting has requested any CPG Carry Forward Funds from previous years be shown in the UPWP in order to be included in the MPO's Federal Aid Agreement with UDOT. This year's CPG Carry Forward balance is $435,715. The Carry Forward balance is a result of funding 12 Full Time Equivalents in the UPWP over several previous years but only staffing 11 until this last year. Also in previous year of an under spent balance in other categories of UPWP that carry forward in the CPG. Staff recommends amending the FY-17 UPWP to increase Category H "Planning Opportunities Contingency" by $435,715. At the August 1st TAC Committee meeting it was recommended that Regional Planning approve the amendment to the UPWP. Mayor J.H. Hadfield moved to amend the MAG MPO FY-2017 Unified Planning Work Program to increase Category H “Planning Opportunities Contingency” by $435,715. Mayor Bert Wilson seconded the motion. The motion carried unanimously. Provo’s Municipal Transportation Utility Fee - Mayor John Curtis In the past Provo received B&C road funds for road maintenance, but those dollars only go so far. Provo City have been bonding to pay for ongoing maintenance, which has caused the bond debt to accumulate. Property tax does not spread the burden equitably across all Provo residents, businesses and not-for- profits. The Municipal Transportation Utility Fee covers all entities, even Provo City facilities, government facilities, and nonprofit organizations will all pay their share. The Municipal Transportation Utility Fee is another funding tool for local governments to fund their local road maintenance and operations. Studies indicate that every dollar spend for maintenance you save $7-$10 that would otherwise need to be spend to rebuild. For more information contact Mayor Curtis, link to video https://vimeo.com/72859312 HB 60 – Majority of the Local Motor Fuel Tax Increase Going to 11 Rural Counties - Andrew Jackson In 2015 when HB 362 passed there was a fixed calculation issued for counties and cities share of Class B and Class C road funds. HB-362 changed the gas tax for $0.245 per gallon to 12% sales tax on the average rack price-the equivalent of $0.294 per gallon. Cities, towns, and counties receive 30% of the motor fuel fund, Class B&C, based on the 50% population ratio and 50% weighted lane mile ratio (1 paved road mile = 5 weighted lane miles and 1 other road (gravel, unpaved, dirt) = 2 weighted lane miles). In FY-2015 cities received 64% of the Class B&C fund and counties received 36%, because cities have a higher population and paved roads. It also modified the weighted lane mile calculation and the B&C hold harmless formula, a formula which originated in 1997 to benefit rural entities. Held harmless counties: Beaver, Box Elder, Emery, Grand, Garfield, Kane, Millard, Piute, Rich, San Juan, Wayne and Rockville City. HH counties have 124,674 people or 4.2% population and have 36% of all lane miles and 25% of all weighted lanes but only 13% of the lane miles are paved. The ULCT projection was approximately 17% increase in B&C funds for cities and counties. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning August 4, 2016 Page 5 UAC initiated HB-60 to allegedly correct the calculation error of HB-362. It was believed HB-60 impact would reconcile the language in HB-362 with the agreed expectation of published gas tax revenue projections. However, the HB-60 hold harmless formula shifted $8.5-8.8 million projected city and town revenues to counties, primarily to the 11 rural hold harmless counties. Now the increase in B&C funds are approximately 8.6% for cities and 35% for counties (81.7% for HH counties and 8.8% non-HH counties). Currently, all B&C funds are on hold. The legislature is planning a special session this fall to correct the language in both bills. For more information contact Andrew or Cameron Diehl, ULCT.
