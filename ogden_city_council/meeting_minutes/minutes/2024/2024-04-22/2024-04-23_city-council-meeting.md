Proposed Ordinance 2024-10 recognizing revenues from various sources in
the General Fund, Capital Improvement Project, Utility, Medical Services,
Fleet and Facilities, Information Technology, Major Grant, and
miscellaneous grant funds
         A memo from the City Comptroller regarding proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2023-2024 mid-year budget amendments came
before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposed amendment totals $7,508,720 and recognizes revenues from
various sources in the General, Capital Improvement (CIP), Water, Sanitary Sewer, Refuse, Storm Sewer, Medical Services, Fleet
and Facilities, Information Technology, Major Grants, and Miscellaneous Grant Funds. Sources of revenue vary from use of fund
balance, interest income, sale of assets, grants, and internal billing. Expenditures in the General Fund are for debt service,
equipment, and wages and benefits, as well as an interfund transfer. Appropriations in the CIP Fund are for various projects.
Excess funds from completed CIPs are also reappropriated to other projects. Appropriations in the four utility funds provide
funding for master plan projects. Appropriations in the remaining funds are for mission-related expenditures.
          Deputy Comptroller Jensen summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to detail the source of
funds and impacts to revenues and appropriations included in the City budget. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the
information packet.

a fire; he noted the cost seems high given that the vehicle was insured. Management Services Director Stout stated that the City
will eventually be reimbursed a portion of the vehicle replacement cost by its insurance company; the City will ultimately pay
approximately $25,000 for the vehicle replacement and the remaining amount will be covered by insurance.

Proposed Ordinance 2024-11 to amend the Lynn Community Plan to
include language that recognizes the area’s historic structures, features,
and properties, and considerations for protection when development occurs
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed community plan
amendments pertaining to historic preservation in the Lynn Community Plan Area came before the Council for consideration. The
memo stated in May of 2022, Council member Hyer submitted a Council Initiative to update the Lynn Community Plan. Council
member Hyer’s intent was to add language to the plan that would state that the City values the historic nature of the area and that
future land use decisions should consider the historic structures and events of the community. After the Initiative submission, a
letter was sent by the Council requesting a recommendation from the Planning Commission on the suggested changes. City
Planning staff and the Planning Commission have worked to develop language meeting the intent of the Initiative. Planning staff
have also performed outreach with both a community meeting and a community survey to receive input on the proposal. The
Planning Commission has forwarded a recommendation to the City Council with changes to the Lynn Community Plan that would
satisfy the Council Initiative. The proposed changes provide new language regarding historic preservation in the area, particularly
the neighborhood around 2nd Street west of Wall Avenue. The proposed language also provides recommendations for local
historic designations, monuments, and markers that would further the work the residents of the area have done regarding historic
preservation.
          Senior Planner Rypien summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
implications of the proposed community plan amendments. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information
packet.
          Council member Choberka inquired as to the resources it takes to study this type of issue in preparation for such a
historic preservation recommendation. Mr. Rypien stated it has taken Planning staff and the Commission approximately a year,
following the initiation of the matter via Council Initiative in 2022. The process could likely be consolidated to six months if
necessary. This led to discussion of the steps involved in the process to consider a community plan amendment and the level of
public engagement involved in the process.
          Council member Hyer indicated it is critical to protect the history of this area of the City; he highlighted some of the
historic elements of the area and the energy of the residents surrounding historic preservation and concluded that he supports this
community plan amendment.
          Vice Chair White asked if it would be appropriate to assign open space zoning to a potential park area in the community
plan area, rather than maintaining a residential zoning designation. Mr. Rypien stated the Council could consider that, but that
would require action independent of the community plan amendment. The late property owner was interested in designating the
property as a historic park, but the current owner would need to petition for open space zoning. The community plan is a guiding
document and provides recommendations regarding future possible uses of the plan area.
           Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-11, entitled:
                    “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden City General Plan by amending the Lynn Community

Proposed Rezone at 1450 Gibson Avenue. Petition to rezone two parcels at
1450 Gibson Avenue and 1483 Wall Avenue from M-1 (Limited Industrial) to
C-3 (Regional Commercial) to allow for multiple-family dwellings
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed rezone at 1450 Gibson
Avenue came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Darcel Stucki has submitted a petition to rezone two
parcels, totaling 6.37 acres located at 1450 Gibson Ave and 1483 Wall Ave from M-1 (Limited Industrial) to C-3 (Regional
Commercial). The petitioner’s intent is to establish zoning on the property that allows for multiple-family dwellings.
          Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and stated the petitioner, in their project narrative, explains that their
goal is for “multi-housing” similar to the apartments and townhomes along 17 th Street. One parcel is owned by Jean W. Shaum
Trust and Mr. Stucki is the authorized representative. The other parcel involved in the request is owned by Utah Department of
Transportation (UDOT), who remains neutral on the request. City Planning staff have made a recommendation to the Planning
Commission that the proposal be denied with the finding that the proposal is not consistent with the General Plan goals and
policies and the Gibson Community Plan. In February of 2024, the Planning Commission held a public hearing regarding this
petition. During this meeting a member of the public stated they felt that Gibson Avenue is not currently built to support
additional multiple family housing in this area. The Planning Commission forwarded a unanimous recommendation of denial,
based on the finding that the proposal does not protect the integrity of industrial uses, nor does it provide buffers between housing
and industrial uses. The Planning Commission had additional findings, stating that they felt it was important to retain existing
industrial lands such as the subject property to promote economic development in the community, that the City has adequate land
zoned to meet future multi-family housing needs, and that rezoning this property would create potential conflicts with existing
industrial uses in the area.
          Council member Choberka referenced a note in the meeting materials about the loss of other manufacturing land in the
area; she inquired as to the location of that land, to which Mr. Brierley answered land surrounding the 24th Street/Interstate-15
freeway interchange. Council member Choberka inquired as to the date that the subject property was rezoned to a manufacturing
use, to which Mr. Brierley answered the property has been zoned industrial since the 1960’s. Property along Wall Avenue was
rezoned approximately six years ago in conjunction with a General Plan update.
          Council member Hyer communicated his feelings that residential uses should not be permitted uses in the City’s
commercial zones; he suggested Planning staff develop a draft ordinance to address this matter in advance of the General Plan
update project.
          Chair Richey invited input from the applicant.
          Mr. Stucki provided a brief history of the property, which was owned by his late mother, Jean Shaum. He received a
postcard regarding the City’s consideration of development of the Gibson Community Redevelopment District and he
subsequently met with Planning staff regarding the potential future uses of the property; he communicated to them that he was
interested in selling the property and was interested in future plans for the property. Planning staff indicated that most of the area
had been zoned for commercial uses (C-3 zoning), but the Gibson Community documents indicated an interest in supporting the
renovation of desirable existing buildings and the removal and redevelopment of outdated and vacant existing buildings,
strengthening Ogden’s employment and taxbase by creating locations for businesses to relocate to or expand within, improving
public infrastructure and connections, providing open space and meaningful and appropriate connections, and facilitating the
development and enhancement of single and multi-family residential units. He read other policies and goals from the
Redevelopment District documents and indicated that the Council unanimously approved that document, though Council members
Graf and Myers were not members of the Council at that time. He noted Planning staff was interested in partnering with someone
to help fulfill those visions for the community, but they acknowledged that a recently built storage facility was something that they
had not envisioned for the community. He stated he has spoken with several other property owners and developers in the area and
eventually received two purchase offers for the property: one from the developer of the townhomes and apartments on 17 th Street
and Wall Avenue, and another from another property owner that successfully rezoned their property for a townhome development.
Those potential buyers approached the City about rezoning the subject property to C-3 and they were told that the rezone would
not be allowed; they backed out of their purchase offers as a result. He stated that at that time, he reread all materials that were
considered and approved by both the Planning Commission and City Council regarding the Gibson Community Plan area, and he
found that his property and the UDOT properties are the only properties that are not zoned to allow for the multi-housing use
desired. He stated he is only interested in selling the property and that will not change regardless of the outcome of the Council’s
decision regarding his application; however, he is concerned that the City is being inconsistent relative to zoning designations and
the uses for the property will be limited. A storage unit development is not what his father and mother would have envisioned for
their property, but that is one of the only uses that would be permitted under the current zoning designation. He discussed
surrounding land uses, specifically salvage yards and auto towing services, and expounded on the goals and policies included in
the Gibson Community Plan and concluded that the decision to deny his application would be contrary to the Plan. The
recommendation to deny from the Planning Commission is not in line with the Community Plan.
          Chair Richey invited Mr. Brierley to respond to Mr. Stucki’s comments. Mr. Brierley indicated that Mr. Stucki’s reading
of the Gibson Community Plan is accurate; there are plans to rezone to R-1-5 as well as C-3 in the area surrounding his property.
Zoning changes from M-1 to C-3 have occurred and he believes that it is possible to make the argument that rezoning the back
half of the UDOT property to C-3 would be consistent with the Plan and surrounding land uses. However, there have not been
similar changes along Gibson Avenue and the Community Plan supports the continuance of the M-1 zoning on the Stucki
property.
          Mr. Stucki referenced a map of the Community Plan area and identified the area surrounding his property that is zoned
C-3; he reiterated his position that he believes there is support for zoning his property C-3 rather than maintaining the M-1 zoning.
In 2016, when the Community Plan was approved, the entire area was residential in nature.
          Council member Choberka inquired as to when the C-3 zoning decisions were made, to which Mr. Brierley responded
between 2016 and 2018. Council member Choberka stated this is a very complicated issue in her opinion; she understands both

          Council member Graf added that he recalls working on the Gibson Community Plan as a Planning Commissioner; there

          Council member Choberka stated she appreciates the history of Fresenius’s growth in the area, but she does not want to

Proposed Ordinance 2024-13 creating a new Flex Industrial Zone to
accommodate a range of industrial and commercial uses that are limited in
intensity but require flexibility and space to adapt as a business evolves
            A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed creation of a Flex Industrial
Zone came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Asilia Investments initially submitted a petition to amend the
A-C (Airport Commercial) zone. Those amendments would have modified and clarified uses in the A-C zone to allow a wider
range of non-aeronautical commercial uses. The A-C zone along with the A-I (Airport Industrial) zone make up the zoning for
operations on and adjacent to the airport. While the changes were given a recommendation of approval by the Planning
Commission, concerns raised by the Council led the petitioners and the City to amend the proposal from changes to the A-C zone
to a proposal of a new zone altogether. The amended proposal is to create a new Flex Industrial zone that would allow flexible
commercial and industrial/manufacturing space. This would be a new zone within the manufacturing classification and would
have some similarities to the M-1 while allowing some additional flexibility in the zone’s allowable uses and interior warehousing
and manufacturing standards. The focus of the initial petition was to develop property on the south end of the airport adjacent to
the Kemp buildings. The zoning on that property is A-C. While the updated proposal is to create a new zone, the petitioner’s goal
is still to develop the property but under the new zone rather than a modified A-C zone. If the Flex Industrial zone gets approval
from the Council, it is anticipated that a petition will be filed to rezone the subject property.
            Senior Planner Rypien summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed
ordinance, with a focus on permitted and condition uses identified in the ordinance. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety,
see the information packet for the meeting.
          Council member Hyer asked if there were any provision in the ordinance that would provide perpetual approval of uses
that serve or support aviation uses at the Airport. Mr. Rypien deferred to the City Attorney to answer that question; he is not aware
of a tool that could be used to provide perpetual approval of any use. Planning Manager Brierley indicated that once a use is
legally established, it can remain on the property as long as it continues to operate. The City can consider a change but would be
required to provide a sufficient amount of time for the property owner to recoup their investment in the land before converting its
use.
          Chair Richey invited input from the petitioner.
          Jake Jackson, Asilia Investments, thanked the Council for their consideration of this application over the last several
months. He has worked to address the questions and concerns that have been asked about the zone and wished to assure the
Council that Asilia will not move to rezone Airport property if the zone is adopted. He feels that creation of the zone will benefit
Ogden, including the area around the Airport and in other areas of the City. A flex industrial zone will provide small businesses
with an opportunity to grow and be part of an entrepreneurial hub.
           Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-13, entitled:

Proposed Resolution 2024-2 authorizing the issuance and sale of not more
than $82,100,000 aggregate principal amount of Subordinated Sewer,
Water and Storm Sewer Revenue Bonds, Series 2024; and related matters
         A memo from the Management Services Department regarding a proposed parameters resolution pertaining to a utility
revenue bond for a 36-inch waterline in Ogden Canyon came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the project
will involve several funding sources and will include proposed water rate increases to pay for the debt service. The project,
Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) Project Number WU003, is estimated to cost approximately $100 million and includes not only
the water line in the Ogden Canyon but the 36-inch concrete lined steel pipe running under Pineview Reservoir from the City’s
wellfields as well. The proposed funding sources include $34.3 million from a State Revolving Loan Fund, $42.6 million from a
federal Water Infrastructure Finance and Innovation Act (WIFIA) loan, $10 million from State appropriations, and $12 million
from City American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funding.
         Laura Lewis, City’s bond counsel from Lewis Robertson and Burningham (LRB) summarized the memo and stated the
bond parameters resolution is required as the City is incurring debt and must comply with state bonding requirements.
              • Bond Parameters Summary:
                        o Discount – 3 percent
                        o Term – 40 Years
                        o Rates – Not to exceed 6 percent
                                ▪ State Revolving Loan Fund – 1 percent
                                ▪ WIFIA Loan – 5 percent
                        o Maximum Par Amount - $82,100,000
                        o Total Estimated Debt Service
                                ▪ State Loan - $39,965,000
                                ▪ WIFIA Loan - $75,060,000
         Ms. Lewis concluded that if the Council adopts the parameters resolution, a public hearing will be set for June 11, with
closing on the bond scheduled for late June or early July. She noted that she has reviewed current interest rates for this type of
project and actually recommends that the referenced five percent interest rate be adjusted to six percent to allow for some
flexibility in the event that interest rates increase between now and the closing date.
