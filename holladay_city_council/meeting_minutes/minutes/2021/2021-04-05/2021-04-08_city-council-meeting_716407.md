VI.     Consideration of Resolution 2021-08 Authorizing the Termination of an Interlocal
        Agreement with Salt Lake City Regarding the Maintenance of Salt Lake City Property.
Mr. Godfrey reported that the City entered into an Interlocal Agreement with Salt Lake City in 2004
that dealt with the 2300 East and 4500 South and Community Garden parcels. When they informed

interest in continuing to maintain the 4500 South and 2300 East parcel. They decided that the best

        a.      I-215/I-80 Presentation.
Becky Stromness, UDOT Project Manager and Courtney Bovee were introduced. Both work on
public involvement for the construction project. The intent is to replace the pavement on the
interstates. One portion will include I-80 from 1300 East to 2300 East and the other portion near
Holladay will involve I-215 between 3300 South and 4500 South. Both stretches will get new
concrete pavement, new bridges, and new pavement. The primary concern is a batch plant being
located near 3900 South. It was reported that the contractor will not have the concrete batch plant
on-site and will use one near the airport. Construction was expected to last all this year and into next
year with completion expected by the end of 2022. Three lanes will be open during the day with
nightly lane closures anticipated between 9:00 p.m. and 5:00 a.m.

Action was being taken to reach residents, area businesses, and drivers throughout the project. The
expectation was for construction to begin mid to late May. UDOT will work with staff to post
information on social media and events will be held to address State-wide coverage. Real-time text
alerts had been a good way to reach drivers and address specific or unplanned delays. There will also
be periodic email updates intended to focus on bigger milestones. UDOT’s 24-hour hotline was
provided.

         d.       Discussion Ranked-Choice Voting.
City Recorder, Stephanie Carlson, described Ranked-Choice Voting and explained that rather than
filling out a ballot for one person, the top three choices are ranked. When ballots are counted, the
first choice is identified. If 50% of the ballots mark one candidate, that candidate automatically moves
forward. If there is no candidate eliminated in the first round, they continue on until a candidate is
chosen. This method works well in cities that have at-large council members because there could be
six or seven people running for office. The benefits were identified as follows:

   •   Eliminates the need for a primary election;
   •   Provides greater voter engagement;
   •   Reduces negative campaigning due to a shorter time period;
   •   A vote counts toward the candidate the voter wants; and
   •   Saves taxpayer dollars.

Ms. Carlson reported that the cities of Vineyard and Payson utilized Ranked-Choice Voting at their
election two years ago and found it to be very easy with low error rates and higher voter turnout.
Ms. Carlson reported that the County has purchased the equipment and are being trained on it. A
decision needs to be made since notice needs to be published in the newspaper on May 1 at which
time the City will specify what seats are open for this year’s election and the type of voting. With

Ranked-Choice Voting, the Candidacy Declaration Period would be August 10-17 rather than June
1-7. The cost of traditional voting would not exceed $41,775. That number would be reduced if there
is no primary election. The cost of Ranked-Choice Voting would be $25,289 but will depend on the
number of cities that share the $10,000 licensing fee. Ms. Chamness commented that there will also
be associated education costs since it will be a new program for residents.

Ms. Carlson reported that currently, the cities of Draper, Lehi, Springville, Riverton, Payson, and

Mayor Dahle expressed his support for Ranked-Choice Voting and considered it to be a less costly

        e.     Continued Property Tax Discussion.
Mayor Dahle reported that the Council reached a consensus on the $8 million bond and the property
tax revenue that would be required based on several variables. Ms. Chamness stated that there were
questions raised at the last meeting that she was unable to answer. As a result Financial Advisor,
Fred Philpot from Lewis, Young, Robertson & Burningham was invited to explore the comparisons
further. Mr. Philpot explained that they are at a crossroads in multiple scenarios, which presents
challenges. The most recent scenarios involved a 50% and 75% increase with and without bonding.
Comparisons were made in previous scenarios with regard to a similar percentage. The differences
between the assumptions and scenarios pertained to the road costs that were included.

Mr. Philpot reported that they changed their assumptions as they relate to sales tax revenue and growth
and added to the model. That infused more cash into the proforma. They also released more fund
balance into the equation from previous scenarios. Council Member Fotheringham stated that they
moved toward the benefits of the option with the greatest impact but found that their revenue was
stronger and they were willing to frontload some of the projects. He did not presume that they were
also adjusting the end result objective, which was to fix all of the roads. Mr. Philpot explained that
they are equivalent outputs with one clarification. He explained that these scenarios utilize more of
the CIP fund balance than was assumed previously. The various scenarios were described.

Mr. Philpot reported that it will be balanced against a 2025 target to ensure that they have
approximately a 25% fund balance in the General Fund and $3 million in the Capital Projects Fund.
Currently, there is approximately $5 million in the General Fund as a fund balance at the end of the
year. Mayor Dahle considered it more palatable than raising property taxes by $30 per month for a
$750,000 home. He preferred a $150 per year increase while still achieving the biggest impact.

Mr. Philpot suggested that it be evaluated on an annual basis. Going forward there are substantial
capital needs that have been delayed. Mayor Dahle asked if the Council was willing to start a
conversation with the community at the 50% level for the City of Holladay. With the conversion,
