4. Public Hearing and possible action regarding proposed amendments to clarify 
the geographic boundaries of the Eastern Summit County Planning District and 
the residency requirements for membership on the Eastern Summit County 
Planning Commission – Jennifer Strader, County Planner  
 
Planner Jennifer Strader said Staff is presenting amendments to the Eastern Summit 
Development Code regarding Planning Commission residency and eligibility.  This 
discussion began in 2013 when concerns were raised that municipal residents were not 
allowed to sit on the Planning Commission.  At that time, the County Attorney’s office 
determined that when the Planning Commission was created in 1995, it was not formed 
as a township, which would have residency limitations.   
 
Concerns have again been raised and the Council has again decided that allowing 
municipal residents to sit on the Planning Commission would be beneficial.  They have 
directed Staff to prepare amendments to the Development Code clarifying this policy.  
They requested that the number of municipal residents that are allowed to sit on the 
Planning Commission be limited to three so as to not constitute a majority.   
 
Staff suggests the Planning Commission conduct a public hearing and vote to 
recommend approval of the amendments to the County Council based on the Findings 
of Facts and Conclusions of Law as found in the Staff report.   
 
Commissioner Henrie said when this came to the Commission in 2013 he thought they 
voted to limit the number to two.  Chair Ure asked if this had ever been codified.

which municipalities can have a presence on the Commission?  Commissioner Clyde

different municipalities.  Commissioner Henrie said there is a lot of talent within the 
municipalities.

1. Discussion regarding possible updates to the Eastern Summit County Zoning 
Maps from Wanship to Oakley – Planning Department Staff 
 
Community Development Director Patrick Putt thanked the public for coming to the 
meeting.  Over time there have been small changes to the Code.  These changes have 
added up to create inconsistencies and problems in the Code.  Over the past 1 ½ years 
the Commission has been working on coming up with some better solutions.  One 
solution was to create definitions so that everyone understands exactly what is meant.   
 
The processes and the required steps have been reviewed.  They have come up with 
solutions that have streamlined the processes while keeping the needed protections.  
They have looked at the zones in Eastern Summit County.  They are trying to determine 
which zones are and are not working.  Do they need any new zones?   
 
Director Putt said on the wall is a map with zoning ideas for Oakley and Kamas.  The 
zone review started in Henefer and has moved southward.  This map will be used to 
help them gain the needed information to create a final product.  Director Putt said 
these maps will be posted online after this meeting.  Woodland will be discussed at the 
next meeting.   
 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
April 2, 2015 
Page 12 of 20 
 
Staff and the Commission have been considering the current zones.  In addition to the 
agricultural zones, they are proposing some zones which will allow for other uses 
through a rezone.  Currently there aren’t many different zones to choose from.     
 
The Commission realizes this community has a long history in agriculture.  Times are 
changing and they believe that in the future, they will be looking at a lot of growth.  The 
strategy is to develop in a way that works for everyone, including the existing and 
future property owners.  This is why they want to provide opportunities beyond the 
current established agricultural zones.  The new zones being considered are:   
 
1. An agricultural zone (AG-1).  This zone allows one unit per acre.  This has been 
mapped on a number of the County roads at 500 feet.  They have tried to avoid 
creating non-conforming lots.  This may replace the highway corridor zone.     
 
2. The AG-6 zone, which is a new zone.  This zone has a one-acre home site with 
five acres of agriculture.  This configuration would allow the property to stay in 
greenbelt.   The zone has the potential to be a problem solver.  In Eastern 
Summit County, 60% to 75% of the AP-40 zone is non-conforming in size.  If the 
AG-6 zone is applied in the right locations, the problem of non-conformity will 
mostly be solved.    
 
3. The AG-100 zone may be changed to an AG-80 zone.  This small change would 
allow zoning to move in 40 acre increments.   
 
Beyond the base agricultural zoning, there would be new zoning opportunities:  
 
4. The rural residential zone (RR) would be one-unit per one-acre.  The difference 
between this zone and the AG-1 zone is that it will have a smaller list of 
agricultural uses.  The minimum lot size might be as small as .75 acre.  It might 
have horses and a barn, but not a mink shed.  
5. The residential subdivision (RS) zone would be a single-family development 
with little to no agricultural uses.  This could be three-units per acre.  It would 
require street improvement, public water, and public waste water.   
6. The recreation commercial (RC) zone.  Blue Sky Ranch is a recent example of this 
type of zoning.  Other possible areas could be around Rockport or Echo.   
7. A rural residential overlay zone will be used if someone wanted to proceed with 
a master plan project.  Clustering may or may not make sense in these 
developments.    
 
Director Putt said they have put these possible zones on the map to help the 
Commission, Staff, and the public see if they may work.  The following criteria have 
been used to help the Commission evaluate where certain zones might be applied.   
 
1. To the greatest extent possible, will this correct the non-conforming lot situation?   
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
April 2, 2015 
Page 13 of 20 
 
2. What type of development is currently on the ground?  
3. What is happening in the nearby municipality?   
Director Putt explained the State requires municipalities to have an area to 
accommodate future growth.  This is called an annexation declaration area (ADA).  
These areas may or may not come into the cities at some point.  Until they are taken 
into a City’s jurisdiction, they are still part of the unincorporated County.  Director 
Putt said they are trying to do a good job of communicating.  They don’t want to 
surprise the municipalities.   
4. What is the geography?  Is it steep?  Is it wet?   
They don’t want to put zoning in the wrong place where it would create problems. 
5. What is the current infrastructure?  What is the current road situation, and what 
will it likely be?  Are there public water systems?  Public waste water systems?   
 
Director Putt said ultimately these changes will end up at the County Council level, 
which will make the final decision.  Mr. Woolstenhulme asked if they are proposing to 
include any of the incorporated areas into the County’s zoning.  Director Putt said they
