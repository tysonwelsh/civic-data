Proposed Ordinance 2021-21 amending Chapters 1, 3 and 5 of Title 8 to
revise provisions related to airport leases and airport fees.
                   A memo from the City Attorney's Office regarding proposed amendments to the City's Airport ordinance came
                   before the Council for consideration. The memo stated City Administration is recommending amendments to
                   Chapters 1, 3 and 5 of Title 8 of the Ogden Municipal Code dealing with defmitions, airport leases, and fees
                   respectively.
          City Attorney Williams summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the proposed
airport fee and lease amendments; fee amendments include the following:
               •   Increases landing fees from $0.75 to $1.25 per 1,000 pounds.
               •   Increases fuel flowage fees from $0.07 per gallon to $0.09 per gallon and eliminates the air carrier discount
                   of fuel flowage fees.
               •   Increases monthly aircraft tie down fees from $35 to $50 for aircraft under 12,500 pounds, and from $50 to
                   $100 for aircraft over 12,500 pounds.
               •   Increases Secure Identification Display Area (SIDA) access fees from $50.00 to $100.00 for two years.
               •   Increases overnight parking fees from $4.00 per day to $5.00 per day.
               •   Establishes a $20.00 monthly fee for usage of the airport office for pickup and delivery o( letters or
                   packages.
               •   Establishes a transaction fee of $500.00 for a new airport ground lease, ground lease renewal, and
                   assignment or transfer of tenancy.
               •   Establishes a transaction fee of $100.00 for review and approval of any sublease agreement.
          Mr. Williams then noted Administration proposes expansion of the duration of ground leases; the cunent ordinance
provides for hanger construction lease tenns of 15 years for a private hangar owner, 20 years for a commercial operator, 25 years
for a fixed-base operator, and a possible five-year renewal. Airport staff has detennined these are insufficient lengths to attract
significant airport development and the Airport is in need of a new standard. Staff has looked to the Federal Aviation Authority
(FAA) guidance to determine appropriate adjustments and they found the following:
               •    Sufficient Years to Amortize Tenant's Capital Investment.
               •   May offer ground rent below fair market value to facilitate amortization.
               •   30-40 years recommended, over 50 years requires FAA approval.
               •   End of Lease -Airport exercises Reversionary Rights.
                        o Airport takes possession of land and improvements.
                        o Rent the land and any improvements for fair market value.

          Airport staff recommends that for new construction, the ground lease should be sufficient to amortize a tenant's capital
investment, or a 40-year maximum term. At the end of the lease, the Airport can exercise reversionary rights by taking possession
of the land and improvements and renting the land for fair market value through a facility lease. For a facility lease after a ground
lease expansion, improvements would be owned by the City through reversion or other action. Leases will be at fair market value
determined by commercially reasonable methods and may vary by age, size, location, fmish, condition, etc. Preference will be
given to prior ground lease or facility lease tenants. The proposed ordinance amendments are consistent with lease agreements;
ground can be leased for a term of years (five to 25), and at the end of the lease, the landlord takes possession of the premises via
reversionary rights. A tenant may remove improvements within 60 days after the end of the lease term and if the tenant fails to
remove the improvements, ownership of such improvements will revert to the Airport as the landlord. Tenants will have the
benefits of their lease agreements. These proposed ordinance amendments are consistent with FAA guidance and Mr. Williams
presented an example of this type of adjustment at the Shreveport Airport; at that installation, the FAA found violations of grant
assurances whereby tenants were given endless ground leases, the airport failed to exercise reversionary rights and take possession
of land and improvements, and the airport failed to charge fair market rent at the end of a lease term. Due to these conditions, the
FAA ordered a corrective action plan for the.Shreveport Airport. Additionally, the proposed ordinance and lease amendments are
consistent with State law; Mr. Williams referenced a Utah Supreme Court case concerning Price Development versus Orem City.
The Court's ruling was that "public property is held in trust for the public and may not be disposed of other than "in good faith
and for adequate consideration." (i.e., fair market value); and "Utah's constitution and statutes must be satisfied, and they require
that the public's money and property be carefully tended, lest public and political pressures persuade local government to transfer
it into private hands without receiving a fair net benefit in exchange." Based on this case law, the City should exercise its
reversionary rights, with that action being held in trust for the public. Mr. Williams emphasized that the Price Development
opinion regarding the public trust was most striking to him; in speaking with the current and past Airport Managers, he has
learned that most of the decisions they made in regard to lease terms was that they were worried about the public and political
pressure from tenants at the Airport. The Court has specifically cautioned Airports to not give in to political and public pressure;
rather, the City is responsible to protect the public trust by exercising contractual reversionary rights.
          Council member Hyer asked if a fixed base operator would be able to secure an extension of their lease if they were
planning to make a significant investment in their hangar. Mr. Williams indicated the Airport Manager would not be able to make
the decision to extend a lease; rather, the Mayor has the ability to exercise his discretion as the property manager to negotiate
something outside of the parameters of the ordinance amendments in the case of substantial capital improvements.

taking. If the ordinance is passed and a government taking action is triggered, arguably every hanger must be bought by the City.
