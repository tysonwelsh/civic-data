         E. Mayor and Council Reports
Council Member Patrick Harris discussed Craig White’s retirement from the sewer board.
Council Member Brad Marlor spoke at a Rotary Club function honoring students with
scholarships yesterday where he spoke on being involved with small thing that really matter. He
was able to have some plaques engraved with a related Mother Teresa quote as a gift to each of
the students and teachers. There was no Architectural Review Committee Meeting in the last
couple weeks.
Council Member Tamara Zander said April 5 she was invited to speak by the Salt Lake Board of
Realtors Government Affairs Committee. She was asked to speak about her perspective and
understanding about the future of the west side of our city, including the annexation. She asked
the Rio Tinto leadership to join her, and Wayne Bradshaw was able to come and speak with her
to the group and help answer questions. They were able to share lots of great information with
those in attendance, including maps, and it was well received.
Council Member Jason McGuire had a meeting with the Arts Council where some great ideas
were shared and asked Art’s Coordinator Tiffany Parker, to put together a schedule for City
Council of the events coming up. The Art Show at the Gale Center is still in progress until April
27, and he encouraged everyone to stop by and see the art displays.
Council Member Don Shelton recently traveled to Europe and shared how grateful he is for our
freedoms. He was able to hear a woman his age share her experience with living under
communist rule, and it made him realize how blessed we are. He met with Mayor Ramsey, City
Manager Gary Whatcott and Engineering Director Brad Klavano to discuss some of the
transportation needs in the city with several of our legislators to help promote those issues.

District Board of Trustees where they discussed some recommendations coming from some of
the member agencies regarding water received this year, including the idea that it was an
adequate amount of water. They decided as a Board to leave it as a Level 1 concern to avoid
losing all the progress that has been made by our communities in regards to conservation. She
attended a board meeting with the Economic Development Corporation of Utah to discuss the
future of Utah’s economy and where it is headed, and was able to meet with the new CEO, Scott

         H. Public Hearing Items
             H.1. Resolution R2023-17, Approving the 2023-2024 Annual Action Plan for the
             use of Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) Funds and authorizing the
             City to enter into a grant agreement for the use of CDBG funds. RCV (By Long
             Range Planner David Mann)

Long Range Planner David Mann reviewed background information from the Council Report
and Attachment A.

Mayor Ramsey asked if the county has more CDBG money this year than they have in the past,
or if they have supplemented it with additional federal funding, as there are significantly less
requests than previous years.

Planner Mann wasn’t sure of the reason for the drop in applications, it could be related to the
readjustment period after the pandemic.

Mayor Ramsey acknowledged the amazing work that has been done in the city with ADA ramps,
to make the city friendlier to those in wheelchairs and anyone else who is helped by those ramps.

Council Member Zander noted there were four applications, three were approved, and asked who
the fourth unapproved application was from.

Planner Mann responded that it was from Utah 211, who has applied in the past. Every year an
application is received from them, staff is able to see there is a need and that they provide a great
service to the state providing information about available services. However, there is a concern
about their qualification for the funds as they do a lot for low and moderate income residents, but
they also do a lot to provide for other groups that do not fall under this funding.

Council Member Zander asked about those who were awarded money last year that hadn’t been
used yet, and whether these three approved groups had used all of their awarded money from last
year.

Planner Mann said both The Road Home and South Valley Sanctuary have submitted invoices to
be used towards those funds from last year; many programs collect all their requests and do one
large request at the end of the period.

Council Member Shelton asked about the ADA ramps and how many there are left to replace.

Planner Mann said with the increased cost of materials, it has limited the amount of ramps the
city is able to replace each year, which has greatly slowed that progress.

         I. Staff Reports and Calendaring Items
Manager Whatcott said the South Valley Chamber has asked to have breakfast with our council,
proposing a date of June 23 here at the Public Safety Building in South Jordan at 8:30 a.m. The
council discussed that date and time.

The council and staff discussed the upcoming Utah League of Cities and Towns Conference.

Attorney Loose asked the council about having some of our legislators attend a meeting in May
and asked for their opinions about which meeting to invite them to.

Mayor Ramsey will be joining the second meeting in May electronically. She also mentioned
that she was appointed to the Growth and Transportation Committee of the UEOC again this
year and discussed some upcoming items.

Director of Administrative Services Melinda Seager discussed the council’s request to have staff
put together and execute some messaging on potholes, as well as short term rentals. She asked
the council some clarifying questions and shared some of their intentions as it relates to that
messaging. The council and staff discussed their desires regarding what information is shared
and the intended focus. She also noted the council recently requested a PowerPoint presentation
regarding information received on the annexation and HTRZ, and asked some additional
clarifying questions.

Council Member McGuire motioned to adjourn the City Council meeting. Council
Member Harris seconded the motion; vote was unanimous in favor.
