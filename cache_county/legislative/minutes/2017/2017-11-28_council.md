PUBLIC HEARING: NOVEMBER 28, 2017 — 5:30 p.m.- CACHE COUNTY TRAILS AND
ACTIVE TRANSPORTATION MASTER PLAN — Amendment to the Cache County
Comprehensive Plan adopting a Trails and Active Transportation Master Plan — Dayton Crites
stated the plan is needed to be eligible to receive several grants and reminded the Council that,
in compliance with Utah law, the plan has no ability to use eminent domain for trails.

Chairman Merrill opened the Public Hearing and invited public comment.

Shirlene Davis, Nibley, asked if the trail indicated in Nibley is Hollow Road. Crites replied it is. In the plan
a trail can be a sidewalk, a quiet road, a dirt path, etc.

Clair Ellis, Providence, is supportive of trails, which raise property values, but thought the plan's
professional language might be an obstacle to negotiation for some individuals. Priorities should be
gaining acquisition and addressing over-improvements. He would like accesses every mile better than
every three miles.

Nathan Hult, North Logan, thanked the Council and Crites and noted that the middle canal easement has
been used for many years by people. New property owners have erected fences and those fences do not
end the public prescriptive easements.

Cache County Council
11-28-2017

Jon Meikle, pointed out the canals were placed to convey water, not people. Those using the easement
for walking have been trespassing. The canal company only has a prescriptive easement to transfer
water, not people. Trails are a great idea, but a way needs to be found to acquire a right to those
easements. You can’t just take it. Canal right-of-ways are private property. Gates placed to keep
trespassers out make it difficult for water users to utilize the canal.

Tim Vitale said Meikle makes an important point, all entities need to work together to make sure private
property rights are protected and the community can have trails available. Over half of the people using
trails use their garages as the starting point.

Paul Stead supports trails and lived with a trail in his backyard before. There were no problems and
people were respectful.

Greg Forbush owns property on both sides of the canal and opposes the canal’s use as a trail. Whether
to use the canal as a trail should be decided by those who live along the canal, not those who want to
walk/run on it.

Norm Goltra supports trails and believes property values would go up. Goltra recommends connecting
with the Logan River task force for some of the trails they are proposing and including that in the plan.
Logan's free bus system allows people to go to trails and return without driving a car.

Debra Hass said what is problematic is even with so many taxpayer dollars where you get “x” amount of
grants, it comes back to what is the role of government. |s it appropriate and fair of government to put
another tax on property owners and put at risk property rights? Should it not be the private sector that
pays for this? Public taxation should not exist that doesn't benefit the person who is paying for it. Is it my
responsibility to pay for something | will never use?

Kris Kvarfordt, Logan, supports trails and asked about the boundary from Mt Pisgah to the far east side of
the county. What are the multiple uses that exist there? As population increases, multi uses of open
space become more and more necessary. The true thing that ties us to government in the long run is
single sourcing our transportation from one place. Need multiple options.

There was no other public comment.

ACTION: Motion by Council member Ward to close the Public Hearing-November 28,
2017-Cache County Trails and Active Transportation Master Plan. White seconded the
motion. The vote was unanimous.

Chairman Merrill asked for the Council's comments.

Council member Worthen represents the area where the middle canal runs and has concerns
that the document lacks an overall approach to private property. The portion regarding the
middle canal talks about prescriptive easement, but that is something that needs to be
determined by the court. Worthen talked with the president of the canal company and he said
the company is neutral and willing to allow the easements to be used if property owners are
amenable to it. Worthen recommends that written permission for use of the easement as a trail
would have to be received from each property owner and a different route would have to be
found where that permission is not forthcoming. Worthen asked that no action be taken on the
plan until she has the opportunity to bring some language addressing this issue to be included
in the plan.

Cache County Council
11-28-2017

Crites reiterated that no property owner will be forced to allow their property or the canal
easement to be used as a trail without the property owner's full permission.

Council member Zilles said the canal company does not “own” the canal, it just has an
easement and right-of-way for maintenance, etc.

Curt Hogan, president of the canal company, agreed the company has easement and does not
own the property. It has a dominant estate easement and has to approve any encroachment
within their easement if anyone applies for it. This is done all the time, for instance, with utility
companies.

Vice Chairman Erickson opined that property owners or canal companies should not be liable
for injury or death that occurs on the trail and hoped there is some way to absolve them of that
responsibility. Hogan said if there is an accident, all entities will be involved in the litigation and
have that expense. This is a concern.

Chairman Merrill said trails have become the new “buzzword.” There must be some method to
answer concerns of both sides.

Council member White noted the plan is just a wish list.

Executive Buttars observed that if you have a plan, you may have land owners who don't want it
to happen, but land changes hands over the years and permission may become available in the
future.

PUBLIC HEARING: NOVEMBER 28, 2017 — 6:00 p.m. — 2018 Budget and/or Tax Increase
Hearing — Chairman Merrill explained the County intends to increase the property tax rate above
the certified tax rate in order to generate an estimated $250,000.00 in additional property tax
revenue. These funds will be used to help cover the costs of Public Defender, operate the
Senior Citizens Center, and maintain and repair county roads. The increase is approximately
2.09% or about $5.00 a year on an average home.

The budget has been presented by the Executive to the Council which has met in several
budget workshops. The final budget will be approved by the second Council meeting in
December.

Chairman Merrill opened the Public Hearing and invited public comment.

Drew Bott, Farrel Lewis, Clyde Hinderliter, Ant Flat Road property owners, spoke opposing the increase
and decried the state of the Ant Flat Road.

Geraldine Niederhauser opposed the increase citing fixed income.

Vivian Christensen, Ken Thornley, Debbie Arnold, all of Cove, expressed dissatisfaction with the state of
their roads and opposed the tax increase.

Curt Pinder, Eric Andrus also opposed the increase wondering when the increases will cease.

Referring to a UAC study Executive Buttars responded that Cache County is 28" out of 29 Utah counties
for revenues. Cache is 26th in expenditures from the general fund and 29" for overall expenditures.

Jason Peterse n asked the Council to make sure the money goes to necessary things and not to things

Chairman Merrill replied that most of the trail funds are coming from grants and the Council is scrutinizing

NOTE 1. NATURE OF ACTIVITIES AND SIGNIFICANT ACCOUNTING POLICIES
Nature of Activities

Bear River Mental Health Services, Inc. (Center) is a not-for-profit
organization whose purpose is to provide mental health services to
residents of Cache, Rich and Box Elder Counties in the state of Utah. The
Center was incorporated as a not-for-profit organization on January 1,

1984. Prior to incorporation, mental health services were performed
through a tri-county governmental association. The Center receives funding
for its programs from various federal, state and local sources. Hl ot

addition, the Center receives Medicaid reimbursement for eligible patients
on a capitation basis. The Center has facilities in Logan, Brigham City
and Tremonton, Utah, with satellite offices in Garden City and Randolph,
Utah.

The Center provides comprehensive mental health services to clients
including: psychosocial rehabilitation, residential services, inpatient
services, individual therapy, group therapy, psychological testing,
medication management, etc. These services are provided for individuals
who are eligible for Utah State Medicaid, the portion of the population
that meets the state of Utah’s definition for seriously and persistently
mentally ill adults or seriously emotionally disturbed children and
adolescents, or those individuals in psychiatric crisis or in need of
inpatient hospitalization.

The accounting policies of the Center conform to generally accepted
accounting principles as applicable to not-for-profit institutions. The
following is a summary of the more significant policies:

Service Fee Revenue

Revenues earned from services provided under third-party reimbursement
and private pay arrangements are recognized on a cash basis due to the
uncertainty of the timing and amount of collection. As this treatment has
an immaterial effect on the financial statements, and due to the
uncertainty of collection which is not susceptible to reasonable estimation
by management, the Center’s treatment is deemed to conform with generally
accepted accounting principles.

Custodial Credit Risk

Custodial credit risk for deposits is the risk that, in the event of a
bank failure, the Center’s deposits may not be recovered. The Center
follows the Utah Money Management Act. The Act requires all deposits of an
entity to be in a qualified depository, defined as any financial
institution whose deposits are insured by an agency of the federal
government and which has been certified by the Commissioner of Financial
Institutions as meeting the requirements of the Act and adhering to the
rules of the Utah Money Management Council. The balance in the Center’s
bank accounts exceeded the FDIC limit of $250,000 by $0 and $51,708 as of
June 30, 2017 and 2016, respectively, which is covered by pledged
securities from Cache Valley Bank

Credit Risk

Credit risk is the risk that the counterparty to an investment will
not fulfill its obligations. The Center’s policy for limiting the credit
risk of investments is to comply with the Money Management Act.

The Center is authorized to invest in the Utah Public Treasurer's
Investment Fund (PTIF), an external pooled investment fund managed by the
Utah State Treasurer and subject to the Act and council requirements. The
PTIF is not registered with the SEC as an investment company and deposits
in the PTIF are not insured or otherwise guaranteed by the state of Utah.

MEMORANDUM 13 NOVEMBER 2017

To: County Council
Subject: Johnson Design Exception request

A request has been made by Rob and Todd Johnson for a Design Exception on a substandard county
road, 8600 South, to allow additional residential development (Exhibit A).

The Cache County Manual of Roadway Design and Construction Standards (Road Manual) section
1.8 Authority and Design Exceptions, specifies that:
“Full justification and documentation must be provided explaining the reasoning as to why the
roadway standards cannot be met, why an alternative design or construction method can meet the
intent of the roadway standards, and including any other relevant information.”

The submitted request for Design Exception provides the following reasoning:

1. The cost to repair the roadway exceeds the value of the property to be developed.

2. The road is currently safe for the current number of homes it services, and reducing the speed

limit on the road would be sufficient to provide an additional level of safety for access to the

property.

The Development Services staff and Director have completed a review of the Design Exception

request and have identified the following:

1. The existing county road 8600 South is known to be substandard in multiple locations
primarily due to a lack of adequate travel lane and shoulder width (Exhibit B). Also, a full
engineering review has not been completed and additional deficiencies may exist that have
not yet been identified.

The request failed to provide justification and documentation explaining the reasoning as to

why the roadway standards cannot be met.

a. Evidence of necessary improvement costs for substandard sections of the roadway in the
form of a professional engineering review and current bid to perform any required
improvements must be provided.

3. The request failed to provide justification and documentation explaining the reasoning to
support the statement that the existing roadway is safe for the number of homes it services.

a. Evidence that the road is safe for the existing number of homes must be provided in the

form of a professional engineering review.

4. The request failed to provide justification and documentation explaining the reasoning to
support the statement that the roadway would be made safer by reducing the speed limit.

a. Evidence that a reduction in the speed limit will make the roadway safer for the proposed

development must be provided in the form of a professional engineering review.

i

Therefore, the Director recommends that the Council deny this request for a Design Exception, as:

1. The request failed to provide any justification, analysis, or documentation for a Design
Exception that considers the full impact of further development.

2. Multiple development requests on this roadway have been made in the past, but have failed
due to the substandard nature of 8600 South. The property associated with this Design
Exception is not unique in its situation, and if approved, additional requests for exception are
likely to follow.

Subject: Zook Subdivision

The proposed Zook rezone request is situated on a parcel of property in Section 26 T 10 N R1 BE
located east of Paradise in a area known locally as Charlieville. The County address for this parcel is 1215
East 8412 South, Access to the proposed rezone is by way of 8600 South which traverses along a side hill
from about 700 East to near 1200 East. No other roadways provide access to this area. Currently, 8600
South provides access to approximately 13 residential properties and several larger agricultural land uses.

Roadway widths in the side hill section are typically less than 20 feet and seem to average about 18 feet.
Along with the narrow pavement width the terrain substantially drops off immediately adjacent to the south
pavement edge about 25 feet to a canal running parallel to the roadway. Terrain to the north side of the
roadway typically rises steeply adjacent to the pavement edge. Travelers on 8600 South are not separated
from the drop off conditions by any type of barrier system. Exrant vehicles leaving the roadway would
likely result in severe property damage and personal injury to occupants. Placement of a barrier, such as
guardrail without other improvements, would likely result in further narrowing of the useable pavement
width.

In regard to the proposed rezone request, adequate and safe vehicle access is not provided by the current
county road system. Further development in this area should not be permitted until the narrow pavement
widths and hazardous conditions cited above are corrected,
