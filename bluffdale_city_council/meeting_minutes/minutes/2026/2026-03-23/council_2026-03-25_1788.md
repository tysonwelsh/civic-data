6.1 Discussion- Short-Term Rental Research. (Staff Presenter, Ellen Oakman).
Associate Planner, Ellen Oakman, reported that Bluffdale City Code (“BCC”) § 11.340.040.F
specifies that internal and detached ADUs must be rented for 30 consecutive days or more, which
prohibits ADUs from being operated as short-term rentals (“STR”). STRs are not addressed
elsewhere in City Code. Mayor Hall remarked that many cities allow STRs, but only once per month.

Cities that allow STRs include St. George, Hurricane, Provo, Washington, Cottonwood Heights, Lehi,
Riverton, South Jordan, South Salt Lake, Herriman, and Lindon. These cities regulate STRs through
business licensing and charge a license fee, and many also charge Transient Room Tax (“TRT”).
Many require initial, annual, or complaint-based inspections. Saratoga Springs, Draper, Orem,

Ms. Oakman provided examples of STR ordinances. Provo has a simple zoning and licensing model
with limited administrative steps, and Cottonwood Heights has a clear structure but a more robust
approval and enforcement process. Because Bluffdale did not currently address STRs, the
recommended first step was to define the term and clarify the City’s position.
Mayor Hall and Council Member Austin stated that they had searched online to determine how many
STRs are currently operating in Bluffdale. City Manager, Bruce Kartchner stated that the City collects
TRT on STRs operated through VRBO and Airbnb. Other cities have tightened regulations due to
complaints, but he was not aware of similar issues in Bluffdale. TRT is not collected on Person-to-
person STRs. Mayor Hall remarked that those transaction would be hard to regulate.
Mayor Hall asked for the Council’s thoughts on STRs and any potential changes to City Code.
Council Member Wilding stated that he does not have strong feelings on the topic. He was hesitant
to fix something that is not broken, but saw a benefit to better defining the term. He was in favor of
laxer regulation because STRs were currently operating in the City without causing problems.
Council Member Lord expressed concern that adding STRs to City Code may create more of a
problem than doing nothing. He asked how any complaints would be addressed if they are not
defined. Mr. Kartchner reported that it would depend on the type of complaint, but it would be
addressed either criminally or through Code Enforcement. Council Member Lord stated that
Bluffdale is not a resort-type city, so it attracts a different type of STR. He was not in favor of making
any changes.
Council Member Smith asked where the existing STRs are located. Mayor Hall stated that they are
in every neighborhood in Bluffdale. Council Member Smith stated that he would not want to see a
lot of starter homes in Independence suddenly become Airbnbs, but he also did not see that as a short-
term risk. He agreed that the term should be defined and indicated that the Council should discuss
whether ADUs should be allowed to operate as STRs. Council Member Aston provided background
on the decision. When the ADU ordinance was adopted, allowing them to be operated as STRs was
a point of contention for some residents. The City Council decided to disallow that use both in
response to those comments and because the purpose of allowing ADUs was to create more housing.

Mayor Hall opened the public hearing. There were no comments. The public hearing was closed.

Mayor Hall asked for clarification on the allowed uses. Ms. Tubbs reported that if the Council elected

to move forward with the proposed change, they would be exchanging the currently allowed uses for

Area A for those allowed in Area B. Mr. Donaldson clarified that the use table would not change;

only the diagram indicating which buildings are in Areas A and B. Preschools and Daycares would

be disallowed because they are permitted in Area A only. Mayor Hall asked if the City Council could

disallow some uses such as Automotive Service. Ms. Tubbs reported that the Council could either

deny the request to modify the diagram or change the table of uses.

Council Member Aston stated that she was on the City Council when Bringhurst Station was
: approved. The City only has small retail areas, and the Council worked with the property owner to

ensure that this building had a retail component. In exchange, they were allowed to build 200

townhomes. Removing the retail would undo all that work by Staff and the City Council and

essentially turn the development into 100% warehousing. She does not believe more warehousing is

needed anywhere in Bluffdale, especially in this area. She was in favor of adjusting the use table to

allow uses such as printing, for example, but believed it was important to retain the retail component.

Council Member Wilding agreed with Council Member Aston and remarked that there was no rush

because the applicant had a long-term tenant in place. The matter needed further consideration. He

would be willing to modify code to add additional uses, but some of the proposed uses did not fit in

that area of the project.

Council Member Smith stated that many cities are discussing the topic of data centers, specifically

their energy and water usage. He was in favor of discussing adjusting the code but noted that there is

a risk associated with opening it up to all the proposed uses, especially data centers.
