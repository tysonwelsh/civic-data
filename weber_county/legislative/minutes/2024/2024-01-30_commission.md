D. THOUGHT OF THE DAY: Commissioner Harvey: Commissioners, I want to invite you to participate with me on
   my message today. Please forgive me for being a little more personal. I became associated with the County in
   May 1994; I was appointed by the County Commission then. Back then were some interesting challenges.
   Many businesses were boarded up and closed; adult movies were being shown at Peery’s Egyptian theater; most
   of the shops on 25th were boarded up with a few unsavory establishments. The caring and visionary leadership of
   many, but especially two of our own Commissioners, sparked a great transformation. The Commissioners at that
   time knew one of the things that needed to happen was to look forward to better tourism. It used to be
   everything came through Ogden; anyone travelling to the West had to go through Ogden. Rail travel eventually
   diminished with the advent of the automobile and airplane. Commissioners Joan Hellstrom and Spencer Stokes
   decided to do something about it. One of the things they did was to save Peery’s Egyptian Theater. Last Friday
   we celebrated 100 years of the theater. Back in 1994, the vote to save the theater failed. But the project did not.
   Those two Commissioners decided they were not going to let Ogden City fail. On December 28th, 1994, they
   sold the bonds that build the conference center, save the theater, and convert this building to our current offices.
   Additionally, proceeds received from the tragic Golden Spike Stockyards fire were wisely used to build the Ice
   Sheet and for a second indoor arena and recreational building. I have a great debt of gratitude to them. I love
   this town, I am passionate about this town. All residents of Weber County have benefitted from what they’ve
   done.

      Commissioner Bolos: I want to echo what Commissioner Harvey said. It takes courage to against the grain and
      to do something that is important to you. This was the catalyst for the revitalization of Ogden. Thank you for
      having the foresight and the courage to see Weber County into the future.

      Commissioner Froerer: I think we need to understand the opportunities we have in Weber County depended on
      Spencer Stokes and Joan Hellstrom and other Commissioners to ensure we have those facilities. That was not an
      easy time. Politics is not always that fun and sometimes you have to climb out on those skinny branches to make
      decisions that benefit future generations. We now get to enjoy the benefits of those decisions. We want to give
      you a memento of our appreciation - our Weber County belt buckle.

      Chair Harvey: There is one key person who was the guide, who helped organize and out everything together and
      always led them to True North and that is Susan Finley. She was always so kind and caring; she made sure when
      you called the Commission office she made sure you had someone to talk to. We have an engraved compass for
      you.

      Joan Hellstrom: When I was five years old, there was a KSL show called Princess Make-Believe; we would
      come down to the Egyptian theater before the Saturday morning show. That’s how dear the Egyptian was. If it
      wasn’t for Spencer Stokes, saving the theater wouldn’t have happened. And a dear friendship was formed.

      Spencer Stokes: It could not have been done without a massive group of community support. I was told by the
      incoming Commissioners that if the bond was not done before they were sworn in, they would cancel this
      project. So on the 28th of December, we sold the bonds. We had a good run, Joan and I. We closed the landfill,
      built a transfer station, built the Upper Valley library, and put a tax in place for the ongoing functions of the
      library. We were pretty much thrown out of office after our great run. But guess what? It’s all paid for and
      serves the people of Weber County well.
    Commissioner Harvey: A lot of times in our service here, we fail to mention those who are the most important to
    us. So I want to give a shout out to Kent Hellstrom and to Mary Stokes who paid a dear price supporting these
    two.
