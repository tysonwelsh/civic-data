¢ Discussion — Mt. Pisgah Road — Prior to the discussion, Chairman Merrill asked
Director Runhaar how the road department is doing with snow removal. Runhaar said
the county has 488 lane miles to plow and will use 250 tons of salt on a bad storm day.
There is not enough salt and Runhaar is trying to find some elsewhere. There is
considerable water damage that will be a concern in the spring.


Cache County Council
01-24-2017

Runhaar reviewed the request by the Mt. Pisgah Limestone Quarry to pave
approximately 2,300 feet of the Mt. Pisgah Road. Runhaar observed there are no
homes on the road, so private construction and maintenance of the road may be viable.

Michael Schugg, Mt. Pisgah Limestone Quarry, assured the Council that the Quarry will
maintain the road for years to come since the estimated supply of limestone at the
Quarry is 400 years.

Council members expressed concerns over liability, but Council member Ward pointed
out the county is liable for the road in its present gravel condition anyway. Some Council
members want to see the agreements with the Quarry before rendering a decision.

Chairman Merrill directed the topic be placed on the February 14, 2017 Council meeting
agenda.

REQUEST TO IMPROVE MT. PISGAH ROAD JANUARY 19, 2017

The following outlines issues with private improvements to public roads where the County cannot
increase services, most specifically to the current request to pave a portion of the Mt. Pisgah road
from Hwy 89/91 to the entrance to a private quarry (see attached map):
1) Maintenance Concerns — When a gravel road is paved, the maintenance level and type change.
Instead of running a grader on the road once or twice a year, a whole new maintenance system
needs to be put in place.

a.

Immediate needs (pot holes, etc.) - The County is required to address a roadway issue
(i.e. a pothole) within 2 business days of being made aware that it exists. Until that
point we do not claim responsibility for vehicle damage. After that time period, our
responsible for vehicle damage or any other accident claims increases, This same time
frame applies to other safety issues that may occur on roadways (washouts, culvert
failures, etc.) and is typically more apt to be an issue on paved roads.

The County is unable to provide any service to this road due to limited resources and the

extreme distance of the Mt. Pisgah Road in question.

Long Term (chip seal or other surface treatments) — After converting a road from gravel
to pavement, a long term maintenance schedule would consist of a chip seal a year after
paving and then regular chip seals every 5 to 8 years.

The County is unable to provide any service to this road due to limited resources and the
extreme distance of the Mt. Pisgah Road in question.

Snow removal — The County is not able to remove snow on any portion of this road
because of the distance required to get to it. Private snow removal is a potential on this
road (with an agreement) as there are no homes or commercial structures that would
require emergency response which could be hindered by lack of winter maintenance.
Signage for safety and/or a gate — There are a number of trucks and passenger vehicles
that get stuck attempting the navigate the Mt. Pisgah road beyond the entrance to the
quarry throughout the year as GPS systems will often list it as an alternative route to the
south end of the valley from the highway. Additional pavement and winter
maintenance may exacerbate the issue and would require additional signage to warn
motorists at a minimum, or a gate at the end of the paved portion of road with a turn-
around as a worst case scenario.

2) Ability for the County to allow private service providers to maintain public roads

a.

The County in the past has not allowed private maintenance on public roads other than
incidental items (farmers will often partially clear a road to feed cattle etc.). The County
has a non-delegable duty to maintain roads sufficient to the potential demand. This
means that in order to approve something like a single family home, the County is
required to provide sufficient year round services to ensure the safety of residents (ie.
fire, EMS, 2 passable lanes of travel, etc.). In this instance, there is limited concern with
allowing winter use because if the road is not cleared, no one will be able to enter the
quarry. No homes or commercial business structures (equipment or repair sheds,
scales, etc. could still be allowed) have been or would be approved.
