6:00 p.m.; discuss proposed 36-inch Ogden Canyon waterline project financing; receive a presentation regarding the Enterprise

Agenda Review

meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Proposed 36” Canyon Waterline Financing Discussion
          A staff memo from Council staff explained the April 9, 2024 work session will be the first of several work sessions to
review the proposed financing package and utility rate changes relating to the 36-inch Ogden Canyon waterline project.
Additional work sessions will be held to answer questions the Council may have about the project and its financing.
          Public Services Director Anderson used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the history of water delivery
through Ogden Canyon. He explained the proposed project, Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) project number WU003 is estimated
to cost approximately $100 million and includes not only the water line in Ogden Canyon but the 36-inch concrete lined steel pipe
running under Pineview Reservoir from the City’s wellfields as well. The project will be completed in three phases: Pine View
Water Line ($22 million), Ogden Canyon 36-inch water line ($57 million), and 42-inch overflow line to the 23rd Street reservoir
($7.2 million). For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and Mr. Anderson centered on the scope of the project and materials to be used; the
impact of the project on Pineview Reservoir; the impact to surrounding artesian wells; and the timing of the project.
          Fred Philpot of Lewis Robertson and Burningham (LRB) Public Financial Advisors used the aid of a PowerPoint
presentation to summarize the water utility fund analysis and financing proposal for the subject project. Proposed funding sources
include $34.3 million from a State Revolving Loan Fund, $42.6 million from a federal Water Infrastructure Finance and
Innovation Act (WIFIA) loan, $10 million from State appropriations, and $12 million from the City’s American Rescue Plan Act
(ARPA) funding. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
Enterprise Fund Transfer Study Presentation
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith explained as required by Utah State Code §10-6-135.5, a city that is proposing to
transfer funds from an enterprise fund to another fund for a purpose not directly related to the goods or services provided by the
enterprise fund must provide notice of the transfers to the users of the enterprise fund. The City must also hold a public hearing on
the transfers prior to adoption or the amendment of the City’s budget. In Ogden, a budgetary transfer from the City’s Water,
Sanitary Sewer, Storm Sewer, and Refuse Utility enterprise funds to the general fund has been done for many years; however, the
requirement to provide notice of the transfers and to hold a separate public hearing is a recent change to state law. These utility
funds are accounted for as enterprise funds in Ogden and are operated with user fees charged for goods and services. In each
annual budget since at least the early 1990s, the City has included a transfer from these four utility funds into the City’s general
fund to act as the collection of franchise and property tax. This includes six percent of enterprise fund charges for services for
franchise tax and an additional six percent for property tax. These funds have been included in the City’s general fund operating
budget. While the City has been making this transfer and using the same rationale for doing so for many years, questions have
been raised in recent years about the impact of this policy if the Council were to make changes to it. It has been explained
generally that if the transfer were not made, general fund revenues would either need to be reduced or that additional revenue
would need to be raised from other sources. This would most likely take the form of an increase in property taxes. To better
understand the impacts of this transfer and any changes that may be desired, the Council office contracted with the City’s financial
advisor, LRB, to perform a study to help the Council better understand the transfer and the impact of a change in policy. It is
anticipated that the mayor’s proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 budget will include the transfer; however, final approval of the
transfer rests with the Council.
          Laura Lewis of LRB used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the results of the study and to inform the
Council of the financial impact of the transfer. The study included an analysis of changes in utility rates if the transfer is not made
and compared those increases to potential property tax changes if the City were to make up the revenue from that source. For a
copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          The Council discussed the appropriate annual enterprise transfer amount and the administrative functions that are funded
through the transfer.

Proposed Creation of the Flex Industrial Zone
          Senior Planner Brandon Rypien explained Asilia Investments initially submitted a petition to amend the Airport
Commercial (A-C) zone. Those amendments would have modified and clarified uses in the A-C zone to allow a wider range of
non-aeronautical commercial uses. The A-C zone along with the Airport Industrial (A-I) zone, make up the zoning for operations
on and adjacent to the airport. While the changes were given a recommendation of approval by the Planning Commission,
concerns raised by the Council led the petitioners and the City to amend the proposal from changes to the A-C zone to a proposal
of a new zone altogether. Mr. Rypien used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the amended proposal to create a
new Flex Industrial zone that would allow flexible commercial and industrial/manufacturing space. This would be a new zone
within the manufacturing classification and would have some similarities to the M-1 zone while allowing some additional
flexibility in the zone’s allowable uses and interior warehousing and manufacturing standards. The focus of the initial petition was
to develop property on the south end of the airport adjacent to the Kemp buildings. The zoning on that property is A-C. While the
updated proposal is to create a new zone, the petitioner’s goal is still to develop the property but under the new zone rather than a
modified A-C zone. If the Flex Industrial zone gets approval from the Council, it is anticipated that a petition will be filed to
rezone the subject property. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and staff centered on permitted and conditional uses in the proposed zone and feedback
from the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) regarding their position on the new zone and potential uses in the zone.
          Chair Richey noted that action on the proposed creation of the Flex Industrial zone will be included on the April 23rd
meeting agenda.
