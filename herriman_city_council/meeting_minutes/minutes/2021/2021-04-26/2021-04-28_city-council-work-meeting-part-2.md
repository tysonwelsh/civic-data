2. Administrative Reports
    2.1. Discussion of the proposed amendments to the Engineering Standards - Jonathan Bowers,
         City Engineer
City Engineer Jonathan Bowers indicated the current engineering standard for roadway cross-sections indicates
private roadways shall be designed and constructed to the same standards as public streets. For purposes
relating to emergency vehicles, all residential units shall be accessible and addressed off a roadway which
conforms to the city roadway cross-section. He proposed public streets be required to conform to the
Herriman City standard roadway widths and allow for private roadways with the following conditions: include
a minimum drivable width of 26-feet, conform to the International Fire Code requirements, include a separate,
designated, and paved pedestrian walk path to all dwelling structures along the road, and the total corridor
width, dedicated for vehicular and pedestrian traffic, shall be a minimum of 53-feet wide. All residential units
shall be accessible and addressed off a roadway which conforms to public or private roadway requirements as
described.

Mayor Watts opined the minimum roadway width should be a minimum of 32-feet to allow for on street
parking for both sides of the road unless there are no structures abutting one side of the street. The Council
agreed. The discussion turned to the requested standard of the park strip and sidewalk to which it would be
required as part of a private roadway.

    2.2. Herriman City Fire Service funding discussion - Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney and Alan
         Rae, Finance Director
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi explained the unsustainable rate for funding for the fire services including the
debt services payment established by the separation agreement. As the City anticipated the withdrawal from
the district, the Council commenced the process to establish a local district to fund the services. He noted the
statute requiring the adoption of a resolution creating the fire district may not collect any taxes until November
2022 which would leave the service unfunded.

                                 Councilmember Jared Henderson arrived at 6:26 p.m.

City Attorney Andrizzi explained the funds could be incorporated into the Herriman City Safety Enforcement
Service Area to collect the tax. This option could present challenges as various districts would have to go
through multiple tax rate changes. He also presented a second option where the Council could consider
increasing the City tax rate by an amount sufficient to fund fire services. If services are funded through this
channel, the Council could elect to maintain the rate at the level and continue to fund the services through the
general fund. Alternatively, the rate could be increased until the fire district is able to collect the tax rate and
reallocate the tax to the district and reduce the City’s tax rate.

The Council relayed their desire to place the levied taxes in the Herriman City Safety Enforcement Service

    2.3. Annexation area update - Blake Thomas, Community Development Director
Community Development Director Blake Thomas reviewed the transportation challenges with the proposed
development and the timeline requirements if the property was to be annexed into Herriman City.
Councilmember Shields touched on the necessity to review a tremendous amount of information for a potential
annexation to be approved prior to the end of October and recommended the City create “working groups”
to help address issues in a thorough and timely manner. These working groups would be public and transparent
and would help determine if the City and the developer had a desire to determine if it would be in the best
interest of both parties to go through the annexation process. After further discussion Council offered direction
to create working groups consisting of staff, Council, and developers.

3. Budget Report
    3.1. General Fund Capital Projects Discussion - Jonathan Bowers, City Engineer
City Engineer Jonathan Bowers offered an explanation of how general fund capital projects are prioritized.
Currently, there are a total of 50 projects on the master list including park replacements, unfinished parkstrips,
unfinished ponds, roadway improvements, safe walk route improvements, among many others. He presented
the step-by-step process to identify the master list. Initially, an exhaustive list of projects from departments is
presented. Second a priority score for each project would be calculated and sorted in descending order based
on those factors. The projects with specific interest are placed on a separate list based on the project type
while maintaining their project identifier and priority from the master list. City Engineer Bowers recommended
maintain the project in order according to the priority determined and identified on the master list.

Teddy Hodges relayed his excitement of the various Arts Council events scheduled over the summer and noted
there was a need for help getting the sets completed for the performances. He encouraged public participation
and asked for anyone wanting to help to let him know.

Megan Cox expressed concern for the intersection of Herriman Rose Blvd and Meadowside Drive due to the
lack of stops signs for drivers on Herriman Rose Blvd (from Main Street to Fort Herriman Pkwy). Most drivers
drive at a speed well over the posted 25 MPH. She opined the intersection needed to turn into a four-way stop
or have a flashing crosswalk installed. She explained cops have patrolled this area and pull-over almost every
vehicle. She stated this was dangerous and someone would be seriously injured if nothing is not changed and
encouraged the Council to take the necessary steps to ensure the patrons of this city remain safe.
