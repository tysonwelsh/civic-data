2.2 Discussion of Draft Bike and Pedestrian Plan
Mike West presented a draft bike and pedestrian plan with seven main goals for the next 5-10
years, focusing on safety and connectivity. Key objectives included updating development codes,
adding missing sidewalks, installing trail lighting, connecting key destinations, and adding paved
trails. The Council’s feedback for consideration included adding a recreational element to the
plan, creating a prioritization plan for implementation, and identifying and filling gaps in the
existing network.

2.3 FRA Train Regulations Update
Luke Seegmiller, Lehi City Traffic Engineer, reported on railroad crossing compliance progress,
noting most locations have been fixed except for three. The most problematic crossing remains at
1700 South in Salt Lake City, requiring a 60-foot clearance from crossing arms. The discussion
included complex calculations about risk catalogs and safety measures required for maintaining
quiet zone status.

2.5) 2300 West Design/Construction Update (300 North- 900 North)
The Lehi City Council engaged in a detailed discussion regarding the design options for 2300
West, specifically focusing on the section between 300 North and 900 North. The discussion
centered around a stretch of road where the right-of-way was purchased in 2005, approximately
20 years ago, so the right-of-way is smaller in that area. Brad Kenison, Lehi City Engineer,
presented several alternatives in response to property owner concerns about drainage, slope and
fencing issues.

The alternatives ranged from maintaining the current design to more extensive modifications.
Alternative 2A, estimated at $165,000, proposed steepening the side slopes slightly and adding a
drainage solution, either as a concrete V-ditch or French drain with gravel, while maintaining the
existing fence. This option required no additional right-of-way and would work within the
approximately 11-foot space from curb and gutter to property line. A more expensive option,
Alternative 2B, suggested the same modifications but included replacing the existing fence with
an 8-foot tall privacy-slatted fence, costing approximately $95 per foot. The most extensive
option, Alternative 3, carried a $790,000 price tag and involved extending the field slope to
property lines, potentially requiring retaining walls and property owner permissions.

After considerable discussion about property owner rights, maintenance concerns, and project
timelines, the Council agreed to proceed with Alternative 2A as the most practical and cost-
effective solution. Mayor Johnson directed staff to communicate this decision to property owners
while allowing them the option to upgrade to taller fencing at their own expense. This approach
was seen as the best compromise, addressing the essential drainage concerns while avoiding
complications with property owner permissions and maintaining the project's schedule.

2. 20 Minute Citizen Input
During the public input period, Scott Murph, a Lehi resident of six years, addressed the Council
regarding the train quiet zone situation. Mr. Murph shared his research on Federal Railroad
Administration regulations and proposed pursuing state legislation in the 2025 session to address
maintenance and funding of quiet zones through UTA. He suggested that UTA has a vested interest
in maintaining quiet zones throughout their system and could potentially oversee their
maintenance. Mayor Johnson noted that the city is actively engaged in this issue and had met with
a congressman that afternoon regarding the matter.

3. Presentation and Reports
3.1) Heart Saver Award Presentation
Chief Robinson presented the Heart Saver Award recognizing the lifesaving efforts that occurred
on August 9, 2024, when a 48-year-old male was found unresponsive in his vehicle. The chief
detailed how bystanders initiated CPR after breaking the vehicle window, followed by Lehi Police
Department assistance and ultimately EMS care from Lehi Fire Department personnel. The patient
was successfully resuscitated and has since been released from the hospital. Awards were
presented to Battalion Chief Jeff Smith, Captain Trent Stanley, Engineer Steve Berenson,
Paramedic Zack Lucero, EMT Kai Hedman, Paramedic Coulter Nordberg, Officers Brandon
Rudolph and Brandon Farnsworth, and bystanders Daniel Tulak and James McLarty.

3.2) Epilepsy Awareness Month Proclamation
Mayor Johnson signed a proclamation declaring November 2024 as Epilepsy Awareness Month
in Lehi City. The proclamation highlighted that epilepsy affects 3.4 million Americans, including
nearly 34,000 Utahns, and emphasized the importance of increasing awareness and education
about the condition while supporting those affected and their families. Sarah Hansen was in
attendance and thanked the Mayor and Council for their support.

5. Consideration of Ordinance #64-2024 the Bowden General Plan Amendment on 5.18
   acres located at 9861 West 9600 North changing the land use designation from VLDRA
   to LDR.
   The applicant, Corey Bowden, was not present at the meeting. Four residents spoke in
   opposition to the amendment:

    Candace Hayward Jones, whose property shares a boundary with the subject property, cited
    concerns about inconsistency with surrounding lot sizes and potential negative impacts on
    neighborhood character. She emphasized that neighboring properties maintain half-acre lots
    and expressed concern about the possibility of future development with smaller lot sizes.

    Ashley Frampton spoke about infrastructure concerns, particularly traffic issues on 1500
    North, and the importance of maintaining the area's half-acre lot character.

    A new resident who had moved to the area two weeks prior expressed that the half-acre lots
    were a key factor in their decision to move to the neighborhood.

    Jared Maruji shared that he and his wife specifically chose the area for its half-acre lots and
    expressed concerns about increased traffic and the impact on the neighborhood's character.

    The Council agreed to move forward with a vote noting that the applicant was notified of the
    meeting.
