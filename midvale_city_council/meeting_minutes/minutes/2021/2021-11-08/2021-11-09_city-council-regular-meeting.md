         A.   CONSIDER ORDINANCE NO. 2021-0-21 STREET CUT MORATORIUM
Keith Ludwig said over the last several years the city has seen an uptake in the amount
of road cuts. These can have a detrimental effect on the longevity of the lifetime of the
pavement. lt is especially frustrating when these cuts happen in recently rebuilt, overlaid
or slurry sealed roads. Staff has d rafted and is recommending implementation of a road
cut moratorium policy. This will help keep our roads not only looking good but will also
help with the longevity. Road cut moratoriums are common and have been implemented
in several surrounding communities. The road cut moratorium policywill be ongoing. The
proposed moratorium is summarized below:

         For new and reconstructed streets and overlaid streets, no road cut permits will
         be allowed for three years after the date of completion.
     a   For those streets recently slurry sealed, no road cuts will be allowed for one year
         after the date of construction.
     a   For an emergency situation the City Engineer will allow road cuts, but the special
         restoration standard will apply.
         The special restoration standard will be in effect following the moratorium.
              o  For new street, reconstructed streets, and overlaid streets, this standard
                 will be in effect for three years after the moratorium ends.
              o  For streets that have been slurry sealed, this standard will be in effect for
                 one year after the moratorium ends.
              o  This special restoration standard will require the asphalt surface to be
                 restored by milling out and then overlaying the area that was impacted.
                 This mill and overlay will be 2" in thickness, extend to 15' either side of the
                 excavated area, and will correspond with lane widths. Those dimensions
                 can be increased if deemed appropriate. For those streets that have been
                 slurry sealed, the area 15' either side of the excavation will be re-slurried.

         There is also a winter emergency provision in place between November 15th and
         March 15th. This will require temporary asphalt in the patch that will need to be
         replaced and then the special restoration standards will apply once weather
         conditlons are suitable.

SUBJECT: Discussion on the formation and purpose of the Midvale City Traffic
Control Committee and the Traffic Control Policies and Procedures Manual the
Committee will refer to as the guide for decision making.


SUBMITTED BY:

Keith Ludwig, Midvale City Engineer

SUMMARY:

City staff and Council members receive many traffic control device installation
requests. Historically, there has not been an olficial procedure to review,
approve, deny, or process these requests. In addition, there has not been
funding identified in the annual budgets for these requests.

City staff is recommending the formation of Traffic Control Committee. This
committee will be chaired by the City Engineer and will consist of 5 members
representing police, fire, engineering, and public works. The purpose of the
committee is to perform a formal review for all incoming traffic control device
requests, taking into consideration the perspective and expertise of each
represented group to review, approve, deny, or recommend additional studies
or options.

To assist the Traffic Control Committee and City staff involved with traffic
control, a Traffic Control Policy and Procedures Manual has been created.

The Midvale City Trafl-rc Control Policy and Procedure Manual is intended for
use by City stafl as guidance for the design, operation, maintenance, and
management of traffic operations in the public right-of-way. The manual
establishes standard processes for control of this work and will help ensure
consistency in their application. This document will also be used by the Traffic
Control Committee as the main guide when making decisions on citizen
requested traffic control device installations.

Fiscal impact depends on the number and type of approved traffic control
device applications. In preparing for the implementation of this program, the

Midvale City Traffic Committee Policy and procedures

The purpose of Midvale City Traffic Committee Policy and Procedures is to Buide the Traffic Committee
members in the review and processing of all traffic control device requests.

The Traffic Committee (TC) will consist of 5 members representing police, fire, engineering, and public
works. The purpose of the committee is to perform a formal review for all incoming traffic control
device requests, taking into consideratlon the perspective and expertise of each represented group to
review, approve, deny, or recommend additionalstudies or options.

The City Engineer (CE) will lead the committee and be the funnel of all requests and processes.

The TC will meet at minimum quarterly (unless there are no pending applications) and more frequently
if there are 3 or more pending applications before the next scheduled meeting.

Application:

lncoming requests for traffic control devices will be submitted through an online application and
directed to the CE. The application is attached in Appendix B. All fields must be completed along with
the names, addresses and signatures of 5 property owners adjacent to the location of the request.

Process:

Upon receiving an application, the CE willcreate a project file and add the application and any other
required documents for the request. A To-Do list will be created and assigned by the CE containin8
individual review tasks for each TC member, which is to be completed before the TC's next meeting,

Review:

The TC will review each request following the Midvale City Traffic Control Policies and Procedures
Manual.

Denial:

lf the TC denies a request the CE will notify the applicant of both the denial and the reasons behind the
decision. lf denied, a duplicate or similar application will not be accepted for one calendar year from
denia I date.

Approval:

lf the TC approves a request, the cE will inform the applicant they need to acquire 60% of approval
signatures within an area determined by the CE that will be directly affected by the request. The
applicant will have 2 weeks to provide the CE with the required signatures. lf the applicant cannot
acquire the required signatures, the request will be denied and will not be eligible for another review for
one calendar year.

lf a project is approved and signature requirements are met, and the cost of the request is less than
S3,000, the CE will forward the request to the Public Works Director for installation.
lf a project is approved and signature requirements are met, and the cost of the request is more than
53,000, the CE will take the request to the CIP Committee or Senior Staff to go through the budget
process.

City Manager Discretioni

The City Manager may overturn an approval or denial decision that is made by the traffic committee but
must provide a written explanation detailing the reasons why via a memo to the CE.

The Midvale City Traffic Control Policy and Procedure Manual is intended for
use by City staff as guidance for the design, operation, maintenance, and
management of traffic operations in the public right-of-way. The manual
establishes standard processes for control of this work and will help ensure
consistency in their application.


1. TRAFFIC POLICY AND PROCEDURES

This manual intends to guide the design, operation, and management of traffic
operations within Midvale City to ensure consistency in the use and placement
of traffic control devices. The most recent edition of the Manual on Unifurm
Traffic Control Deuices (MUTCD) as adopted by the Utah Department of
Transportation (UDOT), together with any revisions to that manual adopted by
UDOT, sha11 guide these policies.

7. SPEED LIMITS
Posted speed limits shall be per the statutory speed limit on a residential
street and based on MUTCD standards for Speed Warrants.

B. ADVISORY SPEED LIMIT SIGNS

Advisory speed signs limit may be used at locations where the maximum safe
speed is more than 10 miles per hour below the statutory speed limit for a
roadway segment. Determination of the maximum safe speed shall be based on
engineering analysis and the American Association of State and Highway
Transportation Officials (AASHTO) design standards, road geometry, and road
tests conducted with a ball bank indicator.

10. SIGN VISIBILITY
All traffic control signs shall be visible from a sufficient distance to make
required decisions reasonably safe. Clear vision areas at intersections and
driveways shall be maintained following Midvale City code unless otherwise
approved by the City Engineer. Landscaping and vegetation shall be kept
trimmed to avoid restricting the visibility of traffic control signs.

12. SPECIAL SIGNS

Special signs shall comply with MUTCD guidelines. The use of special signs shall

16. CROSSWALKS

The City Traffic Committee shall approve all new crosswalk installations. The
installation of new crosswalks shall be based on an engineering study.
Crosswalk markings shall comply with MUTCD guidelines. Crosswalks sha1l be
10 feet in width unless otherwise authorized by the City Engineer. Crosswalks
shall be instailed across all approaches to an intersection controlled by a traffic
signal or roundabout unless otherwise approved by the City Engineer.

Midblock crosswalks should be discouraged unless warranted by unique
situations and an engineering study confirms need. Midblock and crosswalks at
uncontrolled crossings shall be striped with two parallel 24-inch-wide white
stripes. In addition, the use of advance stop bars and pedestrian crossing signs
shall be considered at a1l uncontrolled crossings.

17, STOP LINE PAVtrMENT MARKINGS
The City Engineer sha11 approve the installation of stop line pavement markings.
Stop line markings sha11 be considered for use at locations where an
engineering study has indicated a need to provide an extra indication to
motorists of where they are to stop. Examples include new stop sign locations
at existing intersections, intersections with restricted sight distance,
intersections with a poor stop sign compliance rate, and in advance of marked
midblock crosswalks.

Stop line pavement markings are not generally used before marked crosswalks
at intersections controlled by a traffic signal. However, stop line markings may
be considered for use at these locations if needed to allow the intersection to
accommodate truck turn movements without conflict.

The City Traffic Committee must approve bus stop locations on the public street

to optimize operations of both the bus service and traffic.

27. ROADSIDE MEMORIALS

City employees shall report roadside memorials located within t h e public

reported to a supervisor as soon as practical. When possible, the memorial
owner shall be contacted before the removal and/or relocation.
