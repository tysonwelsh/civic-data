BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Plat Amendment for approximately 1.83
acres located on the east side of Pioneer Rd., at approximately 12258 South Grizzly Hollow
Cv. (Exhibit B). The property is currently zoned RA2. The applicant is requesting that a Plat
Amendment be approved to allow for the modification of easements and no-built zones.

The property is Lot 302 of the Cove at Bear Canyon Phase 3 Subdivision. This subdivision
was recorded on March 3, 1994. The lot includes substantial drainage easements, from a
30-foot wide channel running through the property to a drainage pond. In addition, the
property contains a substantial no-build area along the east of the property.

The Cove at Bear Canyon Phase 3 Subdivision shows those areas that are 30% or greater in
slope and marks them as non-buildable space. All 17 lots within the subdivision contain
some area that is classified as non-buildable. Four of the 17 lots within the subdivision have

ANALYSIS

General Plan and Zoning. The Land Use Map of the General Plan calls for the Residential
Low/Medium Density land use designation for the subject property (Exhibit C). This
category is characterized as follows:




The property has been assigned the RA2 zoning classification, supporting approximately
two dwelling unit per acre (Exhibit D). According to Draper City Municipal Code (DCMC)



Edson                                                             App. # SUBD-193-2021
Plat Amendment Request                       2
Section 9-8-020 the purpose of the RA2 zone is to “foster low density development with little
impact on its surroundings and municipal services; to generally preserve the character of the
City’s semi-rural areas; and to promote and preserve conditions favorable to large-lot family life,
including the keeping of limited numbers of animals and fowl. This zone is intended to promote
a combination of retail and service facilities which, in character and scale, meet day to day
needs of nearby residents.” The RA2 zoning abuts the subject property on all sides, except for
a sliver on the east that is zoned A5 (Agricultural, 5 acre lot minimum).

Subdivision Layout. The existing Cove at Bear Canyon Phase 3 Subdivision shows 22,024
square feet of no-build area on Lot 302 (Exhibit E). The majority of that area is located
along the east of the property, with a small portion located along the north. The applicant
is requesting to reduce the no-build areas on the lot to 15,409 square feet (Exhibit F). The
majority of this area will remain on the east side of the lot. The applicant will also expand
the no-build area along the north of the lot and provide a new no-build area along the
south of the lot. The grading plan shows the areas on the north and south are more
extensive than the original plat provided for (Exhibit G). The proposed house and driveway
will be located partially in areas shown as 30% slope on the grading plan and within the no-
build area on the existing plat. A corner of the house will be located within the 30% slope
area, roughly 630 square feet. As for the driveway, it will move through the 30% slope area
from Grizzly Hollow Cove and then connect to the house. The driveway will impact roughly
1,450 square feet of 30% slope area.

The Hillside Sensitive Lands Overlay Zone prohibits development on 30% slope areas but
does allow the Planning Commission to approve a deviation upon making the three
findings listed in DCMC Section 9-16-040.

            9-16-040: DEVELOPMENT REQUIREMENTS:
            A. Development In General: Slope areas in excess of thirty percent (30%) may not be
            developed, and no more than thirty percent (30%) of a development's slope areas in
            excess of thirty percent (30%) may be included in the area calculation to determine
            density. The planning commission may modify this requirement upon finding that:
            1. No significant harm will result;
            2. The proposed modification will result in a more functional and improved plan;
            and
            3. The developer/builder agrees to comply with any conditions or requirements
            imposed by the planning commission to mitigate any adverse effects which may
            result from the proposed modification.

The proposed subdivision plat will also change the drainage easements on the property.
There is a 30-foot wide drainage easement running through the property. The purpose of
the easement to move water from the adjacent Bear Canyon to the detention area on the
west side of Lots 302 and 301. The proposed plat will modify the 30-foot wide drainage
easement and in its place create a 6-foot to 10-foot wide channel easement. This channel



Edson                                                                App. # SUBD-193-2021
Plat Amendment Request                          3
will allow water to flow to the existing and unmodified detention area. The Draper City
Engineering Division has reviewed the changes and they have determined the proposed
drainage easements comply with code.

Slope Stability Studies. The applicant has submitted a slope stability study, which has been
reviewed and approved by the City’s consultants, Taylor Geotechnical and Simon
Associates, LLC (Exhibit H). The geologic site plan submitted as part of that Study is
provided in Exhibit H of this report and shows grading on the property and those areas
which are unbuildable due proximity to the fault lines. The only portion of the property that
is buildable due to geology is the center of the property, where the proposed house is
located. The slope stability analysis showed that the proposed cuts and fill on the property
would not impact the stability of the slopes on the property beyond DCMC acceptable
factors-of-safety.

Criteria For Approval. The grounds for review and potential approval of a subdivision plat
amendment request is found in Section 17-9-040 of the DCMC. This section depicts the
standard of review for such requests as:

        The Planning Commission may approve the vacation or amendment of a plat by signing
        an amended plat showing the vacation or amendment if the Land Use Authority finds
        that there is good cause for the vacation or amendment; and no public street, right-of-
        way, or easement has been vacated or amended.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION

Staff finds that the application complies with the DCMC and recommends that the Planning
Commission review the request, receive public comment, and approve the application
based on the findings listed below and the criteria for approval, as listed within the staff

the following conditions of approval:

                construction of all buildings on the site.
        3.      A deviation to DCMC Section 9-16-040(A) is approved. Portions of the home
                and driveway may be built within the areas of 30% slope or greater, as shown

Dear Mr. Collier:

The 1.82-acre property is located in a Draper City (the City) Geologic-Hazards Ordinance Slope
Stability Special Study Area (Draper City, 2010). Appendix C of the City Geologic-Hazard
Ordinance stipulates slope stability analyses shall be performed for all sites located within the
Slope Stability Study Area Map. The Applicant is Aaron Edson. The Applicant’s
geologic/geotechnical engineering Consultant is CMT Engineering Laboratories (CMT)

At your request, Taylor Geotechnical (TG) reviewed the above referenced January 28, 2022,
CMT report which determined the stability of the natural and proposed cut and fill slopes
associated with the subject lot yielded the City Geologic-Hazards Ordinance minimum

Geotechnical Engineering Report Summary for Plan Review

For plan review purposes, a summary of the geotechnical engineering recommendations
presented in the June 24, 2021, and July 13, 2021, CMT documents follow:

  1. All organics, topsoil, existing fill, and other deleterious material should be removed from
     below proposed building areas.

  2. The backfill for CMT investigative trenches ET-1 and ET-2 should be removed and
     compacted in lifts where overlain by footings or concrete flatwork.

  3. The residential dwelling may be placed within the suitable building area delineated in
     Figure 9 of the June 24, 2021, CMT report.

  4. Footings for the structures may be supported on suitable natural soils or compacted
     structural fill extending to suitable natural soils.

  5. Footings may be designed using a maximum allowable bearing pressure of 2,000 psf.

  6. Strip footings should have a minimum width of 18 inches.

  7. Spot footings should have a minimum width of 24 inches.

  8. Footings susceptible to frost should be located at a minimum depth of 36 inches.

  9. Footings not susceptible to frost should be located at a minimum depth of 16 inches.

      57 pcf for static conditions.

           b. Seismic Design Category = D2.

      fill/backfill and conduct in-place field density tests on the structural fill/backfill to confirm
