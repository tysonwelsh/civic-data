ORDINANCE 01-17-2023 TEMPORARY LAND USE FOR R3 ZONE ADOPTED:

   Mr. Atkinson explained that at the Planning Commission meeting on January 11th, the Commission
reviewed changes to each of the city’s residential zones and some commercial zones except for the R3
zone. While direction had been given regarding changes to R3, this particular zone was a topic of the
Affordable Housing Task Force. It was recommended by the Planning Commission that a temporary land
use ordinance be adopted for the R3 zone until the Task Force had completed their recommendations to
the City Council. Because of the time limitations on a temporary land use ordinance, the commission
also recommended that the Task Force complete their recommendations by March 31st. This would
give time to implement any potential changes to the R3 zone.
   Councilor Worwood moved to adopt Ordinance 01-17-2023 which institutes a land use restriction on
the R3 zone. Councilor Memmott seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

ORDINANCE DRAFT FOR AMENDING TITLE 10 DISCUSSED:

   City Planner Shauna Talbot discussed with the council the amendments to Title 10 of the Nephi City
code that the Planning Commission has been working on. Ms. Talbot also provided a summary chart
showing all of the zones and their specifications. Several of the amendments to the code include the
following:
     1. Some use changes in select zones to create consistency in terminology between zones.
     2. Setting minimum lot sizes in all zones, including commercial and industrial.
     3. Changing minimum lot sizes in select zones.
     4. Setting minimum frontage requirements in all zones, including commercial and industrial.
     5. Changing minimum setbacks in select zones for main buildings, and requiring setbacks in all
         zones, including industrial and commercial zones.
     6. Changing minimum setbacks in select zones for accessory buildings, and requiring setbacks in all
         zones, including industrial and commercial zones.
     7. Changing the code to have a set maximum height regulation in each zone, not subject to
         planning commission’s recommended approval to city council.
     8. Adding a new one-half acre zone that allows for animal rights.
     9. Recommending deletion of the zoning code related to “Planned Residential Developments”, as
         it conflicts with our “Planned Unit Development” code.

 Because the changes are extensive, the council asked for more time to review the revisions. The
council directed staff to bring the final draft back for adoption on February 7, 2023.

JOHNSON AGREEMENT APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson updated that council that the city had been approached about potential changes to the
property at 720 South and Main Street. He stated that the Johnson family who owns the property have
represented that they would like to refurbish the laundromat and remodel the remainder of the building
for a daycare. The RV Park that has been in violation of the RV park city code would need to be
removed to make room for daycare purposes.
    The city is currently in litigation with the owners over code violations at the RV Park. The proposed
agreement would allow the owners to comply with current and future city code and enable the
proposed improvements. The agreement allows for a reimbursement of the building permit fee if
certain conditions are met. This is in keeping with the intent of the blight ordinance and direction the
Council has given on administering a blighted area. Adhering to the agreement and the milestones
listed, also removes litigatory proceedings for the owners.
   Councilor Parady moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the agreement with the Johnson family.
Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

UTAH STATE DIVISION OF WILDLIFE RESOURCES AGREEMENT AMENDMENT APPROVED:

    Mr. Atkinson explained that over the past several months, the City has been engaged in a process to
transfer property owned by the City with property owned by the Division of Wildlife Resources (DWR).
This transfer of property would benefit DWR by providing more suitable wildlife habitat and benefit the
City by providing land used frequently by Nephi City residents and allow for additional expansion of the
Canyon Hills Golf Course.
   Through the process, some changes have occurred that differed from the original agreement.
Namely the following:
     The Yellow Book Appraisal of the properties showed that the value of the DWR parcel was
         $20,000 more than the parcel owned by the city.
     The cost of the Environmental Assessment (EA) was more than the original estimate.
     The weather and process delays have necessitated a new closing date(s) for the transfer.

 Councilor Memmott moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the amendment (1) to the agreement with
DWR. Councilor Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.
