A zone text change to amend Sections 7-1-109, 7-2-117, 7-10-103, 7-13-517, and 7-14-102 to
increase the construction threshold that triggers certain improvements and to allow the
option of payment in lieu of installation of off-site improvements.

In conjunction with Public Works staff, Community Development staff is proposing an
ordinance change to amend Sections 7-1-109, 7-2-117, 7-10-103, 7-13-517, and 7-14-102 to
increase the construction threshold that triggers certain improvements and to allow the option of
payment in lieu of installation of off-site improvements.

In September of 1984, the City adopted an ordinance (Ordinance 84-38) that required 20 feet of
landscaping along high-image arterials with “any substantial modification to an existing site or
structure in which the estimated construction cost exceeds $50,000”. Later, in March of 1988, the
City adopted an ordinance (Ordinance 88-08) that required curb, gutter, and sidewalk
improvements when “estimated construction costs exceed $50,000 for substantial modifications
to an existing site or structure”. While other improvements like streetlights and street paving
have since been added to the ordinance, the $50,000 construction threshold has remained in place
since the adoption of these ordinances.

Using the CPI (Consumer Price Index) Inflation Calculator from the Bureau of Labor Statistics,
$50,000 in September of 1984 would be worth $146,070 today. Using the same calculator,
$50,000 in March of 1988 would be worth $131,651 today. Hence, staff is recommending an
increase in this threshold from $50,000 to $150,000 to account for inflation.

Staff is also recommending adding the option of payment in lieu of installation of off-site
improvements. Currently, whenever off-site improvements like streetlights, street paving, curb,
gutter, and sidewalk are required, the ordinance allows: 1) the installation of the improvements,
2) a bond guaranteeing the completion of the improvements, or 3) at the discretion of the City, a
delay agreement. The City Council has generally chosen not to approval delay agreements over
the last few years. In addition, Public Works has found that exercising delay agreements has
proven to be difficult. As such, staff is proposing the addition of a payment in lieu of the
installation of off-site improvements as another alternative to installation, particularly where
there are no improvements on adjoining properties to connect to. The use of this new option
would be at the City Engineer’s sole discretion.

GUY WILLIAMS
2272 AND 2296 SOUTH 5600 WEST
ZONE CHANGE FROM LI TO M
3.65 ACRES

Guy Williams, the civil engineer, development consultant, and authorized agent for the property
owners, has submitted a zone change on two parcels totaling 3.65 acres at 2272 and 2296 South
5600 West from LI (Light Industrial) to M (Manufacturing). These two parcels are also within
5600 West Overlay Zone. The subject property is designated as Light Manufacturing in the West
Valley City General Plan.

Zoning on the surrounding property includes LI to the north, west, and south and M to the east.
Neighboring uses include:
     a truck, utility trailer, and RV rental and leasing business called Intermountain Trailer
       Sales and Service to the east;
     an automotive parts and accessories business called Chicks Auto Wreaking and a
       countertop fabrication and installation business called Rogers Marble and Granite to the
       south;
     a motorcycle, bicycle, and parts manufacturing business called Bills Bone Yard and
       Repair and an unapproved storage yard to the west;
     and vacant property to the north.

Included with this report is a map showing property ownership within the surrounding area. For
reference, Six Bugs Holdings, LLC, DCG Goat Corral, LLC, and H&W Development, LLC are
essentially the same entity or partnering entities. Also included is a map showing surrounding
properties with licensed businesses as well as properties without licensed businesses.

The smaller subject parcel to the south includes a 5,350 square foot metal building built in 1983
and two smaller buildings. No businesses are currently licensed on this parcel. The larger of the
two subject parcels to the north includes a 24,900 square foot building built in 2020. The
following four businesses are currently licensed on this parcel:
      a distributor for van upfitting parts called Crewvanco,
      a commercial truck and van equipment sales and installation business called Wasatch
       Clean Energy,
      a vehicle exterior wrapping service called Apex Wraps, and
      a vehicle rentals business called Kingbee Rentals.

The City’s Commercial and Manufacturing Zones use table is included with this report for
reference so the Commission can see the difference between uses allowed in both the LI and M
zones.

Development Proposal

The applicant is proposing the zone change for three reasons. The first is to allow outside storage
of new equipment/truck beds and upfitting parts. The LI zone prohibits outside storage while the
M zone allows it. The second reason for the zone change is to allow additional auto service use
which in this case is vehicle upfitting. The LI Zone includes the following provision:
“Automobile Service shall only be allowed as a Use that is incidental to a Permitted or
Conditional Use”. In addition to adding outside storage, the applicant is proposing to expand and
remodel the larger building on the south parcel to do more vehicle upfitting. The third reason for
the zone change is the LI Zone prohibits overhead doors from facing 5600 West. The plans for
the building on the south parcel include overhead doors on the east side of the building.

The applicant provided the following support for his application:

       “Our goal is to revitalize and redevelop the area in general along with returning the
       zoning to its original intent per the surrounding areas and General Plan. We want to
       update the frontage improvements and onsite improvements to be within city code and
       help beautify the area we are in.”

       “The adjacent land uses are the M-manufacturing zone. The area was moved to LI to
       eliminate the junk yards that surround it. We are working on cleaning up/improving the
       area and turning it into a master planned industrial park like the surrounding properties
       have been allowed to do.”

       “We are going to create new job opportunities for the community by being able to expand
       our business due to the change in zoning and expanded use of the area.”

       “We are requesting to be returned to the M – Manufacturing zone, to better facilitate
       Basin Upfitting, which is a light manufacturing company. We need storage space outside
       for our new equipment/truck beds and upfitting parts.”

If this rezone is approved, a conditional use permit will be required for the outside storage and
because the property is located within 5600 West Overlay Zone. The ordinance requires outside
storage uses to be screened from public view through fencing, landscaping, building placement,
topography, berming, or other methods as determined by the Planning Commission. For high-
image arterial streets like 5600 West, the ordinance was recently amended to require screening of
outside storage by means of a building or a 6’ tall, precast concrete wall meeting certain
specifications.

Area History

A brief history of the surrounding area between 5800 West and 5600 West and from 2200 South
to 2400 South (about 46 acres), which has been referred to as the salvage yards, is helpful
background information.
     Early 1960’s: Development began within the area with some unpaved roads and salvage
        yards.
     1980: By the time of the City’s incorporation in 1980, the area was occupied mostly by
        salvage yard businesses. The zoning in the area at the time was M-2, which was a zone
        for heavy industrial uses. The City also had a M-1 zone in 1980 for light industrial uses.
     1984: The City’s first General Plan designated the area as light industrial.
     1986: The City consolidated the M-1 and M-2 zones into the M zone.
     1999: The City’s General Plan included the goal of relocating or replacing the salvage
        yards from this gateway location.
     2001: The City created the 5600 W Gateway Redevelopment Area to provide incentives
        for redevelopment within the area.
     2004: The City formed a special improvement district (SID) to install utilities and road
        improvements in the area.
     2015: UDOT began to acquire land for the Mountain View Corridor which ultimately
        consumed about 22 acres of the 46 acres. UDOT will eventually sell about 5.5 acres of
        the 22 acres acquired that were not needed for the Mountain View Corridor project.
     2015: The City passed a moratorium or temporary land use regulation on all land use
        applications to reevaluate zoning in the area.
     2016: The City created the LI (Light Industrial) zone and rezoned the area to the new LI
        zone.
     2021: Mountain View Corridor was completed in this area.

In September of last year, the Planning Commission recommended approval of a zone change
application for property at 2256 and 2260 S 5700 W from the LI zone to the M zone. While the
City Council denied the application in November, the Council has since agreed to reconsider this
application on February 20th based on new information from the applicant about additional
improvements proposed.

Outstanding Violations

The City has been working with the owner of the north lot to bring it into compliance with City
ordinances. Items that still need to be addressed on the north lot include completing the storm
tech inspections, removing storage from the right-of-way, installing trees along Clifford St.,
finishing the parking lot review and correcting any deficiencies including the grade change to the
south entrance from 5600 W, completing the demo lot, maintaining the dumpster enclosure
gates, and installing landscaping and streetlights along 5600 W. The south lot is in violation of
current zoning because it is already being used for outside storage. In addition, the south lot
includes unapproved fence materials, fencing within the setback, and weeds within the adjoining
right-of-way.

Improvements

The subject properties have frontage on 5600 W, 2300 S, and Clifford St. The following
improvements are needed along these streets:
    curb, gutter, sidewalk, streetlights, and landscaping along 2300 S;
    5600 W streetscape improvements (sidewalk, streetlights, public easement for sidewalk,
       lighting and utilities, and landscaping, etc.) and
    street paving, curb, gutter, sidewalk, streetlights, and landscaping along Clifford Street
       unless the road is vacated.

As described in the history above, significant progress has been made in transitioning this area
from mostly salvage yards to either vacant property awaiting development or light industrial
uses. If the Planning Commission is supportive of a zone change to the M zone here to
accommodate the outside storage, staff is concerned about the possibility of a future use that
would be counter to the transitions occurring in the area. In addition, staff is also concerned
about the outstanding issues on the properties.

SHANE MCKNIGHT
4113 S RUSHFORD COURT
ZONE CHANGE FROM R-1-8 TO R-1-7
0.17 ACRES

Shane McKnight has submitted a zone change application for a 0.17-acre parcel located at 4113
S Rushford Ct. The property is currently zoned R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential,
minimum lot size 8,000 sq. ft.) with a General Plan designation of Low Density Residential (3 to
4 units/acre). The proposed zone is R-1-7 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size
7,000 sq. ft.).

Surrounding zones include R-1-8 to the east, south, and west and A (Agriculture, minimum lot
size ½ acre) to the north. As a reminder, City ordinance only allows zone changes to R-1-7 on
properties less than 2 acres. Surrounding uses include single unit dwellings on the west and
south, Mountain View Corridor to the east, and vacant property to the north on the north side of
4100 South. The subject property is vacant.

Background

This 7,293 square foot parcel, known as Lot 1A of the Hunter Cove Mountain View Corridor
Subdivision, was created from Lots 1 and 2 of the Hunter Cove Subdivision. As part of the
Mountain View Corridor project, UDOT acquired Lots 1 and 2 of the Hunter Cove Subdivision
and demolished the homes on these lots to facilitate the widening of 4100 South, the termination
of Rushford Court in a cul-de-sac, and the construction of the shared use path west of the
Mountain View Corridor. Following the completion of the Mountain View Corridor project,
UDOT sold the subject parcel in March of 2023.

Since the subject parcel is below the current minimum lot size of 8,000 square feet, the applicant
is requesting to rezone the property to the R-1-7 zone so that it will comply with the minimum
lot size requirement.

If this application is approved, the applicant intends to build a new home on the subject property.
The applicant has submitted a conceptual site plan, front elevation, and floor plans for the
property. The proposed home would be 1,420 square feet in area and include an unfinished
basement and a two-car garage. The proposed exterior materials include brick and fiber cement
siding. The proposed roof pitch is 6:12 with laminated architectural shingles. Based on a
preliminary review by staff, the proposed home meets the City’s current design standards.

Typically, a zone change for residential is accompanied by a development agreement. However,
in this case, a development agreement is not required. The Zoning Ordinance states: “All new
Subdivisions involving a rezone of property, or a PUD, shall participate in a Development

Agreement that addresses housing size, quality, exterior finish materials, Streetscapes,
Landscaping, etc.” Since the subject parcel exists, no new subdivision is involved. While a
development agreement is not required, the Planning Commission could still recommend a
development agreement to the City Council.

In staff’s view, the applicant’s proposal is a good use for a challenging, remnant parcel.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. The application should be approved as it complies with the General Plan.
   2. Continuance.

Impact signs is requesting conditional use approval for outside storage. The property is zoned M,
Manufacturing, and the West Valley City General Plan designates this area as light
manufacturing. The surrounding zoning is also M. Adjacent land uses include light industrial,
warehousing, and outside storage.

The subject property is located just west of a light industrial complex. The overall parcel is 1.9
acres. The eastern edge of the parcel is used as vehicular access for the parcel to the east. The
remaining portions of the parcel are either vacant and have no approved uses or have been
approved for outside storage. This proposal will occupy 0.25 acres of the parcel. The Planning
Commission reviewed a similar application in this space in 2021, ZC 20210003. The approval
at that time was for outside storage of trailers, equipment and materials, but the applicant never
followed through by getting a final grading and drainage plan approved, so the approval
expired.

The applicant for this request is also the owner of the subject parcel, and for the parcel to the
east. The storage area will be used for trailer and sign inventory. Per 7-7-119, outside storage
areas are to be screened from public view. The previous tenant enclosed the area with a chain
link fence, which has slats, so the space is already screened. The Planning Commission is also
tasked with evaluating surfacing based on the 7-7-119 requirements. The applicant is
requesting to use gravel for surfacing. A majority of the site already has gravel, but some of the
areas need more coverage. The gravel surface should be sufficient since the area will not be
used for vehicle storage, and the storage site is quite far from the public right-of-way. The
entire area should have coverage of at least 4 inches of depth of gravel, and it should kept
weed-free. A grading and drainage plan is also required.

The storage area directly north of the subject space was approved last year for automobile
storage, but that approval will expire next month if a grading and drainage plan is not approved,
and pavement added to the site (ZC 20230005). There’s a smaller storage area in the northwest
corner of the property that is paved and has been approved for storage. South of the subject
storage site is an area of the parcel that is roughly 0.60 acres. That area has no approvals and
must remain undeveloped and free of any vehicles or storage. There is also one other small
outside storage area approved in the south-east corner of the adjacent parcel. No other outside
storage than what was previously mentioned in this report is allowed on the subject parcel or on
the adjacent parcel to the east. No vehicles shall be stored in these areas for longer than 24
hours. Drive aisles of at least 24 feet in width must also be maintained between the buildings
and the storage areas or property lines.

The adjacent parcel already meets the required 5% landscaping for the site, so no additional
landscaping is needed. Landscaped areas on the property shall be maintained in good
condition.
