1. 24-288 Council Member Dekeyzer recommending the Council receive a
presentation on Vision Zero

Attachments: Vision Zero in SLC 2024

Council Member Aaron Dekeyzer introduced John Larson, Transportation Director for Salt
Lake City, who presented to the Council on the Vision Zero Safe System Approach.
Vision Zero is a philosophy on how we look at our changing transportation system. The
Vision Zero approach was described as follows: traffic deaths are preventable, integrate
human failing in approach, prevent fatal and severe crashes, systems approach, and
saving lives is not expensive. Speed is the biggest factor related to injuries and fatalities.
Mr. Larson spoke about the effectiveness of designing streets to minimize traffic
injuries/fatalities. He reviewed with the Council the Vision Zero Actions implemented by
Salt Lake City, street and intersection typologies design guide, and transit corridor
makeovers. He provided examples of projects in Salt Lake City. Council questions and
comments followed. Council thanked Mr. Larson for his presentation.
Council moved to General Citizen Comment.

2. 24-289 Public Works recommending the Council receive an overview of the WFRC
Comprehensive Safety Action Plan

Attachments: \WFRC CSAP CC Presentation 08202024

Ryan Kump and Britney Ward, Public Works, provided an overview of the Wasatch Front
Area Comprehensive Safety Action Plan, a plan to provide local governments the means
to make strategic roadway safety improvements. The Wasatch Front Regional Council is
overseeing and leading the program. She reviewed the Safe Streets and Roads for All
(SS4A) grant program which will award $5 billion over 5 years from FY 22 to FY 26. There
are two grant types: Planning and Demonstration and Implementation. More than $1.7
billion has been awarded in FY 2022 and FY 2023. She spoke about the Safe System
Approach which is an approach to transportation system design and operation that
should reduce crash severity and save lives by anticipating human mistakes and
lessening impact forces. Ms. Ward reviewed a detailed safety analysis of intersections,
roadway departures, speed related, and pedestrians and provided examples of case
study project locations in Sandy. The program has established a goal to reduce deaths
and serious injuries for all roadway users by 50% by the year 2040 and to reduce
roadway fatalities and serious injuries by 2.5% each year compared to the preceding
three-year rolling average. Council questions followed.
Council Member Marci Houseman was excused at 7:15 pm to attend an event at the
Canyons School District..
Ryan Kump acknowledged the work that Ms. Ward has done on preparing and filing for
grants. Public Works has been very successful in attaining federal grants for these
projects. More funding is needed to continue work on future projects.

3. 24-287 Sandy Fire Department requesting the Council receive an update on
Wildland Deployment

Attachments: Wildland Deployment Presentation

Chief Ryan McConaghie provided an update to the Council on Wildland deployment of
city fire personnel. He introduced Battalion Chief Matt Stuebner who spoke on the
deployment experience and process. He has been a part of the wildland deployment
program for ten years. Members of the Sandy Fire Department are Red Card certified
which is an accepted interagency certification that a person is qualified to do the required
job when arriving on incident. Deployments are done through Interagency Resource
Ordering Capability (IROC) and Emergency Management Assistance Compact (EMAC).
He spoke about the commitment, reimbursements, training, and benefits of participating
in wildland deployments. The experience is very valuable for our firefighters and he
recognized firefighters in the chambers who were back from a deployment. Mayor
Zoltanski and the Council thanked the fire department for their service and for their help to
other agencies in fighting wildland fires.
