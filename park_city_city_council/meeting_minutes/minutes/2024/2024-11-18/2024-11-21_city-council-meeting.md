 Discuss Transit Pilot Projects & Transit App Updates: 
 Hannah Pack and Tim Sanderson, Transportation Department, presented this item. 
 Pack indicated in Park City Forward, the City’s long-term transportation plan, there was 
 a goal to analyze disruptive ideas for feasibility. They recently studied a vanpool pilot 
 program, a taxi reimbursement pilot program, storage lockers at the Old Town Transit 
 Center (OTTC), and transitioning to a different transit app. 
  
 Vanpool Pilot Program: 
 Pack proposed partnering with Commute with Enterprise, who had been in business for 
 many years. They offered flexible leasing options to meet demand in different seasons. 
 There was a mandatory subsidy by employers using the program and the Transit 
 Department proposed offering a $750 subsidy per month per vehicle with a cap of 
 $80,000 per year.  PARK CITY COUNCIL MEETING

 Taxi Reimbursement Pilot:  
 Sanderson explained this idea was planned for areas not suitable for fixed route transit 
 services. The City would reimburse riders $10 per ride up to $80,000 per year for using 
 a local taxi service.  
 
Storage Lockers and Transit App: 
 Pack stated storage lockers were placed in the OTTC and would be rented for $1 per 
 hour or $10 per day maximum, if approved. She also explained the new Transit app that 
 was launched in April and stated it integrated with UDOT and High Valley Transit. She 
 related the features of the app. 
  
 Sanderson stated funding for these programs could be from the money budgeted for the 
 microtransit service, which was terminated.  
  
 Council Member Toly asked if the 664 households that would qualify for taxi 
 reimbursement included all households or was that the number of primary resident 
 households. Pack indicated there were 224 primary resident households. Council 
 Member Toly asked if the storage lockers would be cleaned out at night or if renters 
 would be charged daily, to which Pack stated either was an option. Council Member Toly 
 asked if the transit app could get people to downtown Salt Lake City to the Trax service, 
 to which Pack affirmed. Council Member Toly asked if they talked to other businesses in 
town about the vanpool pilot and asked if it could be incorporated for the small 
businesses. Pack indicated she met with HPCA and the resorts and there was interest. 
Many of them already used the UTA option but the trip had to begin and end in Salt 
Lake County, so those riders would be ineligible for this program. Council Member Toly 
 asked if that could be done at City Hall, to which Pack affirmed. 
  
 Council Member Ciraco stated he supported the vanpool program and favored putting 
 more money to that program and less money to the taxi reimbursement program. Pack 
 explained the process of billing for the vans. She stated the employees would drive the 
 vans. Council Member Ciraco asked if multiple businesses could use the same van, to 
 which Pack thought it was possible as long as the employer was willing to allow non-
 employees to use it since they were subsidizing the van. Council Member Ciraco hoped 
 the seats could be maximized by sharing the van between multiple employers. He also 
 asserted the storage locker program was a great idea and suggested putting them at 
 various places around town.  
  
 Council Member Rubell asked how they would ensure this vanpool would not 
 cannibalize the UTA vanpool. Pack stated the UTA vanpool was only for Salt Lake City 
 riders, and this would target Summit and Wasatch Counties, so there was no overlap, 
 unless there was a request for service for all City employees. Council Member Rubell 
 referred to the local taxi program and asked if they would be metered, to which 
 Sanderson stated they could do that, and noted there needed to be some way to 
 determine the integrity of the program.

 Council Member Parigian stated a subsidy for a roundtrip on the vanpool would be $6 
 per rider. He suggested grouping riders according to their city of residence. He also 
 thought a taxi reimbursement could be complicated. Pack explained the user would pay 
 the taxi driver and then request reimbursement from City Hall. Council Member Parigian 
asked what the administrative cost would be to run the program. Pack stated they 
proposed an $80,000 cap so the pilot would end after that, and people would not be 
 reimbursed. Council Member Parigian suggested renting the lockers by the month. He 
 asked if the City owned the lockers, to which Pack affirmed, but noted they did not own 
 the software associated with the lockers. Regarding the Transit app, Council Member 
 Parigian felt it was not intuitive for riders like him. 
  
 Council Member Dickey was glad the City was trying different programs. He liked the 
 vanpool, lockers, and transit app. He was willing to try the taxi reimbursement program 
 but he thought there would be a lot of administrative costs and he didn’t think it would 
 get much traction. Mayor Worel asked if the app was bilingual, to which Pack affirmed. 
 Council Member Dickey suggesting adding Uber and Lyft as well as taxis for the taxi 
 program. He was fine with anyone using the taxi program, not just primary residents. 
 Mayor Worel supported the taxi program but did not favor using Uber and Lyft. She 
 wondered about capacity that taxis could handle because they were losing drivers to 
 Uber and Lyft. She suggested talking with the taxi companies to see if they could handle 
 it. She supported the lockers and vanpool programs. 
  
 Council Member Toly stated she supported putting more money into the vanpool 
 program if it was successful. She thought there were still questions about the taxi 
 program that needed to be worked out. Pack summarized the vanpool pilot was 
 supported at the current proposed subsidy and locker prices were supported as well.
