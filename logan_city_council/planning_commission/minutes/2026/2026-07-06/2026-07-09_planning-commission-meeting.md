PC 26-024 Bullen Properties Tower Relocation [Conditional Use & Design Review] RAGE
Development/Bullen Properties LC, authorized agent/owner is requesting approval to replace the
existing 100-foot lattice telecommunications tower, located in the middle of the parcel, with a new
100-foot monopole telecommunications tower. The proposed monopole will be located on the
southeast corner of the 2.98-acre parcel at 1350 North 200 West (TIN 05-042-0009), within the
Commercial (COM) zoning district, in the Bridger Neighborhood.

Staff: Aaron Smith, Planner, reviewed the request for a new wireless communication tower located
at 1350 North 200 West in the Commercial zone. The proposal includes removing the existing 7-foot
wide, 100-foot-tall lattice tower located in the center of the property, relocating the 20-foot by 50-foot
equipment base from the center of the site to the southeast corner, and installing a new 2-foot-wide,
100-foot-tall monopole.

The proposed tower will be setback more than 300 feet from the nearest street and more than 350
feet from the nearest residential area and structure, exceeding the required 200-foot setback. The
tower and ground equipment are also required to meet minimum setbacks of 8 feet from the side
property line and 10 feet from the rear property line. As proposed, however, the ground equipment is
located approximately 7 feet 6 inches from both the side and rear property lines and will need to be
adjusted to comply with the required setbacks.

The Land Development Code (LDC) allows antennas to project no more than 3 feet from the pole.
The proposed design includes a maximum antenna projection of 7 feet 3 inches.

The existing tower is considered a legally existing nonconforming structure because
telecommunication towers in the Commercial zone are limited to 70 feet in height. The LDC requires
nonconforming structures to be brought into compliance before being moved or relocated. However,
federal regulations protecting wireless communication facilities allow deviations from local standards
if the applicant can provide radio frequence (RF) data demonstrating that additional height or

Staff determined that relocating the tower would significantly improve the site and reduce off -site
visual impacts. Moving the tower to the southeast corner places it further from adjacent residential
properties and streets while positioning it closer to existing commercial buildings, approximately 112
feet from the strip mall to the east, and 80 feet from Sam’s Club to the south. Relocating the tower
also improves future development opportunities by removing the tower from the center of the
property.
The LDC requires ground equipment in the Commercial zone to be screened by a solid wall with
perimeter landscaping. The proposal includes an 8-foot chain-link fence with anti-climb wire, similar
to the existing condition. Staff recommended Condition 5 requiring the installation of a minimum 6-
foot solid wall with perimeter landscaping to provide adequate screening.
Because the proposed tower encroaches into an existing landscaped area, Staff also recommended
that existing mature trees be preserved as much as possible to maintain a vegetative buffer between
the tower, equipment base, and adjacent commercial properties.
Proponent: Rock Schutjer explained that relocating the tower would allow future development of the
property while maintaining existing wireless coverage. He noted that the tower has supported two
wireless carriers since 2000 and explained that the current wireless network has been built around
the existing tower height. Lowering the tower would reduce coverage and could require construction
of additional towers to compensate. Mr. Schutjer also noted that with a new 100-foot tower they will
be able to add additional wireless carriers.

Commissioner Maughan asked about the structural stability of a monopole. Mr. Schutjer explained
that a 100-foot monopole would require a foundation approximately 5 to 6 feet in diameter extending
roughly 30 feet below ground.

Public: No public comments were made.

Commission: The Commission agreed that maintaining the existing wireless coverage justified
approval of the requested deviations. The Commission amended Condition 3 to allow the tower to
remain 100 feet in height and amended Condition number 4 to allow the antennas to extend as
proposed.

PC 26-023 LDC Amendment - Wetlands -Continued from the June 11th meeting. [Code
Amendment] Russ Holley/Logan City are proposing amendments to the Land Development Code
Chapter 17.24.110 Development Standards for Wetlands.

Staff/Proponent: Community Development Director Russ Holley explained that the item was
continued from the June 11, 2026, Planning Commission meeting to allow staff time to prepare maps
illustrating undeveloped properties, site acreage, and the impacts of proposed wetland setbacks.

R. Holley reviewed the proposed amendments, which would:
• Better define when a wetland delineation is required and who determines that requirement based
   National Wetlands Inventory (NWI) mapping and site analysis.
• Require development applications involving mapped wetlands to obtain written approval of a
   wetland delineation from the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (USACE) or a written conformation
   form the USACE that wetlands are not present on the subject property, before an application may
   be accepted.
• More clearly define where setbacks are measured from and establish different project categories
   with varying setback standards.

•   Establish no-disturbance areas and replace the current percentage-based disturbance
    allowances with linear setbacks requirements.

As proposed, development projects of 10 acres or more would require a 50-foot setback for
structures, paved parking areas, and driveways, and a 15-foot setback for stormwater facilities,
sidewalks, trails, patios, and other site improvements. Development projects of less than 10 acres
would require a 20-foot setback for structures, paved parking areas, and driveways, and a 15-foot
setback for other site improvements.

For public and recreational projects, the proposal would require a 20-foot setback for structures,
paved parking areas, and driveways, and a 15-foot setback for stormwater facilities, streets, roads,
sidewalks, trails, and similar improvements. Boardwalk trails could be permitted within wetlands and
setback areas with approval from the USACE. For residential subdivisions, no new building lot line
could be established within 10 feet of a delineated wetland. Wetlands and associated no-disturbance
areas would be placed on separate parcels and maintained in accordance with project approvals and
City regulations.

R. Holley explained that the 10-acre distinction was based on the historic logan block size, which
originated from the Public Land Survey System (PLSS) and the townships, range, and section layout
established from the Salt Lake City Base Meridian.

R. Holley reviewed wetland areas located west of 1000 West and presented several large
development sites, identifying each property’s total acreage, building area, and open
space/landscaping area. He also reviewed several vacant properties containing wetlands and
illustrated the percentage of each property that would be affected by wetlands and the existing 50-
foot setback requirement.

Commissioner Maughan asked whether the required setback should instead be based on the
percentage of wetlands present on a site.

Commissioner Doutre suggested that sites with 10 percent or less wetlands could require a 50-foot
setback, while sites with 40 percent or more wetlands could require a 20-foot setback.

Public: Joshua Moliter stated that wetland setbacks should begin at 50 feet and increase for larger
developments to better preserve wetlands in the valley. He commented that wildlife and other species
also have a right to the land and that accommodating new development should not be the primary
objective.

Jeannie Simmonds stated that the Municipal Council’s intent was to preserve wetlands but agreed
to revisit the ordinance because of issues involving recreation facilities and street construction.

Commission: R. Holley noted that developers have the option, through USACE, to mitigate impacts
by removing wetlands from the property when permitted.

Commissioner Duncan agreed with the public comment, stating that developments other than parks
and recreational uses should not be constructed immediately adjacent to wetlands.

Commissioner Doutre recommended removing the proposed distinction between developments
greater than and less than 10 acres by eliminating the 20-foot setback for smaller developments and
applying the 50-foot setback to all private development while keeping the remainder of the proposed
code unchanged.

Commissioner Doutre asked which projects had been prevented from moving forward under the
existing 50-foot setback requirement. Mohammed Abdullahi, City Attorney, responded that one
residential subdivision and one trail project had been unable to proceed because of the current
setback.

Commissioner Lewis stated that establishing a consistent wetland setback would provide greater
certainty for developers than using a variable setback based on percentage of wetlands on a site.

Commissioner Heare agreed with Commissioner Doutre, stating that the 50-foot setback should
remain for private development, while the proposed exceptions for public, recreational, and
residential subdivision projects should remain.

Commissioner Doutre proposed the following amendments to the proposed code:
  • Remove proposed section D.3 relating to private development projects of less than 10 acres.
  • Amend section D.2 so the 50-foot setback requirement applies to all private development
     projects, regardless of acreage.
