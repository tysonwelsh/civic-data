                                         BRIEFING SESSION – 6:00 P.M.

    1.   Commissioner Burggraaf conducted roll call of Commissioners and advised that Commissioner Wendel
         was unable to participate this evening and excused her. All remaining Commissioners were present. He
         turned the time over to Mr. Spung to review the agenda. Mr. Spung advised that the agenda tonight
         consisted of a General Plan Amendment for Cowboy Partners at 1758 West 5400 South from Regional
         Commercial to High-Density Mixed-Use; a zoning map amendment for Jay Bollwinkel at 1633 West 6235
         South from R-1-10 to Professional Office (PO); a General Plan, Zoning Map and Zoning Text Amendment
         for Thackeray Company at 3815-3879 West 5400 South (old K-Mart site); a Zoning Text Amendment to

         drive-up and drive-through uses and corresponding definitions. He discussed all items except for the ones

         Commissioner Burggraaf reminded Staff about his request that the Commissioners be provided refresher
         training in the near future. Mr. Spung thanked him for the reminder and advised it would be scheduled
         as soon as is possible.

         Commissioner Burggraaf gave his report on the City Council meeting he was assigned to attend.

2.         File #1G21 –Chris Zarek (Cowboy Partners) - Recommendation to the City Council for a General Plan
           Map Amendment for the Property Located at 1758 West 5400 South from Regional Commercial to
           High-Density Mixed-Use. (Jim Spung, AICP/Senior Planner).


2.1      Mr. Jim Spung presented this item.              He advised, the applicant Mr. Chris Zarek is requesting to
         change the Proposed Land Use Map designation in the City’s General Plan for the property at 1758 W.
         5400 S. from “Regional Commercial” to “High-Density Mixed-Use.” The applicant is seeking this General
         Plan Map amendment with the intention to develop a four-story mixed-use and multi-family residential
         building on a vacant site in the Legacy Plaza shopping center.

         2.1.1     Mr. Spung went over the conceptual development plans in his presentation, which includes a
                   subsequent Site-Specific Development (SSD) rezone request to be approved by the City Council.
                   He also displayed conceptual elevations of the proposed building. The subject property is 3.83
                   acres on partially undeveloped land in the Legacy Plaza shopping center and has direct vehicle
                   access to 5400 South as well as Redwood Road via shared cross-access easement. The property
                   has surface parking improvements on portions of the lot while much of the lot is an undeveloped
                   (dirt) commercial pad.

         2.1.2     GENERAL FINDINGS: Mr. Spung went over the General Findings as follows:
                   2.1.2.1 This application was initiated by Chris Zarek on behalf of Cowboy Partners.
                   2.1.2.2 The applicant is requesting to amend the proposed Land Use Map in the General Plan for
                           the property at 1758 W. 5400 S. (parcel 21-10-379-034-0000) from “Regional
                           Commercial” to “High-Density Mixed-Use.”
                   2.1.2.3 The subject property is within the Center Point Small Area Plan.
                   2.1.2.4 The subject property is within the Community Commercial (CC) zoning district.
                   2.1.2.5 The applicant’s proposed concept development plan includes a 4-story residential multi-
                           family product.
                   2.1.2.6 City Staff does not support the applicant’s request for the reasons specific in the Staff
                           Report. (stated below)
                           2.1.2.6.1     It is not consistent with the adopted General Plan or Center Point Small
                                         Area Plan.
                           2.1.2.6.2     It would create a high-density residential project in the middle of a vehicle-
                                         oriented commercial center.
                           2.1.2.6.3     It is isolated, out of scale, and out of character with the area and the rest
                                         of the shopping center.
                           2.1.2.6.4     The three commercial centers on the intersection of 5400 South and
                                         Redwood Road are the key economic drivers in Taylorsville. The current
                                         adopted policy is to prioritize and promote retail and commercial uses in
                                         this area.
                           2.1.2.6.5     The project may negatively impact residential neighborhoods and property
                                         owners to the north and west due to building height, density, placement,
                                         setbacks, circulation, etc.

2.3      APPLICANT ADDRESS: Chris Zarek and Troy Hardy spoke for the applicant.                 Mr. Zarek stated that
         retail is ever-evolving with changes. He felt this shopping center meets the criteria for a successful center
         and already has sufficient commercial and there is no need for an additional mid-size mix anchor business
         there. He felt adding residential multi-family would re-energize the whole center. He continued saying
         that Mr. David Wertz (owner of the site) bought this property six years ago and has been trying to attract
         retail businesses to this site to no avail. He has attended retail conferences in Las Vegas many times
         trying to promote that endeavor. The center is losing money with vacancies and has 14,000 square feet
         of vacant shopping area and 100,000 square feet of filled space. Therefore, multi-family units would help
         ease that problem. The project being proposed (Liberty Beacon) is extremely functional and beautiful. It
         is not low-income housing and mixed-use projects strengthen the core of shopping centers. Mr. Zarek
         reviewed Staff’s reasons for non-support of this project and offered the following comments. He said
         they are well aware that they need the Planning Commission’s support to be able to do this and hoped to
         get a different conclusion from Staff by showing the benefit of adding medium-density housing to this
         area, which he felt would attract tenants more readily. He added that the plan they are proposing is well-
         planned and aesthetically beautiful. Staff’s first objection was that it is not consistent with the adopted
         General Plan or Master Plan for this site. Mr. Zarek advised the main reason they were at this meeting

 3.1             Mr. Spung presented this item. He stated that Mr. Bollwinkel, owner’s agent, is requesting a zoning
           map amendment to rezone the property at 1633 West 6235 South from Single-Family Residential (R-1-10)
           to Professional Office (PO). The applicant is seeking this change with the intention to create a new access
           for the properties at 6235 and 6243 South Redwood Road and to allow for the expansion of the parking
           area for the existing office building at 6243 South Redwood Road. If the proposed zoning map
           amendment is approved by the City Council, the applicant will be required to complete a subdivision
           amendment or lot-line adjustment prior to any development or construction to relocate property
           boundaries and record necessary easements for access. They will also be required to complete the site
           plan review process prior to any construction to ensure compliance with all city development standards.
           Subject property is 28,750 square feet (0.66 acres) and is currently a detached single-family residence.
           Mr. Spung advised that the applicant’s proposal to rezone the subject property to Professional Office (PO)
           is consistent with the adopted policies and goals in the City’s General Plan, therefore, Staff recommends
           approval of this zoning amendment request. Commissioner Wright wanted to know if the reason for the
           secondary access is that the Redwood Road improvement project is going to include all the way to I-215.
           Mr. Harper advised it will start at the Great Harvest site and be a three-lane continuous flow intersection.
           Commissioner Wright said his biggest concern is the lack of green space.

                    maximum length of private lanes from 150 feet to 300 feet. The amendment also clarifies
                    current practice – that a private lane exceeding 150 feet in length must have an approved turn-
                    around per City and Fire Code standards.

                    maximum length of private lanes from 150 feet to 300 feet. The amendment also clarifies

           7.1.4    Since there is little undeveloped land in Taylorsville, most opportunities for development are on

                    and developers due to property size, access, utilities, or other factors, making it difficult to
                    maximize its use within the confines of the City’s zoning rules. In addition, an evolving local
                    housing market is placing increased pressure on communities that have traditionally focused on
                    single-family detached residential construction, to shift and accommodate smaller infill products
                    that maximize efficiency and increase affordability. The demand for housing in the Salt Lake
                    Valley has substantially increased in the last decade, which has added to the pressure of

                    access via a shared access from a public street and not require the construction of a full-width
                    public street, which is increasingly difficult to do for infill development. A shared driveway also

8.1        Mr. Spung presented this item. The City of Taylorsville is proposing to amend the Land Development
           Code to clarify and update standards related to drive-through facilities. The City of Taylorsville has
           requested that Staff review the standards regulating drive-through facilities to remove unnecessary
           barriers to development and to simplify the standards. Specifically, the minimum distance to residential
           zoning district requirements has limited the potential use of commercially zoned parcels on major
           corridors that are promising locations for a drive-through facility. In lieu of establishing a minimum
           distance from drive-through facilities to residential zoning districts, Staff believes the standards regulating
           the design of the facility is more important. For example, potential negative impacts can be addressed
           through general buffering/screening requirements, minimum setbacks, and placement and design of
           audible devices used.

8.2        The following summary of the key changes was discussed:
           8.2.1    Update the use permissions in the land use table for drive-through uses. Drive-through uses are
                    no longer a “primary land use”, but rather, “accessory” to the primary business that it serves
                    (bank, restaurant, coffee shop, drug store, etc.).
           8.2.2    Drive-through facilities are now permitted (subject to design standards) in all commercial zoning
                    districts, except Mixed-Use (MU) and Transit Corridor (TC) zoning districts.
           8.2.3    Planning Commission review and/or conditional use permit review is no longer required for
                    drive-through facilities.
           8.2.4    Removed the 250-foot distance requirement for drive-through facilities to residential zoning
                    districts. The design standards address compatibility and design without the need for additional
                    distance requirements.
           8.2.5    Drive-through facilities and queuing lanes are now required to comply with the minimum
                    building setbacks of the underlying zoning district.
           8.2.6    Comprehensive update of the minimum number of stacking spaces required. Restaurants saw
                    the most dramatic change from 9 spaces to 5 spaces.
           8.2.7    Strengthened language related to queuing lane placement on the building site.
           8.2.8    Removed standards limiting the number of queuing lanes allowed.
           8.2.9    Removed language requiring the building to be built at the front setback line. Prohibiting a
                    queuing lane between the building and street should inherently push the building closer to the
                    street.
           8.2.10 Updated off-street parking table to reflect changes in land use table.
           8.2.11 Updated definition for “Business or Financial Services” to remove reference to drive-up facilities.
           8.2.12 New-definition for “Drive-through.”
           8.2.13 Updated definition for “Restaurant.”
