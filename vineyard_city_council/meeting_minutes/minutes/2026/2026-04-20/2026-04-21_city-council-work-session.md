3. WORK SESSION
3.1 Discuss SB-284 and Resolution Pack

Council Member Lauret noted the need to compare the City’s current zoning code with the items
listed in the initial resolution to identify gaps. He requested Planner Cache Hancey provide a
brief report.

Planner Hancey provided background information on the City’s zoning and land use practices
and reviewed the City’s authority under the Utah Municipal Land Use, Development, and
Management Act (LUDMA), Title 10, Chapter 9a of the Utah Code. The presentation outlined
permitted, conditional, temporary, and non-permitted uses, including the review and approval
processes and the City’s ability to impose reasonable conditions on conditional uses.

Staff reviewed the City’s zoning framework, including the base Vineyard zoning code and
special purpose zoning districts, and explained options available when proposed developments
do not conform to existing zoning, including rezonings and development agreements. It was
noted that state law prohibits requiring a development agreement for compliant permitted uses.

Staff also summarized key requirements of SB 284, including posting fees and application
checklists online, timelines and appeal rights related to Planning Commission actions, limits on
third-party appeals, changes to appeal authorities, Planning Commission training requirements,
and updates to detached ADU (Accessory Dwelling Units) standards. Staff indicated the City is
largely compliant but will need to review and update certain code provisions in coordination with
legal counsel.

Staff offered to provide further comments on the proposed resolutions and noted that water rights
issues under SB 284 would be deferred to Public Work Director/Engineer Naseem Ghandour.

Mr Ghandour addressed questions related to water rights requirements under SB 284, focusing
on proportionality and exactions. It was explained that municipalities may only require water
rights in proportion to the actual impact and water demand of the approved use. A city may not
require a developer to dedicate more water rights than necessary for the proposed use.

Staff clarified that while cities are not obligated to provide water rights to developments,
municipalities may adopt policies requiring developers to provide or purchase water rights.
Vineyard’s current structure includes developers purchasing water rights through established fees
tied to water rights already owned by the City.

Staff noted that proposed resolutions contemplate further direction to develop formal policies
regarding water rights requirements. Final legal interpretation and policy guidance on these
matters would be provided by the City Attorney.

3.2. Resolution RDA-2026-02 Hiring Up To Three Advisors For The RDA Board

David Luray presented a resolution proposing authorization to establish up to three RDA advisor
positions to assist with an agreed-upon procedures audit of the Redevelopment Agency (RDA). The
advisors would support the auditor, Chris Harding, by gathering historical records, contracts, and
institutional knowledge related to prior RDA actions, policies, and financial decisions.

Council discussed that RDA documentation over several decades is fragmented or incomplete,
particularly regarding large financial commitments and interlocal participation. The advisors would

assist in reconstructing historical context, evaluating legal and financial frameworks, and supporting

the development of improved policies and procedures. The discussion clarified that this effort is not
a forensic audit and is not anticipated to result in litigation.

Councilmembers asked questions regarding the proposed structure, scope, and duration of the
positions, including whether they should be filled as employees or contractors and whether
permanent RDA staffing should be considered. It was clarified that positions would follow a standard
hiring process, would be temporary and objective-based, and may be adjusted after completion of
the audit.

Council generally expressed support for obtaining additional resources to improve transparency,
accountability, and future governance of the RDA. No formal action was taken at this time.

4. COMMITTEE REPORTS
4.1 Volunteer Changes and Support

Council Member Holdaway reported regarding outreach and coordination with local faith-based and
community organizations for volunteer efforts. It was reported that multiple religious organizations
participated in meetings and expressed strong interest in supporting City events and service projects.

The committee noted that participating organizations expressed a desire for clearer direction from
the City regarding volunteer opportunities, including specific dates, number of volunteers needed,
duration, and required materials. It was suggested that the City improve coordination by annually
identifying volunteer and service opportunities for departments such as the Library, Vineyard Cares,
Recreation Center, Events, and Public Works.

Council discussed the concept of making the City more “volunteer-ready” by compiling and
publishing a centralized list of volunteer needs each year, allowing organizations to plan and commit
resources in advance, Feedback indicated that organizations are willing to fulfill identified needs if
opportunities are clearly communicated.

No formal action was taken.

A proclamation to formally recognize Arbor Day and encourage residents to
participate in tree planting and urban forestry efforts. Arbor Day has been observed
nationally since 1872 and promotes environmental stewardship and community

efforts to plant and protect trees and protect our wetlands.
