       ORDINANCE – Amending portions of Chapter 2 Land Use and Chapter 6 Transportation of
       the Orem General Plan updating the Land Use Map and Pedestrian and Bike Path Map and
       language pertaining to the southwest area

Mr. Bench said the Southwest area was annexed by the City in 2015. At the time of annexation, a
general plan map was adopted by the City and generally included a light industrial designation for
property on the west side of Geneva Road and a low density residential designation for property
on the east side of Geneva road.

With the announcement of the temple site at 1471 South Geneva Road and the additional interest
in development in the general area, the City Council asked staff to review the current general plan
for the area. Based on the review process, staff proposed several changes to the General Plan for
the Southwest area.

Staff held an online neighborhood meeting on May 13, 2020 to discuss the proposed amendments.
A total of 2,455 notices were mailed to the entire Lakeview neighborhood, with 38 individuals
participating in the online meeting. Many comments were received through the process, which
were included in the agenda packet for review. Mr. Bench said many of the participants were in
favor of allowing low-density development around the LDS temple site. During the online
meeting, staff presented four options relating to land use. Based on comments received, staff
developed a preferred option which was also presented to the City Council in a work session on
May 26, 2020 for discussion. In addition to the land use designations, staff also proposed the
addition of several trails within the Southwest area as shown on the Pedestrian and Bike Path Plan.

The proposed changes included an update to the Land Use Map that would include a new land use
designation of Low Density Residential/Southwest (LDR/SW). This new designation would allow
a density between 4-8 units per gross acre. Based on this new land use designation, staff anticipated
proposing a modified Planned Residential Development ordinance for the LDR/SW designation
that would be considered at a later date. Several areas were proposed to be designated as LDR/SW
as shown on the proposed land use map.

In addition, two commercial areas would be designated on the revised general plan including at
the corner of 2000 South Geneva Road and at the intersection of the future Lakeview Parkway and
Geneva Road as shown.

The proposed Land Use Map also showed future areas not part of the City. The intent of the map
was to anticipate future land use designations if those properties were annexed in the future. The
overall density, including future areas, was included in the overall calculations pertaining to all
applicable utilities.

       PUBLIC HEARING
       ORDINANCE – Enacting Section 22-14-21(D) and Standard Land Use Code 5945 Orem
       City Code pertaining to requirements for retail tobacco specialty businesses

Mr. Bench said there had recently been a surge of retail tobacco specialty businesses coming into
the City. Currently there were at least eight businesses that were operating as retail tobacco
specialty businesses in Orem (some of which staff believed were doing so illegally). In contrast,
Provo had four such businesses, Pleasant Grove had two and Lehi had one. Staff was concerned
about the proliferation of such businesses in the City and felt that the over-concentration of such
businesses in Orem was not in the best interest of the City.

The Utah County Health Department was also concerned about the increasing number of retail
tobacco specialty businesses both in Orem and the rest of the county. The county was concerned
about increasing usage of tobacco products (including vaping) in the county and particularly
among minors. County representatives believed that reducing the visibility and accessibility of
tobacco/vaping products would help mitigate the increasing usage of such products.

To address these concerns, staff proposed an ordinance amendment to limit the total number of
retail tobacco specialty businesses that may locate in the City. This was similar to what the City
did with check cashing businesses several years ago. The proposed amendment would limit the
number of retail tobacco specialty businesses to one per 15,000 residents which would mean a
limit of six based on the City’s current population.

According to information from the county, several other cities in the state had already adopted
ordinances limiting the number of retail tobacco specialty businesses including Murray (one per
10,000), West Valley City (one per 10,000), and Lehi (one per 50,000). Other cities such as Provo
allowed these businesses only in industrial zones. Mr. Bench said this proposal was similar to a
proposal from some years ago when the Council limited the number of check cashing businesses
within the city.

Mr. Macdonald asked how the number staff proposed had been calculated. Mr. Bench said it was
simply a number they felt comfortable with and had used the percentages other cities had set as a
guide. Mr. Macdonald said the market would control the number of these specialty businesses in
one city; Mr. Bench said that was a fair point, but there were some of these existing businesses
that were not living up to the agreement of not selling more than 35% of tobacco products.
