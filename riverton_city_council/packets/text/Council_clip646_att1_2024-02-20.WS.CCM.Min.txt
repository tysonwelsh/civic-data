42            Single Family Zoning Housing Type Discussion
43
44   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, provided an overview of the current zoning
45   regulations in Riverton regarding attached housing units, such as duplexes and triplexes, as
46   well as accessory dwelling units (ADUs). He explained that Riverton's regulations only allow
47   attached units in multifamily zones, with single-family zones primarily restricted to detached
48   housing. He clarified the distinction between attached units with separate entrances and ADUs,
49   which are counted as a single unit of density and must be owner-occupied.
50
51   Mr. Lethbridge then discussed how other cities in the area handle zoning for attached housing
     Page | 4 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   units, with some requiring multifamily zoning and others allowing for attached units in single-
 2   family zones under certain conditions. He presented examples of different zoning regulations
 3   from nearby cities like Draper, Herriman, Bluffdale, South Jordan, Sandy, and West Valley City.
 4
 5   Finally, Mr. Lethbridge highlighted various considerations for the Council to discuss regarding
 6   attached housing units, including lot size, owner occupancy, maintenance, traffic, parking, and
 7   utility usage. He emphasized that his presentation was intended to facilitate discussion among
 8   the Councilmembers and not a formal recommendation from City staff.

16            Lighting Ordinance Discussion
17
18   Mr. Lethbridge presented an ordinance draft regarding lighting regulations, particularly focusing
19   on holiday lighting for commercial buildings. The draft aims to provide clearer definitions and
20   standards for lighting, including exceptions for public event lighting and flag illumination. It also
21   proposes a conditional use permit process specifically for holiday lighting, separate from the
22   approval process for overall building lighting and signage.
23
24   Mr. Lethbridge highlighted the need for feedback from the Council on various aspects of the
25   draft, such as the proposed conditional use permit for holiday lighting, the defined holiday period
26   during which lighting is allowed, and additional restrictions that the Planning Commission may
27   consider based on circumstances.
28
29   Furthermore, Mr. Lethbridge clarified that the ordinance draft only addresses holiday lighting on
30   commercial buildings and that separate considerations for residential lighting may be explored
31   in the future. The draft ordinance is scheduled to be reviewed by the Planning Commission
32   before being presented to the Council for further discussion and approval.
33
34   Mayor Pro Tem McCay asked if the proposed ordinance would be retroactive for existing
35   lighting installations on commercial buildings. Mr. Lethbridge said ordinances are typically not
36   retroactive, meaning new ordinances are not applied retroactively. However, holiday lighting, as
37   previously discussed, does not comply with current ordinances. The existing ordinance states
38   that the source of the light bulb or lamp must be shielded, but the holiday lighting examples
39   observed do not meet this requirement and are considered non-compliant. This complicates any
40   argument for grandfathering existing lighting under the new standard.
41
42   Mr. Carter said that once a new ordinance standard comes into effect, operating under the prior
43   ordinance's standard legally is considered an illegal non-conforming use. However, in the case
44   of holiday lighting, which is temporary by nature, this poses a challenge as legal non-conforming
45   use must be continually used. It was suggested that a transitional phase for property owners
46   would be necessary, and the administration of the system would likely be complaint-driven.
47
48   Councilmember McDougal clarified that the lighting approved for a commercial business would
49   be the lighting approved at the time they received their building approvals. Lighting changes
50   made after the site plan approval would not be grandfathered in.
51
     Page | 8 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     February 20, 2024




 1   Mr. Lethbridge said that all elements of building lighting, except for security features, are
 2   considered part of the approved site plan. Any changes to lighting after the initial site plan
 3   approval are treated as amendments to the site plan. Holiday lighting is specifically carved out
 4   and treated as a conditional use, separate from the original approval process. This approach
 5   aims to prevent situations where lighting changes occur without proper review and approval.
 6
 7   Councilmember Pierucci inquired about the envisioned procedure, asking whether it would
 8   involve filling out a lengthy application or just answering a couple of questions.
 9
10   Mr. Lethbridge explained that the application process would be similar to other elements of a
11   site plan, involving a one-page application outlining the proposed holiday lighting additions to
12   the building. The permit would cover various types of lighting, from strings of lights to
13   architectural elements like Santa figures or giant inflatables. The application would undergo a
14   hearing with the planning commission to ensure compliance with standards. The intent is not to
15   burden businesses but to ensure clarity and adherence to regulations. He noted that the
16   process typically takes two to three weeks from application to agenda placement and
17   emphasized that the permit would be a one-time issuance, not requiring annual renewal.
18
19   Councilmember Pierucci preferred setting clear terms for what is allowed during holiday
20   seasons without the need for businesses to obtain an additional permit. His concern stemmed
21   from wanting consistency in the process and ensuring that businesses can freely decorate for
22   holidays without unnecessary burdens. He suggested writing the ordinance to define terms
23   without requiring an additional permit.
24
25   Councilmember Buroker expressed concern about the potential for existing commercial
26   properties to be grandfathered in, especially as residential properties are developed nearby.
27   Additionally, she highlighted the possibility of businesses imitating others with excessive
28   lighting, suggesting that the ordinance should explicitly state that grandfathering will not be
29   allowed and outline specific lighting standards. She proposed incorporating lighting compliance
30   requirements into the business license renewal process to ensure ongoing adherence to
31   regulations.
32
33   Mr. Carter clarified that the City doesn't have discretionary power to determine what is
34   grandfathered in and what isn't. He explained that grandfathering occurs by operation of statute
35   and that new ordinances bring about changes that unfold over time. The status of grandfathered
36   elements can be altered by discontinuous use or by updating site plans to comply with current
37   codes. He emphasized that legal determinations of grandfathering are decided by courts if
38   contested by the City, rather than being within the City's sole discretion.
39
40   Mr. Lethbridge emphasized the intention to work closely with Communications and Business
41   Licensing Department to promptly disseminate information to all businesses once the ordinance
42   is adopted.
43
44   Councilmember McDougal proposed a tweak to the ordinance regarding lighting regulations for
45   commercial properties adjacent to residential areas. He suggested that instead of a 100-foot
46   distance threshold, lights should be required to be turned off at 10 p.m. whenever a commercial
47   property is adjacent to a residential property. The rationale behind this proposal is to address
48   the issue of commercial lights shining into residential areas late at night, especially affecting
49   bedrooms where children are trying to sleep.
50
51   Councilmember McCay expressed concern about the proposed holiday lighting ordinance,

22            Ordinance No. 24-02 – Amending Title 10, Section 10.10 Parking of the Riverton
23            City Code Regarding Parking Regulations
24
25   Mr. Carter introduced an ordinance addressing the storage of commercial vehicles on City
26   streets for extended periods, citing an issue near CR Hamilton where 10-wheelers and
27   commercial dump trucks are stored, causing problems. The proposed ordinance, borrowed from
28   Holladay City, reduces the time limit for continuous parking of commercial vehicles to enable
29   easier enforcement by officers on duty within the same day. He emphasized the safety hazards
30   and interference with sightlines caused by long-term storage of commercial vehicles. He
31   clarified that the ordinance does not require a public hearing and can be approved swiftly or
32   postponed as desired by the Council.
33
34   Mr. Carter clarified that the proposed ordinance does not apply to the storage of commercial
35   vehicles on private property, as other ordinances already address that issue. Instead, it focuses
36   on prohibiting the overnight storage of commercial vehicles on City streets to address traffic
37   circulation and safety concerns. He explained that the ordinance exempts situations where
38   commercial vehicles are servicing adjacent properties.
39
40   Councilmember Pierucci was supportive of the commercial vehicle piece of the ordinance but
41   was not supportive of the recreational vehicle piece. Mr. Carter said that he could take the
42   recreation portion out of the ordinance and come back at a later meeting.
43
44   Mayor Pro Tem McCay said she would like the time to be increased from two hours to three
45   hours. Councilmember Buroker said she was supportive of this ordinance.
