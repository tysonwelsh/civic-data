F. PRESENTATION:
   1. Presentation: Mariko Rollins has earned the designation of Certified Venue Executive (CVE) from the
      International Association of Venue Managers.
      Marty Smith (Director, Culture, Parks, & Recreation): I'm going to explain the significance of her
      certification she has just earned. Representing public assembly venues from around the world, the
      International Association of Venue Managers 7,800 plus active members include managers and senior
      executives from auditoriums, arenas, convention centers, exhibit halls, stadiums, performing arts centers,
      university complexes, amphitheaters, and fairgrounds. IAVM’s mission is to educate, advocate for, and
      inspire public assembly venue professionals worldwide. The Certified Venue Executive is an elite credential
      of the IAVM and is highly regarded by industry professionals. An individual with a CVE designation
      possesses significant years of experience in the industry in addition to holding a top position within a venue
      organization. Earning your CVE acknowledges you are well-versed in all areas of venue management,
      invested in your own professional growth and growth of others, and dedicated to the advancement of the
      venue industry as a whole. The process that Mariko had to go through is fairly extensive. Before you can
      ever apply to begin for the CVE certification, you have to earn enough points to even make the application.
      Those points are earned through serving in executive and management positions within a venue; your college
      degree gets you points; attending schools sponsored by the IAVM gets you points, Mariko has been to the
      Academy for Venue Safety and Security which is a two-year school, and serving on boards are all things that
      earn you enough points to apply. Once you apply and your application is accepted, you then write an essay
      on 10 topics that are assigned to you by the Association. It’s a very long essay that takes a while to write. If
      the essay is accepted and approved, you sit for the written exam which is 250 questions. If you pass that
      exam, you go before a board of industry professionals in an oral exam where they ask you very hard
      questions and if you don't answer them right you fail. Many of the subjects that you're tested on and asked to
      explain in the oral exam include things like sales and marketing, ticketing, crowd management, revenues,
      event management, safety and security contracts, and food and beverage, all things that a CVE knows how
      to do. Mariko went through that whole process and just this last month earned her Certified Venue Executive
      certification and designation.
