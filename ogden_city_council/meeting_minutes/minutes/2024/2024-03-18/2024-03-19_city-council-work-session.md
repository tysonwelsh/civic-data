Proposed Local Historic District – Adams Avenue to Jefferson Avenue, 24th
Street to 27th Street
          Deputy Planning Manager Simpson explained a petition has been filed by Alan Toone to designate an area between
Jefferson Avenue and Adams Avenue and between 24th Street and 27th Street as a historic resource on the local historic register.
Both individual buildings, or portions thereof, and larger districts may be designated as historic resources on the local historic
register. With local historic designation, owners of the property or those within the district would need to comply with standards
established in the City’s Landmarks title and would require Certificates of Historic Appropriateness for significant changes to
structures. The petition filed by Mr. Toone cites the long history of the block and provides background about the homes of many
prominent figures important to Ogden and the larger area.
          Mr. Simpson used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the differences between the national and local historic
register; the makeup of the petition area; and the recommendation by the Landmarks Commission. The Commission made a
determination to reject the petition. The Commission’s determination has been forwarded to the Council with a recommendation
of denial as well. The recommendation was based on the limited time and resources available to the Commission, that the
petitioner did not solicit approval from the property owners in the subject area, and that City staff does not currently have the
resources to properly conduct the studies necessary to designate the district as a local historic resource. For a copy of the
PowerPoint presentation in its entirety see the information packet.
          Discussion among the Council and staff centered on protections afforded to properties included in a historic district and
the sufficiency of the City’s process for reviewing a historic district petition and whether the 60-day timeframe is adequate.
          Chair Richey invited discussion between the Council and the petitioner. Council member Choberka asked Mr. Toone
how many property owners in the proposed district area he had made contact with. Mr. Toone stated just his neighborhood for
now, and that seven different parties, i.e., families, real estate groups, and investors own close to 40 of the buildings in the
proposed district. He stated he is concerned about losing the legacy of the historic homes in this area because not as much value is
placed on these older homes as has been the case in the past. In the future, especially approaching the Olympics being held in
Utah, he would hope that tourists will tour these historic areas of the community and it is important to preserve them and create
something similar to “The Avenues” in Salt Lake City. If something is not done, the areas will not be saved, and this will reduce
pride in the Ogden community.
          Chair Richey stated the Council will take the petition and the recommendation of the Landmarks Commission under
advisement and Council leadership will work to schedule the next action item on the petition.

Proposed Creation of a Flex Industrial Zone (continued from February
20, 2024)
          Senior Planner Rypien explained Asilia Investments initially submitted a petition to amend the A-C (Airport
Commercial) zone. Those amendments would have modified and clarified uses in the A-C zone to allow a wider range of non-
aeronautical commercial uses. The A-C zone along with the A-I (Airport Industrial) zone make up the zoning for operations on
and adjacent to the airport. While the changes were given a recommendation of approval by the Planning Commission, concerns
raised by the Council led the petitioners and the City to amend the proposal from changes to the A-C zone to a proposal of a new
zone altogether.
          Mr. Rypien used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide information about the amended proposal to create a new
Flex Industrial zone that would allow flexible commercial and industrial/manufacturing space. This would be a new zone within
the manufacturing classification and would have some similarities to the M-1 zone while allowing some additional flexibility in
the zone’s allowable uses and interior warehousing and manufacturing standards. The focus of the initial petition was to develop
property on the south end of the airport adjacent to the Kemp buildings. The zoning on that property is A-C. While the updated
proposal is to create a new zone, the petitioner’s goal is still to develop the property but under the new zone rather than a modified
A-C zone. If the Flex Industrial zone gets approval from the Council, it is anticipated that a petition will be filed to rezone the
subject property. For a copy of the PowerPoint presentation in its entirety see the information packet.
          Discussion among the Council and Planning staff centered on the potential for a Flex Industrial zone to threaten the
Ogden Airport; the lack of an existing zoning designation that allows for a quasi-retail/industrial use on the same property;
projects in other communities that would be comparable to the type of development the applicant would like to pursue; ensuring
that a project is compatible with and complements the Ogden Airport; the minimum and maximum square footage called out in
the proposed zone text; and dialogue with Roy City about their feelings regarding the proposed zone creation.
          Council member Myers asked the applicant to discuss the use he would like to pursue on the property he owns. Mr.
Nichols first discussed existing businesses located adjacent to or on the Airport property that are not necessarily compatible with
or complementary to an airport, including the WorkMed clinic, restaurants, Williams International, and Northrup Grumman. The
Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) does not limit the number of people or the uses within a transitional service zone; they
only restrict building heights around an airport. He stated he does not suggest limiting the uses in the zone beyond the restrictions
already imposed by the FAA.
          Chair Richey asked Planning staff to develop a formal ordinance proposal and the Council will review the matter one
additional time in a work session before moving the item forward to a City Council meeting.

Council Business
          Proposed Flagpole Use Ordinance: Council Policy Analyst Burton explained Council staff has received a Council
initiative to regulate, by ordinance, which flags can be flown on City-owned flagpoles and the manner in which the flags shall be
flown. Council staff have received draft ordinance language from the City Attorney’s office regarding city-owned flagpoles and
he used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to review the draft ordinance and he solicited feedback from the Council regarding
the matter. The Council agreed on the idea of a structured policy regulating City flagpole use and Mr. Burton stated that staff will
work to schedule continued discussion and review of the matter.

noted he was blown away by the work done by that Department. Chair Richey agreed; he noted he was impressed by the amount
of training hours each Fire Fighter participates in each year.
