Sam Drown with Concord Homes has submitted a General Plan/zone change application on 3.18
acres involving 4 parcels located at 3201 S Redwood Road. The two west parcels are currently
zoned C-2 (General Commercial) with a General Plan designation of General Commercial. The
two east parcels are zoned R-1-12 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 12,000
square feet) with a General Plan designation of Low Density Residential (3 to 4 units/acre). The
proposed zone for all of the property except for 0.55 acres surrounding the Beto’s restaurant is
RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential) and the proposed General Plan designation is Medium
Density Residential (7 to 12 units/acre). The zoning on the 0.55 acres surrounding Beto’s would
remain C-2 and the General Plan designation would remain General Commercial.

Surrounding zones include C-2 and RM to the north, R-1-12 and R-1-4 (Single Unit Dwelling
Residential, minimum lot size 4,000 square feet) to the east, R-1-12 and C-2 to the south, and C-
2 to the west. Neighboring uses include a strip mall and 3-story condos to the north; single
family homes to the east; single family homes, a vacant lot, and a used car business to the south;
and Utah Barricade to the west. The property includes two homes, the Beto’s restaurant, and
several other buildings.

The applicant is proposing to demolish all of the existing buildings except the Beto’s restaurant
and construct a 31-unit, two-story, for-sale townhome development. The proposed density of the
project excluding the 0.55 acres for the Beto’s restaurant is 9.7 units/acre. All units would be
front-loaded with 2-car garages and include 3 bedrooms. All garages have driveways that are
deep enough to park in. The attached concept plan and application materials from the applicant
provide details on and support for the proposed project.

In reviewing this application, staff suggests that there are two primary questions to answer:

   1. Is this the right location for a townhome development?
   2. Assuming the location is appropriate, does the quality of the project further City goals?

These two questions are explored below.

Is this the right location for a townhome development?

According to the City’s zoning ordinance, zone changes to the RM zone can only be considered
by the City if the following four conditions are met:

   1. For properties without existing Structures which are proposed to be developed, the
      property must be a minimum of one acre and the Density must not exceed 12 units per
      acre. For properties with existing Structures proposed to be redeveloped, the property
      must be a minimum of 20,000 square feet and the Density must not exceed 20 units per
      acre. For properties that are west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West
      Valley Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station or the Redwood
      Junction light rail station, the Density may exceed 20 units per acre;

   2. The property shall meet any one of the following four requirements: (i) have access and

       or (iii) be located west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West Valley

This application meets the first condition in that the property has existing structures, is over
20,000 square feet in area, and has a proposed density below 20 units/acre. For the second
condition, the subject property has access and frontage on Redwood Road, which has a planned
right-of-way width over 100’. On the third condition, the applicant has proposed development
agreement standards, and those standards are addressed later in this report. The fourth condition
does not apply because the project is under 100 units.

While the property is eligible for RM zoning, the City may still deny requests for RM zoning.

The points below may help the Commission answer the first question.
   • There are eight developed or approved townhome developments that front or have access
       on Redwood Road. In addition, the Planning Commission recommended approval of
       another townhome development at 2996 S Redwood Road during the public hearing on
       January 29th, 2025 that has yet to be reviewed by the City Council.
   • As part of the City’s General Plan update effort, staff discussed this property, along with
       other properties along Redwood Road that are likely to redevelop, with both the Planning
       Commission and City Council. The Planning Commission was supportive of townhomes;
       however, the City Council’s preference was for commercial along Redwood Road and
       single family detached homes on the east portion of the property.
   • Redwood Road is currently a 7-lane arterial street that is serviced by UTA bus route 217.
   • Average annual daily traffic (AADT) on this segment of Redwood Road was 26,000 in
       2023. A 7-lane arterial can handle a traffic volume of 40,000 at a level of service (LOS)
       C and 46,000 at a LOS D.

Does the quality of the project further City goals?
City Code requires requests for the RM zone to be accompanied by a development agreement
proposal that addresses dwelling unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, landscaping, and
project amenities. These items, along with other considerations, are addressed below.

Dwelling Unit Sizes

In the zoning ordinance, the minimum unit size for 3-bedroom townhomes is 1,400 square feet.
There are four proposed floor plans with sizes at 1,567, 1,652, 1,673, and 1,698 square feet.

Exterior Materials
For materials, the City’s multi-unit residential design standards state: “All multi-unit residential
Building exteriors shall be brick, stucco, stone, tile, metal panels or fiber cement siding. With the
exception of brick or stone which may be used on the entire Building exterior, no single material
shall comprise more than 60 percent of a Building’s exterior. Where stucco, tile, metal panels or
fiber cement siding are used, at least 20 percent of the Building’s exterior shall be brick or stone.
Wood and vinyl may be used as trim, soffits and/or accent materials only.”

The applicant is proposing a combination of brick and fiber cement lap siding (horizontal) and
board and batten siding (vertical). According to the applicant, the percentage of brick meets the
20% requirement. While the fiber cement siding exceeds the 60% rule, there is a variety in color
and a mixture of horizontal and vertical application and the City has granted an exception in such
cases.

Architecture
Based on a preliminary review of the proposed building architecture by staff, the proposed units
appear to meet many of the architectural requirements in the City’s multi-unit residential design
standards. Areas that fall short of the ordinance requirements are addressed below:
    • The ordinance requires at least 25% of the front façade to be fenestration (i.e. windows).
       Based on rough measurements from staff of the original submittal, the fenestration on the
       front was just under 17%. The applicant has since submitted an updated elevation with
       more windows and the fenestration percentage is now 22% of the front façade. This
       standard is fairly easy to meet for townhomes with rear-load garages and more difficult to
       meet for front-load garages. The City has granted exceptions to this standard for front-
       loaded units; however, an exception is typically granted after the applicant shows that
       they have maximized the window space and there really aren’t practical ways to meet the
       standard. In staff’s view, a sufficient number of windows has been added to meet the
       intent of the ordinance.
    • The ordinance requires three window treatments with at least two window treatments per
       window on the front of buildings. Based on staff review, one treatment – varying the size
       and/or style of windows and window grids – was identified.
    • The ordinance limits the percentage of the front devoted to garage doors to a maximum
       of 60% of the length of the building. The garage door percentage is at 64%. In this case,
       adding more width to the units would likely result in the loss of units. The applicant is
       requesting an exception here due to the limited width of the property and the relatively
       low proposed density for a townhome development. Part of the intent of the most recent
       changes made to the front façade was to deemphasize the garages.

Landscaping
The ordinance requires a total of 50% landscaping and recreation space. The applicant’s concept
shows 50.3%.

Project Amenities
For a project of this size, the ordinance requires one amenity. The proposed project includes one
amenity from the ordinance – a tot/lot playground. The applicant is also proposing a privacy wall
along Redwood Road and vinyl fencing along the north and south sides of the property.

Garages/Parking
The ordinance requires all townhome units to include a 2-car garage. The overall parking
requirement for townhomes is 4.75 with 2 in a garage, 2 in a driveway, and an additional 0.75
within the project. All of the units meet the 4.75 stall requirement.

Other Considerations
Back in 2023, the owners of the Beto’s site requested a parking reduction as permitted in Section
7-9-104 of the Zoning Ordinance. The requested parking reduction, which included a concept
plan with proposed site modifications, was approved at 17 stalls (the approval letter is included
with this report). The development of the proposed project would require changes to the Beto’s
site that are possible because of the approved parking reduction. Staff recommends that the
development agreement includes a provision that the Beto’s site be modified substantially like
the concept plan that was included in the parking reduction request. In addition, the landscaping
on the Beto’s site is in need of maintenance.
The original concept plan included 15 parking spaces along the west side of Lester Street that
would back onto Lester Street. The Public Works Department expressed a safety concern about
this amount of parking backing onto Lester Street, which is a collector street. This concern was
relayed to the applicant, who then submitted a revised concept plan with parking no longer along
Lester Street. The applicant is now showing shared stalls on the Beto’s site.
There is an existing wall along the west side of Lester Street that is located roughly one foot
from the back of the curb. There is no parkstrip or sidewalk along the frontage of the property.
Staff is recommending that the wall be removed and that a parkstrip and sidewalk be installed
along the frontage.
The City Council has expressed concerns about for-sale townhome developments becoming
rentals as well as the possibility of townhomes becoming boarding homes or homes where rooms
are rented to individuals. In recently approved developments, the City has included certain
provisions in the development agreement to address these concerns. Items i, j, and k under the
approval alternative below are such provisions.

C-3-2025
Swenson Development Storage
4053 S. 7200 W.
M Zone, 2.96 acres

The applicant, Swenson Development LLC, is requesting conditional use approval for a self
storage facility at 4053 S. 7200 W. The zoning for this property is M, Manufacturing and the
General Plan designation is Commercial Light Manufacturing. The surrounding zone is M,
Manufacturing to the south, R-1-8 to the north, R-1-10 to the east. There is a park across the
street to the west in Magna’s jurisdiction. To the south there is land that was the old West Valley
City animal shelter and to the east and north are residential homes.

This property was rezoned in 2024 (GPZ-1-24) from A, Agricultural to M, Manufacturing
zoning subject to a Development Agreement and the requirements outlined in Ex. B (see
attached). The site design has slightly changed since that review. As the applicant started doing
more formal design and grading it became clear that it would not be possible for the buildings to
be built at the property lines as originally planned. Therefore, things have been designed in a
more terraced manner internally to the site. There is still one building on the north property line,
Bldg A, and then additional landscaping was added on the interior of the site to accommodate
some of the slope areas.

There will be (4) four main buildings designed as follows:

Building A: 24 Units
Built at the north property line and not to exceed 10’ in height per the Development Agreement.
The north side of the building will be CMU creating masonry wall buffer, and the south side of
the building is a metal wall paneling. There will be an 8’ CMU wall to match the building along
the north property line in the areas that are not occupied by the building itself. That wall will
continue out to 7200 West along the parking area up to within 20’ of that front property line. The
remaining wall area will be 3’ tall to comply with the clearview requirements in City Code.

Building D: 582 Units – 18 drive up.

board siding and windows for the office portion. The building is 3 stories and is approximately
36’10” tall at north end. The height does comply with the setbacks required adjacent to a
residential zone.

Site:
Final installation of the landscaping along the frontage will be deferred as outlined in the
Development Agreement. Once the roadway construction is complete the landscaping on site

minimum of one tree for every 300 SF as well as 50% live plant material coverage not including
tree canopies.

There will be no outside storage at this facility. There will also not be an onsite manager or
caretakers dwelling for this facility.

45' for the rear. These requirements will be verified and reviewed during the formal plan review
