1. Discussion         of Code          Update          Process       and Sensitive          Land       Areas
Francis     Lilly    gave a project             update       on the R-1, R-2, and R-4 Zones.                        He said the R-1, R-2, and R-
4 Zones were enroute                  to the city council,            with     the R-4 being          a "legacy"             district    with     tri-plexes
and four-plexes.            The Residential             Multifamily           (RM)      Zone would             change        to the Residential              Mix
Zone (RMZ).           The current            RM zone is on corridors                   identified      as mixed          use areas. The new RMZ
does retain         many     existing        standards       of the RM including                  building      materials,          massing
requirements,         height       limits,      lot and setback           requirements,            and uses, and the amenities                      were
updated     and revised            with      additional       criteria.      The revised        site criteria         included          buildings         being
required     to be parallel           to the street, entrances                along    street facades           being        oriented       to a hierarchy,
and required         ground        floor     living     space for new multifamily                    projects.        There       were spatial            limits
tables and graphics,               design      graphics,        and minimum            criteria      for amenities             added. Commissioner
LaMar      appreciated          the illustrations            with    definitions        on the design           standards.

Lilly    then described           the proposed            Sensitive       Lands      Overlay        (SLO)        Zone.      The sensitive         lands map
addressed        debris    flows,      faults,     alluvial      fans, and liquefaction.               The city code lias geological
hazards      and Foothills          and Canyons             Overlay       Zone (FCOZ)             in different        sections.         These would              be
combined         into a single         zone. The SLO would                     rely more heavily            on geotechnical             reports          and
introduce        a sensitive       lands map, carg              over existing         standards         and processes            with     minor        updates,
have new requirements                  for soils and water table analysis                       for all development               to assure
foundational         integrity,      prohibit       all construction             on slopes       greater     than 30%, provide                 review          and
assessment         responsibilities           to the contracted               city geologist,       include        language       to reduce        the risk to
the city and the eventual                 owner     of the building/lot,             and include           a dispute         resolution        process.
FCOZ        was designed           to address sensitive               land issues all over Salt Lake                     County      on the hillsides
and canyons,          but it was not found                 elsewhere          in the city,   and it needed            to be. The proposed
ordinance        was very technical.              Lilly      said existing         lots greater       than 30% could              apply        for a
variance.        The geotechnical             submittal        requirements          would       vary based on what hazards                       were
present     on the site. It would              now include            coverage       of additional          hazards         such as alluvial             fans,
wildland      urban       interface,      and saturated             soils. A certified          inspector        would       also be required              on site
to monitor        improvements             being built         as per the building           permit        and/or        the land use approval.                  A

contracted        city geologist          would     review          the geotechnical         reports        and provide          recommendations.


Commissioner   SouIe asked what other cities were doing. Lilly said this proposed ordinance was
more robust than what other cities were using. Commissioner         Soule asked about protecting
consumers from purchasing   sensitive lots. Lilly said liquefaction    or geological hazards such as
fault    study    areas, if a subdivision                 was done, would            have a disclosure               on the property            title.     He
said there were other disclosures                         for alluvial        fans and flood         plains.      There      was not a disclosure                   for
a steep slope. He did not know                      how to solve that problem.                    Commissioner                Soule asked how many
of the proposed           rules were new. Lilly                 said it would         be easier for people                to get a permit           for a
fence or accessory               structure     in FCOZ,          but there were complicated                      waiver       special     exception
processes.        The biggest          change was prohibition                    on development             of lots with         slopes greater            than
30%. Council            Member         Uipi      submitted          an online      comment          asking, "To          speak to Diane's                inquiry,
you stated we could               look    at a solution         to inform         disclosure        if there wasn't            disclosure        in the last
twenty      years. What           does that look           like?"     Lilly     said when        it gets caught,            there could be
disclosures        on title      or when a building              permit        is applied    for.


Lilly    said code was relatively                 silent      on temporary          use standards.           A new section              would      cover
miscellaneous           temporary         uses that were currently                  missed       or fragmented              throughout         the code,
help standardize           procedure          and criteria,          eliminate      previously         vague discretionary                conditions,            and

cover a wide range of temporary                           uses such as construction                 trailers,      shipping       containers,            and
seasonal      retail,     etc.
