5.3 Potential Closed Session Regarding Deployment of Security 
Personnel, Devices, or Systems
22-1220
Attachments: Staff Report
A motion was made by Council Member Stewart, seconded by Council 
Member Stringham, to close the Council Work Session to discuss security

− − − − − − − − − − − − − −
The Council voted to reopen the Committee of the Whole meeting during the closed 
session.
Ms. Megan Hillyard , Director, Administrative Services Department, stated a lot of 
questions arose in early December when the Governor made an announcement about 
TikTok.  Information Services has reviewed the issues with TikTok and will brief the 
Council on that.
Mr. Zachary Posner , Chief Information Officer, Information Services Division, 
delivered a presentation on the Tik Tok situation, which included an overview of TikTok, 
and a history of the events that had occurred from October 20, 2022, when Forbes first 
reporting that TikTok was using location data to surveil United States (US) citizens and 
the Department of Treasury opened an investigation, to December 2022, when Chris 
Page 3 of 9
Council Work Session Meeting Minutes January 3, 2023
Wray, Director, Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI), expressed concerns about the 
potential misuse of the TikTok app; Governor Spencer Cox announced that China ’s 
access to data collected by TikTok could present a threat to Utah ’s cybersecurity; Utah ’s 
Statewide Information & Analysis Center ’s (SIAC) noticed its concerns about algorithmic 
influence and the use of bots; and the Federal Executive branch and US House of 
Representations banned the use of Tik Tok on their devices.  He also reviewed the risks, 
which were low for malware, but high for the potential of influencing public opinion or 
officials, as well as information leakage through the collection and misuse of information .  
TikTok was not doing anything different than what other social media organizations were 
doing, in that they were all collecting data, using algorithms to direct traffic, and know 
global positioning locations (GPS), but TikTok is owned by a China based company, and 
under Chinese law, the Chinese government can step in at any time and gain total access 
to the data.  Only one or two agencies in the County are actively using TikTok, including 
the Salt Lake County Health Department, although others were exploring it to reach out to 
constituents.  He was not sure an outright ban on County devices was necessary, but he 
wanted the Council to be aware of the risks.
Council Member Stringham  stated she was willing to make a motion to ban TikTok 
because of security issues identified by Governor Cox and some additional information 
she learned from researching.  If a specific organization wanted to use it, they should use 
it on a device that would not put the County at risk.  
Mayor Jennifer Wilson  stated before the Council implemented a ban on TikTok, she 
would want time to discuss this with the County organizations using it to find out what 
their arguments might be for using it.  There are pros and cons with any social media, and 
the pros for using TikTok might outweigh the risks.  
Mr. Mitchell Park , Legal Counsel, Council Office, recommended the Council not take a 
vote on a ban today because it was not noticed on the agenda, but to instead bring it back 
next week to ensure it is properly noticed.
A motion was made by Council Member Stringham, seconded by Council Member 
Theodore, to approve legislative intent to look at the TikTok issue and ask organizations 
currently using it to get information back to the Council about their use, and to come back in 
a week and determine the best action.  Council Member Winder Newton amended the 
motion to come back in a week or two to ensure everyone had enough time.
Council Member Harrison  stated at the next presentation, she would like to see ways to 
mitigate risk with any social media the County may choose to use.

Mayor Wilson  stated her office could handle the review of the TikTok issue, but she 
would need several weeks for a broader review of social media policy.
Ms. Catherine Kanter , Deputy Mayor of Regional Operations, asked if the expectation 
was for staff to come back with information, and that any agency utilizing TikTok should 
be prepared for answering what content they were putting on the platform, the reason they 
chose TikTok, and if an alternative social platform could serve the intended purpose.
Council Member Stringham  stated that was what the Council wanted so it could 
understand how and why agencies were using it.
A motion was made by Council Member Stringham, seconded by Council Member 
Theodore, to approve legislative intent to look at the TikTok issue and ask organizations 
currently using it to get information back to the Council about their use, and to come back in 
a week and determine the best action.  Council Member Winder Newton amended the 
motion to come back in a week or two to ensure everyone had enough time.  The motion 
carried by a unanimous vote.
