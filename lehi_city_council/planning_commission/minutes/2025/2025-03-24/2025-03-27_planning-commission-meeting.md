3.4) Public hearing and recommendation of the Hospital and 2100 North Station Area Plan
covering both future transit stations and the land with ½ mile of each station.

Brittney Harris presented the item. The locations were selected partially due to the fact that UTA
owns the existing tracks in the area. Two transportation studies were conducted, which
determined that the South Triumph Station would benefit most from a light rail system and that
the 2100 Station would be best implemented as a BRT (Bus Rapid Transit) station. The stations
may be connected to the Frontrunner line. As of 2020, UTA has taken public comment and
feedback on the stations.

The TOD (transit oriented development) zone was added to Lehi’s city code in 2012. The
stations are coming before the Commission now because UTA wants to ensure there will be
density before building the transit, while the city would prefer the lines be built before risking
heavy density in the area. The plan is a way to show that the city is serious about incorporating
public transit. The city had a lot of input in the plan and gathered its own feedback from the
public. They hope to bring an industrial feel to the aesthetics of the development and encourage
building around the hospital. They aim to create a mixed-use neighborhood with an emphasis on
health and wellness. The plan presented today is a visionary plan and thus has no regulatory
implications. It’s important to address the plan now so that the necessary zoning and regulatory
changes can be made in the code, but it is a guideline, not actual legislation.

Commissioner Everett asked if the reason for having one station be a light rail and the other a
BRT was to maintain 1200 West as a five lane arterial road. Harris explained that it was likely
due to the fact that the stations were determined based on two different studies, but that a third
study also recommended a BRT line to Provo.

Commissioner Jackson opened public comment.

Cami Stevenson thinks that creating high-density housing near the station is a bad idea as there
won’t be enough parking. She thinks that the city shouldn’t do high-density housing and that
people should instead not move into Lehi. She wanted to know why the city paid for the plan to
be made. Additionally, she is worried about the three intersections before the freeway causing
traffic problems.

Paul Carroll has concerns about traffic. His main worries are the timing of the traffic lights and
the ability to get in and out of the neighborhood. He is also concerned about there being no road
that directly connects the two stations. He asked if 2100 North will be widened.

Victoria Richmond is worried about traffic spiking during construction. She is also worried about
transit into the city causing a spike in crime.

Seth Robinson worries that the plan violates the intent of the goals in the Lehi City General Plan.
He feels that UTA has picked the location due to convenience and that the city’s concerns will
not be taken into consideration. He mentioned that Thanksgiving Point is the area where the city
would like to have foot traffic, not near the hospital. He also mentioned the Development Code
Chapter 12 compatibility clause to argue that the station area plan would not be integrating into
the current neighborhood.

Darren Pack seconds the statements already made. He stated that attempted break-ins have
happened in his neighborhood, and he fears that the station area plan would encourage that
behavior. He thinks that new rails should go along existing frontrunner lines.

Mick asked to see the map in more detail and with more context, as he was having a hard time
positioning it in his mind. He also requested a printed map, which the city was unable to provide

him with at the current time. The commission recommended he talk with the Planning Division
after the meeting to fulfil the request.

Kit Harris has lived in Lehi for 64 years and has family ties to the area. His uncle used to farm
pheasants on the land. He is upset about the potential high-rises being built. He frequently sees
children using the footpath near his house and suspects that they’re responsible for the graffiti he
sees around his neighborhood. He worries that the light rail will bring unhoused people into Lehi
after they’ve been driven out of other cities.

Marshall Gary began with an apology for an angry online comment he left on an earlier issue. He
wanted to address some misconceptions about the area plan. He explained that parking is the
limiting factor to having cars on the road, and having less parking will bring traffic in the area.
He also explained that there is no definite connection between high-density housing and crime.
He stated that transit is a wealth accelerator, both for the city and for local businesses. He says
that encouraging other methods of transportation, like walking, will also help with any density
issues the city is experiencing. He mentions that the high-rises being built in the plan won’t be
more than five stories tall, and he is thus in favor. He is looking forward to the plan.

Mike Extram has lived in Lehi since 1993. He explained that mass transit is an important part of
any long-term plan for a city. He is concerned about urban sprawl. He is concerned that the plan
is a proposal and not a plan for execution. He sees it as a conversation driver and agrees that it’s
important to start the conversation now. He wishes he could see the station plans laid out with
the long-term UDOT plan for the interchanges and freeway updates. He doesn’t want Lehi to
become a second spaghetti bowl.

Rob Rogers owns a home on 2100 North. He is concerned about the roundabouts in the plan, and
asked the commission if the city plans on bulldozing homes and existing buildings to put
development in. He also requests more detail on the map so that he as a landowner can plan for
the future.

Blake Lake is frustrated by traffic from the freeway blocking his house frequently. He would like
more road infrastructure in his area.

Huey Arbuckle asked where the light rail will come from if the hospital is the end of the line. He
is unsure why people would use the line. He asked if existing houses will be claimed by the city
or if they will be built around.

Shannon Fairbanks shares traffic concerns. She asked if soundproofing was planned for citizens
living near the tracks.

Landon Fairbanks grew up in the area and has seen traffic get worse over the years. He is
worried that the addition of transit is going to make things worse. He worries that no one will
follow the one car per unit rule for the high-density housing. He also shares concerns about rapid
growth causing water concerns.

Cindy Risbeck has lived in the area for 20 years. She blames the congestion in front of her house
on high-density housing.

Joyce Talon has lived in the area for 30 years. She asked why the blank areas on the plan have
been filled in, and why the area was selected. She doesn’t see the need for more paths and
buildings in the area.

Brittney Harris summarized the online comments. Many people are concerned about traffic
impacting existing neighborhoods.

Commissioner Jackson closed public comment.

Harris gave more context for the project. She and Kim Struthers explained the map and showed
where it was located within the city. She also pulled up the Central Corridor Plan so that the
public could see the larger context of the planned railway stations. She explained that whether
the plan is accepted or not, development would be coming to the area, and that traffic would be
affected.

Commissioner Lockhart asked for a comparison of planned freeway additions and the area plan.
Harris explained that there is no set timeline on either station, as they are neither funded nor
officially planned yet. Struthers explained that the 2100 North/Mountainvew Corridor is under
construction and will be built up to Redwood Road. It is currently in the design stage. 2028 is the
approximate date of construction.

Struthers also discussed city standards regarding parking stalls in relation to dwelling units. He
stated that there is nothing he is aware of that would limit all high-density units to one parking
stall. He reassured current property owners that the plan is not eminent development or a
construction proposal, but an anticipation of what will be built over the next 20-30 years. The
city won’t be condemning properties in order to bulldoze houses. Any destruction of current
property would only happen after current landowners sold to developers with plans for the area.

Commissioner Jackson mentioned that Lehi didn’t choose to become a big city. He’s had a hard
time adjusting to change as well, but pointed out that in every case the property owners had the
right to sell, and the new owners had the right to develop. He explained that the plan is about
anticipating that development, rather than having to respond after its begun and limit the city’s
options. He also supported Marshall Gary’s comment that crime is not caused by high
population.

Struthers addressed concerns about the plan complying with city code. He mentioned the TOD
zoning that is already in the code. He also stated that the city is maintaining low-density
residency in other areas, but as the area is already a major artery it will need to be higher density.
He also explained that the neighborhood integration and the compatibility clause will be utilized
by creating architectural standards that will hearken back to Lehi’s older and historic buildings.
That way the city can still embrace its past without halting all development.

Commissioner Jackson reopened public comment.

Seth Robinson returned to the stand to clarify the parts of the code he was concerned about
including the Lehi City Master Plan, the City Transportation Plan, and Chapter 12.

Commissioner Jackson reclosed public comment.
Commissioner Jackson clarified for the gathered citizens that the Planning Commission is an
appointed recommending body, not the final deciding body.

Harris discussed the intent of the code in relation to the project. She explained that existing
neighborhoods will not be affected but will have construction happening near them. She also
explained that part of the difficulty has come from working with the state which has increased
pressure on the city to create more residential living space. She worries that if the city prevents
growth in areas that haven’t been developed yet, that growth will spill into existing
neighborhoods. The plan is an attempt to maintain and preserve current neighborhoods by having
development happen elsewhere. She stated that the plan follows the intent of the transportation
plan by providing more walkability and expanding the trail network. She concluded by
explaining that the code is a living document, and that interpreting intent is partially up to the
individual.

Commissioner Jackson agreed that the city code is a living document and encouraged the public
to dig deeper into the code when discussing the issue with the City Council.

Struthers addressed the concern over who would be using the new rails by explaining that it
would offer an alternative to frontrunner for people who want to take the train but who may only
want to travel short distances. Anyone travelling between Provo and Draper will use the line. He
clarified that the area plan isn’t zoned for mixed-use, but as a TOD. He also explained that the
compatibility measures will be looked at during development.

Commissioner Jackson recommended that constituents contact their State senators in order to
make changes to UDOT plans, as the state is in charge of the freeways and larger roads.

Harris explained that the 2100 North station will be near 2100 North, not directly on it. She also
showed on the map that the current plan doesn’t anticipate taking any property in order to widen
roads or do other development.

Commissioner Jackson reopened public comment.

Mick returned to the stand to ask about sound barriers.

Commissioner Jackson reclosed public comment.

Gary Ellis explained that any sound barriers would come post-development once the decibel
levels could be measured. He also clarified that UDOT would be the organization in charge of
that. Usually, walls are built once decibels surpass a certain level.

Commissioner Lockhart asked to see the timeline for the next 20 years to assure citizens that
traffic patterns would change over time and that there would be plenty of public meetings
throughout the process. Harris was unable to provide a visual due to technical difficulties, but
assured everyone that it was a long timeline.

Commissioner Eyre asked about the goal to meet moderate-income housing that was mentioned
in the plan. Harris explained that meeting the moderate-income housing goal is not dependent on
adopting the plan, but that the plan may assist in meeting it. Both the moderate-income housing
and the station area plan are required by the state and are punished by a daily fee. Combining
them is one way the city is trying to be proactive about state code, but they are not inherently
linked in any way.

Commissioner Everett has concerns over the 2100 Station because several businesses near where
it will be constructed are car focused. Harris explained that the station plan would likely be more
neighborhood oriented. The planning division doesn’t want all the stations to be competing for
the same businesses and developers. Commissioner Everett is still concerned over creating an
island.

                happen, the station area plan helps Lehi City meet one of the moderate-income

                plan is consistent with the general plan land use elements in regards to TODs and
                building those areas around potential transit opportunities. He included all DRC
                comments, and a recommendation that the City Council listen to the meeting and
                the comments from the public. The intent of a positive recommendation is to
                allow future development of the project that cannot happen until it is in place.

Commissioner Everett asked to include that the commission would like to avoid creating islands
of misaligned amenities that are not relevant to the goals of strengthening community.

3.6) Public hearing and recommendation of Peggy Fisher’s request for review of the Fisher
Main Street General Plan amendment on 0.3 acres of property located at 1680 West Main
Street, changing the designation from NC (neighborhood commercial) to MDR (medium
density residential).

Brittney Harris presented the item. The applicant wishes to build a single family home on the
rezoned portion. The rest of the lot will remain zoned as neighborhood commercial.

Peggy Fisher, the applicant, was present but had no additional comment.

Commissioner Jackson opened public comment.

Gerald Larsen owns the gas station across the street. He shared that there are issues with water
and sewage on the corner of Main Street and 1700. He would like the issues resolved before
development happens.

Cody Larsen asked if it will be single-family or multi-family housing.

Cameron Barret lives in the area. He would also like to know if it will be single-family or multi-
family housing. He also mentioned zoning discrepancies on the website. He asked why only 0.3
acres of the 2.5 acre property will be rezoned. He asked if there were any plans for the yellow
brick house currently on the property. He stated that this is a good opportunity for the city to put
in sidewalks in and rezone the area.
Commissioner Jackson closed public comment.

Fisher explained that she is building a single-family home on the lot to house members of her
family. She is seeking the rezone because building a single-family home will be easier than
adding on to the existing infrastructure.

Harris clarifies that only a single-family home would be allowed on the rezoned portion, as it
would need to be 0.33 acres to have two homes on it. The area is currently zoned as T-H-5,
which means that water hasn’t been finished, and no development can take place until that has
been completed.

Commissioner Eyre asked why the property was being rezoned to MDR instead of a different
zone. Harris said it could also be rezoned to LDR, but that it is likely MDR to match the
surrounding zoning.

Commissioner Kunze asked if sidewalks would be part of the development. Garry Ellis
explained that they would, but only on the portion of land that was being developed.
Commissioner Jackson asked if the neighborhood could be put on the city’s radar as being in
need of sidewalks. Ellis explained that there are budget constraints, and that the city is working
on high-priority areas first, like routes to schools. Ellis stated that he would have public works
look into the flooded sump, ad mentioned that connecting the area to current sewage lines poses
a problem.

Commissioner Jackson reopened public comment.

Gerald Larsen returned to the stand to inform the commission that the sump had been installed
only six months ago.

Cameron Barrett mentioned that the area that would be developed with the new construction
already has a sidewalk, but that it cuts off midway down Main Street. He highlighted other areas
where sidewalks should be put in.

Commissioner Jackson reclosed public comment.

Commissioner Eyre explained that sidewalks are developed by landowners, and that the primary
way the city installs them is by requiring landowners to put sidewalks in when improving
property.

                Motion: Commissioner Kunze moved to give a positive recommendation to the
                Lehi City Council with the findings that the proposed amendment is not
                detrimental to the public health, safety, and welfare of Lehi City, that it is in
                conformance with the purposes, intent, and provision of the General Plan and its
                various elements, that the general plat amendment is consistent with the land uses
                and zoning of nearby and adjoining properties, and that it will not have a
                substantial adverse effect on surrounding properties. She included all DRC
                comments, with a special emphasis on the water drainage issue surrounding the
                property. Commissioner Everett seconded.
                Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner Lockhart, yes. Commissioner
                Eyre, yes. Commissioner Jackson, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes.
