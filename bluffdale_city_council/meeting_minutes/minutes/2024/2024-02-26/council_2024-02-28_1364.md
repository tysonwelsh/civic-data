4.3 Presentation of New Website Design. (Presenter, Amanda Luker).
Communications Specialist, Amanda Luker was pleased to present the City’s New Website
Design. She reported that on February 14, a new website and design was launched. The City
moved to a .gov domain as is required by law. The idea behind a .gov domain is that it gives
immediate validity to a website because you have to be vetted to prove that you are a government
entity. A .gov website is the official website for an entity. Going to a .gov domain name was an
important step for the City as it is growing. It is free to register with the government but took time
to finalize and resulted in a change to all e-mail addresses.

Ms. Luker reported that they wanted to update and refresh the website, which can be done with the
company that hosts the City’s website. They wanted to make sure that it was fresh-looking and
that information is easy to find. Branding is important, which they were able to keep. They also
tried to incorporate a secondary color palette into the website design. Ms. Luker provided a virtual
tour of the website for those present.

She reported that users can sign up to receive the City newsletter electronically. Spotlights were
provided to show events taking place in the City to keep everyone informed. Residents can sign
up for e-mail or text alerts as well. The website features a calendar of upcoming events. A section
was also added containing the City’s Facebook feed. The background was grass with a photo from
the City and the new logo.

Anyone wanting to report a concern can find a tab at the top of the page. There are also tabs to

7.1 Personnel Policy Update, Organization Chart Change from Legislative to

Administrative. (Presenter, Fred Donaldson).
City Attorney, Fred Donaldson, presented a proposed statutory change to Sections 1.70.010 and
020 to eliminate the need for the City Council to approve an Organizational Chart that contains all ,
positions within the City and return it to the City Manager. In most cities that is where those
powers reside. The majority of changes involve moving verbiage to different parts of the same
area of the Code. New language was added at the top to state, “The City Council may create any
department deemed necessary or appropriate for the administration of the City and may prescribe
by resolution or ordinance the organization, power, and duties of such bodies.” If the City Council
