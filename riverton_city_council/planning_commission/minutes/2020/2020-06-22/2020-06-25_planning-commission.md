1.    SUBDIVISION, IHC HEALTH SERVICES IS REQUESTING APPROVAL OF ROSE
      CANYON SUBDIVISION, A THREE-LOT COMMERCIAL SUBDIVISION LOCATED
      AT 5541 WEST 13400 SOUTH.

Zoning Administrator, Tim Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the above
item is for the subdivision of two parcels of land owned by IHC Health Services, to create a
three-lot commercial subdivision. The property is located at approximately 13400 South and
5541 West. The City of Herriman is located to the west of the parcel on 5600 West and to
the north on 13400 South. To the east is a Riverton City Park and to the south is the Autumn
Hills Subdivision. There is an eight-foot masonry wall along the south boundary line and a
six-foot masonry wall along the east boundary line. The total acreage of the property is 7.23
acres. The proposed subdivision is in the C-G (Gateway Commercial) Zone.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the proposal is unique because IHC Health Services is asking
to subdivide the parcels but a master site plan has not yet been developed. IHC Health
Services wants to retain the largest lot, Lot 1, for future development. Lot 2 is a deep lot
with a small amount of frontage while Lot 3 has a large amount of frontage on 5600 West.

Lots 2 and 3 will eventually be built out by commercial developers. All lots meet the minimum
standards for the C-G (Gateway Commercial) Zone and are capable of being built upon.

The proposed plans show that the sidewalk, curb, and gutter will be continued.
Mr. Prestwich raised questions related to power poles and the park strip along 5600 West.
Riverton City ordinance requires that power and utility poles be buried with development.
Mr. Prestwich questioned whether the power poles should be buried now or in the future.

Staff recommended approval of the above item with conditions listed in the staff report.
Mr. Prestwich reported that there are outstanding redline comments in the latest plan
exchange relative to technical details. However, none of the redline comments change the
lot lines or significantly alter the layout from what has been presented.

Commissioner James asked about the condition relative to burying power lines. He
wondered if it would be technically feasible to do that in the area. Mr. Prestwich noted that
the applicant’s engineer had been in closer contact with Rocky Mountain Power but the
general concern related to the three transformers on the pole. Commissioner James asked
if bonding would be possible so that when Lots 2 or 3 are developed, there will be funds
available to handle the necessary improvements. Mr. Prestwich was not sure how a bond
would work since the park strip is located in Herriman. He noted that this proposal will be
seen again as a site plan, and at that point, there would be a better understanding of any
potential Herriman requirements.

Commissioner Rushton asked if the power lines to the north continue overhead.
Mr. Prestwich noted that there is one additional pole and the power lines drop into a
transformer box. There are currently three poles in the area. There is still uncertainty with
the type of configuration Rocky Mountain Power would propose. Mr. Prestwich reported that
while Rocky Mountain Power employees tend to prefer that the power lines stay above
ground, due to ease of access, Riverton City ordinance requires utilities to be placed
underground in different developments. In response to a question, Mr. Prestwich confirmed
that there are now bathrooms in the park to the east of the parcel.

•      DISCUSSION ITEMS

1.     RESIDENTIAL LOT REQUIREMENTS

Mr. Prestwich discussed an item that came up during recent meetings related to residential
lot requirements. The standards state that the frontage can be smaller but 30 feet back, it
must meet a certain width requirement. This standard is for a cul-de-sac or wedge shape.
Staff was looking to create a standard that specifies that from a set number of feet off of the
rear property line, the minimum width must also be met. Peninsulas and panhandles were
also discussed. Staff was looking to create a provision that notes that peninsulas and
panhandles are acceptable if they already exist but cannot be created on new lots.
Mr. Prestwich reported that the Sunday Drive Subdivision brought some of the issues to
light.

Commissioner James asked if it would make sense to quantify numerically the relationship
between the front minimum width and the other sides of the lot. He suggested using a
specific percentage. Mr. Prestwich felt that using a set percentage was a good way to
approach the issue. Commissioner James felt it could cut down discussions later on if a
formula was set in place.

2.     PRIVATE LANE REQUIREMENTS

Mr. Prestwich discussed an item that Commissioner Lefgren brought up in the last meeting
about excluding the private lane area from the lot area. Between one-quarter and one-third
of the City is made up of ancient lots or ancient parcels that were developed in the early
1900s through the 1950s. Frequently those lots have a very small home on the front end of
the parcel with a substantial amount of property behind it. Mr. Prestwich noted that the
policy decision to include the private lane in the lot area may have been an incentive to
encourage redevelopment of the underutilized ground. Mr. Prestwich wondered if
development would be negatively impacted if the lot area incentive was removed. Staff
could create an ordinance stating that the private lane could not be included as part of the
lot area, the private lane requirements could remain the same, or a set percentage could be
determined.

Commissioner James felt that a private lane should be looked at like a road since it was for
vehicular traffic. He was concerned that there was not enough consistency with what was
required in terms of front, side, and rear yard setbacks. Commissioner James felt the
Planning Commission needed to be careful about the issue of private lane requirements.
He thought it was important to take into consideration the net area for a structure and the
open space around it. Potential suggestions were to limit the size of the footprint or reduce
the number of lots that a developer could get from a project.

important to determine strategies that will create more affordable housing and utilize these

excluded from the lot area. Mr. Prestwich stated that if the private lane was created as an
individual parcel and several people had ownership, a Maintenance Agreement would be
needed. All of the lot owners would need to enter into an agreement to handle maintenance.
Commissioner Russell asked if that was the way things were handled when the private lane
was part of the lot area. Mr. Prestwich confirmed this was the case. Commissioner Russell
believed it would be best to leave the ordinance as it was, where the private lane was part
of the lot. Commissioner Rushton agreed.

3.     ACCESSORY STRUCTURE SIZE

Mr. Prestwich discussed issues related to accessory structure sizes. The Riverton City
ordinance limits a detached garage to 25% of the rear yard. However, on ancient lots or
parcels with a lot of acreage, this could lead to plans and proposals for very large detached
garages. Mr. Prestwich asked if the Commission would like to limit the maximum size of an
accessory structure or be more specific than 25% of the rear yard.

Chair Cannon reported that he was also on the Architectural Control Committee at Timber
Lakes. They recently set a limitation for exterior dwelling units that were tied into the square
footage of the main home or the main dwelling rather than the size of the lot. Commissioner
Russell felt that the limitation may work in some places but many of the homes on ancient
lots are 1,500 square feet or less. There needs to be a happy medium with respect to size

limits. 1,500 square feet would be a fairly small garage but 4,000 to 6,000 square feet would
be large and not necessarily appropriate.

Chair Cannon asked for a more detailed definition of an accessory structure. Mr. Prestwich
reported that the accessory structure ordinance includes several different types of
structures, including an agricultural building, which has different rules than an accessory
structure for residential use. The current ordinance specifies that there can be many
different types of accessory structures on one property as long as they do not exceed 25%
of the rear yard. Placing a 25% limit on a one-quarter acre lot or a one-third acre lot could
be limiting whereas a 25% limit on a five-acre lot could lead to very large accessory
structures.
