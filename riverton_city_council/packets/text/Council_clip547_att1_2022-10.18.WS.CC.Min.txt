39          Reimagining Employee Skills
40
41   Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, presented a spreadsheet showing the
42   employment positions that require a college degree. There are 22 positions that require a
43   degree which equates to 13.63% of the overall positions in the City.
44
45   David Brickey, City Manager, said a number of positions could be changed from degree
46   required to experience matched. Mr. Hicks agreed that some positions could be changed,
47   however, there are other positions that eliminating the degree requirement could lower the City’s
48   fraud risk assessment score.

 1            Municipal Waste Hauling Discussion
 2
 3   Craig Calvert, Contract Administrator and Purchasing Manager, said that Waste Management
 4   was the lowest bid for the RFP and has been awarded the contract. He displayed the quote
 5   from Waste Management with comparisons to the current costs.
 6
 7   Mayor Staggs commented that he thought the price would be lower since the City owns the
 8   cans. In response, Mr. Calvert said that recycling has increased which accounts for the overall
 9   increase in this bid. He also explained that Waste Management’s quote was $700,000 lower
10   than the next bid.
11
12   Councilmember Buroker commented that she was surprised that the waste part of the contract
13   decreased and asked if this bid will be affected by TransJordan’s move to their new location.
14   Mr. Hicks said the City pays TransJordan directly so this change will not impact this bid.
15
16   Councilmember McDougal expressed his concern that the City will not get the benefit from
17   recycling because there are no foreign markets for recycling and it is very expensive to recycle
18   locally.
19
20   Mayor Staggs asked if the City needed to determine at this time whether to continue recycling
21   Mr. Calvert said that the bid shows the worst-case scenario for recycling and explained that the
22   City does not need to decide now whether to continue the recycling program. Either party could
23   terminate the recycling portion of the contract with adequate notice.
24
25   Mr. Calvert explained that Waste Management wanted to include contract language for
26   residents that continually contaminate the recycling or overfill the waste cans. Councilmembers
27   and Mayor discussed this idea and asked for a tour of the facility to see how the Smart Truck
28   technology works.
29
30   Mr. Calvert said he will draft a contract with the language discussed and bring this back to
31   Council.

34            Resolution No. 22-65 – Amending the Municipal Fee Schedule for the 2022-2023
35            Fiscal Year
36
37   Mr. Hicks explained the City has a handful of fire hydrant meters available for rent so entities
38   can utilize and pay for water from the City’s fire hydrants. As part of the hydrant rental process,
39   the renter must pay a $1,000 refundable security deposit which is intended to cover the full cost
40   of replacing a meter in the event the meter is broken or is not returned. Staff recommends that
41   the deposit fee be changed from $1,000 to $2,000 because the current replacement cost for a
42   meter is $1,850.
43
44   Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the public hearing.
45
46   Councilmember Buroker MOVED that the City Council approve Resolution No. 22-65
47   amending the municipal fee schedule for the 2022-2023 fiscal year. Councilmember Wells
48   SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs
49   called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: Buroker-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes,
50   and Wells-yes. The motion passed unanimously.
