4. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment, to allow accessory apartments on the ground
   level of existing single-story buildings in the CBD zone. (Attachment F)

  Ms. Corbridge stated this is an amendment to allow accessory apartments on the ground floor in
  the CBD if the building is single story. She stated the CBD Ordinance currently allows loft
  apartments so long as they are above existing retail space, but there is no consideration of existing
  single-story buildings in the CBD. The proposed ordinance would allow the reuse of existing single-
  story buildings in the CBD by allowing the accessory use as a single apartment. In amending the
  Ordinance, the Commission would consider whether it is consistent with the General Plan and the
  character of the CBD zone. She reviewed several strategies of the General Plan, stating while it is
  desirable for taller buildings in the downtown, there are some existing single-story buildings. The
  Ordinance allows a loft apartment above commercial space but there is no provision for single story
  buildings. Staff recommendation is for approval of the language as proposed.

  Commissioner Garner asked about on-site parking for these additional dwelling units, stating they
  should not eliminate the ability of public parking in the downtown. Commissioner Stoker asked
  about examples of these in other areas stating the main use for the downtown should be for office
  or commercial uses. Ms. Corbridge indicated these are limited to a single loft apartment per
  building, and could be occupied by only one family, which could be up to three unrelated people, two
  unrelated with children, or a traditional family. Discussion continued relating to the need for
  additional parking, stating the current standard is for one additional parking space for the residential
  use. While some felt additional parking is warranted, many felt as these are restricted to the CBD
  which is intended to be more dense and walkable, there is no need for additional parking.
  Discussion also centered on the cost of creating parking in urban areas. It was indicated these
  could allow a live/work environment in both residential and commercial style buildings in the
  downtown.

  As there was no additional public comment, a motion was made by Commissioner Graf to close the
  public hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Garner and passed unanimously.

  Commissioner Safsten expressed by reducing the required parking for uses the need for on-street
  parking is increased, which affect the public’s ability to access the particular use. He felt needed
  parking should be accommodated on site for each use having an accessory unit.

  MOTION:      A motion was made by Commissioner Graf to recommend approval of the Ordinance
               amendment as proposed based on the findings it is consistent with the General Plan
               and will meet the zoning requirements and policies including the requirement for one
               parking space to be provided for the residential use. Motion was seconded by
               Commissioner Garner and passed 8-1 wit Commissioners Blaisdell, Boykin, Garner,

               voting no. Commissioner Safsten explained he is not opposed to an accessory
               apartment on single story buildings but felt adequate on-site parking should be
               required as it put pressure on existing public parking.

5. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment, to amend building design requirements for
   accessory buildings in the East Central community (Attachment G)

  Ms. Olson stated the East Central community has design guidelines for accessory buildings
  identifying particular finish material in the community. As residents often purchase small sheds to
  house yard equipment without knowing about these standards, it has been difficult for Staff to
  justify denying a land use permit. Staff is suggesting accessory buildings of less than 200 square
  feet be exempt from these guidelines so long as they are not visible to the street. Many of the
  prefabricated buildings have siding with vertical lines where only horizontal lined siding is allowed.
  There are no restrictions for these in other communities, and while Staff still feels it appropriate to
  protect the historic character of the East Central community it is felt smaller buildings could be
  exempt. Language would prohibit the use of galvanized steel, and buildings would not be allowed
  for either parking or living space. Staff recommendation is for approval of the language proposed.

  Commissioner Graf asked what would prohibit these being used for parking, and wondered how the
  size had been determined. Ms. Olson responded 200 square feet is the cut-off size where no
  building permit is required, and that buildings over 200 square feet require a building permit as well
  as a land use permit. Commission felt if such a building were used for parking, it should meet the
  East Central requirements and asked if there is a reason for changing the rules. Ms. Olson
  responded she has had to deny the use of these sheds often when they have already been
  purchased and paid for, and Staff feels if they are not visible to the street, the size would have little
  impact ono the historic character of the area. Commissioner Safsten expressed concern that
  although they may not be visible to the street, they would be visible to neighboring property owners.
  Chair Southwick stated the appearance of a vinyl shed is less offensive than equipment left in the
  yard, stating often these sheds are purchased in an effort to clean up the view of the neighborhood.

  As there were no public comments, a motion was made to close the public hearing. Motion was
  seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously.

  Commissioner Stoker expressed the City has the responsibility to preserve the historic character of
  the East Central community and felt allowing these could be detrimental. Chair Southwick
  commented the East Central community has older homes on small lots, and many do not have
  garage space. He felt there is a balance needed, where the installation of a small shed would create
  better organization when space is limited. Commissioner Boykin expressed if these were in a side
  yard they could be an eyesore, but would be acceptable if not visible to the street, indicating
  placement on the lot is important. Mr. Montgomery stated corner lots are more restrictive due to
  their double frontage.

       MOTION:          A motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to recommend approval of the
                        Ordinance language proposed based on the findings it increases the types of
                        materials considered for small accessory buildings in the East Central
                        community, does not detract from the historic integrity established by the

                                                                                                     pg. 6
                       existing buildings in the community, and design standards are established
                       which appropriately regulate the appearance of small accessory buildings in
                       the East Central community. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Boykin
                       and passed unanimously, with Commissioners Blaisdell, Boykin, Garner, Graf,
                       Safsten, Sandau, Schade, Stoker and Southwick voting aye.

  existing parking lot will be used to store vehicles only. He also clarified no body work is done.

  Rocky Mountain Car Sales. In considering a conditional use permit, the Commission considers what
  impacts might be created by allowing the use proposed. He stated the proposed use may add lights

  recommends screening be required by installing a 6’ solid fence on both the north and south sides

8. Public Hearing, Preliminary Approval, Fenway Fields Subdivision, 100 W 2nd Street (Attachment J)

  Mr. Shawn Strong stated his intent is to sell the individual units in the 30-unit townhome project and
  desires to subdivide the project into individual parcels.

  Mr. Simpson reviewed the history of this property, stating it had been zoned R-1-6 and had been
  recently rezoned to R-3/CO with an associated development agreement. This request is to create
  individual ownership, with the tenants owning their unit as well as the ground under their unit and
  then common open space is created to accommodate the roadway and landscaping areas and
  limited common area for parking. A homeowners association would be created and restrictive
  covenants recorded with the subdivision plat in order to provide maintenance of the common areas.
  He reviewed some details of the plat, identifying particular corrections which are needed, such as
  including common area on the title. Staff feels the individual ownership is consistent with the Lynn
  Community Plan and will be a benefit to the community and recommends approval.

13. Public Hearing, Proposed Subdivision Ordinance Amendment, for public improvements.
  (Attachment O)

  Mr. Montgomery stated since the City recently adopted the Transportation Master Plan, it has been
  found there are some inconsistencies in the Subdivision Code. Additionally, there are some
  inconsistencies in the Fire Code, Engineering standards and some State regulations. The General
  Plan and Subdivision Ordinance are also inconsistent in how streets are identified. This amendment
  is an effort to create plain and consistent language in the Subdivision Ordinance

  He reviewed the sizes and types of streets stating local/minor streets typically would have a 56’ or
  60’ right-of-way with either 26’ ore 32’ of pavement, with parking allowed only on one side of the
  narrower street. A collector street would typically have 72’ right-of-way where these previously
  ranged from 66’ to 86’ and an arterial street would contain a 100’ right-of-way. In addition, alleys
  provide some access to homes and are typically limited to residential areas, are 20’ or less in width,
  and there are no underground utilities. Cul-de-sacs also are limited to a maximum of 750’ in length.
  The concern with these is that they limit future connectivity. He also stated street lights are also
  identified as a standard for public improvement. Staff recommendation is for approval of the
  Ordinance amendment as proposed

  Commissioner Blaisdell noted street lights are typical at intersections but are often not at mid-
  blocks. Mr. Montgomery stated while there have been standards, they have not been codified, and
  by adding requirements to the Subdivision Ordinance creates a better expectation. He stated the
  proposed amendment would reduce the pavement width of local streets while collector and arterial
  streets would remain the same. Commission also briefly discussed the use of alleys.

As there were no additional public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Schade to close
  the public hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously.

  MOTION:      A motion a me by Commissioner Safsten to recommend approval of the ordinance
               language as proposed based on the findings it is consistent in implementing the
               General Plan and Transportation Master Plan, and provides for orderly development in
               a manner that is efficient and meets the needs for future growth and development
               while protecting existing development. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Graf
               and passed unanimously, with Commissioners Blaisdell, Boykin, Garner, Graf, Safsten,
               Sandau, Schade, Stoker and Southwick voting aye.
