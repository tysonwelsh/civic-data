  Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
  information as follows:

         All surrounding property is zoned M. Neighboring uses include the ARA
         Industrial Park to the south, an auto salvage business to the west, an
         asphalt products business to the east and a recently approved storage lot to
         the north. The subject property is designated as light manufacturing in the
         West Valley City General Plan.

         The applicant has submitted the attached concept plan, which shows a
         proposed building of 310,800 square feet. Also attached is a letter from the
         applicant describing the proposal. The intended use is “office/warehouse
         or distribution”. Given the size of the proposal (over 10 acres), a
         conditional use permit would be required for the use approval assuming
         the zone change is approved.

         Up until the end 2015, the City’s General Plan showed heavy
         manufacturing uses for the area located north of SR-201 between 5600
         West and 7200 West. However, with inclusion of the ARA Industrial Park
         and other large, light industrial projects along SR-201, the General Plan
         was updated to show light industrial uses for those properties with
         frontage along the SR-201 north frontage road. For this reason, the
         Planning Commission recommended a development agreement to
         establish design standards and use restrictions.

RESOLUTION 20-11: AWARD A CONTRACT TO BROKEN ARROW
CONSTRUCTION CORP. FOR THE REPLACEMENT OF THE ROOF FOR FIRE
STATION 74
Jake Arslanian, Public Works, presented proposed Resolution 20-11 that would award a
Contract to Broken Arrow Construction Corp. for the replacement of the roof for Fire
Station 74.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The City has budgeted $75,000 for the roof replacement of Fire Station #74. Bids
       were obtained from three reputable contractors. This procurement is below the bid
       limit set forth by state code 11-39-101, as referenced in City Code 5-3-115.

       The roof of Fire Station #74 has reached its life expectancy under normal usage,
       wear, and tear. After 15 years, maintenance on the roof is now become reactive,
       repairing many leaks and other issues. It is necessary to replace the roof with a new
       membrane. Broken Arrow submitted the lowest bid and proven work history with
       the City.

Councilmember Huynh asked how large the roof is. Jake replied approximately 10,000
square feet. Councilmember Lang asked how close the other bids were. Jake stated there
was one at approximately $75,000 and another closer to $90,000.

The City Council will consider Resolution 20-11 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
February 4, 2020 at 6:30 P.M.

TRANSPORT VAN
Wayne Pyle, City Manager, presented proposed Resolution 20-13 that would authorize the
City to purchase a prisoner transport van.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Frequently, we have several officers at the jail in South Salt Lake at the same time
       waiting to book prisoners. A prisoner transport vehicle would allow one officer to
       safely transport more than one prisoner. This would free up the other officers to
       stay in WVC and respond on calls for service or patrol the city.

       The Utah Beer Tax Grant provides funding for equipment used in alcohol and drug
       related investigations and enforcement. A large percentage of the prisoners we
       transport are under the influence of alcohol or drugs. The grant funding to purchase
       the prisoner transport vehicle has been approved by the USAAV+ Council.

Councilmember Buhler asked if the City will have to hire a full time officer to drive this
vehicle. Wayne replied there would likely be different plans for staffing. Mayor Bigelow
asked if the Police Department could only limit arrestees to alcohol/drug related offenses.
Wayne replied he was unsure but would find out and provide this information to the
Council.

The City Council will consider Resolution 20-13 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
February 4, 2020 at 6:30 P.M.

B.     NEIGHBORHOOD PILOT PROJECT
       Nicole Cottle, CED Director, stated the Neighborhood Pilot Project came from a
       meeting that was held with herself, Paul Isaac, and some leaders in the community
       who expressed concern about ordinance enforcement, community engagement,
       crime, and the general welfare of West Valley City. She stated that the City took in
       the information and thought about different ways of helping which include
       additional enforcement, neighborhood watch, dumpster programs, grant programs,
       etc. Nicole stated that after discussing these options, the City felt that a good
       solution would be to work with residents and combine resources to find different
       solutions to concerns. She indicated that she, along with Steve Pastorik in CED,
       reviewed the Neighborhood Health audit and chose a neighborhood to begin a pilot
       project. They gathered some community leaders and have found community
       partners who want to do various things in the neighborhood as a team to make
       improvements to the overall quality of life. Nicole discussed detailed statistics
       within the neighborhood including education rates, income, diversity, etc. She
       indicated that community partners are looking at adding a park, computers, street
       trees, etc. She indicated that the LDS church has partnered with the City and has
       called 20 service couples (one member of each couple will speak Spanish) to
       volunteer for the City. These couples will attend church in the neighborhood and
       their goal will be to provide support and stabilization to the community. Nicole
       stated that some of this might including helping residents earn their GED, provide
       after school programs to students, help with athletic programs for the youth living
       in the neighborhood, find ways to improve infrastructure, etc. She indicated that the
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JANUARY 28, 2020

       City will be a resource for the things it can, and the community partners will assist
       the neighborhood in ways that government cannot. Nicole indicated that the City is

       Councilmember Buhler asked what the name of the project is. Nicole replied that
