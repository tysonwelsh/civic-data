31       a. Riverton Police Department Report
32
33   Chief Hutson said that April was defined as the month that felt the full impact of policing during
34   the COVID-19 pandemic. He said that, as expected, their calls for service were at a lower level
35   than previous months; domestic violence and fraud calls remained consistent with the previous
36   months, which would be expected with more folks staying home from work and the added stress
37   of the outbreak. He said that reports of traditional property crimes, such as vehicle burglaries,
38   shoplifting, and residential burglaries had reduced to the lowest levels since inception. He said
39   that the traffic accidents and traffic offenses were dramatically reduced, even though there were
40   theoretically more cars in the community; however, he believed it was due to less through traffic
41   as a result of fewer people commuting from the surrounding communities.
42
43   Chief Hutson reported that they were able to make seven arrests for DUI during the month,
44   even though they are making far fewer pro-active traffic stops to keep the officers safe. He said
45   that was a testament to the ability of the officers to focus on the drivers who were most likely
46   impaired and keeping the streets safe as a result.
47
48   Chief Hutson then reviewed the Patrol Activity Report for April 2020 and addressed questions
49   from the elected officials.

 1
 2   Chief Hutson introduced JD Wood, Riverton Police Department Victim Advocate, and said that
 3   he had recently been selected as Unified Police Department’s (UPD) Volunteer of the Year.
 4
 5   JD Wood then reported the following:
 6
 7        “On Dec. 9th 2019 I was hired as the victim advocate for Riverton City Police. Since
 8        then, it has been my goal and focus to look at the crimes occurring in our city that
 9        require a victim advocate and see how I can be of best assistance to them. As I have
10        explored the ways in which I am able to help the victims, I have seen that it is through
11        the VOCA grant and the assistance of our city that I am able to provide resources to
12        them. The VOCA grant that has been awarded to our department is $32,986.72 over a
13        two year span, including a cash match from our city of $6,645.00 and an in kind match
14        through a volunteer in our department of $2,080.00. With these funds, the victim
15        advocate position is able to be funded from the federal grant, allowing our department to
16        allocate the funds they would be spending on items that will help further our department.
17        The funds from the grant are used to pay part of the victim advocates hourly wage, travel
18        and training for the victim advocate, necessary supplies to fulfill my job function, forensic
19        exams for victims and emergency shelter for victims. Since the opening of the Riverton
20        Police department, the victim advocate has been able to work 170 domestic violence
21        cases, 97 assaults, 80 sex offenses, 15 unattended deaths (suicide, unexpected deaths,
22        etc.), 43 protective order violations and 44 child abuse cases. Because of the funding
23        from the VOCA grant that the city is putting money towards to stay compliant, each and
24        every one of these victims have been able to receive prompt and direct help to navigate
25        these challenging events in their lives. Along with the VOCA grant, the cities relationship
26        with South Valley Services has expedited the help that we are able to provide for victims
27        of crime. The office space that the city provides to South Valley Services is something
28        that has positively impacted the citizens of Riverton City who find themselves victims of
29        crime. Because of the relationship that the city has cultivated with South Valley Services,
30        the department was able to establish a mutual agreement with the South Valley Services
31        Shelter that provides immediate safe housing to victims. This service alone is one of the
32        best resources we can provide to victims. This mutual agreement could not have been
33        established as quickly if the city had not developed a partnership with South Valley
34        Services through providing office space and being an ally of theirs. With the strong
35        relationship between our city, our police department and partners surrounding us, such
36        as VOCA and South Valley Services, the victims of crime in Riverton City will be able to
37        receive essential resources that will help them navigate the challenges that confront
38        them.”

28        a. Ordinance No. 20-13 - Rezone, RD Development Located at 11845 South Redwood
29           Rd, Proposed Rezone to RM-6-SD, Residential Multi-Family with a Maximum of 6
30           Units Per Acre, with Specific Development Designations Limiting Density and
31           Configuration, RD Development, Applicant
32
33   Development Services Director Jason Lethbridge explained that the public hearing was
34   regarding a proposed rezone of several properties totaling approximately 12.8 acres located on
35   the east side of Redwood Road at approximately 11845 South. He said the subject properties
36   were currently a mix of zoning with single family residential (RR-22 and R-3), Professional
37   Office and Neighborhood Commercial Zones. He said the current General Plan was a mix of
38   Medium Density Residential and Professional Office. He said that nearby zoning included R-4
39   and R-3 to the south and R-3 across the canal to the east, and to the north was RR-22 zoning
40   with larger lots. Across Redwood Road to the west was RM-8 south of 11800 South and RR-22
41   north of 11800 South.
42
43   Mr. Lethbridge said the applicant requested RM-6 SD zoning, which was residential multifamily,
44   6 units per acre. He said that the “SD” stands for Specific Development, which was a means to
45   apply special rules and regulations for a development. In the application, he said the SD would
46   assign areas within the overall development specific lot sizes and unit types.
47
48   Mr. Lethbridge explained that the SD designations as original proposed by the applicant were
49   based on a concept layout. For the purpose of clarity, he said staff divided the project area into
50   three sections. Under the original application, Area 1 would consist of the southern portion of
51   the properties. There would be a maximum of 14 lots in that area, with a minimum lot size of
     Page | 7 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     May 19, 2020



 1   8,000 square feet. Other than lot size, all setbacks and other standards for the area would be
 2   consistent with the R-4 zone. Area 2, which was the northern portion, would be developed
 3   consistent with the detached single-family requirements of the RM-6 zone, and included a
 4   minimum lot size of 5,000 square feet. The lots adjacent to the north and east boundaries of the
 5   area would be a minimum of 6,000 square feet. There would be a maximum of 29 lots in that area.
 6   Area 3 was the smaller parcel just to the south of the existing commercial site on Redwood
 7   Road. The property may be developed consistent with the attached unit standards in the RM-6
 8   zone, with the exception that up to 5 units may be attached. A maximum of 10 units would be
 9   allowed in that area.
10
11   Mr. Lethbridge said the Planning Commission forwarded a recommendation with modifications
12   to the original proposed designations. Specifically, the Commission recommended Area 1 would
13   be limited to 10,000 square foot lots (1/4 acre). Area 2 would be limited to 8,000 square foot
14   lots, and Area 3 would remain as shown, with 10 attached multi-family units. The Commission
15   also recommended that the main road connect through from 11800 South to Laurel Chase
16   Drive, but that no connection be made to Redwood Road through Area 3. However, the access
17   point for Area 3 was approved with the commercial project to the north as a shared drive by
18   UDOT, with the requirement that future development of the Area 3 property expand and improve
19   that access. Eliminating that requirement for development of Area 3 may conflict with UDOT’s
20   approval, and leave the existing shared access under sized.
21
22   Mr. Lethbridge further explained that the RM-6 zone included a requirement that perimeter
23   fencing comply with Section 18.155.080 of the Riverton City Code where development is
24   adjacent to a single-family zone. That section reads as follows:
25
26   18.155.80    Fencing between noncompatible zones/uses.
27          (1) Noncompatible Zones. A solid core decorative precast concrete or integrally colored
28              and textured block, brick, or other masonry fence with a minimum height of six feet
29              shall be required between noncompatible zones. Both sides of the fence shall
30              receive equal treatment with respect to pattern, color, etc. Hollow, foam core,
31              fiberglass/concrete mix, or other alternative fence types are not permitted, except as
32              noted below.
33          (2) Fencing Height. Fencing shall be a minimum of eight feet in height between
34              commercial/industrial zones and residential zoning of any type, and between
35              multifamily development and single-family residential zones.
36
37   Mr. Lethbridge said that, based on the underlying RM-6 zoning, which was a multi-family zone,
38   the project would require an eight (8) foot precast fence along the entire perimeter, with the
39   exception of properties adjacent to the canal and Redwood Road, where six (6) foot would be
40   allowed. He said the RM-6 zone also included a requirement for open space. Section
41   18.45.180(11) specified that where individual lots were utilized in a project over 10 acres, which
42   was the case in the proposed project, a minimum of one (1) acre of open space. He said the
43   ordinance required that open space be privately maintained, unless otherwise approved. The
44   conceptual plans show open space that complies with the minimum area requirement, and
45   specific treatment and configuration would be approved as part of a site plan application.
46
47   Mr. Lethbridge said the question of road connections had been a significant issue with the
48   application. Laurel Chase Drive currently stubs to the southern boundary of the project area,
49   and the northwest corner of the project area was the intersection of Redwood Road and 11800
50   South. Much has been discussed on the potential connection of Laurel Chase Drive to that
     Page | 8 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     May 19, 2020



 1   signalized intersection, and the possible impacts of that connection on the surrounding road
 2   network. H e s a i d t he applicant had presented several conceptual layouts showing alternative
 3   road connections. Traffic impacts were typically reviewed with a rezone, and were an important
 4   consideration; however, the road connection was not specific to the application or to a particular
 5   density. He said the City would need to address the road connection irrespective of the eventual
 6   density of the site. He said that final technical design of a roadway system was typically made with
 7   the site plan/subdivision design for a project. As stated above, the Commission recommended
 8   that Laurel Chase Drive be connected to Redwood Road as a through public right-of-way. He
 9   continued to say that traffic-calming was typically addressed during the site plan/subdivision
10   process, and the types and placement of traffic calming employed were dependent to some
11   extent on design issues reviewed in that process.
12   Mr. Lethbridge said the Planning Commission heard the item on April 23, 2020; at that meeting,
13   the Commission voted to recommend approval of the proposed rezone with modifications to the
14   proposed lot sizes and density.
15
16   The Council reviewed with staff the lot sizes, density, and land uses as recommended by the
17   Planning Commission, and considered the amendments proposed by the applicant. Appropriate
18   lot sizes were reviewed by area. The Council agreed with the 10,000 sf minimum in Area 1, and
19   was supportive of the amendment for Area 2 to 6,000 sf lots. Connectivity with Redwood Road
20   was discussed, both at 11800 South and through Area 3. The Council felt connectivity in both
21   areas was important. Fencing was discussed, and the applicant proposed solid vinyl along the
22   southern boundary, adjacent to the existing housing. The Council also reviewed with staff the
23   need for drainage and stormwater storage within the pond area of the property.
24
