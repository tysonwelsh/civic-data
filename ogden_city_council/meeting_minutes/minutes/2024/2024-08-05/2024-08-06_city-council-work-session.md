Proposed North Ogden Municipal Boundary Adjustment
         Cooperstown Partners, LLC has requested to adjust the boundary line between Ogden City and North Ogden City at 1514
North Washington Boulevard. The applicant is in the process of receiving approvals for a mixed-use development project in North
Ogden, directly north of the subject boundary line. If approved, the boundary adjustment will bring 1.74 acres into North Ogden
City to be part of the petitioner’s mixed-use development.
         Senior Planner Rypien used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the implications of the proposed boundary
adjustment; he presented several aerial images to identify the current boundary line and the proposed shift of the boundary line
and noted the petitioner plans to erect a six-foot tall precast concrete wall on the boundary line as well as to install a six-foot wide
landscape buffer along the wall. City Administration has negotiated an interlocal agreement with North Ogden City that includes a
mechanism for sharing of property taxes on the subject property for a period of 50 years after the boundary adjustment. A
municipal boundary line adjustment is initiated by adopting a resolution, following by a 60-day waiting period before the City
boundary line adjustment can be executed. Staff would like to know if the Council wishes to act on the proposed resolution on
August 20, 2024, with scheduled action on the boundary line adjustment on November 12, 2024, to meet the 60-day notice
requirement. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Discussion among the Council and staff centered on the historical discussions of this matter, the potential tax revenue to
be generated on the property for the 50-year period, and the reason the applicant pursued development in North Ogden rather than
annexing the entire property into Ogden City for development. The Council heard from the applicant, Rick Scadden, regarding his
efforts to pursue development of the property in North Ogden City; they also heard from representatives of North Ogden City
regarding the negotiation of the interlocal agreement. North Ogden City Manager Call emphasized that the developer has not been
offered any tax incentives to pursue his development in North Ogden rather than Ogden City. He also addressed a comment
provided by Ogden City staff regarding a delinquent sewer agreement between North Ogden and Ogden City and indicated he will
research that matter and ensure that North Ogden City fulfills their end of the agreement if necessary.
          The Council then engaged in discussion of the appropriateness of considering a boundary adjustment or trading land;
they ultimately concluded it may be appropriate to consider a general City policy to govern future boundary adjustment requests,
but that they would consider action on the proposed boundary adjustment resolution on August 20. Chair Richey invited Council
members to reach out to staff if they have additional questions regarding the petition.

Proposed Firefighter Retirement Revisions
          Human Resources Manager Briskey explained City Administration has proposed four new Emergency Medical Services
(EMS) personnel with the Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 budget. The position title is Transport Ambulance Technician –
AEMT/EMT. Human Resources has recommended that the Council approve a resolution that would provide these positions with
Utah Retirement System (URS) retirement benefits under the Firefighter Retirement System. Prior to 2023, EMS personnel were
not eligible to be covered under the Firefighter Retirement System. House Bill 183, which passed during the 2023 Legislative
Session, authorizes employers to elect to cover full-time certified or licensed EMS personnel under the Firefighter Retirement
System. For this benefit to take effect with URS, the Council must adopt a resolution to elect coverage in conjunction with the
approval of the City budget. Fire Chief Mathieu added that approval of this resolution will improve the Department’s ability to
hire quality employees in these positions.

Proposed Amendments to Diversity Commission Ordinance
          Mayor Nadolski explained that the current proposal is to amend the Diversity Commission ordinance to align the
language of the ordinance with the requirements of House Bill (HB) 261, Equal Opportunities Initiative, which was passed during
the 2024 general legislative session. Administration also proposes a joint resolution to rename the Diversity Charter the
Community Engagement Charter and reaffirm the values, mission, and vision of the original charter. He introduced Diversity
Affairs Coordinator, Linda Lartigue, who reviewed the proposed ordinance amendments as follows:
               • Renames the Diversity Commission the Ogden Community Engagement and Opportunity Commission.
               • Acknowledges a change in name of the office of diversity affairs to the office of community engagement.
               • Restates the purpose of the ordinance as promoting opportunity in the community.
               • Modifies the membership qualification requirements to remove expertise, advocacy experience, community
                   involvement, student status, profession, education, and references to personal identity characteristics.
               • Aligns the appointment process with other city committees. Appointment is made by the Mayor with advice
                   and consent of the City Council.
               • Adds that the Commission shall not engage in or promote prohibited discriminatory practices based on personal
                   identify characteristics.
               • Makes various grammatical, structural, and other technical corrections.
          City Attorney Williams then expounded on the intent of HB261; the bill “prohibits an institution of higher education, the
public education system, and a governmental employer from taking certain actions and engaging in discriminatory practices based
on “personal identity characteristics such as race, color, ethnicity, sex, sexual orientation, national origin, religion, or gender
identity.” The bill further prohibits “engaging in or maintaining a policy, procedure, practice, program, office, initiative, or
required training that is referred to or named diversity, equity, and inclusion.” Agency directors are required to conduct a review
of existing programs and offices to evaluate compliance with the new bill beginning on July 1, 2024, and to file a compliance
report on or before August 1, 2025. He added that Administration has recommended additional ordinance amendments that would
apply to all City committees:
               • Removes the requirement for diversity relating to personal identify characteristics.
               • Limits service of a board member serving in a term that has expired to not more than six months beyond the
                   date of the expired term.
               • Makes various grammatical, structural, and other technical corrections OCM §3-18-1, et seq. (Ogden Diversity
                   Commission).
          Discussion among the Council and Mayor centered on the nominating and interview process for appointment to the
Ogden Community Engagement and Opportunity Commission. The Mayor and Council also agreed to uphold the charter and the
original intentions for the Diversity Commission and to embrace diversity in the Ogden community while continuing to address
disparities.
          Chair Richey then read from the Diversity Charter a few points that he wished to emphasize and invited Diversity
Commission Chair, Fernando Morales, to address the Council. Mr. Morales spoke to the values and diversity of the community
and indicated willingness of the Commission to continue to work toward improving the community and actively engaging the
residents of the City’s diverse population. He noted HB 261 has forced the removal of certain terms from the City’s ordinance, but
it cannot eliminate the spirit of the Diversity Commission and its charter. There are great leaders in the community that will carry
on the legacy of the Diversity Commission.
         Chair Richey concluded that action on the proposed Diversity Commission ordinance amendments is scheduled for
August 20, 2024.
