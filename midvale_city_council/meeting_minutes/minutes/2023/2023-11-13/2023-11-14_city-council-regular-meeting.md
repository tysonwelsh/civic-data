     A. CONSIDER ORDINANCE NO, 2O23-O-15 AMENDING MIDVALE
               MUNICIPAL CODE CHAPTER2,24 CITY COMMITTEES
Matt Dahl said Chapter 2.24 of Midvale City Code establishes standing committees, the
rules for creating new committees, and the ability to recognize community organizations.
Midvale City staff is proposing to modify Chapter 2.24 to ptovide the City Council with
more flexibility to create new committees and to remove the recognition of community
and neighborhood organizations.

Chapter 2.24 indicates that:
"The city council may form commiftees composed of its own members and/or others for
such purposes as it deems appropiate."

Chapter 2.24 places regulations on how city committees may be formed and operated,
which creates uniformity, but also limits the options of the City Council. Staff proposes
that Chapter 2.24 be simplified to indicate that the City Council may form new committees
by resolution that will establish the rules by which the committee will operate. This would
allow for committees to be created to meet specific community needs without conflicting
with Midvale City Code. Outside of the standing committees established specifically in
City Code (e.9, Audit Committee, etc.), the Harvest Days Committee is the only active
city committee. Staff anticipates that the proposed change will allow the City Council to
find creative solutions for forming a new community council and formalizing the city's
relationship with the Community Violence Coalition.

Staff also proposed deleting Article lll. Community/Neighborhood Organization
Recognition. Staff believes that this chapter was originally created to recognize the
Midvale Community Council, regulate its operation, and let it be independent. However,
regulated independence creates liability issues for the City and complicates the
administration of the community organization. Staff believes that it would be better for the
community organizations envisioned by this policy to become formal committees of the
city or remain independent and work with the city by agreement. Given that this chapter
of code has not be utilized in many years, if ever, staff believes that the deletion of this
chapter of code would have no impact on the City.

STAFF'S RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommended adopting Ordinance 2023-C-15 amending Midvale Municipal Code
Chapter 2.24 Cily Committees to provide more flexibility in creating new committees and
removing the article regarding community organization recognition.

        C.   DISCUSS MAYOR PRO-TEMPORE SELECTION DISCUSSION
Matt Dahl said under Midvale's form of government, Utah State Code gives the City
Council the authority to select a Mayor Pro-Tempore as follows:

.10-3b-302(2xa)lf the mayor is absent or unable or refuses to act, the council may elect
a member of the council as Mayor Pro-Tempore, to preside at a council meeting and to
perform, during the mayor's absence, disability, or refusal to act, the duties and functions
of the mayor.

.10-3b-302(2xb)The municipal clerk or recorder shall enter in the minutes of the council
meeting the election of a councilmember as lt/ayor Pro-Tempore under Subsection (2)(a).

Historically, the City Council has approved monthly rotation for city council members to
serve as the Mayor Pro-Tempore. This method of assigning the Mayor Pro-Tempore is a
tradition, but is not required by City or State Code, or by the Midvale City Council Rules
of Order and Procedure.

A city council member has requested a council discussion regarding the selection and
term of the Mayor Pro-Tempore. They have proposed having the city council consider
selecting a single Mayor Pro-Tempore, and possibly a back-up Mayor Pro-Tempore, that
would serve for the entirety of the year. This proposed change would not require a formal
change in rules or policy unless the Council would like to formalize the method of selecting
the Mayor Pro-Tempore.

        F. DISCUSS RECERTIFICATION OF THE JUSTICE COURT
Mariah Hill said Section 78A-7-103 of the Utah Code requires that municipaljustice courts
be recertified by the Utah Judicial Council every four years. The certification for the
Midvale City Justice Court ("the Court") expires in January 2024. To qualify for
recertification, Midvale City must submit the following three documents:

1.     An affidavit submitted by Judge Vo-Duc certifying that the Court meets the state
       requirements for a Class I Justice Court
2.     A letter from the city attorney attesting to the viability of the justice court, and
3.     A resolution adopted by the City Council

The Administrative Services Department and the Legal Department are preparing these
documents and will have them for your review on December 6,2023.
