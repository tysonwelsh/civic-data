Proposed Resolution 2025–7 adopting a contract with HBME, LLC to
conduct the City’s Fiscal Year 2025 through 2027 financial audits
          A memo from Council staff regarding the proposed Financial Auditor Contract came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated the City’s contract for financial auditing consulting services has expired and a new contract
needs to be approved beginning for the 2025 fiscal year (FY). In January 2025, the office of the City Council published a request
for proposals (RFP) for firms to perform auditing services. In response to the RFP, only one firm submitted a proposal and that
was HBME, LLC. HBME has been the city’s financial auditor for the previous five years. The proposal was reviewed by an
internal review committee, consisting of staff from the Council office and Administration, and was also reviewed by the Financial
Audit Committee. After its review, the Financial Audit Committee forwarded a recommendation of approval to the Council for
auditing services with HBME. The proposed contract for services is for three years beginning with the fiscal year 2025 audit with
an optional two-year extension for audits of fiscal years 2028 and 2029.
          Council Assistant Executive Director Symes summarized the memo and stated a Request for Proposal (RFP) for
Financial Auditing Services was published on the City’s website, two separate times in the Standard Examiner (January 11 and
18), and was sent to the purchasing division’s list of businesses. An internal review committee met to review and score the
submitted proposal from HBME, LLC and has proposed the Council approve the professional services agreement with the firm.
          Acting Chair Hyer asked if HMBE was the only firm that submitted a proposal, to which Mr. Symes answered yes and
noted that he has contacted their references and found other cities have only received a proposal from HMBE when they
conducted recent RFPs for the service. It seems the pool of firms offering this service in the area is shrinking.
           Council member Richey stated one aspect of HMBE that is very attractive is that they have several different auditors on
staff that have different skill sets and areas of expertise; this helps to ensure that a different auditor is working on the City’s audit
each year.
           Council member Myers stated he participated on the internal review committee and found HMBE to be highly qualified
in their auditing procedures. He has been very impressed by HMBE as well as the City’s financial team.
           Chair White introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2025-7, entitled:
                    “A resolution of the Ogden City Council adopting a contract with HBME, LLC to conduct the City’s Fiscal
                    Year 2025 through 2027 financial audits.”

Public Comments
          Cliff (Vigilante) referenced the Council’s posted rules of decorum and stressed the importance of upholding and
following the United States Constitution and its First Amendment.
          Walt English stated he is seeking justice against six Ogden police officers, citing numerous state and federal laws they
have allegedly broken. He concluded Ogden’s Police Department has been ranked as the second worst Police Department in Utah
due to lack of accountability and transparency and emphasized that the Department does not take citizen complaints seriously.
          Laura Lewis shared her concerns about the police presence near her neighbor's house, which was one of the individuals
referenced by Mr. English as being improperly targeted by the Police Department. She noted she listened to the Mayor’s podcast
recently and appreciated the detail with which he discussed some issues the community is facing. She also referenced tonight’s
work session discussion regarding fees for the new Marshall N. White facility, noting she feels the proposed fees are too high.
          Deborah Darrington expressed gratitude for the support received from the Your Community Connection (YCC). She also
suggested the City create a program where residents and non-residents can set up a payment plan for memberships at the Marshall
N. White Community Center. She concluded by referencing the City’s recent acquisition of property at 2325 Madison Avenue,
noting she feels the City’s revitalization of the property would help to benefit the entire neighborhood. She owns property near
2325 Madison Avenue and is willing to work with the City to help improve the area.
          Heath Satow spoke in support of the proposed zone change that was reviewed by the Council during tonight’s work
session meeting; the property owner is a longstanding community member whose businesses have strengthened the community. It
is a challenging property, but since it is located within walking distance of the heart of downtown Ogden, the proposed use is
appropriate as it aligns with the City’s goal of increasing housing density in the area. He would prefer a smaller, self-funded
project like this one versus a taxpayer subsidized mega-project completed by outside developers. The City needs to stop getting in
the way of good, local developers. He concluded by noting he would like to know the status of the paid parking program for
downtown Ogden and whether the City is considering abandoning the system altogether or how it plans to address the significant
debt obligation associated with building parking structures as part of the WonderBlock project.

Mayor Comments
           Mayor Nadolski addressed Mr. English’s comments and indicated he will not comment on ongoing prosecutions. He also
thanked Ms. Lewis for her comments and for listening to his podcast; he felt it appropriate to find a venue for providing more
information about City issues and he hopes to be able to express to the public that he takes his job very seriously and bases his
decisions on thoughtfulness and rationale. He then asked Public Services Assistant Executive Director Nelson to address the
comments about Marshall N. White Center fees. Mr. Nelson stated that anyone who pays the entry fee to the Center will have
access to the amenities there without paying additional fees, unless a certain amenity has been rented by a private group and is not
open to the public for a period of time.
           Mayor Nadolski then referenced Mr. Satow’s comments about the parking program; the parking advisory committee had
their last meeting recently and they are working on a final recommendation to be submitted to the Council. He is very mindful of
the debt burden associated with the construction of new parking structures, but he does not want decisions on the parking program
to be based solely on that burden. He then announced Mr. Nelson has been appointed to the Deputy Chief Administrative Officer
position for the City and he congratulated him on that achievement; Mr. Nelson is also the City’s City Engineer, and he will keep
that role for some time until some major projects in the City are completed.

Council member Comments
          Acting Chair Hyer referenced Mr. Walsh’s comments and lack of decorum during the public comment period; he noted
that he has a great deal of respect for the U.S. Constitution and the First Amendment, but it does not afford citizens the right to
disrupt a public meeting by causing chaos. He thanked the members of the public who respectfully addressed the City Council.
          Council member Myers referenced Mr. Satow’s comments about the proposed rezone of 2850 Washington Boulevard
and noted that the citizen and property owner that Mr. Satow referenced, the Daley family, is not actually the owner of the subject
property. Rather, they own the parcel of property in front of the property that was the subject of the rezone application.
