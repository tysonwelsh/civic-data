Cedar Crest Village Overlay (Hoytsville Area)
Twenty-five (25) Hoytsville area property owners of 49
parcels consisting of approximately 1,038 acres.
East Hoytsville area, Summit County
Village Overlay Planning
Summit County Council
Update
On October 20, 2022, the Eastern Summit County planning Commission held a work session
on the Cedar Crest Village planning efforts. Staff provided an update on the status of the
project. The applicants and consultants for Ivory Development and  Larry H Miller Real Estate,
LLC, who represent property owners within the overlay area, gave a presentation. The
presentation synthesizes the visioning and concept planning that has occurred to date.
The Commission requested staff schedule and notice a public hearing so the consultants can
provide the presentation to the public. There will be an opportunity for public comments and
to actively engage with the Cedar Crest Village Committee, as well as the planning
Commission.
Staff and the Committee are working on additional opportunities for the public to engage and
comment on the village plan moving forward. Staff and the Committee continue to work
towards a draft village plan, an implementation ordinance, specific design elements, and a
public outreach strategy that stays true to the Committee's early vision for this area.

Next Steps
Staff will continue to meet monthly with the Village Overlay Committee to review a draft village
plan and ordinance, as required by the Code. Further refinements to the future land use map
and concept plans for character areas, as well as specific design elements, are also anticipated
as the draft village plan and ordinance are formalized. The Committee requested on-going
opportunities, like this public hearing, and additional work sessions, to receive feedback from
the public and the Commission as this plan evolves. Additional public outreach events will be
announced on the County website, and a link will be provided to a Cedar Crest Village website
the consultants are working on.

To Mr.  P Barnes,
I  have been  living in Wanship since 2001.   I  moved to Wanship because I wanted to live in a rural area.  I
wanted to live somewhere where wilderness and open space was part of my backyard.   I wanted to live
somewhere I could have a little land and have animals.   I wanted to live a community where I would be
safe.  There are no words to express how devastated I am about the purposed sub division that may go in, in
Hoytsville/Coalville,   7,000 houses.., wow! !   Once we lose the open land, we will never get it back.  This area is
zoned to be rural not to be a city.   If people want to live in a city, then there are many places in other counties
they can go.   But once the sub division is built we will lose the rural area forever.   Changing this so, some
people can make money and hurting all of us who moved up here and want to live in this rural area is
devastating.
A sub division like this will have other disastrous effects:   more traffic, the crime rate will go up and water
shortages will become a bigger problem.  Water is a problem already.   For two years we have had restriction
during the summer.  We depend on the water to grow crops and to live.  The lack of water will have damaging
effects.
It is ironic that on the county website the pictures are of open spaces and wildness.   Obviously, this is how the
county wants to be seen.   Beautiful mountains and open space. Wilderness is our natural habitat too.  We
need open spaces to connect with who we are.  It becomes harder and harder to find quiet open places to
go.  And adding this huge development will make that even worse.  We need not take everything and leave
nothing to nature.  Wilderness and open space need to be protected and valued.
I know that the people making this discission won't care, but I will leave Utah if this development goes
through.   I  have lived  in Utah since  1984.   I  have been a teacher for Utah  Public School System since 1997.   And
I have given a lot to Utah.   Please, please don't allow this to happen.
Thank you
