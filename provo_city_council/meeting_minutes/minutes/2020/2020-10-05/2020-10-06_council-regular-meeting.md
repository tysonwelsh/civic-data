 1.        A presentation by Counselor Shannon Ellsworth commemorating the ratification of
           the 19th Amendment of the United States Constitution. (20-236) (0:15:52)

Ms. Ellsworth wanted to recognize the centennial anniversary of the Nineteenth Amendment, which
gave women the constitutional right to vote. She shared a history of this important event with the
council.

         “The centennial anniversary of the Nineteenth Amendment gives us the opportunity to
         celebrate 100 years of women’s constitutional right to vote. In this uncommon year we can

Provo City Council Meeting Minutes - October 06, 2020                                                   Page 1 of 19
honor the women who fought for women’s suffrage and who promoted women’s full and
equal exercise for their right to vote and to participate in our democracy.

In 1848, one year before Provo was founded, the Seneca Falls Convention was held. This
was the first women’s rights convention. One participant declared that one-half of the
moral and intellectual power was missing from government as long as women could not
participate.

Decades later the Utah Territory first extended suffrage to women on February 12, 1870,
and just two days later Salt Lake City held a municipal election. On her way to work as a
schoolteacher, 24-year-old Seraph Young stopped by city hall. She walked into the building,
cast her ballot, and made history as the first woman in the United States to vote under a
women’s equal suffrage law. However, seventeen years later, a federal act disallowed the
enfranchisement of the women residents within the Utah Territory.

At a territorial suffrage meeting in 1894, Martha Hughes Cannon advocated for women’s
rights in Utah arguing that “one of the principal reasons why women should vote is that all
men and women are created free and equal.” When the Utah constitutional convention
was approved and included women’s suffrage in the new state constitution the following
year, Martha was the first woman in Salt Lake City to register to vote. Then in 1896 Martha
Hughes Cannon ran as a candidate for state senate, and swept the election.

In August of 1900 women of Utah County urged the county council to reconsider its plan to
nominate only men for county offices, and they held a Provo Ladies Protest.

Alice Paul was a Quaker and a vocal leader of the twentieth century women’s suffrage
movement in Washington DC. In January 1917, Paul and over 1,000 protesters began the
unprecedented practice of picketing outside of the White House. Alice Paul and her
colleagues stood each day at the gates with signs. They endured verbal attacks and physical
assaults, and eventually the local police arrested them on flimsy charges. Alice Paul and
others were sentenced to jail for seven months because of their protesting methods. In jail,
Alice Paul organized a hunger strike but doctors force-fed her and threatened to send her
to an insane asylum. Alice Paul´s resolve never wavered, she said, “When you put your
hand to the plow, you can't put it down until you get to the end of the row.”

After media coverage and public outcry over her treatment, President Woodrow Wilson
finally announced his support for women’s suffrage. But it took two more years for the
Senate, the House, and 36 states to approve the amendment that guarantees women the
right to vote. Achieving this milestone required a long and painful struggle. This victory
took several decades of protest and agitation. Few early supporters lived to see the final
victory in 1920.

The early campaign for women’s suffrage lasted more than 70 years, but ratification did
not ensure full enfranchisement. Many American women were unable to vote long into the
20th century based on discriminatory state laws. Decades of struggle were necessary to
include African Americans and minority women in the promise of voting rights.

       our schools, and our communities to register to vote and to participate in every single

 8.     Resolution 2020-36 authorizing the issuance of Wastewater Revenue Bonds of Provo
        City, Utah. (20-102) (1:05:47)

      Motion: An implied motion to approve Resolution 2020-36, as currently constituted, has
              been made by council rule.

Dave Decker, Public Works Director, presented. In late 2018, the City applied for a loan from
the State Department of Environmental Quality for construction/remodeling of our wastewater
treatment plant. This was necessary in order to meet phosphorous discharge regulations
required by the state by the end of 2020. To give us time to make improvements to the
treatment plant, the state gave Provo City a variance until 2025 to meet those regulations.
Terms of the agreement included a $2 million principle forgiveness and loan of $75.8 million
with an interest rate of 0.5% repayable over 20 years. We would pay interest only until the
project was completed and the principle payments would not exceed $50,000 per year until
2027. This was not a general revenue bond, it was a utility revenue bond repaid by wastewater
user fees paid each month by the residents. The last step to approving the loan was a public
hearing and resolution authorizing the issuance of the bonds associated with the loan.




       Provo City Council Meeting Minutes - October 06, 2020                           Page 7 of 19
Eric Hunter, bond counsel for Provo City, reviewed the bond documents with the Council. The
item included a public hearing required by state law and a resolution establishing the
parameters for the bonds, as presented by Mr. Decker. The main bond document was a
supplemental indenture, which would solidify and finalize the bond terms pursuant to the
existing wastewater general indenture. If approved, the resolution would authorize publication
of a 30-day bond issuance notification.

Chair Handley opened the public hearing. There was no response.

Mr. Harding thanked those that had worked on this and the state for their favorable loan

neighborhoods. The proposal would make the following changes in the LDR and VLDR zones:
     LDR Zone
           o Reduce the maximum density from 15 units to 12 units.
