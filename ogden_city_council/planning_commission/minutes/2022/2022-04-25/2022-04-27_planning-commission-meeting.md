1.   Discussion: Make Ogden Ordinances

     Ms. Jessica Garrow stated this discussion is to provide an update to the Planning Commission of the
     processes and to identify key issues before actual ordinances are created. She reviewed the study
     area and indicated meetings have been held with stakeholders within the community and their goal is
     to provide language which will be clear and understandable while incorporating the concepts and
     standards identified in the Make Ogden Plan. She stated consideration is given to land uses, building
     height, setbacks, parking, and the public realm.

     New zoning designations were identified as commercial-mixed-use, commercial-entertainment,
     commercial-downtown, residential-multi-family-vertical, and residential-multi-family horizontal. Land
     Use, building height, setbacks, building materials parking and screening were concepts of
     consideration identified in the Make Ogden Plan. Each of these were presented with proposed code
     changes for each of the following categories: CBD, CBDI, 25th Street, CP-3/C-3, and R-4.

     The presentation suggested commercial uses are preferred on the ground floor of multi-story
     buildings, with residential units allowable only on upper floors. Commission felt there may be
     situations where a complete building could be multi-family residential, indicating there often is no
     market for small commercial space, and there is a need for residential uses to support commercial
     activity. Commission suggested uses such as daycare could be desirable within the downtown to
     accommodate the needs of working parents.

     There also is a recommendation to continue to restrict the use of drive-up windows for restaurants,
     and question arose as to whether drive-thru banking also should be restricted. Discussion continued
     relating to other uses which might also provide drive-up services such as a laundry or pharmacy. It
     was noted while these provide a needed service to some, the traffic also affects the pedestrian
     experience of the downtown area. Parents with small children or those with special needs often
     depend on the ability to use drive-up windows. Commission also suggested uses such as tattoo
     parlors and pawn shops should be restricted in the downtown. Commission felt building height may
     have no restrictions on corridor streets, but should be limited to 3-4 stories in neighborhoods such as
along Grant Avenue. Commission also discussed punch windows as identified in the Make Ogden
Plan or whether curtain walls which are all glass or glass ribbons on walls should be allowed.
Commission felt the character of existing buildings in the downtown is or vertical windows, and this
appearance is desirable to continue, suggesting a maximum amount of glazing might be identified.
Commission felt transparent windows on the ground floor are important to allow for display areas and
invite customers. Commission felt while wood should only be used as an accent material in most of
the downtown, but could be more of a main building material on residential streets.

Parking is suggested to be restricted on the lower two floors of multi-story buildings with screening
and landscaping required similar to current regulations, with no utilities allowed to be visible.
Commission felt consideration should be given to providing additional access points for parking
structures. It was stated two access points often work best on a corner property where access can
come from two different streets.

Discussion continued relating to cell towers, light poles and small cell sites. Commission also asked if
it may be feasible to eliminate the two-step process of both the Planning Commission and Mayor
reviewing CBD site plans.

As there was no additional business, the meeting was adjourned at 7:45pm.
