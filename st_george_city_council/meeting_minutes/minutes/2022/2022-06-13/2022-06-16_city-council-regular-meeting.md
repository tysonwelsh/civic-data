     Public hearing to receive input on the Fiscal Year 2022-2023 Budget and
     consideration of a resolution to formally adopt the Tentative Fiscal Year
     2022-2023 Budget.

      Budget was presented during the May 5th City Council Meeting and has been
      available on the City's website and in the City Recorder's Office. State Law requires

      to receive input on the proposed Fiscal Year 2022-2023 Budget and approval of the
      resolution to adopt the Tentative Fiscal Year 2022-2023 Budget, including

DETERMINE THE RATE OF TAX FOR 2022/LEVY TAXES UPON ALL REAL AND
PERSONAL PROPERTY/RESOLUTION:
     Consider approval of a resolution determining the rate of tax for 2022 tax
     year and levying taxes upon all real and personal property within the City of
     St. George.

      BACKGROUND and RECOMMENDATION: State Law requires that fiscal year entities
      approve the proposed certified tax rate (CTR) on or before June 22nd of each year. If
      an entity is requesting property taxes which exceed the revenue amount which the
      proposed CTR generates, the entity must decide to go through the Truth in Taxation
      process. The proposed CTR for tax year 2022 is .000684 and it is proposed that the
      city increase the 2022 tax rate to .000869 to generate additional revenues for Fiscal
      Year 2022-2023 for the purpose of generating funds for the Safe St. George 5-Year
      Plan. This resolution temporarily establishes the requested tax rate of .000869 and
      directs staff to proceed with the Truth in Taxation process. Staff recommends
      approval of the resolution to increase the proposed certified tax rate and commence
      with the Truth in Taxation process.

PURCHASE OF EXCESS CYBER INSURANCE/RESOLUTION:
     Consider approval of a resolution authorizing the purchase of Excess Cyber
     Insurance.

      BACKGROUND and RECOMMENDATION: Cyber crimes, including cyber extortion, and
      data breaches have increased around the world. Cyber criminals are targeting local
      governments for increased cyber extortion. Cyber extortion increased 518%
      between 2020 and 2021 to an average ransom claim for $5.3M to release its stolen
      data to an entity. The City has received a quote for a $2M excess layer of cyber
      protection, with a $750,000 sublimit for cyber extortion, from Ironshore for FY23.
      Staff recommends authorization to purchase this excess cyber insurance for
      $157,000.
