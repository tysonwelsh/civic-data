Citizen Public Input

               Jeff Scott asked the Council to consider a bike park on Cemetery Hill at the north
and east areas. The west side does not have anything like it, and if done right, it could be

use, it would need to know who would retain liability, maintenance, and overall responsibility for
that.

parking.

Action Items

Improvements to 9180 West 201

                 Chip Hill, Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT), stated he reviewed this
intersection with the Council in 2019, but since then, there have been several additional accidents,
including fatalities. Based on crash data, signal timing, measured speed, as well as the geometry
of the roadway, UDOT is moving forward with a right in and a right out alternative. All other turn
movements will be removed, and a center concrete barrier will be put through the median to
prevent people from turning. The height of the barrier will match what is there now. As part of
the process, UDOT will hold public hearings as the project moves forward.

               Council Member Barney asked if there would be a stoplight there.

               Mr. Hill stated there will be a stop sign like there is now.

New Sign at Pleasant Green Cemetery

                 Rori Andreason stated the Council voted to reconsider its decision on the
Pleasant Green Cemetery sign. The Council can either vote to replace the old sign, continue with
the new design, which she just got pricing on, or choose a different sign. If the Council wants a
different sign, it should put that out for a request for proposal (RFP).

              Council Member Hull stated companies have given the Council bids – two to
replace the old sign that was damaged and another with a new design. She asked if those
companies expected someone to win that bid.

              Ms. Andreason stated that was not a bid process. She just asked Nunny
Nicholes, Pleasant Green Cemetery, to get some quotes on what it would cost to have the sign
put back. Then, the other company developed a design, and the Council voted to go with that
because the rendering looked nice, but she just received the pricing for it. It would not be fair to
that company to have others bid on its design, unless the Council paid for it.

              Council Member Hull stated the bid process is very controlled and very private,
and companies do not know what others bid. If this was opened up to a bid process, that would
be a problem because the company that designed the new sign has made its bid public.

                Nunny Nicholes, Pleasant Green Cemetery, stated he did not ask companies for
a new design.

               Council Member Barney stated he reached out to a metal fabricator and asked if
it would design a new sign and provide a price. The cemetery is under new ownership, so the
Council could put up something more robust, more long-term that would stand the test of time,
and that had a bigger image. That is what appealed to him about the new design versus the
existing sign.

             Mr. Nicholes stated he was fond of the old sign, which has been there over 50
years. The design on the sign is not flawed; it would still be standing if someone had not hit it.

             Mayor Peay stated the insurance company will only pay to replace the sign that
came down, so if the Council went with a different one, the cemetery would have to pay for it.

                Council Member Pierce stated the Council already voted to go with the new sign,
and it should let that stand. Continuing the conversation is frustrating.

              Council Member Prokopis stated he was fine with going either direction, as long
as the process was fair for all parties. It did not seem fair that one company was given one set of
expectations and the other two were not given that same opportunity.

               Council Member Hull stated there is a possibility those two companies could
come back and sue the Magna Metro Township, saying it did what was asked of it and then the
Council went in another direction without giving it a chance to bid that way.

                Ms. Andreason stated she did not think that would happen because they were
just asked for a quote to replace the existing sign. The cost for a new sign is completely different.

               Council Member Prokopis stated he was expecting a $2,000-$4,000 quote to
replace the existing sign, but the bids were quite a bit more than that.

                Council Member Barney stated the price is probably feasible for a 25-foot span
sign that is laser cut and finished, and uses rebar, steel tubing, and steel plate

              Council Member Barney, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to initiate a
formal process to seek a design, and then a formal process to seek a bid for fabrication. The
motion passed unanimously.

               Council Member Hull stated that is the best way to go to make sure the Council
is following good procedures.

Night Out Against Crime

                Council Member Hull stated Night Out Against Crime is August 1, 2022, from
5:00 to 8:00 PM. The Magna United Coalition is working with the Town Council on this. There
are already close to 30 vendors, and the Unified Fire Authority, the Unified Police Department,
and the Highway Patrol will be there. There will be hot dogs, a coke shake, and a snow cone
snack that will give out free snow cones for the first 400 people. This is an important event that
may help reduce crime in the Magna Metro Township. However, funding is needed. and it will
cost about $4,000. The Magna Metro Township has money from last year that it never paid to
the Town Council. She asked if some of that could be put in the coalition’s budget or if the Council
could use that to pay the bills.

                  Todd Richards stated he would prefer that the Town Council take care of that.

              Rori Andreason suggested this be covered through the coalition since there is
grant money that can help cover this.
