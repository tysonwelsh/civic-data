BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY

This application is a request for approval of a Subdivision Plat Amendment and Deviation
for approximately 1.07 acres located on the north side of 12600 South, at approximately
995 East 12600 South (Exhibit B & C). The property is currently zoned TC. The applicant is
requesting that a Subdivision Plat Amendment be approved to modify the common
property line between two lots and a Deviation to keep the street improvements as they
currently are.

The Carol Lee Subdivision was recorded in 1998. The properties were zoned RA2
(Residential Agricultural, 20,000 sq ft lot minimums) at that time. They were rezoned to the
TC zone when that zone was created. The original plat included three lots. This application
is looking only at Lots 2 and 3. Lot 3 was illegally subdivided in 2003 into a 0.13 acre piece

3 now wishes to legally amend Lot 3 and sell a portion of the lot to the owners of Lot 2.

The Planning Commission reviewed this item at their June 13, 2024 meeting and continued
the request. The request at that time sought to create three lots out of the Lots 2 and 3.
The Planning Commissioners determined that creating a third lot that could be developed
under the TC zone was inappropriate given the residential nature of the street and asked
the applicant to bring back an amended plat showing two lots. The plat has been amended
as requested.

The plat amendment will vacate the south side Public Utility Easement on Lot 4 where the
lot will be extended to the south. It will retain the easement on the north side. Lot 5 will
have the west side easement eliminated, but will retain the east side easement. Only one
side easement is required under DCMC. In addition, the original plat showed all easements
as being 10-feet wide. The plat amendment reduces easements to 7-feet wide, with the
exception of the easement along 12600 South. That will remain at 10-feet wide. The
minimum required width for easements under DCMC is 7-feet.

South Valley Sewer District has asked for an easement along the west property line. A
condition of approval has been included requiring that provision prior to plat recordation.

Street Improvement Deviation. The applicant is requesting to deviate from the required
street standards (Exhibit H). Specifically, the applicant is asking to eliminate the curb,
gutter, and sidewalk requirements along 12600 South and 1015 East that would typically be
required with a new or amended subdivision plat. No street improvements to either street
are proposed.

DCMC Section 17-5-030(D) states: ”Curbs, Gutters, Sidewalks: Curbs, gutters, and sidewalks
shall be installed on existing and proposed streets by the subdivider in all subdivisions. The
Planning Commission may, after receiving a recommendation from City staff, modify or
waive the street design requirements.”

There are seven homes and one commercial business on 12600 South. The street is a
public road that is 50-feet wide. Two of the properties, the commercial business on the
corner of Fort St. and the home adjacent to it contain curb, gutter, and sidewalk. The
remainder of the street does not. A new cul-de-sac at the end of 12600 South was
approved with the Bainbridge Subdivision. That new cul-de-sac will also not contain curb,
gutter, or sidewalk. If curb, gutter and sidewalk were provided on the frontage of Lots 4
and 5 it would create drainage concerns for adjacent properties.

The 1015 East street is a private roadway serving two homes. The proposed Lot 4 will
become a corner lot on 12600 S and 1015 East, with access to both streets. There is no
curb, gutter, or sidewalk on 1015 East.

Criteria For Approval.

Subdivision Plat Amendment:
The grounds for review and potential approval of a subdivision plat amendment request is
found in Section 17-9-040 of the DCMC. This section depicts the standard of review for such
requests as:

        The Planning Commission may approve the vacation or amendment of a plat if the
        Planning Commission finds that there is good cause for the vacation or amendment,
        and no public street or municipal utility easement has been vacated or amended.

Street Improvements:
The grounds for review and potential approval of a Deviation to curb, gutter, and sidewalk
installation is found in Section 17-5-030(D) of the DCMC. This section depicts the standard
of review for such requests as:

        Curbs, Gutters, Sidewalks: Curbs, gutters, and sidewalks shall be installed on
        existing and proposed streets by the subdivider in all subdivisions. The Planning
        Commission may, after receiving a recommendation from City staff, modify or waive
        the street design requirements.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION

Staff recommends that the Planning Commission review the request based on the findings
listed below and the criteria for approval, or denial as listed within the staff report. The
Planning Commission should also review the request, hear from the applicant, and receive
public comment prior to making a decision.

If the Planning Commission decides to approve the request, staff recommends they include
the following conditions of approval:

         1.      That all requirements of the Draper City Engineering, Public Works, Building,
                 Planning, and Fire Divisions are satisfied throughout the development of the
                 site and the construction of all buildings on the site, including permitting.
         2.      That per DCMC Section 17-9-070, the plat shall be recorded within one (1)
                 year from the date of approval or it will become null and void. A one-time six-
                 month extension may be requested prior to the expiration of the plat.
         3.      Address the outstanding comments and redlines outlined in Exhibit A prior
                 to plat recordation.
         4.      That an easement acceptable to South Valley Sewer District be provided on
                 the plat prior to recordation.

Dear Brien,

Per your comment review, we are requesting a deferral for the Street Improvements along the
North Side of 12600 South Street After careful consideration and evaluation of the circumstances,
we believe that deferring the improvements at this time is both prudent and strategically
beneficial.

One key factor influencing our decision is the current state of adjacent frontages. As we have
observed, there are no improvements present along other frontages adjacent to the specified
section. Implementing improvements in isolation on this segment could result in a disjointed
streetscape and a lack of continuity with the surrounding area.

Furthermore, we are aware that the parcel to the east is in the process of development.
Coordinating the improvement efforts with the development of this adjacent parcel presents an
opportunity for a more cohesive and integrated approach to streetscape enhancements. By
synchronizing the improvements with the development timeline of the neighboring parcel, we can
ensure a harmonious streetscape design that enhances the overall aesthetic appeal and
functionality of the area.

Additionally, deferring the improvements until the neighboring parcel undergoes development
allows for a more efficient allocation of resources and minimizes potential disruptions to existing
infrastructure. By consolidating improvement efforts during the development phase, we can
optimize construction logistics and streamline project management processes.

In conclusion, we believe that deferring the improvements along the short section of street
frontage until the adjacent parcel undergoes development is the most logical and strategic course
of action. This approach ensures a cohesive streetscape design, maximizes resource efficiency, and
minimizes disruptions to existing infrastructure.

We appreciate your understanding and cooperation in this matter. Should you have any further
questions or require additional information, please do not hesitate to contact us.
