a Notice of Declaration regarding electronic meetings without an anchor location due to COVID-19 dated
November 12, 2020, and effective for 30 days.

2. BUDGET DISCUSSION
a.     Introduction
Council Member Worthen, Chair of the Budget and Audit Committee, reported the Budget and Audit
Committee had been working on priorities for the upcoming budget cycle. Budget priorities identified by
the Council in January of 2020 were: community engagement; aesthetics; long-term plan; communication;
regional influence; and economic development. The following budget priorities were identified by the
Council through email communication during November of 2020: employee retention; road maintenance;
public safety – west-side, technology; reserve levels (General Fund, CIP Fund); and economic
development.

Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck showed General Fund ending balances from FY2016 –
FY2020, with a FY2021 estimate. She shared a comparison of general fund reserves for several cities in
the area. West Jordan had a General Fund Reserve balance of $11 million (19% of General Fund revenues)
in FY2020. Responding to an electronic poll, Council Members answered the following questions:

       •   What is the “right” amount of reserves?
             ➢ Response: 100% (4 Council Members) in favor of 25% or $15 million

Council Member Whitelock asked if the current Council would be able to put something in place to
prevent future Councils or Mayors from spending reserves built up by the current Council. Ms. Steck
responded the Council could enact a reserve fund balance policy with a specified minimum. Council
Member Whitelock said she would want to enact a adopt fund balance policy. Chief Administrative
Committee of the Whole Minutes – December 3, 2020                                                 Page 2

Officer Korban Lee clarified that a future Council would be able to change any policy enacted by the
current Council.

Ms. Steck explained her revenue assumptions for the upcoming budget cycle, and showed a comparison
of 2020 property tax rates in many northern Utah cities. (Council Member Jacob joined at 6:33 pm)

        •   How much revenue does the Council want to dedicate to CIP?
               ➢ Response: 100% (4 Council Members) in favor of 3% or $1.8 million
        •   Does the Council support minimal inflationary property tax increases? (1%=$153,000)
               ➢ Response: 60% (3 Council Members) indicated “Yes”, 40% (2 Council Members)
                   indicated “No”

Council Member Whitelock said she would be able to support a minimal inflationary property tax increase
but would not want implementation to occur in 2021 because of the financial impact the pandemic had on
many households, and because 2021 was a Council election year. She clarified that she was in favor of
residents paying the same monetary amount in property tax to the City in 2021 as they paid in 2020.
Council Member Jacob said he believed locking in the same payment amount would unfairly advantage
some residents, and disadvantage others more than the current system. He said he was in favor of locking
in a rate rather than a monetary amount. Ms. Steck agreed that locking the rate would be the same as an
inflationary increase.

Council Member Pack acknowledged that no one wanted to run for office in a year with a property tax
increase but commented that the Council was there to do the best for the citizens. He said kicking the can
down the road did not accomplish anything good. Council Member Pack suggested an independent
committee look at the data and make a recommendation to the Council and suggested using an inflationary
index for property tax increases, fees, and employee compensation.

Council Member Worthen said as a citizen she would rather deal with small increases every year than less
frequent large increases. Council Member Whitelock said she understood the City should not kick the can
down the road, but said she was not in favor of implementing a property tax increase in a Council election
year because of the many different increases the Council had already needed to impose over the last few
years. Council Member Worthen said she believed 2021 was going to be financially difficult for many
people and said she would be in favor of waiting a year to adopt an inflationary property tax increase.

Council Member Jacob said he believed holding the property tax rate constant would prevent the Council
from needing to impose larger increases down the road and would be a responsible move for the Council
to make, even in a Council election year. Council Member Worthen said she felt the issue should be
viewed with the immediate needs of the citizens in mind rather than from an election perspective.

Ms. Steck commented that the City received significant federal assistance to help reduce impact of the
pandemic on citizens. She suggested riding through the next year with that resource, pushing an
inflationary property tax increase out a little further knowing the Council was supportive of the general
idea. She said she agreed with the suggestion to put a committee together to look at data and make
recommendations.

Mr. Lee pointed out that Council elections were every odd year, and asked if the Council would support
an inflationary property tax increase every even year. Council Member Whitelock said she believed a
small increase every year would be better for the citizens; she just did not think 2021 was the right year
for implementation. Council Member Worthen said she agreed.

Mr. Lee asked if the Council would be interested in issuing a truth-in-taxation statement every year, even
if the Council did not implement an increase, to get citizens used to the idea. Council Member Whitelock

repeated her belief that 2021 was not the right year to begin because people had already dealt with so
much.

Council Member Pack said he believed small increases every year were less impactful than occasional
large increases. Vice Chair Green said he agreed an incremental increase was often more palatable. He
spoke of concern for homeowners, particularly elderly, if property values were to exceed the inflationary
rate. Vice Chair Green said he did not think he was in favor of implementing an increase in 2021 given
ongoing uncertainties citizens were dealing with.

        •   Does the Council support a minimal inflationary property tax increase for the upcoming fiscal
            year?
               ➢ Response: 40% (2 Council Members) indicated “Yes”, 60% (3 Council Members)
                   indicated “No”

Council Member Jacob pointed out that with two Council Members absent, the survey results regarding
an inflationary property tax increase were inconclusive.

        •   Is the Council interested in discussing the 5% utility transfer (FY2021 = $2,469,625)?

Council Member Pack commented that the previous utility transfer became a political issue and said he
would be in favor of discussing a policy decision. Council Member Jacob said he was opposed to the
previous utility transfer, and said he agreed it needed to be a policy decision, not a way to occasionally
plug a hole in the budget.

Ms. Steck said Staff suggested the utility transfer in the FY2021 budget cycle as a proposed policy that
would provide a steady and stable revenue stream to the General Fund and alleviate the need for large
property tax increases. If the utility transfer were removed from budget calculations, a 16.14% property
tax increase would be needed in the next budget year.

Council Member Jacob said he believed it would be difficult to defend utility rates if the utility transfer
were treated like an on-and-off switch. Vice Chair Green said he agreed the utility transfer should not be
turned on and off. He said he philosophically viewed the water utility transfer as treating the water system
like any other utility – collecting a utility franchise tax to compensate the City for use of right-of-way and
roads.

Council Member Whitelock said she felt the utility transfer was a good policy decision when she voted in
favor. Council Member Worthen said she agreed.

        •   Which parts of employee compensation are Council Members expecting to reinstate?
              ➢ Merit/Steps ($1,000,000)
              ➢ Cost of living adjustments 2.7% ($1,000,000)
              ➢ Career ladder ($60,000)
              ➢ Sick leave buyout ($60,000)

Administrative Services Director Steck explained that the sick leave buyout (up to 3 days per year) reduced
the City’s long-term liability for sick leave. She explained that career ladders provided an opportunity for
employees to improve skills and certifications and move up positions, and said she knew the Steps plan
was important for public safety employees. Council Member Whitelock commented that employees in the
private sector did not necessarily get merit/step increases or cost of living adjustments just because they
stayed with a company another year. She said she struggled with the idea. Ms. Steck responded that steps
were important in public safety for employee retention and mentoring.

Responding to a question from Council Member Whitelock, Ms. Steck said she believed the City could
fund all four employee compensation items in the next fiscal year. She pointed out that employee retention
would save the City money in the long-run. Council Member Pack said he wanted to honor City
employees, and said he believed the City should fund the compensation items if Ms. Steck believed they
would fit in the budget. Ms. Steck said the compensation items would be difficult to sustain long-term if
the Council were not willing to implement an incremental property tax increase. Mr. Lee said he was
confident the City would lose employees if some form of merit and cost of living increase were not
regularly provided.

Council Member Worthen pointed out the Council had already indicated employee
retention/compensation as a top priority, and suggested letting Staff put together a recommendation of
what that should look like.

Vice Chair Green asked what growth rate would be sustainable and reasonable to expect for the budget.
Ms. Steck said she believed it was clear the Council was wanting a sustainable long-term plan, which was
her goal, and the goal of the Mayor and Mr. Lee as well. Vice Chair Green said he believed a measured
approach was needed.

        •   What services are the Council Members expecting to reinstate?
              ➢ Response: Facilities maintenance – 4 Council Members
                               Parks maintenance – 3 Council Members
                               Staffing (as justified by department needs) – 3 Council Members

Chief Administrative Officer Lee emphasized that he would not be interested in restoring a service or
position just for the sake of restoring it. He said administration would be analyzing and looking closely at
what was needed to most effectively and efficiently provide necessary services. Vice Chair Green
expressed the opinion that service level to the citizens should be the priority.

Administrative Services Director Steck showed a CIP Fund reserve comparison (West Jordan FY2020
balance = $2.5 million), and asked the following question:

        •   What is the “right” amount of CIP reserves?
              ➢ Response: $1,000,000 – 1 Council Member
                               $5,000,000 – 1 Council Member
                               More than $5,000,000 – 3 Council Members

Council Member Jacob commented that choosing the right amount of capital reserves was difficult.
Responding to a question from Council Member Pack, Ms. Steck explained Sandy City’s policy of never
touching their 11% General Fund reserves, and using CIP reserves for required maintenance. Vice Chair
Green said he believed the West Jordan CIP reserve balance should be higher than $2.5 million. Mr. Lee
commented that Sandy City always estimated revenues conservatively, with any amount over the estimate
going to CIP reserves.

        •   What revenue sources do Council Members want dedicated to CIP, General Fund savings at
            year end, or General Fund budgeted transfer?

Administrative Services Director Steck recommended starting small if the Council wanted to budget an
intentional transfer to CIP. Council Member Whitelock said she would want to budget a modest intentional
transfer, with General Fund savings at year end also transferred to CIP. Council Member Worthen said
she agreed. Vice Chair Green said he believed the Council should intentionally budget a transfer to CIP,
even if the City could only afford a very small amount to start. Council Members Pack and Jacob said
they agreed the allocation should be intentional.

   Council Member Worthen reviewed the priorities indicated by Council Members in a survey during
   November 2020: (1) employee retention; (2) road maintenance; (3) public safety – west-side, technology;
   (4) reserve levels – General Fund, CIP Fund; (5) economic development. Council Members discussed that
   technology should possibly have its own bullet point between (3) and (4), pushing economic development
   off the “top 5”.

   Referring to water rates, Council Member Whitelock stated she did not want residential water users
   subsidizing commercial water users. Council Member Pack said he agreed it would be nice for users to
   pay for what they were using, but recognized that it may not always be 100% level.

   Going back to budget priorities, Council Member Jacob said he would move economic development to
   the top of the priority list, because economic development funded everything else. Council Member
   Whitelock said she agreed.

   Vice Chair Green asked each Council Member if they wanted anything added to the list. Council Member
   Pack said he would want to ensure restricted funds remained restricted, such as funds set aside for the art
   center. Council Member Whitelock said she agreed with Council Member Pack, and said she wanted
   administration to consider all the items on the list as priorities, not necessarily in any order of importance.
   Council Member Worthen said she believed the funds set aside for the art center should be used to keep
   the City’s financial foundation stable rather than letting it sit in an account.

   Council Member Jacob said he would really like the City to be able to do zero-based budgeting, building
   a budget from zero and justifying everything. He said he believed the biggest priorities should be funded
   first (public safety), with the Council going to the residents to justify tax increases for whatever was
   unfunded at the bottom of the list.

   Referring to the art center, Council Member Green said the City had the money, and could be creative to
   get more money if needed. He said citizens were going to need things to feel good about when the
   pandemic was over, and said he would rather build the art center sooner than later. He suggested using
   RDA funds to help with aesthetics and maintenance. Council Member Green spoke of the need to fund
   wellness measures for first responders.

   Mr. Lee thanked the Council for their willingness to schedule extra meetings to discuss the budget, and
   thanked the Budget Committee for all their work.

   3. ADJOURN
   Council Member Worthen thanked everyone for their contributions and moved to adjourn the meeting.
   Council Member Whitelock seconded the motion. All voted in favor and the motion passed unanimously. The
   meeting adjourned at 9:02 p.m.
