                                  PLANNING COMMISSION MINUTES
                                           Meeting of October 28, 2021
                            Logan City Council Chambers (290 N 100 W Logan, UT 84321) www.loganutah.org

Minutes of the meeting for the Logan City Planning Commission convened in regular session on
Thursday, October 28, 2021, Chair Dickinson called the meeting to order at 5:30 p.m.

Commissioners Present: Regina Dickinson, Sandi Goodlander, Roylan Croshaw, Ken Heare, David
Lewis, Jessica Lucero, Jordy Guth

Commissioners Excused:

Staff Present: Russ Holley, Mike DeSimone, Tanya Rice, Bill Young, Ben Anderson, Mohamud
Abdullahi, Jeannie Simmonds (City Council)

Commissioner Croshaw moved to approve the minutes from the October 14, 2021 meeting as
submitted. Motion seconded by Commissioner Guth. The motion was approved unanimously.

 PC 21-064 Cache County Food Pantry [Design Review Permit] Courtney Wallace/Cache
 County Food Pantry, Inc., authorized agent/owner, request a design review permit to build a
 new 9,240 SF building to the west of their existing building to use for additional food storage.
 The project is located at approximately 350 South Main in the Commercial (COM) zone; TIN
 02-052-0029; -0001. (Ellis Neighborhood)

STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a design review permit to construct a new 9,240 SF
warehouse building for food storage. The single-story warehouse-style building is proposed to be
located in the rear of the property and will match some of the same materials and colors of their
main building located on Main Street. The proposal includes an additional driveway off of 400
South and 13 new parking stalls. Access to the new building is gained via Main Street, 300 South,
and the proposed new access from 400 South. The proposed plan includes the demolition of two
existing buildings to construct the new building and parking as well as the demolition of a single-
family home on 400 South to create the driveway and new entrance. The proposed driveway is


38 feet wide, which exceeds the Land Development Code standard width of 24 feet for a double
driveway, this will need to be considered by the commission. The proposed parking and driveway
setbacks will also need to be considered. Building design standards will not apply to the east
façade as it is not visible from Main Street. The commission will need to consider if design
standards will apply to the south façade where a small portion of the building may be visible from
400 South once the existing home and trees are removed.
R. Holley reviewed an alternate plan that was recently submitted by the applicant. The new plan
includes additional landscaping, open space, and a sidewalk connecting to 400 South. The
proposed driveway is 16-feet wide with a narrower approach from 400 South and includes 16
angled shared parking stalls along the west side of the proposed driveway


Commissioner Croshaw asked about the proposed 16-foot wide driveway and how will a longer
vehicle be able to park without blocking the driveway. He asked if the semi-trucks will be able to
enter this driveway easily from 400 South.

Commissioner Heare asked about the church and their willingness to share parking. R. Holley said
that there has not been a shared parking agreement submitted yet, but the applicant has stated
that the church needs the extra parking.

PROPONENT: Matt Whittaker explained that they are growing, and this new warehouse will help
keep up with the growing demand. The increased storage will allow for more space in their store
area so more individuals can be in the store at a time. It will also provide the required cleanroom
needed to package the meat that they receive. They are proposing the new driveway to address a
current safety concern with the semi-trucks that are pulling in from Main Street while the parking lot
is full of customers. Commissioner Lucero asked how often they get semi deliveries. Matt stated
that semi’s come about once a week.

PUBLIC: Kevin Forth, has lived at 47 West 400 South for over 30 years, directly to the West of the
proposed driveway. His concern is with the traffic on the new driveway that is less than 25 feet
away from his kitchen window, and the exhaust that will enter their home. He feels that this
driveway will turn into a shortcut through the block for traffic to avoid the light at 300 South. He
asked if there could be a privacy wall installed along his eastern property boundary and a gate at
the new entrance off 400 South.

Ruth Forth, who lives at 47 West 400 South, addressed her concerns with the added pollution
coming in their windows, 400 South not being wide enough for semi traffic, and the added safety
concerns the semi-trucks will bring. She stated she is also concerned with the irrigation water that
flows onto her property right where the project's dumpster will be located. She spoke about the
added noise pollution once the trees are removed, the light pollution from the additional lights of
the parking area, and her concern that neighbors did not receive notice about the project. She also
asked about the power pole that is being proposed to move and how long they will have to be
without power when that takes place.

COMMISSION: In response to some of the public comments, Commissioner Dickinson mentioned
that the future land use of this area is designated commercial and that the lighting is addressed in
the conditions of approval. R Holley added that there was a public notice mailed out to all
homeowners within a 300 feet radius of the project. He said that typically when a power line is
moved, the homeowner should only be without power for a couple of hours. Their irrigation will still
have to flow to their property, it may just need to be rerouted. R. Holley also mentioned that It
would be up to the property owner to address any issues that arise with traffic cutting through the
block.


Commissioner Dickinson asked if a wall could be conditioned. M. DeSimone said that per the
parking standards in the Land Development Code when a parking area abuts a single-family
property, fencing, berming, or landscaping can be required.

Commissioner Goodlander said she would like to see the number of customers that visit the food
pantry to determine the parking and landscaping requirements. She would also like to see some
sort of privacy barrier between the parking lot and the existing home.

Commissioner Croshaw agreed and said that there needs to be a better landscape plan along the
driveway/parking lot. Commissioner Guth said she agreed and would also like to continue this
project. Commissioner Lucero agreed that there needs to be an alternate parking plan as well as a
plan with the shared area and the church property. Commissioner Lewis is not in favor of the
proposed location of the garbage cans and agrees that there needs to be more planning with the
driveway and the church property to see if the homeowner can have more privacy. Commissioner
Heare said he is in favor of continuing.

Commissioner Goodlander stated that she is fine with the building design as is, where it sits so far
back from the road. Commissioner Lucero agreed


MOTION: Commissioner Heare moved to continue PC 21-064 to the November 18, 2021 meeting.
Commissioner Croshaw seconded the motion. The motion was approved unanimously.


Moved: K. Heare               Seconded: R. Croshaw                  Approved: 7-0

Yea: Guth, Goodlander, Croshaw, Dickinson, Lucero, Lewis, Heare            Nay:           Abstain:
