Rocky Mountain Power- Blue Sky Legacy Award
         Rocky Mountain Power has selected Ogden City for the 2021 Blue Sky Legacy Award. A representative from Rocky
Mountain Power (RMP) will announce the award. City representatives will officially accept the award at a luncheon later in
October.
         Rocky Mountain Power Regional Business Manager, Kirk Nigro, reported the Blue Sky Legacy Award will be given to
the City officially on October 21,2021 but he felt it important to announce the City's receipt of the award in this setting. Ogden
City and RMP have been community partners for 20 years and have accomplished several meaningful projects for the area. The
Blue Sky Legacy Award is a chance for RMP to recognize forward thinking organizations, like Ogden, that initiate sustainable
measures that help their communities come together to support renewable energy. Ogden City has been a Blue Sky Energy partner
for 15 years and has led by example to create a more sustainable future; through the Blue Sky Program, the City has supported
over 146,000 megawatt hours of renewable energy. Because of Ogden's example, its citizens are also supporting renewable
energy and sustainability. Seventeen percent of Ogden's residents participate in Blue Sky or their own on-site solar generation.
Eight Blue Sky grant projects have been completed in the City, as well as additional energy saving projects that have resulted in
over four million kilowatt hour savings each year. These are incredible milestones and RMP is grateful to Ogden for its
leadership; Ogden is just the second city in the State of Utah to receive this award.

Proposed Resolution 2021-14 adopting Amendment #1 of the Annual Action
Plan for the period July 1, 2021 through June 30, 2022, and directing that
this document be submitted to the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban
Development (HUD)
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider a resolution
adopting Amendment# 1 of the Annual Action Plan (AAP) for July 1, 2021 to June 3 0, 2022. The memo stated as a requirement
to receive federal funding from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), the City's Community and
Economic Development Department prepares and submits to the Council what is known as a five-year Consolidated Plan
(ConPlan) and an AAP. The AAP is used to outline how federal Community Development Block Grant funds, HOME funds and
other federal and non-federal program funds will be used by the City in the upcoming fiscal year. The amendments proposed with
this mid-year update include adjusting the HOME homeownership value limits. When the city provides assistance to homeowners
for HOME-assisted housing units, the purchase price and appraisal value may not exceed 95 percent of the median purchase price
for the City. The BUD-derived fonnula for determining home values used data from July 2019 through June 2020 and was
implemented beginning in June 2021. The data lagged behind the market and has limited what the City can do based on these
lower values. The City, per HUD guidelines, performed its own market study to determine more accurate values using more recent
data. The proposal is to amend the FY22 Annual Action Plan to include the higher values from the City's study.
          Community Development Manager Ogden summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
summarize the purpose of the proposed amendment. The first amendment deals with the HOME Grant program, which
encompasses Own in Ogden down payment assistance, the Quality Neighborhoods Program, and Community Housing
Development Organization (CHDO) projects. The amendment will not have a fiscal impact but is intended to adjust affordable
homeownership limits. Currently, limits are determined by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD); the
price /value for HOME assisted single-family housing units may not exceed 95 percent of median purchase price in the Ogden
area. HUD determines the maximum purchase price based on 95 percent of median purchase price using data on FHA insured
mortgages and FHFA securitized mortgages. The maximum purchase price is $294,000 for single family housing units as of June
2021. The proposed change would allow Ogden City to determine HOME program affordable homeownership limits. HUD allows
the City to determine HOME affordable homeownership limits and these limits would be based upon Ogden's Housing Market
Study from August of 2021. The study will be submitted to HUD for review and approval and, once that is completed, the City
will be able to raise the maximum purchase price limit. The motivation to complete a housing market study is the dramatic
increase of the price of single-family homes; prices increased by 33 percent between July 2021 and June of2021. He expounded
on the methodology of the Housing Market Study, concluding that ifHUD approves the study, homeownership limits would be
adjusted by property type as follows:

Proposed Ordinance 2021-47 amending the Municipal Code to allow
director approval of minor amendments to conditional use permits
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed Conditional Use Permit
(CUP) amendments came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Planning Department is proposing to change
the approval process for certain CUP amendments. Staff currently refers all CUP amendments to the Planning Commission, but
this process is time consuming and inefficient. The proposed zone text amendment would allow certain minor changes to CUPs to
be approved administratively, by the Planning Manager. Doing so would allow minor amendments to be approved more quickly,
improve customer service, and make better use of staff resources.
          Deputy Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on
the proposed text amendments. He noted one of the strategies identified by the City Council is to create the most inviting,
responsive local government in the state by continuing to promote a business-friendly environment and by reducing bureaucracy
and revising unnecessary regulations for development. The City's current code regarding CUPs dictates that once issued, a CUP
shall not be enlarged, changed, extended, increased in intensity, or relocated except by approval of the Planning Commission. The
proposed text amendment would allow minor amendments of approved CUPs to be reviewed at the staff level. He stated minor
amendments could be defmed as one of the following:
               •   A modification to a telecommunication facility that replaces, modifies, or adds equipment on an existing tower
                    or structure or modifies ground equipment and that does not increase the height or outer dimensions of the
                   facility.
               •   An exterior change that would not create new or increase reasonably anticipated detrimental effects.
               • A change to a different conditional use within the same use categmy that would not create new or increase
                   reasonably anticipated detrimental effects on surrounding propetties or the environment.
               • An addition that would increase the floor area or outdoor area occupied by the conditional use by no more than
                    10 percent of the originally approved floor area or outdoor area, up to a maximum of2,000 square feet.
               • A modification to a parking, loading or maneuvering area that adds no more than 2,000 square feet of
                   impervious surface.
          The Planning Commission recommended approval of the proposed text amendments.
          Vice Chair White asked if there is a fee associated with a CUP amendment application, and if this amendment will result
in that fee being eliminated. Mr. Brierley stated there is a fee and it will remain in place upon approval of the amendments.
