Community Business Survey Results —- EDCUtah
Business Development Manager Meess explained that Ogden City is amember community of Economic Development
Corporation of Utah (EDCUtah), a partner of the Governor’s Office of Economic Opportunity (GOEO) that assists with statewide
corporate recruitment. EDCUtah provides member communities with an annual community business survey that can identify
opportunities and challenges for business growth. EDCUtah conducted a survey for Ogden in June 2023 and provided a report
(attached). Ogden’s Business Development Division will be working with EDCUtah to conduct annual surveys going forward.
Matt Hillburn, EDCUtah, presented the results of a business survey conducted for Ogden in June 2023 by EDCUtah.

while those that would expand elsewhere noted real estate costs and customer base location as factors.

e 57 percent of respondents have a positive outlook on Ogden’s future, slightly below the state average of 69

percent and significantly higher than the national average of 27 percent. \

permitting.

Affordable Housing and Home Ownership Discussion — City Efforts

The City Council is interested in ascertaining whether there are additional policies or programs that the City could
undertake to facilitate home ownership. The Council has invited developers, experts in commercial lending, non-profits, and
investment groups to share their expertise with the Council. The Council hopes that these discussions will lead to new
opportunities for home ownership in Ogden. The discussion this evening, will be a report from the City’s Community and
Economic Development Department regarding the City’s past and current efforts to provide home ownership options in Ogden.

Community Development Manager Smith stated the Community and Economic Development Department wished to
receive feedback from the Council based upon the input they have received over the course of the last several weeks from real :
estate and financing professionals. He facilitated discussion among the Council regarding topics of shared interest, such as the
ability to use RDA funds for unique housing projects that may be different than projects completed using federal grant funding,
such as funding from the Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD); items outside of the City’s control, such as
shifts in the real estate market, and demands for certain types of uses; housing projects with deed restrictions; the need to perform
an updated inventory of the City’s housing stock; the appropriate ratio of rental and owner-occupied units for the City of Ogden;
the amount of open land still available in the City for development; concerns expressed by the public regarding the number of
apartment units built in the community in recent months/years; and the ability to address certain housing needs/matters during the
project to update the City’s General Plan.

_ Proposed Subdivision Ordinance Amendments

J Planning Manager Brierley explained that in response to changes to State Code, the Administration is proposing a series
of changes to the City’s subdivision regulations. Subdivision regulations, located in Title 14 of the City’s Code, govern the
division of real property for the purpose of sale or development. The subdivision code, zoning code, outdoor sign code, and
landmarks code together constitute the City’s development code. The City’s subdivision regulations generally cover the review
process for preliminary subdivision plans and final plat recordation, requirements for specific information such as public
improvement plans and geotechnical studies, as well as the approval processes and some associated timelines. The subdivision
code works in tandem with the zoning code where the subdivision code regulates the review and approval process of the division
of property while the zoning code establishes development regulations like minimum lot size, setbacks, building height, and
allowable uses. During the 2023 legislative session, the state legislature passed SB174 which establishes regulations on how cities
could regulate their subdivision process. Most notably, SB174 limited the approval of a preliminary subdivision application to one
administrative land use authority, removing the ability for a legislative body to approve that application. This is in response to
many smaller cities across the state requiring multiple approvals for subdivisions from staff, the Planning Commission, and the
City Council. The frequency and repetition of these approvals have slowed the approval process in many cities. In Ogden, the City
Council is not involved in the subdivision approval process so the elimination of the legislative body as an approval authority does
not impact Ogden. However, state code now allows only one approving body for the preliminary subdivision application. Ogden
City currently requires this application to be approved by the Planning Commission and the Mayor.

The changes proposed for the subdivision regulations include amending the approval process for a preliminary
subdivision plan to that of only the Planning Commission and no longer the Mayor, changes and updates to the staff level review
process to be more up to date and consistent with best practices and other elements of the code, updated definitions, and a change
to the appeal authority for subdivisions from the District Court to the Board of Zoning Adjustment. The changes also rewrite and
update the regulations for other subdivision related processes such as parcel and lot combinations, parcel boundary and lot line
adjustments, and amendments to recorded subdivisions which can include plat vacations, the vacation and/or recordation of
dedicated property, and the vacation of recorded plats and dedicated rights-of-way.

Council Deputy Director Symes stated that action on the proposed ordinance amendments has been scheduled for January
9, 2024.

Council Business

Council Initiative — Newspaper Noticing Requirements: Vice Chair Richey has submitted a Council Initiative to review ( )
the requirements for newspaper notifications. The Council reviewed and ultimately did not adopt proposed changes removing
newspaper notifications earlier this year. Vice Chair Richey has expressed interest in revisiting newspaper noticing requirements
to look at possibly shortening the required time for newspaper notification. Vice Chair Richey is not proposing to eliminate the
requirement.

Council Deputy Director Symes reported he met with Planning Manager Brierley and City Recorder Hansen regarding
the implications of the Council’s rejection of proposed amendments to newspaper noticing requirements; the Council accepted the
proposal to shorten the required time for newspaper notifications responsive to the concerns expressed by Administrative staff and
Council staff indicated they will submit a request for recommendation from the Planning Commission and will work with legal
counsel to draft a new ordinance for Council consideration.

Snowplow Parking Ordinance Discussion: The Administration is requesting amendments to Ogden Municipal Code §
10-5-21 to clarify parking limitations during winter months as well as during and directly after snow events. The amendments
also clarify the Mayor’s authority to exempt streets that have inadequate off-street parking as exempt from the ordinance. The
Council has reviewed input received through a Flash Vote survey to determine how the proposed changes might impact residents.

The Council will be reviewing how other jurisdictions address parking during winter months.

Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized her conversations with Administration regarding the Council’s
concerms regarding the proposed amendments to the City’s winter parking regulations; Administration has drafted amendments to
the ordinance responsive to these concers. They have removed the November 1 to March 15 time frame. The ordinance prohibits
on-street parking between the hours of 2:00 a.m. and 6:00 a.m. when it is snowing or within 24 hours after a snowstorm. Certain
streets or types of properties shall be exempt from the ordinance as well.

Discussion among the Council and staff centered on whether it is necessary to cite the source of weather forecasts that
residents should rely upon to determine whether a storm is imminent and they should avoid parking on the street; whether
residents with insufficient off-street parking space would be allowed to park their vehicles at nearby public properties, such as a
parking lot for a City park; the manner in which the City will enforce the ordinance.

Council member Lopez stated that he can support the ordinance if Administration is willing to define enforcement
protocol in the actual ordinance. The Council and Administrative staff discussed whether it is appropriate to include
administrative functions in the Code. |
