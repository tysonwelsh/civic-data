    Proposed Fiscal Year 2021-2022 Annual Action Plan Amendment #2
              Community and Economic Development Project Coordinator Fuentes explained that as a requirement to receive federal
    funding from the U.S. Deparhnent of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), the City's Community and Economic
    Development Deparhnent prepares and submits to the Council what is known as a five-year Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) and an
    Annual Action Plan. The Annual Action Plan (AAP) is used to outline how federal Community Development Block Grant funds,
    HOME funds and other federal and non-federal program funds will be used by the City in the upcoming fiscal year. When the
    Annual Action Plan is submitted to HUD in May of each year, specific dollar amounts for entitlement grant funds, program
    income funds, and carry-over funds are estimated for the next fiscal year. Part way through the year, after the specific dollar
    amounts are known or when program income from loans and other programs are received, an amendment must be made to the
    plan to update the Annual Action Plan budget. She noted Administration is proposing the Council approve the second amendment
    to the Fiscal Year (FY)2022 Annual Action Plan budget. The proposed budget amendment reflects changes resulting in an overall
    increase of$1, 722,69 5 in funds for FY2022 over the previously adopted FY22 Annual Action Plan budget. This total includes an
    increase in adjusted program income and carry-over funds. The majority of the budget changes with this amendment include
    reconciling actual program income and cany-over funding that was estimated for the remainder of the FY2021. One other
    significant change proposed is the consolidation of the CDBG-CV funded Microenterprise Loan Fund and Small Business Loan
    programs to the newly proposed Business Recovery Fund. This change is specific to the programs funded from CARES Act
    funding related to COVID relief; the non-CBDG-CV funded Micro enterprise Loan program and Small Business Loan program
    remain funded with CDBG funding.
              There was brief discussion among the Council and staff regarding the manner in which increased funds will be used, with
    Ms. Fuentes noting that there is no increase in funding, but rather a reallocation of funding for identified needs.

    Proposed Business Recovery Fund Program
              Community and Economic Development Project Coordinator Fuentes explained as a part of the Annual Action Plan and
    the City's administration of Community Development Block Grant funding, program guidelines are developed for specific
    programs targeting specific needs. The program guidelines serve as general parameters for how the city will use funding for that
    particular program. These programs guidelines are referred to in the Annual Action Plan but are developed and adopted entirely
    by the city. Amending the program guidelines is done through a resolution adopted by the City Council. The Administration is
    proposing the Business Recovery Fund for use ofCDBG-CV funds. The CDBG-CV funds, received as part of the 2020 COVID-
    19 relief-related CARES Act, were received by the City in 2020 and included in the FY2021 Annual Action Plan budget under
    CDBG-CV funded Microenterprise Loan and Small Business Loan programs. The initial funding was placed under these
    programs, but funding was separated by the source. The proposed Business Recovery Fund would consolidate the funding
    received in 2020 into one newly created program funded solely by CDBG-CV funding. Program guidelines, similar to the
    Microenterprise Loan and Small Business Loan programs, are proposed to provide standards for use of the funds. The program is
    anticipated to operate until the CDBG-CV funding is expended.

Alley Vacation - 1730 Washington Boulevard
          Deputy Planning Manager Brierly explained a developer has two parcels that are separated by an alley. The developer is
petitioning to vacate the portion of alleyway that separates his two parcels for the purpose of consolidating and developing the
parcels together. The alley is a public right-of-way that belongs to the City. The Planning Commission unanimously recommended
approving the vacation. They felt that there was good cause to vacate, and that no public interest nor person would be harmed by
the action. All other properties that use this alley would still have access through the remaining portions of the alley. The Planning
Commission also recommended reserving an easement for any utilities that may now exist in the vacated area and recommended a
condition that the applicant provide a recordable plat for the vacated area combining the alley into the adjacent subdivision lot.
          Council member Stephens asked if the applicant would pave the area that will be vacated. Mr. Brierly stated that the
section that is part of the proposed development will be repaved, and it is likely that if the subdivision is approved, the applicant
will be required to pave the alley to the south.
          Council member Lopez asked if the applicant would pay the City for the portion of the valley for which they are
requesting a vacation. Mr. Brierly indicated that the property was deeded to the City at no cost, so the City is not seeking
compensation in conjunction with the vacation. Vice Chair White asked if the City has incurred costs for maintaining the alleyway
and whether compensation should be sought to recoup those costs. Mr. Brierly indicated the alleyway has not been maintained i1i
the same fashion as other public rights-of-way.

Consideration of certifying the November 2. 2021 Municipal General
Election results
          A memo from the City Recorder came before the Council to consider certifying the November 2, 2021 Municipal
General Election results. The memo stated Section 20A, Chapter 4 of the Utah State Code sets forth the election review process,
designates the legislative body- the City Council- and the Mayor as the Board of Canvassers and outlines the Board's duties for
municipal elections. The City Recorder acts as the election official. The Board of Canvassers is responsible for certifying the
official election results of municipal elections. Canvassing of retums for a General Election must occur no sooner than seven (7)
days after the election and no later than fourteen (14) days after the election. After reviewing the retums, the Board must certify
the results of the election and file a report that includes the following information:
               1. The total number of votes cast in the Board's jurisdiction;
               2. The name of each candidate whose name appeared on the ballot;
               3. Each office that appears on the ballot;
               4. The number of votes for each candidate;
               5. The total number of votes given in the Board's jurisdiction to each candidate;
               6. The number of ballots that were rejected; and
               7. A statement certifying that the information contained in the report is accurate.
          City Recorder Hansen summarized the memo and reviewed the Canvass Report for the November 2, 2021 General
Election. There are 33,000 registered voters in the City and there were 8,817 total votes cast. She indicated the duly elected
candidate for the At Large A position is MarciaL. White; for At Large B, Bart E. Blair was elected; Angela Choberka was elected
in Municipal District 1, and Ken Richey was elected in Municipal District 3. Ms. Hansen then briefly reviewed the report
regarding the number of ballots that were disqualified and cited the reasons for disqualification, noting that voters are notified of
the potential for their ballot to be disqualified and given time to rectify the issue from which the disqualification would result. She
concluded that it is her recommendation that the Board certify the results of the November 2, 2021 Municipal General Election.
          Council member Hyer referenced the report of disqualified ballots; he asked if the County Election Officials assume any
nefarious motives behind the submittal of ballots that are ultimately disqualified. Ms. Hansen stated that a common mistake is
signature of the voter on the ballot envelope not matching the voter for which the ballot was assigned; this occurs commonly when

the property from R-1-6 to R-3. The Council voted to extend this item during their October 5, 2021 meeting; the matter was
discussed again on November 9, 2021 and the Council extended consideration of the ordinance and directed staffto negotiate a
