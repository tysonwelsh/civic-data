        The purpose of the joint work session was to review the agendas for the Special City Council, Redevelopment Agency
(RDA), and Municipal Building Authority (MBA) meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.; discuss the proposed Fiscal Year
(FY) 2025-2026 City budget, including General Fund Revenues, Business Depot Ogden (BDO) Lease Revenues and
Expenditures, City Attorney’s Department, and Public Safety Employee Group proposals; discuss the proposed FY 2025-2026
MBA budget; discuss Council/Board/Trustee business; and hear Council/Board/Trustee comments.

Agenda Review
       Chair White and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agendas for the City Council, RDA, and
MBA meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

City Council:
Proposed Fiscal Year 2025-2026 Budget;
         Presentation – Public Safety Employee Group Representatives: TJ Scott, President of the Ogden City Police Benefits
Association (OPBA), provided the Council with information regarding the current sentiment of the employees of the Police
Department. Employees are pleased with how they have been treated by City Administration and the City Council and there are
not too many concerns regarding employee turnover in the coming fiscal year (FY).
         John Thomas, Vice President for the Fraternal Order of Police (FOP), echoed the comments made by Mr. Scott; he has
been associated with the Ogden Police Department for 28 years and has witnessed good and bad times in terms of employee
morale. The employees of the Department area currently happy with the recognition they have received for the work they do in the
community and for compensation and benefits improvements. He applauded the Administration and Council for prioritizing City
employees but asked the Administration to consider an employee manpower study responsive to the population growth in the
community, namely the increase in apartment and multi-family complexes being built in recent months and years.
         Vice Chair Hyer thanked Mr. Thomas for his recruiting efforts for the Department and asked him what his experience has
been relative to the reasons people choose not to work for Ogden, or choose to move to another agency after having worked for
Ogden. Mr. Thomas stated there are many different reasons that people leave the City, but he has not heard any recent complaints
about pay or morale that has caused someone to move to a different agency. The current culture of the Department, and the
community as a whole, makes, his job as a recruiter easy.
         Alex Sanders, President of Ogden Fire Association (OFA), echoed the information expressed by Mr. Scott and Mr.
Thomas regarding the current morale of the Public Safety Departments of the City; prospective employees are eager to work for
Ogden City based upon call volume and efforts to improve the compensation and benefit package for employees. Additionally,
firefighters feel supported by the City’s Administration and City Council. He then communicated standard operating procedures
(SOPs) provided by the National Fire Protection Agency (NFPA) that are based upon a community’s population, geographic
area/size, and call volume. He played a short video that illustrated the formula used to determine SOPs and asked the Council to
consider this information as they are planning for future improvements to fire response infrastructure, equipment, and staffing
levels. He concluded that the types of residential units in the City has changed dramatically over the past several years and the risk
associated with responding to a fire in a high-rise or multi-family structure is much higher than for a single-family unit structure.
Increased staffing/apparatus will improve the level of safety for citizens in the Ogden community. He then used the aid of a short
PowerPoint presentation to offer a comparison of Ogden’s staffing levels and call volume with other cities, such as Salt Lake,
West Valley, and the Unified Fire Authority and South Davis Metro agency. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the
information packet for the meeting.
          There was brief discussion among the Council and Mr. Sanders regarding the factors that have contributed to instances of
understaffing in the Fire Department.
          City Attorney’s Department: The City Attorney’s Department is funded primarily through the General Fund. The
department also has two positions that are partially funded by grants through the Victims of Crime Act (VOCA) and the Violence
Against Women Act (VAWA).
          City Attorney Williams approached the Council and communicated the significant budget line-item changes for his
Department are as follows:
               • New position – Digital Evidence Technician (grade 135).
               • Wage and benefit increases, including the new position salary, total $193,225.
               • IT Contract maintenance charges reduced by $1,650
          The new position recommendation is based upon recommendations from the Management Audit that was conducted for
the City several years ago. The duties of the new position have been handled by a part-time position over the course of the past
year, but the position has yielded significant benefits to the City, and it would be a positive shift to convert the position to a full-
time position.
          There was brief discussion among the Council and Mr. Williams regarding the types of legal matters for which the City
relies upon contract attorneys or the Weber County Attorney’s Office.
          General Fund Revenues: Finance Director Sorensen explained revenues for the City, which include Ogden City
Corporation, Redevelopment Agency, Municipal Building Authority, and Strike Force funds, are projected to increase 7.5 percent.
The overall changes include increases in tax revenues, County funds, collections, recreation fees, and additional interest income.
The total General Fund increases included in the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2025 budget are $4,879,775, about 5.42 percent over
the FY 2025 adopted budget. Several of the City’s utility Enterprise Funds show increases over FY2025. The majority of these
increases are from the annual CPI increases for the water, sanitary sewer, storm sewer, and refuse funds. Additional increases in
the water utility revenues come from rate increases needed to cover the Canyon Water Line bonded debt. He used the aid of a
PowerPoint presentation to offer a comparison of proposed revenues for FY 2025-2026 with the same line items in the FY 2024-
2025 adopted budget. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          The Council asked questions regarding the factors that have resulted in increases or decreases of certain revenue sources;
there was also discussion about financial trends and forecasts that are being relied upon to formulate revenue projections for FY
2025-2026.
          Business Depot Ogden Lease Revenues and Expenditures: Finance Director Sorensen explained lease revenue from
Business Depot Ogden (BDO) is accumulated throughout the fiscal year for appropriation in the next fiscal year. This ensures that
the City has an accurate figure when determining the amount available for appropriations rather than an estimate of how much
might be generated during the year. The accumulated full-year fund balance as of June 30, 2025, will be $15 Million. The Mayor
is recommending appropriating $14,614,300 in Fiscal Year 2026. He used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to illustrate the
annual increases in BDO lease revenue dating back to 2013, as well as the projected uses of the funding through FY2041. The
proposed BDO lease revenue projections for FY2026 include:
               • Marshall White Bond Payment - $1,476,650
               • CBD Mall District (Debt Service/Reserve/Capital) - $250,000
               • Quality Neighborhoods Funding (year 5/5)-Pulled in FY25 - $0
               • Union Station Land Purchase (year 3/7) - $780,300
               • WonderBlock Debt - $ 3,019,750
               • Public Arts - $100,000
               • Public Arts Maintenance - $2,500
               • CIP Fund - $2,933,000
               • Airport Fund - $453,300
               • Golf Fund - $1,287,850
               • Critical Project Contingency - $150,000
               • Parking Structure Debt – MBA - $2,589,950
               • Parking Contingency - $150,000
               • Airport Fund for Operations - $500,000
               • Elections – Even Years - $121,000
             • Funding for Junction Interfund Loan through 25 th Street RDA - $500,000
             • GWCU Loan – St. Anne - $300,000
         Mr. Sorensen also offered a breakdown of the $2.93 million in funding being allocated to the CIP projects fund. For a
copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Discussion among the Council and Mr. Sorensen centered on any guidance included in the ordinance that governs the use
of BDO lease revenue regarding a percentage that should be unspent each year. Mr. Sorensen indicated the ordinance does not
provide such guidance.

Municipal Building Authority:
Proposed Fiscal Year 2025-2026 Budget;
          Finance Director Sorensen explained the current proposal is to set a public hearing for the Fiscal Year 2025-2026 MBA
budget totaling $4,198,900, as proposed. The budget maintains a balance of $5,000 for any expenses related to financial reporting
and the remainder is debt service for WonderBlock parking infrastructure. The line-item revenue page of the budget for the MBA
indicates the funds will come from lease payments, interest income, and bond proceeds related to the WonderBlock project.
Administration has proposed to transfer BDO lease revenue funds to the Parking and Mobility Fund, which is shown in the line-
item revenue files as lease payments.

Council/Board/Trustee Business
        The Council and Public Services Director Anderson discussed traffic flow plans for the City during the upcoming Ogden
Marathon event scheduled for the coming weekend.

Council/Board/Trustee Comments
        There were no additional Council/Board/Trustee comments.


         The meeting adjourned at 5:45 p.m.
