Councilor Ellsworth replied to Whipple with questions about owner approval.

Melia Dayley said that she did research on ADU’s from local cities- Provo has 444 ADU’s currently. Orem

David Wright of Provo spoke on neighborhood input on other items as well as this one and is against the

Janessa Smith of Provo is in favor of ADU’s. She shared about a young family living in neighborhood and

Tom Peterson of Vineyard spoke on the need for supportive income during his residency in Provo and
the value of vitality of the neighborhoods with ADU. He spoke on parking concerns. He stated ADU’s can
solve problems for the inflation that has been here for the past two years and will help with

Councilor Whipple spoke on complaints on ADU and current enforcement practices. She shared that if
neighbors do not make complaints, the city does not have opportunity to enforce.

Chair Handley thanked the public for sharing their concerns. He provided some insight from the denial
of the citywide approach from last year and reminded public of his vow to explore other options. He said
he feels the staff and council are listening to citizens. He shared that ADU’s could be a tool for the crisis
of low-income homes in Provo. He stated he is in support of ADU’s.

Councilor Hoban said while an ADU may make homes more affordable by having supplemental income,
it does not change the cost of home, in fact makes it more expensive. He discussed impacts it could have
on the community. He shared his concern of the westside having most a lot of undeveloped land and
stated he is worried about developers only making homes with ADUs. He requested Bill Peperone to
come up and share his opinion.

Bill Peperone, Director of Development Services, spoke about historical price of homes. He compared
the cost of completing a finished home versus the cost of building with an unfinished basement. He
stated he does not see an issue of undeveloped land since the cost to complete a home with an ADU is
more for the builder.

Councilor Hoban then asked Mr. Peperone questions about minimum lot sizes. He asked for
confirmation from Brian Jones that STD5 would be excluded from this ordinance.

Brian Jones confirmed that it is excluded.

Bill Peperone answered questions on lot sizes as well as parking concerns for ADU homes.

Councilor Hoban stated he got his questions answered and ultimately said he feels good about moving
forward with being in support of ADU’s.

Councilor Ellsworth spoke on the time and complexity of this ordinance and thanked the public for their
feedback and concerns. She stated that this proposal lacks simplicity and fairness and feels the biggest
hang-up is the signature process. She asked for the Planning Commission to find a path to adopt that
would better.

      Motion:       Councilor Ellsworth made substitute motion to remove 14.30.030.9 from city code.
                    Chair Handley seconded that motion.

Brian Jones, City Attorney gave his analysis of the purpose of that section of the code. He stated it does
not conflict with the State code at this time, because the way it is written is only if the State requires
internal connections between the ADU and principal part of the dwelling.

Councilor Hoban spoke on the need for an internal connection and stated it gives the homeowner the
opportunity to covert back and forth from ADU to a single-family dwelling. He asked Brian Jones if it
states an internal connection is required in our code now, and if enforcement is possible.

Brian Jones again confirmed that the city cannot enforce that code.

Councilor Ellsworth gave her thoughts on internal connections and how we should not be telling
homeowners how to make changes to their properties.

Councilor Fillmore agrees with Councilor Hoban’s concerns and questions. He said he is opposed to this
motion.

Councilor MacKay asked Scott Johnson, Code Enforcement Supervisor, if this code is being enforced.

Mr. Johnson responded no.

Councilor Ellsworth commented that she agrees with Councilor Fillmore and his thoughts.

Councilor Shipley stated he is in support of the motion.

      Vote:         The substitute motion was approved 6:1 with Councilors Ellsworth, Handley, Hoban,
                    MacKay, Shipley, and Whipple in favor. Councilor Fillmore opposed.

Councilor Fillmore shared his concerns and the complex issues with ADU’s. He commented that Provo is
currently in compliance with state law. He also requested a 6-month progress report on how this
ordinance is doing if passed.

Councilor Shipley spoke on balance of competing interests. He shared that ADU homes and
neighborhoods have given positive feedback. He promised that as a housing committee member, he will
keep an eye out and would recommend adjustments and changes as needed.

Councilor MacKay stated she wanted a majority vote for ADU’s.

Councilor Hoban asked Scott Johnson about permit status, complaints about ADU’s and monitoring of
the ADU change for 12 months.

Mr. Johnson agreed he could provide the reports to council.

Councilor Hoban discussed the issue to codify the process of neighbor anonymity.
