City Manager, Mark Reid reported that in prior years, Horrocks Engineers conducted the C ity's
Impact Fee Facilities Plan ("IFFP"). Thi s year, it was put out to bid and they contracted with Cody
Deeter from EFG to perform the analysis and serve as Lead Consu ltant.

Mr. Deeter reviewed the impact fees and stated that hi s goal was to help the Council make a good
deci sion. He explained that the purpose of an impact fee is defined in the statute and involves a
payment with new development to mitigate the impact on new infrastructure. He stressed the
importance of understanding what the impact wi ll be financially on the City. It is driven by statute
and changes frequently. A methodology is utilized to define and calculate the im pact fee.

They begin with demand and determine what is expected in terms of growth over the next six to
10 years. This timeframe is used fo r engineering purposes and because the Impact Fee Act requires
impact fees to be spent or incumbered within six years.

The second aspect was identified as Level of Service. They determine the current level of service,
how to maintain it, and whether there is excess capacity in the cutTent systems that can fill the
demand . If there is available capacity, the value of the excess capacity should be determined.
Original values must be used, which is a legislative mandate. If the excess capacity does not
sufficiently meet the demand, new faci lities must be considered.

They next look at project fundin g. If debt is involved, interest carrying costs can be included as
part of the impact fee calculation. Mr. Deeter noted that debt is not carried in any of the faci lities
being funded . The impact fee refers to the fee per unit being calculated. Mr. Deeter described
each section.

Demand Analysis involves calculating how much growth will occur in the next 10 years. After
examining the City map they determined which areas are most likely to develop in the next 10
years. They then addressed the number of residential, commercial, and non-residential units. They
were then converted into Equivalent Residential Connections/units (ERC/ERU). It was noted that
non-residential units must be compared/equated to residential units. Mr. Deeter estimated that
there are approximately 5,580 ex isting ERCs. By 2030, based on the map, 8,520 are expected.
The majority are residential with 500 comm ercial. The ERCs must be considered when calculating
the Transportation Impact Fee and the number of new daily trips that will be generated. The
calculation presented wo uld acid nearly 40,000 new daily trips.

Level of Service. Mr. Deeter explained that the level of service for parks includes the amount of
investment in parks and trails per residential unit. Currently, the City spends $10,562 per resident
on parks based on the current level of service. Public Safety numbers are based on the sq uare
footage of building space per ERU , which is currently 4.6 square feet of building space per
residential unit. For commercial, the level of service is based on how much equipment should be
spent on the commercial ERU. Anything in excess of $500,000 can be included in the level of
service for comm ercial. Mr. Deeter clarified that the large rolling stock can only be funded by
commercial or paid tlu-ough a separate mechanism through the General Fund.

In the Water Department, culinary and pressurized water is driven by standards outlined by the
State. For stormwater, the level of service fo r storage is calculated using a 100-year storm event
over a 24-hour period. It must drain full y within 48 hours. The con veyance is based on a 10-year
storm event over a 24-hour period.

In terms of the level of service for Transportation, Service Level B is a level of measurement for
how much capacity is on the road. He pointed out that it is cuITently at a Level of Service A. A
Level of Service D standard is c urren tly used. Mayor Timothy clarified that impact fees cannot
increase the level of service. Mr. Deeter explained that the current level of service can be

Mr. Kartchner explained that Bluffdale C ity has a procurement policy as part of the internal control
of the City's assets, paiiicularly over cash. The policy is broken into tiers with $0 to $500 requiring
approval of the department head only. Purchases of $500 to $5,000 requires a purchase order,
which must include three telephone, email, or internet bids. Pmchases between $5,000 and
$25,000 require written bids from companies that may be providing the services or goods.
Purchases over $25,000 are required to go through the legal solicitation process, most of which
tends to be proj ects that are significantly higher in cost. Mr. Ka1ichner explained that this has been
the price break down for at least 17 years.

The internal controls were set specifica lly to ensure that the City is safeguarding its assets. In
doing this, it could be required that every purchase over $.99 be run tlu·ough an appeal process, but
thi s can eliminate efficiency. It then becomes a balancing act to ensure that staff is getting the
needed services effi ciently, but simultaneously ensuring that controls are put in place that will
prevent abuse. As staff has evaluated groupings, it was brought to their attention that they are a
little bit low compared to other cities and the State. Thi s knowledge caused the evaluation of what
other cities were doing and Bluffdale' s own internal systems to see what would be more practical.

Mr. Kartchner recommended that any purchases of $2,000 or less require supervisor approval.
Any purchases between $2,000 and $8,000 would require a Purchase Order including three
telephone, email, or internet bids. Purchases between $8,000 and $3 0,000 would require written
bids from vendors. Anything in excess of $3 0,000 would require a formal RFP process.

With the Purchase Order, all purchases are approved in advance by the City Manager. Purchases
less than $2,000 are approved by the supervisor. Ultimately, the City Manager also reviews and
approves it as the check run is approved. The City Treasurer also monitors each check to ensure
that they are in conformance.

Mr. Ka1ichner confirmed that the separation of duties is in order and added that there is a Contract
Accountant who does the bank reconciliations to ensure that they are maintaining segregation . As
additional accounting staff is hired, however, certain accounting responsibilities could be brought
in house.

Mr. Reid stated that Mr. Kartchner was asked to make a proposal that would be similar to what
other cities do and what would be reasonable. The request came mostly from Public Works
Director, Shane Paddock since his depaitment buys most of the goods that would end up in a
Purchase Order. Mr. Reid support requiring three bids to be obta ined. Several factors are involved
other than just the dollar amount. Mr. Kaiichner stated that it relies heavily on the depaii ment
heads doing their j obs. That is a regular conversation while cutting checks, when questions are
raised about the purpose of a check. There is foll ow up on purchases to ensure that the finances
are being carefully handl ed.
