        1.2      Mayor’s Report

      Mayor Kristie Overson explained that there had recently been a ribbon-cutting for the new
state liquor store in the city. All new businesses in the city were appreciated and she looked
forward to the tax revenue it would bring to Taylorsville.

She expressed her thanks to members of the council and to Wayne Harper for joining her in
representing the city at the ChamberWest gala which had been held the night before. It was a great
deal of fun and she appreciated Councilmember Armstrong for his role on the Chamber’s Board
of Governors. Four local businesses had been nominated for awards--Penny Ann’s Café, Hidden
Peaks Coffee and Roasting Company, Summit Vista and Perspicacity, LLC.

The Unified Fire Authority’s Board meeting the previous day included a discussion of the current
severe drought conditions—the extremely hot temperatures, fireworks, and restricted areas. She
reminded everyone that there was an interactive map at UnifiedFire.org which showed areas with
fireworks restrictions in place. The city had received inquiries from residents regarding fireworks
restrictions and the city was closely monitoring both the State and Salt Lake County for any
decisions regarding fireworks. In the meantime, she cautioned everyone to be very careful.

Mayor Overson then read an email from a resident who worked as a bus driver for Jordan School
District. She had recently been driving a busload of kids back to school after the end-of-schoolyear
Lagoon Day. The gist of the email was:
        … a student suffering from heat stroke was feeling worse by the minute. I let the
        teachers know that there was a St. Mark’s Emergency Clinic close by. I pulled up
        to the clinic by the ambulance entrance for the student to get care. I then moved
        the bus over by the fire station where there was more shade to let other students off
        the bus so they could cool down. I went over to the fire department. The doors
        were locked (because the firefighters were on a call) but a gentleman opened the
        door and I told him what was going on. Even though I had interrupted a meeting
        of fellow citizens, they told me to let the kids get as much water as they needed and
        to come inside to cool down. I truly appreciate them for opening the doors and
Taylorsville City Council Minutes

        allowing us to interrupt their meeting. Please thank all those people who
        accommodated us.

Mayor Overson’s “take-away” was that this was a very calm and collected resident who knew the
city, including a safe place to take the kids when they needed help. That’s what Taylorsville was
all about—neighbors helping neighbors.

Tomorrow would be the mayor’s last meeting as a board member of the Unified Police Department
Board of Directors. As Taylorsville ended its contract with UPD, it did so on friendly terms and
with mutual respect. As far as the new Taylorsville Police Department, in-house training had
begun the previous Monday for the entire department. She expressed her great respect and
appreciation to Jay Ziolkowski and the rest of the transition team for their efforts.

Regarding the current state of local parks (both county and city-owned), due to the severe drought
and water shortage, everyone would be seeing brown grass. Regarding private property, the code
compliance officers would not be issuing citations for brown or yellow lawns during the drought.
If the grass or weeds were exceedingly high, that was a different issue.

The Centennial Plaza and Mid-Valley Performing Arts Center outside city hall involved carefully
planned landscape designs that would accommodate Movies in the Park events, outdoor concerts,
farmer’s markets, parade watching and open play space. Water-wise landscaping had been chosen
and when the project was complete, there would be less than half the grass than had previously
been on site, with a reduced need for water. The city was being very careful about water usage
and Mayor Overson asked that residents do their part to be very cautious in their water usage and
particularly with any fireworks.

Councilmember Burgess expressed appreciation to the Green Committee who was the group that
let the children into the fire station. They felt it was the right thing to do at the time.

        3.3      Bangerter Environmental Study – Brian Allen

       Brian Allen of Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) explained how excited he was
to be in Taylorsville and commented on the positive atmosphere that was evident in the room. He
was there to give an overview of various UDOT projects in the city.
    • An environmental study was being conducted to convert Bangerter Highway into a full-
        fledged freeway system
    • Part of that conversion would involve the 4700 South interchange in Taylorsville
    • The 2700 West project had not yet been funded
Taylorsville City Council Minutes

The purpose of the 4700 South project was to improve mobility and reduce the severity and number
of crashes. It would improve quality of life, better connect communities, promote a strong
economy, and keep Utah moving.

There was a fast, aggressive schedule for the project. The environmental phase was to be
completed in 2022 with construction in 2023. The Jordan Valley Aqueduct might need to be
relocated and if so, the project would take three years rather than two.

Mr. Allen illustrated the public outreach that would take place, including presentations to the city
council at all major milestones. UDOT was communicating with the public through a variety of
channels and would be holding an open house at Kennedy Jr High so that the project team could
better understand the community’s needs and interests. Eventually a public hearing would be held
as required by law.

Councilmember Harker asked if it was an overpass or an underpass that would be designed for the
intersection. Mr. Allen explained that the presence of the aqueduct meant that it would have to be
an overpass that was built.

        6.1 Public Hearing and Resolution No. 21-21- To receive public comment regarding a
        Resolution of the City of Taylorsville to reallocate approximately $758,694.52 of funding
        from FY2015, 2016, 2017, 2018 and 2019 CDBG Annual Action Plans to Improve the

        Viability of the Carriage Square Shopping Center located at 4166 South 1785 West through
        Updated Signage, Parking Area Design and Resurfacing, and Building Façade
        Improvements – Ken Donarksi

      Ken Donarski explained that tonight was a public hearing to accept comments regarding the
proposed reallocation of CDBG funds from the 2015 through 2019 budget years. The city had a
great opportunity to enter into a cooperative agreement with Carriage Square to undertake a
redevelopment project. The area was originally built in 1977 as a retail and office space
development. The center had served the city well during that time and it now consisted of a variety
of small businesses, many of which were minority-owned. After almost 45 years, the center
needed a face lift and a partnership between the business owners and the city to rebuild the failed
parking lot, replace and widen sidewalks in front of the businesses, enhance the landscaping and
irrigation system, and potentially assist with replacement of the center signage. The business
owners would agree to update, repair, or replace the building facades, remove all the shipping and
storage containers, repair roofs, update building interiors and coordinate individual business
signage. In short, it was a win-win for both the city and the business owners. This project could
save and create jobs and businesses, increase traffic from Taylorsville and its surrounding
communities, and bring in increased sales and property tax revenue.

Councilmember Barbieri asked what Plan B was if the business owners were unable or unwilling
to do their part. Mr. Donarski explained there would be a written agreement in place before the
city put any funds into the project. Councilmember Barbieri felt Carriage Square was a real gem
but was concerned that with so many different business owners involved, there might not be
consensus.

Economic Development Director Wayne Harper agreed that Carriage Square had a great deal of
potential but that there were nearly 44 different owners, many of whom were second or third
generation families. Mr. Donarski had met with them at least ten times, ensuring buy-in from the
entire group. Although there were still discussions to be had, each owner would need to sign the
agreement for the CDBG funds to be used.

Councilmember Barbieri agreed the fact that the development was governed by a property owner’s
association would make things easier. Scott Harrington stated that ideally, work would begin in a
couple of months.

      Chair Cochran opened the public hearing at 7:41 p.m.

Bill Mertlich stated he served on the governing board of the Carriage Square Property Owner’s

other business owners had talked for some time about making improvements and this project was

        7.3      Resolution No. 21-26 – A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville approving an
                 Interlocal Cooperation Agreement with the Salt Lake County Officer Involved
                 Critical Incident Task Force – Jay Ziolkowski

      Mr. Ziolkowski explained that under this agreement, if a TVPD officer were to be involved
in a critical incident, other agencies would handle the investigation to ensure there was a third-
party review and that the incident was not merely investigated internally. Likewise, TVPD would
be available to handle such investigations for other police departments that were parties to the
agreement.

Tracy Cowdell explained this was an excellent model and he had a tremendous amount of
confidence in the task force. He explained that the process outlined in the agreement was part of
ensuring things were done as they should be.
