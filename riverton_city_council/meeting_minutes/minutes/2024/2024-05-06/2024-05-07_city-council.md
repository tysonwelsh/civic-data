Presentations

       Southwest Aqueduct Reach 2 Project Team Presentation

Kevin Rubow, from Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District presented details about the
Southwest Aqueduct project. The project involves installing approximately two miles of 66-inch
diameter welded steel pipeline, running from 13400 South to 11800 South. The new aqueduct
aims to meet the increasing water demands of the communities in the Salt Lake Valley. This
presentation is included in the packet.

Christina Nelson, Bowen Collins Associates, and Brittany Kidd, Horrocks Engineering, provided
examples of typical aqueduct easements, showcasing how the pipeline will be laid down along
3200 West. In some areas, the pipeline will be in the public right-of-way, potentially requiring
minor disturbances to curb, gutter, and sidewalk.

Councilmember McDougal asked if permanent structures affected by the construction would be
restored. Ms. Nelson said that only allowed uses will be replaced such as sod and gardens.

Discussion Items

         Budget Review and Establish Time Line for Budget Process

Mayor Staggs provided an overview of the proposed budget. He highlighted several key points,
including the historical analysis of the general fund over the past decade, which showed an
average annual increase of around 6% or 4% when excluding stormwater. The proposed budget
includes funding for additional personnel, such as police officers, code enforcement, animal
control, and other positions.

Mayor Staggs also addressed the topic of recycling, proposing to eliminate curbside recycling in
favor of regionalized recycling efforts to reduce contamination rates. He emphasized the
importance of maintaining financial prudence and highlighted various projects included in the
budget, such as the culinary water Greenwell and improvements to secondary water systems.

Mayor Staggs clarified that adopting the tentative budget does not represent final approval, as
the Council will finalize the budget by the second meeting in June as required by state law.

Councilmember McDougal provided an update on recent developments from the Trans Jordan
board meeting regarding potential fee increases. He explained that due to higher-than-
anticipated construction costs and inflationary pressures, there may be a need for a mid-year
fee increase. Trans Jordan charges the City a mass rate per ton for waste disposal, with the
City's trucks being weighed and tonnage assigned accordingly. The Council must decide
whether to cover overages through the general fund or pass the cost along to residents on a per
capita basis.

Councilmember McDougal also emphasized the importance of regional recycling efforts, noting
that such initiatives could improve recycling effectiveness by reducing contamination rates.

Councilmember Haymond suggested addressing the potential subsidy of waste management
services sooner rather than later to avoid a situation where a large fee increase becomes
necessary in the future, similar to what happened with sewer fees. He recommended
considering smaller adjustments or regional solutions to address the issue proactively.

Councilmember Pierucci expressed agreement with the Mayor's perspective on budgeting and
fee increases, highlighting his reluctance to raise rates for residents when there are sufficient
funds available in other areas. He views it as a matter of reallocating public funds rather than

Councilmember McCay expressed frustration with the varying levels of commitment to recycling

         Public Comment Discussion

Councilmember McCay said she had some concern about disruptive behavior in Council
meetings, but also a desire to balance the right to free speech with maintaining order and
productivity. She believes that continuing the discussion and exploring ways to address
disruptive behavior while still allowing for public input could be beneficial.

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, addressed the issue of managing disruptions in meetings,
emphasizing the importance of clarity and adherence to existing policies and procedures. He
noted that Utah law provides a procedure for ejecting individuals from meetings for disorderly
behavior, expressing support for lobbying the legislature for improved procedures. He
highlighted the need to focus discussions on topics within the City's jurisdiction and suggested
cutting off individuals who veer off-topic.

Councilmember Pierucci suggested that a time limit could help manage disruptions by providing
a clear endpoint to public comment sessions. He inquired about the feasibility of incorporating
such a policy within existing Utah statutes and policies.

Mr. Carter clarified that the Council operates within the framework of a limited public forum,
allowing regulations on time, place, and manner during public comment sessions. He explained
that time regulations, such as setting a time limit for each speaker and an overall cap for the
public comment session, are permissible as long as they apply uniformly to all speakers. He
proposed reducing the current three-minute time limit to two minutes and implementing a 15-
minute cap for the entire public comment session. He emphasized that this approach would
operate on a first-come, first-served basis, with speakers who do not have the opportunity to
speak in one meeting given priority in the next.

efforts were made to maintain financial stability without imposing additional fees. Notably, the
City had seen no net increases in fees for about a decade, despite inflationary pressures.

and police, resulting in a reduction in property taxes and an increase in service quality. He
highlighted the City's financial health, including a triple-A bond rating, low debt per capita, and
modest increases in the general fund over the past decade.

Public Hearings/Action Items

         Ordinance No. 24-06 – Amendment to Sections 18.215.030(2) and 18.135.300,
         updating standards and requirements related to exterior commercial lighting

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, provided an update on the proposed
changes to the City's ordinance regarding exterior commercial lighting. He explained that the
Planning Commission had recommended several amendments to address deficiencies and
changes in the City. The key changes focused on defining fully shielded fixtures, regulating
lighting oriented towards residential properties, and addressing commercial holiday lighting. The
ordinance specified that exterior lighting on buildings facing residential areas must be turned off
at 11 pm or at the close of business, whichever is earlier. Temporary holiday lighting during
November to January would also require site plan approval and must be turned off by 11 pm or
at the close of business.

Mr. Lethbridge clarified measurement standards for residential properties within 100 feet of
commercial buildings and discussed the proposed holiday lighting regulations, including
designated holidays and time limits. He emphasized that while the ordinance included
recommendations from the Planning Commission, additional changes could be made based on
Council input.

In response to clarifying questions by Councilmember McCay, Mr. Lethbridge noted that most of
the current lighting in the City already meets the proposed standards, which are based on
industry norms. Input was gathered from cities with similar standards and ongoing projects to
ensure practicality and accessibility of compliant lighting options.

Councilmember McDougal suggested adjusting the timing requirement to 10 o'clock, aligning it
with existing noise ordinances and other lighting regulations. Councilmember Pierucci was not
supportive of this change.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments;

Heather Bennett explained that she was supportive of aligning the timing of exterior lighting with
noise regulations, specifically for the side of buildings facing residential properties. The intent is
to address concerns related to noise and ensure a peaceful environment for residents, without
impacting the operations of businesses.

Mayor Staggs called for additional public comments; there being none, he closed the Public
Hearing.

Councilmember McDougal MOVED that the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 24-06 –
Amendment to Sections 18.215.030(2) and 18.135.300, updating standards and
requirements related to exterior commercial lighting with modification in Exhibit A,
Section 3 to be 10:00 PM. Councilmember Haymond SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs
called for discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as
follows: Haymond-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes, and Pierucci-no. The motion passed with a
3 to 1 vote.
