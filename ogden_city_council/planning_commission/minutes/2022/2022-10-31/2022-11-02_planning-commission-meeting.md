5. Request for Honorary Street Name, for Miles Goodyear to be added to A Avenue and Pomona to be
   added to B Avenue.
  Mr. Brad Timothy stated he has been involved with the Mountain Man Rendezvous for a number of
  years, which has held its annual event at the Fort Buenaventura. This group desires to keep survival
  and other skills of early trappers in the area as well as to educate fourth grade students. It has come to
  their attention there has been little recognition of Ogden’s original fur trader, Miles Goodyear and are
  wanting his memory to be recognized. While there is now a Miles Goodyear Park, until recently, there
  has been no acknowledgement to recognize Miles Goodyear. This proposal is to request an honorary
  street name of Miles Goodyear for A Avenue as well as Panoma for B Avenue, recognizing his wife and
  representing native Americans. He stated while Staff feels an additional sign on 24th Street creates a
  cluttered appearance due to there already being three signs, he felt visibility from 24th Street is
  important.
  Mr. Brierley stated when considering an honorary street name, the Commission is to determine there is
  a public purpose, whether it satisfies the General Plan and whether it is consistent with the street
  patterns and whether there are impacts on existing streets. This petition is to add honorary names of
  Miles Goodyear to A Avenue and Panoma to B Avenue. He reviewed some history relating to Miles
  Goodyear, stating he was the first European settler in the Ogden area, and there is little recognition
  given to him. This petition would identify A Avenue as Miles Goodyear and add Panoma to B Avenue in
  recognition of his native American wife. The honorary names would extend from 24th Street south to
  their terminus. He stated the General Plan identifies human history as part of its community identity
  objectives, stating items unique to Ogden City including fur trapping should be celebrated. He also
  reviewed the West Ogden Community Plan, stating it directs observance of the rich history of the area.
  He stated as the proposal is for an honorary name, the existing names of the streets of A Avenue and
  B Avenue would be retained, and there should be no confusion or conflicts in providing emergency
  service to the area. He noted there is an existing street identified as Goodyear Avenue within a
  subdivision on Harrisville Road, but indicated the intersection at 24th Street already has three signs,
  identifying 24th Street, and an honorary name of Martin Luther King Jr. Street indicating an additional
  sign may appear cluttered. Staff recommends approval of the honorary street names as proposed with
  the condition a sign not be installed at 24th Street as an additional sign may create confusion and
  appear cluttered, noting the recommendation of the Planning Commission will be forwarded to the City

6. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance and Map Amendment, to implement Make Ogden Downtown
   Plan.
  Mr. Brierley stated the City adopted the Make Ogden Downtown Plan in 2020. The effort of this plan is
  to create downtown as a destination, improve the streetscape and walkability and build a thriving
  downtown while acknowledging its history and existing buildings. The intent is to increase density and
  provide housing options by better utilization of land. This in turn increases the value of the downtown,
  creates a more walkable downtown and creates jobs. He stated there needs to be both private and
  public investment in the downtown in order to create a more financial future of the area. He stated the
  Make Ogden Downtown Plan contains design standards identifying uses and density, and this effort is
  to incorporate those into the Zoning Code. He introduced the consultants from Design Workshop who
  have been working on implementing the Plan into the code.
  Ms. Jessica Garrow stated the Make Ogden Plan had been adopted after an extensive public process
  as a means to both protect the existing character of the downtown and to make it a more desirable
  place to live, work and play. The proposed language is to provide clear direction and standards
  relating to land use, density and design. Much of the focus is to provide a pedestrian-friendly
  environment by identifying and protecting the public realm. She also reviewed the public process in the
  implementation of the Plan into the Zoning Ordinance, stating they had met with stakeholders and
  focus groups as the process progressed. She stated the focus groups had identified building height
  and streetscape improvements as a concern, indicating the view corridors are important to retain. She
  stated as they reevaluated land uses, creating more options for entertainment and eliminating many
  auto-related uses. She identified the downtown project area with the existing zoning map and
  identified proposed new zone districts, which would be Commercial-Entertainment, Commercial—Mixed
  Use and Commercial-9-Rails as well as a specific zone designation for Historic 25th Street. Residential
  areas surrounding the downtown would be identified as Residential Multi-Family Vertical and
  Residential Multi-Family Horizontal.

Ms. Marianne Stuck discussed the seven elements identified in the Make Ogden Plan as Land Use,
Public Realm, Parking, Building Height, Building Articulation, Setbacks and Service Areas. She
discussed the process of working with the existing code and the implementation of the Make Ogden
Plan and have received input from both the Planning Commission and stakeholders of he downtown.
She discussed changes added since the work session of the Commission last month relating to vertical
design to include providing view and shade studies and added standards for mural colors.
Ms. Garrow displayed a new list of land uses, stating the intent is to make the Code more clear and
identified potential uses for each of the new zones, with no maximum density for residential uses and
identified the maximum building height for each zone, indicating a height transition area would exist

25th Street is required to be commercial on the ground floor, while upper floors could be residential and
as buildings increase in height, their floor area would be reduced. Ms. Stuck discussed building
setbacks and articulation stating it is preferable to build to property line with ground floor entries facing
the street. The code identifies building materials, transparency and massing with earthtone colors,
allowing murals only on one side of a building, with accent colors or murals limited to exceed 10%.
She identified the three zones identified in the public realm being a transition zone, sidewalk zone and
an amenity zone. A minimum of a 6’ walkway would be required as well as maintaining the tree
canopy. Ms. Garrow discussed parking standards, stating one space is required for residential uses,
but in other areas parking options may be limited. She also identified service area requirements,
stating utility companies should be involved in the design process. She then discussed the review
process, stating the current ordinance contains few tools. The proposed code identifies standards and
many item will then be able to be reviewed by Staff rather than the Commission.

7. Request for Encroachment Permit, to allow balconies over the right-of-way for 2242 Washington
   Boulevard.
  Mr. Thayne Fischer indicated he is requesting balconies to be installed as part of a renovation of an
  existing building which will house retail on the ground floor with residential units above. He stated
  balconies which overlook onto Washington Boulevard are desired for the upper level for which will
  create value to the units. He has already obtained an encroachment permit from UDOT and is now
  seeking approval from Ogden City, stating he had previously understood only UDOT approval was
  necessary. He expressed concern the recommendation of Staff limits the size of the balconies and
  makes them unusable for potential residents, stating they need to be adequate in size to be functional
  and the size and design as proposed had been approved by UDOT.
  Mr. Simson stated while the Planning Commission had previously reviewed this site, the balconies had
  not been identified at that time and were not approved. UDOT has since approved a 6’ encroachment
  into their right-of-way for these balconies subject to Ogden City’s approval. While the City supports the
  proposed use as residential units on the upper level, there is concern about pedestrian safety as the
  building extends to the property line and the encroachment would be above the walkway. The City
  Engineer has expressed concern about liability of the use of the sidewalk, stating it should be a safe
  place for pedestrians. Their concern is that items from the balconies could fall onto pedestrians and
  cause injury, noting the applicant has provided the required liability insurance. He indicated while there
  are examples of upper-level balconies within the CBD, these typically extend over the private property
  rather than the right-of-way. Staff recommends approval of the encroachment subject to the size of the

8. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment, Motel Conversion Ordinance.
  Mr. Brierley stated there has been some interest in converting existing motels into affordable housing
  units, and while the City is in favor of multiple housing, there is concern that these remain safe and
  serve a housing need without creating a concentration of poverty or crime. Staff is suggesting the
  ordinance identify this as an option so long as there is a range of variety in unit sizes, and while studio
  units would be allowed, some units should be one and two-bedroom as studio units are typically only
  for temporary residency. Standards also are proposed to identify minimum kitchen facilities, including
  adequate electrical power for both a full range and refrigerator as well as a full kitchen sink. Existing
  standards for multi-family units in commercial zones also are applicable, including the requirement for
  amenities, main entry facing a public street, building materials as well as existing parking standards.
  Staff is recommending 15% of a project be one bedroom and 10% be two bedroom, and if more than
  sixteen units exist, an on-site manager would be required. He reviewed other details of the proposed
  amendment, and recommended it be approved and forwarded to the City Council for adoption.
  Commissioner Southwick expressed concern about the need for on-site management, asking if the
  same were required of new multi-family buildings in commercial zones. Commissioner Schade asked
  if the number of residents per unit would be limited and the family definition applied for these the same
  as throughout the City. Commissioner Safsten stated the studies in the report seem to recommend on-
  site management and felt it is appropriate. Mr. Brierley also clarified while there have been some
  conversations relating to motel conversions, the pending ordinance became effective and no
  applications have been made and there are no properties with vested rights that would not be covered
  by this amendment. He also responded to a question about lighting, stating while motels are typically
  well lit, there are current standards relating to containing light within a property, and vehicle lights being
  screened from both access and parking areas. He stated additional language could be added to clarify
  the need for a lighting plan to be approved as part of the site plan process. Chair Sandau expressed
  favor of on-site management while Commissioner Southwick felt it should not be a requirement as it
  would not support affordable housing.
  Mr. Thor Diach asked about the scope of the ordinance. Chair Sandau responded it would allow
  existing hotels or motels to be converted into long-term housing, but would not require any particular
  structure to do so. The language would be effective for both single-story and multi-story hotels or
  motels. Concern was expressed it may create a concentration of low-income housing in a particular
  area, but the variety of unit size would provide an opportunity for tenants in varying circumstances to
  have housing.
As there were no additional public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to close the
public hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Southwick and passed unanimously.
        MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to recommend approval of the
                  ordinance language as proposed based on the findings it is consistent with the
                  General Plan and purposes of the Zoning Ordinance as well as State and Federal laws
                  relating to affordable housing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Shale and
                  passed 7-1 with Commissioners Aaberg, Blaisdell, Safsten, Schade, Shale, Shinoda
