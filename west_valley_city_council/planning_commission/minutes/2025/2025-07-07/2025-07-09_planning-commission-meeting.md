Z-5-2025: Petition by VALOR PROPERTIES LLC requesting a zone change from LI (Light
Industrial) to M (Manufacturing). The property is located at 2342 South 5600 West and is 0.6
acres in size. Staff - Brock Anderson at 801-963-3361 or brock.anderson@wvc-ut.gov.

Valor Properties LLC has submitted a zone change on two parcels totaling 0.6 acres at 2342 South 5600
West from LI (Light Industrial) to M (Manufacturing). The property is located within the 5600 West
Overlay Zone. The subject property is designated as Light Manufacturing in the West Valley City
General Plan.

The property, consisting of two parcels, is surrounded by LI zoning. Neighboring property to the north
and west have historically had auto wrecking and salvage-type uses but are mostly vacant currently. The
property to the south is now owned by Maverik and will be developed for a convenience store and fuel
center. Across the street to the east are automobile and truck sales uses. The property includes two
connected buildings that total roughly 2,600 sq. ft.; both built around 1990. The front building along
5600 West has an all-brick exterior, and includes some office and shop space, and the structure behind
that is a metal shop building. The property currently has an active business license for a general
contractor’s office.
The City’s Commercial and Manufacturing Zones use table is included with this report for reference so
the Commission can see the difference between uses allowed in both the LI and M zones.

Development Proposal

The applicant recently acquired this property. Valor Properties LLC operates a mechanic shop and
construction business, and the building is currently being used for their construction office only. The
applicant tried to get approval for automobile service, but that use as a primary use is not allowed in the
LI Zone. The LI Zone only permits automobile service if it is incidental to another permitted or
conditional use, such as auto sales. The applicant would also like to use the back of the property as an
outside storage yard for both the construction business and the automobile service use. However, outside
storage is prohibited in the LI Zone. Given the LI zone limitations on automobile service and outside
storage, the applicant is requesting the M zone.
The applicant is proposing some improvements to the property including new concrete and asphalt,
updating the building’s exterior with paint and a new roof and gutters, as well as new landscaping. The
property has frontage in an area where the 5600 West Streetscape is required, so in terms of landscaping,
staff mentioned to the applicant that if the Planning Commission recommends approval for the rezone,
staff will recommend including in the development agreement that the 5600 West Streetscape be
installed along the frontage of both parcels. The
applicant was agreeable to that.

If this rezone is approved, a conditional use permit will be required for the outside storage area. For
screening of outside storage along 5600 West and other high-image arterial streets, the ordinance
requires screening by means of a building or a 6’ tall, precast masonry wall meeting certain
specifications. In the applicant’s proposal they’ve indicated vinyl fencing for screening, but staff
recommend that if the rezone is approved, that the storage area be screened with a masonry wall for the
area that has visibility along 5600 West. The storage area should also be paved.

Area History

A brief history of the surrounding area between 5800 West and 5600 West and from 2200 South to 2400
South (about 46 acres), which has been referred to as the salvage yards, is helpful background
information.
    • Early 1960’s: Development began within the area with some unpaved roads and salvage yards.
    • 1980: By the time of the City’s incorporation in 1980, the area was occupied mostly by salvage
        yard businesses. The zoning in the area at the time was M-2, which was a zone for heavy
        industrial uses. The City also had a M-1 zone in 1980 for light industrial uses.
    • 1984: The City’s first General Plan designated the area as light industrial.
    • 1986: The City consolidated the M-1 and M-2 zones into the M zone.
    • 1999: The City’s General Plan included the goal of relocating or replacing the salvage yards from
        this gateway location.
    • 2001: The City created the 5600 W Gateway Redevelopment Area to provide incentives for
        redevelopment within the area.
    • 2004: The City formed a special improvement district (SID) to install utilities and road
        improvements in the area.

The two properties that were rezoned from LI to M previously both included development agreements
that required substantial changes to the existing buildings on the properties. The building exteriors in
both examples were drastically altered to make the buildings look like new structures. These building
exterior changes were provided to make the buildings more compatible with the requirements outlined in
the LI Zone and commercial design standards for new construction, rather than allowing for plainer or
metal warehouse buildings that are allowed in the M Zone.

The rezone request for the 2256 S 5700 West property was due to the property owner, at the time,
wanting to have truck service as a primary use and a small area for outside storage. The

proposed automobile service was not for the typical small car repair or auto body shop, but for larger
truck repair and service. Also, the outside storage area was not for a very substantial portion of the
property.

The rezone application at 2272 South 5600 West was also related to automobile service and outside
storage, however both uses were similarly not for the typical auto service or outside storage businesses.
Wasatch Fleet and Apex Wraps are the businesses operating on the property. They do vehicle upfitting,
so while it is technically automobile service, it is not the traditional small auto repair business. Also, the
outside storage yard they were requesting was not for construction materials or typical equipment
storage. They were requesting to store new truck beds, upfitting equipment and upfitting parts.

Although both instances included requests for automobile service and outside storage uses, their actual
business operations are not for what would be considered traditional small auto repair uses or outside
storage yards. There are a couple of questions that the Planning Commission may want to evaluate when
considering this rezone request. First, is the proposed use of the property something other than what has
historically been done in the surrounding area? The area was rezoned to the LI Zone to spur
redevelopment. Second, does the proposal help raise the bar for the area and include changes that will
elevate the property along this gateway into the City?

ZT-3-2025: Petition by EMANUEL SHAOULIAN requesting an amendment to Section 7-6-301 of
the zoning ordinance and the addition of Section 7-7-117.5 to the zoning ordinance to allow
medical and dental office in the M (Manufacturing) zone on properties that are partially or
entirely within 500 feet of a residential use or residential zone. Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-
3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov.

Emanuel Shaoulian is proposing an ordinance amendment to Section 7-6-301 of the zoning ordinance
and the addition of Section 7-7-117.5 to the zoning ordinance to allow medical and dental office uses in
the M (Manufacturing) zone on properties that are partially or entirely within 500 feet of a residential
use or residential zone.

The applicant owns several office buildings along Decker Lake Drive between 2770 South and 3100
South. These buildings are experiencing significant vacancies due to changes in the office market. The
applicant is proposing this change to provide more tenant options for his office buildings. Included with
this report is supporting information from the applicant. For reference, the office market data from
CBRE shows an even greater office vacancy rate for the West Valley/Lake Park submarket than what
the applicant is suggesting.

Prior to the comprehensive update to the Zoning Ordinance that occurred in March of 2017, medical and
dental offices were allowed in the M zone and commercial zones. At the time, staff recommended that
medical and dental offices be prohibited in the M zone for at least two reasons. First, commercial zones
tend to be located next to residential zones where medical and dental office patrons come from while
manufacturing zones tend to be located further from residential zones. Second, at the time, staff was
concerned about the State forcing a permanent homeless shelter in an industrial location within the City,
which would have significantly impacted our police and fire calls for service. While not at an industrial
location, the State-owned temporary shelter at 3381 South Redwood Road has certainly confirmed the
concern regarding increased call volume. While medical and dental offices aren’t homeless shelters,


                       Minutes of WVC Planning Commission Public Hearing, July 9, 2025
                                               Page 13 of 15
shelters do at times have nearby medical services tailored to transient populations. Hence, the City was
trying to make an industrial location less desirable for a homeless shelter in that support services could
not be located nearby.

The applicant’s proposed requirement that medical and dental offices in the M zone be located within
500 feet of a residential use or zone addresses staff’s first reason to prohibit this use in the M zone
because medical and dental office patrons would be close to new medical and dental offices within the
M zone. On the second reason, the City is still concerned about having a permanent homeless shelter
forced on the City. However, in staff’s view, the proposed ordinance change is unlikely to increase the
chances of having a permanent homeless shelter in the City because there are so many other factors in
play besides the proximity of supporting medical services.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. The ordinance change should be approved.
   2. Continuance. This application should be continued for reasons determined during the public
      hearing.
   3. Denial.
