4.     PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND RECOMMENDATION on a City
       Initiated Text Amendment Application to Amend Title 8 of the Bluffdale City Code
       (Water), Title 9 (Building, and Title 11 (Land Use) Pertaining to Water Efficiency and
       Residential Landscaping Requirements. (Application 2021-25) - Staff Presenters,
       Jennifer Robison and Dan Tracer.

Dan Tracer reported the staff report and explained that Bluffdale City receives all of its drinking
water from the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District ("NWCD"), which has a contract with
the City of Bluffdale. The contract is renewed every tlu·ee years, with a negotiated water rate. The
scarcity of water has led the JVWCD to stretch its water supply to the extent possible. Accordingly,
all of its member agencies have been asked to establi sh and enact new water efficiency standards.
Once the City does that, the NWCD will be willing to increase the amount of water delivery to the
City in a newly negotiated contract for the next tlu·ee years. The consequences of failure to make
the changes will result in the City being charged a much higher water rate. The additional cost will
be passed on to the residents . The plan to address the needed changes includes revised landscaping
requirements for new development and redevelopment in where 50% or more of the landscaping is
being redeveloped. It will not affect existing homes or businesses.

Mr. Tracer next reviewed the process to be implemented to notify people of the proposed changes.
The bonding process would continue as it does currently. Developers will need to verify
compliance with the landscaping requirements to have their bonding money returned to them.

In response to Commissioner Loumis ' question regarding the type of water to which these standards
would apply, Mr. Tracer stated that it would apply to any landscaping, regardless of the water
source, whether culinary or secondary water. It would include water from a private well. In
addition, the requirements would apply to new construction and landscaping that was revised by
50% or more.

Commissioner Walston understood and supported the proposed changes in light of the cmTent
drought. However, he noted that applying the requirements only to new or significantly modified
landscaping represents a very small percentage of the water being used. He asked if any other
measures were being taken to encourage water conservation City-wide. Mr. Tracer explained that
the proposed plans were created by JVWCD. They felt that if the proposed standards were applied
to new construction, they would be able to meet their obligations for water delivery. As for the City
of Bluffdale, Mr. Tracer reported that the City is also taking measures to conserve water, as

evidenced by parks that are not as green as they might normally be. Senior City Planner, Je1mifer
Robison added that the City is pursuing measures to conserve water. Mr. Tracer state that non-
functioning landscaping areas are receiving less water so that functional areas, such as soccer and
baseball fields, can be kept green.

Mr. Tracer next reviewed the City Ordinances that have been revised. He stated that NWCD is
requesting changes to the front and side yards only and not back yards. For residential properties,
the turf will cover no more than 35% of the area. The remaining area will include "water-wise
landscaping." For commercial properties, the turf will be 20% or less, which is close to what is
already taking place.

In areas that are not turf, the NWCD has provided a list of resources on https://localscapes.com/ for
landscaping that uses minimal water and works well in the Utah climate. Turf would not be
allowed on slopes because it will be difficult to water. Turf would also be eliminated in park strips
and in landscaped areas that are narrower than eight feet wide. The intent was to minimize the use
of turf in less functional areas. Drip and bubbler systems were requested to ensure the proper
amount of water. Mulch and additional ground cover were also priorities to help keep the soil
moist.

Mrs. Robison reviewed the text amendments that would need to be modified to reflect the changes
in the different chapters of the City Code. The bonding language was modified to be consistent
throughout City Code. The definitions were updated to reflect the proposed changes. The
landscaping bond language was modified to remove the specific amount to be charged for the bond.
The practices were to remain essentially the same.

               appearance.
