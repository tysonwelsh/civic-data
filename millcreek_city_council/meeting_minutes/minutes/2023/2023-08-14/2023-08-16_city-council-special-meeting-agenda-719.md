1. Discussion           of Code         Update            Process      and Sensitive           Land       Areas
Francis      Lilly     gave a project              update      on the R-I,        R-2, and R-4 Zones.                    He said the R-1, R-2, and R-
4 Zones      were enroute            to the city council,               with     the R-4 being           a "legacy"             district    with    tri-plexes
and four-plexes.             The Residential               Multifamily           (RM)    Zone would               change        to the Residential            Mix
Zone (RMZ).            The current               RM zone is on corridors                identified        as mixed          use areas. The new RMZ
does retain          many     existing           standards       of the RM        including         building         materials,        massing
requirements,          height       limits,        lot and setback           requirements,           and uses, and the amenities                       were
updated      and revised           with         additional       criteria.    The revised        site criteria           included          buildings     being
required      to be parallel            to the street, entrances               along     street facades             being       oriented      to a hierarchy,
and required          ground       floor         living    space for new multifamily                   projects.          There      were spatial        limits
tables and graphics,               design          graphics,      and minimum            criteria      for amenities              added. Comi'nissioner
LaMar      appreciated           the illustrations             with    definitions       on the design               standards.

Lilly    then described           the proposed              Sensitive       Lands         Overlay        (SLO)        Zone.      The sensitive          lands map

assessment         responsibilities           to the contracted                 city    geologist,       include        language       to reduce        the risk to
the city and the eventual                 owner       of the building/lot,                and include          a dispute          resolution        process.
FCOZ        was designed           to address         sensitive         land issues all over Salt Lake County                              on the hillsides
and canyons,            but it was not found                elsewhere           in the city,      and it needed to be. The proposed
ordinance        was very technical.                Lilly      said existing            lots greater         than 30% could            apply        for a

present      on the site. It would             now include              coverage          of additional          hazards         such as alluvial             fans,

to monitor        improvements             being      built      as per the building                pen'nit     and/or      the land use approval.                    A
