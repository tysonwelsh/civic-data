    2.1. Discuss Fiscal Year 2024 proposed budget amendments – Kyle Maurer, Finance
         Director
Finance Director Kyle Maurer presented the proposed budget amendments. He stated there
had been a few challenges with the 2024 budgets and some assumptions had changed since
the original budget had passed the year before. He went over some broad categories of what
these amendments covered, and stated one of the larger changes was due to a State auditor
directive restricting their development related revenue to only development related
expenditures. He thought the easiest way to do this was to move it to its own fund so it would
no longer be in the General Fund. He continued to say they had discovered that some of the
revenue projections for 2024 had been a little too aggressive.

Finance Director Maurer stated they had discussed at length direct subsidies to public safety
funds that were not seen on the budget itself and so those would be eliminated, and technical
corrections would be made regarding changing the way of how they categorized those within
the fun on the overall financial statements within the city as well as reducing expenses to
match anticipated revenue.

Finance Director Maurer offered a summary of the General Fund showing overall changes
that were being proposed. The biggest one was moving $6.5 million of licenses and permits
July 12, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                              Page 3 of 25


out of the General Fund. He stated there was a proposal to reduce sales tax a little bit due to
some concern that sales tax growth might be slowing.

Finance Director Maurer offered highlights on the revenue changes and stated they had
property taxes over-budgeted by about $331,000, and that number was easy to project
because the State told them what to expect for property taxes. Councilmember Shields asked
why it was off, to which Finance Director Maurer replied the previous finance director had
made a projection about what growth would be but the growth did not match his projections.

Finance Director Maurer stated energy tax was one of the few revenues they were expecting
to increase because emery rates had been increased by the companies and that development
related revenues would be moved to a separate fund. He noted they had over-budgeted that
number and had reduced it from $6.5 Million to $3.7 Million in the new fund and the use of
fund balance which was in excess of the three-month reserve by $881,000 would go to fund
Capital Projects.

Finance Director Maurer highlighted the major expenditure changes. He commented that
every category had reductions and pointed out that Council had approved advanced vehicle
purchases. He mentioned the transfer from General Fund to Capital Projects had been over
budgeted.

Finance Director Maurer stated they were proposing personnel reductions and they had new
positions approved in the 2024 budget that they were recommending not funding at this time
which included street technicians, an IT technician, an arborist assistant and staff engineer.
There were some currently vacant positions they were proposing to eliminate which were
the Economic Development Manager, accountant 1, GIS, and Planning Engineering interns.

based off of the December 2023 PCI which would be the benchmark used going forward to
keep HR consistent with inflation rates. Councilmember Shields asked if not hiring these
employees was strictly due to the budget or based on restructuring of departments. Finance
Director Maurer replied it was due to the budget.

    2.2. Discussion on potential changes to City leash laws – Nathan Cherpeski, City
         Manager
City Manager Cherpeski stated currently the City had a very standard leash law and Council
had opted to keep the ordinance as it was presented in the parks, but this was in a different
section. He commented there had been a meeting where an ordinance from Park City had
been presented stating the owner was responsible for their dog, but he was considering what
would be the safest, which would be using a leash because it was the safest and it was easy
to enforce. He suggested that the City did not want to be in the business of certifying people
that they were in control because then they inherited responsibility.

Councilmember Shields stated they could have an issue either way and someone was going
to sue someone else at any time if they did not like the result of something.

Councilmember Ohrn stated her only reason for continuing to push this was that they created
laws that punished the responsible people because there were a few bad apples and she
thought there was a standard for what reasonable control was. City Attorney Sheeran stated
it was defined in the ordinance of what control meant and people thought they were in control
within the ordinance when they were not because everyone would perceive what ‘control’
July 12, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                             Page 10 of 25


was differently. Councilmember Ohrn relayed her frustration of ordinances that encouraged
people to break the law and she thought this put their animal control people in a bad situation
because they were asking them to turn their head at the people breaking the ordinance.

Councilmember Henderson disagreed and stated the ordinances were there to help the City
resolve problems and he looked more towards the legal and enforceable elements and the
ordinance was there if there was a problem and offered them a way to resolve issues. He
stated if the ordinance was too ambiguous it did not do anything to solve it and dragged it out
further than it needed to be and ordinances were there to educate people.

Councilmember Ohrn disagreed and thought the ordinance was there for a purpose, there
were not suggestions, they were laws. Councilmember Henderson stated to him that was
the purpose. Councilmember Ohrn stated the ordinances were the laws of the city and if you
violated them, there were consequences. Councilmember Shields thought the bottom line
was that language had come back as such because they had had people make an appeal to
allow them an accommodation to have their pets off-leash on the trails and he thought it was
reasonable to allow for this language if people had adequate control of their dogs and if they
did not have control of their dogs and something happened, then they were liable.

City Manager Cherpeski stated the code as presented did not change Title 7 where they were
not allowed to be off leash in a park or at events and if they wanted to allow this, they would
really need to look at their definition of ‘at large’. Councilmember Henderson stated he would
agree with whatever helped protect the city the most. City Attorney Sheeran stated liability
would be minimal unless they implemented permitting and from an enforcement aspect the
language would make it difficult to prosecute because of the ambiguity of the language.

Councilmember Shields stated the argument got complex when you start discussing the size
or the breed of the dog and stated he did not care, if a person’s dog bit somebody they had
to deal with that. Attorney Sheeran stated he agreed but prosecution would be harder with
this language. Councilmember Ohrn asked how many of these cases they had a year. Chief
Carr stated he thought his guys did more education than enforcement and most of the cases
were nuisances or dogs being at large without a person in the equation.

City Manager Cherpeski stated they needed to consider people who were just naturally afraid
of dogs and their interpretation of a dog encounter. He thought the ‘at large’ definition
needed to be reexamined but if they were okay with it, they could bring it back for a work
meeting. Councilmember Ohrn was concerned about the definition of ‘control’. City
Manager Cherpeski stated they would refine this and bring it back for discussion.
