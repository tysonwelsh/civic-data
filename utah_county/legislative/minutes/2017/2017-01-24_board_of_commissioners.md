2.     ADOPT A RESOLUTION CONCERNING THE BUS RAPID TRANSIT PROJECT AND
       ASSOCIATED AGREEMENTS
(Track B, 00:01)
Provo resident Diane Christensen expressed her concerns that the Utah Transit Authority (UTA) is not honoring
lease agreements, and overall behaving unethically in regards to changes in the Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) project.
She referred to a letter dated July 6, 2016 addressed to the Federal Transit Administration as proof that revisions are
occurring and the original agreements are not being honored. The letter was from the cities of Orem and Provo, as
well as the Mountainland Association of Governments, the Utah Department of Transportation, and the Utah Transit
Authority. Ms. Christensen summarized the letter as showing bad faith actions; if the Utah County Commissioners
feel they must move forward with BRT based on previous contracts, the breach of those contracts should nullify the
project. Ms. Christensen provided copies of the letter and her comments to the Board (a copy of which can be found
attached to these minutes).

(Track B, 07:10)
Provo resident Lisa Clark stated the public should be included in Utah Transit Authority meetings as directed by the
Open and Public Meetings Act, since tax dollars are involved in the Bus Rapid Transit project. She questioned the
validity of UTA meeting behind closed doors for most of 2016.

(Track B, 08:20)
Orem resident Hans Anderson talked about a petition which had circulated the previous year, acquiring the
thousands of signatures needed to place the BRT issue on the Provo and Orem City ballots. Mr. Anderson quoted
excerpts from the Orem City resolution and mentioned access to businesses changing along University Parkway –
that was not supposed to occur as part of the agreement. He stated the commissioners are ignoring the will of the
citizens and ‘working in the dark’ without the knowledge of the public.

(Track B, 13:05)
Commissioner Lee noted Steve Meyer, Capital Projects Director for the Utah Transit Authority, was invited to the
meeting today and unable to attend. Commissioner Ivie read for the record a statement from Mr. Meyer, as Mr.
Meyer had requested via e-mail (a copy of which can be found attached to these minutes). Mr. Meyer’s letter
addressed the Executive Committee for the Provo Orem Transportation Improvement Project (TRIP), UTA public
meetings, and suggested revisions for the Board’s proposed resolution.

(Track B, 18:30)
Commissioner Lee confirmed he is a member of the Executive Committee, and had raised concerns within that
committee regarding the openness of the meetings – in his opinion, anyone can speak at open public meetings.
Commissioner Lee mentioned a vote during the January 19, 2017 Executive Committee meeting in which he voted

1.     PRESENTATION BY SOLTIS INVESTMENT ADVISORS
(Track B, 40:36)
Brent Moore, Tyler Finlinson, and Bill Wallas of Soltis Investment Advisors came forward to present on the
services their company provides, giving printed documents to the commissioners (a copy of which can be found
attached to these minutes). Mr. Moore provided some background and explained they are not an insurance office,
but instead serve as advisors for retirement plans. Mr. Finlinson added comments about potential upcoming federal
regulations, and how Soltis would work directly with the county’s current provider – Prudential – to protect the
privacy of the employees and provide the most efficient and accurate analysis. Mr. Wallace noted the importance of
making the needs of the employees a priority.

Commissioner Graves commented on the county Request for Proposals (RFP) process, asking why the Board would
move forward with an audit by Soltis only to have them cease and desist if Soltis wants to participate in the RFP.
Tyler Finlinson replied Soltis would not participate in an RFP; instead, they would assist the county in navigating
that entire process, as a third party familiar with the inner workings of businesses such as JP Morgan, Fidelity, and
Prudential. They discussed upcoming county Investments Committee meetings with Utah County Human
Resources Assistant Director Mark Brady. The group also discussed the Employee Retirement Income Security Act
(ERISA), common audit findings, and the variety of options for employees – those closer to retirement age should
focus on stability, for example, while others can make growth a priority. Commissioner Lee mentioned the county
retirement fund is nearly one-hundred million dollars, a large amount by any standard but especially for government.

Mark Brady noted NFP currently serves as the investment advisor for the county. The group addressed fee
structures with Utah County Deputy Attorney Robert J. Moore, and volunteered to submit the audit results to NFP.
Tyler Finlinson pointed out the audit would take two or three weeks, and Commissioner Graves asked the county
Investments Committee to seriously consider this option.
