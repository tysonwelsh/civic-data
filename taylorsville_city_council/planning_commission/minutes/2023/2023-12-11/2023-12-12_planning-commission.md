                                   Zoning Text Amendment


 6.       Public Hearing and Recommendation to the City Council for a Text
          Amendment Affecting Several Sections of the Taylorsville Municipal Code
          to Align City Standards With Utah Senate Bill 174, Related to the
          Subdivision Review Processes ( Jim Spung, AICP / Senior Planner)



      Mr. Spung explained that the State legislature had made changes to the Land
Development Code when they met earlier in the year which mandated that certain
changes be made to the processes city' s use in the subdivision review/ approval process.
Additionally, the legislature had set a deadline of February 1, 2024 for city codes to align
with the new laws as outlined in SB174. He pointed out that the new law pertained only
to residential development— not commercial or multi- family development.


One change was that a public notice and public hearing were no longer required for a
preliminary subdivision approval, although the Planning Commission would still be asked
to review and approve the preliminary plat in a public meeting. The reason legislators
chose to remove those requirements was that if a subdivision application met city
standards, the city was required to approve it. Therefore, it made no sense to encourage
public comment on something about which the city had no discretion. Mr. Spung went on
to explain several other changes that were being proposed in light of the recent legislative
changes.



Commissioner Wendel pointed out that it appeared there was a movement to revoke
almost all power previously reserved for planning commissions throughout Utah.

As far as public hearings were concerned, Chair Quigley was of the opinion that allowing
the public to express themselves directly to elected officials was important. He felt there
was value in the work of planning commissions as well.

Commissioner Wright felt public hearings were important and said it was unfortunate that
they seemed to be devalued by the legislature. These changes were clearly a benefit to
developers.


Mr. Spung stated that state law would still allow a single public hearing to be held with
respect to a plat, so the City could still require a public hearing in such a case if the
Commission felt it would be helpful, but only one.

Commissioner Wright reminded the rest of the Commission about a previous application
involving development along 4700 South across from Macey' s. It wasn' t until the second
public hearing that the commission realized that the applicant had not done everything
necessary. So to only have one public hearing could cause some problems.

The new law gave more authority to the planning department director, although he/ she
would retain the option to refer a matter to the Planning Commission if it was unusually
complex or controversial.



Commissioner Wendel           asked what constituted an "   unusually complex" application. Mr.
Spung    responded       that the   actual   language in the draft
                                                               went into more detail. He

mentioned a flag lot off of 6235 South that he felt was more complex than many other
more typical applications. A property within a sensitive area overlay such as a flood plain
would also be considered more complex. He offered to review the new language to ensure
it was sufficiently clear.

Chair Quigley mentioned that it left a great deal up to the director as far as determining
what was and was not complex enough to warrant involvement of the Planning
Commission.


Commissioner Wilkey pointed out that the public expected the commissioners to send
applications through due process. The new law circumvented that ability which was not
what their friends and neighbors expected.


Chair Quigley responded that the Commission was not meant to represent their friends
and neighbors. They were a citizen board, but it was not necessarily appropriate for them
to poll the residents before making decisions.

Commissioner Wilkey indicated that while she didn' t want to poll her neighbors, she felt
the Commission should work in their best interests.


Commissioner Wright agreed that the commissioners were not present in order to simply
rubber-stamp" applications but were expected to weigh- in on what would best benefit the
community.


Mr. Spung stated it sounded like the primary concern was for flag lot reviews, which he
agreed      were   unique.   He indicated those could be removed from this process and
wondered if that would alleviate the Commission' s concerns.


Commissioner Wright said he would also like to see it limited to creating no more than
two lots.

Commissioner Wendel said she preferred that the Planning Commission give the Director
authority rather than having the director decide what the Planning Commission would or
would not have the opportunity to weigh in on.

Commissioner Wilkey preferred that regarding the reference to unusually complex
applications, that they " shall" be referred to the Planning Commission rather than just

 may" be referred to them.

Commissioner Wendel pointed out that our government is built upon a system of checks
and balances, and she did not like to see the Commission' s role being minimized since
there were very good reasons for its existence. Chair Quigley agreed.

To recap, Mr. Spung said he would clarify that flag lots weren' t eligible for the
administrative plat process, it would be limited to 2- lot subdivisions, and he would include
some clarifying language regarding the director's referral authority to the Planning
Commission.


Commissioner Russell wished to clarify whether SB174 had already been approved by
the legislatureor if it was expected to be addressed in the next session. Mr. Spung

responded that it was already adopted law.

Regarding subdivision plats, Mr. Spung explained that the City must publish a
comprehensive list of submittal requirements and fees. It also now had only 15 calendar
days in which to review an application once it was deemed complete.


Developers must address in writing every single comment made by the City during a
review, which would be very helpful to the city.

Mr.   Spung then discussed various timelines and review cycles with the Planning
Commission.


Commissioner Wendel wondered how the Planning Commission could be effective in
providing feedback based on the limitation on reviews. She hoped the City Council would
support the role of the Planning Commission and the important work it did.

Chair  Quigley opened the public hearing at 7: 32 p. m. However, no comments were
offered, so he closed the public hearing before calling for a motion.

Commissioner         Wendel        indicated   she   was   not   comfortable   sending   a   positive

recommendation         to the Council, although she understood that the Commission was
required to follow state statutes.        Commissioners Russell and Wilkey felt similarly.

Mr. Spung asked if they would be comfortable making a positive recommendation if the
director were removed as the decision- making body for administrative plats, or if the
administrative draft procedure was eliminated entirely. Plus, there would be the addition
of the preliminary review including a public hearing.
