Attorney Strachan shared the background for this application. The proposal is for minor 
changes to the MPD (Master Plan Development) and Subdivision approval processes

(Chapters 11-4-5 and 11-4-12 of the Code), as related to wastewater systems in Summit 
County. A Work Session was held at the last meeting and the Planning Commission seemed 
generally fine with the proposed changes. Staff recommends holding a public hearing and 
moving forward with a positive recommendation for the proposed changes. 
The minor changes include some minor changes to the MPD process and adding a signature 
block for the Eastern Summit County Wastewater Conservancy Special Service District. 
This Special Service District oversees wastewater in all of Eastern Summit County except 
the municipalities. Another change is to remove the requirement of a positive 
recommendation from this district and change to a recommendation from either the Health 
Department or the Special Service District, depending on the type of approved wastewater 
systems. There are some Code changes going directly to County Council, which include 
wastewater systems of three lots or fewer to be reviewed only by the Health Department, 
with the larger community systems being reviewed by the Special Service District. 
Planner Milliner stated another change is with some conflicting language in Section 11-4-
5 with the number of lots for the MPD process to be triggered. The number of lots that will 
trigger the MPD process is now four, so that will be changed from ten to four. 
Chair Clyde suggested some minor changes to allow some discretion for the Health 
Department review to not be mandatory. Another section has wording that municipal 
systems may be determined to be “reasonably available.” The language is open to 
interpretation and allows for lawsuits. Jurisdictional problems may prevent connecting to a 
municipal system. Attorney Strachan stated the “reasonably available” language is directly 
from the Health Code and she will bring that up with the County Council.

4. Public Hearing and possible action regarding potential amendments to the Eastern 
Summit County Development Code, Section 11-7-16, Appeal procedures. – Ray 
Milliner, Principal Planner  
 
Planner Milliner shared the background for this discussion. The Attorney’s Office 
proposed amendments to the appeals process, to clarify the process for someone who 
wants to appeal an Eastern Summit Planning Commission decision. The process or review 
of appeals will not change. A few months ago, there was an appeal in Snyderville Basin that 
came in at the last minute, with an unsigned application and no fee paid. This incident was 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
July 18, 2019 
Page 4 of 17 
 
reviewed by a judge who stated the Code does not state the requirements for a signature 
and paid fee at the time of application or a certain time of day the application must be 
turned in by. This Code amendment is an attempt to clarify those issues of required time of 
submittal, what is required in the appeal, and procedures of processing the appeal. Staff 
recommends a review of the Code amendment, conduct a public hearing, and forward a 
positive recommendation to the County Council. 
Chair Clyde asked for clarification of the nature of the appeal. Attorney Strachan stated 
that it reverts to State Law that requires appeals be heard de novo, unless the Ordinance 
specifies otherwise. 
Chair Clyde suggested that the appeal time include a provision for holidays. Attorney 
Strachan stated she will ask about including clarifying language consistent with the rules 
of procedure. 
The public hearing was opened, no public comment was made. The public hearing was closed. 
 
Findings of Fact  
1. In 2017 it was determined by the County Council that a more formal process for appeals of 
land use decisions would be beneficial.  
2. In 2017 an appeal policy was adopted by the Council.  
3. The proposed changes are consistent with the policies and procedures that were adopted in 
2017 and likewise, those policies are now being required under the code changes.  
4. The proposed amendments to the Eastern Summit County Development Code clarify the 
process for appeals in the Code and brings it into conformance with current County practices and 
what has been the practice of Courts of Law. The proposed Code language does not remove any 
existing restrictions that would unduly affect nearby property owners.  
Conclusions of Law:  
1. The amendment is consistent with the goals, objectives, and policies of the Eastern Summit 
County General Plan.  
2. The amendment will promote the public health, safety and welfare better than the existing 
regulations for which the amendment is intended to change.

Findings of Fact:  
1. The applicant submitted a Conditional Use Permit for an accessory building greater than 2,000 
square feet in size at property located at 720 E Chalk Creek Road.  
2. The subject property is currently zoned Agriculture - 5 (AG-5).  
3. Accessory buildings exceeding 2,000 square feet in size are subject to a Conditional Use 
Permit in the AG-5 zone district.  
4. Surrounding land uses are primarily agriculture and single-family homes.  
5. Staff finds no conflict with the adjacent agricultural operations.  
6. The proposed use does not require any changes to the existing infrastructure and does not 
propose to install either water or sewage facilities at this time.  
7. There are no visually sensitive areas or wetlands/floodplains in the area.  
8. There are no additional roads proposed as part of this permit.  
9. The accessory building had not caused adverse impacts to existing infrastructure.  
10. The applicant is the landowner of the lot in question.  
11. The size of the proposed structure is compatible with similar structures that have been 
approved in this zoning district.  
12. Plans for the proposed structure indicate that the structure will meet appropriate setback 
requirements.  
Conclusions of Law:  
1. The application complies with applicable requirements in Chapter 11-2 of the Development 
Code.  
2. The application complies with the Conditional Use Permit criteria in Section 11-4-7 of the 
Development Code.  
3. The application complies with the Eastern Summit County General Plan.

2. Discussion regarding the Echo Wind Divide Conditional Use Permit, 100 MW 
utility-scale wind energy generation facility that would interconnect with the Rocky 
Mountain Power transmission system approximately 7 miles southeast of Evanston, 
Wyoming. – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner shared the background for this discussion. The Echo Divide Wind 
Generation Facility is a proposal for up to 39 windmills. The windmills are approximately 
500 feet tall and are used to generate electricity. The applicant proposes to place these 
windmills on an approximately 5800-acre location about seven miles west of Evanston, 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
July 18, 2019 
Page 12 of 17 
 
Wyoming on the Utah Wyoming border. The applicant is looking for direction from the 
Planning Commission. The output from the facility is about 100 megawatts, which would 
provide power for just under 22,000 homes through Rocky Mountain Power. Access to the 
site would be through the Wahsatch exit and Yellow Creek Road, with no additional 
accesses proposed. There would be significant grading and redoing of roads to the site in 
order to accommodate construction equipment to build the windmills and facility. Once the 
facility and windmills are built, some of the roads will be reduced in size and revegetated. 
Site specific issues include steep slopes, wetlands and streams. There is a stream that runs 
through the site, but it is not a year-round stream. There are no proposals to put windmills 
in the stream or wetlands sites or on steep slopes. 
The applicant does not have a specific number of turbines planned, but the number ranges 
from thirty to thirty-nine. The applicant is asking the Planning Commission to approve a 
general site location to determine the number and location of the turbines. Staff would like 
the Planning Commission to determine if this site is appropriate for the use. The turbines 
are really big and there is no way to screen them. 
The applicant stated that the disturbance would be larger than those in Spanish Fork 
Canyon. Equipment tends to get larger over time to be more efficient. These would be 
similar to the wind turbines east of Evanston. The applicant is still in the process of 
evaluating alternatives to size. There is a constant competition to evaluate all available 
equipment for the latest and best product in the cheapest fashion.

potential layouts buffered by 200 meters and grouped together. The actual disturbance
