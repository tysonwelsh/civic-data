4.     Public Hearing – A Petition to the Planning Commission to Forward a
       Recommendation of Approval to the South Salt Lake City Council for All or Only
       Portions of the Ordinance Amending Multiple Sections of Title 17 to Correct Technical
       Errors, to Amend Certain General Development and Design Standards, and to Amend
       Certain Subdivision and Platting Standards.

Action Item

Applicant:     South Salt Lake, Woodbury Corporation, Bobby and Jenna Fenn, and Andrus Builds

Ms. Ungricht reported that the above matter pertains to amendments to Title 17 of the South Salt
Lake City Municipal Code. The intention was to correct technical errors, amend certain general
development and design standards, and amend certain subdivision and platting standards. There
were three applications associated with the proposed update, which included South Salt Lake. The
last update to the Code was in September 2022. Since then, a list of errors and typos had been
collected. Woodbury Corporation also applied for an Ordinance amendment as had Bobby and
Jenna Fenn and Andrus Builds. All of the applicant petitions were included in the one update. That
was the reason the Planning Commission was being asked to forward a positive recommendation for
all or portions of the amendments. The update would make the Code more user-friendly, remove
artificial barriers, and correct typos. Staff was looking for direction and a recommendation to the
City Council for the amendments.

The proposed amendments to Title 17.06 – General Development Standards related to typos in the
section. It was also proposed to allow tandem parking in enclosed garages in the Townhome
Overlay. That change would allow buildings to wrap around a corner. It would also provide more
flexibility, however, both stalls would need to be enclosed in a garage. Ms. Ungricht noted that
another amendment was to clarify the frontage requirement tied to the number of drive approaches
allowed. At the moment, that was not very clear and the language did not meet the intent. It was
also proposed that discretion be added for the drive approach width and location. The City Engineer
will have discretion based on site requirements and studies performed by a licensed Professional
Engineer.

Ms. Ungricht reported that the next amendment was applied for by Bobby and Jenna Fenn as well
as Andrus Builds. The proposal was to remove the requirement for single-family dwellings to have
at least one of the two required stalls located within an enclosed garage. The request was to remove
that requirement and simply require that there be two parking stalls anywhere on the property. This
may lead to more vehicles being parked outside and may increase the number of vehicles parked on
the street. On the other hand, it will also enhance the livability in the R-1 Zone. It will allow people
to expand their homes into the attached garage and create more living space. Staff realized that there
were changes that could be made to make the R-1 Zone more livable in general. That was on the
list to look into further in the future. Staff was open to direction.

Another proposal was to amend the formatting of the parking stall requirement table. Ms. Ungricht
reported that it was a bit confusing with respect to the way it is currently and the change in formatting
will add clarity. Another proposal was to change the amount of bicycle parking for restaurants to
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Meeting                                                   10
June 15, 2023


increase the amount of bicycle parking from 1 per 25 stalls to 1 per 15 stalls. This would make it
possible to reduce the reliance on personal vehicles. There was also a proposal to clarify that outdoor
seating for restaurants did not count toward the total seat count for parking requirements. Chair
Pechmann asked about patios that are enclosed during the winter. Community Development
Director, Jonathan Weidenhamer reported that based on the technical read of the Code, that would
not count toward the total seat count for parking requirements. Additional discussions could be had

Ms. Ungricht noted that another recommended amendment was to reduce the parking stall depth and
drive aisle from 20 feet to 18 feet and from 26 feet to 24 feet. That was the standard across the

a Site Plan. Commissioner Bellina asked how large vehicles will be addressed if the parking stall

additional 2.5 feet. Other jurisdictions rely on the extra feet when there is curb and gutter. It would
not necessarily assist with the internal parking unless there is some type of landscaping but the edges
would all theoretically provide 20 feet of parking in total.

Another proposal was to modify the drive-thru lane stacking requirements. Ms. Ungricht explained
that the City currently requires five stacking stalls behind the order box but for some restaurants that

Engineer could require additional stalls. This would ensure that there are appropriately stacked
drive-thrus throughout the City. She noted that there is also a proposal to amend the parking
requirements to allow up to 20% parking reductions in certain districts. It was clarified that this is
more of a reformatting than a rewrite as there are currently 20% parking reductions allowed in the
TOD-Core, Downtown, and East Streetcar Districts. This would add more requirements that need
to be met and there would be some reformatting as well for clarity.

It was proposed that there be clarification about deliveries versus loading areas. Deliveries are
packages and third-party food services where loading areas accommodate new appliances or
someone moving into a property. Ms. Ungricht noted that an error had been found in the Code.
Currently, overlays for parking lots are not required to have a Building Permit, but this was not the
case. Other proposed amendments relate to the landscaping section. Article III would be created.
This would make the Code more user-friendly and easier to find landscaping standards. As part of
that, landscape conservation incentive program standards would be added. The City is not currently
eligible for the incentive program but residents have reached out about that. Residents who join the
program are eligible for rebates. The standards would be added so interested residents would be
eligible for the rebate program. She reviewed the necessary standards as follows:

   •   Limit the amount of turf grass in the front and side yard areas to 35% for any new single-
       family residential development;
   •   Limit the amount of turf grass in the front and side yard area to 20% for any new townhome,
       multi-family residential, commercial, or flex developments; and
   •   Prohibit turf grass in park strips and areas less than eight feet in width in new development.

Chair Pechmann asked if the proposed language addressed remodels versus new construction.
Ms. Ungricht denied this. It would state that a single-family dwelling unit is required to have two
off-street parking stalls. This would apply to new development as well. For a parking stall, it
would just need to be on a paved surface. That was the only requirement. Mr. Weidenhamer
explained that generally, the size had been 9’ x18’. He pointed out that there are other
requirements as well. For example, it could not infringe on a neighboring property and some
setbacks need to be followed. The amendments to Title 17 would give more discretion to the City
Engineer. He explained that there was a desire to add more clarity to the code language so there
was more consistency for applicants. He acknowledged that there were a series of livability issues
within the R-1 Zone. Mr. Weidenhamer stressed the importance of livability.

need to have two parking stalls that meet other design standards. Anyone who wants to could
