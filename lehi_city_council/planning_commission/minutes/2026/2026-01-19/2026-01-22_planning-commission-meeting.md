3.1) Public hearing and consideration of James Whitmore’s request for conditional use
approval of the SAJ Lehi RV Storage located at approximately 4300 North Digital Drive

Katie Bussell presented the item. The property is zoned for light industrial and does not have
utilities. Setbacks meet code. There are 12 redline comments, which mainly focus on technical
updates to the site.

James Whitmore, the applicant, was present. He asked if he would need a special permit for the
eight-foot concrete fence he plans to install. Bussell explained that eight-foot fences are typically
required for the zone the property is in, and it is part of the submitted site plan, so no additional
steps are needed. Whitmore explained that he hopes the construction will improve the land and
clean up the appearance of the property.

Commissioner Eyre opened and closed public comment.

Commissioner Jackson shared that he feels this has been through the Commission enough times
that no further discussion is needed.

                Motion: Commissioner Jackson moved to approve the item with the findings that
                the proposed plans meet the code requirements of a 20-foot setback and a
                masonry fence, and that the plans are in order after several rounds through the
                Commission. He included all DRC comments. Commissioner Su’a-Filo seconded.

                Vote: Commissioner Eyre, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes. Commissioner
                Jackson, yes. Commissioner Gehman, yes. Commissioner Su’a-Filo, yes.

                Motion passed unanimously.

3.2) Public hearing and recommendation of JDH Development’s request for an exception
for a required trail connection to a master planned trail and to meet the block length; the
exception is requested due to grade on Vivian Estates Phase 2B located at approximately
2600 North and 600 East.

Katie Bussell presented the item. The plan has been previously approved by City Council with an
included trail connection. The applicant has requested that it be removed, due to the steep
grading on the site.

Austin Cooper was present, representing JDH Development. He shared some pictures that
highlighted the steepness of the grade. He explained that the installation of 600 East impacted the
build site and made the trail connection significantly more difficult to include. He explained that
the grade in places may go over the maximum allowed 8%. He also shared concerns about the
trail connecting perpendicularly with a busy street.

Commissioner Su’a-Filo asked if Lehi City installed 600 E. City staff explained that Lehi City
installed the road, and that the developer was asked to put a hold on construction until it was
completed. Rather than the planned straight route, the road cut into the planned lots, and caused
the changes in slope where the trail was planned. Kim Struthers explained that the city worked

with the developers, and that it was a long process that went through many iterations. He
suggested stairs as an option that may work with the steep slope.

Commissioner Su’a-Filo asked how the trail system would be accessed without the included
connection. Cooper explained that people would have to exit the development and go around it to
access the trail.

Commissioner Eyre asked if the primary concern was the engineering challenge. Cooper
affirmed that it was. He explained that in the initial concept, the trail was asphalt. Stairs would
have to be made of concrete and would be more expensive to build and maintain.

Commissioner Kunze asked if there was any other connections provided in lieu of the trail, as it
is a connectivity requirement. Cooper explained that there is not anything provided in lieu. He
also explained that he feels the city has taken quite a bit from the developer with how the
installation of 600 E worked out. Creating the trail will cut into each adjacent lot and render
about 100 square feet of each lot unusable. JDH is already unsure about the ability to build
homes on certain lots and may have to combine lots to make them functional.

Commissioner Jackson asked if the concern was the financial impact, or the impact on the lots.
Cooper explained that from an engineering standpoint, JDH can’t make the trail make sense.
Installing it in a better space will render different lots unusable and would result in needing to
shift utilities. Commissioner Jackson admitted that Lehi is responsible for the issues with
installation, but that he was also uncomfortable with ignoring connectivity standards. JDH has
met with the city and has discussed possibilities of reimbursement from the city.

Commissioners Jackson and Su’a-Filo asked city staff their opinions on the images depicting the
slope. Staff confirmed that the pictures are accurate to the slope on site.

Commissioner Eyre shared his positive experiences living next to the trail system. He is very
defensive of connectivity standards, as his neighborhood has bad connectivity and it severely
limits how much use the trails get. He thinks that removing the access will hurt the neighborhood
and the community. He likes the idea of a staircase.

Commissioner Jackson reemphasized that this is a result of the city’s actions in installing 600 E.

Commissioner Gehman agreed that stairs are the best solution. She argued that stairs would
make the trail less accessible than if it were sloped. Commissioner Eyre argued that stairs would
be more accessible than not having any access at all. City code does not require trails to be ADA
compatible.

The Commission consulted Gary Ellis. Ellis explained that initially a 40-foot wall was planned
along 600 E but has since been lowered and/or eliminated. He explained that HadCo was
compensated for the loss of land that resulted from the 600 E installation. He mentioned that
other trails in the area will be up to 6%, so 8% is not an absurd ask. The city recommends
installing the trail at a diagonal, in order to keep it mostly flat. Cooper explained that putting the
trail in that location would cause lots and utilities to shift. Ellis also recommended some
improvements be made to separate the trail from the street. Commissioner Jackson asked what

would be more expensive for the city, footing the bill for shifting utilities or for stair installation
and maintenance. Ellis didn’t have an exact answer but assumed the costs would be similar.

Commissioner Jackson asked what the developer would prefer if Lehi City is footing the bill.
JDH would prefer installing stairs, as it would not delay construction on lots that would
otherwise need utilities moved. Cooper isn’t sure what the cost would be, as he hasn’t run the
numbers.

Commissioner Eyre opened public comment.

There was one online comment from Montaigne Hamilton. She thinks the connectivity standards
are not needed in this situation, as there is nothing to connect to, but she worries that allowing an
exception will set a precedent.

Commissioner Eyre closed public comment.

Commissioner Jackson is in favor of installing stairs and retaining walls to maintain the
buildability of adjacent lots. Commissioner Su’a-Filo argued that stairs would create undue
maintenance responsibilities for the city, and money down the drain. He doesn’t like the idea of
adding an ongoing cost to the city.

Commissioner Gehman shared that she would like trails to be accessible, even if its not required
by code. She also understood that it would create issues for the city and/or the developer if this
standard is upheld. She shared that she would almost prefer not to have a connection in that case.

Gary Ellis explained that, given the grade, normal outdoor stairs would be about what is required
for the site anyway. Wide landings would be needed, which would make the stairs marginally
more accessible.

Commissioner Eyre mentioned that the property has been devalued, but that the city has already
remediated the cost. He sees this as a connectivity standards issue.

Commissioner Jackson felt the city should be the entity to put connectivity in, as it is the fault of
the city that the issues have been created. He views it as part of the social contract between the
city and its residents.

Commissioner Eyre mentioned other properties where the Commission required pedestrian
access, even in cases where road connections were excepted.

                Motion: Commissioner Jackson moved to give a negative recommendation to the
                Lehi City Council with the findings that the proposed trail is a part of the
                connectivity standards that the city does not want to compromise on, and that it
                can be achieved in a safe manner by installing stairs on the 8% grade. He
                recognizes that Lehi City created the situation and has a responsibility to the
                citizens to meet the promised standards. He included all DRC comments.
                Commissioner Su’a-Filo seconded.


                Vote: Commissioner Eyre, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes. Commissioner
                Jackson, yes. Commissioner Gehman, yes. Commissioner Su’a-Filo, yes.

                Motion passed unanimously.
