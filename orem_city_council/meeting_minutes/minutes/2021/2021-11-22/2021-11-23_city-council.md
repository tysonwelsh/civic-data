        UPDATE – UDOT Geneva Road and 1600 North Project

Geoff Dupaix, Andrew Gwynn, and Eric Rasband from UDOT presented five projects they are
working on in Orem City. Those projects include the following:

   •    Widening Geneva road from 1800 south to University Parkway to five lanes. Mr.
        Macdonald asked why they decided to just expand to 1800 south, and not all the way to
        2000 south. Mr. Gwynn responded it is because they expect 2000 south to become less
        used as Lakeview Parkway is developed, hence a widening wouldn’t be needed. He
        stated construction will take place during the summer of 2023 and said the current
        “project value” is around 20 million dollars.

        UPDATE – Parks Master Plan

Mr. Bybee introduced Lisa Benson from Landmark Design and stated the city has worked with
her many times in the past. He also expressed that citizen input is a large part of this Parks
Master Plan update.

Ms. Benson started the presentation by showing a community survey polling adequate park, trail,
and recreation availability. She stated some of the main take-aways from this survey are that
citizens would like to see existing parks upgraded, as well as improving and adding more
walking and biking trails. She then introduced a graph showing Orem’s under 20 population is
shrinking, and senior age groups are increasing in size. Ms. Benson stated Orem currently has
299.02 acres listed for parks. She then said citizens are also concerned with park “walkability,”
(Are parks within walking distance from their neighborhood?) Ms. Benson subsequently
presented a map showing gaps in park walkability, and suggested where parks could be added to
cater to more citizens.

Ms. Benson expressed that citizens are happy with the 3.05 Park Acres per 1,000 People stat. She
then went on to say the city would need about 37.3 extra park acres by 2050 to keep up with
demand, and went over other options the City could do to meet future needs. Ms. Benson then
showed services that citizens expressed they would like to see more of. These included more dog
parks, pickleball courts, splashpads, and mountain bike trails. She then expressed how arts &
cultural events/facilities are an important part of the master plan and suggested the council
continue to support the growth of them. Ms. Benson then presented a map showing all current
and planned bike trails and lanes. She went into detail on these and explained how the city could
be more bikeable. She continued by presenting a table showing “amenities to meet population
standards”, which shows that Orem is doing well, but should look into adding more basketball,
pickleball, and volleyball courts. She continued by going through the proposed costs of these
upgrades and additions, which totals an estimated $67,322,500.

General discussion ensued regarding park and trail location, as well as costs and capital
replacement needs.

Ms. Benson finished by presenting an action plan outlining the next steps the city can take to
meet park needs and goals.


       DISCUSSION – Orem High Residential Parking District Amendment

Mr. Paraskeva stated the City has received a citizen petition requesting an expansion to the Orem
High Residential Parking District. He requested that the council consider expanding the current
residential parking district, adding one street and a cul-de-sac immediately south of the current
district.

General discussion ensued regarding parking enforcement and how parking complaints are
received. Mr. Paraskeva stated they issue 2-3 citations per day.


       DISCUSSION – Utah Fit Premise Act

Mr. Downs introduced the Utah Fit Premises Act to the council. The Fit Premise Act sets a
standard for livable conditions that Landlords must meet for their property to be rentable. Mr.
Downs then showed examples of properties that do not meet the Fit Premise Act, such as
dangerous mold growing in an apartment complex in Orem, and stated this act would enforce the
basic upkeep of rental property.

General Discussion ensued regarding the reporting, enforcement, and the fixing of these issues.

       DISCUSSION – Statewide Opioid Litigation Settlement

Mrs. Schriever stated the State of Utah has agreed to enter into a national opioid litigation
settlement. The State of Utah has been in negotiation with three distributors and one
manufacturer since July on the terms of the settlement. She stated the State is eligible for
between 151 million and 270 million to address the opioid epidemic. Mrs. Schriever then stated
that if more political subdivisions, such as counties and cities join this settlement, the state will
receive more money. She then proposed that the City of Orem consider a resolution authorizing
the city to start engaging in these settlement discussions. Mrs. Schriever also expressed that the
dollars gained from this settlement will probably not make their way down to Orem, and will
more likely be distributed between the State and County governments.

The Council expressed they were all in agreement that the City should move forward with the
Resolution of participation.
