Emissions Program Update — Bear River Health Department — Lloyd Berentzen

stated there is a change in the direction of the emissions program and is asking for
direction from the Council. Currently, vehicles are tested using either the two-speed idle
program (TSI) for vehicles 1996 or older and the OBD-II test for vehicles 1997 or newer.
Data on the handout given Council members indicates under 10% of county vehicles
require the TSI in Cache County driving under 2% of the total miles traveled in the
county and by 2025 those vehicles will only be 4% of all county vehicles and will be
under ‘2% of the total miles traveled. The EPA will do away with any credit for TSI by
2025. Berentzen is considering three options:

1. Require all vendors to invest in new equipment (because the old equipment is
outdated) that will cost from $10,000 to $15,000 for the TS! program serving less
than 10% of the vehicles and less than 2% of miles traveled.

2. Have the Health Department invest in the new equipment and administer the TSI
program to take care of that smaller portion of the vehicle population.

3. Knowing the EPA is giving less and less credit for TSI programs and almost
everything relies on OBD-II testing, amend the county statute and the Board of
Health's ordinance in order to only require OBD-II testing on vehicles 1997 or newer;
discontinuing the requirement for TSI testing on vehicles 1996 or older.

Josh Greer, BRHD emissions specialist, said he has been in contact with the
Department of Air Quality for a long time to get the data on the handout given Council
members so he is quite certain of its accuracy.

After some discussion the Council favored Option 3. Berentzen will bring a document to
a future Council meeting with the proposed language for changes to the statute and
ordinance.

¢ Design Exception — Private Road Access West of Smithfield City — Director
Runhaar explained this would be a private road that will come from Highway 218 and

run 200 feet south to provide access to about fourteen homes in three separate
subdivisions. The request is far in excess of what is allowed. The Manual does not
permit dead-end roads longer than 500 feet — this one is about 2,400 feet in length.
The other subdivisions have access from Highway 218 or 800 West. Director Runhaar
recommends denial.

Andy Rasmussen, property owner, remarked that Birch Creek creates a challenge in the area
regarding house orientation and 800 West is a main corridor road for the future and nobody
wants to front on to a busy road. This design exception will work well for the subdivisions
involved. The subdivisions will be a tax benefit to the county with no cost for the road.

Brad Shumann, adjacent property owner of previous Tom Pitcher split subdivision, did not favor
800 West as the access road because the access road would come within 50 feet of his house
and supports the design exception for a private road. The private road would work well for the
three subdivisions and not adversely affect his property's value.

with private roads in the county and said this request is against what the county is
working to do.

A request has been made by Joshua Cowley for a Design Exception on county road 2600 North to

The Cache County Manual of Roadway Design and Construction Standards (Road Manual) section
1.8 Authority and Design Exceptions, specifies that:
“Full justification and documentation must be provided explaining the reasoning as to why the
roadway standards cannot be met, why an alternative design or construction method can meet the
intent of the roadway standards, and including any other relevant information.”

The June West Cowley Lot Split Subdivision 3“ Amendment has been approved by the Planning
Commission with the condition that the proponent obtains a design exception from the Road Manual
for the surface material type and structural fill of 2600 North (Exhibit B).

The submitted request for Design Exception provides the following reasoning:
1. The cost to the proponent to meet the standards is not justified.
2. Minimal impact to the roadways will result from the amendment.

The Development Services staff and Director have completed a review of the Design Exception
request and have identified the following:
1. The Road Manual specifies the following:

a. §2.1-A-3 Local Road, Table 2.2 Roadway Typical Sections: Roads with approximately
40 to 1500 Average Daily Traffic (ADT). This includes roadways that have the capacity
for moderate to low speeds and moderate volumes. This category provides a balance
between through traffic movements and direct access. These facilities move both regional
and local rural traffic with emphasis on local movements.

b. Table 2.2 Roadway Typical Sections: Local roads must meet the minimum standards of a
66-foot-wide right-of-way, two 10-foot-wide paved travel lanes with 2-foot-wide
shoulders (1-foot-wide gravel and 1-foot-wide paved) for a total width of 24 feet.

c. §2.4-A-1-c: Development on inadequate roadways is not allowed, and any substandard
sections of roadway access must be improved to meet the minimum standards specified
in the Road Manual.

d. Table A-8 Typical Cross Section Structural Values: The minimum structural composition
for gravel roads requires 14” depth of granular borrow, 6” depth of road base, and paved
roads required an additional 2.5” depth of asphalt.

e. §2.4-A-4-b: The review of requests for development on existing roadways must occur
through the Design Exception process.

f. §1.8 Authority and Design Exception: Consideration and evaluation of a design exception
to the Road Manual standards requires full justification and documentation explaining the
reasoning as to why the roadway standards cannot be met, why an alternative design or
construction method can meet the intent of the roadway standards, and including any
other relevant information.

RESOLUTION 2018 - 17

A RESOLUTION APPROVING THE FILING OF CROSS-APPEALS TO 2018
APPEALS FILED BY TAXPAYERS SUBJECT TO CENTRAL ASSESSMENT

IT IS HEREBY RESOLVED BY THE CACHE COUNTY COUNCIL THAT:

WHEREAS, Utah Code Ann. § 59-2-1007 allows a county to object to an assessment and
request a hearing with the Utah State Tax Commission (“cross-appeal”) within thirty days from
when an owner of a centrally assessed property objects and requests a hearing (“appeal”);

WHEREAS, the following owners with centrally assessed property in Cache County have
filed an appeal with the Utah State Tax Commission contesting their 2017 assessment: 1.
CenturyLink.

WHEREAS, it is in the best interest of the County that it file a cross-appeal(s) so that the
County can fully participate in the proceedings initiated by the owner(s), including subsequent
appeals to the District Court or Utah Supreme Court arising from such proceedings, with the
purpose to protect the County’s financial interests and the equality of the tax burdens of
taxpayers within its jurisdiction.

NOW, THEREFORE, the Cache County Council resolves as follows:

1. That cross-appeals are authorized to be filed by legal counsel and pursued in
response to the appeals initiated by the following owners: 1. CenturyLink.

2. That the Cache County Council directs the prosecution of the cross-appeal(s) under
the advice of legal counsel.

3. That in the event an appeal by an owner not identified above is subsequently
discovered, legal counsel is authorized to file a cross-appeal using his or her discretion to ensure
timeliness, but such cross-appeal must be subsequently brought before the Commission as soon
as possible for ratification.

4. This Resolution shall be effective immediately upon its adoption.

Adopted by the County Council of Cache County, Utah, this 31st day of July, 2018
