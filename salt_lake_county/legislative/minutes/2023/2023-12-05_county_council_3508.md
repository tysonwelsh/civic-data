with administering each of the various fees, as defined in the fee schedule.  In 
summary, the 2024 proposed fee schedule for unincorporated Salt Lake 
County provides reasonable fees that are fair and comparable to other 
jurisdictions.  In most cases, the fees remained the same, but some fees may

Lake County, Utah, Amending Section 2.08.110 of the Salt

“Discrimination Prohibited,” Amending Section 13.04.020 of

County Code Of Ordinances, 2001, Entitled “Multiple 
Occupancy Changing Facilities Designated By Sex,” In Order 
to Designate and Enforce the Sex -Specific Use of Certain 
Changing Facilities at Salt Lake County Recreational 
Facilities, and to Also Provide Sex -Neutral Facilities for All 
Patrons of Salt Lake County Recreational Facilities
23-1140
Attachments: Staff Report
Parks and Recreational Facilities Amendments Ordinance 
Final Draft 11.27.23 (002).RAFL
Presenter: Councilwoman Dea Theodore. Second by Councilmember Sheldon 
Stewart & Councilmember David Alvord. (Approx. 2:55PM, 1 hour.)
Discussion/Direction
Council Member Theodore  reviewed the ordinance, stating she worked on 
this ordinance for a long time, with help from Mitchell Park, Legal Counsel, 
Council Office, and the District Attorney ’s Office.  There were two reasons 
for bringing this forward - the first was because someone did complain that a 
biological male was in the female changing room; and secondly, the Council 
approved free recreation passes to children in the County, without a policy in 
place for County changing rooms.  Women and girls want their privacy when 
they are in a changing /locker room.  This ordinance provides for a 
reasonable accommodation for anyone not wanting to use a sex -specific 
changing room.  She wanted to ensure the safety and privacy for all 
recreation users. 
Page 12 of 16
County Council Meeting Minutes December 5, 2023
Council Member Alvord  stated County facilities have gender neutral 
changing areas, sometimes called family changing rooms, so no individual 
would be forced into a specific gender restroom or locker room with this 
ordinance.  The ordinance is not centered around transgender people; it is 
centered around a major loophole in the County ’s policy for its recreation 
facilities that allows men to go into women ’s locker rooms, and staff cannot 
do anything about that because of the County ’s non -discrimination policy .  
He could not in good conscience invite children as young as age 10, without 
supervision, into the recreation centers with this loophole.  He was fully 
prepared to support this ordinance today. 
Mr. Martin Jensen , Director, Parks and Recreation Division, stated Parks 
and Recreation currently has a patron code of conduct policy in place for 
lewdness, which was updated 12 months ago through the District Attorney ’s 
Office.  That patron code of conduct allowed recreation centers to take 
action against anyone behaving lewdly.  That action could be informing the 
individual that their behavior was inappropriate, asking them to stop the 
behavior, or banning them from using the recreation center up to a year.  
The majority of Parks and Recreation facilities have individual changing 
rooms in them, but the older facilities do not have space or resources to be 
able to modify existing facilities.  Salt Lake County is building more 
individual changing rooms in its new facilities for use by any gender.  Those 
changing rooms have a lockable door, and a restroom, sink, and shower in 
them.  
Council Member Stringham stated this all came down to privacy, and 
making sure private spaces were available and protected.  There are many 
complex social and legal implications with this issue, and it is before several 
state and federal courts.  The best and most cost -effective course would be 
for the County to wait on this ordinance.  This was an issue for the courts 
and the State Legislature, and the County could not afford to be in a lawsuit 
now.  
A motion was made by Council Member Stringham, seconded by Council 
Member Bradley, to table a vote on this item and watch to see what decisions 
the federal courts and the Supreme Court made, and then tailor a policy at that 
point.  The motion carried 8 to 1, with Council Member Alvord voting “Nay.”

Council Member Bradley  stated it was premature for him to vote on the 
ordinance today.  He wanted to take time to digest what had been said by 
members of the public and what the County was doing to provide 
sanctuaries for people who wanted privacy.  Tabling a decision on this issue 
and waiting to see what the Legislature does first is a good idea.   
Council Member Alvord  stated he was disappointed the Council voted to 
table this ordinance.  Council Member Theodore did her due diligence in 
creating this, and it was good policy.  In lieu of this not passing, he would be 
asking Council staff, in conjunction with the Parks and Recreation Division, 
to work with his office to develop an ordinance or other legislative initiative 
that would require signage at the Salt Lake County recreation centers to 
better inform patrons that changing facilities were not separated by biological 
sex at birth.  Patrons deserve to know in advance of entering changing 
facilities that they may encounter individuals of the opposite sex in a state of 
undress and have the option of making another choice.  
Council Member Winder Newton  stated she had been concerned about 
the enforcement of the ordinance, so thought tabling it and continuing a 
discussion on it was great.
