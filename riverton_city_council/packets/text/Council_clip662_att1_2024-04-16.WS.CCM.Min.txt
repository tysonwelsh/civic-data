 9   development goals. This year marks a significant review period for the general plan. A

14   Mr. Lethbridge said the text component of the general plan serves as a narrative outlining the
15   City's characteristics and overarching goals. It encapsulates broad objectives such as promoting
16   moderate-income housing and ensuring housing diversity. Specific areas of focus, such as the
17   western commercial district, may be highlighted, along with strategies to achieve citywide goals.
18   Priorities like trail development and traffic management feature prominently in discussions and
19   will be reflected in the text portion of the general plan update.
20
21   Mr. Lethbridge said the commercial designations in the general plan are categorized based on
22   intensity, similar to residential zoning classifications. Community commercial areas typically host
23   light retail and office spaces that integrate well with nearby residential zones. On the other hand,
24   regional commercial zones accommodate larger-scale developments, such as big-box stores,
25   and may include light manufacturing and industrial uses. The Suburban Land Reserve project
26   area, or Western Commercial District, is a unique zone highlighted in the general plan. When
27   considering commercial development, factors like building size, use types, and their impact on
28   surrounding properties are taken into account. The aim is to ensure that the chosen commercial
29   activities align with the character of the area and minimize adverse effects on neighboring
30   residential communities.

36   Mr. Lethbridge outlined the division of responsibilities between the City Council and the
37   Planning Commission regarding land use decisions. He clarified that the City Council makes
38   final decisions on general plan amendments, zoning changes, ordinance amendments, and
39   recommendations of plats. The Planning Commission serves as the land use authority for
40   residential subdivisions, commercial site plans, and conditional use permits, with the Council
41   retaining authority over decisions related to easements, rights of way, and financial obligations.
42   The Planning Commission also recommends action to the Council on general plan zoning
43   changes and ordinance amendments related to land use. He noted that while additional layers
44   of approval can be added through the conditional use permit process, the roles and
45   responsibilities outlined are unlikely to change at both the city and state levels.

 3             Ordinance No. 24-03 – Amending Riverton City Code Title 13, Chapters 5 and 10
 4             (Culinary and Secondary Water)
 5
 6   Mr. Carter said this ordinance centered around proposed changes to the utility code. The utility
 7   billing department sought to modify regulations so that utility accounts would be established
 8   solely in the name of property owners, rather than allowing them to be set up with renters. This
 9   change aimed to streamline account management and ensure that any outstanding debts would
10   remain the responsibility of the property owner, even if tenants vacated the property without
11   paying. However, the proposed amendment was not adopted by the Council previously due to
12   the need for refinement in other sections of the ordinances related to the maintenance of fire
13   hydrants.
14
15   Mr. Carter said the issue regarding fire hydrant maintenance is nuanced and depends on the
16   property rights conveyed to the City through the subdivision process. If a fire hydrant is located
17   within a public right of way, it is the City's responsibility to maintain it. However, if the hydrant is
18   situated on private property or within a commercial subdivision that the City does not own or
19   have access to, then the responsibility falls on the property owner. The ordinance has been
20   amended to clarify these distinctions, providing a clearer understanding for stakeholders.
21
22   Councilmember McCay expressed agreement with the idea that utility accounts for rental
23   properties should be maintained in the owner's name to ensure accountability. However, she
24   raised concerns about the severity of the penalties outlined in Section 2 of the ordinance.
25   Specifically, she questioned the inclusion of imprisonment as a potential punishment for
26   violations, suggesting that it might be overly harsh. She believes that fines and water shutoffs
27   alone could address the issue without resorting to more severe penalties.
28
29   Mr. Carter emphasized that while most cases of utility billing account issues involve situations
30   where individuals fall behind on their bills, resulting in actions such as water shutoffs or sending
31   the account to collections, these instances typically do not lead to criminal charges. However,
32   there are rare occasions where individuals illegally tap into a neighbor's utility account to supply
33   water to their own property, constituting theft. In such cases, criminal charges may be pursued
34   to address the unauthorized use of utility services.
35
36   Councilmembers and staff continued discussion about whether to include the language in
37   Section 13.05.030, bullet number 2 which has language about giving false information on an
38   application and the punishment for that offense.
39
40   Councilmember McCay MOVED that the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 24-03 –
41   Amending Riverton City Code Title 13, Chapters 5 and 10 (Culinary and Secondary
42   Water), excluding Section 13.05.030, bullet number 2.              Councilmember Pierucci

44   called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: Buroker-yes, Haymond-no, McCay-yes,
