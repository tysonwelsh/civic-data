      3. 2         Taylorsville   Police Department    Report — Chief Cottam



 IJ Chief Brady Cottam recognized Det. Ben Cameron, and Officers Rye Jones and Cade Coon
for their outstanding work, explaining that they had been named the Police Employees of the
Month for January, February and March 2025 respectively.

The Chief provided an overview of crime statistics and data, noting a decrease in traffic accidents
and auto theft cases. He addressed trends in citations and response times, pointing out that
priority -one calls were being responded to in the four -minute range which was outstanding.                He
also announced that beginning July 1, Utah Highway Patrol would now have more responsibility
for criminal activity taking place on Bangerter Highway, although TVPD would still be available to
assist when needed and could even take the lead on some high -priority situations where their
experience and equipment was needed.


         3. 3      Victim    Advocate    Report



 IQ TVPD victim advocate Merari shared a case study highlighting the importance of her work.
She described assisting a woman who felt hopeless and unbelieved, helping her obtain a
protective order and supporting her through multiple court hearings. The case resulted in three
different charges for violation of the protective order against the perpetrator and a successful
prosecution.


Alexandra,      another     victim   advocate,    emphasized   the collaborative   effort between   the victim

advocates and police officers in supporting victims and providing necessary documentation for
successful court proceedings.

         3. 4      Real Day City Presentation — Howard Mitchell

 IR Chair Harker introduced Howard Mitchell who presented the concept of "Real Day," a Utah
registered nonprofit initiative aimed at encouraging people to disconnect from screens and social
media on the first day of every month. The goal was to combat excessive screen time and social
media use, especially among youth and families and instead to engage in real -life activities such
as sports, arts, volunteering or self -care.


Mr. Mitchell suggested ways for the city to incorporate                  Real Day into existing events and
programs.

         6. 1   Public Hearing and Consideration    of Resolution No. 25- 10 A Resolution of the
                City of Taylorsville Approving Year -End Adjustments to the 2024- 2025 Fiscal Year
                Budget — Scott Harrington



 IQ Assistant City Administrator and Chief Financial Officer Scott Harrington presented the year-
end adjustments to the FY2024- 2025 budget. He explained that there were eight changes
primarily dealing with vacation payouts for retiring or departing employees. Revenue needed to
be adjusted up by approximately $ 101, 000 and he mentioned that there might be one more
adjustment ( involving insurance deductibles), so he recommended that the Council wait to
consider approval of this resolution until the June 4th meeting.

                      City of Taylorsville Approving the Budget for Fiscal Year 2025- 2026 — Scott
                      Harrington



Mr. Harrington presented the proposed budget for FY 2025- 2026. Some key points included the
following:
    o
            Total revenues were up by $ 607, 352 over last year
    o
            State liquor fund was projected to go up 4%
    o       Interest income was still performing well
    o $
             670, 000 in new revenue via the Salt Lake County Local Option Tax ( 1/ 5 of a 1/ 5)

Regarding          expenditures,   design     and      construction        of   improvements     at   Summit   Park   and
Taylorsville       Park would continue; the election could cost as much as $ 102, 000, but that was only

if a primary became necessary in each of the three races;                              roadway    safety    enhancement
expenditures were needed in both the General and Capital Funds, including micro -paving, slurry

to continue to invest in rotating the city fleet but of note was the fact that there was no tax
increase recommended this year.

Some challenges with preparing this budget were:
      Insurance premiums had increased 15. 5%
            Estimated decrease in sales tax revenue

            Rising cost of service provider contracts
            Relocation of the justice court to West Jordan ( although a long- term savings was
            anticipated)

            Reduced federal and state funding
