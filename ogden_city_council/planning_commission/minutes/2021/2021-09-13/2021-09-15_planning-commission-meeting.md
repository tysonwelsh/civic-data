1. The CBD zone and Make Ogden combination

  Mr. Montgomery indicated the City has received a grant and is working with Wasatch
  Front Regional Council on updating the Ordinance for the Central Business District.
  The goal is to have the new ordinance language adopted by July 2022. This also
  would be a good time to incorporate the recommendations of the Make Ogden Plan to
  be in the Ordinance. He stated the current ordinance requires Commission review for
  all outside work within the CBD but has limited standards, addressing transportation,
  landscaping and architectural form and character and compatibility with surroundings
  as well as authority to review landscaping, setback and parking waivers or reductions
  and encroachment permits. The intent of this discussion is to brainstorm and identify
  items and issues which may have been a concern in the past as well as to identify
  whether more particular standards should be in the CBD Ordinance. He asked the
  Commission to consider what is lacking for their review of items in the CBD.
  Commission suggested the balance between aesthetic concerns and the needs of
  businesses has been a concern. Items often are not anticipated until they are before
  the Commission, making it difficult to identify concerns. It was indicated the Make
  Ogden Plan focused on larger buildings while there is little opportunity for new large
  buildings as the CBD is mostly built out. The focus should be more toward remodeling
  of existing buildings. Consideration also might be given to where the appropriate
  boundaries of the CBD zone should be to keep items in context. Discussion continued

  relating to existing space within the CBD, both developed and vacant as well as
  existing and desirable uses in the area. Commission noted while office and
  commercial uses are important, there also needs to be residential to support those
  uses. It was indicated it is important to have a mix of both rental and owner-occupied
  housing, as renters typically support local businesses while owners create
  neighborhood stability. Commission continued to discuss housing options, density
  caps and efforts for affordable housing as well as quality of design.

  Addressing design concepts of the CBD, Commission felt it is important that
  architecture is well designed and good quality, suggesting some designs standards
  might be desirable to assure buildings are compatible with existing surrounding
  buildings. It was noted the CDD could be divided into sections where different
  standards might be adopted. The form of the buildings often is as important as the
  land use. It was felt standards might create a better downtown experience for both
  residents and visitors to the downtown.

  The concept of waivers which are now considered by the Commission was next
  discussed. It was indicated many buildings occupy the entire parcel of land and there
  is no space available for either parking or landscaping which warrants a waiver of
  these standards. Discussion continued relating to the need for parking if the intent is
  for a more walkable area, whether downtown parking should remain free or whether
  meters or pay parking lots would be more appropriate and the use of public parking by
  residents of multi-family projects who charge an additional fee for parking. It was
  suggested some formulas might be developed when considering waivers or
  reductions. Commission felt paid parking is important to the downtown.

  Considering architectural standards, Commission felt modern architecture is
  appropriate in some areas, but not on Historic 25th Street. Both building materials and
  architectural lines and color compatibility are important. Commission agreed with the
  recommendation in the Make Ogden Plan that stucco is not appropriate for the
  downtown. Make Ogden also identified specific districts within the CBD and it is felt
  different areas may have different standards.

2. Water wise landscaping, land use planning and water conservation

  Mr. Montgomery noted with the existing drought in the area, there has been much
  interest in water conservation and low-water landscaping. While the Ordinance does
  allow some water-wise landscaping, there may be other ways to conserve water such
  as recirculating, reusing or recycling water. He stated the Planning Staff has been
  working with Water Services in developing design standards for low-water plants,
  noting there needs to be more effort towards educating the public relating to the
  maintenance of low-water landscaping.
