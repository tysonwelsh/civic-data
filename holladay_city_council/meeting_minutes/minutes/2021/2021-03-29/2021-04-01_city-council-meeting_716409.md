       a.      Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling Update.
Mayor Dahle reported that the above item related to the Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District
and noted that it pertained to a change in the governing model. Council Member Gibbons noted that
what was being discussed was a change in the legal structure and not in the actual work. If the City
Council was interested in moving forward, a resolution would be needed. There would need to be
unanimous approval from all of the sister cities in the County as well as Salt Lake County. Council
Member Gibbons reported that half a dozen cities had already passed a resolution in support.

Pam Roberts, Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District General Manager, shared an Executive
Summary written by legal counsel, Rachel Anderson. She shared some history related to the Wasatch
Front Waste and Recycling District (WFWRD), which was created by Salt Lake County and
established in 1977. The Administrative Control Board (ACB) was created in 2010. The idea behind
the creation of the ACB was to give cities in the district a seat at the table.

Ms. Roberts reported that the WFWRD separated from the County Council in 2013. When the
separation took place, ACB accepted all of the assets and liabilities, including personnel. Ms.
Anderson clarified that Salt Lake County decided to make the ACB as independent as possible and
delegated as much authority as they legally were allowed. What would make the district completely
independent would be to convert to a local district. At the time of the separation, there was no legal
mechanism for the WFWRD to do so. However, a statute was passed in 2013 to allow a special
service district to convert to a local district. They were now ready to focus on that conversion.

Ms. Anderson discussed boundaries and shared an example related to Sandy City. The WFWRD was
currently unable to do anything themselves as it relates to boundaries. She felt it would be beneficial
to have primary control over those types of decisions. The process laid out in the 2013 statute required
Salt Lake County to pass a resolution declaring their intent to convert. There would also need to be
a public hearing and municipal consent. All of the municipalities within the district needed to consent
to the conversion. Once all of those steps had been taken, the conversion would be filed with the
State.

Council Member Fotheringham asked about the nature of the Board moving forward with a local
district model. Ms. Anderson explained that the intention was to maintain the same type of Board
composition that currently existed. Each municipality would be able to appoint one member from the
City Council to represent their community. Council Member Petersen believed the local district
model would reduce the Salt Lake County representation to one member instead of the currently
appointed two members. Ms. Anderson confirmed this. Council Member Petersen believed this
would bring more balance to the Board and expressed her support.

Ms. Roberts addressed concerns related to the WFWRD having taxing authority. She explained that
they already have taxing authority. However, they currently need to go to the County to have them
levy a tax on behalf of the them. Ms. Roberts stated that she had no intention of wanting to levy a

that the Board would want to keep that process the same.

Council Member Gibbons expressed support for the conversion. He wondered if there would be
cross-agency County benefits that would need to be replicated. Ms. Roberts explained that WFWRDt
coordinates with Salt Lake County Public Works for emergency planning and they also have an
Emergency Management Coordinator. Ms. Anderson added that since the separation, the County
treated WFWRD as an independent entity.

Discussions were had about tax levies. Ms. Anderson explained that if the WFWRD became a local
district, there would already be a cap in place. However, they did not intend to impose a tax. City
Attorney, Todd Godfrey commented that even though the requirement for voter authorization of a tax
levy would be lost in the change to a local district, there would be a cap on the levy that was not very
high. He felt that would be a benefit. Mayor Dahle wondered if the WFWRD would gain the ability
to impose a tax. Ms. Anderson clarified that they technically already have that ability. However, the
steps that would need to be taken would be different. She reiterated that there was no intention to
levy a tax.

        d.      Update on Moratorium.
Mayor Dahle explained a moratorium was put in place while the City Council looked into the various

Council Member Petersen thanked everyone who worked on the item.

        e.     Property Tax Discussion.
Mayor Dahle commented that the City Council was not in a hurry to reach a final decision on the
property tax increase. He noted that this City Council could be the Council that focused on the
property tax increase that was required in order to maintain the long-term infrastructure of the City.
Ms. Chamness reminded Council Members that there was a roundtable community discussion
scheduled for April 15, 2021. She felt it would be useful to have a property tax increase number at
that time.

Ms. Chamness shared the Financial Sustainability Plan: Updated Scenario Analysis document. She
noted that a few different scenarios were prepared. The process involved changes based on basic
assumptions. For instance, sales tax revenue grew at a much faster pace than originally anticipated

Holladay City Council Meeting Minutes 04-01-21                                                7
in the model. The previous assumption included a 4% increase in sales tax revenue projections for
2021. However, the updated assumption was 10% growth. The updated assumption for 2022 was
5% and the numbers returned to normal in 2023, with an assumption of a 4% increase.

Ms. Chamness reported that the changes to the sales tax assumptions created a $3 million change over
a period of five years. There was also the addition of the legislative funding, which was $300,000 a
year over five years. This would generate an additional $1.5 million. Ms. Chamness posed a number
of questions to the City Council related to the overall increase:

   •   Does the City Council want to eliminate the debt that the prior model assumed? The assumed
       $7 million bond could largely be replaced with the use of the capital projects fund balance and
       the general fund balance; and
   •   Does the City Council want to issue debt?

Ms. Chamness explained that this was a decision the City Council would need to make. It would
influence the rate of increase for the property tax. Ms. Chamness walked the City Council through
the first possible scenario which was to eliminate debt with a 75% upfront tax increase. It related to
the elimination of the assumed $7 million bond. If the Council chose that option, it would assume a
$3 million transfer from the General Fund to the Capital Projects Fund. It would use a large portion
of the existing capital projects fund upfront to pay for proposed projects.

Ms. Chamness shared a slide related to the transfer of funds. She noted that the City Council's goal
was to keep $3 million in the fund balance. This particular scenario would leave the Council short of
that goal. While the scenario would eliminate the need for a bond, it would spend the capital projects
fund down to a level that the City Council previously indicated they did not want to go below.
Discussions were had about transfers and the 75% tax increase.

The second scenario was outlined which was to Issue Debt: 50% Upfront Tax Increase.
Ms. Chamness reported that it was the closest scenario to the 92% scenario recommended by the
Citizen Advisory Group. The only assumptions that were changed related to the sales tax and the
addition of the legislative funding. The bond amounts remained the same and this scenario would
preserve a larger portion of the existing capital project fund balance. The debt would be issued as
originally planned but would capitalize on historically low-interest rates.

Ms. Chamness informed the City Council that they could choose to focus on a combination of
scenarios or none of the presented scenarios. Discussions were had about the next bond in 2026.
Mayor Dahle noted that the presented scenarios were adjustments based on additional revenue. He
believed the second scenario would allow the City Council to execute the plan proposed by the Citizen
Advisory Group, but with a 50% tax increase instead of a 92% increase. It could also include a policy
statement that each year, there would be an anticipated inflationary adjustment to take care of City
expenses over time.

Council Member Fotheringham had difficulty understanding how the new revenue pieces would
create that much of a change in terms of the percentages. Discussions were had about previous
scenarios from the Citizen Advisory Group. Both Council Member Fotheringham and Council
Member Gibbons asked for additional information to fully understand the nuances of the different
scenarios. Council Member Gibbons expressed concerns about the 92% increase recommended by
