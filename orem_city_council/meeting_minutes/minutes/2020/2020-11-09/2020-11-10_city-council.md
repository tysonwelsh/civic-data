      ORDINANCE – Amending Appendix A of the Orem City Code by amending Standard
      Land Use Code 6413 from Automobile Repair to Automobile Repair (Mechanical Only)
      and enacting Standard Land Use Code 6414 Auto Body Repair and Paint

Mr. Bench stated Business Licensing requests the City Council clarify standard land use code 6413
Automobile Repair by adding the parenthetical “(Mechanical Only)” to clarify that this SLU applies to
mechanical repair only and not auto body or paint work. Business Licensing also requests that the City
Council enact SLU Code 6414 (Auto Body Repair and Paint) to apply to repair shops that focus on auto
body repair and paint work. This change is requested because auto body repair and painting businesses have
additional state permit requirements that don’t apply to mechanical auto repair businesses. This amendment
will help the Business License division determine specific permitting requirements for the two different
types of businesses at the time of licensing and will also aid in enforcement efforts in an area where some
businesses have been less than cooperative with Neighborhood Preservation Officers.

Mr. Sumner inquired if there were some of the paint shops in residential areas. Mr. Bench indicated there
are a few that do not have a license and by going in this direction it would help the NPU Officers in
regulating that issue.

Mayor Brunst opened the public hearing at 6:36 p.m. Hearing no public comments, the Mayor
closed the public hearing and brought the item back to the City Council for further discussion.

Mayor Brunst moved to approve an Ordinance Amending Appendix A of the Orem City Code
by amending Standard Land Use Code 6413 from Automobile Repair to Automobile Repair
(Mechanical Only) and enacting Standard Land Use Code 6414 Auto Body Repair and Paint. Mrs.
Lauret seconded the motion. Those voting aye: Richard F. Brunst, Jeff Lambson, Debby Lauret,
Tom Macdonald, Terry Peterson, David Spencer, and Brent Sumner. The motion passed.

       ORDINANCE – Amending portions of Section 22-24-2 The Hub District (HD) zones of the
       Orem City Code pertaining to enacting the Edge zone adjacent to University Parkway and
       modifications to the overall height requirement and permitted uses in the Edge zone and other
       amendments including enacting Sections 22-24-2(J) Sidewalks and Landscaping and 22-24-
       2(K) Alternate Development Standards for the HD-E Zone. In addition, amending Section
       22-5-3(A) and the zoning map of the City of Orem by rezoning portions of the properties
       located generally at 314 East University Parkway, 212 East University Parkway and 1315
       South State Street from the HD zone to the HD-Edge zone

Mr. Bench stated the Development Services Department requests the City Council amend portions
of Section 22-24-2 pertaining to the Hub District (HD) zone. One of the main purposes of the
proposed amendments is to create a new Edge zone within the Hub District. The proposed Edge
zone would be in three general locations adjacent to University Parkway with a depth of 200 feet
from the back of the sidewalk creating an area for retail uses with no minimum height requirement.
The HD Core zone currently requires a minimum height of three stories for new structures. This
makes it difficult for certain new retail uses such as restaurants to develop adjacent to University
Parkway. Because the properties adjacent to University Parkway are highly desirable for retail
uses, staff believes that eliminating the three story height requirement for certain retail uses along
the frontage of University Parkway would strengthen the City’s retail sales tax base and promote
economic growth in this area. Staff proposes the creation of the HD-Edge zone in certain areas
along the frontage of University Parkway. The Edge zone would not have a minimum height
requirement of three stories as is required in the HD-Core zone. Uses in the Edge zone would be
limited to retail uses to ensure that the City maximizes the retail opportunities along the University
Parkway corridor. The new Edge zone will also help property owners to develop retail pad sites
adjacent to University Parkway without compromising existing leases with the big box tenants
behind which often have provisions in their leases limiting the height of structures that may be
located in front of them. The proposed amendments also include updating setback requirements
for parking lots adjacent to the street. A minimum six foot (6’) landscaped setback would be
required from the back of the sidewalk when adjacent to parking stalls, drive aisles or drive thru
stacking lanes. The proposed new Sections 22-24-2(J) and 22-24-2(K) will clarify sidewalk and
landscaping requirements as well as alternatives in developing in the Edge zone. In addition to the
ordinance amendments, the Development Services Department requests the City Council rezone
the properties located generally at 314 East University Parkway, 212 East University Parkway and
1315 South State Street from the HD-C (Core) zone to the HD-E (Edge) zone as shown on the
proposed map. The Development Services Department held a neighborhood meeting through
Zoom on October 22, 2020 at 5:50 p.m. with two individuals plus City staff in attendance. The
two attendees made positive comments regarding the proposed changes. They represented property
within the proposed Edge zone.

opportunity to add centrally located greenspace to our city’s landscape. It also has the potential
to benefit a large proportion of our city’s population who currently have extremely limited access
to greenspace. During the last City Council meeting, Councilmember Tom Macdonald voiced
concern about Orem’s citizens being like kids on Christmas with pie-in-the-sky expectations for
the park. We would respectfully counter and say we should be like kids at Christmas! This is an
amazing, exciting opportunity and it would be a shame to not recognize it as such! Now, does
this mean that we as citizens should get all of the pie-in-the-sky amenities at this park? No! We
realize that there are fiscal constraints. However, preserving all nine acres with the bare
minimum cost if needed now so that amenities could be added later as funds become available is
a much preferable option than to sell a large proportion of the acreage. The sale and the finality
of the loss of this land would be a short-sighted decision. We have the chance to create a large
space that generations of citizens will enjoy. We humbly ask that the city refrain from selling
part of the acreage even if this means that the park may not have as many amenities. Preservation
of all nine acres as green space should be the highest priority. Orem has a great track record of
encouraging the growth of businesses in our community. In this case, please refrain from making
the best business decision in terms of dollars and cents but instead make the decision that will
have the greatest benefit for the current and future families of Orem. We can’t think of a better
way to honor our city slogan “Family City USA” than creation of the full nine-acre Hillcrest
Park.

Mayor and Council Members: Thank you for your time and willingness to consider my below
message in regard to the zoning of 2000 South and Geneva Road. I have been directly working
with Davies Design Build as the operating partner for convenience stores, gas stations and QSR
food establishments. We currently operate in Utah and Colorado markets. My business as a retail
and fuel operator began in 2014 in Panguitch, UT. Prior to 2014, my time was spent managing
the operations finance team for an ultra-low-cost carrier airline and was a CPA with Deloitte
prior to the airlines. This letter is specific to the environment concern discussed in the Planning
Commission meeting held on 10/27/2020.
New gas station construction is very differently than construction completed 10+ years ago.
Below are some of the methods and equipment we use to ensure environmental compliance,
which is synonymous with our success as operators and investors.
1. Underground storage tanks (UST): new installs have multiple options to provide continuous
monitoring of an environmental release. This is completed through Interstitial Monitoring, which
means the USTs have sensors between the exterior shell of the UST and the fuel holding tank of
the UST. These alerts are instant when the sensor is triggered (see page 4-5).
2. Automatic Tank Gauging (ATG - Inventory monitoring): this provides automatic inventory
monitoring vs manual sticking the tank (like a dip stick on car oil). This is implemented at all our
sites that have USTs. The tank readings flow directly to our IT system and allow us to
automatically monitor inventory and identify any variance in fuel gallons (see page 3).
3. Statistical Inventory Reconciliation (SIR): this is a method of statically tracking fuel
inventory to monitor if an environmental release may be taking place. The IT system we use in
connection with the ATG make this an automated process within our business (see page 6).
4. Electronic Line Leak Detection: This piece of equipment actively tests the fuel lines in the
ground on a regular basis. Example: 3 people are pumping on the unleaded line, when the final
fueler hangs up the nozzle, the electronic line leak detection will engage and pressurize the line.
If pressure is lost there will be an automatic stop to that fuel line that would need to be addressed
before that line can pump more fuel. This testing can be completed every time a fueling events is
finalized (i.e. after 30 seconds of no fuel flow, the test will begin).
5. Double Walled Flex Fuel Piping: If there is a leak in the main pipe, it is released into the
exterior piping and the pressure will push it back to a sump containment. This release would be
noted through a sensor that identifies liquid in the sump containment or an A/B inspection. Many
older sites may have single walled piping, which could result in a line leak directly into the earth
(see page 9).
6. Under dispenser containment (UDC) and sump pump containment will be installed to
separate fueling equipment from any direct ground contact, providing an additional layer of
environmental protection.
7. A/B Operator Monthly Inspections: This inspection includes evaluation of the entire fueling
system and documenting it for the state and internal records.
8. Tank tightness testing & pipe tightness testing: this is required by the state each year, we
have a good relationship with our contacts at DEQ and have a great track record of compliance.
