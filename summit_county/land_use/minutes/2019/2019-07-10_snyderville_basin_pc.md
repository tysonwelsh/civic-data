2. Public hearing and possible action regarding the Snyderville Basin Development 
Code Amendments to review potential amendments to Section 10-9-22 Appeal 
Procedures of the Snyderville Basin Development Code- Ray Milliner, Principal 
Planner  
 
Planner Milliner asked Attorney Brackin to comment on the language change Staff is 
recommending.  Attorney Brackin said the appeal procedures have been in the Code for 
approximately 20 years.  There has never been an issue until recently.  The County 
thought this procedure was thoroughly understood.

A challenge was made of a decision rendered by the County Manager.  The Code grants 
10 days for an appeal to be submitted to the County Council.   At 4:57 p.m. on the 10th 
day, an incomplete and unsigned form was submitted by email.  It arrived at 4:59 p.m.  
The Planning Department secretary was getting ready to leave for the day and didn’t 
see the email until the next day.   
 
Attorney Brackin added that no money was submitted at that time.  The applicant tried 
to call at approximately 5:10 p.m. to pay with a credit card, but the secretary had left for 
the day.   The next day she noted the activity, but the application was incomplete, there 
was no signature, and no money had been paid.  The applicant was told the time to 
apply for an appeal had expired.   
 
The applicant took the County to court.  The judge ruled that the form didn’t clarify that 
the form had to be submitted by the close of the 10th business day neither did it specify 
that a completed form had to be submitted.   
 
As a result of this ruling, the County decided to amend the language to specify those 
items.  The new language requires that by 5:00 p.m. on the 10
th day, a completed form 
with a “wet” signature (not electronic), and payment has to be received.  This will make 
sure that the requirements are clear.  It is also stated that if these requirements are not 
met by that time, there is no appeal.  
 
The public hearing was opened.  No comments were made and the public hearing was closed. 
 
COMMISSION COMMENTS AND QUESTIONS          
Commissioner Fine asked Attorney Brackin to give an example of criteria #3 found in 
the Staff Report.  Attorney Brackin answered this language clarifies that simply because 
someone files an appeal; it does not mean the appeal will be granted.   She said this also 
specifies the zoning is not being changed and an additional land use is not being 
created.

Commissioner Dickey asked why a wet signature is required.  Attorney Brackin 
answered the County wants to ensure that the person signing the application has the 
authority to do so.  They have discovered that not everyone who submits an electronic 
application has that authority.   
 
Commissioner Dickey asked when the clock on the 10-day time period begins.  
Attorney Brackin answered the appeal period begins the day after the decision is 
rendered.  It would be 10 calendar days for the day of the decision.  Commissioner 
Dickey suggested that be spelled out to avoid confusion.  He said in his experience 
there are different times when the clock begins for different deadlines.  Sometimes, it is 
work days, other times three extra days are given if the document is being mailed.  
Attorney Brackin said that a sentence can be added to clarify when the appeal period 
begins.  An example will be given to eliminate any confusion.   
 
Commissioner Dickey made a motion to forward a positive recommendation to the 
County Council for the attached ordinance as outlined in the Staff Report and as 
written below.  An additional sentence will be added describing when the 10 day 
appeal time-period begins.  Commissioner Fine seconded the motion.  All voted in 
approval. 
 
F
INDINGS OF FACT 
1. In 2017 it was determined by the County Council that a more formal process for 
appeals of land use decision would be beneficial. 
2. In 2017 an appeal policy was adopted by the Council. 
3. The proposed changes are consistent with the policies and procedures that were 
adopted in 2017 and likewise, those policies are not being required under the 
Code changes.    
4. The proposed amendment to the Snyderville Basin Development Code clarify the 
process for appeals in the Code and brings it into conformance with current 
County practices and what has been the practices of Courts of Law.  The

C
ONCLUSIONS OF LAW 
1. The amendment is consistent with the goals, objectives, and policies of the 
General Plan. 
2. The amendment will not permit the use of land that is not consistent with the 
uses of properties nearby. 
3. The amendment will not permit suitability of the properties affected by the 
proposed amendment for the uses to which they have been restricted.   
4. The amendment will not permit the removal of the then existing restrictions 
which will unduly affect nearby property.  
5. The amendment will not grant special favors or circumstances solely for one 
property owner or developer.  
6. The amendment will promote the public health, safety and welfare better than 
the existing regulations for which the amendment is intended to change.
