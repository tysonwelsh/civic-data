2. Consideration of Honorary Street Name, for 675 N. (Attachment 2)

   Mr. Jesse Redden indicated he had lived in the Ron Claire area as a child and has since moved back into
   the community. He is proposing an honorary street name to honor Satnam Singh, a long-time owner of
   the corner market on 675 North Monroe and had recently been killed during a robbery at the store.


                                                               [1]
Responding to the Staff recommendations, he stated existing honorary names have been added to
recognize civil rights leaders as well as to recognize area high schools. While Mr. Singh is not well
known beyond this neighborhood, his contribution to the surrounding community has been significant
due to his friendliness and kindness. He stated Mr. Singh has operated the store for more than 20 years
and residents of the area consider him a great friend and mentor. He felt as 675 North is a
neighborhood street, the addition of an honorary name would not create confusion, indicating the
honorary name does have the support of the neighboring community. He felt the honorary name is
consistent with the Mt. Lewis Neighborhood Plan in that it creates a neighborhood identity and would
increase community pride. Because this street is important to the community, recognition of the
contributions of Mr. Singh serves the surrounding community, creating a local public purpose, but not a
City-wide public purpose. He also stated while the City Council is currently considering a new policy
relating to honorary street names, the petition was submitted under the current code and should not be
affected by any new policy or standard.

Mr. Brierley stated the request before the Commission is to consider adding an honorary street name to
675 North honoring Satnam Singh. He stated the petition contains much information about the
contributions of Mr. Singh to this community as well as support from surrounding residents. He noted
an honorary name does not change the street name, and the directional name of 675 North would
remain, but the designation of an honorary name would allow an additional nameplate to be added to
the sign. Local addresses along 675 North would not be affected. He reviewed some life history of Mr.
Singh and stated his life merits remembrance. He stated 675 North is a local meandering street,
extending from Jefferson to Gramercy, with the Super Grocery store located at 675 North Monroe.

He then reviewed the Ordinance relating to honorary street names and the role of the Commission
relating to their recommendation, stating considerations of the Commission influede whether the
honorary name serves a public purpose, its effect on utility, emergency and delivery services, whether it
is consistent with street patterns of the City as well as compliant with the General Plan. Existing
honorary names recognize either national heroes or schools, with a more recent one recognizing a local
historic figure of a particular area. Due to this most recent request, the City Council is currently
reviewing the honorary street policy and in the process of creating a better ordinance by which these
are determined. While discussions continue, this petition has been submitted and is to be reviewed by
the current ordinance relating to honorary street names. He suggested if the Commission favors
approval of the petition, the name be extended to the full length of 675 North, stating due to the
curvature of the street, the existing numbering system may be somewhat confusing. He stated due to
the name being honorary, the actual street numbering system remains, and the addition of the honorary
name would not affect emergency services such as police and fire protection, or the connection to
online map services. He stated many neighbors have signed the petition and are in favor of the addition
of the honorary name and mailing addresses will not be affected. He stated no negative impacts are
created by the proposal, and it is consistent with the General Plan and the Mt. Lewis Community Plan in
that it creates a community identity, indicating the community plan suggests an entry marker be placed
at this corner to identify the community. Staff recommendation is for approval of the petition to appl
the name to 670-675 North between Gramercy and 950 East pending their adopti9n of a policy guiding
the use of honorary street names.

Commissioner Stoker expressed the definition of what serves a public purpose is vague, and expressed
concern relating to the cost to the City to install such signs. She felt while there is current support of
the legacy of Mr. Singh, over time, that may become lost and the designation becomes meaningless,
stating historic political leaders are nationally recognized. Commissioner Safsten stated there are other
items which warrant the attention and funding of the City Council, such as utility improvements, crime
reduction, and felt while there is some value for this area, it is not the highest priority. Commissioner

                                                  [2]
  Blaisdell expressed concern the establishment of an honorary name for this community may set a
  precedent for future decisions and expressed concern the policy is currently under review by the City
  Council, with no direction at this point as to what warrants an honorary street name. She also
  expressed concern with the timing of recognizing a person. Commissioner Southwick felt the
  Commission has the obligation to act on the petition submitted under the current regulations, and as
  there is much support from the surrounding community, and it does promote community identity,
  favorable action is needed.

  Chair Graf asked for public comment, but there was none.

  MOTION:      A motion was made by Commissioner Southwick to recommend the honorary street
               name be approved based on the findings it will not affect the community in terms of
               emergency services and has the support of neighborhood residents, extending the length
               of the honorary name to continue to 670 North and follow the length of the meandering
               street. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Garner and failed on a tie vote 4-4, with
               Commissioners Garner, Sandau, Southwick and Stoker voting aye and Commissioners
               Blaisdell, Safsten, Schade and Graf voting no.

  Commission discussed the proposal, stating the request does not change the street numbering but
  would be added to existing signage. As proposed, It would not change the ability to find a particular
  address on the street. Commissioner Safsten asked if there would be support to establish a name
  change which would not be honorary but more permanent, but limited to the portion of the street known
  as 675 North. Mr. Montgomery stated there is a different process for permanent street name changes,
  and additional noticing is required as it would affect utility companies, emergency response and mail
  delivery, and that existing mailing addresses would be required.

  MOTION:      A motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to recommend denial of the petition to add
               the honorary street name to 675 North based on the findings there is no specific
               ordinance or policy in place for the guidance of the Planning Commission and recommend
               the City Council to adopt a set of rules to be used in the future relating to honorary street
               names. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Schade and failed by a 3-5 vote with
               Commissioners Blaisdell, Schade and Graf voting aye and Commissioners Garner, Safsten,
               Sandau, Southwick and Stoker voting aye

6. 12th Street Corridor Special Exception, to reduce accessway width for Sienna Village, 645 12th Street.
   (Attachment 6)

  Mr. Matt McConkie stated he is proposing to develop a 22-unit townhome project with garages in the
  center and street-facing units. He is proposing the amount of pavement be reduced to serve the rear
  unit.

  Ms. Corbridge stated this project was approved under the guidelines of the 12th Street Corridor Zone
  and is currently under site plan review. It has been found the accessway width behind buildings 3 and 4
  does not meet the ordinance requirement of 30’. With the required 8’ landscape setback. The request is
  to reduce this access to 26’. She stated the requirement is based on typical garages facing garages
  where more maneuverability is needed, and this proposal shows garages facing green space in the rear.
  The 12th Street Corridor zone allows the Commission to grant special exceptions if it is found the intent

                                                     [4]
  of the Ordinance is satisfied. The proposed drive width is adequate for fire safety and maneuverability
  and is preferrable to reducing the width of landscaping in the rear. She indicated the proposal is
  consistent with the General Plan and the intent of the 12th Street Corridor Plan in that front doors face
  the public street. The request would not impact the existing land uses and would not affect the
  character of its surroundings. While the width reduction to 26’ has been approved by the Fire Marshal,
  the Group Dwelling Ordinance also requires a 28’ width. A second option would be to reduce the aisle
  width to 28’ and reduce the rear landscape setback from 8’ to 6’. Staff recommendation is for approval
  of the proposed exception to reduce the aisle width to 26’.

  Commissioner Schade noted the plan provided does not show the sidewalk extending to the street
  between Buildings #2 and #3 and wanted to be assured it would be required. Commissioner Stoker
  expressed it would be better to have adequate maneuvering space and reduce the landscaping. Ms.
  Corbridge indicated this is a viable option, but has not yet been discussed with the applicant.

  MOTION:       A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to approve the reduced rear aisle width
                from 30’ 26’ based on the findings it does comply with the General Plan and the 12 th Street
                Corridor Plan and does not impact existing property owners. Motion was seconded by
                Commissioner Garner and passed unanimously with Commissioners Blaisdell, Garner,
                Safsten, Sandau, Schade, Southwick, Stoker and Graf voting aye.

7. CBD Site Plan, for Wonder Block Phase I, at 26th & Grant Avenue. (Attachment 7)

  Mr. Robert Taylor stated this is a request for the first phase of the Wonder Block project, consisting of a
  parking structure to serve both the residents within the project, the general public and the adjacent
  State courts building. He stated the overall concept plan for the project development had been
  approved in January, and is patterned similar to a mixed use development in Denver and complies with
  the Make Ogden Plan. Phase 1 consists of an 8-level parking structure accommodating 800 parking
  spaces intended to provide parking space for jurors and courts personnel as well as proposed
  residential, office space and hotel within the project. He stated while a wall is proposed for a mural, he
  would also like to explore the option of other options, including kinetic art for this space. He also
  indicated the final finish material on the Grant Avenue side has not yet been determined.

  Mr. Brierley stated this request is for site plan approval for a parking garage consisting of a 3,500
  square footprint with eight parking levels, providing 800 parking spaces. He stated while the building
  exterior shows little, brick, it is anticipated to be surrounded by other buildings and its surface will not be
  highly visible. The site plan review of the Commission is to determine whether the building material and
  architectural lines relate to existing buildings in the CBD as well as to assure utilities are adequately
  screened and sidewalk and landscaping treatments provide safe and convenient access. While most of
  the surrounding buildings have a brick exterior, this is a building on the interior of the project and the
  street level will not be visible. The proposed façade will feature metal accents with a mural art wall
  similar to other parking structures in the downtown area. The architectural lines fit with existing
  buildings in the area. Staff recommendation is for approval. He also indicated the applicant is
  reviewing options relating to subdividing this lot.

  Commissioner Schade expressed concern that the building has only one entrance/exit point and
  accommodates 800 parking spaces, stating if the main use is for offices in the downtown, all workers
  would be entering and exiting at the same time, creating a bottleneck in the street as well as within the
  structure, suggesting it would be better if there were more options for entering the facility. He also
  asked if pedestrian bridges will be provided for residents who would be expected to park in the
  structure. Chair Graf asked about the timing for development of Phase 2, and whether the development
  of brick buildings to screen the view would be done quickly. Commissioner Blaisdell asked about refuse

                                                      [5]
  control, stating it appears dumpsters are within the building and questioned whether there is adequate
  space for them to be emptied. Commissioner Sandau asked about security within the structure.
  Commissioner Southwick asked how this structure compares with the parking structures at the
  Junction, stating these each have two access points. Mr. Montgomery responded the Junction Parking
  structure accommodates 790 stalls in the south structure, and while it is serviced by two access points,
  one is more consistently used. He felt access could be a problem for concerts or events at the
  amphitheater, but not for typical daily use.

  Mr. Taylor responded indicating the parking specialist has indicated there is a tumbling effect of parking
  as users of the same space are spread throughout a 24-hour period, with the structure supporting 3,000
  vehicle trips per day. He stated changing technology allows available parking space to be identified.
  He reviewed the proposal for trash removal, stating some will be accommodated by roll-out cans and
  the dumpster in the garage will be serviced regularly. He also stated various forms of security are being
  considered, including surveillance cameras to be monitored at a central location.

  Mr. Taylor Lee, 3470 Adams Avenue asked if electrical vehicle charging stations will be available within
  the structure. Mr. Taylor answered the project is required to provide 1% of the total parking to be
  charging stations, but anticipates it will be close to 3%. Discussion continued relating to electrical fees
  associated with vehicle charging with the Commission feeling these should not be a burden on the
  public but would be better if paid by users. Mr. Taylor indicated the intent is to provide solar power for
  much of the facility and it should not be a burden to Ogden City.

  Commission also discussed ingress and egress of the proposed parking structure and suggested a new
  design might be warranted. They expressed particular concern relating to fire exit potential. Mr.
  Montgomery stated the City has relied on information from parking and traffic experts who have
  indicated the single access should be adequate for most uses, but would only be a problem for event
  parking.

  MOTION:       A motion was made by Commissioner Stoker to recommend approval of the site plan
                based on the findings the proposed site plan meets zoning standards, has adequate
                utilities, convenient pedestrian and vehicle access, adequate landscaping and screening, is
                compatible with surrounding buildings and architecture and the purposes of the CBD
                zone. Approval is subject to satisfaction of Staff comments identified in the Staff Report
                as Attachment 6 as well as other applicable departmental requirements. Motion was
                seconded by Commissioner Sandau and passed 7-1 with Commissioners Blaisdell, Garner,
                Safsten, Sandau, Southwick, Stoker and Graf voting aye and Commissioner Schade voting
                no. Commissioner Schade explained his vote is based on the height of the building as well
                as the single access and his feeling a better project is warranted for this location.

8. Petition to Vacate the 1st Street between Washington and Adams Avenue. (Attachment 8)

  Mr. Mike Bastian stated he represents the property owners and is requesting an undeveloped portion of
  1st Street be abandoned by Ogden City. He stated it appeared to have been incorporated as part of the
  Wangsgard’s parking lot. A recent title report identifies its location as well as the location of property
  lines on Adams Avenue. The intent is to construct a large apartment complex and commercial space
  on this vacant property.

  Mr. Simpson stated a portion of First Street had been vacated previously and is now associated with
  property at 101 Jefferson Avenue. He reviewed the remaining portion of the right-of-way, indicating it is
  undeveloped and tapers towards Washington Boulevard. The platted right-of-way is isolated and
  provides no connection to either Washington Boulevard or Adams Avenue. When reviewing a petition

                                                     [6]
  to vacate a public street, the Commission is to consider whether there is good cause for the vacation,
  whether the vacation will injure any property owner and whether it is consistent with the General Plan.
  He reviewed the Hillcrest/Bonneville Plan, indicating its direction for Washington Boulevard is to allow
  higher density residential developments by combining smaller parcels and redeveloping old strip
  commercial space. The vacation as proposed would support and strengthen the commercial node and
  the existing right-of-way has been an area where dumping has occurred and is a potential location for
  homeless camps. He stated the proposal is for a multi-family group dwelling project with commercial
  which will be reviewed at a later date. This is the first step, with future steps being to rezone the
  property to all be commercial, and then to combine the various parcels into one subdivision, and finally,
  to obtain approval of the project. He stated if there are any utilities within the right-of-way easements
  will be required to be preserved for their service. The existing private canal appears to be in a private
  right-of-way and the developer will be required to work with the canal company to assure service is
  continued. He stated research indicates the existing right-of-way was not created as part of a
  subdivision and the applicant will be required to complete research to determine legal methods of
  obtaining the right-of-way, likely to obtain approval for its purchase from the City Council. Staff
  recommendation is for approval, subject to the following conditions:
    1. The petitioner obtain approval and complete the rezoning of the vacated portion of the 1 st Street
        right-of-way to the new east zoning boundary line of parcel 12-058-0013 and the west portions of
        parcels 12-058-0013 & 12-058-0016.
    2. The petitioner obtain approval and complete a subdivision plat process for the vacated portion of
        the 1st Street right-of-way to the new east zoning boundary line of parcel 12-058-0013 and west
        portions of parcels 12-058-0013 & 12-058-0016.
    3. Any utilities within the 1st Street right-of-way be preserved with utility easements.
    4. All department staff comments be satisfied.

    MOTION:     A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to recommend approval of the street
                vacation as requested based on the findings there is good cause for the street vacation,
                neither the public interest or any person will be materially injured by the vacation as
                proposed and easements will be retained for any existing utilities, and the vacation is
                consistent with the General Plan. Approval is subject to the following conditions:
                1. The petitioner obtain approval and complete the rezoning of the vacated portion of the
                   1st Street right-of-way to the new east zoning boundary line of parcel 12-058-0013 and
                   the west portions of parcels 12-058-0013 & 12-058-0016.
                2. The petitioner obtain approval and complete a subdivision plat process for the vacated
                   portion of the 1st Street right-of-way to the new east zoning boundary line of parcel 12-
                   058-0013 and west portions of parcels 12-058-0013 & 12-058-0016.
                3. Any utilities within the 1st Street right-of-way be preserved with utility easements.
                4. All department staff comments be satisfied
                Motion was seconded by Commissioner Sandau and passed unanimously with
                Commissioners Blaisdell, Garner, Safsten, Sandau, Schade, Southwick, Stoker and Graf
                voting aye.

9. Public Hearing, Petition to Rezone, the west portions of 101 and 131 Adams Avenue and the 1st Street
   right-of-way between Washington and Adams from R-1-6 to C-3. (Attachment 9)

  Mr. Mike Bastian stated in consideration of developing this area, he has determined to retain the
  residential character on Adams Avenue and will construct two new single-family homes, but desires the
  rear of the existing parcels as well as the 1st Street right-of-way to be rezoned to C-3 in order to create a
  multi-family/commercial project.


                                                     [7]
Mr. Simpson stated the request is to rezone 99’ of property to commercial, retaining a lot depth for the
single-family homes of 100’. He further described the area proposed to be rezoned and stated the
portion of the 1st Street right-of-way being vacated is included. The project would also include re-
subdividing lots, and the commercial area would not include the lots facing Adams Avenue. This does
not create any nonconformities for these lots and they would remain single-family in both use and zone.
He reviewed the Hillcrest/Bonneville Community Plan, stating it supports high density residential
housing and redevelopment of big box commercial uses into mixed use commercial space as they
become vacant, stating this concept fits with current market trends. The rezoning is consistent with
Community Policies as it will create definite edges between types of development with adequate
buffers, will reflect the prevailing character of the area and will not cut across property boundaries once
the street vacation and subdivision are accomplished. Staff recommendation is for approval, subject to
the following conditions:
1. The petitioner obtain approval and complete the street vacation process of the portion of the 1st
     Street right-of-way between Washington Blvd. and Adams Avenue.
2. The petitioner obtain approval and complete a subdivision plat process for the vacated portion of
     the 1st Street right-of-way to the new east zoning boundary line of parcel 12-058-0013 and west
     portions of parcels 12-058-0013 & 12-058-0016.
3. An updated legal description and map be provided for the vacated portion of the 1 st Street right-of-
     way to the new east zoning boundary line of parcel 12-058-0013 as being included with the rezone.
4. Minimum R-1-6 setbacks and rear yard coverage standards be maintained for the existing home
     and garage on parcel 12-058-0016.
5. Minimum 100’ lot depths be maintained for parcels 12-058-0013 & 12-058-0016.

11. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment, for regulating food trailers. (Attachment 11)

  Ms. Corbridge indicated the City Council has directed Staff to address food trailers being allowed within
  the public right-of-way. Existing regulations allow food trucks in the right-of-way but limit food trailers
  to private property. As Staff has analyzed this, it is felt they should meet the same requirements as
  food trucks. The main difference between trailers and trucks is trailers are typically longer and require a
  tow vehicle. While these both generate some foot traffic, there is concern that existing food businesses
  not be impacted. This proposal would treat food trailers similar to food trucks in that the number per
  lineal block would be restricted, time limits would be applicable and consent from the adjacent owner is
  needed. In addition, the food tailer would be required to remain hitched to the tow vehicle while
  operating in the right-of-way. Staff is concerned that existing parking for adjacent businesses not be
  eliminated and suggest the length of both food trucks and trailers be limited to two parking spaces or
  40’ in length, and overnight parking would be prohibited. She reviewed policies of the General Plan,
  stating the amendment is supported as it is a means to revitalize downtown, provide food service
  options, and promote walkability. Both trucks and trailers would be allowed in the Central Business
  District and all commercial zones, but restricted from operating in residential zones except by permit.
  Staff recommendation is for approval of the proposed amendment.

  Commissioner Schade noted the language proposed limits the time of operation to two hours, similar to
  street parking regulations, but felt two hours is insufficient time for food trucks and trailers to set up
  and serve. He also stated the language limits the size of the canopy over the sidewalk, and indicated
  most trucks have a window larger than 2’ and would need a 3-4’ canopy. Commissioner Blaisdell
  clarified these are not allowed in front of existing restaurants except upon consent of the restaurant
  owner. Commissioner Safsten expressed concern that the 40’ length may be restrictive for both the
  tow vehicle and the trailer. Discussion continued relative to how these uses are licensed and how tax
  revenue is obtained. It was indicated these are licensed in the city where the business operates, but
  their mobility allows them to travel throughout the overall community. Tax revenue is paid to the city
  where the business is licensed, and often there is no tax benefit to the city where they operate.
  Commission also asked if verbal consent is adequate or whether a vendor would be required to obtain
  written consent from existing restaurant owners.

As there were no public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to close the public
   hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Stoker and passed unanimously.

  MOTION:       A motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to recommend approval of the proposed
                ordinance amendment based on the findings it is consistent with the General Plan and will
                be in the best interest of the interests of City, subject to food trailers and trucks to have

                                                   [10]
                the same regulations in terms of length, being prohibited from residential zones except by
                special permit and no overnight parking is allowed, and trailers are required to remain
                attached to their tow vehicle when parked in the public right-of-way. Motion was
                seconded by Commissioner Garner and passed 7-1 with Commissioners Blaisdell, Garner,
                Sandau, Schade, Southwick, Stoker and Graf voting aye and Commissioner Safsten voting
                no, stating it is not fair to existing businesses that have to pay taxes in Ogden City and
                revenue from these trucks are typically going to other communities.

12. Public Hearing Proposed Ordinance Amendment, to amend PI, NC, CBD, R-3 and R-4 areas in the East
   Central Community to prohibit painting or covering brick. (Attachment 12)

  Mr. Montgomery indicated this is a follow-up to the previous decision of the Commission relating to
  painted brick in the East Central community. This amendment is to include areas within the historic
  planning community which are not zoned residential to comply with the same standards. He reviewed
  the history of the East Central Bench Historic District, stating its intent was to provide an opportunity for
  property owners to apply for historic tax credits. In 2010 the Zoning Ordinance was amended in an
  effort to protect the historic community by establishing standards, including prohibiting the covering or
  painting of existing brick exteriors. These areas are within the community but the 2010 ordinance was
  limited to single family residential zoned properties. This amendment would include the PI, NC, CBD, R-
  3 and R-4 areas within the Central Bench community, establishing the same standards in an effort to
  preserve the historic character of the overall community. Staff recommendation is for approval of the
  amendment as proposed.

  Commissioner Blaisdell asked how enforcement occurs and what the record for compliance. Mr.
  Montgomery stated when it comes to the attention of the City that a building has been painted, Code
  Enforcement contacts the owner and informs them of the zoning violation. Upon receipt of this letter,
  the owner is give 15 days to either come into compliance or contact Code Enforcement and establish a
  timeframe for compliance. If no action occurs a civil citation is sent and the property owner is required
  to pay fines, beginning at $125, then $250 and $500. If no action occurs, the City then pursues court
  action and often liens are placed on the property. He stated about 50% of those who have received
  notice of violation have removed paint. Discussion continued relating to the removal of paint and its
  effect on the underlying brick and determination as to how it is determined that brick preservation can
  only be done by covering the surface.

  Ms. Sue Wilkerson, 2563 Jefferson Avenue stated most work is done on the weekends when no code
  enforcement personnel are available, and once completed, it is difficult and costly to remove. She felt it
  is a current trend to market homes and has no overall benefit for the community. She felt better
  education and enforcement is warranted. She expressed support of including these zones as part of
  the regulation so there is a clear standard for the community.

  Ms. Shalae Larsen, 2550 Jefferson, stated it is important to maintain the integrity of the historic fabric
  of the East Central Community. She stated most paint jobs are done by “flippers” who have no long-
  term investment in the community and the cost of upkeep is then given to the new property owner who
  is unaware of the illegal paint job. She felt there should be higher penalties for these, suggesting paint
  contractors also might be penalized. She indicated some type of permit might be established for
  exterior paint jobs.

  Mr. Montgomery responded permits are typically associated with an inspection and often are related to
  life safety, but it is conceivable that a no-fee permit might be established similar to a land use permit.
  He further explained improvements which have recently been made to the City web site to clarify there

   owners of the specific regulations of this area.
