Pre-Council, 5:30 p.m.
1. Welcome and Invocation
Mayor Johnson welcomed everyone and noted that all Councilmembers were present. Councilor
Condie gave the invocation.

2. Presentations and Reports
2.1 Discussion of Letter of Intent for Compass Pointe and Sanctuary
Marlin Eldred stated that the letter was emailed to the Council. He said that that letter of intent
will act as the framework for the development agreement. This letter is non-binding and states
that the city will negotiate in good faith. Mr. Eldred stated that the Redevelopment Agency
would still need to approve the tax increment.

2.2 Fireworks Discussion
Chief Jeremy Craft briefed the Council on his concerns with fireworks this year due to the
drought conditions. He said that his proposal to restrict some areas in the city will help mitigate
much of the larger hazards while still allowing for some fireworks in designated areas, including
some City parks. The Fire Department will also forego the early morning salutes this year. The
Council agreed to move forward with the proposal.

4. Consideration of Concept approval for the Micron Area Plan, changing the Land Use
   Map of the existing Micron Area Plan. (Tabled from the June 8th meeting.)

    Motion:     Councilor Condie moved to untable this item. Councilor Hancock seconded the
                motion.

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Yes; Councilor Hancock, Yes;
    Councilor Koivisto, Yes; and Councilor Southwick, Yes. The motion passed unanimously.

    Scott Bishop with DR Horton stated that they’ve been working with staff and have made some
    modifications. They moved the open space to the west side to create more of a buffer between
    the current residents and the active adult community zone. They also changed the triangle piece
    to open space. Mr. Bishop presented a detailed fiscal analysis with the current plans and the
    proposed plan which showed that the amenities will greatly improve. He stated that the acreage
    of open space isn’t the same, but the amenities will be much better.

    Highland.

    Brad Kenison stated that they are encouraging the east west connection in this plan, but the
    location is currently in Draper, so the city hopes to annex that also. It’s also possible that the
    property owner could grant an easement for a road. Mr. Kenison also explained how the total
    number of ERU’s were calculated, which was based on the sewer capacities.

5. Consideration of Concept approval for X Development’s 2100 North Subdivision, a 65-
   lot residential development located at approximately 2100 North 2300 West.
   Erin Towner was representing the applicant. He said that they are proposing that the private
   drives be widened to 32 feet which would allow for 28 feet of asphalt to allow for parking on
   the private drive. They are also proposing a 20-foot driveway. They are requesting 66 lots for
   density in lieu of trail improvements.

    Mr. Struthers stated that they haven’t done the calculations to determine the exact number of
    bonus lots for improvements to the trail.

    There was discussion regarding the 2100 north access from this development. The applicant
    stated that they removed the access because it may not be legally required and the neighbors
    don’t want it. Brad Kenison, Assistant City Engineer, stated that the City won’t seek the permit
    for access until the applicant shows it as part of the concept plan. Councilor Hancock stated
    that the Council has received comments both in favor and against this access. He said that it
    may relieve traffic in some area, but it could increase traffic in the neighborhood to the south.
    Mr. Struthers stated that the staff’s opinion is that the access is important for connectivity. Mr.
    Ryan Wood stated that the access is required by code, so an application should be made to
    UDOT. If UDOT says no, then the city can’t require that access. Mr. Wood also stated that the
    Council could do a development agreement that waives the connectivity requirement. Mayor
    Johnson stated that he would like to see a traffic analysis by the traffic engineer to help
    determine which scenario would be best, with or without an access to 2100 North. Mayor
    Johnson stated that there is a huge issue with the subdivision on the north of this development
    because there is not an access to 2100 North and he sees this creating that same issue.

    Mayor Johnson also stated that the applicant is requesting a 3 lot density bonus, but the
    calculations have not been done to determine if that’s the correct number.

    Jason Lethbridge stated that he was concerned with a 2100 access because of safety concerns.
    He said that the access will be used to bypass the intersection, so it may not be best for the
    neighborhood. He supports the concept as proposed.

    Angie Lethbridge stated that the proposed plan in fair, and she doesn’t want to see smaller lots.

    Skyler Beltran stated the homes are needed, and that the Council should hold the developer to
    what’s required in the development code.

    Matt stated that an access to 2100 North should be required.

    Breanne Franklin stated that she was glad to see that the developer removed the access because
    she was concerned for her children’s safety.

    The Council agreed that it was important to review the data and traffic studies to determine if
    an application for an access to 2100 North should be made.

    Motion:     Councilor Condie moved table this item and direct that a traffic study with the City
                Traffic Engineer be conducted and that the applicant work with staff to determine
                if an application to UDOT should be made for an access to 2100 North. Councilor
                Condie seconded the motion.

    There was discussion of including a timeline.

    Amended Motion: Councilor Condie clarified that if it’s determined that the access is not
            needed then the applicant does not need to go through the process of applying for a
            permit and that the applicant can return and petition for a development agreement.

6. Consideration of Ordinance #25-2021, a General Plan Amendment on approximately
   13.05 acres located at approximately 1700 West 1500 North, changing the designation
   from VLDRA (very low density residential agriculture) to VLDR (very low density
   residential).
   Steve Tripp was representing the applicant. He stated that he is applying for the zone change
   because of the other zone changes that have been made. He said that the 22,000 square foot
   lots aren’t desirable and discussed other reasons why he felt the Council should make the
   change.

    Council Koivisto stated that she would like to see what the applicant could accomplish within
    the current designation.

    The Council agreed that they would like to see this area have more of an open space feel with
    the larger lots and they were also concerned with infrastructure needs if they continue to make
    even small changes like this, it could have a negative impact overall.

    Mayor Johnson asked for public comments.

    Mike Brems stated that he is an adjacent property owner and asked that the Council not change
    the General Plan.

    Bart Peacock was also opposed to the change.

    Motion:     Councilor Koivisto moved to deny this application with the findings that increasing
                density in the VLDRA zones will take away density and utility capacity from other
                areas better suited for density such as the TOD areas along UTA’s corridors.
                Councilor Southwick seconded the motion.

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Yes; Councilor Hancock, Yes;
    Councilor Koivisto, Yes; and Councilor Southwick, Yes. The motion passed unanimously.
