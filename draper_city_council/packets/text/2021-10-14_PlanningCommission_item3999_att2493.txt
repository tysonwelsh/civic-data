Dear Scott,

The purpose of this letter is address Draper City’s concerns about the offset accesses along
Golden Harvest Road. There are two existing accesses to the American Preparatory Academy
(APA) on Golden Harvest Road.

The Arco Design build TIS showed two new accesses to the proposed development, the
westmost is approximately 80 feet to the west of the APA exit (the APA charter school has one
way counter-clockwise flow with an exit only to the west). While the accesses are supposed to
have 200 foot spacing, the TIS discussed the potential conflicts at these accesses and
determined that these were not Opposing Upstream Driveways as described in the (page 4-14
of the 2019 Draper City Transportation Master Plan). Therefore, the City Engineer has the
ability to waive the access spacing. The traffic study discussed why these accesses were not
Opposing Upstream Driveways in the TIS. “From the site plan, the proposed access is slightly
offset to the west from the existing access. This is typically not recommended, but there are
several factors in this study that make it acceptable. First, the School Access is in reality an
exit only as cars are not supposed to turn into this one-way circulation. Therefore, the school
access mostly operates as a right turn egress. Without left turn ingresses to the school and left
turn ingresses projected for the new access, the center median overlap that is caused by the
offset is not an issue.” (see Page 13, immediately below the Table Seven analysis).

Similarly, the APA Charter School’s eastmost access is offset approximately 120 feet to the
west of the proposed Arco’s eastmost access. This access combination was also determined not
to have Opposing Upstream Driveways. Per the study, “From the site plan, the proposed
access is slightly offset to the east from the existing access. This is typically not recommended,
but there are several factors in this study that make it acceptable. First, any congestion at the
existing Pony Express Center access is to the west of the access (in the morning school rush)
and therefore, it would not block the proposed access. The center median does not cause an
overlap for left turn ingresses for either access so the offset is not an issue.” (see Page 14,
immediately below the Table Eight analysis).
Based on the above discussion of how these offset accesses are oriented and that they do not
have Opposing Upstream Driveways, that the accesses spacing distances proposed in this site
plan are sufficient enough that conflicts are not an issue, and it is recommended that the
minimum access spacing can be waived to the actual distances provided in the site plan.

 I.   Introduction and Summary

 The proposed development site is located at approximately 13000 South and Pony Express Drive in
 Draper, Utah. The site has frontage along Golden Harvest Road from approximately Lone Peak Parkway
 to Pony Express Drive. Access will be on Golden Harvest Road (two proposed accesses) and Pony
 Express Drive (three proposed accesses). The current site is approximately 22 single family residential
 homes/lots. The site is planned as the home of the new EPG Pony Express Project, which includes a light
 industrial building and a furniture store.

 Trip projection and traffic analysis in this report was performed for the AM and PM peak hours.

 II. Proposed Project

 The EPG Pony Express Project at completion is conceptually planned to include the following land uses:
       • A 253,825 square foot warehouse
       • A 125,700 square foot furniture store

 III. Study Area Conditions

 A. Roadways and Intersections

 The Lone Peak Parkway and Golden Harvest Road intersection is fully developed with three lanes in each
 direction on Lone Peak Parkway and one lane in each direction on Golden Harvest Road (westbound
 traffic has a left turn pocket).

 Lone Peak Parkway has a 35 mph speed limit in this area. During the AM Peak Hour (from
 approximately 7:40 to 8:20 a.m.) a crossing guard activates a school crossing that reduces the speed limit
 to a school zone of 20 mph. Lone Peak Parkway has two lanes northbound, but one of these lanes turns
 into a northbound right turn lane (i.e., drop lane) at the intersection with Golden Harvest Road. There
 appears to be an existing striping/signing issue with this northbound “Right Lane must Turn
 Right” area as vehicles often have not merged until nearly at the intersection. This appears to be an
 immediate potential safety issue as this sudden merging has accident potential. It is recommended that
 Draper City review/modify the signing/striping plan at this intersection regardless of whether the
 proposed development is approved. UDOT Standard Drawing, ST-6 Intersection Pavement Markings
 (Mandatory Right Turn Lane Detail) has been included in the appendix to this report. It shows that
 additional signage and an 8” Lane Drop Stripe could be considered. Also included is a similar detail from
 UDOT Standard Drawing ST-11 for Bicycle Lane Pavement Markings.

 The Pony Express Drive and Golden Harvest Road intersection is also part of the study area. This
 intersection is a tee intersection (with Pony Express Drive paralleling I-15). It has one lane in each
 direction along Pony Express Drive (with room for a center median or a wide shoulder with new
 development) and one lane in each direction on Golden Harvest Road. Pony Express Drive has a speed
 limit of 40 mph. Golden Harvest Road has a 25 mph speed limit.

 Figure One shows the location of the development. Figure Two shows the conceptual site plan. The
 proposed accesses on Golden Harvest do not line up exactly with the existing access across the street. The
 report will discuss this with recommendations. There are three proposed access along Pony Express
 Drive. For simplicity, these will be analyzed as a single access point. This is worst case analysis from a
 traffic perspective.

 VI. Conclusions/Recommendations
 VII.

 The study shows that the Lone Peak Parkway and Golden Harvest Road intersection will not have a
 significant impact from the proposed development. The analysis section of this report discusses in detail
 the existing impact of the school traffic that causes some congestion for approximately 15 minutes during
 the AM Peak Hour.

 There appears to be an existing striping/signing issue with this northbound right turn at the Lone
 Peak/Golden Harvest intersection. This “Right Lane must Turn Right” area has existing conflicts as
 vehicles often have not merged until nearly at the intersection. This issue and suggested improvements are
 discussed in the introduction section of the report.

 The study shows that the Pony Express Drive and Golden Harvest Road intersection will operate at LOS
 B or better with the proposed development.

 The study shows that the American Preparatory Academy Access and Golden Harvest Road intersection
 will operate at LOS C or better with the proposed development. The analysis section of this report
 discusses why the offset of the proposed access is not an issue.

 The study shows that the Pony Express Center Access and Golden Harvest Road intersection will operate
 at LOS B or better with the proposed development. The analysis section of this report discusses why the
 offset of the proposed access is not an issue.

 The study shows that the Furniture Store Accesses and Pony Express Drive intersections will operate at
 LOS A or better with the proposed development.

Deviations requested and explanations:

DCMC Section 9-22-040(C)(2) reads “Buildings with facades one hundred feet (100') or greater in total
length shall have at least one significant facade variation from the primary wall plane whose depth is at
least five feet (5') and whose width is at least twenty feet (20'). There shall be no uninterrupted facades
one hundred feet (100') or greater in length.”

        The building design does not meet strict compliance of this code but is consistent with the intent
        of the code. On three of the four building facades (north, east, and south) there are multiple
        architectural features which provide depth and variation from the wall plane; however, these
        features are 16’ wide, and 2.5’ deep, which does not meet strict compliance with the DCMC’s

        have horizontal variation to keep open the possibility of this becoming a dock wall (future
        separate site plan submittal). We believe, however, that the architectural design of the building

        height variations occurring in some cases within 50’, and in some cases in excess of 50’. We
        believe, however, that the variance in parapet height around the building meets the general intent
