Proposal 
The applicant is requesting conditional use approval for a Major Accessory Building to be 
located at 4463 N Old Ranch Road.  The applicant proposes to use this Accessory Building for 
private entertainment purposes.      
 
According to 10-8-17 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code, this 2,338 square foot 
structure is classified as a Major Accessory Building and thus requires a Conditional Use Permit.

Background 
Based on a 2020 Settlement Agreement, this parcel is a legal lot of record and is therefore 
eligible for development.  The property is zoned Rural Residential.  In addition to the proposed 
Major Accessory Building, the applicant plans to construct a single-family dwelling, one Minor 
Accessory Building, and one Secondary Accessory Structure will be on the site.  Only the Major 
Accessory Building is dependent on this Conditional Use permit. The Planning Department is 
simultaneously processing the Low Impact Permit required for a single-family dwelling on a 
parcel outside of a subdivision.  
 
Section 10-2-10 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code contains the Chart of Allowed and 
Permitted uses.  In the Rural Residential zone, a Major Accessory Building requires a Conditional 
Use Permit subject to review and approval by the Snyderville Basin Planning Commission. The 
applicant applied for a Conditional Use Permit on October 9, 2024.  The code defines a Major 
Accessory Building as: 
  
ACCESSORY BUILDING, MAJOR: A building or structure greater than two thousand 
(2,000) square feet but not exceeding twelve thousand five hundred (12,500) square 
feet in size which is subordinate to, and the use of which is incidental to that of, the 
principal building, structure or use on the same lot. 
 
4463 N Old Ranch Road is 3.41 acres.  According to 10-8-17, a lot between 3 and 4 acres can be 
conditionally approved for one Major Accessory Building of up to 7,500 square feet.

Public Notice, Meetings and Comments  
Notice of a public hearing for this Conditional Use Permit application was published in the Park 
Record and courtesy postcards were mailed to property owners within 1,000 feet of the subject 
property.  
 
As of the date of this report, 2/19/2025, one public comment has been received and is copied 
below.   
 
Ranch Place lies directly north of Parcel PP-112.  Following the record snowfall in 2023, 
the stream that runs along Old Ranch Road jumped its banks on Parcel PP-112.  This was 
partially due to the installation of a culvert over the stream by the prior property owner 
that was undersized and installed at too high of an elevation.  In 2023, Ranch Place 
homeowners stacked hundreds of sandbags along the banks of the stream on Parcels PP-
112 and PP-112-A (with permission of property owners)…. Even so, the stream still 
jumped its banks and followed the west boundary of Osguthorpe’s farm until it reached 
Ranch Place.  The Ranch Place HOA hired a contractor to deepen a diversion ditch 
around Ranch Place to get the water to the Swaner Preserve without inundating the 
homes along Abilene Way. 
 
Photo below shows flooding west of the stream on Parcel PP-112.  All of this water 
inundated the field upstream of Ranch Place causing major flooding in numerous 
basements.  Had we not done emergency ditch work, the damage would have been 
much worse. 
 
For those living downstream, there is a reasonable expectation that upstream property 
owners take appropriate actions to keep a stream within its channel.  The prior owner 
instead worsened a bad situation by installing a culvert that was undersized and 
installed at too high an invert elevation. 
13
10 
 
 
As a condition of approval of any development on Parcel PP-112, the Ranch Place HOA 
respectfully requests that the property owner take appropriate actions to mitigate the 
risk of future flooding downstream.

DESCRIPTION:
A temporary sediment barrier consisting of entrenched filter fabric
stretched across and secured to supporting posts. 
APPLICATION: 
- Perimeter control: place barrier at down-gradient  limits of disturbance
- Sediment barrier: place barrier at toe of slope or soil stockpile
- Protection of existing waterways: place barrier at top of stream bank.
- Inlet protection: place fence surrounding catch basins
INSTALLATION/APPLICATION CRITERIA:
- Place posts 6 feet apart on center along contour (or use pre-assembled
unit) and drive 2 feet minimum into ground.  Excavate an anchor trench 
immediately up-gradient  of posts.
- Secure wire mesh (14 gage min. With 6 inch openings) to up-slope  side of 
posts. Attach with heavy duty 1 inch long wire staples, tie wires or hog rings. 
- Cut fabric to required width, unroll along length of barrier and drape over barrier.  
Secure fabric to mesh with twine, staples, or similar, with trailing edge extending 
into anchor trench. 
-Backfill trench over filter fabric to anchor. 
LIMITATIONS:
- Recommended maximum drainage area of 0.5 acres per 100 feet of fence. 
- Recommended maximum up-gradient  slope length of 150 feet
- Recommended maximum uphill grade of 2:1 (50%)
- Recommended maximum flow rate of 0.5 cfs
- Ponding should not be allowed behind fence
MAINTENANCE: 
- Inspect immediately after any rainfall and at least daily during prolonged rainfall. 
- Look for runoff bypassing ends of barriers or undercutting barriers. 
- Repair or replace damaged areas of the barrier and remove accumulated   
  sediment. 
- Re-anchor fence as necessary to prevent shortcutting. 
- Remove accumulated sediment when it reaches 1/2 the height of the fence.

DESCRIPTION:
Temporary sediment barrier consisting of a row of entrenched and 
anchored straw bales.
APPLICATION:
- Perimeter Control: place barrier at down-gradient  limits of 
  disturbance. 
- Sediment barrier: place barrier at toe of slope or soil stockpile
- Protection of existing waterways: place barrier at top of stream bank.
- Inlet Protection.
INSTALLATION/APPLICATION CRITERIA:
- Excavate a 4-inch minimum deep trench along contour line, i.e. 
  parallel to slope, removing all grass and other material that may 
  allow underflow. 
- Place bales in trench with ends tightly abutting, fill any gaps by 
  wedging loose straw into openings.
- Anchor each bale with 2 stakes driven flush with the top of the bale. 
- Backfill around bale and compact to prevent piping, backfill on uphill 
  side to be built up 4-inches above ground at the barrier.
LIMITATIONS: 
- Recommended maximum area of 0.5 acre per 100 feet of barrier
- Recommended maximum up-gradient  slope length of 150 feet.
- Recommended maximum uphill grade of 2:1 (50%)
MAINTENANCE: 
- Inspect immediately after any rainfall and at least daily during 
  prolonged rainfall.
- Look for runoff bypassing ends of barriers or undercutting barriers.
- Repair or replace damaged areas of the barrier and remove 
  accumulated sediment.  
- Realign bales as necessary to provide continuous barrier and fill 
  gaps. 
- Re-compact soil around barrier as necessary to prevent piping.

The applicant requests a subdivision plat amendment to increase the size of the building 
envelope on Lot 3 of the Ecker Hills Plat C Subdivision, located at 3356 Buckboard Drive. The 
requested amendment would increase the size of the building envelope by 1,129 sf to a total of 
4,770 sf. The requested amendment would provide space for a future addition to the single-
family home on the property and would also bring previous grading that was done outside of 
the building envelope into compliance.

Background 
The Pinebrook Consent Agreement (Agreement) was recorded in 1993 and approved residential 
development in the Ecker Hills area. Exhibit B-1 of the Agreement depicts the general layout of 
residential subdivisions, identifies steep slopes where applicable, and generally outlines 
acceptable building envelopes (Exhibit E in this Staff Report). Schedule 1 Section III of the 
Agreement suggests that building envelopes were included to ensure that development occurs 
on the least sensitive portion a site, quoted here:  
 
“III. Critical Areas. The specific plan shall prohibit development in critical areas as shown 
on Exhibits B, B-1 and C and as defined in Section 5.3 of the 1993 Snyderville Basin 
Development Code, or as amended. 
A. Slopes. Development shall be prohibited in areas which include slopes 
exceeding 30%, except where recommended by the Planning Commission and 
approved by the Board of County Commissioners and as indicated on Exhibit 
B-1.”  
 
The recordation of the Ecker Hills Plat C Subdivision (the subdivision) in 1995 created twelve 
residential lots with defined building envelopes based on Exhibit B-1 of the Agreement. The 
purpose of this application is to review an amendment to increase the size of the building 
envelope on Lot 3 of the subdivision. Building envelopes are meant to contain all construction 
and construction disturbances except for driveways, so adequate space within the envelope 
should be available for any development, grading, and construction staging.   
 
According to the original plat, the building envelope on Lot 3 was 3,263 sf, which was the 
smallest in the subdivision (Exhibit D). In 2014, the first amendment to Lot 3 was approved, 
which increased the size of the building envelope to accommodate space for a covered patio 
attached to the home (Exhibit C). When the 2014 plat amendment was reviewed, Planning Staff 
and the Snyderville Basin Planning Commission (SBPC) learned that grading occurred outside of 
the building envelope, but the extent to which was unclear. Neither a slope analysis, site plan 
with natural grade topography, nor evidence of the extent of the grading disturbances were 
submitted with the application.  
  
The 2014 owners created two options for the SBPC to consider: the first only included the space 
necessary for the expansion, and the second included a much larger area. According to meeting 
minutes, because of the lack of detail the SBPC was only comfortable approving the smaller 
proposal (Exhibit D). The approved 2014 amendment increased the envelope by 378 sf for a 
total of 3,641 sf, which is its current size.  
 
The property now has new owners, who again want to expand the building envelope to create 
space for an addition to the home. The addition is not subject to this review, however, the 
proposal would increase the size of the building envelope by 1,129 sf for a total of 4,770 sf. This 
proposed amendment would incorporate all the land area that was inappropriately disturbed 
into the envelope, which would bring the property into compliance with the Agreement and 
49


Ecker Hills Plat C Subdivision. The proposed second amendment would provide adequate space 
for an addition to the home.   
 
This request is consistent with the larger proposal submitted to the SBPC in 2014, which 
proposed a 4,867 square foot envelope. An envelope of this size is consistent with other lots in 
the subdivision as the largest is 13,955 sf. 
 
According to the topographic survey and steep slope analysis submitted with this application, 
the proposed expansion will not encroach into any slopes in excess of 30%, however, that 
survey only reflects the current conditions on the Lot. Staff cannot find documentation of the 
natural grade topography prior to any development. The only evidence of steep slopes are 
included in Exhibit B-1 of the Agreement, which only generally identifies steep areas. 
Additionally, the original surveying company that prepared the first plat amendment no longer 
has the materials on file. The topographic survey and slope analysis submitted with this 
application (Exhibit G) are the best evidence available as to the natural grade on the property.   
 
The Agreement establishes that subdivision plat amendments follow the review processes and 
criteria in the Code. It also identifies additional requirements that any development occurring in 
slopes in excess of 30% must first be approved by the SBPC and then the County Manager. 
Because the proposal does not encroach into steep slopes, the SBPC is the final land use 
authority for this subdivision plat amendment.  
 
Analysis and Findings 
Section 10-3-18 (I) of the Snyderville Basin Development Code sets the following grounds for 
vacating or changing a plat: 
 
Standard 1: If the county manager or land use authority is satisfied that neither the public nor 
any person will be materially injured by the proposed vacation, alteration or amendment, and 
that there is good cause for the vacation, alteration, or amendment, the county manager or 
land use authority may vacate, alter or amend the plat, any portion of the plat, or any road or 
lot. 
  
Analysis: The requested amendment would allow future construction on the property to 
occur in previously disturbed areas that are compatible with the existing topography. 
The amended building envelope would also correct noncompliant grading that is 
inconsistent with the purpose of a building envelope. Staff and service providers 
reviewed the proposal and raised no concerns regarding the proposal. Staff finds no 
evidence that the public nor any person would be materially injured by the amendment. 
If future construction is pursued, the Building and Engineering Departments will review 
the proposal to verify that the construction is safe and structurally sound. COMPLIES 
 
Standard 2: No plat amendment shall be approved which results in an increase in density.


 
Analysis: Density is defined in the Code as, “The number of dwelling units per acre with 
respect to residential land uses.” The applicant is requesting to amend the location of a 
building envelope on an existing residential lot. The amendment would not result in an 
additional residential unit. COMPLIES

20, 2025, no public comment has been received.
