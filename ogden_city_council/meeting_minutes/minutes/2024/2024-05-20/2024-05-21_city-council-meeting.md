Proposed Ordinance 2024-14 adopting the proposed Tentative Budget of
Ogden City for Fiscal Year 2024-2025
           A memo from Council staff regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 tentative budget came before the
Council for consideration. The memo stated on May 7, 2024, Mayor Benjamin K. Nadolski presented his proposed FY 2024-2025
budget. The proposal includes an estimated 1% increase to property taxes which would result in an increase of $183,000 to
property tax revenue. The City Council must approve an increase to the certified tax rate and provide notice to the public in
accordance with the Truth in Taxation requirements. The Truth in Taxation hearing is scheduled for August 6, 2024. Additionally,
the proposal would transfer $1.8 million (12 percent of $15 million) from Business Depot Ogden (BDO) Lease Revenue to the
General Fund to supplement General Fund wages and benefits. The City Council must approve an ordinance amendment to allow
for this transfer from BDO Lease Revenue. The proposal includes an automatic four percent increase to the base rate and tiers for
all water uses for a five-year period in addition to the approved blended inflation adjustment (annual CCI and CPI adjustment) to
fund the 36” Canyon Waterline replacement. The City Council must approve an ordinance increasing the water rates. There are
two changes proposed as Schedule A changes so far. The first is an adjustment for the addition of the new Service Writer/Stores
Clerk position. This was not included in the proposed budget. As proposed, an additional $60,400 would be used from Fund
Balance as well as a reduction in Part Time Wages of $23,250 to total the $83,650 needed for the position. The second change is
specific to the final Entitlement Grant numbers received from the Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) for the
Annual Action Plan. The Council adopted an updated Annual Action Plan on May 7. The corresponding changes are included in
this proposed Schedule A change. The adjustments include a total reduction of $41,275 in the Major Grants section of the budget.
includes sixteen (16) new positions across all funds. Relative to staffing of the City, there are ten (10) positions proposed from the
General Fund, one (1) position proposed from the Medical Enterprise Fund, one (1) position from the Fleet and Facilities Internal
Services Fund, and one (1) new position to staff the Parking and Mobility Enterprise Fund. In addition to the new positions
proposed, there are three (3) proposed reclassifications and three (3) proposed title changes. The City is in the process of finalizing
an updated compensation study. As a result of not receiving the study in time for the budget presentation, Administration is
proposing to hold off on any proposed compensation changes and salary schedule changes until the study is complete. The one
exception to this is an adjustment for Justice Court judges whose salary ranges are set by the state. It is anticipated that the study
and the associated recommendations will be available for the Council’s consideration of the final budget in August. While no
specific changes are proposed, the Mayor’s budget has included compensation changes and additional funding in each
department's budget. The total amount set aside for the anticipated compensation changes is $4,657,950. This includes $3,131,275
for the General Fund and $1,526,675 for all other funds. Once a final proposal can be presented, changes within each department’s
budget will be included in Schedule A-1 changes with the final budget. Finally, the proposed Fiscal Year 2025-2025 Budget
includes funding for 42 capital projects. This includes two RAMP projects, 12 one-time projects, and 28 perpetual or ongoing
projects. A total of $4,347,850 is proposed to be transferred from BDO Lease Revenue to fund capital projects through the Capital
Improvement Fund. An additional $390,000 for golf course irrigation upgrades, $150,000 for Critical Project Contingency, and
$100,000 for Airport projects is proposed to be transferred from BDO Lease Revenue for capital projects as well. There is a total
of $20,938,550 budgeted from other sources for capital projects. This includes Class B & C Road Funds, Active Transportation
Funds, grants, and utility enterprise funding.
           Council Deputy Director Symes summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide Council

Public Comments
          Kerry Wayne stated he lives on 1200 West in Marriott-Slaterville, which is often referred to as Ogden’s Waterfront,
Boyer Beach, and Mosquito Coast; he and his neighbors have been asking for over two years for the City to do something to
address the water problems on 1200 West and today is the first day in eight months that there has not been standing water in the
location. There was an article in the Standard-Examiner over a year ago in which former Chief Administrative Officer Johnson
was quoted as saying that the problem was being fixed, but still nothing has been done. He has spoken with Mayor Nadolski about
the issue, but Mayor Nadolski is not happy with him and has asked him to no longer contact his staff and to take all of his
complaints to Marriott-Slaterville City. However, he has found that Ogden City is no longer communicating with Marriott-
Slaterville and that is problematic. Marriott-Slaterville is planning to improve 1200 West with expansion to occur on the Business
Depot Ogden (BDO) side of the road, so Ogden City needs to be involved. He is only asking that the City be understanding of
what the residents on 1200 West have lived through. The Boyer Company created the problem, and the water will no longer drain
in the area because incorrect materials were used; he will continue to call each time there is standing water and he implored all
parties to work together.
          Teresa Bramwell stated that she came to finish the story she started last week about ‘the gatekeeper’. She does not have a
great deal of experience with government, but she feels the way she has been treated by Ogden City is a violation of her
constitutional rights. She cited the Fourth Amendment of the United States Constitution and noted that she feels a ‘gatekeeper’ in
Ogden is keeping people from accomplishing anything with their property in order for others to come in and purchase the property
and profit from it. The first experience she had with the gatekeeper was when he was employed by the Police Department; the
second time, he came to her home as a representative of the Fire Department following a fire at her home. He entered her home
and took her electric meter with him, and she has never been informed of how to retrieve it or what became of it. She then
received a notice from the Code Services Division about the lack of a notice of vacancy on her home following the fire. She came
to the City and found the gatekeeper was now the ‘boss’ of the Code Services Division.
          Heath Satow stated he is disturbed to hear that the current Administration is not treating the residents of Marriott-
Slaterville any better than the previous Administration did; the City is making a great deal of money from BDO and some of that
should be used to make those residents whole. He then addressed the City’s budgetary process, citing a general fund increase of
eight percent when compared to last year. He noted that this does not include any increase related to the large waterline project in
Ogden Canyon. He has seen similar increases that outpace population growth, but he is not seeing significant efforts to reduce
costs or redundancies. The Administration is making sure that all City employees are receiving pay increases to offset the impact
of inflation, but residents are not receiving the same consideration. He urged the City to enlist an outside consultant to review the
budget and determine if any budget reductions could be made.
