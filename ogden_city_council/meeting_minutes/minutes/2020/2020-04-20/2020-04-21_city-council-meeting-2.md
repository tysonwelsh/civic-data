Proposed Joint Resolution 2020-2 of the City Council and Mayor Caldwell
establishing governing financial principles relating to general financial
management, user fees, capital improvements, debt, investments and
reserves
         A memo from Council staff came before the Council to consider a joint resolution establishing financial principles for
Fiscal Year (FY) 2021. The memo stated each year the Council and Mayor jointly adopt financial principles to guide and direct
budgetary and financial management decisions throughout the fiscal year. Prior to FY2015, the principles were adopted with the
budget. Beginning in FY2015, the Council began adopting the principles prior to receiving the Mayor's budget as way to provide
additional guidance during preparation of the Mayor's proposed budget. The current proposal is to have the Council and Mayor
adopt the proposed financial principles for the 2021 fiscal year; no significant changes are proposed this year.
         Chair Choberka introduced in writing proposed Joint Resolution 2020-2, entitled:
                  "A joint resolution of the Ogden City Council and Mayor Michael P. Caldwell establishing governing financial
                  principles relating to general financial management, user fees, capital improvements, debt, investments, and
                  reserves."

2019 to June 30, 2020 by $778,175 in anticipated revenues and
appropriations with a gross adjustment of $778,175
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider
amendments to the Fiscal Year (FY) 2016-2020 Consolidated Plan (ConPlan). The memo stated as a requirement to receive
federal funding from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), each year the City's Community and
Economic Development Department prepares and submits to the Council what is known as an Annual Action Plan. The Annual
Action Plan (AAP) is used to outline how federal Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) funds, HOME funds, and other
federal and non-federal program funds will be used by the City in the upcoming fiscal year. When the plan is submitted to HUD in
May of each year, specific dollar amounts for entitlement grant funds, program income funds, and carry-over funds are estimated
for the next fiscal year. Part way through the year, after the specific dollar amounts are known or when program income from
loans and other programs are received, an amendment must be made to the plan to update the Annual Action Plan budget. Often
when the mid-year amendment is proposed, changes in funding levels within certain programs and line items are proposed as well.
The amendments proposed with this item are related to the increased funding to help with the COVID-19 related economic
conditions. The proposed additional funding for this and other related programs is a combination of shifting funds between
programs and additional program income received throughout the 2020 fiscal year. This is the second amendment to the FY20
Annual Action Plan. The FY2016-2020 Consolidated Plan is proposed to be updated as well to include the Urgent Need National
Objective certification.
          An additional memo from the City Comptroller regarding proposed amendments to the Fiscal Year (FY) 2020 budget
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated this budget amendment is related to the second Annual Action Plan
(AAP) mid-year budget amendment for FY 2020. This action amends the City's budget to reflect the changes proposed in the
Annual Action Plan budget submitted to the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). The proposed budget
amendments total $778,175.
          Community Development Manager Ogden summarized the memo and stated the purpose of the proposed Annual Action
Plan FY 2020 (AAP FY20) Amendment #2 is to update Program Income estimates for the current year. Amendment #1 was
adopted on December 17, 2019 with the purpose of updating the budget to reflect accurate carry-over amounts and updated
Program Income estimates. Since that date, additional Program Income above the Amendment #1 estimates has come forward,
and these amounts must be included in the current budget to allow for current year expenditures. This reflects an increased
velocity and amplitude of investment in housing and business development projects. A second purpose is to introduce the CDBG
Urgent Need National Objective into the current Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) and Action Plan. We must meet one of three
national objectives when we use our CDBG funds - Low-Moderate Income Benefit, Alleviation of Slums and Blight, or Urgent
Need. The Urgent Need objective is only allowed under declared emergencies. The current COVID-19 pandemic has been
detennined by HUD to meet this objective. In order to use this objective, we must include it in our Consolidated Plan and our
Action Plan. The advantage of this objective is added flexibility in documenting the creation or retention of jobs for businesses
impacted by this crisis. This will allow businesses to borrow funds from our existing business loan programs and not be required
to document incomes of beneficiaries, while still meeting the other HUD requirements. This makes funds more accessible and
broadly available to impacted businesses. Mr. Ogden then summarized the proposed AAP FY20 Amendment #2 budget changes:
               •    Increase the budget $778,188 to recognize new Program Income and updated Pl estimates;
               •    Re-allocate $250,000 CDBG from Target Area Public Improvements and $100,000 CDBG from Infill
                    Housing to Small Business Loan Program;
               •    $156,635 increase to Special Econ Dev Projects to budget Pl from business loan payoffs;
               •    $446,678 increase to Quality Neighborhoods from homes sold and repaying CDBG and HOME ;
               • $54,674 increase to HELP program to budget PI from HELP loan payoffs; and
               •    $120,201 increase to CDBG and HOME Administration
          Additional Program Income funds have been prograimned to complete viable projects and programs for the period July 1,
2019 to June 30, 2020. Mr. Ogden concluded Administration is recmmnending that this public hearing be continued until next
week; an additional amendment to the Citizen Participation Plan (CPP) is needed and it would be appropriate for the Council to
act on all three items at the same time. Proposed changes to the CPP include reducing the public comment period from 30 days to
five days for substantial amendments; allowing virtual meetings instead of on-site in-person meetings; and making ConPlan
documents available online instead of at the library and other depositories. These amendments are responsive to the COVID-19
pandemic; the City is trying to make funds available to assist local businesses with their cashflow needs.
          Comptroller Stout then summarized the memo regarding the companion budget amendment; this amendment is based on
the current Major Grant Fund budget, less items budgeted in the Major Grants Fund, not included in the AAP, compared to the
amended action plan budget.

Proposed Ordinance 2020-13 amending the Fiscal Year July 1, 2019 to
June 30, 2020 by a total of $2,147,800 for Capital Improvement Plan
Project FI032 - Seismic Retrofit and Remodel of Fire Station 4
           A memo from the Management Services Department regarding a proposed Fiscal Year (FY) budget amendment for
seismic retrofit and remodel of Fire Station #4 came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposal is to
transfer $1,073,900 from Medical Services to the Fire Station #4 Capital Improvement fund. This project received $1.4 million in
FY2019. However, project bids were significantly higher than anticipated and an additional appropriation is needed to complete
this project.
           Comptroller Stout summarized the memo and stated the funds needed to increase the project budget are available in the
Medical Services Fund; once the transfer is made, the balance in that fund will be approximately $1.3 million.
           Council member Lopez asked why the Medical Services Fund has such a significant balance. Ms. Stout stated that fund is
an enterprise fund that receives revenue generated by ambulance services; the charges for those services are deposited into the
fund to support ambulance operations and fund balance results when charges for services exceed the expenditures from the fund.
Total ambulance billing in a year is approximately $10 million, but net revenue is approximately $6 million, so a fund balance of
$1.2 million is not too significant. Council member Lopez stated that his question is rooted in concern that ambulance charges
may be too high if the balance in this fund is $1.3 million. He has heard of some citizens not calling for paramedic service because
it is too expensive to go to the hospital in an ambulance. He wondered if it would be appropriate to have a discussion about that
matter. Fire Chief Mathieu explained ambulance rates are set by the State Bureau of Emergency Medical Services (EMS); this is
one of the few areas in medical services that are regulated by the State. Rates are based upon input, data, and contractual
obligations. He then provided an overview of the billing system and indicated that unfortunately one of the reasons medical
services are expensive is because those that can pay are paying for those that cannot. The City's overall gross to net collection rate
is 3 9 percent. The City also has a generous relief policy that gives individuals the ability to apply for waiver or reduction of
charges for ambulance services. He concluded that revenues are reinvested in facilities and equipment. Council member Lopez
thanked Chief Mathieu for that explanation.
           Council member Stephens stated that he appreciates the Chief's response to the management audit completed for his
Department in 2007; this project is very important for providing service to the community.
