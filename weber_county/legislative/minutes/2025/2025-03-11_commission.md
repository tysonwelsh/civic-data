F. PRESENTATIONS:
   1. Recognition of Duncan Olsen for earning his Certified Venue Executive (CVE) designation.
      Marty Smith (Culture, Parks, & Recreation Director): The International Association of Venue Managers
      represents public assembly venues from around the globe. IAVM's nearly 8,000 active members include
      managers and Senior Executives from auditoriums, arenas, convention centers, exhibit halls, stadiums,
      performing art centers, university complexes, amphitheaters, and fairgrounds worldwide. IAVM's mission is
      to educate, advocate for, and inspire public assembly venue professionals worldwide. The Certified Venue
      Executive is an elite credential of the IAVM association and is highly regarded by industry professionals.
      An individual with a CVE designation possesses significant years of experience in the industry in addition to
      holding a top tiered position within a venue or organization. Earning your CVE acknowledges you as well
      versed in all areas of venue management, invested in your own professional growth and the growth of others,
      and dedicated to the advancement of the venue industry as a whole. I just wanted to explain a little bit the
      process to becoming a Certified Venue Executive; it's not an easy task. Before you can ever apply, to start
      the process you must acquire points through the association. The way you acquire those points or garner
      points is through serving in one of the top two leadership roles at a venue for a certain amount of time,
      attending Venue Management School, other educational tracks, serving on boards, volunteer hours, and
      numerous ways to gain points. Once you've earned enough points you can make an application to start the
      testing process. If you are accepted, your first step is to write an essay. That essay contains numerous
      questions regarding subjects within the venue management industry. If that essay is accepted, you're then
      invited to sit for a 250 question test that takes many hours to accomplish. If you pass the test, you have to go
      for an oral exam before a panel of Industry experts. Once you pass that you're given the Certified Venue
         Executive. We're proud to announce that our very own general manager of the Golden Spike Event Center,
         Duncan Olsen, has earned his Certified Venue Executive and is the 15th in Utah to receive it.

         Duncan Olsen: I want to thank Marty (Smith) and Todd (Ferrario) for supporting me and Jennifer Graham
         who pushed for me to start the process. I didn't really want to do it but as I got into it I kind of fell in love
         with gaining the knowledge and understanding of not just my venue but all venues throughout the county as
         well as the world. I've built some really good, really cool relationships with people and have learned a ton. I
         could not have done this if it wasn't for the support from my leadership as well as my team back at the
         Fairgrounds; they do such a great job and make me look far better than I am. And I appreciate the
         Commissioners for the support as well. Thank you.

         Commissioner Harvey: This is not something you can go and do over a summer. Duncan has been working
         on this for 8 years. He joins Cassie and Marty who have earned their CVE and Jay who is working on his.
         We have great efficiency in our facilities in Weber County.
