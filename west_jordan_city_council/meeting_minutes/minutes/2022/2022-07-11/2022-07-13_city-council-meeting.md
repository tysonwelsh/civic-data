 6. REPORTS TO COUNCIL
         a. Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District Report and Public Comment Period
            Jordan Valley Water Conversancy District Board Member Lyle Summers explained that a
            growing service area and increasing water demands required additional water supplies
            and infrastructure, costing millions of dollars. He presented a proposed property tax
            increase for water service purposes (annual increase of $6.96 for a $550,000 residence).
            The additional property tax revenue would support treatment plant upgrades and
            expansion; two new storage reservoirs; new wells and groundwater development; new
            booster pump station; and new debt/bonds to be issued.

             A public hearing was scheduled for August 10, 2022 at the Jordan Valley Water
             Conservancy District Administration Building. Bart Forsythe with JVWCD reported the
             current certified tax rate was .000296, and the proposed certified tax rate would be
             .000319.

             Chair Whitelock opened the public comment period at 7:17 pm.

         c. Code Amendment – Grave Decorations, Markers and Monuments
            Cemetery Sexton Kelly Dolar oriented the Council regarding proposed amendments to
            City Code Title 8, Chapter 14, Section 7 regarding Grave Decorations and Section 8
            regarding Markers and Monuments. He said the proposed amendments were intended to
            bring clarity to expectations of family members.

            Mr. Dolar answered questions from the City Council. Chair Whitelock suggested the Mayor
            should be included in the language granting the Chief Administrative Officer or designee
            the ability to grant an exception for the height of a burial marker. Mr. Lee responded that
            the designation of the Administrative Officer was a carry-over from the change in
City Council Meeting Minutes – July 13, 2022                                             Page 4
            government and could be changed. Vice Chair Green said he would prefer to leave the
            right to grant that exception to the Mayor.
            Chair Whitelock said she agreed.

            Chair Whitelock opened a public hearing at 7:32 pm

            Comments:
            None

            Chair Whitelock closed the public hearing at 7:33 pm

            Council Member McConnehey said he would like to see accommodation for certain types
            of benches. Mr. Dolar said he recently saw a creative way to get around the no-bench rule
            with something similar to a bench that met the ordinance. Council Member McConnehey
            said he did not want individuals to need to be creative and circumvent Code to have a
            bench.

            Chair Whitelock commented that time-of-need at the cemetery was very emotional. She
            expressed the opinion that the Code had many limitations she did not see in practice at
            other cemeteries. Chair Whitelock expressed concern that the Code was too restrictive
            and took the personal element out of something that was very personal. Mr. Dolar said he
            did read through ordinances for other cemeteries in the Salt Lake Valley when putting
            together the proposed amendments, and said the proposed language was not restrictive
            compared to some. He commented that the City did not have the resources to clean
            decorations off grass. Decorations would be allowed on headstones, but not on grass.

            Council Member McConnehey said his father was interred at the West Jordan Cemetery
            four years ago, and his mother had commented on how much better and tidier the
            cemetery had looked recently. However, he listed several decorations they had put out in
            the past that would not be allowed with the proposed amendments. He said he wanted to
            be careful not to negatively impact those with limited financial means who wanted to keep
            the memory of a loved one alive and found a way to do so that could be kept orderly.
            Council Member McConnehey said he believed the proposed language was a good start.

            Council Member Bloom said she sought out and visited cemeteries across the country as
            a place of peace and solitude. She said she was able to understand the staff perspective,
            and agreed the proposed language was a good start, but hoped more flexibility could be
            found to balance maintenance needs with needs of the public. She said she would want to
            include the ability to install benches.

            Mr. Dolar commented that under the current ordinance, the only approved decorations
            were flowers and flags. Decorations were cleaned up every Tuesday. He emphasized that
            decorations needed to be confined to headstones to allow regular maintenance of the
            lawns.

            Vice Chair Green commented that the Council needed to decide if the “sole discretion”
            referred to in Lines 19 and 20 should be assigned to the Sexton or to staff. He pointed out
            that “sexton” should uniformly be either capitalized or lower-case throughout the
City Council Meeting Minutes – July 13, 2022                                         Page 5
            document. He expressed a desire to include language specifying that VA emblems would
            be allowed to be affixed to headstones.

            Chair Whitelock summarized that four or more Council Members indicated support for
            allowing benches, and for allowing VA emblems. Mr. Anderson said he also heard a desire
            to provide more flexibility for individuals to be able to memorialize loved ones.

            MOTION:        Council Member McConnehey moved to TABLE Ordinance No. 22-30
