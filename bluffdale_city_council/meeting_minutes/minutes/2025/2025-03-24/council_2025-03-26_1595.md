       4.3     Community Garden Report(Presenter,Emily SwansonX

Community Garden Chair, Emily Swanson reported that registration has begun for the garden's
seventh season. Out of84 boxes, only 10 remain open for the year. Eight are community boxes
with flowers, strawberries, mint, herbs, squash, and a sensory box. Residents pay $30 per box for
individual boxes and agree to garden organically, complete a community job, weed in and around
their box weekly, not grow squash or mint family plants, plant by June 1, and clean the box by
October 18 each year. Ms. Swanson appreciates the Recreation Department taking care of the
registrations now,as she believes it provides a better experience for participants.

The Community Garden encourages square-foot gardening methods. A drip irrigation system is
provided. Gardeners are encouraged to fill their four-by-twelve-foot boxes with plants and rotate
crops throughout the season. Due to high turnover, most participants are new to gardening, so
there is always a need for education.


Ms. Swanson reported that the Community Garden is located on Heritage Crest Drive in
Independence, and most participating gardeners live in Independence. It was started in 2019 on
land owned by the Jordan Valley Water District and leased to the City for $20 for 20 years. In its
first year,24 boxes were built, and they had a total of13 gardeners. The first season was successful,
but they had a squash bug infestation and issues with low water pressure.

In 2020, Carl Harris from Draper approached the City about building a gazebo. The structure was
dedicated to Melanie Harris' brother, who died of cancer and left money for Mr. Harris to build
community and humanitarian projects. The City built steps that year and added a second tier with
20 more boxes.

        7.1    Storm Drain Presentation. (StaffPresenter,Shane Paddock\

Public Works Director, Shane Paddock provided a presentation on Bluffdale's storm drain system
and reported that the City provides information to educate residents on the importance of paying
attention to what they put into storm drains, including lawn clippings and leaves. Everything that
is left on the asphalt will go into the storm drains.


The Environmental Protection Agency ("EPA") and Utah Department of Environmental Quality
regulate stormwater management systems and require municipalities to have a Stormwater
Management Plan that is renewed every five years.


Regulations include:

     • Utah Pollutant Discharge Elimination System("UPDES")permits
     • Outfalls, which are sampled monthly
     • Pollution controls

     • Maintenance requirements


The Streets Department handles all stormwater system maintenance. Storm drain inlets often fill
with leaves in the fall. Ifthey are not cleaned up,they go into the stormwater system. Occasionally
illicit spills like concrete also go into the storm drains, and those items must be cleaned out. They
currently manage 179 storm drain basins, almost 4,200 storm drain boxes, and over 4,000 storm
drain pipes. They have been unable to complete full maintenance on the system due to a lack of
equipment or facilities. There are currently six Streets Department employees, but 14 employees
would be needed to complete all needed tasks, five of whom would be dedicated to storm drain
maintenance.


They hope to move into the new Public Works facility in June 2025, which will help with
maintenance. It has a Vactor discharge station, which is a dewatering station to separate oils and

        7.3     Budget Discussion (StaffPresenters,Stephanie Thaver, and Bruce Kartchner\

Finance Director, Stephanie Thayer provided an overview of the budgeting process. Municipal
budgets serve a different purpose than business budgets. In business, the budget is a goal, which
may or may not be achieved. In government, the expenditures side of the budget is called
"appropriations" and is the maximum amount the City is allowed to spend. It is against State law
to spend more than the budget allows. Spending is controlled through the budget and the City's
procurement policy. The budget amount is the maximum that can be spent, and sometimes the
City spends less than expected for a variety ofreasons. For example,the Fire Department expects
to have fiill shifts at each station daily, but for a time those positions were not filled. That resulted
in an unspent budgeted amount.


Revenue projections are estimates that can be affected by things like consumer spending and the
economy. The City uses a conservative approach to revenue projections. Their objective is to be
fiscally responsible and financially sustainable while meeting Bluffdale's current and future needs.

By State law, the budget must be balanced; revenues must equal expenditures. Balancing must
include an unappropriated fund balance between 5% and 35% ofthe final revenue totals.


Each year, they budget the City's restrained resources by breaking income and expenses into
different funds for different purposes.


The General Fund covers most City services and operating expenses. As a service organization,
the bulk of expenditures are in personnel costs.

The Water Fund operates like a business, and as such it includes depreciation. No other funds
include depreciation.


Restricted funds are set up to collect and expend money for very specific things. The money in
these funds cannot be spent on anything else.


   • Police and Fire Protection Fund: Revenues are generated by the Public Safety Fee, grant
       monies, and 100% of property tax revenue.
   • Capital Funds Projects
   •   Roads Fund

   • Impact Fees
   • Redevelopment Agency Funds


Different types ofexpenditures include primary services like street cleaning and maintaining parks
and discretionary services that may or may not happen as funds allow,each of which can be either
recurring or nonrecurring. Examples of recurring primary services are cleaning park restrooms
and garbage pickup. An example ofa nonrecurring primary expense is the cleanup ofParry Farms
Park after it flooded.


Recurring revenues include property and sales taxes. Nonrecurring revenues include Building
Permits, development fees, and grants. Stability is also considered. Property taxes are the City's
most stable revenue stream. Sales and franchise taxes related to energy costs are slightly less
stable. Sales tax has been a stable form ofrevenue for the last few years, but during the economic
downturn of 2008 through 2010 it dropped significantly.           The least stable revenues are
development-related fees, which can be very volatile. Assistant City Manager, Bruce Kartchner
indicated that the less stable revenue and nonrecurring expenses do not always match the fiscal
year. It can be challenging when expenditures are needed in a certain fund, but the associated
revenue occurred in the previous year or will occur in a future year.

Ms. Thayer noted that matching types of expenditures and revenues is very important to a
structurally balanced budget. Not only should recurring expenditures be funded with recurring
revenues, but the most critical recurring expenditures should be funded with the most stable
recurring revenue. It is up to Staff and the City Council to balance the different needs and wants
of Bluffdale's citizens.


The budgeting process begins by inputting requests from all department heads. Major projects are
then considered, including associated carryover costs to other fiscal years. Prior year data is
reviewed, as well as current events and trends, and that information is used to project revenues.
Using a budget model, projected revenues and expenditures are compiled and balanced. When
expenses are higher than revenues, adjustments must be made. The budget is then brought to the
City Council for review. After approval of the Tentative Budget, the City Council will continue
to review and discuss it until the Final Budget is adopted.


A Budget Review Committee was started in 2024, and they will meet in mid-April to discuss the
Draft Budget. A public hearing will be required prior to passing the Tentative Budget, and the
Final Budget must be passed at a public hearing in June. Adjustments and amendments are made
throughout the year to account for actual revenues and unexpected expenses.

Underlying principles used in the budget process are:

   • A long-term view for sustainable financial stability.
   • Maintain adequate, stable reserves.
   •   Provide services and service levels residents need and want.

   •   Prioritize costs.

   • Match revenue t)q5es to cost types.
   • Restricted funds cannot be used to pay for general operating expenses.

Truth in Taxation requires that when property values rise, property tax rates must fall. The adjusted
tax rate for the year is called the Certified Tax Rate ("CTR"), which provides the City with the
same amount of property tax revenue it received in the previous tax year plus any generated by
additional growth; for example, new homes and businesses. Tax rates are determined by the Utah
State Auditor's Office. Mr. Reid added that the rate cannot be adjusted by anyone in the City
without a Truth in Taxation hearing. Based on property values, keeping the CTR the same could
indicate a tax increase or decrease even though the number is the same.


In response to a question from Mayor Hall, Ms. Thayer confirmed that the State does not consider
inflation when setting the CTR. The only way to keep up with inflation is for the City to adjust
the rate, which requires a Truth in Taxation hearing. Mr. Kartchner stated that the Utah Taxpayers
Association, which is a taxation watchdog group, recommends that cities increase their tax rate
every three to five years to keep up with inflation.


Mr. Reid stated that the CTR was specifically set so that the City could make required bond
payments, and bond payments do not change. However,20 years later, $1 million is not worth as
much as it was when the rate was set. That is why the Utah Taxpayers Association recommends
regular increases every three to five years. Bluffdale has had three or four property tax increases
since its incorporation in 1979. The CTR was passed in 1985.


Bluffdale receives approximately 10% of property tax revenue, and any increase would only be to
that 10% of revenues. Nearby cities like Riverton and Herriman have the same tax rate for the
school district, mosquito abatement. Salt Lake County,etc. as Bluffdale. A $800,000 home would
create the same amount ofproperty tax for all agencies except the city or any public safety district.
Mr. Kartchner added that the county library system's CTR is approximately one-half that of
Bluffdale.


Ms. Thayer provided information on the City's historical CTR, which indicates that the rate has
declined over the past five years. Rate increases occurred in 2017 and 2019. The 2025 rate will
be announced in June.
