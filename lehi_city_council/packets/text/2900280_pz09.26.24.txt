59   3.2) Public hearing and recommendation of Corey Bowden’s request for review of the
60   Bowden General Plan Amendment on 5.18 acres located at 9861 West 9600 North (1500 N
61   2800 W) changing the land use designation from VLDRA (very low-density residential
62   agriculture) to LDR (low density residential).
63   Commissioner Kunze asked if this was a county or city property and staff clarified that it was
64   annexed into the city.
65
66   Jill Jones was there on behalf of the applicant. She stated that they requested a change based on
67   a plot map of Coolidge Farms, proposing smaller 0.33-acre lots to offer more affordable homes,
68   which would better meet local demand and generate more tax revenue for the city.
69
70   Commissioner Everett asked Ms. Jones why she opted to change the general plan to LRDR
71   instead of pursuing a PUD, which would allow for more green space and was suggested by the
72   city. Ms. Jones responded that while they would consider it, it wasn't their first preference.
73
74   Commissioner Chair Jackson opened the public hearing.
75
76   There were comments on Granicus in opposition some of the reasons where the lot size not
77   matching with the neighborhood. schools and traffic.
78
79   Candace Jones- expressed opposition to the proposed general plan amendment changing the land
80   use from VLDRA to LDR. She argued that the amendment was inconsistent with surrounding
81   properties, which had larger lot sizes, and would negatively impact home values and
82   neighborhood cohesion. Ms. Jones also cited concerns about increased traffic, overcrowded
83   schools, and inadequate city infrastructure, including the lack of public transit and bicycle lanes.
84   She urged the city to deny the amendment and collaborate with the landowner on solutions that
85   would benefit the broader community.
86
87   Ashley Frampton- expressed concerns about the proposed zoning change, echoing points made
88   by Ms. Jones. Her primary worry was the overcrowding at North Point Elementary, which
89   already has nearly 1,100 students, with more housing developments planned in the area. She

     Lehi City Planning Commission                    2                                 September 26, 2024
 90   emphasized that adding more homes would exacerbate large class sizes and strain the school's
 91   ability to support students, particularly those with English as a second language.
 92
 93   Jared Marbuji-opposed the amendment, expressing concerns about increasing traffic and
 94   overcrowded schools. After moving to Lehi in April 2023 to raise a family, he was disappointed
 95   by the rising traffic, particularly from 2100 North, which funnels through his neighborhood. He
 96   also worried about the impact of large class sizes on his children's education.
 97
 98   Camille Nielsen-opposed the amendment, sharing similar concerns as the previous speaker. She
 99   has children at multiple schools in the district, including a child with special needs, and noted the
100   increasingly chaotic traffic in the area. While she values the family-friendly community they
101   moved to, she felt the rising traffic and changes were disrupting the peaceful environment they
102   sought.
103
104   Cassie Meyers- opposed the amendment, citing concerns about heavy traffic on unsafe roads and
105   the dangers around North Point Elementary. She noted the high demand for larger half-acre lots
106   and stressed the importance of preserving them in the community. As a realtor, she believed the
107   designation change wouldn’t significantly impact the property’s value.
108
109   Commissioner Chair Jackson closed the public hearing.
110
111   Commissioner Lockhart noted that school population issues are outside the planning
112   commission's jurisdiction, but acknowledged the concerns raised. They encouraged residents to
113   research the upcoming November ballot proposition about splitting the Alpine School District,
114   which could provide more local representation and faster responses to school overcrowding
115   issues. She than followed up cornering the amendment stating that while a diverse range of
116   housing types is important for addressing Utah's affordable housing crisis, half-acre lots should
117   not be sacrificed in every situation. She emphasized preserving these lots, as there is a market for
118   them, and encouraged the applicant to explore other options without resorting to a general plan
119   amendment.
120
121   Commissioner Eyre asked about amount of lots depending on size to staff and those were
122   discussed with staff member Jacob Curtis.
123
124   Commissioner Everett expressed concern about the minimal affordability difference between
125   half-acre and third acre lots, stating that it doesn't significantly impact affordability for most
126   people. He emphasized the importance of preserving half-acre lots for hobby farms and animals,
127   which he believes is part of Lehi's legacy, and viewed the general plan amendment as a step
128   backward for the city.
129
130   Commission Chair Jackson acknowledged the validity of Ms. Jones' points on the importance of
131   zoning changes but emphasized that amendments should offer significant benefits to the city
132   rather than just favoring the developer. He viewed the proposed change as neutral to beneficial
133   for the developer and felt it would disrupt the surrounding area, concluding it wasn't in the city's
134   best interest.
135
136           Motion: Commissioner Lockhart moved to recommend denial to Corey Bowden’s
137           request for review of the Bowden General Plan Amendment on 5.18 acres located at 9861
      Lehi City Planning Commission                     3                                September 26, 2024

139           (very low-density residential agriculture) to LDR (low density residential) including all
140           findings that the proposed general plan amendment is not in conformance with the
141           purposes intent and provisions of the general plan and its various elements. In addition to
142           that, the amendment is not consistent with the land uses and zoning of nearby, adjoining
143           properties and all DRC comments. Commissioner Eyre also wanted to draw attention to
144           the minimum of the AG fencing noting that they can make an agreement if they so
145           choose. Commissioner Kunze seconded.

147           Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes;
