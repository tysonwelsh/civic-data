 2.        Administrative Reports
           2.1. Discussion pertaining to a Master License Agreement for Small Cell Towers within
           Herriman City - Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi explained the Utah Legislature passed a bill to permit a wireless provider to install
small wireless facilities within the right-of-way under specific conditions. The bill also contemplated to allow
municipalities to establish a permitting process for providers to install the facilities within the jurisdictional
boundaries. Attorney Andrizzi referenced City Code to allow the issuance of nonexclusive licenses governing the
installation, construction, operation, use and maintenance of wireless facilities and structures in the right-of-way.

Attorney Andrizzi presented a Master License Agreement to allow city staff to execute the licensing agreements
with multiple providers without the need to present each request to the Council for consideration as the City
would look to issue the permits nonexclusively. He looked to the Council to approve the form of the agreement
to be used as a baseline for each provider requesting to install small wireless facilities within the City.

Attorney Andrizzi offered alternatives to the Council including to amend the current ordinance to include specific
fees, rates and require payment to be consistent. The ordinance could establish the permitting procedure and
outline any other terms contemplated in the agreement. He also offered the alternative to disregard the Master
License Agreement and review each licensing agreement from each interested provider within the City. State
Code notated the rates would be similar to each applicant.

Councilmember Ohrn asked for staff recommendation. Attorney Andrizzi suggested the Council consider to
approve the form of the agreement to enable staff to execute the nondiscriminatory contract would be the best
practice. Councilmember Ohrn relayed concerns over health safety of the wireless towers. Attorney Andrizzi
responded he had heard concerns relating to 5G; however, there was no scientific evidence to support the claims.
The requirements imposed by the Utah State Legislature for municipalities to allow the small cell towers within
jurisdictional boundaries was discussed. Mayor Watts asked if the City had the ability to control the aesthetics of
the small cell towers. It was confirmed there were design standards outlined in the Master License Agreement
and site license. Community Development Director Blake Thomas added the desire of staff would to have the
provider install their own poles to reduce maintenance and liability from the City.

Representatives were present. He explained the policy for a municipality to withdraw from the taxing district. He
looked to the Council to offer direction whether they would like to pursue the direction to formal adopt a
resolution to withdraw from the taxing entity while remaining with the services offered by the Unified Fire
Authority to ensure benefits received are in the best interest of Herriman City’s residents.

Finance Director Alan Rae indicated he had compared the operating budget expenses of the Unified Fire Authority

City. He presented a calendar outlining the withdrawal process timeline.

City Attorney Chase Andrizzi offered the statutory process once the resolution had been executed and concluding
with the negotiation of the final terms for the separation. The Council relayed their support to have the resolution
up for consideration at the next meeting.

          2.3. Detention Ponds, Retention Ponds and Streetscape Projects - Jonathan Bowers, City
          Engineer
City Engineer Jonathan Bowers pointed out the unfinished detention ponds and outlined the improvement
qualifiers which included the improvement which would otherwise be completed by a developer, improvement
along an areas which has already been developer, the developer project has been completed, paid a fee-in-lieu, or
a project where it’s warranty has expired, or an improvement owned and maintained by the City. City Engineer
Bowers highlighted the estimated project costs which totaled to approximately $850,000.

City Engineer Bowers asked the Council what funding mechanisms should be considered as well as the type of
landscaping should be installed to minimize the financial burden. Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn asked why impact
fees could not be utilized for these projects. City Engineer Bowers responded the burden was typically placed on
the developer to provide and convey the property, and suggested impact fees could be used for the conveyance
aspect. He explained the rational of not placing detention ponds in the Impact Fee Facility Plan.

City Engineer Bowers explained the substantial cost increase if the City chose to xeriscape the park strips. He
acknowledged the grants offered by Jordan Valley Water, which would have required standards. Councilmember
Ohrn relayed her expectation to get the project prioritized and completed. City Engineer Bowers informed the
Council they would set the priority. Councilmember Ohrn reported she would look to do each project with water
efficiency in mind. City Engineer Bowers indicated they had not collected estimates for that specific type of
landscaping. Councilmember Smith recognized these projects would be out of the General Fund, and ask for a list
to be given to the Council to be able to prioritize projects.

Councilmember Steve Shields asked if projects which have a Master Development Agreement were given open
space credit and did not finish the project. Community Development Director Blake Thomas indicated he would
look at the Rosecrest Master Development Agreement to determine what had been included.
