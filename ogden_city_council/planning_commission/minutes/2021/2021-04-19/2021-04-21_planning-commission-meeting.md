1. Discussion: Loss of right for dwelling structures in commercial zones

   Mr. Montgomery explained nonconforming uses were initially defined in the Zoning Ordinance in 1951 when zoning
   laws were established in Ogden. Because Ogden had nearly 100 years of built environment prior to that time, the
   Ordinance recognized uses as being legal even though they may not conform to adopted standards of the
   Ordinance. The intent was that as time passed, there would be an ability to redevelop properties and the uses
   would be abandoned and future uses would conform with the zoning regulations. Until 1972, Ogden operated
   under a hierarchy of zoning regulations, whereby a single-family home was permitted in all zones, with each zone
   then adding more uses. Then in 1972, zoning philosophies changed, and it was determined there should be a
   separation of land uses as it was felt unlike uses created adverse impacts, and single-family homes were
   eliminated as permitted uses in commercial and industrial zones.

   He reviewed the history of development along Washington Boulevard, indicating homes were built in order to take
   advantage of streetcars, but over time, Washington has become a transportation corridor and the desirability of
   residential uses was reduced. Even though many residential uses remained, many of these homes were converted
   to commercial or office uses, often with added parking, while the character of the building remained as homes.

   Ordinance revisions were later made which then allowed homes which had become vacant to again be reused as a
   home, as well as allowing the reuse of nonresidential buildings in residential zones. There is no provision to allow a
   loss of right as a residential use to be re-established if the home had been converted to a use which is permitted in
   the zone. Due to recent housing demands, some of these homes are now being marketed as residential even
   though their ability to be used as a home has been lost. This discussion is to consider whether the Ordinance
   should be amended to allow uses according to the original construction and intended use of the building even if the
   nonconforming right has been lost, and if so, should there be a requirement to remove parking lots which might
   have been created to accommodate a business, thus making it return to the original appearance.

   Commissioner Southwick stated it seems some of the recent ordinances accept this philosophy, whereby mixed
   use buildings are now allowed in many areas. Chair Graf asked if there are situations along Washington where
   allowing the reuse of buildings would create problems. Mr. Montgomery stated while the zoning along Washington
   was changed many of the lots are not deep enough to accommodate commercial uses, and there has always been
   concern about how far commercial zoning should extend past the Washington Boulevard corridor. Commissioner
   Garner noted some may have had commercial storefronts but were also the primary residence of the shop owners.
   Commissioner Safsten noted there had been many homes which were converted to multi-family and much effort
   has been made by the City to return these to single-family homes. He also felt it is more desirable to return a
   building to its original use, stating renovation should be encouraged rather than demolition. Commissioner
   Southwick noted as there is currently so little housing available, what is there sells quickly and is often overpriced.
   Commission felt allowing these homes to be reused would improve affordable housing in the community and
   renovation can spur investment and bring new life to the surrounding community. Commissioner Schade
   expressed concern that if there is a requirement to remove asphalt and install landscaping, it could add to the
   overall cost of a home, and contribute to it then becoming unaffordable. Commissioner Southwick stated many
   factor in the cost of improvements when considering purchasing a home, and unless it is purchased by an investor,
   most buyers are not concerned with the cost of remodeling or landscaping, while other buyers are looking for
   homes that have recently been remodeled.

   It was indicated there has long been concern that Ogden has an overabundance of commercially zoned property
   and while high traffic commercial is desirable in some areas, as well as apartments are permitted in commercial
   zones, the ability to allow homes to be re-used would be beneficial. It was also felt a residential use would be more
   desirable than a building becoming vacant. Commissioner Blaisdell asked if gentrification might become an issue
   as property values may change. Commissioner Safsten felt gentrification may be desirable in some areas as there
   often becomes under-utilized and under-valued properties. He stated to bring older properties to a higher status or
   middle-class standards may be helpful, but there also may be properties where basic services such as water and
   power are not available. Discussion continued relating to investment properties and stigma of rental units as well
   as inflated property prices and stability of neighborhoods. It was indicated while a home might have once been
   affordable, due to increased prices and limited incomes, it becomes unaffordable over time. It was indicated home
   ownership improves the stability of neighborhoods and the shortage of housing stocks increases property values
   and makes homes unaffordable. It was felt by providing more flexibility in the Ordinance, the ability of home
   ownership would improve. It was indicated these provisions would have little effect in some areas such as the East
   Central or Mt. Ogden communities as there are few commercial zones. Commissioner Stoker asked if there are
   any incentives provided by Ogden City citizens as it seems there have been an abundance of out-of-town
   developers who then sell properties, stating citizens want to live here and would rather not rent from developers.
   Mr. Montgomery stated many of the homes in commercial zones have limited lot sizes and there is little ability for
   homes to be removed and replaced with apartments. He stated if a home were voluntarily removed, its ability to be
   rebuilt as a home would be lost, and the property would be required to meet the standards of the zone. He
   reviewed the Own in Ogden program as well as emergency home loan and rehabilitation grants available through
   Ogden City. Commission also felt it is important for residential buildings to retain the appearance as residential
   with front yard landscaping, sidewalks and limited fencing. Concrete in front of these homes should be removed.

   Mr. Montgomery stated as this ordinance is developed, the intent is to also clarify its intent and existing standards.
   Discussion continued relating to accessory dwelling units, the original intended use of buildings, home occupation
   businesses and live/work situations. Commission felt it is important to provide flexibility for the reuse of residential
   structures but expressed concern some unintended consequences might occur over time. Commissioner Sandau
   noted commercial areas often are more noisy than residential areas and owners may then complain about the
   noise. It was suggested the ordinance contain standards that maintain a residential appearance. It was suggested
   some areas might be identified where residential uses in commercial zones are prevalent such as Washington
   Boulevard and sections of Grant Avenue.

   Mr. Montgomery stated based on this discussion, he hopes to have an amendment ready for consideration at the
   May 5th meeting.

As there was no additional business before the Commission, the meeting was adjourned at 7: 13pm.
