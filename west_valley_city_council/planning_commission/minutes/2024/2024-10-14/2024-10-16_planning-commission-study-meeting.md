GENERAL PLAN/ZONE CHANGE APPLICATIONS
GPZ-7-2024 Petition by VanTrust Real Estate requesting a General Plan change from Medium
Density Residential (7 to 12 units/acre) and Large Lot Residential (2 to 3 units/acre) to Light
Manufacturing and a zone change from A (Agricultural, minimum lot size ½ acre) and A-1
(Agricultural, minimum lot size 1 acre) to M (Manufacturing). The property is located at 5892
and 5998 W Parkway Blvd. on 37.53 acres. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545 or
steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov).
DISCUSSION: Steve P. presented the applicant’s new concept for this lot, that was denied by
City Council in June of 2023. The landowner is Ivory Land Corporation who generally doesn’t
build industrial development, so they would like to sell it to a developer if the lot can be rezoned
to Manufacturing. The tenants are unknown at this time; however, the possible industrial uses are
general light industrial, an industrial park, manufacturing, and warehousing. The new concept
would realign the canal along the north side of the property to run parallel to Parkway Blvd. and
extend Stegner Way along the south side of the property to eventually connect to 5900 West.

GP-1-2024 Petition by Kristofor Kvarfordt requesting a General Plan change from Low
Density Residential (3 to 4 units/acre) to Small Lot Residential (4 to 7 units/acre). The property
is located at 5332 W Highbury Parkway on 12.85 acres. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545
or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov).

DISCUSSION: Steve presented the applicant’s first community concept plan for 67 single-
family homes with an HOA; density would be 5.2 units per acre. The property currently has a
development agreement that states the property shall be developed as single-family detached
homes, and all lots shall be at least 8,000 square feet in area. The proposed average lot size is
5,526 square feet with a range from 4,997 to 9,604 square feet. By ordinance, the increase in
density must be compensated by an increase in community design and amenities such as
greenspace and walking trails.

Commissioner Drozdek asked why the backyard fences are going to face Highbury Pkwy,
because the townhomes across the street have their front yards facing the street which looks
more inviting. Steve replied it’s a combination of the lot layout and privacy for the residents.

Commissioner Winters stated there is a benefit in having the homes face inward because there is
a fair amount of traffic on these roads. Children tend to play in the front yards, so inward facing
homes would be safer, where they can play and not have to worry about traffic. Steve P. added to
that preference because Highbury Pkwy isn’t meant to have curb cuts for garages and cars
pulling in and out.

Chair Lovato asked if the City decided to amend the General Plan and the development
agreement and then the applicant doesn’t go through with the PUD, would the decreased lot sizes
be allowed in future development. Steve P. replied that new developers would have to submit a
new concept plan, and the City would have to approve it.

Commissioner Porter asked if these will be for-sale homes. Steve P. confirmed that they will be,
and the development agreement proposes that the HOA only allow 30% of the homes to be
rented out by future owners.

Commissioner Drozdek asked if this development has any relevancy towards the affordable
housing act that the State Legislature passed. Steve P. replied that the State is encouraging cities
to move towards smaller lots and more density. However, in this case, these homes would likely
not be considered affordable and probably start in the six-hundred-thousand-dollar range.
Commissioner Winters asked if there is a benefit in keeping the homes and lots large in future
developments. Steve P. replied that there is a mix of product in Highbury already. There are 2
apartment complexes, 3 town home developments, and single-family homes. The feedback he
has received from residents is they want single-family homes to go in this area.


GPZ-6-2024 Petition by Kristofor Kvarfordt requesting a General Plan change from Medium
Density Residential (7 to 12 units/acre) to High Density Residential (12 to 20 units/acre) and a
zone change from R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square feet)
to MXD (Mixed Use). The property is located at 5035 W Lake Park Blvd. and 2736 S Corporate
Park Dr. on 26.34 acres. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov).
DISCUSSION: Steve presented the second community concept plan by Mr. Kvarfordt. It
included 144 four-story apartments, 136 six-story apartments, 32 for-sale town homes, 56 rental
townhomes, and 67 fractional owner condos for a total of 435 units on 26.3 acres. Amenities
would include a surf pool (membership required), a clubhouse and pickleball courts. The
proposal did meet the number of parking stalls the Code requires.
Chair Lovato asked if traffic on Sandwell Drive will have traffic calming? Steve P. replied that
Sandwell Drive would be extended and terminated into a cul-de-sac. Traffic calming was not
proposed but it could be discussed. Chair Lovato is concerned about the amount and speed of
traffic and would prefer a way to slow them down. He is also concerned more multi-family
homes are being built before single-family homes.
Steve P. added that this property has been before the Planning Commission three separate times.
It was tabled a couple of times and ultimately the Planning Commission recommended to deny
the application. He will include the minutes from those meetings in the packets for the
Commissioners.

CONDITIONAL USE APPLICATIONS
CA-9-2024 Petition by Geneva Rock Products, Inc. requesting conditional use amendment
approval to add a Hot Mix Asphalt Plant at their sand and gravel mining facility. The project is
located at 6852 West 6200 South, on 108.87 acres total (9-acre project area) and is zoned M
(Manufacturing) Staff – Jody Knapp at 801-963-3497 or jody.knapp@wvc-ut.gov).
DISCUSSION: Jody presented the Applicant’s request to add a hot mix asphalt plant on their site
for the next 30 years. The site currently consists of a 11-acre concrete batch plant and an 88-acre
sand and gravel mining area. The hot mix asphalt plant would be a 100’ tall facility and operate
24-7 to accommodate nighttime paving projects. Approval is subject to landscaping standards for
high image arterials for the 6200 South frontage road. Public Works will be looking at adding
curb, gutter, and sidewalk improvements on 6200 South.

Commissioner Drozdek asked what the traffic and environmental impacts would be by adding
the hot asphalt plant. Jody replied that they must get permits and approval from the State and
Department of Air Quality.

C-17-2024 Petition by Concrete Deco, LLC requesting conditional use approval for a retail

C-18-2024 Petition by Skyline Electric requesting conditional use approval for an
office/warehouse with outside storage. The project is located at 4686 South 6000 West, on 6.00
acres and is zoned M (Manufacturing) Staff – Jody Knapp at 801-963-3497 or jody.knapp@wvc-
ut.gov).
DISCUSSION: Jody presented the Applicant’s request for a 40,000 square foot building to house
their office and light manufacturing. There will be outside storage for electrical equipment,
pickup trucks, trailers, and small excavation machines. This lot is visible from 4700 South so
frontage landscaping will be added. A masonry wall is required to screen the outside storage so
different options were discussed. The property has a very steep slope, so the plan is to match
grade with 4700 South down to the train tracks and build a safety fence.
Commissioner Winters asked if the grade on the north side of the building descends on the
homes-across the train tracks, or if it ascends above the homes. Jody replied that the grade will
be higher than the rooftops of the homes.

DISCUSSION: Brock presented the applicant’s request for a 5,000 square foot Bricks Corner

looks as though it will be somewhat protected since it looks like it's underneath an eave. Brock
replied that there's not necessarily an eave, but the applicant needs to address some more relief
elements, so they potentially might be adding some awnings and things. Brock will request more
specific information on the exact material they're wanting to use.
