SCHEDULES ITEMS

       ORDINANCE – Enacting Section 9-2-15 of the City of Orem Code related to Targeted
       Residential Picketing

The City had recently seen groups of individuals gathering to picket and protest outside the homes
of certain government officials. There had also been calls to stage even larger-scale protests and
picketing in front of the homes of specific government officials with estimates of several hundred
protesters expected to participate. This "targeted residential picketing" could be intimidating and
threatening to the individuals who were the focus of the protests and was a serious disruption to
the feeling of peace and safety that every resident was entitled to enjoy in their home.

In order to protect individuals who were or may in the future be the focus of targeted residential
picketing, staff proposed the adoption of a targeted residential picketing ordinance. The proposed
ordinance would make it unlawful to engage in picketing that was specifically directed at a
particular residence or the occupant(s) of that residence. The U.S. Supreme Court had held that
targeted residential picketing ordinances were legal and did not violate the First Amendment.
Individuals would still be able to exercise their First Amendment right to protest as long as they
did not focus or direct their picketing toward a specific residence in violation of the ordinance.

Mr. Earl reviewed the definition of picketing. He said having protesters or picketers outside one’s
home could be disturbing and intimidating, may expose people to unwanted speech as they were
a “captive audience” as it were. He said full opportunity would still exist for individuals to exercise
their rights for free speech and the intention was not to suppress speech rights of any particular
viewpoint. Mr. Earl said the proposed ordinance would make it unlawful to picket within one
hundred (100) feet of the property line of a target house, and any violation of this section of code
would be a Class B misdemeanor.

In response to a question from Mayor Brunst, Mr. Earl clarified that it was 100 feet from the
property line. Mr. Macdonald asked if it was possible to extend beyond the 100 feet. Mr. Earl said
going beyond that ran a greater risk that a court would see it as too restrictive and potentially
unconstitutional.

Mr. Sumner said this would be a tool that public safety could use if it became necessary and was
not with the intent to stop people exercising their right to free speech. Mr. Earl said that was
correct, and any picketers that behaved in an inappropriate manner may be charged with disorderly
conduct.

Discussion continued regarding “free speech zones” within a municipality, public sidewalks and
streets as public forums, the effect within neighborhoods on those not the target of the protest, and
the various tools available to the public safety officers in maintaining safety in the community.
There was particular discussion on whether a physical line should be drawn at the 100 foot mark
from the property line. Mayor Brunst felt the line would be needed for protesters to know where it
was. Mr. Davidson said he had spoken with Chief Adams about whether it was prudent to mark

the 100 foot line. Chief Adams said his team was having conversations with the neighbors on
Governor Herbert’s street as well as the security team in place to protect the governor. Chief
Adams said the goal was to educate people on what was allowed and to maintain the peace in the
neighborhood while still allowing people to express their viewpoints. He said drawing an actual
line may incite more aggression, which they wanted to avoid. They did not want to have a large
police presence but rather be on hand to keep everyone protected and safe.

Mr. Sumner thanked Chief Adams and his team for speaking to the neighbors individually to keep
them in the know, as he himself was a neighbor of Governor Herbert. Mr. Sumner said previous
protests in the cul-de-sac had not been violent, but there had been lots of loud noise including
angry, profane yelling and the blowing of air horns. Mr. Sumner said the neighbors were relieved
to know the police department would be there as support on Sunday.

Mr. Macdonald asked about scheduled protests the city had been made aware of; Chief Adams
said he was only aware of one scheduled for Sunday.

Mr. Peterson expressed concern about passing this ordinance two days before the event. He said
he was generally in favor of the proposed ordinance, but the timing gave him pause. Mayor Brunst
said that was understandable, but he felt this was an appropriate time to act and ensure safety and
peace for Governor Herbert and his family. In response to Mr. Peterson’s question, Mr. Earl said
this ordinance was not targeting any one group, person, position, or viewpoint. It would apply now
and in the future to any protest.

Mayor Brunst then moved, by ordinance, to enact Section 9-2-15 of the City of Orem Code
related to Targeted Residential Picketing. Mr. Sumner seconded the motion. Those voting aye:
Richard F. Brunst, Jeff Lambson, Debby Lauret, Tom Macdonald, Terry Peterson, David Spencer,
and Brent Sumner. The motion passed.
