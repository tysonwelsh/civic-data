CARES Act Overview
¢ City received allocation end of June 2020.
* Federal guidance states that funds can only be used to cover expenses that:
1. Are necessary expenditures incurred due to the public health emergency
with respect to the Coronavirus Disease 2019 (COVID-—19)
2. Were not accounted for in the budget most recently approved as of March
27, 2020 (the date of enactment of the CARES Act) for the State or
government; and
3. Were incurred during the period that begins on March 1, 2020 and ends on
December 30, 2020.
* The County issued the following restrictions with the City’s allocation:
1. Cannot be used as revenue replacement (also a Federal requirement).
2. Grantee, subgrantee, or recipient cannot also receive funding from the
County for the same loss or expense.
* Federal guidance also dictates that grantees, subgrantees, or recipients cannot
receive multiple CARES Act disbursements for the same expense.
* The City would be responsible if duplicate payments are received.

U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). The HUD funding is passed
through to cities within the county, including Midvale, through a variety of programs and
processes. Salt Lake County has been working on the renewal of their Urban County
certification, which is required in order to receive their annual allocation of funding from
HUD. Salt Lake County was notified on August 19th by HUD that contractual issues were
identified that must be addressed by September 18th in order to receive the Urban County ]
certification. HUD provided guidance regarding the language that needs to be modified in
the current interlocal agreements between Salt Lake County and its partner communities.

B. DISCUSSION AND ACTION REGARDING RESOLUTION NO. 2020-R-41
AUTHORIZING THE EXECUTION OF THE CDBG GRANT AGREEMENT
BETWEEN MIDVALE CITY AND SALT LAKE COUNTY FOR FUNDING
THE MIDVALE CITY ADA IMPROVEMENT PROJECT
Nate Rockwood said in the fall of 2019, Midvale City applied to Salt Lake County for a
Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) in the amount of $160,000. The grant was
requested in order to fund the Midvale City ADA Improvement Project (Project). The
Project is intended to install or reconstruct 20 American with Disabilities Act compliant
sidewalk ramps in the Copperview and Central Midvale neighborhoods. The Project is
intended to be bid out in January 2021 with construction to proceed as weather allows
during the Spring of 2021.
In the Spring of 2020, Salt Lake County approved Midvale’s grant request for the Project.
In order to receive the approved grant funds, Midvale must enter into a grant agreement
with Salt Lake County. Midvale Staff has worked with Salt Lake County to develop the
terms of the Grant Agreement. The Grant Agreement includes standard requirements
included in all Salt Lake County CDBG Grant Agreements, as well as Project specific
terms. The attached CDBG Grant Agreement includes the following terms (among
others):
e Grant Amount: $160,000
e Midvale City Program Contribution: $50,000
e Period of Performance: July 1, 2020 — June 30, 2021
e Agreement Expiration: August 31, 2021
e Scope of project: Construction and Reconstruction of 20 ADA compliant sidewalk
ramps.
e Methods of Disbursement: Lump sum reimbursement at completion of Project or
periodic payments throughout Project construction.
FISCAL IMPACT:
The fiscal impact of the CDBG Grant Agreement is minimal. The City is committing to
spend $50,000 on the design and construction of ADA ramps. The City’s contribution will
come from 2 funds already budgeted for street improvements that include ADA ramp
construction. The $160,000 grant will be added to the FY21 Budget for implementation of
the ADA Improvement Program.

C. CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 2020-R-42 AUTHORIZING THE
IMPLEMENTATION OF A SMALL BUSINESS PROMOTION GRANT
PROGRAM TO MIDVALE BUSINESSES USING CARES ACT FUNDS
Nate Rockwood said on or about June 9, 2020, Salt Lake County allocated $993,657to
Midvale (the City) as an indirect recipient of the CARES Act pursuant to the terms and
conditions outlined in the agreement between Midvale and Salt Lake County. Pursuant to
the agreement these funds are to be used for COVID-19 relief initiatives and in doing so
the agreement also provides in part that the City may make subgrants for further
distribution to any other private or public entities within its jurisdiction given that is
complies with all certifications, restrictions and requirements for the use of federal funds.
Therefore, in an attempt to further lessen the financial burden brought on by COVID-19,
staff, with the guidance of Salt Lake County proposes to implement a Small Business
Grant Program. This grant program will provide up to $5,000 in reimbursement for
business advertisement and COVID-19 related expenses to qualifying Midvale
businesses with the intent of dispensing a total of $100,000 in CARES Act funds to local
businesses.
The Small Business Grant application and agreement are attached and comply with the
restrictions and conditions of the agreement between Salt Lake County and Midvale. A
summary of the program is as follows:
e Total amount of CARES Act Funds to distribute: $100,000 with the possibility of
going up to $150,000 depending on availability of remaining funds.
e Grant award amount: Up to $5,000 in reimbursement for:

o Business promotion & advertising for expenses incurred and paid during
the period beginning September 16th and ending October 20th in
accordance with the terms and conditions of the grant.

The Series 2020 Bonds will be issued for the purpose of (a) refunding all or a portion of
the Issuer’s outstanding (i) Water and Sewer Revenue Bonds, Series 2010A and Series
2010(B) (together, the “Series 2010 Bonds’), (ii) Water, Sewer & Storm Water Revenue
Bonds, Series 2013 (the “Series 2013 Bonds”) and (iii) Water, Sewer & Storm Water
Revenue Bonds, Series 2014 (the “Series 2014 Bonds” and collective with the Series
2010 Bonds and the Series 2013 Bonds, the “Refunded Bonds”) and (c) paying costs of
| issuance of the Series 2020 Bonds.
PARAMETERS OF THE SERIES 2020 BONDS
The Issuer intends to issue the Series 2020 Bonds in the aggregate principal amount of
not more than Nine Million Dollars ($9,000,000), to mature in not more than eleven (11)
years from their date or dates, to be sold at a price not less than ninety-seven percent
(97%) of the total principal amount thereof, and bearing interest at a rate or rates not to
exceed five percent (5.0%) per annum. The Series 2020 Bonds are to be issued and sold
by the Issuer pursuant to the Resolution, including as part of said Resolution, a General
Indenture of Trust and a Supplemental Indenture of Trust (together, the “Indenture”) which
were before the Council in substantially final form at the time of the adoption of the
Resolution and said Indenture is to be executed by the Issuer in such form and with such
changes thereto as shall be approved by the Issuer; provided that the principal amount,
interest rate or rates, maturity, and discount of the Series 2020 Bonds will not exceed the
maximums set forth above. The Issuer reserves the right to not issue the Series 2020
Bonds for any reason and at any time up to the issuance of the Series 2020 Bonds.
REVENUES TO BE PLEDGED
The Series 2020 Bonds are special limited obligations of the Issuer payable from the net
revenues of the System.

B. DISCUSS CANNABIS PHARMACY PROXIMITY ISSUES
Nate Rockwood said medical cannabis production establishments and medical cannabis
pharmacies were first authorized by the state through HB 3001 in Dec. 2018. The City
Council had several discussions about the related City Zoning Ordnance throughout
2019.
When the City adopted language addressing medical cannabis uses (production
establishments and medical cannabis pharmacies), Council adopted the requirements in
state code with minimal changes. We did this by referring directly to state code
requirements or by copying the language verbatim out of state code. The areas where
these uses are permitted follow state code: production establishments are permitted in
the Clean Industrial zone and medical cannabis pharmacies are permitted in all zones
except those used primarily for residential use (SF and RM zones). By refencing state
code, the City’s ordinance will remain current as changes are made by the State.
The only additions the City made to the process deal with Midvale-specific implementation
concerns. The City require a site plan, floor plan, architectural elevations, and a security
plan with Business License applications (these are also required by the state, so no extra
work is required by the applicant). Where proximity concerns cross city boundaries, we
require a letter from the adjacent municipality indicating there are no proximity issues
within range on their side of the boundary line.
The City also defines the use and establish parking requirements based on other similar
use types. The following is a summary:
