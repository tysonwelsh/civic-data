    2.1. Update on the Skyridge HOA open space surplus property request - Blake Thomas, Community
         Development Director
Community Development Director Blake Thomas indicated this discussion was to offer a follow-up from the
August 25, 2021 meeting when the Council directed staff to research the possibility to offer a deed with a
reversionary clause if the property was ultimately being used for an unintended purpose. He indicated the
Rosecreast open space calculation did not include any interior development open space, which essentially meant
granting this request would not adversely affect the open space requirement outlined in the Master Development
Agreement.

Director Thomas noted the property was deeded to Herriman City by Rosecrest as open space. The total
development plan of 945 acres included 180 acres being dedicated as open space. Director Thomas presented
photos of the area which depicted how the open space was being utilized as a parking lot with no improvements.
It was also noted no parking enforcement was being conducted for the area.

                                 Councilmember Jared Henderson arrived at 5:37 p.m.

Director Thomas continued with the construction of the Juniper Canyon Trailhead was being constructed and
did not believe patrons for the trail would access the trail through this open space parcel as it abutted a private
road. Councilmember Ohrn expressed concern of setting a precedence and believed this request was similar to
residential encroachment issues the Council has discussed. Director Thomas suggested this situation could be
compared to a fee-in-lieu. The Homeowner’s Association (HOA) wanted to install improvements and utilize the
area as a parking lot having 22 stalls. Mayor Watts indicated the HOA would have to appropriately compensate
the City to use the area as a private parking lot.

City Attorney Chase Andrizzi interjected the Council could surplus the property to achieve the highest and best
value to the City which could take nonmonetary value into consideration. Councilmember Henderson
questioned the cost to install the infrastructure. It was noted the HOA estimated the cost would total $75,000.
City Attorney Andrizzi recommended the property be deeded to the HOA with a reversionary clause if the
property was used for anything else, the ownership would come back to the City. Councilmember Henderson
requested staff compile a list of options available to the Council for a future meeting discussion. Councilmember
Ohrn concurred the dollar amounts needed to be available for the Council to make an informed decision and
adequately show the improvement cost to offset the actual appraised cost of the property. City Attorney Andrizzi
advised the Council as this was not an improved open space parcel, the City should properly notice the HOA
of the encroachment of City owned property. The Council agreed and requested signage be placed on the open
space.

City Manager Nathan Cherpeski clarified Council direction to add investment costs and alternatives to consider.
Director Thomas opined the HOA may not have the desire to purchase the entire parcel. Councilmember Ohrn
responded they would have to purchase and maintain the entire piece of property. Councilmember Shields

    2.2. Discussion pertaining to a request from Lakeridge @ Rosecrest HOA to have Herriman City
         assume ownership of the private roads within the private development - Jonathan Bowers, City
         Engineer
City Engineer Jonathan Bowers indicated a request was received from the Lake Ridge at Rosecrest HOA wanting
to exchange ownership responsibilities from private to public. The Engineering and Public Works Departments
conducted roadway and utility improvements which would need to be addressed.
    • Roadway Cross Section requirements:
            o Minimum 53 ft Right-of-Way (ROW) needed. The existing corridor width is 50 ft. An additional
               3 ft would be necessary to comply.
            o The existing Public Utilities Easements (PUE) need to be adjusted such the 10 ft width extends
               from the edge of the new ROW.
            o Three additional feet of asphalt required. Minimum asphalt width is 28 ft, whereas the existing
               asphalt width equals 25 ft.
            o Core samples and evaluation of the existing pavement and base course thickness required to
               confirm compliance with minimum thickness requirements.
            o Type A, 2.5 ft wide high-back curb and gutter required, whereas the existing curb and gutter is 2
               ft wide mountable curb and gutter.
            o 5 ft concrete sidewalk and parkstrip required on both sides of the road. The existing roadways
               do not include any sidewalk.
            o Type A drive approaches required for each private driveway for access onto the roadway.
            o Missing storm drain infrastructure that is required to comply with Herriman City Engineering
               Standards.
            o Private property encroachment into roadway corridor shall be mitigated and improved.
            o Horizontal location of all streetlights are located approximately 6 inches from the required
               location of the curb and gutter. Compliance requires a minimum of 30 inches from the top back
               of curb.
    • Streetlight System requirements:
            o All lights are “daisy chained” off two existing power panels. Compliance would require each
               residential light shall be connected to a power source via a junction box.
            o Only two junction boxes were found for the entire system, whereas compliance would require a
               junction box for each streetlight.
            o All streetlights are currently fused inside the streetlight pole hand hole. Compliance would require
               wires are fused in the junction box and a ground rod, grounding all grounds.
    • Water System requirements:

              requirements for various addresses.
          o Several private properties have water meters not located as a result of overgrown sod and

questioned the deficiency with the storm drain infrastructure. City Engineer Bowers explained the engineering
standard requirements and how a deviation could be granted.

Mayor Watts asked for operational and maintenance costs associated with the request. It was discussed the costs

Councilmember Shields relayed his position to proceed with the request after the right-of-way has achieved
compliance with City standards. Councilmember Ohrn requested to know the complete financial impact for
maintenance this request would have. City Manager Cherpeski offered a rough estimate for the roadway
maintenance costs. Mayor Watts relayed his desire to not take over the ownership and maintenance

He opined this development could meet the standards and relayed his position he would not accept any
substandard roadways. The road would have to be up to full engineering standards for the Council to consider
obtaining ownership and maintenance responsibilities. The Council agreed.

     2.4. Discussion pertaining to parking enforcement issues and challenges - Councilmember Shields,
          requestor

HOA private areas. He believed this discussion would create an understanding of what resident experiences are
with towing companies. Councilmember Smith clarified this situation relates to a contract between the towing
company and the HOA. The parameters of the said contract were determined by the HOA. Councilmember
Shields confirmed and pointed out there needed to be a difference between active loading and unloading of a

Councilmember Henderson talked about issues surrounding the predatory approach conducted by towing
companies and questioned what, if any, the Council could do to mitigate concerns. City Attorney Andrizzi
highlighted the statutes governing towing companies were covered under the State and the City could not have
additional restrictions or conflicts. He indicated the Council to add parameters in an attempt to mitigate life
safety concerns; however, the lane of operation was narrow.

Councilmember Shields reiterated if predatory actions were being conducted on private property, the Council
did not have grounds to make changes. He encouraged for a policy to come to fruition which would work well
in these types of situations. Councilmember Henderson agreed a reasonable standard should be met.

       9.1 Discussion and consideration of a draft ordinance to amend Herriman City Code Title 10
                Chapter 29 to adopt regulations for reuse of metal shipping containers as accessory
                structures (File No. Z2021-051) - Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy offered an overview of the draft ordinance to adopt regulations for metal shipping
containers to be used as accessory structures. The proposed changes would require a building permit for
permanent use, require security lock and safe egress, compatible roof structure would not be required for
residential use, size restrictions based on lot size, conditional use permit requirement for permanent residential
and commercial uses except for agricultural and temporary uses. City Planner Maloy reviewed the maximum
allowable size of the shipping containers for different zoning designations.

City Planner Maloy explained no metal shipping container could be placed on top of another shipping container.
A principal building, dwelling unit or agricultural operation shall be located on the lot. The placement of the
shipping container must be to the rear of the principal building or dwelling unit and on the rear half of the lot
unless screened so it would not be visible from the street. It must also be finished in a manner which would
minimize the visibility from abutting properties. The maximum size and minimum setbacks would be governed
by the zoning designation as depicted in the land use table.

City Planner Maloy continued with the requirements for the exterior treatments notating they must be rust-free
and painted a neutral earth-tone color or a color which would complement the primary structure. No commercial
advertisements or graphics shall be visible from an adjacent agricultural or residential land use or from any street
within the agricultural or residential district. City Planner Maloy presented the land use table. Councilmember

       9.2 Consideration to Award the Construction Contract of the new Wide Hollow Trailhead -
              Jonathan Bowers, City Engineer
City Engineer Bowers Jonathan explained the City received bids from five contractors for the new Wide Hollow
Trailhead project. The initial budgets estimated the cost of the project at a higher amount than anticipated and
the City conducted a budget amendment due to the discrepancy. After the 30-day bid agreement the apparent
low bidder for the project requested their bid to be withdrawn from consideration.

The engineering consultant recommended awarding the contract to Newman based on a technicality regarding
references. Acme challenged the recommendation and have since submitted the references which have been
reviewed.

City Engineer Bowers offered some considerations for the Council to consider bringing the project within the
allocated budget including removing the installation of the restroom, reduce the scope of the project including
the restroom, remove the challenge staircase feature, and keep the existing boulder in place. Both companies
have confirmed their willingness to remove those items from their bid, if desired. City Engineer Bowers
indicated the Council could reject all of the bids and start the project over. This option would allow staff to
reevaluate the scope of the project to stay within the previously approved budget and could have a potential for
additional bidders and pricing to be considered; however, did not know whether the proposals would be less
expensive to construct the project.

Councilmember Henderson moved to reject the bidding contract for the construction of the Wide Hollow Trailhead project.
Councilmember Smith seconded the motion, and all voted aye.
