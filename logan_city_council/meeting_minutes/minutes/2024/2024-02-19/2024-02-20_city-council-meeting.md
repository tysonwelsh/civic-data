Logan resident LuAnn Harris addressed the Council regarding the property on 1400 North
where the current Cache Valley Mall is located. She is concerned about more apartments
being located in this area and asked if traffic studies have been completed. She feels that
apartments should not be built in this area and instead it should be a park. She is also
concerned about a proposed cell phone tower being placed in a City park. She is also
concerned about the future of the Logan Cemetery and having enough space for future
burials.
Logan resident Joshua Molitor addressed the Council. He reviewed the General Plan on
the current Woodsonia project location site at 1400 North. He feels that some of the General
Plan should be rewritten and should go back to the actual “Master Plan”. He said it would
be nice to know what citizen participation has gone into the Woodsonia project and said
there has been a lot of opposition to this project.
Logan resident Matthew Fatuesi addressed the Council. He is asking for a raise from the
City of Logan where he is currently working as a part time janitor. He referenced the cost
of living for his reason of requesting a raise. He also referred to traffic on 1400 North and
decreasing the amount of traffic on the roads. He is also concerned about the Mayor and
Council not addressing questions during the questions and comments period.
Logan resident Bill Bower addressed the Council who stated that he will speak during the
public hearing regarding the alcohol ordinance.
Logan resident Kaleb Shoell addressed the Council regarding the canal. He is grateful to
the City for the amazing trail system that we have. He quoted Mayor Daines who said in a
previous Council meeting, “You know that Utah is a private land State.” He said the canal
issue has nothing to do with private land and as a tax paying citizen he is all for private
land. He understands private land and feels this canal issue is a public access point that has
been there for many years and is a public rights issue. He asked that the City file a
declaratory judgement with the District Court in regard to access to the trail. He has never
seen any action taken on this issue and would like that to occur. He again asked that the
City file a declaratory judgment or if not a judgement, then he feels the City should say,
this is something we don’t want to fight for now or in the future. He asked what precedent
the City is going to set and what will the City fight for in the future and for the citizens. He
also asked if the City Attorney can post a cease and desist on the access point to the canal.

State of the City Address – Mayor Daines

Mayor Holly H. Daines
State of the City Address, February 20, 2024

Good evening! I’m delighted to present a State of the City message. I’ve just completed
my sixth year as Mayor.

As I reflect on what we’ve accomplished, especially this last year, I’m so grateful for the
support of the City Council members and the fine work of our excellent Executive Team,
along with the city’s dedicated employees. Running the city is truly a team effort. I’ll
share highlights from each department, framed in the context of my administration’s
goals.

When I ran for Mayor, I promised four things:
  1) Fiscal Responsibility
  2) Downtown Revitalization
  3) Focus on Infrastructure
  4) Enhance and Connect City Trails and Parks

#1, Fiscal Responsibility
The budget is how you get things done. The Finance Department has practiced years of
good financial management by budgeting conservatively and setting aside funds for
future projects. That philosophy, combined with a great economy, has positioned us to
make major investments in capital projects and infrastructure, all while balancing our
budget and maintaining a good fiscal position. This year the economy has slowed and
sales tax is down, so we will be reducing our capital budget and being more conservative
this year.

We appreciate our IT department which is part of Finance. They’ve been laser- focused
on addressing increasing threats to our cyber security and have upgraded training,
procedures and software.

The second goal is Downtown Revitalization and we’ve made excellent progress. We
opened the beautiful new Carol and Jim Laub Plaza in September, and Parks opened the
skating rink in November with festive holiday activities on Center Street and the plaza.
Council member Mark Anderson got sponsors for fireworks, and you can see from this
photo it was a fabulous end to the celebration. The ice rink has been a great success!
We’ve had over 11,000 skaters use the rink.

Our parks team will program fun, free activities in the plaza all summer for the public to
enjoy. We have a generous sponsor who offered to underwrite a series of concerts on the
stage; the team has lots of other great ideas as well.

The new Library is a wonderful accomplishment for our Library patrons and for
downtown. We’ll cut the ribbon and open the Library on Monday, March 4. This is
another project where generous donors enabled us to add extras which we couldn’t have

Goal #3 is a focus on Infrastructure
In addition to the projects downtown, kudos to our Public Works department for their
other infrastructure projects. Public Works’ multiple divisions include Engineering, GIS
mapping, Streets, Water, Fleet and Shops and the Facilities Team. It is our largest city
department.

They keep the water flowing, sewer heading to the treatment plant, streets repaired and
plowed, vehicles operating and buildings maintained, and GIS keeps track of all our
infrastructure.

They adopted a new Transportation Master Plan, negotiated with the Railroad to
upgrade two intersections on 600 West with new signals and safety features, at 1400 and
1800 North.

If you’ve driven the new road and bridge at 100 West which merges with US 89/91,
you’ve likely blessed them for that excellent project which was a $12 Million, multiyear

Goal #4 is to Enhance and Connect City Trails and Parks
The Parks & Recreation Department has a wide variety of responsibilities, from the 53-
acre cemetery to the 162 acre golf course – named one of the “Nine Best Public Golf
Courses in Utah” for 2023, the Aquatic Center, which recorded over 15,000 patrons last
summer, and for one additional year, the Rec Center. Our joint operation of the Rec
Center will terminate June 30 of 2025 to allow the School District to accommodate more
high school sanctioned sports and other student needs at Logan High School. Parks &
Rec is rethinking how they provide recreation programs as we transition out of the
facility – which has been a great partnership for 48 years.

The Cemetery staff coordinated hundreds of volunteers to paint a good portion of the
perimeter fence on Tenth North, clean up pinecones and needles and clean headstones.
This was a massive undertaking, and they will be asking for volunteers this summer to
finish painting the remainder of the fence. We appreciate volunteers!
Parks staff also take care of our many wonderful parks and trails and provide activities
for our citizens which help create a sense of community, including the traditional 4th
and 24th of July events, Center Street Halloween Festival, and Christmas events. They
helped with the Great Pumpkin Weigh Off as well.

Some exciting news for parks this year was $750,000 from the state’s Utah Outdoor
Recreation Grant program. It will help fund the new Logan Outdoor Recreation
Complex. Parks applied for one large RAPZ grant to provide matching funds and
received $450,000 from RAPZ last year.

This park is in cooperation with the Environmental Department as they close the Logan
Landfill. There is nearly 200 acres of land that was part of the landfill and protection
zone which will be repurposed into recreation space. In addition to providing the land,
significant funding for this project is coming from the Environmental Department as part
of their diligent stewardship of funds, over many years, planning for the landfill closure.
We are grateful for their excellent management.

A park this large will likely be a ten year project, but Phase One, beginning this summer,
will commence with the installation of the road, a Woodruff Neighborhood Park, a 60-
acre competition disc-golf course – keeping much of the existing natural landscaping and
wetlands – and an 11 acre dog park. Trails will connect all areas of the complex and tie
into both the city and county Trails Master Plan. Phase Two, in a few years, will turn the
landfill “trash hill” into a 90 acre mountain bike park. Other Phases will come later.

Another trail-related project coming soon is the bridge across the river at Trapper Park
so we can access the 47 acre, grant-funded conservation easement along the Logan
River. Parks received a RAPZ grant to pay for bridge design and subsequently another
grant for construction. The next step is to seek funding to put in the trail. Staff also built
new restrooms at Trapper Park and paved the parking lot.

We get excellent marks in this area as well as we’ve focused on the goal of expanding
and connecting parks and trails.

I’ve mentioned the Environmental Department and their key role in closing the landfill
and transitioning much of their land to recreation space. They too manage a large
portfolio with solid waste collection, wastewater treatment and the new composting
facility, landfill management and the forestry division, which does everything from
trimming and planting trees to mosquito abatement to handling Christmas lights and
decor.

The Forestry Division, working with parks, received a $300,000 grant to help with new
tree plantings, replace aging trees, and help with a tree inventory.

Environmental successfully concluded the downsizing of our solid waste collection
program last October and assisted other cities as they transitioned to private collection.
We sold 17 garbage trucks and all containers used by other cities to the new entities.
Because our dedicated employees worked a tremendous amount of overtime during the
transition, we reduced our personnel by 17 equipment operators through attrition and
retirement, without laying off any personnel. We are now “right-sized” to serve our
citizens.

The Environmental department manages the Wastewater Treatment Plant, which is now

I mentioned the Library building earlier but wanted to acknowledge that with a reduced
staff, squeezed into a temporary facility, library staff added over 1,500 new library cards

Once the new library opens, patrons can check out a telescope, Spiral Jetty Backpack,

wonderful collection of books. Folks can use the new community rooms, maker space
and more! As part of the move to the new building, Library staff handled each and every
one of the 113,000 print and AV items in their collection.

I also have to thank both the Library staff and the other employees at the Service Center

in cramped quarters in an effort to keep the library operating and to save the city money.
I’m so impressed that everyone works together for the greater good!

The Light and Power department also has some major infrastructure projects underway.
In the last year they brought online two new substations to keep up with growing demand
and have started a two year project to add 10 megawatts of natural gas generation. The
gas generators will help balance the significant addition of solar power in our portfolio
because it can ramp up quickly when variable renewables go offline.

L&P is relocating a transmission line on 1000 North to accommodate the road widening
project and upgrading conductors on the 400 East line to the hospital to handle larger
loads.

They’ve contracted for sediment removal at 2nd dam to facilitate more hydro power.
Sediment is getting in hydro intakes and damaging equipment.

Economic Development
Our Economic Development Director continues to work with our legal team and
spearheaded the redevelopment of the aging Cache Valley Mall. We worked with Logan

Economic Development has also been heavily involved in the construction of the Laub
Plaza and in assisting significant housing projects downtown to get folks living, working

Community Development also has a wide range of responsibilities from Planning
Commission and Historic Preservation to building permits, plan review, building
inspection, and code enforcement. They’ve updated our code with new Landscaping &

They are tasked with implementing the Public Art Master Plan and have coordinated

Providing Public Safety, which is Police and Fire Service for our citizens is a huge part
of what cities do. Our Public Safety employees are selfless individuals who do amazing
work to serve our community.

Let's start with Police: we have 94 employees – which includes patrol, School Resource
Officers; investigations, including the Drug Task Force, administration and dispatch.
Police responded to over 22,000 total incidents in 2023, with 2,400 arrests. Of those,
officers had 215 DUI arrests, making our streets safer for all.

One stat we're very proud of is with over 35,000 contacts, there were only 91 incidents of
“use of force”. The council members and I review camera footage of all use of force
incidents monthly. We are continually reminded of the difficult situations our officers
have to deal with, and their professionalism. The reviews also serve as training
opportunities, continuing to improve officers skills in stressful situations.

Our city employees are our most valuable resource, and the Human Resources

RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommended that the Planning Commission recommend approval to the
Municipal Council of the proposed amendments to Chapters 17.11 District and Corridor
Zone Uses, and 17.62 Definitions of the Land Development Code (LDC).

REQUEST
This code amendment makes changes to the Logan City Land Development Code
Chapters 17.11 District and Corridor Zone Uses, and 17.62 Definitions. This amendment
also includes changes to the Logan City Municipal Code (LMC) Section 5.10 Alcoholic
Beverages, but the Planning Commissions purview is to make recommendations on the
LDC portion of the amendment only. There is a considerable number of connections
between the amendment to the LDC and the LMC, and changes to one may affect the
other. To place the amendment to the LDC within the context of the whole amendment, a
summary of the changes and review of the LMC amendment are included in this report.
The proposed amendments change the administrative procedures for alcohol licensing to
better conform with state and local laws, policies, and procedures; and makes changes to
the local restrictions regarding alcohol uses, including bars, taverns, brew restaurants,
package agencies, off-premise beer, manufacturing, and distribution.

      State code.
  • Rewrote the application process to align with current local and state processes.

Municipal Code 5.10
The proposed amendment includes extensive revisions to Section 5.10 within the
business licensing chapter of the municipal code specifically dealing with regulations
regarding alcohol licensing. Staff has reviewed the code section and identified extensive
redundancies, areas that do not align with State code, and areas that do not correspond to
current policies and procedures. This has resulted in a code section that is challenging to
administer and is not user friendly for staff or the public. One of the intents of the
                                                                                     14 | Page
             Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ February 20, 2024
amendment is to alleviate the administrative issues with the alcohol licensing code by
consolidating and clarifying the administrative and application process for alcohol
licensing. These changes are contained in the revised Article I. Administration, which
includes the definitions, license requirements, application and renewal process, review
process, enforcement, and appeal process. The reworked article consolidates several
sections of the previous code into a single administrative section.

Article II Local License Requirements articulates the local restrictions that go beyond
State code. This contains limits on total licenses and codes that pertain to unique alcohol
uses, such as the Brew Restaurant and Taproom. Previously, the code had extensive
redundant sections and articles for various license types that had no specific local
restrictions. Those sections have been removed or consolidated into the streamlined
Article II to make locating local restrictions easier and more intuitive.

The amendment includes several policy changes, including permitting additional taverns,
expanding manufacturing opportunities, and revising the permitted locations of bars,
taverns, and brew restaurants. The changes include the addition of a couple of new use
types, including a taproom in the TC-1 zone, and a small brewery in the CS and IP zones.
The proposal to expand the number of tavern licenses is intended to allow for innovative
products to develop in downtown. The opportunity to include manufacturing in
conjunction with a tavern license through the new taproom use will incentivize local
business and entrepreneurship. An item for the Commission to consider is whether to
generally expand tavern licenses or instead specify those licenses for taproom uses only.
This could be done by creating a unique population limit for taprooms (such as 1 license:
25,000 population), instead of increasing the total number of tavern licenses.

Changes to the Brew Restaurant include allowing it in areas where restaurants are
permitted and updating the definition of a Brew Restaurant to permit sales of beer for off-
premise sales. The change to permit off-premise sales would still require the majority of
beer manufactured to be consumed on-site. This change would allow more flexibility for
Brew Restaurants to market and distribute their products.

The proposed Taproom use aligns closely with the Brew Restaurant model and would
permit manufacturing in conjunction with a bar or tavern. As proposed, the Taproom
would be subject to the population limits of either a bar or tavern, depending on the type
of license they acquire from the State. The use would also be limited to no more than
5,000 sf gross area to limit the impacts and scope of alcohol manufacturing in downtown.

Another policy change is to remove several general prohibitions from Section 5.10 and
instead address those uses through zoning regulations. This is particularly applicable to
the current tight restrictions on manufacturing, which only allow for manufacturing as
part of a Brew Restaurant. The proposal is to expand manufacturing uses and refer to
zoning to identify the permitted location and scale of the manufacturing.

There is also a general prohibition in the current code regarding Package Agency. A
Package Agency license is a license granted by the State to allow the sale of heavy beer
and liquors that are typically required to go through the State Liquor Store system. These
                                                                                   15 | Page
             Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ February 20, 2024
licenses are closely regulated by the State and are only available in a few instances, the
most applicable being at a brewery or winery. Acquiring a Package Agency license
would allow a brewery in Logan to bottle and distribute their heavy beer products without
going through a state liquor store. The State has a proximity restriction for a Package
Agency (600’ pedestrian travel, 200’ straight line from community locations). With the
clear enumeration from the State regarding who can acquire a Package Agency license
and where they can be located, Staff supports the removal of the local prohibition.

Staff also proposes the removal of proximity restrictions for off-premise beer sales
(grocery or convenience stores). The current restriction is 600’ pedestrian travel or 200’
straight line from a community location such as a park or school. The State no longer
restricts the proximity of off-premise beer sales and staff’s recommendation is to follow
suit with the State and remove the local restriction. Eliminating the restriction will make
it easier for neighborhood-serving markets and convenience stores to find suitable
locations to develop.

There is a brief reference to homebrew in the current code addressing alcohol content.
Homebrew has an extensive State regulation that is completely adequate to regulate home
brewing. Staff recommends removing the local restriction and yielding to the State
regulations for homebrew. State regulations include the total volume that can be brewed
(100 gallons per individual), sets whom and where the alcohol can be consumed, and
regulates the sale of the alcohol.

Land Development Code 17.11 & 17.62
Proposed changes to the Land Development Code address alcohol use as it pertains to
zoning. 17.62 Definitions have been updated to consolidate all alcohol-related definitions
into one area and add definitions for a Bar, Small Brewery, Brewery, Distillery, and
Winery.

Chapter 17.11 District and Corridor Zone Uses is updated to include both the new uses of
Taproom, Small Brewery, and Brewery, Distillery, or Winery, as well as address
permitted zones for the various alcohol uses. The retail use changes include limiting the
locations of bars and taverns. Bars would no longer be permitted in the TC-2, MU, COM,
CS, and IP. Removal from the TC-2 and MU zones is to reduce conflict between bars and
zones that are primarily residential. Removal from the COM, CS, and IP is to remove
bars from zones that are primarily car-oriented. An exception in the use chart has been
carved out to allow bars in conjunction with a manufacturing location in the CS or IP
zones, or within a hotel. These changes are similar for a Tavern, other than no exception
for a hotel. It is also proposed to change taverns and bars from permitted to conditionally
permitted in the TC-1 zone.

The Brew Restaurant Use is currently limited to only the TC-1 zone. This was a
deliberate decision made at the adoption of the use in 2008 to help attract a Brew
Restaurant to downtown. Staff’s position is that expanding the Brew Restaurant zoning to
include other areas where restaurants are located may incentivize additional development
of innovative restaurant options. The proposal is to allow a Brew Restaurant in the COM
and MU zones.

             Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ February 20, 2024
Regarding new uses, the Taproom would be conditionally permitted in the TC-1 zone,
which aligns with proposed change to bar and tavern uses. The Small Brewery use would
be permitted in the IP zone and conditionally permitted in the CS zone. The small
brewery would be limited to 15,000 barrels per year and no more than 10,000 sf. Staff
found that the 15,000 barrels a year is a standard dividing scale between large and small
breweries within the industry. Staff had included a land use for a Brewery, Distillery, or
Winery, but is proposing that the city start small with manufacturing and not permit
larger scale manufacturing in any zone at this time. Staff recommends keeping the
Brewery, Distillery, or Winery use listed in the use table even if it is not permitted to
clarify that larger breweries would not fall under other manufacturing uses in the land use
table.

GENERAL PLAN
The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP), adopted in 2008, identifies this property as Town
Center (TC). The General Plan, a nonregulatory visioning plan, describes TC areas as
being a hub of the City and Valley and reflects a vibrant downtown, with a variety of
commercial, mixed-use, and institutionally uses and activities. The TC designation
covers land from 200 South to 500 North and staggers between 100 East and 200 East
along the eastern side of Downtown Logan. The TC designation staggers between 100
and 150 West along the west side of Downtown.

Land Development Code (LDC) - Zoning
The TC-2, which encourages dense, walkable commercial and residential development in
the downtown area with unique and interesting building designs. This zone allows a
density of up of to 30 units per acre along with a mixture of commercial uses. The City
went through a deliberate and robust process in 2018 to redefine the Town Center zoning
districts which culminated in the adoption of Ordinance Number 18-07. This year-long
zoning process established TC-2 in the outer-ring areas of downtown as a way to buffer
or transition the residential neighborhoods further east and west of Downtown. The goal
of the zoning strategy adopted in 2018 was to further implement the visions expressed in
the General Plan through a methodical transition of uses and structure.

Useable Outdoor Space and Landscaping
The LDC 17.10.070 requires 10% landscaping and an additional 10% useable outdoor
space in the TC-2 zone. The LDC 17.32 generally describes useable outdoor space as

and enjoyment of the residents and their guests of the development. Useable outdoor

recreational areas, and natural open space areas but do not include parking lots,
