VI.   OLD BUSINESS

1. Re-Create 248 Transit Study: Alternative Selection:
Connor Campobasso, Tim Sanderson, and Julia Collins, Transportation Planning Team,
introduced the Re-create 248 project and stated they would address feedback from the
previous Council meeting. The team collectively reviewed the project history, defined the
Locally Preferred Alternative (LPA) and why it’s helpful, discussed how the future
footprint would answer questions and reduce impacts, and asked the Council to
consider action on the LPA resolution. Campobasso stated that through Level One
screening, they were able to narrow it down to two alternatives: exclusive bus lanes and
light rail. Staff recommended side-running bus lanes because they would provide
operational flexibility, reduce the impact, and would be compatible with the current
system. Campobasso indicated that the LPA was the transit option selected by the
community to move forward, which is both a critical step to access grant funding, as well
as a shared milestone that can be used across FTA, Utah Olympic Committee, and
partners. In addition, it also allows for reimbursement of project costs from LPA adoption
forward. Most importantly, selecting the LPA means that if Park City were to get a
federal grant in the future, everything from the LPA adoption and forward will be
federally reimbursable.

Campobasso stated that the next phases of the project will include selecting a concept
to advance. They would refine the design and move the concept to the NEPA review
process. He furthered that the team met with HOAs and several business owners
regarding property impacts and they are committed to working with them throughout the
process. Mayor Dickey asked staff to further define the difference between a dedicated
side-running bus and a BRT. Campobasso stated that the stops would look similar to
the A-Fresh Market stop. Sanderson further explained that BRT is more of a concept,
but that side-running bus lanes are dedicated to buses only, are out of mixed traffic, and
have priority at intersections.

Council Member Toly inquired how Council will be involved in the conversations with
commercial property owners who will be affected by project. Campobasso stated that
many of the big picture concepts will present themselves during the NEPA stage, which
is where staff will have more conversations with Council regarding impacts. Council
Member Toly asked how this conversation would align with the conversation regarding
the Bonanza 5-acre parcel. Chris Eggleton, Economic Development Director, stated that
without moving forward with an LPA today, Brinshore Development, would not have the
necessary information to make those considerations in the plans. Council Member Toly
asked if staff would be meeting with Park City Mountain Resort, Deer Valley, and the
Wintzers on the Iron Horse side of Bonanza Drive, to which Campobasso affirmed.
Council Member Toly asked if reversible flex lanes could be included in this model.
Campobasso stated that it is included and will be evaluated as a design consideration in
the NEPA phase.

Council Member Ciraco stated that he shares similar concerns about private property
owners but added that he would like to move forward with the LPA to see what options
staff return with. Council Member Zegarra stated that he is comfortable moving forward
because the project remains under Park City's control, consideration for wildlife is being
given, meaningful funding options may now be pursued, and the LPA is not legally
binding. Council Member Parigian asked what UDOT’s core goals for this project are.
Campobasso stated that it refers to the charter goals developed with UDOT, which
means that their operations are maintained so that travel lanes are not reduced. Council
Member Parigian asked how the chosen method was evaluated in order to determine
that it was the most supported alternative by the community. Campobasso stated that
there were several outreach events, workshops, visits to the Prospector community, and
a year-long hotline for those with questions. Council Member Parigian inquired about
the changes made since the last meeting. Campobasso stated that there were changes
to the maps and outreach to business owners. Council Member Parigian asked why
there is a rush to sign the LPA. Sanderson stated that Council direction was to move
quickly and that staff is encouraging the LPA because that would mean the City is
reimbursed for the project. Eggleton added that the LPA signals to the community and
its partners the direction the Council would like to take. Council Member Parigian asked
why the LPA decision requires a resolution. Sanderson stated that it shows the federal
government and UDOT that Park City is committed to the project.

1. Open and Public Meetings Act (OPMA), Government Records Access and
Management Act (GRAMA), and Ethics Act Training:
Margaret Plane, City Attorney, presented on Open Public Meetings Act (OPMA),
GRAMA, the State’s Public Records Act, and Municipal Ethics Act (MOEEA). Plane
presented a short video on the Open and Public Meetings Act, which ensures
government actions and deliberations are conducted openly. Plane shared that Council
Members may not text about any item on the agenda during an open and public City
Council Meeting. She stated that in 2025, the City handled 581 GRAMA records
