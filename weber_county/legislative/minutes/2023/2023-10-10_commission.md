F. PRESENTATIONS:
   1. Goal Foundation Presentation:
            Chair Froerer: We are very honored to have a presentation from Eric Bauman, Executive Director of the
            GOAL Foundation. They do a lot of great things for this community, for the county, and the Wasatch front,
            primarily in the marathon business. The Ogden Marathon is one of the premier marathons not only in Utah
            but in the western United States.
            Eric Bauman (Executive Director Goal Foundation): It is an honor to be part of an organization that works so
            closely with the County and together we provide so much value. The mission of GOAL is to inspire the greater
            Ogden community and its visitors to “Get Out And Live” by participating, volunteering, and spectating at
            recreational and cultural events, programs, and activities. We do this in three ways. The first is the production
            of events. The other two pieces we do are supporting events as they come through Weber County with
            volunteer recruitment and volunteer management. Final piece is providing community programming through
            a combination of youth programming and family programming free to the community. These are all geared to
            get people living healthy lifestyles. GOAL stands for Get Out And Live.
            The produced events are great partnerships with the county. The first one to kick off our event year is the
            Winter Running Series starts in February with four different races. The 5k, 10k, and 10 mile are in the lower
            valley while the half marathon starts in Eden Park. Last year we had just shy of 600 participants in the half
            marathon, which lines up well for training for the Ogden Marathon. The second event was this year’s 22nd
            Ogden Marathon which had over 6300 participants from 47 states and 7 countries. The Ogden Marathon
            course is certified as a Boston Marathon qualifier with 98 participants qualifying this year. It was voted the
            Best Marathon in Utah for 2023. The free Finish Line Festival had 15k attendees this year with an overall
            $1.6M in economic impact. The third event is the El Doce endurance mountain bike event at Powder Mountain.
            There are 6 hour and 12 hour endurance races that totaled 443 participants this year. There is a festival at the
            base of Powder Mountain during the events. The Northern Utah Trail Fest had its second run this year with
            substantial growth over its first year. It’s a collaborative event with Weber County Parks and Recreation as
            well as Trails Foundation Northern Utah. It’s a festival for all trail users with mountain bike race, trail running
            race along with a large festival with four bands, food trucks, a kid’s zone, guided hikes, and the Nature Center
            with their telescopes.
            The community programming events included 3 weeks of youth mountain bike camps with 59 participants, 4
            weeks of indoor rock climbing camps at The Front Climbing with 74 participants, 4 free guided community
            hikes, 6 guided hikes with the Golden Hours Senior Center, free yoga in the park during Wednesdays in
            September, and Shoe Give Back programs for kids with Youth Impact and actively aging seniors at the Golden
            Hours Senior Center.
            I’m completing four years and in November I will be retiring. There are so many pieces to all of this and none
            of it would be possible without your support. I have had nothing but overwhelming support from the
            Commission during that four years. There is a huge law enforcement component, Scott Mendoza and his help
            with the permitting process is fantastic to work with, Todd Ferrario and Parks and Recreation are so
            accommodating, Emergency Management helped us through our road cave in in the canyon a week before the
            marathon and all of the flooding. There are so many more and I don’t want to miss anybody. It’s been an
            honor and a gift working in an organization to see the impact that is being made.
         Chair Froerer: GOAL Foundation is one of our prime non-profits that we see huge payback with the
         community. You help us showcase what is great about Weber County. Thank you for all you have done.
         Weber County and the Wasatch front from what you have done.
         Commissioner Harvey: Eric, you’ve been a good spark plug for that organization, you have lifted people up,

       INSTALLATION OF PUBLIC IMPROVEMENTS ASSOCIATED WITH THE WEBER/MORGAN
       CHILDREN’S JUSTICE SUBDIVISION.
         Rod Layton (Children Justice Center): As you know we have been working on this project for the last three
         years to build a new CJC at 1845 Jackson on 1.89 acre property owned by the Ogden School District. The
         county had to submit application for a subdivision, which is complete. The subdivision will require certain
         improvements must be made. To do that, financial guarantees is needed from Weber County to Ogden City
         for $100,400.65 for those improvements. As those improvements are completed that money will be returned
         to Weber County.

    2. APPROVAL OF A CONTRACT WITH COLLEEN COEBERGH TO PROVIDE SPECIAL
       CONFLICT COUNSEL.
         James Retallick (Public Defender): The County Attorney filed 11 co-defendant RICO actions (Racketeer,
         Influence, and Corrupt Organizations) . The allegation was there was an organized ring bringing drugs into
         the correctional facility and distributing those. With that number of co-defendants, we had to find attorneys
         that were not conflicted out. Between some private attorneys, contract attorneys, and our full time attorneys,
         we were able to assign all but one. We were requested and were granted $10k from the IDC to cover the last
         defendant. This contract will be reimbursed by the IDC and they said to resubmit if the total exceeds $10k.
         Commissioner Bolos moved to approval of a contract with Colleen Coebergh to provide special conflict
         counsel; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Bolos – aye; Chair Froerer – aye

    2. Public hearing to and take action on a request to vacate the west side public utility easement within lot 89 of
       the Elkhorn Subdivision Phase 4, includes approval of related Ordinance.

         Felix Lleverino (Planning): This subdivision was originally designed for some roads out from the main road,
         Elkhorn Drive. The developer vacated these little stubs out so road portion but left behind 10 foot public
         utility easements in the middle of a lot where they do not belong. The owner, Dan Green, requests the
         easements be moved to their appropriate location along the edge of the lot boundary. The developer, county
         attorney, and the county engineer have reviewed and found appropriate. The owner has collected letters of
         consent from utility providers consented along with the Wolf Creek Water District agreeing to work with the
         owner to relocate a secondary water line. Staff recommends approval to vacate. The relocation will be
         covered by the deed and will be separate from the Ordinance to vacate.
