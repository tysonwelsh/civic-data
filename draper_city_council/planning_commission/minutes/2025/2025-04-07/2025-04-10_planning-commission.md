       B.      Action Item: Mercer Mountain Estates Preliminary Subdivision Plat Request.
               (Administrative Action)
               On the request of Pratt Dimond, representing Terra Koa, LLC, a Preliminary
               Subdivision Plat for 25.912 Acres located at 2635 East 15040 South. Known as
               Application SUB-245-2018. Staff Contact: Jennifer Jastremsky, 801-576-6328,
               jennifer.jastremsky@draperutah.gov.

Community Development Director, Jennifer Jastremsky presented the Staff Report and stated that the
property is located near Hidden Canyon Estates off of Suncrest Drive. The proposal is to have an
access gate and access road at Canyon Point Road. An aerial map was displayed to identify the
location of the property. There are a mix of land use designations and the City has agreed to some
land swaps and changes to get a reduced grade on the proposed road. According to the Zoning map,
most properties are zoned RR-22, which has a residential one-half acre lot standard with a few
properties near Canyon Point that are zoned RM in the C3 Zone. This subdivision will be a gated
community. The property was removed from the Suncrest Development Agreement in 2015 and is
tied to the current Code. There is a very specific Gate Ordinance that provides a turnaround, a pull-
out lane for the mailbox, and a call box for guests.

Ms. Jastremsky reported that the City has a Public Access Easement to facilitate public use of the
City owned property adjacent to Canyon Point Rd and Suncrest Dr. There are 50 lots in the
subdivision and a retention pond on the south end. The lot sizes range from 12,617 to 25,000 square
feet in size. The lots located on the cul-de-sac are the largest. Substantial cuts and fills will be needed
within the subdivision to construct the road. The applicant entered into an Amended Development
Agreement that was presented to the City Council earlier in the year that approved additional fill areas
on City property. The Development Agreement also approves slope easements to be placed on City
property for the cuts and fills that need to take place for the roadway. They will be recorded with the
final plat and allow the applicant to complete the grading to construct the road. A rendering of the
proposed road was presented and showed where the proposed homes will be located.

Commissioner Nixon asked how many of the proposed homes are allowed in a single family
subdivision before a secondary access road is required. Ms. Jastremsky reported that the number is
typically 30 but the City approved 50 as part of the Development Agreement. The applicant, Pratt
Dimond, confirmed that the requested change to the road design was from the City so the grade of
the road will be reduced by about 2% and ultimately be safer. Appreciation was expressed to staff
for the work done for the road designs.

       C.      Public Hearing: H&B Supply, LLC Home Occupation Conditional Use Permit
               Request.
               (Administrative Action)
               On the Request of Nicholas Roble Representing H&B Supply, LLC., a Conditional
               Use Permit for a Home Occupation providing Firearm Transfer Services on 0.18 acres
               located at Approximately 2166 East Eagle Chase Drive. Known as Application 2025-
               0023-USE,        Staff     Contact:      Nick      Whittaker,      801-576-6522,
               nick.whittaker@draperutah.gov.

City Planner, Nick Whittaker, presented the Staff Report and displayed Vicinity, Aerial, Land Use,
and Zoning maps of the property. The property is a residential, low-medium density land use and is
designated as RM in the Suncrest Development Agreement. The proposed Home Occupation
Conditional Use Permit (“CUP”) is a Firearms Transfer Service. When someone orders or sells a
firearm online to a local buyer, the Transfer Service receives the firearm first, ensuring that all
requirements are fulfilled for the buyer to obtain the firearm. The buyer would then come to the
applicant’s home, complete documentation, and receive the firearm. The process takes about 30
minutes to complete the process. The applicant anticipates one to two buyers to come to the home
each week. The Site Plan demonstrates the location of the customer’s proposed designated off-street
parking space. The floor plan shows entry through the north side of the home and the designated
space where the transaction will occur. Mr. Whittaker next presented an analysis of the potential
impacts and mitigation and stated that the identified potential impacts include customers coming to
the home, customer vehicle parking, and related traffic. The proposed mitigation involves limiting
the anticipated number of customers coming to the home per week to two. Appointments will be
required and limited to one customer at a time. Reserved off-street parking is provided for customer
vehicles. An exterior photo of the property was displayed.

Commissioner Fowler asked if this is a permissible use for a home occupation in the neighborhood
and if it is part of the Suncrest Development. Mr. Whittaker did not know if it was part of the Suncrest
Covenants, Codes, and Restrictions (“CC&Rs”) but confirmed that it is an allowed use per City Code.
Planning Manager, Todd Draper, reported that “home office” is listed as a permitted use and as a
conditional use, the applicant can seek deviations from the other criteria such as not having customers.
The deviation in this case is to allow customers to come to the home. While the property is part of
the Suncrest Homeowners Association (“HOA”), staff does not enforce the HOA rules. The HOA
will determine how to enforce its rules but it is a different process and could override any decision
made by the Commission. Ms. Jastremsky explained that the property owner must abide by the HOA
rules and adhere to City standards. As a result, approval is required from both bodies. There is
nothing, however, in the Development Agreement for the Suncrest area, that prevents home

occupations. The Suncrest Development Agreement requires the City to seek HOA approval for new
homes so that architectural standards can be verified.

Commissioner Fowler stated that one public comment was received that referenced standards but it
was unclear whether they were addressing part of the Development Agreement or the HOA
regulations. Staff’s recollection was that they were addressing the HOA regulations.

Commissioner Shah asked how HOA approvals have been handled in the past. Ms. Jastremsky stated
that staff does not look to enforce HOA approvals with few exceptions. Mr. Whittaker stated that in
this case, the Commission’s role is to analyze City Code and determine whether the request is
compatible.

Commissioner Nixon noted that additional emailed comments from residents were received earlier in
the day.

The applicant, Nicholas Roble, introduced himself and clarified his role as a Type 1 Federal Firearms
License Holder (“FFL”). He is a firearms dealer who is allowed to purchase, sell, and transfer
standard firearms across state lines. He confirmed that he is not required to have a storefront and can
conduct business in a home office setting.

Mr. Roble indicated that he read the written public comments and can work with neighbors who
expressed concerns. Mr. Roble addressed the public comment related to the CC&Rs mandating that
the activity not be detectable by sight, smell, or sound from outside the dwelling unit. He confirmed
that business conducted from the home will not be discernable from outside the dwelling.

Chair Adams confirmed that Mr. Roble will be conducting Bureau of Criminal Investigations (“BCI”)
background checks on all transfers. He uses an online check system and web checks are conducted
in accordance with federal law.

Chair Adams commented that unlike other home businesses, the backgrounds of Mr. Roble’s clients
are thoroughly investigated. The result will be to bring a potentially higher caliber of customer to the
neighborhood compared to other home businesses that do not require background checks.

Commissioner Squire asked why background checks cannot be completed before the client comes to
pick up the weapon. Mr. Roble explained that ATF regulations specify that the client must fill out
Form 4473 in his presence after which the background check is completed. Chair Adams explained
that a gun cannot be purchased off the internet and must go through a third party where a background
check is conducted.

Commissioner Squire asked Mr. Roble about his previous experience as an FFL. Mr. Roble reported
that he is a Retired Special Agent with 18 years of military and law enforcement experience. He is a
disabled veteran and this is a small business that he owns.

Chair Adams opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing was
closed.

throughout the day, which has a much different impact than what the applicant has specified in his

City Planner, Todd Taylor presented the Staff Report and stated that the subject properties are located
north of 13800 South off of 300 East. There are two parcels with each having an existing home. The
current land use designation is Residential Low Medium-Density and the subject property is
surrounded on the north, east, and south sides by that designation. Across 300 East there is a
Residential Medium-High Density land use designation. The current zoning of the northern parcel is
RA-1 and RA2 on the southern parcel. There is also RA1 and RA2 on the east side of the property
and the north and south and across 300 East is RM1. The applicant is requesting to go from the
Residential Low-Medium Density land use designation to Residential Medium-High Density with a
rezone from RA1 and RA2 to RM1. A Google street view of the subject properties was presented
showing the two homes. Site photos of the southern home were also presented along with the
driveways of the northern home.

A historical question was raised regarding the two adjacent properties to the south parcel being one-
half acre in size but being in an RA1 zone. There was confusion as to why the property was not
rezoned with the rest of the development. Ms. Jastremsky was unsure of the history.

A question was raised about the properties across the street and if they are in the RM1 Zone.
Mr. Taylor explained that the homes currently being built are part of Concord Farm Lane and he
confirmed that they are zoned RM1.

The applicant, Robert Lanthier, reported that he is the owner of a small business in the City. He
purchased the business about five years ago and the home has existed since 1947. He considered it a
nuisance. His goal was to build on the backside for his personal home and develop smaller lots on
300 East. He noted that housing is not affordable. He hoped to develop a private lane, if possible.

Chair Adams opened the public hearing.

Matt Smith , resident, gave his address, which is just south of the subject property. He thanked the
Planning Commission for their service to the community. Mr. Smith considered Mr. Lanthier to be
a friend. Mr. Smith was opposed to the proposed changes to the Land Use Map and Zoning Map
because they are inconsistent with the surrounding area. The current Development Plan proposes to
build seven homes in the space, which is inconsistent, especially with development on the east side
of 300 East. Also, if the current proposal to change the Land Use Map and the zoning changes
approved, anyone could change the development plan. His understanding was that according to the
Municipal Code, The RM1 designation could allow for apartments, townhomes, twin homes, and
condominiums. He also believed that the land use and zoning change could serve as a precedent for
additional changes to the north, south, and east of the property. He was concerned that the proposed
change will devalue the properties to the north, south, and east of the development. Mr. Smith

reported that he bought his home 10 years ago and would be severely disappointed if the proposal
advances. He respectfully asked that the Planning Commission vote to deny the proposed changes.

Carolyn Phippen , resident, reported that she lives a few houses away on Brown Farm Lane. She
noted that the area consists of one-half and one-acre properties. Infill of up to seven homes on 1½
acres is completely inconsistent with what exists. What is taking place on the west side of the street

Commissioner Shah agreed and stated that with a Development Agreement he would be more inclined

redeveloped or subdivided with the exception of one lot directly to the north of the north parcel.
