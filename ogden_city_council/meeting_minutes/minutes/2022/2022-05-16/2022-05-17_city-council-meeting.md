Proposed Ordinance 2022-14 vacating a portion of 1° Street_between
Washington Boulevard and Adams Avenue and authorizing the Mayor to
hold the vacated portion for sale

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed right-of-way vacation came
before the Council for consideration. The memo stated a developer is requesting to vacate a portion of the 1‘ Street right-of-way
to make room for a large development at 120 Washington Boulevard. The portion requested to be vacated is approximately 15,793
square feet, or .363 acres. This right-of-way was originally preserved to allow a corridor for a future road to be connected between
Adams Avenue and Washington Boulevard, but it was never developed. It is important to note that a portion of Ist Street has
already been vacated on the 101 Adams Avenue property. This is not a complete right-of-way, and it would be unfeasible to
develop a connecting road on this remaining portion. If the right-of-way is vacated, the developer would still need to obtain
ownership of the land before it could be developed. Vacating the right-of-way simply removes the City’s ability to access and
develop a road on the property and clears the way for alternative development to take place on the land. The Council discussed
this item during the April 19, 2022 Council Meeting. There was some concern about nearby development, and the Council wanted
further information about the purpose of the right-of-way vacation and the associated rezones before deciding on this item. Staff
from the Administration and Council office met with the developer and viewed the site plan. The main reason for vacating and
rezoning this right-of-way is to create more space for parking and pickleball courts. The developer has also stated that this will be
an opportunity to clean up the area north of the development. The rest of the development will include a large apartment building,

Proposed Ordinance 2022-18 amending the Zoning Map to reclassify
property located at 101 and 131 Adams Avenue from R-1-6 to C-3 to
develop a multi-family/mixed use project
A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed rezone of 101 and 131
Adams Avenue came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the properties in question are located to the north
and east of the old Wangsgard’s grocery store at 120 Washington Boulevard. Each of the properties has an R-1-6 designation, but
the developer is requesting to rezone a total of approximately .842 acres to C-3 so that they may be incorporated into a larger
multi-family/mixed-use development. Below is a description of the rezone request on each property:
e 101 Adams Avenue: This is a .5-acre vacant lot. The request is to rezone .308-acres of the rear portion of the
lot. This parcel is owned by the petitioner.
e 131 Adams Avenue: This is a .34-acre lot with a home that faces Adams Avenue. The request is to rezone .171
acres of the back yard. This parcel is not owned by the petitioner.
e 1“ Street Right-of-Way: The request is to rezone a .363-acre portion of the right-of-way between Washington
Blvd. and Adams Avenue. This is a vacant piece of land. Part of the right-of-way is owned by the petitioner,
and the other part is owned by an adjacent property owner. The right-of-way is owned by the city.
Relative to 101 and 131 Adams Avenue, it is important to note that if the rear portions of these lots are rezoned, then the
rezoned portion would need to be subdivided out and combined with the rest of the C-3 properties. 131 Adams Avenue has a
home on it, but this rezone/subdivision will not affect the home. 101 Adams Avenue is a vacant lot, but there will be sufficient
land to build a single home on the property even after the rezone and subdivision take place. The Planning Commission
recommended approval of the rezone. The Planning Commission also recommends the following conditions:

Proposed Ordinance 2022-21 to allow food product manufacturing for
human consumption within the BEI Zone

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed text amendment for the
Business Exchange Industrial (BEI) zone came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated a petitioner has requested
to change requirements in the BEI zone to allow for manufacturing of food for human consumption. The purpose of the change is
to allow an ice cream manufacturing facility. The BEI zone does not currently allow food manufacturing because the zone was
originally intended to be an area for manufacturing, research, and development of outdoor recreation products; however, the
Planning Commission recommended approval as they felt that food manufacturing should be permissible because there is a food
manufacturer, Ogden Distillery, across the street.

Deputy Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and presented an aerial map to orient the Council to the
location of the properties located within the BEI zone. There is just one undeveloped lot remaining in the BEI zone and there is
presently an opportunity to locate a business on that lot if the text amendment is approved.

Proposed Ordinance 2022-22 adopting the proposed Tentative Budget of
Ogden City for Fiscal Year 2022-2023

A memo from Council staff regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2023 Tentative Budget came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated on May 3, 2022, Mayor Caldwell presented his proposed Fiscal Year 2022-2023 Budget.
Administration is requesting a few minor changes to the proposed FY2023 Budget prior to adopting the tentative budget; key
changes include a decrease to the General Fund to reduce the payment for the 17th Street Development Agreement, moving funds
within the Police Department budget to cover wages of three (3) police officer positions, increasing the line item in the Sewer
Fund to cover additional sewer district charges, and moving funds within the Refuse Fund to cover a Clean & Green program. The
changes adjust the City Budget up by $244,325. A tentative budget is adopted after holding an initial public hearing. A second
public hearing is held at the time of adoption of the final budget. During the budget hearings, the Council outlines their proposed
changes to the budget and accepts public input. Once the final public hearing is closed, the Council makes its changes and adopts
the final City budget. By State law, a budget must be adopted by June 30. An exception to the timing is allowed if the Council is
considering a proposed property tax increase. This evening, the Council will consider setting a public hearing on the FY2022-
2023 Tentative budget for June 7, 2022.

Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide
Council staff's review of the Mayor’s FY2023 Tentative Budget. The Council has adopted financial principles for FY2022-2023,
including:

Conservative revenue estimates:
Balanced budget;
Employee salaries as a budget priority;
Investments;
Fund Balance;

e Operating Reserves.

The Council’s budget goals and guidelines include establishing goals of the upcoming budget, setting policy regarding
tax rates, and establishing priorities for spending. The Mayor’s proposed budget includes a modest property tax increase to
generate an additional $2.8 million to cover funding priorities for employee wage increases, 19 new employment positions,
American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds, and the Marshall White Center (MWC) Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) Project.
Ms. Eller-Smith then presented a chart comparing general fund revenues from the adopted FY2022 budget with the FY2023
tentative budget; revenues are projected to increase by nearly $11 million, or 15.69 percent. Using the same comparison, the total
City budget will increase roughly $35 million, or 16.46% when compared to the adopted FY2022 budget. She summarized the
proposed staffing changes contemplated in the proposed budget; 16 new positions will be assigned to the General Fund and seven
new positions will be assigned to other funds, including Airport, Medical, Miscellaneous Grants, and Golf. In total, there are 23
new positions, four eliminated positions, 22 reclassifications, and 2 title changes. She then provided a chart comparing year over
year wage and benefit costs from the FY2022 adopted budget and the FY2023 proposed budget; in total, wage and benefit costs
