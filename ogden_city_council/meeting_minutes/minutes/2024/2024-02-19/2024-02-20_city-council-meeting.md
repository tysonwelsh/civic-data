Proposed petition vacating a portion of Cahoon Street between F and G
Avenues in order to develop a mixed-use project
           A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed partial street vacation came
before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposal is to vacate a portion of Cahoon Street, located between F
and G avenue, that is 137.9 feet long with a total area of 9,320 square feet. The petitioners understand the City’s need to plan for
utilities in the area and have proposed to offer the City a utility easement along the north side of the property. The next step for
consideration of this proposal is to hold a public hearing during the City Council meeting.
           Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and stated the petitioners are seeking the vacation of right-of-way to
develop a mixed-use project on the site with commercial on the ground floor and residential above. A rezoning petition will need
to be submitted for consideration, as the current zoning is Single-family Residential (R-1-5) and Regional Commercial (C-3).
Planning Division staff found that the petitioner’s concept plan is consistent with several of the General Plan’s goals and
principles, including the plan’s economic development and housing goals. The subject portion of the right-of-way is unimproved
and is not currently used by the public. The City Engineering Division has expressed the need for the City to reserve the portion of
right-of-way for future utility planning. The petitioners have proposed to provide easements or land dedication for utility routing.
Although the petitioners have proposed to provide area for utility routing, Planning and Engineering staff still expressed concerns
with the proposed vacation because the Utah Department of Transportation’s (UDOT’s) plans for the nearby I-15/24th Street
interchange are not yet finalized. He concluded that during the Planning Commission meeting on December 6, 2023, Planning
Division staff recommended denial of the proposed vacation, and the Planning Commission, during a public meeting,
recommended denial of the proposal, finding that the potential for material injury existed relative to the City’s ability to plan for
utilities in the area.
           Mr. Brierley then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the City’s desired water utility infrastructure
improvements for the area; property tax revenue implications for the petitioner’s proposed use of the subject property; and the
basis for the Planning Commission’s recommendation for denial of the petition. For a copy of the PowerPoint presentation in its
entirety, see the meeting information packet.
           Chair Richey invited input from the petitioner.
           Luke Kanard and Steve McCleary approached the Council and also used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss
their development plans for the property and options for negotiating a favorable outcome for the City and themselves to provide
by way of an easement for the future water utility project needed in the area. Mr. McCleary emphasized that he feels the City’s
position on the petition is unfair and somewhat punitive and it is restricting his private property rights. He has proposed options
for the City to consider and is willing to be flexible in providing the easement, but the City staff seems unwilling to entertain those
options.

Proposed Ordinance 2024-3 rezoning property located at 218 Chester
Street from R-1-6 to R-2S in order to allow for owner occupied townhomes
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed rezone application came
before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Rick Southwick has submitted a petition to rezone two parcels, totaling
2.38 acres located at 218 Chester Street, from R-1-6 (single-family residential zone) to R-2S (two-family and small lot residential
zone). The developer’s intent is to establish zoning on the property that allows for owner occupied townhomes.
          Deputy Planning Manager Simpson summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on
the applicant’s development proposal of the subject property and to summarize the Planning Commission’s recommendation of
approval of the proposed rezone. For a copy of the PowerPoint presentation in its entirety see the information packet.
          Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-3, entitled:
                   “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the zoning map of Ogden City, as adopted by Section 15-3-3 of
                   the Ogden Municipal Code, to reclassify as two-family and small lot residential zone (R-2S) property
                   heretofore classified as single-family residential zone (R-1-6), generally located at 218 Chester Street, and
                   providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final passage.”
          Council member Myers inquired about the cost of running utilities to the property if it were to be developed as single-
family homes rather than townhomes. Mr. Simpson stated that for a typical infill project with just a few single-family homes,
installing required utilities may be cost-prohibitive. However, those costs can be more easily absorbed for a multi-family project
like the one that has been proposed. The beauty of the R-2S zone is that it provides increased density, but places restrictions on
unit ownership responsive to public concerns about increases in rental properties.
          Vice Chair White asked how many units can be located on the property under the R-2S versus the R-1-6 zone. Mr.
Simpson discussed minimum lot sizes for the R-1-6 zone and R-2S zones; the minimum lot size for the R-2S zone is much smaller
and the developer will be able to build several more units on the property under that zone.
          Council member Hyer asked if unit stacking is allowed in the R-2S zone, to which Mr. Simpson answered no.
          Chair Richey invited input from the petitioner.
          Gary Cannon and Rick Southwick approached and discussed their plans to build a project that will include owner-
occupied units versus rental units. Mr. Cannon provided home ownership statistics for the State of Utah; the cost of living and
housing prices continue to increase and 75 percent of eligible property owners in the State of Utah could not quality for a median
priced home in the State of Utah if they did not already own a home. He stated that the type of product that he and Mr. Southwick
plan to build on the property is in demand at the present time; any improvements will be brought up to City standards and they
will pursue local builders and developers to complete the project. A deeded right of way access has been recorded on the property
and there are no access issues. They concluded by expressing their excitement about the City’s adoption of the R-2S zone and they
hope to build a project that will set a high standard for future developments.
          Council member Hyer asked what tools will be used to ensure owner-occupancy of the units. Mr. Southwick stated that
the zone requires that each unit be individually platted, which will be done. Marketing of the project will include references to
available grant money for first-time home buyers, which will also increase the restrictions on owner-occupancy. He stated that
over the past four years, the City has approved over 2,000 new units and of those, there is just one townhome project that was built
for owner-occupancy. He noted that more of that type of product is needed in Ogden, and he will pursue deed restrictions if
needed.
          Mr. Simpson added that the City could also consider a development agreement that would impose owner-occupancy
requirements. Council member Choberka stated that a development agreement will not control the occupancy of units once they
are sold by the developer. Mr. Simpson stated that is correct, but it is something that could help to ensure the developer’s
commitment to owner-occupancy is memorialized in a recorded document.

Proposed Ordinance 2024-4 amending standards for group dwellings
           A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed zoning text amendments
pertaining to group dwellings came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Mike Bastian has submitted a petition
to update the City’s group dwelling ordinance. Group dwelling developments are those that have more than one residential
building on a lot, and which are planned as a single development. The group dwellings ordinance, Chapter 10 of the City’s zoning
code, provides standards for the development of small lot group dwellings, neighborhood group dwellings, and mixed-use group
dwellings. Mr. Bastian has submitted the petition in an effort to provide better standards for development under the group
dwelling ordinance and to address excessive square footage requirements and setback requirements. Mr. Bastian indicates that
better regulations would allow for the development of better multiple family residential product.
           Senior Planner Rypien summarized the memo and stated the proposed amendments address the issues petitioned by Mr.
Bastian as well as other elements of the group dwelling ordinance. This includes updated definitions, changes to lot area
requirements, setback and building orientation amendments, landscaping requirement changes, amendments to parking
requirements, open space and recreation space updates, and the removal of density bonuses for group dwellings. The ordinance
itself also contains a number of grammatical updates and changes to the wording of certain sections. Many of these changes are
not substantive and do not change the underlying policy within this section of code.
           Chair Richey invited input from Mr. Bastian.
           Mr. Bastian asked for clarification on the number of building types that will be required in a project of various sizes. Mr.
Rypien stated that for a lot size of two to four acres, two building types will be required and for lots larger than four acres, three
building types will be required. Mr. Bastian discussed the difference between building ‘style’ and building ‘type’ and noted that it
is more difficult to achieve multiple building types than it is to achieve different building styles.
         Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-4, entitled:
                   “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Section 15-2-4,
                   Section 15-2-5, Section 15-2-6, Section 15-2-8, and Section 15-2-20 to amend and adopt definitions relative to
                   group dwelling units; by amending Chapter 10 of Title 15 to address group dwelling units; by amending 15-41-
                   7 to revise uses relative to group dwelling units; and by providing that this ordinance shall become effective
                   immediately upon posting after final passage.”
         Chair Richey then called for public input regarding the proposal.
         Angel Castillo stated she is supportive of these creative ideas but encouraged the Council to pursue the entire solution of
allowing small buildings upon the smaller lots.
         Rick Southwick stated he feels this is a very positive amendment and the changes will enhance the quality of group
dwelling projects in the community.

dwellings. Mr. Pate stated he will save his public comments for later in the meeting.

approval of that zone for the Chester Street property and Administrative staff has taken notes of the concerns expressed by the

tonight. He then referenced Mr. Satow’s comments; he has asked City Attorney Williams to provide an update on the Q Factor
projects as well as the Brown’s Ice Cream building.

property and they have expressed their willingness to follow the requirements that were placed on the previous owner of the
property, which includes completing improvements to secure a certificate of occupancy by the fall of 2024. He stated, however,
