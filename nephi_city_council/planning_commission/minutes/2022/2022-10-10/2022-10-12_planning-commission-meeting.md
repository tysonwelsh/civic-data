Work Session 6:00

      1.      Planning Commission By-Laws review
      Commissioner Peterson opened the work session. The time was turned over to Shauna
      for review of the planning commission by-laws. Shauna stated that City Attorney Kasey
      Wright reviewed the by-laws and made the necessary corrections. She stated that
      he only made one change to the document.

      Much discussion was had regarding term limits. It was mentioned that Karl has been on
      the commission since 2010. Shauna brought up the point that the city council may
      impose a term limit, but this is an advisory council. Heather said that she values Karl’s
      opinion that comes as a result of those years of experience. Donald Ball, resident,
      suggested that commissioners could serve on the panel for 8 years. At the end of the 8
      year term, their name could be submitted to the mayor for a performance review. Based
      on the review, the commissioner could be re-appointed for another 4 years. Skip, Karl, &
      Heather all voiced their approval of Donald’s idea. Ann suggested the verbiage: “The
      mayor may choose to extend an invitation to continue service,” and said it should be
      added after “8 year term.” Skip brought up the fact that 1 term is not enough time to
      learn everything.

      Shauna stated that she feels as the major items on the agenda are checked off, we can
      start to focus more on training. Karl asked if public notice is given for work meetings, to
      which Shauna responded that it is. Shauna also mentioned that, at a meeting she
      attended last week, that some cities are starting to limit public comment at their
      meetings. Fran expressed that if people come to the meetings, they usually care about
      what is happening. She suggested that instead of limiting public comment, a time limit
      could be enforced.

Alan asked if a newly-appointed mayor could technically “clean house” and appoint a
whole new council. Shauna confirmed that it could happen.

Fran asked if they could vote to keep the chair in longer, if they are doing a good job and
no one else wants the responsibility. Ann asked if they should eliminate the statement,
“no more than 2 consecutive years.” Cory suggested they leave it in so that someone
else can have a chance at the position. This prevents “power hungry” situations. Ann
reminded the commission that this doesn’t go into effect until the city council approves it.
Fran agreed that it should be kept in, but the person appointed can turn it down.
Discussion was had about how the secretary is appointed. The suggestion was made to
change “city clerk” to “recorder.”

Turning to Article 5, Shauna pointed out that a conflict of interest within the commission
needs to be on file with the city. That doesn’t mean the commissioner can’t vote or
comment; the conflict of interest just needs to be made known. Shauna said she will look
into this- do we need something on file? Alan doesn’t like the idea and doesn’t feel it’s
ethical to be forced to vote on something when there is a conflict of interest. He would
rather see the vote tabled until the next meeting when someone else could vote in his
place. Heather reminded him that the commission is an advisory board. Skip state that
while that is true, it’s also why we’re transparent. He stated that commissioners should
abstain from voting in the instance of conflict of interest, to which Alan and Ann both
agreed.

Ann suggested adding “close public hearing” as a separate item. She also stated that
“public comment” and “commission discussion” needs to be separate. The order should
go as follows, “Staff presentation, Public comment, Close hearing, Action item-
Commission discussion.”

Ann asked if there were any other questions or concerns, and stated that this can easily
be adjusted. Shauna said she will make the appropriate edits and have Attorney Wright
review it again.

Cory made a motion to take a short break before the regular meeting started, and Karl
seconded the motion. The vote was unanimous and the work session was closed at 6:48
p.m.

6.   138 kV Project Plan Zoning Options
     Seth explained that the city is anticipating putting together a high voltage
     transmission line. He showed a map of the current substation and the proposed


                                                                             5|Page

     new line. He said the larger substation between Nephi and Mona is connected to
     a number of power sources and is a very reliable substation. By putting in our
     own 138 kV line, we go from a capacity of 29 megawatts to 85 megawatts. He
     said in order to connect the necessary properties, there is a proposed annexation
     for that area. Ann asked what the motivation is for connecting to this substation.
     Seth and Skip both said that it will be much cheaper than Rocky Mountain
     Power. Seth showed the commissioners the proposed zoning once the properties
     are annexed. Fran asked if the property owners asking for annexation are aware
     that this includes a zone change. Seth said that they should be aware, and said
     one of the purposes of this project is to manage growth by using zoning. Alan
     asked if the intent was to put this project in that area because you anticipate
     annexing more land going in that direction? Seth said no; the purpose is to get
     the transmission line. Fran asked how this aligns with their concept plan. Shauna
     told her that it aligns exactly with their concept plan. Seth told the commissioners
     that he wrote an 11-page staff report discussing how this project connects to the
     general plan and which goals it meets, and which goals it doesn’t meet. It’s called
     the Level 2 Staff analysis. Ann requested a link for easy access, and Seth agreed
     to share it. Seth stated that when utilities are extended, growth usually follows.
     Heather asked if there are any proposed parks for this area. Seth confirmed that
     this quadrant doesn’t have any as of right now. Fran pointed out that they should
     have put in some sort of a walking path on the north end of town, amidst all the
     discussions concerning the new property developments. Heather suggested that
     a 55+ community would be nice in that area because of the proximity to the
     hospital. Seth suggested that perhaps a limit could be put on rezones after a
     certain percentage is developed. He told the commissioners that no decision is
     needed tonight; he just wanted to introduce the concept for future discussion.
     Seth stated that when you urbanize or develop irrigated land, you use about 25-
     40% of the water that is currently being used on that land. Alan replied that you
     also lose 100% of the agriculture on that land. He asked if this is the only location
     suitable for the substation. Seth answered yes- it’s not exact, but it needs to be
     parallel to that line. Skip stated that it’s the easiest, most direct, and most cost-
     efficient route. Fran asked if this matches the plan and no one disagrees, can we
     just pass it on to the city council right now? Ann would like to think about it for a
     bit first. Shauna thinks it would be nice to have a corridor of beauty when
     entering the city, rather than entering straight upon commercial property. Seth
     mentioned the Red Barn in Santaquin and said that agribusiness or agritourism is
     a way to preserve valuable farmland. Ann suggested that the topic be left for the
     commissioners to process and visit it again next month.

     b.     Staff Presentation
            Shauna presented the Title 10 updates to the commission. She stated

            property rights and Fran said it sounded like economic discrimination.
            Heather mentioned that if more open space is required, the building will
            be smaller. Shauna said she will check with other cities to see how they
            are handling this.
