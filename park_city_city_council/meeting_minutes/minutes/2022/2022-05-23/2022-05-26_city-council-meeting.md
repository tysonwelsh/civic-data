WORK SESSION

Discuss Special Event Requests: Art Festival, Miners Day, Car Free Sunday,
Bonanza Art Park, and Special Event Parking Rates:
Arts Festival: Jenny Diersen, Special Events Manager, and Jonathan Weidenhamer,
Economic Development Manager, presented this item. Diersen reviewed the terms for
the Kimball Arts Center (KAC) Arts Festival and indicated they requested a five-year
contract. They also asked for the City to waive fees and hard costs up to $180,000 with
a 10% annual increase for inflation. Hilary Gilson and Aldy Milliken, KAC, were in
attendance and Milliken reviewed the benefits of the education program. He felt KAC
was impactful in the community and stated the in-kind help the City gave to the Arts
Fest event went to help the education program. Gilson also stressed how much the
$180,000 City fee waiver was needed, and noted the event fee usually didn't come near
that amount.

Council Member Toly asked if anything was removed from the previous contract.
Diersen noted insurance standards were increasing. She asked if they should continue
reporting economic impact from the event, since Council had discussed eliminating that.

Council Member Toly asked if a minimum or maximum footprint was discussed. Diersen
stated if KAC asked to change the footprint it would have to come to Council. Milliken
indicated they had reconfigured the layout for the benefit of the businesses. Council
Member Toly asked to see similar outreach as Park Silly Sunday Market (PSSM). She
asked if the 10% inflation was realistic. Diersen stated it was not feasible that the costs
would be maxed out. Council Member Toly liked having the 12 Park City artists at the
festival and thought that was a good local element of the event. Gilson indicated in
2020, the festival moved away from working exclusively with the Park City Professional
Artists Association and opened it to any artists living in Wasatch and Summit Counties.
They tried to keep the number to 15 for each festival. Council Member Toly asked
Milliken and Gilson to think about what could be done to maintain vibrancy on Saturday
evenings.

Council Member Rubell expressed concern with the $180,000 fee waiver request. He
stated this was a good event, and requested some spots be reserved for Park City
artists. He wanted to know the debt impact, and what the economic activity would be on
a similar weekend without the Arts Festival. Then he would be informed on how much
he was willing to spend on the festival. He suggested the City could sponsor the Friday
Locals’ Night. He appreciated the other activities, but thought those should be
addressed separately than the event.

Council Member Dickey hoped to understand the jump in cost from $145,000 to
$180,000. Gilson indicated they mimicked the previous contract. She was concerned
the first responders' costs would increase which would drive up the total fees. They also
wanted funds to be able to continue with their education program. Council Member
Dickey confirmed the $180,000 was only a buffer if needed.

Council Member Gerber asked if there would be a Council liaison sitting in on the
negotiations, to which Diersen stated the liaisons were Council Members Dickey and
Rubell. Council Member Gerber encouraged them to be involved. She knew there would
be outreach and thought there would not be negative feedback. Because Diersen
indicated the 10% inflation was not needed, she was fine with eliminating that. Council
Member Doilney agreed to eliminate the 10% inflation. He cautioned against carving out
all the KAC activities, and indicated Council should trust they were a good partner and
not micro-manage them.

Mayor Worel asked if economic impact studies were needed to receive grant money, to
which Milliken affirmed. Mayor Worel stated she would like to see the report since they
were having the study done anyway.

Car-Free Sunday: Diersen reviewed the event began to stimulate business during
COVID and allowed businesses to move out to Main Street. The City did not want to
sponsor the event. The Historic Park City Alliance (HPCA) wanted the fees waived and
requested the City still be the applicant. If approved, this event would begin June 5th.

grant applications, they would get help from the Silly Market organizers to help with
activities on Upper Main to support the car-free event. Mayor Worel asked when they
would know about funding, to which it was indicated they would know the middle to end

would be a weird dynamic since PSSM was not allowed to advertise their event, but the

from the Restaurant Tax Grant would not all go to marketing.

FY2023 City Manager’s Recommended Budget: Operating Budget Presentation
and Review:
Jed Briggs, Budget Manager, reviewed the Budgeting for Outcomes process used in
creating the annual City budget. He gave the historical context and indicated in FY21,
the budget reflected COVID in the cuts, hiring freezes, and capital deferrals. In FY22,
the City restored critical cuts and increased pay levels to be competitive. In FY23, there
were historic sales tax revenue highs, the community demanded services, and the City
needed to address inflation and competitive pay and benefits.

Briggs displayed a slide on BFO Program scoring. Council Member Rubell noted this
would be discussed in detail at the next retreat. He asked why the variance was
different than the scoring. Briggs gave examples.

Heinrich Deters, Trails and Open Space Manager, reviewed his budget request for two
full-time rangers. Council Member Toly asked if the City had utility vehicles for the staff.
Deters stated the staff was approved but the vehicle was not, although he had existing
funds to purchase the vehicle. Council Member Rubell asked where they would patrol.
Deters stated staff knew the peak times for trail use. They would continue to collect data
and prioritize rangers in certain places. Two rangers would flow in town twice a day.
There would be one ranger at Bonanza Flat during the week and two on weekends.
There was an overnight parking aspect for Bonanza Flat and he didn't know how that
would be monitored. Council Member Rubell asked how citations would work for
property outside City limits. Deters stated Wasatch County agreed that citation revenue
received would go to the City. He felt the revenue could justify an additional staff
member.

With regard to Transit to Trails program, Deters stated there was a $13,000 grant from
the Central Wasatch Commission (CWC) which would bring funding to $60,000. The
program would provide four routes up and four routes back on the weekends. Until the
grant funding and the RFP bids were received, he couldn't give firm details. Council
Member Rubell asked about winter routes, to which Deters stated that was a goal for
the department, but there were challenges regarding access. Council Member Rubell
indicated he would favor earmarking money to support a Bonanza Flat winter program.
Council Member Toly asked who drove the transit. Deters stated it was a private shuttle
and it could go out to bid for winter programming. Council Members Toly and Rubell
favored putting a placeholder for extended programming in the winter.

Council Member Gerber was not in favor of winter programming and thought the City

Kim Fjeldsted, Transit Manager, reviewed the Transit budget requests. She indicated

stations.

Budgeting for Outcomes
              Budgeting for Outcomes

• Create direct link between what Council wants and how the budget
  is allocated
• Council and the public can see how taxpayer dollars are achieving
  their goals
• Prioritize budget based off of better cost estimates for projects,
  programs, and services
• Staff time and energy optimized by directing them toward the
  most important strategic outcomes
• Better clarity and more well-fined outcomes
• Increase Council satisfaction with budget process
              Budgeting for Outcomes

• Recognized as a public finance best practice by:
    Government Finance Officers Association (GFOA)
    International City Managers’ Association (ICMA)
    Alliance for Innovation
• BFO (or Priority Based Budgeting) practiced by over 250
  organizations across North America
                     BFO Strategies
• Priority-driven budget process based upon Council’s priorities,
  goals, objectives, and desired outcomes


• Internal budget committees develop the final budget proposal.
  Committees are made up of cross-departmental staff, including
  the Results Team, CIP, Pay Plan, Benefit, and Fleet committees,
  and other ad hoc committees as needed


• The Results Team formulates recommendations based on total
  BFO score, manager’s request, established need, available
  resources, and related performance measures

                                            Transportation

Park City Transit has provided fare-free public transportation in the Park City, UT area since 1975. Today, Park City
Transit’s 9 bus routes, 75 full-time and seasonal operators, and 45 vehicles (13 of which are zero emission buses
at a cost of $1 million per bus) help transport residents, visitors, festival-goers, and employees alike year-round, 21
hours a day, 365 days a year.



Park City, as a Rural Transit Agency, receives federal grant funding through the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT)
Last year we received $11 million for operations (overall budget $11.8 million).



Before the pandemic, we moved 2 million riders (2019). In 2020, during the pandemic, we moved 1 million. Our ridership is
coming back, in 2021 we moved 1.5 million people. Transit is an important service for the community.

• 2 points = Other focus area identified as a Council Desired
  Outcome
• 1 points = Bid includes some justification on how it
  addresses the Desired Outcomes
• 0 points = Bid includes bad or no justification on how it
  addresses the Desired Outcomes
       2. Portion of Community
       Served by Program
Programs that benefit or serve a larger segment of the City’s residents, businesses
and/or visitors will receive a higher score for this attribute compared to programs
that benefit or serve only a small segment of the population.
Weight = 1.0
• 4 points = Program benefits/serves the ENTIRE community
  and adds to their quality of life
• 3 points = Program benefits/serves a SUBSTANTIAL
  portion of the community and adds to their quality of life
• 2 points = Program benefits/serves a SIGNIFICANT portion
  of the community and adds to their quality of life
• 1 points = Program benefits/serves SOME portion of the
  community and adds to their quality of life
• 0 points = Program benefits/serves only a SMALL portion
  of the community, but still adds to their quality of life
  3. Reliance on City to Provide Program
Programs will be graded on the degree of reliance residents have on the City to provide a
service as compared with their ability to obtain the same or a similar service from another
intergovernmental agency or a private business. Programs for which residents can only look
to the City will receive a higher score for this attribute compared to programs that are
offered by other providers.
Weight = 1.0
• 4 points = City is the sole provider of the service and there are no
  other public or private entities that provide this type of service
• 3 points = City is the sole provider but there are other public or
  private entities which could be contracted to provide this service
• 2 points = Program is only offered by another governmental, non-
  profit or civic agency
• 1 points = Program is offered by other private businesses not within
  City limits
• 0 points = Program is offered by other private businesses within City
  limits
      4. Change in Demand for Program
Programs demonstrating an increase in demand or utilization will receive a higher score for this attribute compared
to programs that show no growth in demand for the program or service. Programs demonstrating a decrease in
demand or utilization will actually receive a negative score for this attribute.
Weight = 1.0
• 4 points = Program experiencing a SUBSTANTIAL increase in demand of 25% or more
• 3 points = Program experiencing a SIGNIFICANT increase in demand of 15% to 24%
• 2 points = Program experiencing a MODEST increase in demand of 5% to 14%
• 1 points = Program experiencing a MINIMAL increase in demand of 1% to 4%
• 0 points = Program experiencing NO change in demand
• -1 points = Program experiencing a MINIMAL decrease in demand of 1% to 4%
• -2 points = Program experiencing a MODEST decrease in demand of 5% to 14%
• -3 points = Program experiencing a SIGNIFICANT decrease in demand of 15% to 24%
• -4 points =Program experiencing a SUBSTANTIAL decrease in demand of 25% or
  more
  5. Mandated to Provide Program
Programs that are mandated by another level of government (i.e. federal, state or
county) will receive a higher score for this attribute compared to programs that are
mandated solely by the City or have no mandate whatsoever.
Weight = 1.0
• 4 points = Required by Federal, State or County legislation
• 3 points = Required by Charter or other incorporation
  documents OR to comply with regulatory agency standards
• 2 points = Required by Code, ordinance, resolution or policy
  OR to fulfill franchise or contractual agreement
• 1 points = Recommended by national professional organization
  to meet published standards or as a best practice
• 0 points = No requirement or mandate exists
  6. Effectiveness of Proposal
Program demonstrating meaningful measures to gauge effectiveness will receive a
higher score. If budget increases are requested, manager addresses how performance
measures will be affected by the request.
Weight = .75
• 4 points = Program includes meaningful and effective
  performance measures
• 3 points = Program includes reasonable performance measures
• 2 points = Program includes adequate performance measures
• 1 points = Program includes below average performance
  measures
• 0 point = Program includes bad or no performance measures
  7. Cost Recovery of Program
Programs that demonstrate the ability to “pay for themselves” through user fees,
intergovernmental grants or other specifically dedicated revenues will receive a higher score
for this attribute compared to programs that generate limited or no funding to cover their
cost.
Weight = .5
• 4 points = Earmarked funds exist specifically for the program (such as a
  grant or debt)
• 3 points = Program draws on flexible revenues which are specific to the
  Fund (such as Water, Transit, and Golf Funds) or program draws
  entirely on non-earmarked funds for which it has a particularly
  reasonable claim (Recreation, Ice, BPE fees, Admin IFTs, etc.)
• 2 points = Program draws mostly on non-earmarked funds for which it
  has a particularly reasonable claim (Recreation, Ice, BPE fees, Admin
  IFTs, etc.)
• 1 points = Program partially draws on non-earmarked funds for which
  it has a particularly reasonable claim (Recreation, Ice, BPE fees, Admin
  IFTs, etc.)
• 0 point = Funds are not identified or funds are subject to much
  competition (such as the General Fund)
  8. Cost Savings/Innovation/Collaboration

Programs that demonstrate ability to find savings or innovate will be scored higher.
Weight = .25
• 4 points – Major cost savings or efficiencies are gained
• 3 points = Obvious cost savings or efficiencies are gained
• 2 points = Some cost savings or efficiencies are gained
• 1 points = Little cost savings or efficiencies are gained
• 0 points = No cost savings or efficiencies are gained

RE: Proposed Parking Rate Increase for Events

On behalf of the HPCA, we respectfully summitted our comments and feedback regarding the proposed
parking rate increase during specific events. The HPCA Board has reviewed proposed rates and the
board voted against supporting the increase as presented. Concerns around the rate increase include:
    •   The increase in rates, along with inflation, seems very ill timed, and is not a guarantee that the
        increase in rates will force people to make the decision to take alternative transportation to Main
        Street.
    •   Events don’t help most of the businesses on Main Street. In fact, most events that have an activity
        keep people in the streets and not in the stores/restaurants.
    •   It has not been proven that what we saw last summer in terms of business levels will be
        the same this year. We had a boom last year, which most attribute to Covid and the
        difficulties in traveling. There is evidence that we will have a slower summer season due to
        international travel opening, costs of flights (it is cheaper to go to Europe than travel
        within the US). So far April and May have shown very slow business trends, which have
        many businesses nervous.
    •   An increase in parking rates may work during the winter season months, when we are
        more "tourist driven" and in our peak season. But for the months outside of the winter
        season, when we are all struggling for consistent business, an increase in parking rates,
        makes Main Street less attractive to our visiting guests, and locals.
    •   Friday rate increase on Local’s night during Arts Festival should follow other local pricing (i.e.
        Miner’s Day and Halloween)
    •   Displaces employees during an already challenging time to find employees. Makes employment
        on Main Street less attractive.

We hope that the council will take our concerns into consideration when reviewing the rate increase.

Rhonda Sideris
Rhonda Sideris
HPCA Board President
