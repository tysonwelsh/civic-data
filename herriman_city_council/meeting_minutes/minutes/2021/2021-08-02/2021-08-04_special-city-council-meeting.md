                                    SPECIAL HERRIMAN CITY
                                      COUNCIL MINUTES
                                      Wednesday, August 4, 2021
                                      Approved October 27, 2021

The following are the minutes of the City Council Meeting of the Herriman City Council. The meeting was held on
Wednesday, August 9, 2021 at 5:30 p.m. in the Herriman City Hall Council Chambers, 5355 West Herriman Main
Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was posted in the City Hall, on the
City’s website, and delivered to members of the Council, media, and interested citizens.

Presiding: Mayor David Watts

Councilmembers Present: Jared Henderson, Sherrie Ohrn, Steve Shields, and Clint Smith.

Staff Present: City Manager Nathan Cherpeski, Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas, Assistant City Manager
Tami Moody, City Recorder Jackie Nostrom, Finance Director Alan Rae, City Attorney Chase Andrizzi,
Communications Manager Jonathan LaFollette, Deputy Chief of Police Cody Stromberg, and Sergeant Brent
Adamson.

6:00 PM - SPECIAL MEETING:
      1. Call to Order
Mayor Watts called the meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. and welcomed those in attendance.

        2. Public Hearing
                2.1. Truth in Taxation public hearing to consider the rate of tax and levying taxes upon all
                     real and personal property in Herriman – Alan Rae, Finance Director
Finance Director Alan Rae indicated residents have been paying taxes to the Unified Fire Services Area (UFSA) to
provide fire and emergency services through Unified Fire Authority. In January 2021, Herriman City withdrew from
UFSA and replaced it with the Herriman City Fire Service Area (HCFSA). Director Rae explained this was a tax
reallocation and not a tax increase. The total amount of property taxes Herriman residents pay would not change.
The tax would generate approximately $5.7 million dollars the city would use for fire services. This same amount
would have been paid to the Unified Fire Service Area would cost approximately $4.5 million and the difference
would be used for current and future fire station needs.

Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn clarified why residents would see an increase on their tax notice based on market value;
however, the mil rate would decrease for a property if the value increased as determined by the state. Director Rae
confirmed.

In response to a question posed by Mayor Watts, City Attorney Chase Andrizzi explained the reasoning behind this
being incorporated into the Herriman City budget instead of the Herriman City Fire Service Area was due to the
timing of the creation of the district as set by the Utah Legislature. He further explained the City would transfer the
collection of funds dedicated for fire services to the district and reduce the tax rate for the City in 2022. Mayor Watts
added this tax was not allowed to be channelled through the Herriman City Safety Enforcement Area due to tax rate
limits. The Council explained this reallocation was to ensure the taxes collected for Herriman fire services stayed in
the community and would not subsidize other areas.

                                      Councilmember Steve Shields arrived at 6:12 p.m.

Councilmember Jared Henderson explained he was the representative for the Unified Fire Area (UFA) and clarified
the fee paid to UFA for services was a contract fee based on number of employees and equipment plus an added cost
related to calls for service. He notated this contract fee was reviewed on an annual basis and the Unified Fire Service
Area (UFSA) wanted to obtain an additional bond to raise funds to remodel old stations as well as construct new
ones. Herriman City had been paying a surplus for several years and are currently still paying bond obligations for
other areas. He indicated Herriman City needed to be able to control their portion of the funds, and in order to
facilitate this goal, would have to go through the truth-in-taxation process.

Councilmember Clint Smith explained the challenging situation was the City could not increase the tax rate into the
Herriman City Safety Enforcement Area and commended the communications team for their efforts pushing the
information out to the community to help residents understand the reallocation. He reiterated the plan to conduct
another truth-in-taxation process in 2022 to reallocate the fire service funds to the Herriman City Fire Service Area.
Councilmember Shields explained it was the desire of the Council to fund ongoing and future fire services in the
current tax rate residents were currently contributing.

Mayor Watts opened the public hearing:

Mike Jensen asked for clarification of the increase. He encouraged the Council not to increase taxes as people’s
income has not increased due to the pandemic.

Melissa Pfleiger opined the City has not protected the residents in the past when it comes to the amount of growth
and passed on the cost for infrastructure to the developers. She expressed appreciation to the explanation provided
and questioned whether the savings accumulated through this reallocation based on the anticipated mil rate would be
reduced as it was unfair to pass on the excess funds to the residents. She stated the Council should only increase the
amount by the actual cost.

Ronald Koller requested the Council promote sales tax and bring business into the city for sustainability as the City
could not live of property taxes.

Larry Sumsion explained his bill does not reflect an 87-percent increase; however, was a substantial increase. Mayor
Watts requested Mr. Sumsion to speak with Finance Director Alan Rae. Mr. Sumsion asked what services the City
receives from the taxing Herriman residents.

Councilmember Ohrn moved to close the Public Hearing. Councilmember Smith seconded the motion and all voted aye.

Finance Director Rae explained the notices were disseminated by Salt Lake County with increases as an average and
the actual changes would be different for each one. Herriman City has had the same tax rate since incorporation and
in 2020, approximately $800,000 was received from property taxes. He acknowledged the substantial effort going into
sales tax and noted in 2020, approximately $11 million was brought into the City. He concurred with the statement
made about cities not being able to live off property taxes.

Finance Director Rae wanted to address a comment pertaining to the “slush fund” and explained there absolutely was
a need for the additional funding as one of the City’s fire stations was essentially condemned. The excess would
initially go towards replacing the facility. Councilmember Henderson added there was still ongoing obligations
through the UFSA as depicted in the separation agreement over the next ten-years and reiterated there would never
be a “slush fund”. He added the Council wanted to ensure the tax dollars would remain in Herriman City and would
pay for future needs as the city continued to grow. Director Rae explained the need to collect the proposed tax rate
and outlined the repercussions if the City chose a reduction. Councilmember Smith acknowledged the current deficit
of the City relating to fire response and noted this would need to be addressed moving forward. The goal would be
to meet ongoing and upcoming needs within the existing tax rate.

Councilmember Ohrn explained the growth was intended to pay for itself as each new home and commercial building
would have to pay for the service as to not negatively impact the existing community. Mayor Watts observed the
current conditions of Fire Station #103 and iterated the need to get the facility to a working condition.

         3. Discussion and Action Items
               3.1. Discussion and consideration of an ordinance adopting a final budget; making
                    appropriations for support of Herriman City for the fiscal year beginning July 1, 2021
                    and Ending June 30, 2022, and determining the rate of tax and levying taxes upon all
                    real and personal property within Herriman City – Alan Rae, Finance Director

Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn moved to approve Ordinance 2021-16 adopting a final budget and making appropriations for the support
of Herriman City for the period beginning July 1, 2021, and ending June 30, 2022, and determining the rate of tax to be set at .001997
for levying taxes upon all real property within the Herriman City. Councilmember Jared Henderson seconded the motion.

The vote was recorded as follows:
Councilmember Jared Henderson       Aye
Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn          Aye
Councilmember Steven Shields        Aye
Councilmember Clint Smith           Aye
Mayor David Watts                   Aye
