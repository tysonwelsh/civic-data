         The purpose of the work session was to receive presentations regarding the proposed Ogden City Fiscal Year (FY) 2025-
2026 budget specific to the Police Department, Management Services Department, Employee Compensation and Benefits, Public
Services Department, and proposed Schedule A changes; and to discuss Council business and hear Council comments.

Council Business
        Council Executive Director Eller-Smith provided the Council with information about the National League of Cities and
Towns conference scheduled for this fall in Ogden City; she asked the Council members to inform her if they would like to attend.
        Council staff then reviewed the format for the budget open house to be held later this evening at 7:00 p.m.

Proposed Ogden City Fiscal Year (FY) 2025-2026 Budget
         Police Department: Deputy Police Chief Ziegler reported Police Department operations are funded primarily by the
City’s General Fund; the proposed FY 2025-2026 budget represents an increase of 4.5 percent, or $1.3 million, when compared to
the adopted FY 2024-2025 budget. He used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss significant changes to the budget
including:
              • Elimination of a Police Records Specialist to help fund a new Marketing Position. This position is one of two
                  new positions proposed under Marketing intended to help create efficiencies for the marketing and
                  communications functions of the city.
              • Wages and benefit increases totaling $749,375.
              • Crime Lab services increases totaling $51,300 based on the new amount from Weber County.
              • Travel and training increases totaling $112,000.
              • Professional and technical increases totaling $89,250 due to Weber County Animal Shelter increases.
              • Increase in IT contracts by $131,075 related to body worn cameras.
         In FY 2017, Ogden City assumed management responsibilities for the Weber Morgan Narcotics Strike Force, providing
financial oversight of the Strike Force subject to City policy and rules for financial disbursement, purchasing, etc. Ogden City
provides 6 officer/agent positions, 1 Sergeant, 1 Lieutenant, and 1 Office Assistant to the Strike Force. Cities that do not
contribute personnel to the effort pay an annual fee. The bulk of the Strike Force funds come from state and federal grants, and
funds are managed in a Trust and Agency Fund.

         Management Services Department, Management Services Capital Improvements, and Employee Compensation and
Benefits: Management Services Director Stout used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to review the budget and Capital
Improvement Plan (CIP) project requests for her Department; the Department is funded through the City’s General Fund and the
proposed FY 2025-2026 budget will increase 5.28 percent, or $374,400, when compared to the adopted FY 2024-2025 budget.
She summarized significant changes to various areas of the Management Services Department budget as follows:
              • Administration:
                         o Wage and benefit increases.
                         o One new full-time positions – Management Analyst (Grade 170) ($194,600). Funding is offset by
                              reductions in other accounts.
              • Marketing:
                         o Wage and benefit increases.
                         o Two new full-time employees- Marketing & Communications Specialist (Grade 130) (208,350). One
                              position is transferring from Cultural Services. A Police Records Specialist is being eliminated to
                              provide funding for the other position.
              • Justice Court:
                         o Wages and benefit increases.
              • Human Resources:
                         o Wages and benefit increases.
              • Comptrollers:
                         o Wages and benefit increases.
              • Fiscal Operations:
                         o Wages and benefit increases.
              • Purchasing:
                         o Wages and benefit increases.
                         o Elimination of a Fiscal Support Specialist (Grade 125) to provide funding for new position in MS
                              Administration.
              • Recorders:
                         o Wages and benefit increases.
                         o Reduction in part-time funding to provide funding for new position in MS Administration.
         The Department also includes internal service funds for Information Technology (IT), Fleet and Facilities, and Risk
Management; these funds are used to account for the cost of services provided to City departments by these divisions. New to the
FY 2024-2025 budget was the addition of the Parking and Mobility internal service fund; the FY 2024-2025 budget for this
service was $851,725 and the proposed FY 2025-2026 budget is $2.7 million.
         The Department also includes a non-departmental fund, which is used to account for expenses for general building
maintenance, Interagency and Interfund transfers, contingency funds, management studies, and other operational expenses that are
not department specific. The budget for this fund will increase by 7.23 percent, or $895,425, when compared to FY 2024-2025.
         There are seven requested Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) projects from the Management Services Department for FY
2025-2026:
              • Critical Project Contingency - $150,000
              • Union Station improvements - $205,000
              • Ballistic and Security Solutions for Facilities’ - $350,000
              • General Facilities Improvements - $660,000
              • Fire Facility Maintenance and Replacements - $1.5 million
              • Fire Station Exhaust System Replacement - $180,000
              • Fire Station #5 Roof Replacement - $55,000
         The funding requested in FY 2025-2026 for these projects is $660,000. Future phases of the projects are included in
future years of the CIP.
         Finally, Ms. Stout discussed Administration’s employee compensation proposal for FY 2025-2026; the tentative budget
includes $3.27 million for cost-of-living adjustments (COLAs) and merit adjustments. This includes amounts needed for a 2.97
percent COLA adjustment City-wide, a 1.5 percent merit increase for general employees, and a four percent public safety step
increase. Health care costs will increase by two percent and the increase will be absorbed by the City rather than passed on to
employees; there are no changes in dental costs and only minor changs in retirement funding requirements.
         Throughout her presentation, Ms. Stout responded to questions from the Council regarding various items in included in
her budget, including staffing changes; changes to functions in the Department aimed at increasing efficiencies; and the addition
of the new Parking and Mobility internal service fund.
          Public Services Department, Utilities, and Public Services Department Capital Improvement Projects: Public Services
Director Anderson used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss his proposed budget for FY2025-2026; the Department is
funded through the General Fund and through two Enterprise Funds – Golf and Recreation. The net change of the Public Services
budget for FY2026 is an increase of $1,372,125. This increase is due to the net of the following specific changes:
               • Administration:
                        o Wage and benefit increases.
                        o Increase equipment and IT costs.
               • Parks and Cemetery:
                        o Wage and benefit increases.
                        o Increased equipment, parts, chemical (weed treatment, etc.), clothing and personal protective
                            equipment, tree maintenance contract costs.
               • Streets:
                        o Wage and benefit increases:
                        o Increased equipment costs.
               • Engineering:
                        o Wage and benefit increases and reclassification of the City Surveyor (G165) to Engineering Director
                            (G185).
                        o Increased equipment costs.
               • Recreation:
                        o Wage and benefit increases including additional part-time funding for the Marshall White Center, a
                            portion of which will be offset by additional revenue from the Center.
                        o Increased recreation programming costs.
                        o Increased equipment costs.
          The FY2025 budget proposes some staffing changes for Public Services. A new position-- Engineering Manager--is
being proposed but will be treated as a reclassification. The City Engineer position is currently charged to the Mayor’s Office.
There are several other reclassifications proposed throughout the Department, but no staffing changes in the Golf or Recreation
enterprise funds.
          Mr. Anderson also discussed Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) projects for his Department; his budget proposal includes
62 projects under Public Services General Fund as Engineering (EN) and Dino Park (DI), General City (GC), Golf Enterprise
(GF), Parks (PK), Parkway (PY), Recreation General Fund (RG), RAMP (RM), Water Utility (WU), Sanitary Sewer Utility (SA),
and Storm Sewer Utility (SU). He expounded on a few of the vital CIP projects, including the Lester Park improvement project;
Mt. Ogden 25th Street and D Avenue improvements in preparation for the I-15/24th Street interchange; and Valley Drive
Intersection improvements.
          Throughout the presentation, Mr. Anderson engaged in discussion with the Council regarding topics such as specific
utility projects; employee turnover and current staffing levels; and volunteerism in the Department.
          Proposed Schedule A Changes: Council Deputy Director Symes stated Council staff has been working with
Administration staff about potential Schedule A changes to the budget; there are no proposed Schedule A changes at this time. He
facilitated discussion among the Council about changes they would like to see identified in Schedule A and indicated he will use
the feedback provided to continue discussions with Administration staff. Topics discussed including employee access to mental
health services and an employment position dedicated to management of trails throughout the City.


         The meeting adjourned at 6:49 p.m.
