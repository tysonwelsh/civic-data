       Well-being
       The confluence of social, economic, environmental, cultural, and political
       factors that people and their communities have determined are necessary
       for them to thrive and reach their full potential is known as community
       well-being. These factors shape a resident’s sense of well-being in Sandy.
       Community health is a vital component, ensuring residents have access to
       healthcare and support services that enable them to live healthy lifestyles.
       At its core, a community with a high degree of well-being provides
       environments where residents can lead fulfilling lives alongside other
       residents in Sandy.

       Parks & Recreation
       Residents of Sandy take immense pride in the city’s extensive network of
       parks, trails, and open spaces. They view the parks and open space system
       as central to Sandy’s community identity and value its contributions to a
       positive quality of life for residents of all ages.

       The city has adapted to changing demographics and limited land
       availability for new park and recreation spaces. The focus of planning
       for parks and recreation in Sandy has largely shifted from creating new
       parks to preserving, enhancing, and diversifying its existing facilities. The
       Parks and Recreation Department aims to realize this vision by providing
       exceptional programs and facilities that enhance the residents’ access to
       community gatherings and opportunities to interact with their neighbors.

       Increased and improved access to the system is important to the Parks
       and Recreation Department and the community. The department intends
       to align the Sandy Parks and Open Space network with the Trust for
       Public Land’s 10-minute walkshed standard that ensures all residents are
       within a 10-minute walk of a park. Feedback from the community found
       a high degree of resident satisfaction with the existing parks, recreation,
       and open space opportunities in the city. While Sandy’s Parks and
       Recreation system’s quantity of amenities is on par with similarly sized
       cities nationwide, it is also clear that the community desires additional
       amenities such as splash pads, outdoor pools, dog parks, and bike parks
       to further enrich the community’s collection of parks and recreation-
       based amenities.

       Sandy parks do many great things for natural resources. They conserve open space and reduce the urban heat
       island effect by being landscaped areas. The grass-covered areas, however, are highly watered. The Parks and
       Recreation Department has taken steps to reduce water usage in parks, one of which is to reduce the amount of
       land being watered.

       Choosing which areas to reduce in turf is an important consideration. As more residences transition to water-wise
       landscaping, parks may be one of the only areas of large, grass-covered areas. Reducing sports fields is not a priority
       to reduce water usage. Instead, it is the unusable turf spaces that should be targeted for turf reduction. Traditional
       turf grass in park strips requires significant amounts of water to maintain its lush appearance, especially in regions
       with hot, dry climates like Utah. By converting these grassy areas to more water-efficient landscaping options,
       such as native plants or drought-tolerant vegetation, substantial water savings can be achieved relatively

       Educational Opportunities
       For All Ages
       Sandy has a strong foundation of existing educational
       opportunities ranging from its public and private schools to its
       community-engaged learning opportunities at various locations
       across the city. Beyond formal education at the city’s public and
       private schools, Sandy residents can continue their learning at the
       Sandy Library, Sandy Museum, the Alta Canyon Sports Center,
       Dimple Dell Recreation Center, and the Sandy Senior Center.
       The encouragement of lifetime learning through educational
       opportunities offered at these various locations strengthens
       the community by fostering knowledge-sharing and increased
       engagement within the community.

       SCHOOLS
       Sandy’s public schools are governed by the Canyons School
       District. In addition to the public sector, the city has several
       private and charter schools serving from Pre-K to 12th grade. In
       total Sandy has eighteen elementary schools, six middle schools
       and seven high schools. Some of the private and charter schools
       offer specialized learning opportunities such as advanced science
       and technology courses. Sandy’s diverse educational landscape
       underscores the City’s commitment to providing a range of
       education opportunities to youth across all grade levels and
       educational ambitions.

       Higher education opportunities are available at Salt Lake
       Community College’s Miller Campus, offering degrees in
       Culinary Arts and Automotive Services, along with support
       services for community members seeking further education.
       Residents also have access to larger nearby institutions within
       a two-hour drive, including the University of Utah in Salt
       Lake City, Utah Valley University in Orem, Brigham Young
       University in Provo, Weber State University in Ogden, and
       Utah State University in Logan, for a wider range of educational
       opportunities. From Pre-K to higher education, Sandy offers a
       competitive educational landscape.

       Support for the Senior-Aged Population
       Addressing the needs of the senior-aged population is fundamental to
       nurturing a supportive and inclusive community. Upholding the city’s
       family-oriented values entails ensuring that senior-aged residents can
       age in place comfortably while maintaining a high quality of life. The
       Salt Lake County-operated Sandy Senior Center is integral to the needs
       of senior-aged residents, welcoming all seniors aged 60 and above from
       Monday to Friday. By providing essential aging services, the center
       empowers Sandy’s older generations to maintain independence and well-
       being. Offering a diverse array of programs and activities, including
       arts and crafts, educational sessions, social dances, and physical activity
       classes such as yoga and tai chi, the center ensures there’s something for
       everyone. Moreover, special events, holiday meals, and courses on topics
       such as dealing with dementia further enrich the center’s offerings,
       reflecting its commitment to addressing the holistic needs of Sandy’s
       senior community. In addition to the services provided by the Sandy
       Senior Center, several senior living facilities can be found in Sandy.
       These facilities offer various levels of care and accommodation options
       for residents. The ability to support Sandy’s older residents and empower
       them to age in place is key to sustaining the overall community’s sense
       of identity and tradition.

               Support Diverse Local Economy

       A number of different factors support a diverse local economy in Sandy.
       The availability of cultural engagement and social interaction opportunities
       in Sandy directly feeds into the level of economic prosperity that the city is
       capable of. By leveraging existing commercial spaces, the city can foster the
       creation of dynamic cultural and social hubs that offer a blend of office, retail,
       entertainment, housing, and open spaces. Capitalizing on these opportunities to
       provide dynamic hubs of community activity helps stimulate economic growth,
       attract investment, and cultivate an ecosystem that benefits local businesses,
       residents, and visitors.

       For the past several decades, Sandy’s existing commercial areas have played a
       pivotal role in contributing to the city’s economic base and creation of public
       spaces that enable community interaction. As these commercial centers take
       the next step in transitioning to complete Neighborhood Activity Centers,
       there’s a unique opportunity for the centers to deepen their contribution to
       Sandy’s cultural and social landscape. In addition to a robust offering of social
       and cultural amenities within The Cairns, the Neighborhood Activity Centers
       can be thought of as areas that bring cultural and social amenities closer to
       residents, at a smaller and more palpable scale. By fostering strong connections
       between businesses and the neighborhoods they serve, these centers can catalyze
       the creation of future public spaces that enhance the vibrancy of community
       interactions and overall quality of life.

                                                                                                  the Hazardous Materials Transportation Regulation (HMTA) in
                                                                                                  1974, which prevented the transport and use of these hazardous

                                                                                                  exposure is of notable concern, as this radioactive gas can seep

                                                                                                  risks such as lung cancer. Ensuring that building foundations are
                                                                                                  properly sealed and older homes are regularly tested for radon is
                                                                                                  crucial.

                                                                                                  health stemming from toxic substances in aged housing units.
