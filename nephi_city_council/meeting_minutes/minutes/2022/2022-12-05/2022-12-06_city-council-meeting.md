PUBLIC COMMENT:

RESOLUTION 12-06-2022 -REVISED SECTION 125 PREMIUM ONLY PLAN AND PERSONNEL POLICY
ADOPTED:

   City Administrator Seth Atkinson reviewed with the council that the Internal Revenue Service requires
employers to draft a Section 125 plan to provide an HSA contribution plan. This plan is also necessary if
the employer provides any pre-tax payroll contributions. The city staff has explored the option of
allowing employees to opt out of health insurance if they can: a) show that they are covered by another
insurance group plan, and b) choose to opt out of the city’s health plan.
   Mr. Atkinson stated that staff recommends that if an employee opts out of health care coverage,
then they be given $250.00/month as a taxed cash-in-lieu payment or a $300.00/month pre-tax
contribution to their 401k plan. This provides a benefit to the employee while also saving the city some
funds.
   Mr. Atkinson also reviewed revisions to the Personnel Policy. The opt-out option and changes to the
Salary Increases section was added to Article VI of the Personnel Policy.
   A Section 125 plan had already been adopted by the Council in 2018. The revised plan incorporated
the opt-out option. Councilor Worwood moved to adopt Resolution 12-6-2022. Councilor Ostler
seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

Work Session convened at 8:06 p.m.

       1.    The council discussed the Ockey water agreement. The council expressed concern about
            the idea that this agreement is a perpetual agreement and they wanted to protect the wet
            water beyond the 5 million gallon mark. Councilor Memmott had met with Mr. James Ockey
            and he reported on Mr. Ockey’s concerns and he had tried to work out options that
            protected the city’s interests along with satisfying Mr. Ockey. Mr. Atkinson said that he
            would revise the agreement and see if he could get Mr. Ockey to sign.

       2.   The council reviewed the annual meeting schedule draft for next year.

       3. The council discussed the RV park code revision proposals. Mr. Wright suggested the
          following changes:
        Owner/ Tenant Agreement:
         All tenants sign a 30-day agreement. If they stay longer then they sign another 30-day
           agreement
         In the lease agreement there is a clause" if there is a disturbance or the police are called,
           they are moved and can/will be removed at the tenant’s expense".
         Laundry facilities and restroom onsite

     Ordinance change:
        Add to the Ordinance spacing, landscaping, maintenance requirements and restrictions on
           what can be added to RVs.

    Compliance:
        Increase the annual business license fee for RV Park owners to cover bi-annual inspections
           of the RV park to determine if it is in compliance with City Code;
        The RV Park owner is required to call the city and schedule the inspector.
        If the RV park is not in compliance after the inspection, then it has 30 days to come into
           compliance.

       6.      Mr. Atkinson reported that The Department of Justice is requiring a letter to be signed by
              me regarding the continued use of Federal Government property being used by Nephi City
              Police Department. In accordance with President Biden’s executive order 14074, advancing
              effective accountable policing and criminal justice practices to enhance public trust and
              public safety. Law enforcement agencies must notify their civilian government body of its
              intent to request property from federal sources to include federal funds or grants and notify
              the local community of its request for property transfers, purchase from federal funds,
              agencies or sub-contractors including existing contracts or grants.

       7. Chief Michael Morgan addressed the council about the fact that the police force is required
          by law to replace their radios by 2024. He was relying on a verbal agreement that he had
          with the county that they would help fund the radios with homeland security funds and other
          federal funds. He learned this past week that the county is not planning on helping with this
          funding. Chief Morgan expressed his disappointment with the county and is worried about
          how we are going to fund this upcoming expense.

       8. Councilor Worwood moved to adjourn. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion
          passed on a unanimous vote.
