           3.2        West Valley Animal Services Report – Maranda Weathermon/Melanie Bennett

Ms. Weathermon wished to touch on improvements and pitfalls seen within the department since
her last report. The shelter had seen a 6% increase from the previous year which meant there had
been 237 more animals than the previous year, each of which needed at least 15 minutes of care
per day. Officer response time had improved from 27 to 21 minutes for priority 1 calls and 26
minutes for weekend and overnight calls. The shelter was averaging 1000 calls per month which
required a physical response by officers plus approximately 350 telephonic cases. All of these
calls were answered by a single dispatcher.
    • Removal of dead animals from roadways had seen a 184% increase
    • Stray animal calls were down 4%
    • Animal adoptions up 11%
    • Taylorsville residents walking into the facility had increased slightly by 2%
    • Animal death in the shelter was down 30%
    • 432 cats had gone through the trap/neuter/release program which was up from 360 the
        previous year. 220 of the 432 were from Taylorsville.
    • The return-to-owner rate had remained unchanged at only 30%
    • Volunteering had been on hold in the early months of the pandemic, but the shelter now
        had a new Volunteer Coordinator (a volunteer himself). The volunteer population was now
        up 400% over the previous quarter
    • Lack of medical care (for spay/neuter) was slowing down the processing of animals.
        Average length stay for adoptable dogs and cats was 22 days and 24 days respectively. The
        national average stay was just nine days. The addition of a veterinarian on staff would
        make the national average attainable.

The shelter lobby was open once again after having closed in the early days of the pandemic. They
still tried to reduce shelter traffic as much as possible for the safety of both staff and the public

The virtual adoption fair had resulted in increased adoptions, increased donations and increased
volunteerism. Creating an Amazon wish list had resulted in 700 pounds of animal food being
donated in the first week.

Chair Harker asked if the shelter was fully staffed. Ms. Weathermon responded that unfortunately
it was not. The Chair also asked her to expand on what Ms. Heatley had said regarding the need
for a veterinarian. Ms. Weathermon responded that if there was a vet on staff, there would no
longer be a bottle neck of cases waiting for a spay/neuter procedure before an animal could be
adopted. Creating such a position could also result in more affordable vaccinations, resulting in a
healthier, happier pet population while becoming a new revenue source for the City. Cruelty cases
could be handled in house rather than outsourcing animal care to a private provider. For example,
staff was not allowed to treat upper respiratory difficulties which could be taken care of onsite if
there were a veterinarian on staff. West Valley/Taylorsville Animal Shelter was the second largest
municipal jurisdiction in the valley. She stated that if we wished to be a progressive, well-rounded
organization, it was time we started acting like one. Having a professional veterinarian on staff
was the next, natural step toward doing so.

          3.3         Police Department Transition Update – Jay Ziolkowski

Jay Ziolkowski explained that a charter had been created which created an identity for the project
management team. It provided timelines and benchmarks the team hoped to achieve prior to July
1. The community outreach meeting resulted in some great information and analysis for the team.
Communication and marketing became very important as far as how the community would be able
to interact with the team. They had also worked with Unified Police Department on policy content
and identified a vendor for putting policies together for the City. Finally, they worked on some
initial budget projections that thus far, appeared to be in harmony with what the city administration
and council had already anticipated. He had been and would continue to work with Kim Horiuchi
on updating the city website as the team moved along the timeline and reached the benchmarks.


Mr. Ziolkowski pointed out how crucial it was to identify and meet community expectations with
the new police department. He had provided the Council with a list of community members who
attended the first outreach meeting. They varied in experience, background, and ethnicity and
together, the group conducted a SWOC analysis which addressed the strengths, weaknesses,
opportunities and challenges of forming a new police department.

Councilmember Armstrong asked if the City was on track for the new department to begin
operations on July 1. Mr. Ziolkowski responded that it was possible that the team was slightly
ahead of schedule. He asked that the councilmembers review the document he had provided them
and let him know if it accurately reflected what they were hearing from their constituents regarding
what they wished to see in the police department.

Mayor Overson stated that the administration would make sure the Council received all
information prior to publication. The website would also be kept up to date so that the community
would be kept in the loop as well.

Mr. Ziolkowski pointed out that Unified Police had been very gracious and good to work with. He
met with them regularly to discuss things from assets and liabilities to dispatch channels. They
had indicated a preliminary willingness to share policy so that the City did not need to start from
scratch.

Councilmember Cochran inquired about the current sentiment of UPD officers and support staff.

Mr. Cowdell interjected that the City had an agreement with Salt Lake County and UPD to be very
careful with its relationships with their employees. The City needed to be very mindful to refrain
from using UPD employees to do City business—no one could be employed by both entities
simultaneously. The City had made some commitments to the County and UPD and intended to
honor those commitments.
In response to Chair Harker’s question regarding what the administration needed at that point from
the City Council, he explained that their most important role would be to provide advice and
consent to the Mayor’s appointment of senior leadership in the police department. Funding was
also a part of their role as the legislative body.

Mr. Cowdell continued by explaining that positions were not being promised. There would be a
traditional hiring process wherein jobs would be posted, applications would be received and hiring
decisions would be made. He anticipated that applications would be received from police officers
from a variety of departments across the Salt Lake Valley.

Councilmember Barbieri mentioned that she had completed the UPD’s 13-week citizen’s academy
and it would be great if that agency would be willing to share their presentations so that
Taylorsville did not need to re-invent the wheel. She stressed that having a citizen’s academy was
a priority for her.
