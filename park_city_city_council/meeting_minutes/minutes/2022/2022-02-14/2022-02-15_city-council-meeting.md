II. OLYMPIC AND PARALYMPIC DEBRIEFING
Chair Robinson opened the meeting. Colin Hilton reviewed his role with 2002 games
and stated he had been working with Utah Legacy Foundation since 2006. This
organization shared the Olympic history and promoted winter sports. He noted 100% of
the Utah Olympic venues were still in use and were busier than right after the games.

Catherine Raney Norman shared she was a four-time Olympian and noted the Olympic
team moved to Park City in 1999. She currently worked with the Youth Sports Alliance
(YSA). This community came together to support the Olympic and Paralympic
movement.

Fraser Bullock reviewed his role in the 2002 games. After September 11, 2001,
everyone worked hard to come together for the games, and he was still involved today.

Darren Hughes discussed the pride of the Olympic volunteers and indicated they were
unique. Public opinion polls were distributed and 80%-89% of respondents favored
having another games in Utah.

Tom Kelly stated YSA had put 15,000 youth in the sports program in the past 20 years.
Youth got involved to have fun, and along the way they learned life lessons.

Bullock recognized people who made the 2002 games happen, including Myles
Rademan, Brad Olch, and many others. At the last games, the organizers were
amateurs. Now, the infrastructure was in place and the committee could do more things
like sustainability. They wanted to understand the County and City’s issues, concerns,
hopes, and aspirations. This was not about 17 days of games, but rather making a
permanent change. He noted the Olympic budget would be $2.2 billion in 2030 dollars.
He wanted to be a leading sports legacy in the world. They wanted to make sure
athletes and residents had a good experience. He asked what Council wanted as an
experience for residents. Bullock noted the committee wanted to have the bid ready by
mid-2022. The awarding used to be done in an International Olympic Committee (IOC)
session seven years in advance of the games. Now, there would be a dialogue process.
That process was flexible and the games could be awarded at any time. The Olympics
date was not set, but they were looking at 2030 and 2034. The committee hoped for a
2030 bid.

Bullock stated Utah was a solid candidate since all the venues were in place. Other
competitors included Vancouver, Canada, who hosted in 2010, and Sapporo, Japan.

Barcelona, Spain, was interested as well. Bullock felt the most important element was
unity. The IOC looked at the attitude of the community and that was the reason for
polling. One reason the committee wanted to host again was for the rising generation
who didn’t see the 2002 games. It was also for enduring legacy to be in place.

Chair Robinson asked if future games would set aside funds to continue the legacy.
Bullock responded that out of the $300 million in the Legacy budget, $180 million would
go to the foundation. He felt that money would permanently endow these facilities to
keep them maintained as well as pay for coaches. Another $130 million would go to
hosting events.

Council Member Toly related her experience watching the Olympics in 2002. She asked
if the new normal took into account future weather conditions. Bullock stated that was
another reason to bid for 2030. A climate study was done and it reported Utah was one
of the better places to host the games, with weather and snow making abilities. Hilton
noted the committee would rely on the expertise of the City and County staff to make
decisions in sustainability and other issues.

Council Member Gerber asked what the difference was in poll numbers the first time
Utah bid on the Olympics. She stated there was a lot of unity in 2002, and asked what
solutions could be brought to resolve residents’ concerns. Bullock stated the popularity
of the games was 60% before the games and 95% after the games.

Council Member Doilney stated there were several Olympic Legacy Communities in
Utah. He asked how Utah did as a legacy community compared to the rest of the world.
Bullock stated the legacy story of Utah was incomparable. The venues and programs
were so busy. That story was not told anywhere else in the world. It happened because
of the endowment left after the 2002 games. Norman added there was an effort to keep
the venues viable. Now the focus was on getting youth from other ethnicities involved in
winter sports.

Hilton and the committee wanted to know what the City/County vision was and what
could get done by a certain date. Chair Robinson stated this was exciting and indicative
of the A-Team they had. He liked the focus on the communities. He acknowledged there
was frustration among some regarding traffic congestion. He wanted to focus on the
quality of life. He thought there would be unity, but it had to be worked out. He heard the
games would just bring more people. There were a lot more people here now, and there
needed to be smart planning. Hilton stated this was a kickoff meeting. The agenda and
handout that was distributed contained draft thoughts and he hoped the group could
come up with a draft joint statement that this group valued these topics. The bid
committee would also like some guiding principles. Different communities might have
different guiding principles. Hughes stated the games delivery principles that were
distributed were their values and he hoped many of the topics could reflect this group’s
vision.

Mayor Worel stated it was time to have a community conversation about the games.
She asked when polling would occur on the local level. Bullock thought it would be nice
to have a way to gather information from the residents to assess the feelings of the
community. The National Olympic Committee would do a poll and the IOC would do a
poll. Hughes noted a platform was being prepared to gather public input. Hilton stated
the committee would follow the City/County lead in sharing information and gathering
input.

Vice Chair Armstrong stated there were perceptions in the community that the Olympics
put the light on Salt Lake City and there was no focus on Park City. Growth in the
community was a concern. Renewable energy, sustainability, and water availability
were also issues of concern. He thought the rising generation was a reason for the
games but the greater legacy that would be left was the impact left after the games. The
legacy was far beyond the Olympic facilities, but if the games would have enduring
positive impacts or lasting negative impacts. Hilton stated there were things that would
start with the games and grow from there. Council Member Stevens stated one thing
that made Summit County and Park City special was a devoted and engaged public.
She encouraged forums on the depths of the issues to make them part of the legacy.

Council Member Toly asked what effect it would have on the bid if the majority of the
community was against the games. Hilton stated if there wasn’t a genuine excitement
for the games, it might be decided not to have big night celebrations on Main Street.
They could turn to the resorts to see if they would be interested in being a venue. He
thought the community in general was very supportive of hosting a future games.
Bullock stated if that was the community sentiment, there was work to do. Council
Member Toly asked what relationship the committee had with Los Angeles, California,
and what could be learned from them. Bullock stated Los Angeles had fantastic games
and the committee wanted to support them. Discussions with Los Angeles were positive
and they were working through the issues, such as sponsorships.

Chair Robinson suggested the polling be more than a yes/no questionnaire. He hoped
for an information meeting with a detailed questionnaire. He asked what the committee
recommended for convening and building unity for the games. Hilton stated there was a
subcommittee for host venue communities and he wanted a regional host venue
community committee. He indicated there should be Council liaisons for the Olympics
and they could be part of this group. The group could give and receive information.
Bullock suggested convening the City/County Councils joint meeting every six months
so everyone would be going in the same direction.

Bullock reviewed the challenges stated today and noted the committee supported the
entities. He wanted the future games to leave a lasting legacy that was positive for the
community. Hilton indicated the committee would bolster the local efforts to resolve
challenges. Council Member Gerber suggested telling the 2002 Olympic story since
there were many who did not live here 20 years ago. Council Member Dickey thought
the group should meet the community where they were; in farmers markets, at grocery
