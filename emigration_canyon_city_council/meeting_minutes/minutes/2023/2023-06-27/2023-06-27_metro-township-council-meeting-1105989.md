Dark Sky
Matt Starley, Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, reviewed the
following Ordinance considering provisions on night lighting:
ORDINANCE NO. 2023-06-01

Date: June 27, 2023

AN ORDINANCE OF THE EMIGRATION CANYON METRO TOWNSHIP
COUNCIL AMENDING SECTION 19.73.110 PROVISIONS ON NIGHT LIGHTING
RECITALS
WHEREAS, the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District provides services to the five Metro
Townships in the Salt Lake Valley, unincorporated areas, and the Town of Brighton; and
WHEREAS, the Emigration Canyon Metro Township adopted each county ordinance in effect at
the time of its incorporation pursuant to Utah Code Ann. Subsection 10-2a-414(3) and has
authority to amend or repeal the county ordinance when it determines it is necessary; and
WHEREAS, the Emigration Canyon Metro Township is a municipality and has authority to
regulate Night Lighting in general pursuant to Utah Code Ann. Subsection 10-3c-103 (2);
WHEREAS, Emigration Canyon Metro Township has authority to adopt amendments to ordinance
language controlling night lighting pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 10-9a-501 in accordance with
the Municipal Land Use, Development, and Management Act, (“MLUDMA”), Title 10, Section 9a,
Utah Code, to encourage lighting practices that minimize light pollution, glare, light trespass, and
skyglow to preserve and enhance views of the night sky; and
WHEREAS, the Council deems it necessary to amend its ordinances in order to maintain the rural
atmosphere and community charter of the metro township; protect ecological conditions and
nocturnal wildlife within the Metro Township; Maintain nighttime safety, utility, and security;
encourage lighting practices that promote energy conservation; and for the protection and
preservation of the public health, safety, and general welfare.
BE IT ORDAINED BY THE Emigration Canyon METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL
as follows:
1. Section 19.73.110 is repealed and replaced to read as follows:
[LAND USE ORDINANCE]: The following Section 19.73.110 is hereby repealed and replaced in
its entirety with the revised Section 19.73.110 attached hereto as Exhibit A | Land Use
Amendment Resolution | 19.73.110 Night Lighting.
2. Severability. If a court of competent jurisdiction determines that any part of this Ordinance is
unconstitutional or invalid, then such portion of this Ordinance, or specific application of this
Ordinance, shall be severed from the remainder, which shall continue in full force and effect.
3. Effective Date. This Ordinance will take effect immediately upon posting and publication as
required by law.

Legislation Converting Townships to Cities and Towns
H.B. 374
Cameron Platt stated the legislative interim committee met on June 14, 2023, and
was presented with information on the metro townships’ wants. The letter supporting the
conversion of the metro township was referenced and the committee agreed to forward the bill.
Representative Teuscher has agreed to support it and was assigned to work with the metro
townships. If the bill passes, Emigration Canyon Metro Township would be converted to a town
based on its population. There is not a lot of difference between a metro township and a town. He
hopes to have a draft to the legislative interim committee by August so there is time to work
through the bill prior to the holidays. The bill will go before the legislature in 2024 and if it passes,
it will go into effect after the governor signs it. After the bill passes, an election will need to be held
to elect the mayor and council seats, in addition to holding a Truth in Taxation hearing.
Council Member Brems asked if the metro township is converted to a town and
is able to enact a property tax, is it one tax with sub-categories? Would the town write a check to
Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA) if it stayed with them?
Mr. Platt stated a municipality has several taxing revenues (e.g., sales, property,
and franchise taxes). The special service tax would continue if the town chose to stay with
SLVLESA. Taxes would be collected by the Salt Lake County Treasurer and passed on to
SLVLESA. Other taxes, depending on the type of tax, would go into either a general or enterprise
fund. Emigration Canyon Metro Township would have control over its entire budget.
Council Member Brems stated if the metro township contracts with another law
enforcement agency, it would be hard for SLVLESA to deal with.
Mr. Platt stated sometime this fall, Salt Lake County will understand how base
services and canyon patrol will work. Salt Lake County requested that those staying with
SLVLESA also contract with the Sheriff’s Office. All the metro townships with the exception of
Copperton, have said it wants to remain as close to the current law enforcement model until UPD
is able to separate everything out.
Council Member Brems stated if the metro township contracts with the Sheriff’s
Office, it will not be part of the decision-making process anymore.
Mayor Smolka stated he recommended an advisory board be created.
Council Member Brems stated it would be a non-binding board.
Mayor Smolka stated that is correct; however, it would carry a lot of weight. If the
boards end up with weighted voting, Emigration Canyon is pretty much an advisory board
anyways.
Council Member Brems stated setting up two police departments will cost more
money than creating one larger UPD and the metro township may end up getting less in services.
