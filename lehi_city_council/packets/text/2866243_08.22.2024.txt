293   2.9) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for a Development Code
294   Amendment adding the Critical Homeownership Overlay Zone.
295   Brittney Harris presented information on the item. She expressed that a collaborative effort with
296   the city has been put into this item to address affordable housing and homeownership.
297
298   Jason Harris presented on the development code amendment and the overlay. He discussed the
299   exponential growth in Utah's home prices, noting that the average cost for an existing home was
300   approximately $600,000, with new construction being much higher. The primary goal was to
301   prioritize homeownership. He mentioned that Paul Hancock and Heather Newall were essentially
302   the sponsors behind the overlay, while he was involved as a concerned resident of the City,
303   particularly regarding home affordability and rising costs.
304
305   He raised the question of what could be done to reduce home prices in order to maintain
306   availability. He explained that the land price constitutes the majority of a home's structure cost,
307   and by lowering the allocation of land cost, they could significantly reduce home prices. The
308   zone overlay and required Development Agreement would grant the City Council full discretion
309   to work with builders on plans to make housing more affordable. Builders would submit two
310   concept plans: a base concept and an attainable concept, with a target price agreed upon through
311   concessions. This plan would then be submitted to the City Council for review and approval.
312
313   Building equity was highly encouraged by remaining in the home. In Lehi, affordability was still
314   relatively high compared to surrounding communities. The criteria for homeownership would
315   require the homes to be owner-occupied with a set target price, and they would be offered to
316   critical and essential workers, Lehi residents, and first-time homebuyers.
317
318   Concerns with government overreach were discussed. Brittney Harris clarified that all
319   participation would be voluntary and could provide affordability, especially with the State
320   introducing funding to assist with housing.
321
322   Concerns with condensing lot sizes were discussed and how that could impact current residents.
323   Concerns hindering property owners' ability to grow equity were also discussed and where the
324   equity would go if the ten-year plan was not met.
325
326   Commissioner Eyre asked if the projected numbers would be higher and if it would meet
327   infrastructure needs. Brittney stated if there were critical infrastructure concerns it would be
328   denied. Craig Chambers stated setting precedence would be more of a political issue rather than a
329   legal issue and that all development agreements come through the Planning Commission and
330   City Council.
331
332   Chairman Jackson asked if Council Member Paul Hancock would like to address the item, as his
333   name had been referenced throughout the presentation and conversation. Paul Hancock
334   expressed that he wanted to hear the questions and concerns, explaining why he was willing to

      Lehi City Planning Commission                    7                                    August 22, 2024
335   sponsor an idea of this magnitude. He noted his concerns with housing affordability, stating that
336   his children would likely never be able to afford a home.
337
338   Mr. Hancock stated that without a development agreement, the target price would not be locked
339   in, potentially increasing, and defeating the purpose of the concept while failing to hold the
340   developer accountable. He also stated that the city should not absorb additional costs and
341   mentioned that the mandated legislation regarding ADUs was concerning, emphasizing the need
342   for a proactive approach.
343
344   Chairman Jackson opened the public hearing.
345
346   Marshall Gary stated that existing subsidies needed to be acknowledged. He noted that
347   investment fraud was at an all-time high, even though first-time homeownership was being
348   incentivized. He expressed his excitement for the concept and looked forward to finding more
349   people who shared his enthusiasm. He also emphasized that being "house poor" was a real
350   concern, and that the people who needed this the most were those unable to attend the meeting.
351
352   Alyssa Daly voiced her support for the concept and disclosed that she worked as a realtor. She
353   shared that buying her first home years ago was a frightening experience, and that was years ago
354   when the market was different. She echoed the comments made by Lehi staff, stating that they
355   were at the forefront of examining housing from a new perspective. She remarked that Lehi had
356   a strong economy due to its healthy housing efforts, but that advantage was slowly disappearing.
357   She was worried about the children’s future and feared they might be priced out of a community
358   they loved.

359   Michelle Stallings spoke as a private resident of Lehi City, rather than in her capacity as a
360   Council Member. She mentioned that she was unaware this item was coming up and would have
361   preferred to see it discussed during a Work Session, as it raised concerns. She emphasized that
362   the General Plan was meant to manage growth and infrastructure, and expressed concern that it
363   would be disregarded if a development agreement like this was implemented. She also worried
364   that zoning designations, which were intended to protect residents, were being used to control
365   land use for builders' profit. Stallings was particularly uneasy about City Council setting target
366   prices, acknowledging that there were many subsidies but feeling that this approach was an
367   overreach. She hoped that the entire Council would carefully consider an item of this
368   significance.

369   Kenneth Glade strongly opposed the item, stating it was "ridiculous" that it was even being
370   heard. He acknowledged that Mr. Harris had been transparent from the beginning, but pointed
371   out that Harris wanted more density, housing, and growth, which the residents of Lehi did not
372   want. He argued that this was exactly what the village overlay was about and noted that policies
373   and procedures were in place to avoid limiting builders from buying high and selling low. He
374   recalled that Lehi was affordable twenty years ago, which had benefited him, but expressed
375   concerns about government officials intervening in housing costs, calling it poor public policy.
376   Glade believed such policies would lead to secretive, backdoor developments and was concerned
377   about public oversight, expressing his complete opposition to the concept.

378   Chairman Jackson closed the public hearing.
