   1. Recognition of Weber County for its support of public defense and of the constitutional rights of its citizens:
            Matt Barraz (Utah Indigent Defense Commission Executive Director): I want to thank the Commission for
            allowing us time on the agenda to thank you for what you do for your constituents through the Public Defender
            office. I assumed this role in 2021; I was really thrilled to hear Weber County creating a Public Defender
            office at that time. There are so many measurable benefits to having this office. They do not have to worry
            about running a business but can focus on their clients. Weber County is the only county in the state where
            the Public Defender office is a part of the county itself. That is why the IDC has been so interested in being a
            partner with Weber County as well. We helped with the startup costs of the new office. We continue to
            dedicate a significant portion of our grant money in ongoing funds to Weber County. This fiscal year we are
            going to contribute 23% of the budget for this office.
            Adam Trupp (Utah Indigent Defense Commission Assistant Director): I really appreciate you having us out
            for this. This is a huge step. No other county has taken this kind of step; no other county has been this
            dedicated. I have been working on this issue for more than 10 years. What has been really gratifying is seeing
            Weber County step up when no other counties have. As you know administrating the criminal justice system
            is one of the fundamental purposes of government. Providing the representation on the defense side is a civil
            liberty and a protected right. We have a letter we would like to leave with you. Part of our responsibility is to
            help fund the mandate the state has given. Grant funding helps support improvements in the public defender
            system. We are here to listen to what you need. If you know anyone in a position of authority in the legislature,
            it’s always helpful to reach out.
            Mr. Barraza: We want to recognize the great hire you made in Jim Retallick. He is a tremendous leader full
            of ideas, energy, and creativity. He has made great recent hires in Mark Augustine and Professor Lancaster
            who are starting the Social Services portion of the office that can really be transformative in the lives of the
            people they serve.
            Jim Retallick (Public Defender Director): I just couldn’t be more proud of the people working for us. This

            Commissioner Bolos: Thank you to everyone on Jim and his team. I’ve worked with him on the CJCC and
            gotten insight into exactly what his department does. I appreciate the work, thank you to everyone.
            Chair Froerer: My first relationship with the IDC side was actually while the appropriations committee I
            chaired was responsible for funding the IDC. We feel this is a model for the state of Utah going forward.
            When I asked Jim what’s the P and L (profit and loss) on this, understanding in this field of work it’s hard to
            put a number on it. Jim said, we do have a ‘P’ and it’s the success we see in keeping people off of drugs, out
            of jail, putting them as a valued member of the community. There is a cost to the county but at the end of the
            day I think this saves us money.

    2. Public hearing to consider a county initiated ordinance amendment to implement water-wise landscaping
       regulations that align with the Weber Basin Water Conservancy District and State of Utah turf removal
       incentives, ZTA 2023-04.

         Steve Burton (Planning): The proposed Ordinance amends the county’s design standards for commercial,
         manufacturing, institutional, multi-family, and single family uses as it relates to water-wise landscaping in

         the Western Weber Planning area and not the Ogden Valley Planning area. Western Weber Planning
         Commission forwarded a positive recommendation in July 2023. Ogden Valley Planning Commission
         unanimously denied the proposal on July 18th for the following reasons:

                  1. The proposed text amendment is not consistent with the General Plan.
                  2. The proposed text amendment is not in the best interest of the general public.
                  3. The commission would like to see these incentive programs applied to residents of the Ogden
                     Valley Planning area without adopting the proposed landscaping restrictions.

         The proposed ordinance limits the amount of turf grass to no more than 35% of the front and side yard of a
         lot or parcel (and for the Western Weber Planning area only) or no more than 3k square feet of the entire lot
         area or parcel, whichever is greater. The second requirement is no more than 15% of the total lot area can be
         irrigated turf grass for commercial, industrial, institutional, and multi-family development. The third
         requirement states that irrigated turf in a park strip and other yard areas less than 8 feet wide is prohibited on
         all projects. All non-conforming landscaping needs replaced if a land owner makes site improvements or
         building expansions that modify 25% of the site or 20k sq ft. With these new regulations, affected residents
         would be able to take advantage of reimbursement programs through the state and through Weber Basin.
         The staff recommendation is for approval for only the Western Weber Planning area.
