PUBLIC HEARINGS
1.     Continued- Residential Site Grading Permit – 4932 South Waimea Way – (R-1-10
       Zone).
Chair Ricks opened the public hearing.

Rita Wright gave her address as 4931 Naniloa Drive and reported that she is employed by
another city. She recognized the importance of volunteers such as the Planning Commission
Members. She reported that in 1967, her father, who was a realtor in Salt Lake City, took the
family to Shadow Mountain Estates in Holladay and described it as the most beautiful place in
the Salt Lake Valley. She referenced Holladay Code Section F, which describes the character of
the area and the beautiful views of Mt. Olympus. Ms. Wright became concerned when her
neighbor showed her the proposed concrete wall and fence that will stand far beyond the grade
and fence line. Ms. Wright stated that the proposed project will impede the view of Mt.
Olympus for her and her neighbors.

Rick Jensen gave his address as 4941 Naniloa Drive and stated that he lives behind the home of
Nicholas Tanga. The original proposal was for a six-foot fence but in reality it exceeds eight feet
and is not in compliance with the Holladay Code. It also ends to the middle of his back yard. He
considered the best scenario to be for the fence to continue to his property line although doing so
would impact Ms. Wright’s view. His second choice was an angled fence. Mr. Jensen’s
preference aesthetically was for it to be a full fence. Renderings of the site were displayed.
Possible alternatives and details were discussed.

The applicant, Nicholas Tanga, gave his address as 4032 Waimea Way and confirmed that staff
visited his property since the last meeting. Mr. Tanga wanted the fence to be continuous and of

opposed to making a recommendation for a request that is out of compliance with current
ordinances. He explained that the previous situation involved the fence being built incorrectly.
He saw no reason to continue that situation. He commented that aesthetics should not dictate the
final outcome and suggested that the Commission consider the neighbors below.

Troy Holbrook put the emphasis on the neighbors that are directly impacted by the fence. The
goal was to have a continuous horizonal fence. The neighbors seemed to be willing to work
through the issues. Chair Ricks stated that Ms. Wright would like six feet to preserve her view
and Mr. Jensen prefers nothing if the alternative is a step down to 10 feet.

Ms. Wright and Mr. Jensen made the Commission aware of their concerns. There was
discussion about stepping the fence down or sloping it and the heights of the retaining wall and
fence. At the property line the maximum height is eight feet. It was noted that the property
owner could build a separate wall inside the property line. There was discussion about whether a
fence needs to be placed on top of a retaining wall in order to meet Code.

The Commission defined their role in the process and how a solution could best be reached. Mr.
Hobbs stated that retaining walls without a fence can create a fall hazard. Three feet of the
retaining wall needs to be above ground to avoid a fall hazard.

2.      Conditional Use Permit – Home Occupation – 1860 East Frontier Road – R-1-21.
City Planner, Jon Teerlink, presented the staff report and stated that the application is for a home
occupation for a home-based business. Recent State requirements have changed how home-
based businesses are licensed based on the type of business and whether customers and/or
employees come to the home. The City of Holladay licenses home-based businesses with just a
license. Planning Commission approval is not required if the business is solely an office use
with no employees or customers. When customers are expected to come to the home, the
requirements are different. The subject proposal is for a cosmetic business involving micro-
blading. It was acknowledged that the applicant submitted her narrative and business plan. Mr.
Hobbs stated that the proposal will have minimal impact on the neighborhood. Staff
recommended approval.

The applicant, Karina Luna, reported that she spoke to her neighbors and all expressed their
support.

Chair Ricks opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing
was closed.

Chair Ricks mentioned that the homeowner has a two-car garage that would allow up to two cars
to be parked in the driveway. She suggested that condition number three be amended to limit the
applicant to no more than one client at a time. It was noted that the business will operate from
10:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m.

Commissioner Lloyd moved to approve the application for a home occupation for “Luna
Cosmetic Artistry” located at 1860 East Frontier Road subject to the following:

Findings:
      1.       A Home Occupation with customers is a conditionally allowed for this location.

       2.      No other Home Occupations are on file as being in operation with ¼ mile of
               this location.

3.      Residential Site Plan Review, Construction Near Upper Canal – (R-1-21 Zone).
Mr. Hobbs presented the staff report and stated that item three is a request from the applicants,
the Runnebohms, for consideration and approval to place a shed on their property in a position
that is within a 20-foot offset from the Upper Canal. The curtilage was altered years ago so that
any structure positioned close to a waterway is required to have a separation of 20 feet. The staff
report contained the application, the staff commentary, and special exceptions and conclusions of
law used to evaluate the application. Mr. Hobbs recommended that the applicants move the shed
to the backyard. The site plan provided by the applicants showed the shed in a location that
appears to be in the fence setback. The issue for the Commission was the special exception to
place the shed 11 feet from the bank of the Upper Canal. Subsequent to receipt of the
application, staff received comments from neighbors that Mr. Hobbs provided to the
Commission. Mr. Hobbs stated that all of the comments were in opposition to the proposal for
various reasons including aesthetics, precedent setting, and safety issues.

Mr. Hobbs reported that staff was sympathetic to the application, however, there was some
miscommunication. The applicant halted construction on the project when a complaint was

The applicant, Clayton Runnebohm, was identified as the owner of the subject property. It was

Thomas Peters gave his address as 2796 East Valley View Avenue and commented that the

were upset because the location was not discussed. The location of the shed is contrary to the
purpose of the ordinance and is not in compliance. Mr. Peters discussed concerns about safety
and property values. He commented that the shed belongs in the backyard.

Rebecca Baugh, who resides at 2808 Valley View, understood the applicant’s concerns about the
retaining wall location. She did not believe the shed would work well if placed in the backyard.
She favored the proposed shed location.

Mrs. Runnebohm stated they had no intention of negatively impacting the neighbors’ property
values. She felt that the neighbor’s complaints were uncalled for. If the request is not approved,
Mr. Runnebohm asked for an exception to the 20-foot setback.

Madeline Bart gave her address as 2808 East Valley View Avenue and asked about the ability to
place the shed in the side yard.

There were no further comments. The public hearing was closed.

Chair Ricks encouraged the neighbors to communicate with one other. She read a statement
from the engineer addressing exceptions to the setback. She suggested the applicant consider the
canal exception and discuss it with the City Engineer.

Commissioner Lloyd moved to deny the special exception to waterway protection area
standards at 2802 East Valley View Avenue and requested that the homeowners meet with the
City Engineer on an alternate location. The findings included the letter from the City
Engineer and the setback on the canal, which is currently 20 feet with no accessory buildings
allowed in the front yard. Commissioner Layton moved to amend the motion to specify that a
stream exception may not be needed. Commissioner Mackin seconded the amended motion.
Vote on motion: Chris Layton-Aye, Alyssa Lloyd-Aye, Ann Mackin-Aye, Jan Bradshaw-Aye,
Troy Holbrook-Aye, Chair Marianne Ricks-Aye. The motion passed unanimously.
