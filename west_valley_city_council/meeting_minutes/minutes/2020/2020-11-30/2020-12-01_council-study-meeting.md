C.     FAST FOOD ESTABLISHMENTS WITH DRIVE-UP WINDOWS
       Steve Pastorik, CED, presented a PowerPoint presentation summarized as follows:
              - Why discuss this topic?
                      o Staff receives frequent inquiries about developing new fast food
                         establishments with drive-up windows in areas where they are
                         prohibited.
                      o Many restaurants without drive-up windows have struggled
                         during the pandemic.
                      o Restaurants that have historically not had drive-up windows are
                         now seeking to add them.
MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – DECEMBER 1, 2020
                       -5-


         -   Current Regulations
                o Fast food establishments with drive-up windows are allowed in
                    the C-2, C-3, M and MXD zones.
                o The following overlay zones restrict fast food establishments
                    with drive-up windows:
                o 5600 West Overlay Zone
                o Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone
                o Jordan River Overlay Zone
         -   Overlay Zone History
                o 5600 West Overlay Zone
                         When first adopted in 2004, this overlay zone prohibited
                            fast food establishments with drive-up windows
                            altogether. It was amended in 2012 to allow fast food
                            restaurants with drive-up windows in multi-tenant
                            buildings under certain conditions
                o Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone
                         This overlay zone has prohibited fast food
                            establishments with drive-up windows since 1996.
                o Jordan River Overlay Zone
                         This overlay zone has prohibited fast food
                            establishments with drive-up windows since 1998.

  Councilmember Lang asked if the City Council allowed a restaurant in a multi-
  tenant building near WinCo. Steve replied the building north of the Maverik Gas
  Station on 2700 West could have a drive-up but does not. Councilmember Lang
  clarified that the Café Rio on 5600 West now has a drive-up since Dunkin Donuts
  does not operate any longer. Councilmember Christensen stated that is accurate.
  Councilmember Lang stated that she doesn’t see a need to change the ordinance or
  overlays at this time. Councilmember Buhler agreed and noted that major changes
  should not be made based on the current pandemic status. He noted that he is unsure
  why the Council originally banned drive-ups but he believes it was to encourage
  more sit-down restaurants. Mayor Bigelow stated that he is aware of sit-down
  businesses who want drive-up windows. He indicated that as long as these
  businesses still maintain the majority of their business inside, he doesn’t see an
  issue with allowing them to have a drive-up as well. Councilmember Lang stated
  that drive-up businesses are often a nuisance for residential homes due to their late
  business hours, bright order boards, etc. Councilmember Buhler agreed that there
  needs to be a separation between residential areas and commercial businesses such
  as these. He stated that businesses like Olive Garden, who are not near residential,
  may benefit from a drive-up. Councilmember Christensen stated that he doesn’t see
  a need to change current regulations. Mayor Bigelow stated that businesses with
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – DECEMBER 1, 2020

       drive-up windows have been busy during the pandemic but other restaurants have
       struggled to be competitive. Councilmember Christensen stated that many
       restaurants can’t have drive-up windows based on limitations of their property. He
       indicated that it would be difficult to design a regulation that covers every aspect.
       Councilmember Buhler stated that it would be difficult to enforce as well.
       Councilmember Lang stated that she feels changing the ordinance will open the
       floodgates to more businesses like grocery stores. She indicated that she is
       concerned about changing things too quickly in response to COVID.
       Councilmember Buhler agreed but noted that he doesn’t see a major issue with
       drive-ups except that the City doesn’t want a significant increase of restaurants like
       McDonalds, Burger King, etc. Steve noted that restaurants were interested prior to
       the pandemic but it has accelerated the process. Mayor Bigelow stated that he
       appreciates drive-up pharmacies, banks, etc. He questioned what industries need
       drive-ups and which ones don’t. Mayor Bigelow indicated that better defining the
       ordinances might help. He suggested encouraging more full-service restaurants,
       discouraging more fast-food restaurants in one area, still providing drive-ups to
       businesses that need it, etc. Mayor Bigelow questioned how much the City should
       influence the market. Mayor Bigelow stated he would like to know more about the
       restaurant industry, where the industry is going, and how fast-food and full-service
       restaurants are differentiated. Councilmember Buhler stated that this would be
       difficult to define. Mayor Bigelow stated he would like to further explore the
       definition of restaurant and understand the industry and the direction it’s heading
       better.
