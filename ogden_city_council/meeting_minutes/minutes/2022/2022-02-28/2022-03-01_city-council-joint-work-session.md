The purpose of the joint work session was to review the agenda for the Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.;
review the GOAL Foundation Annual Report; discuss a proposed land use transfer/infill project at 2300 Quincy Avenue; discuss
Council/Board business pertaining to budget goals and guidelines and financial principles; and hear Council/Board comments.

Agenda Review

Chair Nadolski and members of Council staff briefly reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council
meeting, scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

GOAL Foundation Annual Report

Eric Bauman, Get Out and Live (GOAL) Foundation Executive Director/CEO, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation
to summarize the annual report for the GOAL Foundation. The presentation touched on topics such as 2021 race events and the
number of participants/volunteers in each event; camps and programming and the number of participants in each; special events;
and goals and race events planned for 2022, most notably the return to an in-person Ogden Marathon. He concluded by thanking
City Administration and the City Council for their ongoing support of the GOAL Foundation, especially over the last few years
that have been very challenging due to the COVID-19 pandemic.

General discussion among the Council and Mr. Bauman focused on the City’s ability to make specific improvements at
the Ogden Marathon finish line that would help the GOAL Foundation to market the event through social media; opportunities for
the GOAL Foundation to expand their programming; and the idea of continuing to offer a virtual option for in-person race events.

Proposed Land Transfer — 2300 Quincy Avenue infill project

City Administration is proposing the transfer of two parcels from the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency to Ogden City
for the development of a seven-lot infill subdivision at approximately 2300 Quincy Avenue. The majority of the parcels on the
infill site are owned by Ogden City with the exception of the two subject parcels. In order to develop the site, all properties must
be under common ownership. Ogden City Community Development Division is planning on developing the project.

Deputy Community Development Manager Smith used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the proposed land
transfer; he reported the land transfer will facilitate a small subdivision under the Quality Neighborhood Initiative. The available
land is large enough to accommodate eight single-family homes and architecture would reflect the surrounding historical
neighborhood; an existing red brick historic building will be restored and included in the project. If feasible, homes will be
designed with accessory dwelling unit (ADU) ready spaces built into the homes and waterwise landscaping will be implemented
for each property. He presented a plat map to identify the two parcels that are subject to the land transfer, after which he provided
an aerial photograph of the surrounding neighborhood to identify homes in the area that have already been renovated by the City.

Discussion centered on plans for the ADU space in each home and the price point of each of the homes in the project.
Council Deputy Director Symes reported that if there are no objections to the proposed land transfer, it will be included
on the City Council and Redevelopment Agency (RDA) meeting agendas on March 15, 2022. No objections were voiced.

Council/Board Business.

Budget Goals and Guidelines: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reported Council staff are proposing minor
changes to the prior Fiscal Year (FY)2023 Goals and Guidelines. The significant changes from the FY2022 Guidelines for the
FY2023 Guidelines are summarized below:

e Reformatted to remove redundant language.

e Added additional language to highlight the Council’s focus on providing competitive salaries and benefits to
employees.

e Revised Utility Fee language to include reference to the Blended Inflation Adjustment (BIA) and limiting
increases to the BIA unless a detailed analysis is submitted for consideration.

The significant changes from the FY2022 Goals for the FY2023 Goals are summarized below:

e Included a statement that all goals should ensure that the diversity of the community is reflected in the City’s
programs and services.

e Prioritized implementation of the Make Ogden Plan.

e Added additional focus on completing a community center.

e Corrected the name of the Key Initiatives.

e Added a goal to provide a sufficient housing mix to support business growth.

e Added a focus on speed reduction.

e Added a goal to increase arts grant funding.

The Council offered suggestions for clarification of specific goals and guidelines, with Ms. Eller-Smith noting she will
utilize the feedback to adjust the document before it is presented to the Council for formal approval.

Financial Principles: Council Deputy Director Symes reported these principles are used to guide financial management
and budgetary decisions throughout the fiscal year. Changes proposed for Fiscal Year 2023 include the following:

e Revenues — Addition of language setting forth the goal of going through the truth in taxation noticing and
public hearing process at least every other year in order to consider changes to the certified tax rate.

e Employee Salaries — Rewording to reflect the 2023 Compensation and Benefits Plan.

e Strategic Plan — The proposed amendments clarifying that the General Fund strategic plan will be updated
every other year.

Chair Nadolski called for a recess at 4:56 p.m. to allow the Council to convene in their fact-finding work session.
The meeting reconvened at 5:26 p.m.

Council/Board Comments

Project/Initiative Tracker: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reported Council member Lopez has requested a
tracking mechanism for Council initiatives and projects; she presented a spreadsheet that is color coded to communicate the status
of all Council initiatives and facilitated discussion/review of the document.

Council Parade Float: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reported City Administration has located a trailer that has
been modified to be used as a parade float for the City Council; she presented a photo of the float and indicated it can be used for
the Pioneer Days parade and the Christmas parade. Council Policy Analyst Watkins has enlisted the Youth Council to help
decorate the float if the Council is supportive of using the float. The Council voiced their support and indicated they would like
the Youth Council to ride on the float as well.

The meeting adjourned at 5:41 p.m.
