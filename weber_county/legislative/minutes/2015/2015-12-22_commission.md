         Scott Mendoza, of County Planning, summarized the proposal that included amending
         definitions, creating a new defined term, creating more flexibility in the development standards
         and work force housing requirements, adding approximately 12 new uses to the Land Use table in
         the Resort Zone, allowing more administrative or staff level approval for commercial types of
         development that are in the Resort Zone for developments above 6,200 feet elevation, and
         allowing more flexibility regarding resort signage and landscaping requirements.
         Mr. Mendoza noted that this proposal was submitted last summer and has gone through two work
         sessions and one public hearing with the Ogden Valley Planning Commission, one public hearing
         with the Western Weber Planning Commission, and the county commissioners have had the
         information for some time. He recommended taking public comments only today and adopting
         an ordinance at a later date with direction from the commissioners.
         Paul Strange, with Summit/petitioner, summarized the requested amendments including the
         following: defining detached lockout and lockout sleeping room; addressing buffer areas,
         reducing setbacks and lot size requirements, addressing height of buildings above 6,200 feet,
         adding flexibility to workforce housing standards, allowing additional kitchens in detached
         lockouts, allowing placing certain miscellaneous improvements (i.e., BBQ pits) outside a building
         envelope; giving more flexibility to the Planning Director regarding building design review,
         allowing the Planning Commission to approve the applicant’s design plan for their community,
         addressing the parking plan, accessory apartment, and the sign plan relating to egress/ingress.
         Mr. Mendoza addressed Commissioner Bell’s questions including that Snow Basin had not
         offered formal comments, but noting that these amendments offer more flexibility to resorts.
    4. PUBLIC HEARING REGARDING AMENDING COUNTY LAND USE CODE AS FOLLOWS: GENERAL
       PROVISIONS (TITLE 101), DEFINITIONS (SECTION 1-7); SUBDIVISION (TITLE 106), GENERAL
       PROVISIONS (CHAPTER 1); STANDARDS (TITLE 108), HILLSIDE DEVELOPMENT REVIEW
       PROCEDURES & STANDARDS (CHAPTER 14); & OTHER SECTIONS OF THE COUNTY CODE TO
       PROVIDE FOR ADMINISTRATIVE EDITS RELATED TO CHANGES IN THE SUBDIVISION CODE &
       RELATED TO CHANGES TO THE NAMES OF THE PLANNING COMMISSIONS & PLANNING AREAS

         Charles Ewert, of County Planning, stated that the majority of the changes are administrative in
         nature. He noted that in 2012 the Subdivision Code was amended and at the same time the
         county was going through a codification process. That code inadvertently did not get into the
         final codified version and the language to enact the codified version also repealed all other code
         versions, thereby repealing the 2012 code accidentally. The Planning Division discovered this,
         worked with the County Attorney’s Office to verify that their actions based on that code are still
         valid, and today’s action is to go through the formal process.

         Chair Gibson invited public comments on the three public hearings and none were offered.

H. PUBLIC COMMENTS:
       Cory Combe, of Uintah, asked why he should pay to develop someone else’s property stating that
       he spent hundreds of thousands of dollars to put in the improvements. One of the last sections of
       Skyline Drive that he put in connected the existing Skyline in Uintah to Wasatch Drive. His
       street did not quite get to Wasatch and the county made an agreement with him, and he put it all
       in, going through Mr. Lucero’s property. This was done without purchasing Mr. Lucero’s
       property and the county put a holding strip on Mr. Lucero’s property on both sides of Skyline.
       When Mr. Lucero developed, he paid a prorated share of Skyline Drive. Mr. Combe said that
       now the county is buying that property and asked if the county is encumbering a holding strip to
       recuperate the money for the improvements. Mr. Combe also stated that on Friday he saw a plan
       for four flag lots on a 20-foot street and he thought that this was a conflict to the county’s
       regulations.
       Sean Wilkinson, Community and Economic Development Department Director, said that Mr.
       Combe was referring to the Mountain’s Edge Subdivision (4 lots) recently submitted to County
       Planning. It has frontage off a new section of Skyline that will be developed, and one lot will
       also potentially have access off of Combe Road. The developer is proposing using 20-ft. wide
       access easement for all of the lots and it will be a driveway for two of the lots across their own
       front property lines, the two easternmost lots will use that 20-ft wide access as the main access to
       their properties. Mr. Wilkinson explained that one of those lots cannot access off of Combe Road
       and the other lot does not have any road frontage. He also explained the difference between what
       is considered a flag lot and a lot using a private right-of-way, as in this case. He will meet with
       Mr. Combe after this meeting on his two issues.
