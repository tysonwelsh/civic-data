Safe Street For All Implementation Plan – Shawn Eliot, MAG (3:56) Safe Street for All (SS4A) is a newly created federal funding program intended to help local governments improve roadway safety. SS4A provides $1B annually from FY22-FY26 MPO Regional Planning Committee September 1, 2022 Page 3 nationwide; up to $150M statewide, up to $50M for MPO’s, and up to $30M for individual entities. The funds are only available at the MPO and local government level due to the complexities of administering federal grants. MAG staff recommends that regional partners collaborate on both planning and implementation phases to maximize the possibility of receiving grants through what is anticipated to be a highly competitive program. Jim Price reported staff research suggests that $1.25M be sought for two Safety Action Plans covering all of MAG by designating $1M for Utah county and $250k for combined Wasatch and Summit counties. USDOT requires a minimum 20% local match, or $250k in non-federal funds. The intent of SS4A is to transform roadway corridors on a high-injury network with safety improvements to control speed, separate users, and improve visibility, along with other measures that improve safety for all users. Jim Price stated that the Provo FrontRunner Pedestrian Bridge and Lehi Main Street Bridge projects are great examples of how the grant can be applied as they increase the level of safety. Applicants for Implementation Grants must first have a Comprehensive Safety Action Plan that include the following: • Evaluates policies and practices • Assess safety issues throughout the region • Identify needed improvements • Create concept reports with cost estimates The following scenarios were discussed: • Have MAG staff lead the effort to obtain and use federal funding under the SS4A program on behalf of all MAG constituent entities. • Have MAG program 100% of the required $200k matching funds by using $187k TIP and $13k TAG funds. Mayor Packard moved that the MPO Reginal Planning Committee recommend that MAG lead the effort to obtain and use federal funding under the Safe Streets for All program, on behalf of all MAG constituents, and that MAG program $200,000 Exchange Funds. Mayor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. A correction was noted in the motion. It says recommend rather than move. The amendment to the motion was approved. MPO Regional Planning Committee September 1, 2022 Page 4 Regional Zero Fare Study Update – Shawn Eliot, MAG (16:21) MAG, WFRC, UTA, and UDOT have partnered on a Regional Zero-Fare Study that kicked-off in June 2022. The project partners are working with a consultant team led by Nelson\Nygaard Consulting Associates to meet four primary goals: • Evaluate the operational, financial, and community impacts of zero-fare transit • Gauge transit values of stakeholders from across the service area • Provide guidance around replacement funding • Objectively inform decision making processes around the future of zero-fare transit The study is in the process of conducting interviews. The main issues expressed is that it is free and safety concerns. Currently, the study is reviewing UTA financial trends, rider profile, fare policy, and fare usage to provide useful data. The data collected will be used to complete a literature review and produce three case studies for agencies that have evaluated zero-fare. Stakeholder engagement will include interviewing legislators and key decision makers in the transit field and updating policy and community member groups on study progress. Next steps include: • August-October Identify zero-fare alternatives and evaluate operational, financial, and community effects • October-November Inform decisionmakers on the tradeoffs of zero-fare alternatives • November-December Produce final report and deliver final presentation Mary DeLaMare-Schaefer reported that UTA will conduct an on-board transit survey in mid- October to determine who is riding UVX and will report the findings.
