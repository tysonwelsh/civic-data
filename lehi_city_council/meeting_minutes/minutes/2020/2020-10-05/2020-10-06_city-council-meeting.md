2. Receipt of a feasibility study from Magellan Advisors and discussion regarding whether
   Lehi City should offer public telecommunication services or cable television services
   John Honker, with Magellan, reported on the fiber feasibility study for Lehi City. He stated that
   many cities have built a fiber network and then leased that to providers to provide broadband,
   instead of providing it themselves. Mr. Honker stated that they conducted a statistical survey to
   help them understand the demand in the residential and business community. They also studied
   the market competitors and what the take rate could be for Lehi. He also discussed the
   advantages and disadvantages of providing its own broadband service. The State puts some
   heavy regulations on the city if it wants to provide a retail product. The regulatory restrictions
   aren’t as steep if the city chooses to do a wholesale model.

   Mr. Honker stated that they also researched the regulatory impacts to each option which is
   detailed in the report. He stated that telecommunications companies are known to oppose
   municipal projects where the municipality is the provider. This also creates a political risk.

   Mr. Honker discussed the option of the city building the infrastructure which would be a
   wholesale/partner model. This would have a two-year time frame where construction would
   begin in 2022 with the first costumers having access in March of 2022 and then it would take
   another 3 years to roll out and connect the majority of the market. The cost to build the network

City Council and Planning Commission Joint Work Session (5:30 p.m.)

1. Presentation of the Proposed Thanksgiving Point Area Plan Amendment
   Nathan Ricks presented the most recent Area Plan Amendment proposal. He stated that when
   they looked at the traffic study, they assessed the area on both sides of the frontrunner tracks.
   He noted that in previous meetings, the City has stated that the west side isn’t ready for
   development, so they’ve focused on the east side where they are proposing increased density.
   They talked about adding some more office in addition to residential with retail/service on the
   first floor. They are proposing a walkable pedestrian friendly urban environment in Lehi which
   has not been done here. Mr. Ricks then presented on the traffic study that was conducted by
   Hales Engineering which showed 2 scenarios without the Clubhouse Drive extension. The first
   scenario was preferred which would have less office space, more residential and no hotel.

   Councilor Koivisto asked that they change the legend so that it reflects how the City defines
   density. She stated that she would like to see what amenities would be included in the project.
   Mr. Ricks stated that it’s difficult to define that specifically right now because they don’t
   know what the allowed density will be, but they will provide amenities.

   Ryan Hales explained how the added residential and retail can balance the traffic in the area.
   He said they have a huge opportunity with the transit station. He stated that there are some
   improvements that can be made to the roads that can mitigate the traffic. He said the goal is to
   manage the traffic not eliminate it. He said that it’s important in a project like this to have a
   good mix of uses to maximize internal capture. The existing uses are off balance because of
   all the office space. Mr. Hales also explained that even without the project, Triumph
   Boulevard needs to be widened and Clubhouse Drive extended by 2050. This project would
   accelerate the second of these improvements by 10 years. Councilor Koivisto inquired about
   who would do the improvements to these roads. Mr. Hales stated that this doesn’t show who
   would be responsible for the cost, it just shows what needs to be completed. Councilor
   Koivisto stated that this will increase traffic and asked that Stack Development construct the
   roads. Mr. Ricks stated that these are regional roads and would be needed regardless if this
   project happens or not. Councilor Hancock stated that he would like to determine who covers

3. Discussion of the proposed General Plan update.
   Kim Struthers wanted to discuss the new density areas that are being proposed and make sure
   they are following the Council’s vision before staff begins intensive infrastructure calculations.
   He stated that they looked at areas that could accommodate higher density. Mike West, Long-
   term Planner, stated that they are going to look at the numbers and how that ties into our overall
   vision. Ross Dinsdale, City Assistant Engineer, stated that they put together a map to show areas
   that would be impacted most by the update. He asked that the Council look at the map to see if
   those densities were what the Council had envisioned, or if they would like more or less. Mr.
   Struthers also explained that the Main Street density and State Street density will be different
   because of the Main Street height limitations.

   Mr. Dinsdale stated that the city’s current buildout projections are at 140,000. This new update
   would increase that to about 170,000 – 200,000 people.

   Councilor Hancock stated that he had some concerns with the higher densities on Pioneer
   Crossing and other areas to the south. Councilor Albrecht stated that the areas that will be most
   concerning are the areas that have the largest change, such as moving from low density to high
   density. Councilor Hancock stated that as they continue to analyze this, it may be helpful to
   have the percentage increase of density in each area. Lorin Powell, City Engineer, stated that
   most areas of change only increased by one general plan designation.

   Mayor Johnson was concerned that if all they build are condos and townhomes, they may have
   an issue in the future as the demographics change. Mr. Struthers stated that they still have areas
   of stability that could be developed as single family with larger lots.

   Councilor Condie was concerned with anything over 150,000 population. Councilor Hancock
   stated that going into this he thought that the city should maintain that 140,000 population, but
   shift the densities around to achieve that in areas that make sense. He would prefer that they
   focus on densifying the TOD areas instead of just adding density in various locations.

   Councilor Koivisto asked if there was pressure from the State to increase the population in Lehi.
   Mr. Struthers replied that they are encouraging a solution to the housing affordability issue, and
   the easiest way to address that is with high density. He said that they could look at this regionally
   too, because if they density here, then people won’t have to travel as far to access I-15,
   businesses and employment. Councilor Hancock stated that he’s comfortable with the 140-150
   population range as long as the density is done in the right places. He also expressed concern
   with the amount of density being proposed in north Lehi by Traverse Mountain.

   Mr. Dinsdale stated that their next step is a utility analysis to determine what the right densities
   may be.

   Mayor Johnson suggested that they take more of the green areas that are proposed for higher
   density and move that density to the TOD areas. That might be more palatable in the future. Mr.
   Dinsdale suggested taking a closed look at each area being proposed as an increase and identify
   areas that they would be comfortable changing or not.

   Mr. West stated that as a long-term planner, he looks at the challenges we’re faced with as a
   metro area, and how other metro areas have developed and the consequences of that. He stated
   that they are already a large employment center which creates a lot of demand for housing and
   travel. He has researched many TODs that have shown the higher the density and population in
   a TOD the more benefits you see.

   Commissioner Matt Hemmert stated that they’ve identified these TOD areas and that it’s
   important to make sure they are developed correctly. Commissioner Ellis stated that he’s
   concerned when we offer a little more density to where it creates a problem, but not enough to
   where it offers a solution.
