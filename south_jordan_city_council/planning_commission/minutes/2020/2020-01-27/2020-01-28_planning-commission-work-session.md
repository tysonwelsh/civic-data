 Public Policy. It is the intent of the Legislature that the state, its agencies and
 political subdivisions take their actions and conduct their deliberation openly.
 Open Meetings. All meeting are to be open to the public (with limited exceptions)
   “Meeting” is the convening of a public body when a quorum is present.
   A meeting is not “a change gathering or social gathering.”
   “Quorum” is a simply majority of the membership of a public body.
   Be careful of a meeting after a meeting.

 Voting. Need a minimum of three votes to pass.
 The Planning Commission makes “administrative decisions,” which includes the
 following:
   Site Plan Approval, Preliminary Subdivision Review
   Conditional Use Permits
   Recommendations for Rezone (which may include a Land Use Amendment)

 Administrative decisions = low discretion. See Utah Code § 10-9a-801(3)(c)(i).

 Let’s use an hypothetical example to show us the application process and how the
 Planning Commission is involved.

   A less-than-popular and devious developer, Sheeran Development Co, is under contract
   to buy three parcels along South Jordan Parkway. The developer is wanting to put a single
   story duplex: one side a law office and the other side a dentist office.

   When the developer approached City staff, they reviewed the property’s location, zoning
   map, and land use designation.
 Because two of the parcels have a zoning designation of residential, 1.8 lots per
 acre (R-1.8 Zone), the developer will need to rezone the property.
 Under the Uses table found in City Code, the developer can have an “office” and
 “medical office” as a permitted use in the professional office zone (P-O Zone).
 A rezone application must include:
   A complete form with an owner’s affidavit.
   Payment of applicable fees.
   Legal description of area to be rezoned.
   Concept plan, which includes elevations of the structure, photographs describing
   the property, access plan, and a list of names of subject properties within 300 feet.
 Rezoning a property is a “legislative act.”
 The Planning Commission reviews the application at a public hearing and
 “make[s] a recommendation on the rezoning to the City Council.”
 Consider the following when reviewing the application:
   What is the future land use designation for the area?
   Does the proposed zone change ‘fit’ the area?
   If granted, what would be the impact on the neighborhood (generally)?
   What uses are allowed under the zone?

 What not to consider:
   Architecture
   Landscaping
   Specifics (like fencing)

 Because the restaurant is listed as a “conditional use” in the P-O Zone, the
 developer is also requesting a conditional use permit to operate the restaurant on
 the north end of the property.
 To deny or condition a CUP application, there must be substantial evidence of a
 detrimental effect.
 Once you’ve identified a detrimental affect, you may impose a condition “to
 achieve compliance with applicable standards in City Code. See Utah Code § 10-
 9a-507.
 First ask, “What is the applicable standard for the __________________ detrimental
 effect?”
   For example, the applicable standard for parking is found in City Code ________________
   and requires ________ spaces.
 Second ask, “What conditions could we impose to mitigate (not eliminate) the
 ____________ detrimental effect.”
   As stated before, you do not have to impose a condition even if a detrimental effect is
   identified. But if you do impose a condition to mitigate the detrimental effect, the
   condition must be least restrictive to the applicant.
 Third ask, “Does the imposed condition achieve compliance with the standard in
 the first question?”

 What is a “Conflict of Interest?”
 Utah Code Section 10-3-1301 et seq. states that an officer or employee may not
 receive compensation for assisting any person or entity in any transaction with the
 city or town without making a written and oral disclosure to the mayor and public.
 After stating a conflict of interest, Utah Code does not require anyone who complies
 with the disclosure to abstain from voting or participating in the discussion. It is
 good practice, however, to recuse yourself from voting if there is a conflict of
 interest.
