3.      APPROVE AND AUTHORIZE THE COMMISSION CHAIR TO SIGN AN AGREEMENT WITH
        BIOLOGICAL MEDIATION SYSTEMS, LLC
(Track A, 19:45)
Utah County Deputy Attorney Carl Hollan noted current code does not allow anyone to build or use a privy within
the county. Commissioner Graves and Utah County Public Works Director Richard Nielson mentioned the United
States Forest Service and other locations in the county with operational privvies, including a problem in American
Fork. The commissioners discussed the options, with Mr. Nielson pointing out a denial of this proposed contract
would render one of the restrooms at Vivian Park inoperable for the remainer of the Summer, as well as a facility at
Nunn’s Park in Provo Canyon. Commissioner Graves also mentioned the greater health risk to the community when
members of the public incorrectly dispose of human waste because restrooms are not functioning. Utah County
Health Department Director Ralph Clegg reviewed the Board of Health calendar with the Board, suggesting portable
toilets as a temporary solution.

                 Commissioner Ivie made the motion to approve the agreement with Biological
                 Mediation Systems, LLC, with the stipulation that construction and installation
                 should not be completed until an ordinance addressing the privy codes has been
                 approved, and authorizing the Utah County Public Works Department to
                 acquire portable toilets for use at Vivian Park and Nunn’s Park in Provo
                 Canyon during that interm as discussed in Consent Agenda Item No. 3. The
                 motion was seconded by Commissioner Graves and carried with the following

1.     ADOPT (OR DENY) AN ORDINANCE TO AMEND THE UTAH COUNTY LAND USE
       ORDINANCE, CHAPTERS 2, 3, 5, 6, AND 7 OF THE UTAH COUNTY LAND USE ORDINANCE,
       RELATED TO THE ESTABLISHMENT OF MINOR SUBDIVISIONS; THE DEFINITION AND
       NOTICING OF, AND THE PROCEDURES AND REQUIREMENTS FOR, LARGE SCALE
       DEVELOPMENTS; THE DEFINITION OF COUNTY COMMISSION; THE GENERAL
       PROVISIONS, APPLICATION REQUIREMENTS, STANDARDS, AND CONDITIONS FOR
       PLANNED SUBDIVISIONS; THE POWERS AND DUTIES OF THE PLANNING COMMISSION
       AND COUNTY COMMISSION; THE DEVELOPMENTAL IMPACT STATEMENT CHECK LIST;
       AND THE DEFINITIONS RELATIVE TO THE TRANSFER OF DEVELOPMENT RIGHTS
       SENDING OVERLAY ZONE AND TRANSFER OF DEVELOPMENT RIGHTS RECEIVING
       OVERLAY ZONE (Continued from the March 28, 2017 Commission meeting)
 (Track A, 27:20)
Commissioner Lee referred to the previous discussion during the March 28, 2016 Commission meeting regarding
this proposed ordinance, noting the suggested changes to delete a phrase had not been made in the last two weeks.
The phrase removes the County Commissioners from a part of the process. Bryce Armstrong of the Utah County
Community Development reminded the Board that any revisions made by them would require this agenda item be
referred back to the Utah County Planning Commission, including the sentence mentioned by Commissioner Lee.
The commissioners expressed concern with the amount of time it would take to return to the Planning Commission,
and back to this Board, for any pending projects. Utah County Deputy Attorney Robert J. Moore and Civil Division
Chief Deputy Attorney David Shawcroft discussed statute, with Mr. Moore noting potential applicants for this
season do not have to wait for this proposed ordinance – they can follow the current building process.

Commissioner Ivie outlined suggested changes for the proposed ordinance, including restrictions in referrals back to
the Planning Commission and making sure the County Commissioners maintain oversight. He also would like to
ensure streamlining the process does not negatively impact the opportunity for members of the public to log
complaints. The commissioners discussed the subdivision process with Mr. Moore, Mr. Shawcroft, and Mr.
Armstrong, with input from Brandon Larsen of the Utah County Community Development Office. When
Commissioner Lee referred to a discussion about floods earlier this year to express a lack of confidence in the staff
moving quickly on this issue, Mr. Armstrong responded his office was working on an overall comprehensive and
detailed plan to address flooding – they were planning on returning to present the full proposal to the Board later in
the year, rather than piece-meal as it is composed.
