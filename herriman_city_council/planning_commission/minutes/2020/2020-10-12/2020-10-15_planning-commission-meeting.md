        1.3    Discussion of the Moderate-Income Housing Annual Report
FFKR Susie Petheram stated an annual report was due December 1, 2020, to demonstrate progress. She
presented housing gap data which indicated between 2016 and 2018 Herriman increased the number of
affordable and available units. Review of the residential building permit data revealed additional affordable
units have been added since 2018.

SB24 Moderate Income Housing Strategies chosen by Herriman:
   • Strategy 1 – Carefully monitor affordability in proportion to overall to ensure affordable housing needs
      are anticipated.
   • Strategy 2 - Allow for multi-family or townhome mixed-use development as buffers between
      commercial and single-family residential areas.
   • Strategy 3 – Consider accessory dwelling units in appropriate areas. Allow Accessory Dwelling Units
      (ADU) to be integrated into residential neighborhoods.
   • Strategy 4 – Support higher density and/or moderate-income residential development in the Towne
      Center, near the future Salt Lake Community College campus, and adjacent to commercial and
      employment centers.
   • Strategy 5 – Continue to encourage PUD neighborhoods with small lot sizes and support the
      integration of small lot sizes into developments to offer a mix of housing within the same
      neighborhood.
   • Strategy 6 – Require the highest standard of design, function, and appearance for all multi-family
      developments. They should be compatible with surrounding areas.
   • Strategy 7 – Designate areas in the future land use and development framework where multi-family
      development will be permitted to take advantage of existing and planned infrastructure. Support a
      range of multifamily housing types to appeal to a variety of population demographics.

Chair Berbert asked the number of units planned near the SLCC campus. Assistant City Planner MacDonald
indicated around 600 were entitled for Creek Ridge, and 300 were entitled next to the SLCC campus. The

            1.4    Discussion of the Draft General Plan
    FFKR Susie Petheram provided the residential types and lot sizes for each district. District 1 had a good
    combination of single family and multi family units. She noted attached unit calculations did not include
    common areas. District 2 contained mostly single-family homes. District 3 contained all single-family homes.
    District 4 was mostly single-family, with multi-family and condos in the southern section clustered around
    MVC.

    Commissioner Jacobson requested totals and percentages for the City overall. He believed many areas defined
    as single-family were subdivided condominiums, townhomes, or apartments and those under .07 were
    attached. FFKR Susie Petheram reported the average lot size was 1.8 acres, with a large spike of very small, .03
    sized lot numbers. Commissioners requested the calculation without the lots larger than 5 acres, as they would
    likely to be subdivided at some point. Commissioners requested an estimate for what would be needed in the
    future, with the newest approved apartment complexes included, to identify gaps. They requested what the
    balance of housing should look like for a well-balanced community, when graphed. Commissioner Palmer
    stated the WFRC released the balance for affordable housing, broken down by residence type percentage.

    For the next step FFKR Susie Petheram will send a signup sheet and meet with small groups to look at the
    future land use map, to calibrate future land use to match building patterns and zoning.

       3.1      Request:      Discussion and Consideration of a Revised Land Use Master Plan for
                the South Hills Development, as an amendment to the Master Development
                Agreement
                Applicant:    John Lindsley
                Address:      3600 West 15800 South
                Zone:         R-2-15, MU-15, R-1-15, R-M, R-1-10, and A-1
                Acres:        604.50
                File Number: C2008-044-09
Assistant City Planner MacDonald identified the location, displayed an overview of the project and explained
the amendment was requested to add more property. The applicant requested the addition of approximately
150 acres near Camp Williams. The property must be developed in compliance with the 2008 General Plan,
under which the MDA was vested. The applicant was allowed 4.5 units per acre, however, when developed it
must comply with the General Plan. The addition of pods 35 and 39 increased the commercial and open space.
The land use comparison indicated the total housing units increased from 1268 to 1502, with increased low
and medium density residential housing, and a decrease in Multi-Family Stacked and Townhome units.

The Commission tonight may approve the addition of property, change of uses, and assignment of density.
Each pod would return to Commission for final site plan approval.

Applicant Reed Dixon with Infinity Consultants for Wasatch Commercial was present to stand in for John
Lindsley. He stated the MDA allowed for addition of the requested property. He identified open space areas
and residential and commercial pods.

Density allotments of total maximum residential units were transferred from Rosecrest to South Hills.
Commissioners requested removal of the the clause that allows amendments on future MDAs. City Attorney
Andrizzi explained the decision came down to contractual rights between the parties when they were
negotiated. It was explained the land use plan did not differentiate between open space and parks, and the
proposed plan has more open space than the original. The Commissioners expressed a need to focus on larger
lot sizes.

Applicant Representative Dixon stated the ten acre park would have lots of amenities, in addition to twenty to
thirty acres of trails and open spaces. Commissioners expressed the opinion that some of the open space was
not usable.

                 Address:       12058 S Carlsbad Way
                 Zone:          C-2 (Commercial)
                 Acres:         0.538

Assistant City Planner Macdonald explained the applicant requested a site plan review for a drive-thru
restaurant known as Ziggi’s. The site plan proposed 12 stacking spaces for the drive-thru, and 9 parking
spaces, including 1 ADA space. The landscaping plan and elevations were displayed. The signage will be
presented later. The sidewalk connected to the next pad, which had not been installed yet. Parcel A would be
public open space with benches.

Applicant Cory Gust identified the location for the open space.

Commissioner Palmer expressed concern with limited walkability. Commissioner Jacobson thought more
sidewalks were needed.

Assistant City Planner MacDonald responded the master sidewalk plan was in place to ensure sidewalk
connectivity and walkability and identified areas that connect. Commissioners identifed areas where more
sidewalks were needed and discussed options for increased connectivity.

Applicant Gust explained Ziggi’s would connect to the sidewalk master plan. Additionall, all buildings would
be connected on the perimeter, and interior of the development. The outer sidewalk was already installed per
Applicant Gust.

Requirements
   1. Receive and agree to the recommendations from other agencies.
   2. Submit final landscaping plans showing types, sizes, and placement of plant material to
      the Staff for review and final approval as part of the engineering review.
   3. All building elevations shall meet the approved Anthem Design Guidelines. The building
      elevations are approved as submitted.

     4. No signs are approved with this request; a separate approval will be required. No signs are allowed
