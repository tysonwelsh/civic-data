ACTION ITEMS:

Consideration of a proposed resolution creating Voter Participation Areas –
Resolution 21-59 – Craig Carlston, Assistant City Attorney

At the December 7, 2021, Council meeting, City Attorney Kymber Housley addressed
the Council and explained that in 2019 the Legislature passed a law requiring all
municipalities to have voter participation areas. These voter participation areas are
specifically required for initiatives and referendums. The City was required to pass it at
the end of the year, and it was passed in June of that year, but that law also required that
by January 1, 2022, voter participation areas be reviewed. The presentation will be after
due to the date due to the date of the next Council meeting. As the voter participation
areas were being put together County sent out a new map, and adjustments have been
made.

Craig Carlston, Assistant City Attorney stated that the State Code requires that cities have
voter participation areas (8) that are required for initiatives and referendums. The State
first created the areas in 2020, a verification two years later in 2022, and will again be
required in ten more years. The City relies on the Cache County Voter precinct map to
determine the 8 voter participation areas.

                                                                                     6 | Page

Chair Simmonds requested further clarification as to the necessity for voter participation
areas.

Mr. Carlston answered that initiatives are directed by Legislation, while a referendum is
when Council passes a law and a portion of the public are in disagreement requiring a
vote by registered voters to determine if the law passes or not. The purpose of voter
participation areas is that should any of the two instances arise if there is a high
concentration of voters in one area that a single area may determine the outcome or be the
only area receiving the benefits of the law. In the instance of an initiative or referendum,
the requirement is 75% of the total voter participation.

Councilmember Jensen asked if the voter participation areas will be used in any other
manner.

Mr. Carlston responded that would not be the case as voter participation areas are strictly
only used for initiatives and referendums.

Budget Adjustment FY 2021-2022 appropriating: $16,229 funds received for police
overtime shirt reimbursements; $1,600,032 funds received from the Cache County
Council of Governments to be used for 100 West Corridor improvements; $250,713
funds for the 100 West 600 to 800 South Roadway agreement; $5,000 a grant the
Police Department was awarded from the State of Utah, the Alcohol & Drug-Free
Committee (ADF) funds will be used to purchase police equipment; $100,000
additional funds for utility billing credit card fees – Resolution 22-01 – Richard
Anderson, Finance Director

Finance Director Richard Anderson addressed the Council and explained the proposed
budget adjustments. Mr. Anderson said the credit card fees associated with utility billing
are built into rates for every fiscal year, but not into the budget. The good news is that
citizens are paying more often with a credit card, but the bad news is that there has been a
20% overall increase in credit card participation.

Councilmember Jensen asked what the fee percentage is.
Mr. Anderson answered that typically the fee is 2%. The target is a low dollar amount
anything less than $3,000. Anything larger such as for corporations if they elect to still
use a credit card, the credit card fees are directly billed back to them. There are forms of
payment that do not cost fees such as a bank draft. It is an option for a future that may
possibly be incentivized as opposed to using credit cards. If fees were to be charged there
would be less participation which may require hiring more staff to deal with the increase
of in-person or over-the-phone payments.

Vice Chair López asked for confirmation if the fees were already estimated in the budget.

REZONE – Consideration of a proposed rezone. Jason Thompson/Miller Real
Estate Enterprises LLC., authorized agent/owner, request a zone change of a 2-acre
lot from Commercial (COM) to Commercial Services (CS) to build a new 8,000-
11,000 SF automotive service and repair shop. The project is located at 175 West
1950 North in the Commercial (COM) zone. (Bridger Neighborhood) – Ordinance
22-01 – Tanya Rice, Planner

Planner Tanya Rice addressed the Council regarding the proposed rezone.

Existing Zone: COM (Commercial)
Intended for retail, service, and hospitality businesses.
Located along a collector and arterial streets near mass transit.
Vertical residential development is encouraged when combined with commercial uses.
* Limits land uses that are industrial in nature.

Proposed Zone: CS (Commercial Services)
Fills a need between commercial and industrial land uses.
Not intended for residential uses or heavy industrial uses.
* Less restrictive than COM regarding the range of permitted uses.

The primary concern is that there is an NR-6 zone (residential housing) right across from
the proposed location. The Planning Commission voted 7:0 in denial of the rezone
proposal.
