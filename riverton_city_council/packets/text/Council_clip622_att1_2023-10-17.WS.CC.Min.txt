41            Bangerter Highway Project and Mountain View Corridor Project
42
43   Marwan Farah, Project Manager for UDOT, presented an outline of the timeline for the
44   Bangerter Highway project:
45
46           September 7, 2023: Meet the Contractor event.
47           December 2023 to August 2025: Design work for the three interchanges.
48           January 2025 to September 2025: Relocation of Rose Creek and pedestrian bridges
49            demolition.
50           October 2025 to November 2025: Heavy construction on the full interchange bridges.
51

 1   Mr. Farah also mentioned early activities, including the demolition of the 13400 South and 2700
 2   West pedestrian bridge and utility investigations. The construction work would begin with the
 3   13400 South and 2700 West interchanges and then progress to the 9800 South interchange.
 4   He provided an overview of the construction process and how traffic would be managed during
 5   the construction phase. The goal was to complete all three interchanges by November 2025.
 6
 7   Mayor Staggs asked if there was a date for taking the bridge off. In response, Mr. Farah said
 8   the bridge relocation was scheduled for November 4.
 9
10   Mr. Farah mentioned the westward shift of the highway's alignment, which would impact utilities,
11   such as water lines, gas lines, and sewer lines. The good news was that the design-build team
12   had come up with innovative solutions to minimize impacts on local streets and businesses. He
13   highlighted the plan for road closures and detours during the mainline work phase. Northbound
14   traffic was expected to be free-flowing, with left turns allowed into Herriman. The details of the
15   detours were still being optimized by the design-build team.
16
17   Mr. Farah explained the traffic phasing plan for the Bangerter Highway project. He detailed the
18   phases, closures, and the impact on traffic during each phase of construction. He discussed the
19   work involving the relocation of the pedestrian bridge and the extension of Rose Creek. He
20   highlighted the importance of timing these activities to account for low-flow periods in Rose
21   Creek and minimize disruptions. The project's goal was to optimize detours and minimize
22   inconveniences to the community during construction.
23
24   Councilmember Pierucci expressed gratitude for the presentation and acknowledged that the
25   western part of the City, which is within his district, would be significantly affected by the
26   upcoming project. He highlighted the short-term challenges the project might pose and the
27   anticipated long-term benefits for regional traffic flows. He inquired about the plans to
28   collaborate with the CIC, the signals timing team at UDOT, and the measures to monitor and
29   mitigate impacts on east-west traffic during the construction phase.
30
31   Mr. Farah explained their plan for traffic management during the project. He mentioned the
32   intention to coordinate with the traffic operations centers and traffic engineers to make
33   adjustments to signal timings. He provided examples of how signals would be monitored and
34   adjusted to accommodate detours during the construction, emphasizing their commitment to
35   making necessary signal timing adjustments.
36
37   Councilmember McCay expressed concern about increased traffic and congestion, especially in
38   areas bordering her district. She inquired about the evaluation process and emphasized the
39   existing congestion issues during rush hours and the need to address these concerns as traffic
40   increases due to the construction. In response, Mr. Farah explained that traffic monitoring is
41   conducted through CCTV cameras and they can track delays and traffic congestion, and there
42   are traffic engineers on the ground to monitor the situation.
43
44   Oanh Le-Spradlin, Project Manager for the Mountain View Corridor, presented an update on the
45   project. She explained that Utah is finishing up phase one of the Mountain View Corridor and is
46   now focusing on phase two, which involves grade separating the middle portion of the corridor
47   to turn it into a freeway. The project spans nine miles and affects five cities. The approach is
48   innovative and designed to minimize impacts on the communities. The phase will result in a new
49   four-lane concrete freeway with grade-separated interchanges and the installation of 22 bridges
50   at 16 locations, covering 13 interchanges. The current budget for the project is 490 million
51   dollars.

19            Ordinance No. 23-15 – Text Amendment to Section 18.235.040(6), Landscape
20            Design Standards, Addressing a Single Word Clarification, Amendments
21            proposed by Riverton City
22
23   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, said the text amendment concerns the
24   waterways landscape design standards that were recently adopted. Jordan Valley Water
25   Conservancy and the Department of Water Resources reviewed these standards before their
26   adoption, and they identified a deficiency in one portion of the code. To maintain consistency
27   with their requirements is essential, especially for making flip your strip funding available to
28   residents.
29
30   Mr. Lethbridge said the proposed change is simple, involving a one-word modification. The
31   section that currently states "turf or sod may not be used in park strips or landscaped islands" is
32   suggested to be changed to "turf or sod may not be used in park strips or landscaped areas."
33   This change does not affect the functionality or interpretation of the code. The Planning
34   Commission has reviewed this amendment and recommended approval, and the City concurs
35   with this recommendation. While this is a public hearing, no feedback has been received from
36   the public, except for Jordan Valley and the Department of Water Resources. The process
37   should be straightforward.
38
39   Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.
40
41   Councilmembers and staff discussed the changes and asked clarifying questions.
42
43   Councilmember Buroker MOVED that the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 23-15 – Text
44   Amendment to Section 18.235.040(6), Landscape Design Standards, Addressing a Single
45   Word Clarification, Amendments proposed by Riverton City. Councilmember Pierucci
46   SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs
47   called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: Buroker-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes,
48   Pierucci-yes and Wells-yes. The motion passed unanimously.

 3            Artificial Turf Discussion
 4
 5   Mr. Lethbridge explained that the City is looking into potential ordinance amendments related to
 6   artificial turf use, which has become more prevalent even in residential settings. Currently, the
 7   City's ordinance is not explicitly clear on whether artificial turf is allowed. The primary questions
 8   for discussion include:
 9
10           Should artificial grass be allowed in commercial and multifamily as well as single-family
11            residential situations?
12           Should artificial grass count towards required landscaping percentages, especially in
13            commercial projects?
14           What standards should be applied regarding the quality of artificial turf (e.g., grass blade
15            length) and ground preparation?
16           Should there be differentiation between commercial and residential uses of artificial turf?
17           How should repair and replacement of artificial turf be handled, especially in areas
18            where traditional landscaping may be required?
19
20   Mr. Lethbridge said he would like to gather feedback from the Council and the planning
21   commission to address these questions and eventually make informed amendments to the
22   ordinance.
23
24   Councilmembers agreed to have further discussion in a future work session on this topic.
