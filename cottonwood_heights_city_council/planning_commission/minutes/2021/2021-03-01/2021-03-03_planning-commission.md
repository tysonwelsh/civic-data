(Project SPL-21-002) – A Public Hearing to Receive Comments and Take Possible Action on a Request from Utah First Federal Credit Union for Site Plan Approval of a New Financial Institution at 1166 East Fort Union Boulevard in the MU (Mixed-Use) Zone.

Senior City Planner, Andy Hulka reported that the request for site plan approval of a new Utah First Credit Union to be located at 1166 East Fort Union Boulevard.  It is a permitted use in the Mixed-Use Zone. The request was reviewed by the Architectural Review Commission (“ARC”) and approved with conditions.  A visual rendering was displayed.  A three-lane drive-thru was proposed with a widened main entrance and exit.  Mr. Hulka reported that the site plan has been reviewed by staff, meets the setback requirements, and was determined to be compliant with the requirements of the Mixed-Use Code.  The City will require a wider sidewalk as part of the Fort Union Master Plan and a pedestrian connection.  The property owner owns four of the parcels, three of which are designated parking stalls.  There are a total of 74 stalls, which exceeds the required 13 stalls.  The building site includes five parking stalls, two of which are handicapped stalls.  The proposed landscaping was described.  

Lighting features were next discussed.  Mr. Hulka reported that the design guidelines recommend parking lighting lot not exceed 18 feet in height.  Mr. Hulka presented a visual rendering of the elevations and materials.  The building is a modern design with different materials proposed throughout.  A branch in Farmington was referenced.  The ARC issued a Certificate of Design Compliance and recommended the sidewalk along Fort Union Boulevard be widened to six feet.  The addition of a streetlight was proposed along the frontage as well not to exceed 18 feet in height.  No street trees were to be planted within 30 feet of the drive-thru exit.  Staff conducted a review and found that the request complies with the applicable ordinances.  Mr. Hulka reported that staff will conduct a full technical review with Engineering, Public Works, and the Fire Department to ensure that all items are addressed.  

In response to a concern raised regarding backlighting on the building, Mr. Hulka indicated the ARC did not have an issue with the illumination and believed it will be beneficial to provide lighting in the area.  Commissioner Mills expressed concern with potential brightness and believed it could be done subtly and discretely.  A site visit was recommended.  Mr. Hulka explained that the site plan review is to ensure that the request meets all Code requirements.  With the newly adopted outdoor lighting standards, the applicant will have to submit a full lighting plan and comply with all requirements.  Commissioner Bevan expressed concerns with the lighting and asked if the lighting ordinance applies to the new light poles or if the applicant just has to replace what currently exists.  Mr. Johnson confirmed that anything new must comply with the new ordinance.  

Commissioner Mills referenced the overlay of the Fort Union Master Plan and asked why eight-foot sidewalks were not proposed if the intent is to remove the park strip.  He asked if a walking trail was considered since the property abuts Little Cottonwood Creek.  Mr. Hulka explained that the six-foot sidewalk was requested because it was also proposed with the two townhome projects to the east.  After further discussion with the ARC, it was determined that the trees will be moved back leaving additional room to potentially accommodate an eight-foot sidewalk.  The Parks Plan calls for a trail along Little Cottonwood Creek but had yet to be adopted.  Mr. Hulka confirmed that the property owner will be responsible for any trees that impede traffic.  

Mr. Johnson stated that staff is interested in exploring any opportunities to partner or collaborate on easements, trails, or improvements.  

(Project GPA-20-001) – A Public Hearing to Receive Comments on a City-Initiated Proposal to Adopt a Parks, Trails, and Open Space Master Plan as an Addendum to the Cottonwood Heights General Plan.  

Mr. Hulka provided an update on the City-initiated proposal to adopt a Parks, Trails, and Open Space Master Plan as an addendum to the Cottonwood Heights General Plan.  It has been a work in progress for a number of years and takes inventory of the existing parks, trails, and open space and makes recommendations for the future.  An interactive map was displayed.  Recommendations include east side open space preservation, preservation of the hillside, public amenities when projects develop along Fort Union, the gravel pit, and the Old Mill area.  A Future Trails Map included potential options for trails throughout the City.  He indicated that funding for such projects could come from the City budget, additional fees, and bonding in addition to other programs and donations.  The importance of moving forward was emphasized.  Mr. Hulka stated that if there are no major concerns or changes requested during the public comment, staff will move ahead with finishing touches and finalizing items in preparation for adoption.  

Adjournment.

Commissioner Rhodes moved to adjourn.  Commissioner Mills seconded the motion.  The motion passed with the unanimous consent of the Commission.  

The Work Session adjourned at 5:58 p.m.

(Project SPL-21-002) – A Public Hearing to Receive Comments and Take Possible Action on a Request from Utah First Federal Credit Union for Site Plan Approval of a New Financial Institution at 1166 East Fort Union Boulevard in the MU (Mixed-Use) Zone.

Chair Coutts reported that the above item is a request from Utah First Federal Credit Union for site plan approval of a new financial institution located at 1166 East Fort Union Boulevard. 


Senior City Planner, Andrew Hulka presented the staff report and stated that the request is for site plan approval for the Utah First Federal Credit Union, which is located in the former Famous Dave’s Restaurant building.  Renderings of the project were displayed.  The proposal is for a single-story building with three drive-thru lanes.  The entrance was to be expanded to allow traffic to flow between the credit union and the adjacent office building with the existing one-way egress along the east side, which will remain to serve the drive-thru.  There are 74 parking stalls on the parcels owned by the applicant.  Five stalls are immediately adjacent to the building with 69 shared stalls in the larger parking lot.  The Landscaping Plan included multiple trees that would either be preserved or planted.  The Architectural Review Committee (“ARC”) recommended trees be moved behind the sidewalk to allow room for adequate growth.  

An exterior rendering was displayed.  Four new parking lot light poles were proposed around the building up to 18 feet tall that will meet all of the City’s requirements for outdoor lighting.  The exterior consists of aluminum composite materials, a porcelain rain screen, and resin panels that will be internally illuminated.  Photos of a recently completed branch were referenced.  The City recommended having a turn-off time for the illuminated panels while the ARC felt it was a good quality design and recommended the lights remain on.  The property is located in the Gateway Overlay District and requires a Certificate of Design Compliance from the ARC.  Terms of approval included the following:

Install a new six-foot sidewalk along Fort Union Boulevard in front of parcel 22-20-454-003.
Install one streetlight along Fort Union Blvd. compliant with Section 12.24.190 “Street Lighting” of the City Code. 
New parking lot lights shall not exceed 18 feet in height. 
No street trees along Fort Union Boulevard may be planted within 30 feet of the “egress only” drive-thru exit.
The applicant shall work with the City to relocate the street trees to the park strip area south of the sidewalk. 

Mr. Hulka explained that the request is not for a Conditional Use Permit but involves the review of the site plan for compliance with the Code.  The intent was to ensure compliance and not impose additional conditions.  He confirmed that the City will review technical specifications.   

Rob Merrick, the Project Architect representing Utah First Credit Union, reported that the proposed building measures 3,200 square feet.  The access will be expanded to allow for traffic flow between shared buildings.  He stated that the lighting is not intrusive especially with the closest unit across the street being 200 feet away.  The intent was to keep as much existing vegetation along the creek bed as possible to preserve the integrity of the property.  

Chair Coutts opened the public hearing.  There were no public comments.  The public hearing was closed.  

Commissioner Bevan stated that his questions were answered during the Work Session.  He wished to maintain consistency with the width of the sidewalks throughout.  CED Director, Michael Johnson, confirmed that the sidewalk matches what is seen elsewhere.  The Fort Union Plan effectively picks up at 1300 East moving eastward.  Although the proposed location was not clearly addressed, it is in the Midvalley Plan. 

Chair Coutts indicated that consideration of a trail behind the property as discussed in the Work Session was simply a request and not a mandate.  She asked if the neighboring property owner was agreeable to the proposed design.  Mr. Merrick confirmed that he was. 

Commissioner Rhodes moved to approve Project SPL-21-002 based on the following:

Findings:  

The proposed use described in the report is a permitted use within the MU-Mixed Use Zone. 

A public hearing was held in accordance with local and state requirements. 

A Certificate of Design Compliance was issued by the ARC on February 25, 2021. 

Commissioner Mills seconded the motion.  Vote on motion:  Dan Mills-Aye, Lucy Anderson-Aye, Jessica Chappell-Aye, Jesse Allen-Aye, Craig Bevan-Aye, Douglas Rhodes-Aye, Chair Chris Coutts-Aye.  The motion passed unanimously.

(Project GPA-20-001) – A Public Hearing to Receive Comments A Public Hearing to Receive Comments on a City-Initiated Proposal to Adopt a Parks, Trails, and Open Space Master Plan as an Addendum to the Cottonwood Heights General Plan.  

Chair Coutts reported stated that the above item involves a public hearing to receive comments and a public hearing on a City-initiated proposal to adopt a Parks, Trails, and Open Space Master Plan as an addendum to the Cottonwood Heights General Plan. 

Mr. Hulka presented the staff report and stated that the matter has been before the Planning Commission for several months and was previously reviewed by the Parks, Trails, and Open Space Committee.  The Plan was published in the March newsletter and allowed residents to provide feedback.  As funds are available for new projects and opportunities present themselves, staff turns to their master plans to help guide their decision-making process.  Connectivity of trails was reviewed.  Mr. Hulka stated that the plan is to move forward, prepare a final draft, and present it to the Commission for a recommendation to the City Council. 

Commissioner Bevan believed there is a nice plan in place and appreciated the effort of staff and their forward-thinking. 

Commissioner Chappell commented that while reviewing the plan, she noticed multiple east to west plans and expected to see more north to south and possibly something along Highland Drive.  She asked if improvements along Highland Drive were in the works.  Mr. Hulka remarked that it has not been specifically discussed but he was willing to add it to his notes for further review.   

Commissioner Allen asked how the City’s prioritization process is determined should additional funds become available.  Mr. Johnson reported that when they have grant opportunities, they review it as staff and make a recommendation based on available funding and grant criteria.  It is then taken to the City Council to receive their legislative priority.  Mr. Hulka explained that leaving the prioritization vague allows them to adapt to what may be upcoming.  Commissioner Allen agreed with having theoretical criteria.  With the Council’s legislative priorities, he believed there may be a place for regional partnerships for projects like the Bonneville Shoreline Trail. 

Commissioner Mills emphasized the importance of preserving wild spaces and the need to keep them as wild as possible.  He explained that people back east in urban areas are encouraged to leave a portion of their property or development to parks. He too was in favor of seeking out partnerships with other entities, especially municipalities.  

Chair Coutts opened the public hearing.  There were no public comments.  The public hearing was closed.  

Chair Coutts encouraged public input and stated that the matter would be continued to the next meeting scheduled for April 7, 2021.
