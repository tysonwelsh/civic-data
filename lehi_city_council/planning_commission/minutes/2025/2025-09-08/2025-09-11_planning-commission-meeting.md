3. Regular Agenda

3.1) Consideration of Clyde Capital Group’s request for approval of revisions to the Vistas
at the Point Amenities removing the amphitheater from their required amenities (Tabled
from the May 8th meeting).


Jacob Curtis presented the item. Two major updates have occurred since the last time Planning
Commission heard this item: the amphitheater has been converted to a dog park, and a
conceptual plan of the overlook park has been submitted.

Adam Ostler was present, representing Clyde Capital Group. He gave an overview of the plan.

Commissioner Lockhart asked what other amenities were considered for the spot. Ostler said
they had looked at pickleball courts and playgrounds, among other options. It is a high-amenities
area with the surrounding trails. Commissioner Lockhart asked why the amphitheater was being
removed. Clyde Capital Group wanted to make sure the amenities that are put in are used. Their
research indicates that amphitheaters require lots of community planning as opposed to dog
parks which are more spontaneously used.

Commissioner Eyre asked for clarification on a box drawn on the development plan. It
represented a pickleball court, pump track, and playground area that have already been put in.

Commissioner Everett asked about the monetary value of the dog park vs the amphitheater, as
well as construction details for the dog park. The park will have a double-gated entry, and a
fenced area for off-leash play. The prices are comparable.

Commissioner Kunze asked what the play area would be made of. Ostler wasn’t sure.

Commissioner Lockhart read the listed required amenities as appears in city code and asked for
clarification on which have been met. All amenities have been met according to the original
agreement with City Council; the amphitheater will just be replaced with a dog park.
Commissioner Lockhart asked if a dog park would fulfil the need for a community gathering
space that the amphitheater would provide. Jacob Curtis agreed that it is not a traditional meeting
space, but that it is a space where people can gather and talk, rather than an event space like an
amphitheater. Kim Struthers mentioned that many of the amenities have been aged up as they are
expecting the demographic of the development to be older (a gym has been installed in place of
playgrounds for example). He emphasized that the commission should only focus on the replaced
amenity.

Commissioner Eyre stated that as the overlook park was always part of the planned community,
he doesn’t think of it as an additional amenity that makes up for the lack of an amphitheater.
Struthers explained that the developers are planning on making the park nicer than previously
planned. Commissioner Eyre expressed concern over taking value into consideration when
numbers were not presented to the commission. Commissioner Jackson seconded his sentiment.
He expressed frustration at the applicant not providing information to the commission.

Commissioner Eyre mentioned that he thinks that the exchange of a dog park for the
amphitheater is a positive one, and he agrees that the dog park will likely get more use. He
doesn’t think it’s possible to compare them one to one, but he sees it as an important part of a
neighborhood where there are no yards.

Commissioner Lockhart said that she thinks the intent of a community gathering place should be
met. She said it would be nice to zoom out and look at surrounding parcels to see where other
amenities lie nearby.
Kim Struthers brought forth the original approved package presented by the applicant, which
listed the amphitheater’s price at $250,000. Commissioner Lockhart asked if the commission had
previously approved amenities that don’t meet city standards. Struthers explained that there are
amenities suggestions in the code, but that Planning Commission has the final decision on which
amenities are approved.

Ostler returned to the stand to address some concerns. He clarified that the main concern of the
commission should be the dog park, as the amenities standards have been met. Commissioner
Lockhart clarified that the approved change originally included an amphitheater. She worries that
the community is losing benefits if this is approved, as the trails are a city-run project. Ostler
stated that $200,000 has been budgeted for the dog park. Struthers explained that the trails are a
big value for the city, and the developer put them in rather than the city having to.

Commissioner Eyre likes the idea of a dog park as an amenity. He thinks it would be a positive
and attracting feature for the neighborhood.

Commissioner Lockhart asked if the development would allow pets. It will.

Commissioner Jackson asked if any practical steps would be taken to make the park inviting to
people without dogs. Ostler explained that the dog run area would be fenced off. It will be
surrounded by trees to make it more of a green space, and there will be benches scattered around.
He also clarified that the park itself would be made out of grass.

Commissioner Eyre mentioned that the dog park at Willow Park is the cheapest the city could
build, but he still sees it heavily used at all times despite the fact that it isn’t close to any homes.
He feels the dog park would get a lot of utility in the area, especially with the proximity of the
units. Commissioner Lockhart still felt that it would be an exclusionary amenity.

Commissioner Eyre asked if the HOA would cover the maintenance of the dog park. Ostler
confirmed it would. Michelle Grow, Ostler’s coworker, came to the stand to explain that the
amphitheater had to be relocated to an area that wouldn’t have as much appeal due to the wind
study from the flight park. She mentioned that there is intent to put more amenities in the park
than are required, like a pet wash station, intentional shading, and high quality turf. Both the
overlook park and the dog park would be connected to the trail system, which would establish a
good pedestrian flow.

Commissioner Lockhart felt as though a dog park wouldn’t meet the intent of a TOD, and she
worried about losing the spirit of a TOD in the area, as no commercial development has gone in.
Kim Struthers explained that there is opportunity for commercial development nearer to the
station in the TOD, but only residential has been put in so far.

Commissioner Jackson asked if staff had any input on the intent of the amenity as a community
gathering place. Struthers emphasized that it is up to commission to decide if the dog park meets
the amenity criteria.

                Motion: Commissioner Everett moved to approve the proposed request to create
                a dog park in lieu of amphitheater, using an equivalent value as justification being
                placed into the clubhouse and the overlook park to be consistent with the
                amenities requirement as required by code, recognizing that at face value the dog
                park as a gathering place may not meet a TOD requirement for a gathering place,
                but could, depending on use of the trail system, that the amphitheater in its
                proposed location was unable to be constructed due to the flight park, and that this
                allows development in that space for a use consistent with the parameters of the
                wind study. He included all DRC comments.

Commissioner Eyre seconded the motion with the stipulation that the clause about the dog park
not meeting the definition of a meeting space be struck, as there is no definition in code for a
community meeting space. Commissioner Everett agreed.

Commissioner Lockhart asked the commission if they thought people would ride Trax to get to
the dog park. She clarified that the amphitheater wasn’t an impossibility, just that the
amphitheater in one specific location was not possible. The amphitheater could still be built.

Commissioner Everett withdrew his motion. He appreciated Lockhart’s comment on location and
said that the burden of proof should be on the applicant, not the commission, to justify the
replacement.

Michelle Grow returned to the stand to explain that where the amenity would be located, the
aesthetic and noise from surrounding commercial development would be less than ideal for an
amphitheater. Additionally, parking would be an issue. Commissioner Jackson asked where the
amphitheater was originally supposed to be. Grow didn’t know. Commissioner Kunze asked how
tall the buildings were in the development. Grow was uncertain but knew that they were shorter
than planned due to the wind study. Struthers shared that according to his memory they’ve gone
down to three stories from the original five. Commissioner Kunze asked if the wind study took
buildings into consideration. It did. Commissioner Eyre wondered why two four story buildings,
or even one story buildings, would have a different effect on the area than an open space like an
amphitheater.

Commissioner Kunze said that the planned amphitheater was a small, sixty seat venue. She
didn’t think that at that size it would be a good gathering space. Jacob Curtis clarified that when
the plan was originally approved, there were no other amphitheaters in the city.

Commissioner Everett expressed frustration at having to dig out answers as a commission rather
than being presented with pertinent information by the applicant.

Commissioner Lockhart stated that the TOD is supposed to be both an origin and a destination
point. She doesn’t know if a dog park would be a destination people traveled to. She explained
that she is cautious about swapping out amenities due to previous issues in Traverse Mountain.
She also wanted to preserve the TOD as it has been a big city project, and she didn’t want to
undermine it. Commissioner Eyre argued that a dog park would bring out more people than a
sixty-seat amphitheater would.

Commissioner Lockhart asked if the HOA would allow public access to the dog park, as it is
supposed to be a private amenity. Ostler explained that the intent is for the park to be maintained
by the HOA and used as a public space. Commissioner Lockhart asked staff if this was a normal

arrangement. Struthers explained that the amenities being discussed are meant to serve the
community living in the TOD according to his understanding of code.

Commissioner Lockhart asked what parts of the planned commercial development will bring
people in. The commercial development is being worked on, but residential has been the priority.
The commission discussed how commercial development could be a draw.

Commissioner Lockhart asked why the dog park is closer to transit than to residential
development if its intended to be for residents.

Commissioner Kunze mentioned that the trail ends at the dog park. The commission asked to see
the trail system. The commission expressed discomfort with Mountain Point Boulevard.
Struthers explained that it is a one-lane each way road with train tracks in the middle. It is a low
traffic road with protected bikeways on both sides.

Commissioner Lockhart said that she made the motion to table this item in May in order to help
the developers avoid the 12 month resubmittal period. She didn’t want to give a negative
recommendation at this meeting for the same reason. She explained that she doesn’t like the
location of the park and suggested that the commission table the item again in order to give the
developers another shot.

Commissioner Jackson clarified that the burden of proof is on the applicant. He shared concerns
that it wasn’t clear whether this was an HOA amenity or a TOD amenity.

                Motion: Commissioner Lockhart moved to table the item to give the applicant an
                opportunity to provide the burden of proof for the amphitheater and wind study,
                and why the amphitheater can’t go where the dog park is being suggested, to
                provide additional support for the evidence of the dog park being for the residents
                and potentially moving it closer to the residents. The commission would like to
                see other design options, and to see the intent of the code being met.
                Commissioner Eyre seconded the motion.

                Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner Lockhart, yes. Commissioner
                Eyre, no. Commissioner Jackson, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes.

                Motion passed four in favor, one against.

3.2) Public hearing and consideration of Colby Anderson’s request for approval of
revisions to the Moto United Site Plan to include an 8-foot fence located at 405 South
Millpond Drive

Jacob Curtis presented the item and clarified that the fence would actually be located at 411
Millpond Drive. He explained that there are no grade elevation issues or safety concerns. This is
a personal request for security purposes. There is a space on the lot used for loading, and the
applicant wants to protect inventory.

Andrew was present, representing Colby Anderson. He had no additional comments.

Commissioner Everett asked what CMU security fence was. It is a masonry block. The purpose
is solely for security; there are no noise concerns.

Curtis clarified that this is a light industrial zone that is not adjacent to residential.

Commissioner Jackson opened and closed public comment.

                Motion: Commissioner Kunze moved to approve the item with the findings that
                the proposed use at the proposed location will not be detrimental or injurious to
                property or improvements in the vicinity, and will not be detrimental to public

                Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner Lockhart, yes. Commissioner
                Eyre, yes. Commissioner Jackson, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes.

3.4) Public Hearing and Recommendation of Lehi City’s request for a review of a
Development Code Amendment to Chapter 37, Connectivity Standards.

Jacob Curtis presented the item. This is primarily Mike West’s project. Many minor changes are
being implemented so that city code matches UDOT terminology, as well as other specific
details being reevaluated. Some changes have been influenced by recent projects.

Commissioner Everett asked if the ten foot wide path for culs-de-sac included landscaping. Kim
Struthers explained that the paths themselves would be ten feet wide, and that landscaping would
be included in the requirements.

Struthers explained that the major change to connectivity standards would allow developers the
opportunity to supplement a bike or pedestrian trail rather than connecting a stub road.

Commissioner Lockhart asked if this would allow the city to demand traffic-calming features on
streets not part of the parcel if overspill traffic comes into neighborhoods. It would be more of a
negotiation than a demand. Commissioner Lockhart asked if the Fire and Police Departments had
weighed in. Garion Rowett, the fire marshal, looked over the plan in DRC. He wants to use the
shared-use paths for emergency vehicles.

Commissioner Eyre asked if there is anything in the new code discussing curb cuts from a path
onto a road, as he has concerns for cyclists. Kim Struthers explained that they were included in
the most recent update to the city’s design standards. Struthers went on to say that the city has
looked into different indicative tiles for crossings that would make moving around easier for
blind residents.

Commissioner Lockhart shared that she sees the low traffic neighborhood circulation
improvements clause as a great example of putting residents first. She worried about not being
specific enough on the requirements for the multi-use paths, which would allow developers to
cut corners and result in the paths being unusable by emergency vehicles. Mike West has added
this note already.

Commissioner Jackson opened public comment.

Peter Ovard approves of the suggested changes. He thinks they make sense, and he agrees with
Lockhart on the implementation of item 6.

Commissioner Jackson closed public comment.

                Motion: Commissioner Lockhart moved to give a positive recommendation to the
                Lehi City Council with the findings that it is in line with he intent of the city’s
                code in that it adds and updates definitions, it adds provisions to the circulation
                plan requirement that the city may require modal filters, street closures, or access
                management improvements, it’s changing “trail” to “shared use path” throughout
                the traffic to be more specific and match UDOT terminology, and it removes the
                hard maximum block length requirements for the optional master plan trail and
                greenway exceptions. She included all DRC comments. Commissioner Eyre
                seconded.

                Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner Lockhart, yes. Commissioner
                Eyre, yes. Commissioner Jackson, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes.

                Motion passed unanimously.
