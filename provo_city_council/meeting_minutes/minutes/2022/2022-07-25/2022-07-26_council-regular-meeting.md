7.         An ordinance amending the zone map classification of approximately 32.6 acres of real
           property, located at 3701 W Center Street, from Agricultural (A1.10) to Airport Industrial
           (AI) and authorizing a related development agreement. (PLRZ20210263) (40:53)

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2022-26, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Mr. Wright shared the desired development for this area. He said the planning commission is in favor
with the condition that a development agreement would be implemented, which would provide more
information requested by Public Works.

Chair Hoban gave information that was provided in the work session regarding traffic. He said the city is
limited on what we could do to limit traffic in the area.

Councilor Fillmore asked about long-term plans of widening center street to accommodate the traffic.

Rob Hunter, Transportation Engineer, answered. He also discussed other projects along Geneva Road
which could help this development.

Chair Hoban opened public comment.

Jonathan Hill, chair of Fort Utah Neighborhood, shared support from the surrounding neighborhood. He
suggested that the city try to limit use of center street and encourage traffic to use Lakeview Parkway.

Chair Hoban asked Mr. Wright about the traffic patterns of Center Street being a concern for Public
Works.

Mr. Hunter said there will be an impact on Center Street during the development of Lakeview Parkway.
He discussed the some of the factors that are to be considered and said the focus right now is to find an
alternate route.

Melanie McCoard, of Provo, suggested the city uses this development as an opportunity to widen the
road and that the developer should be responsible for it. She added that within the development
agreement there should be language that mandates the traffic patterns.

Becky Bogden, chair of the Lakewood Neighborhood, said a truck route for Lakeview Parkway should be
used and that proper signage would need to be added.

Lindy Doller, of Panattoni Development, addressed some of the concerns that had been brought up. She
said the developers would be happy to provide signage close to the project site. She also shared that the
impact fees they will be paying for the project will go towards the improvements on Center Street.

Councilor Fillmore asked if the impact fees had been quantified.

Bill Peperone, Development Services Director, explained that the project is at the zoning stage and that
the impact fees are assessed at the building permit stage. He also shared that the Capital Improvement
Plan from Public Works would determine how those fees would be used.

Troy Jensen, of Provo, said Provo has the highest impact fees in the state.

With no other comments, Chair Hoban closed public comment.

Councilor Ellsworth shared her excitement for the project. She also shared that without a transportation
analysis, it would be hard to determine how much this project would have an impact on the roads. She
added that she wants to be cognizant of the residents that would be impacted by more use of Lakeview
Parkway.

Chair Hoban called for a vote.

      Vote:        The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Hoban,
                   MacKay, Shipley, and Whipple in favor.

David Wright, chair for Rock Canyon Neighborhood, thanked Chair Hoban for his kind words. He asked
to see examples of the concerns that John Magness brought up in a different meeting. He shared his
concerns for changing the program and said he feared it would not allow the neighborhoods to be
represented.

Jeff Hammond, of the Rock Canyon Neighborhood, said there has not been much outreach within his
neighborhood. He expressed his support for the changes proposed.

Troy Jensen, of the Franklin Neighborhood, shared his distaste for the council appointing individuals as
neighborhood chairs.

Mary Miller, chair of the Spring Creek Neighborhood, said she believes this will shut out many residents
in her neighborhood. She expressed her concern for residents who are not well represented including
residents who do not speak English and those who do not have access to technology.

Warner Woodworth, of Provo, asked if this is a solution to a problem. He asked if enough research had
been done and if this change will support a better quality of life for citizens.

Sharon Memott, of the Edgemont Neighborhood, said she feels like that if the council appoints chairs,
the chairs would be working for the council and not their own neighbors. She pleaded to keep elections
for neighborhood chairs.

Becky Bogden, chair of the Lakewood Neighborhood, shared elections make the residents feel like they
are in control of who is representing them. She said she appreciates the desire to make the
neighborhood program better, but she thinks council is moving in the wrong direction.

Dave Knecht, of Provo, shared his concerns for online participation.

Johnathon Hill, chair of the Fort Utah Neighborhood, shared things he liked and disliked about the
proposed changes to the program.

Chair Hoban shared that the neighborhoods could still have their elections and nominate the chairs for
their neighborhood.

Brian Jones shared that the appointment of a neighborhood chair would happen during a public meeting
in which the entire neighborhood could attend.

Melanie McCoard, of Provo, suggested some funding be used for elections. She shared when she was
chair, she applied for a grant from the program to use. She also recommended more staffing for the
program.

Boyd Memott, Edgemont neighborhood, said that democracy is an experiment. He also shared his
concern for the appointment process.

Zach Green, of the Joaquin Neighborhood, shared his thoughts on the program.

Carolyn Wright, of Provo, shared and experience she had with Councilor MacKay. She shared that the
open dialogue that can be had at a neighborhood meeting is much more effective to residents. She
stated she fears the council will lose support if they make all the changes proposed.

Chair Hoban closed public comment.

Councilor MacKay read email from Kim Santiago.

Councilor Whipple read email from Susan and David Keller.

Councilor Fillmore shared one of the concerns of the council is underrepresentation. He expressed his
thoughts on elections versus appointments by council. He suggested the city help neighborhoods create
legitimate elections.

Chair Hoban stated that he does not believe this is a democratic process. He said the purpose of the
neighborhood program is to improve communication between the neighborhood and the city.

Mr. Jones addressed attendee who was interrupting and reminded them that they did not have the floor
to speak.

Councilor Handley expressed his gratitude for those who shared their thoughts. He said he is saddened
to hear the pain and frustration from neighborhood chairs who have given so much to the program. He
shared he would love more civic engagement and said it is the goal of the program.

Councilor MacKay explained the agenda would be created and published by the city staff, but the items
would be provided by the neighborhoods and districts.

Karen Tapahe confirmed Councilor MacKay’s statements.

Councilor MacKay also shared that there are voting features on Zoom. She shared her thoughts on the
proposed program.

With no other discussion, Chair Hoban adjourned the meeting.

Adjournment

The meeting was adjourned by unanimous consent at approximately 8:03 PM.
