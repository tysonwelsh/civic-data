The purpose of the joint work session was to review the agenda for the Municipal Building Authority and
Redevelopment Agency meetings, scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.; discuss the following areas of the Fiscal Year (FY) 2023
Budget: public safety employee group, General Fund revenues, Business Depot Ogden (BDO) Lease revenues, and the Attorney’s
Department; review the Municipal Building Authority FY 2022-2023 budget, and discuss Council/Board/Trustee business.

Agenda Review

Acting Chair Lopez and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the Municipal Building
Authority (MBA) and Redevelopment Agency (RDA) meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Fiscal Year 2022-2023 City Budget

Public Safety Employee Group Input: Representatives from the Ogden Fire Association (OFA), the Ogden Police
Benefit Association (OPBA), and the Fraternal Order of Police (FOP)—the public safety employee groups—have been invited to
address the City Council on issues relating to the proposed FY2023 budget.

John Thomas, Vice President of the Weber County FOP, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the entity’s
position on the proposed FY2023 budget and their gratitude for the efforts of City Administration and the City Council to increase
wages and provide other benefits that help in recruitment and retention efforts of the Police Department.

Alex Sanders, President of OFA, also addressed the Council regarding efforts to increase pay of public safety employees;
he thanked them for the recent actions taken but noted that the Fire Department is still understaffed, and it is still difficult to
recruit and retain quality employees.

Detective Ann Stellman, President of OPB, echoed the gratitude expressed by both Mr. Thomas and Mr. Sanders; over
the past several years she has engaged with employees in many other Departments and Divisions of the City and heard their
concerns about wage disparity. Employees are very grateful for the efforts of City Administration and the City Council for the
shift they have made and the programs they continue to pursue to make employment with Ogden City more attractive.

The Council engaged in discussion with both Mr. Thomas and Mr. Sanders regarding improvements in their respective
Departments and the remaining barriers to recruiting new employees. There was also a focus on the guidelines for the “Own in
Ogden” housing program that provides incentives for City employees who choose to live in Ogden.

General Fund Revenues: Comptroller Stout used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed
revenue distribution in the FY2023 budget; revenue sources include taxes, licenses and permits, intergovernmental revenue, fines
and forfeitures, transfers, and other. Total revenue included in the FY2023 budget is $78,387,025. She summarized property tax
revenue details and sales tax growth dating back to 2013, after which she provided a comparison of the FY2022 and FY2023
budget relative to other revenue sources.

Discussion among the Council and Ms. Stout centered on topics such as the timing of the City’s receipt of revenues
throughout the year; elimination of incentive pay for Public Safety employees; and the cause for the dramatic increase in parking
fines.

Business Depot Ogden (BDO) Lease Revenues: Deputy Comptroller Sorensen used the aid of a PowerPoint
presentation to discuss the FY2023 budget proposals pertaining to BDO Lease Revenues. He reviewed a chart detailing revenue
appropriations for ordinance/agreement requirements, arts, Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) projects, and non-CIP projects and
expenditures. There was brief discussion among the Council and Administration staff regarding the scope of various CIP projects
that will be funded with BDO lease revenue. There was also a brief focus on other funding sources available for certain CIP
projects, including America Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funding.

Departmental Budget Review — Attorney: City Attorney Williams provided a brief overview of his Department budget;
the only significant change to his budget when compared to the FY2022 budget is in relation to personnel costs; the 13 percent
general employee cost of living adjustment (COLA) will apply to his Department. He then briefly discussed accomplishments and
challenges the Department has faced over the past few years.

Fiscal Year 2022-2023 Municipal Building Authority Budget

Deputy Comptroller Sorensen explained the current proposal is to adopt the FY2022-2023 Municipal Building Authority
(MBA) budget totaling $5,000. The budget maintains only a small balance for any expenses related to financial reporting. The
MBA budget no longer includes debt service for Lindquist Stadium as those bonds were refunded during the 2020 fiscal year and
are paid through the City’s General Fund and not the MBA. There was brief discussion among the Council and Administrative
staff regarding projects in the City that could be completed utilizing an MBA bond; Chief Administrative Officer (CAO) Johnson
noted that Administration does not want to retire the MBA at this time as there may be the possibility and need to issue future
bonds under the MBA.

Council Business

Council Calendar: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reviewed the Council’s calendar and meeting schedule for
the remainder of the month of May.

City Code: Acting Chair Lopez noted he heard from a resident who was interested in opening a haunted house in Ogden
City but has found the use is prohibited in Ogden City. He inquired as to his options for pursuing a code amendment to allow that
type of business. Chief Administrative Officer Johnson stated he will ask Planning staff to research the matter and provide a report
to the Council.

Flip the Strip Program: The Council briefly discussed the status of a proposal from the Planning Commission to
consider ordinance amendments to make residents in Ogden City eligible to receive a rebate from the Weber Basin Water
Conservancy District for completing a ‘flip the strip’ project.

The meeting adjourned at 5:39 p.m.
