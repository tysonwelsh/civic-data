Park City Library Annual Update - FY 2019:
Adriane Herrick-Juarez, Library Director, presented this item and stated the newly
renovated library was going into its fifth year of operation. She presented a report of
statistics regarding library users during Fiscal Year 2019 and noted an increase in visits
over FY 2018 by 5,500. A new policy implemented last year was ending the tradition of
charging fines for overdue library items. The library increased its social equity emphasis
by tripling the number of Spanish and bilingual programs, which currently served 1,400
Spanish speakers. She also discussed programs and events for neuro-diverse people
and other demographics in the community.

Herrick-Juarez discussed the success of the children's, youth, and teen programs. She
also reviewed the upgrades to the library's extensive databases. She thanked the
Friends of the Library, the community, and the support of the Council for their help in
making the library such a success.

2. Consideration of Ordinance 2020-12, an Ordinance Repealing Land
Management Code § 15-3-5, Driveway Standards for Private Driveways within
Platted, Unbuilt City Streets:
Rebecca Ward, Land Use Planner, presented this item. She indicated the repeal of this
section of the code would be for applicants for conditional use permits (CUP) for
driveways in public rights-of-way. Staff was working on a replacement code in which
requests would not be allowed through the CUP process. The Planning Commission
voted five to one with the dissenter wanting this to remain in the code until the
replacement code was approved.

Ward reviewed the history of this section of the code. She referred to an Engineering
memo from 1998 that included 11 criteria, and when the City updated the Land
Management Code (LMC) in 2000, some of those recommendations were codified and
people were able to apply for CUPs if they met the criteria. Since 2000, only two CUPs
were approved, and they could be revoked if necessary. Ward indicated the new code
would be on the March 26th agenda for approval, but in the meantime, the Council could
consider any of these proposals or an applicant could petition the City to vacate a public
right-of-way for a driveway.

Council Member Worel asked if there were any applications that would be affected by
this repeal. Ward stated the current applications could proceed through the process.
The repeal would only affect new applications going forward.

Council Member Doilney discussed the two options in the staff report and thought there
was a contradiction. Erickson stated there were 700 encroachment agreements, most
being snowmelt encroachments. Those were different than expanding a driveway on a
City right-of-way. In March, the encroachments would be broken down and explained.

Council Member Henney felt CUPs were a gray area and he favored specificity. He
knew there were certain situations where they worked, but this use was not one of the
scenarios that was in a gray area. He preferred applicants coming to request a
variance.

Council Member Joyce felt this right-of-way was the City's and it shouldn't be given
away if an applicant could prove mitigation. He thought rights-of-way were the City's to
negotiate, not the applicant's to claim. He appreciated the quick action for changing the

                         PROPOSAL
The Planning Commission voted 5 – 1 to forward a positive
recommendation to repeal § 15-3-5 and recommends that the Council:

(1) Maintain a role for the Planning Commission, at a minimum as an
    advisory board, within the application process for private driveways
    in the platted, un-built right-of-way;

(2) Retain the public notice process for certain encroachments; and

(3) Clearly outline a new policy in a Resolution or the LMC to provide
    clarity for the public, and future staff, Commissioners, and City
    Council members.

            RESOLUTION No. 8-98
                    VACATION OF ROW
July 9, 1998 – Council City passed Resolution 8-98

• Outlines the City’s policy on vacation of rights-of-way

• Requires in part that proposals must:
   • Compensate the City for the loss of the right-of-way;
   • Dedicate open space, trails, or public access beyond what is

   • Dedicate any other public amenity deemed in the best interests of

of ________________, 2020, with a retroactive date of January 30, 2020, by and
between PARK CITY MUNICIPAL CORPORATION, a Utah municipal corporation,
(“City”), and FUTURE IQ, a Minnesota corporation, (“Service Provider”), to amend the
Park City Municipal Corporation Service Provider/Professional Services Agreement
signed and executed by the parties on the 2nd day of April, 2019.

       WITNESSETH:

       WHEREAS, the parties entered into a Park City Municipal Corporation Service
       Provider/Professional Services Agreement on April 2, 2019, (hereinafter
       “Original Agreement”);

       WHEREAS, part of the scope of services is for Service Provider to provide
       services related to Park City Vision 2020 until completion;

       WHEREAS, the term of the Original Agreement ended January 29, 2020;

       WHEREAS, the process is still ongoing; and

       WHEREAS, the parties desire to amend the Original Agreement to include the
       additional scope of services reflected below in Section 1.b. and to compensate
       Service Provider for such services.

       NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the mutual promises made herein and
other valuable consideration, the parties hereto now amend the Original Agreement as
follows:

1. AMENDMENTS:

   a. EXTENSION OF TERM. The term of the Original Agreement shall be
      extended to a termination date of March 31, 2020.
          SCOPE OF SERVICE. The Scope of Services shall be amended to include
          the following: Additional Focus group engagement /analysis - $5,000
          Reconvene TT group / Leadership- $2,500
          Community Future Summit #2 - $2,500
          Council Retreat -$2,500
          Additional Community Engagement Sessions -$2,500
          Sustainable Tourism Think Tank $10,000

   The fee for the Project shall be increased by Twenty Five Thousand Dollars           Formatted: Indent: Left: 0.25"
   ($25,000.00) for a total not to exceed amount of Eighty Five Thousand Dollars
   ($85,000.00). Said increase shall be for the additional services reflected above.
