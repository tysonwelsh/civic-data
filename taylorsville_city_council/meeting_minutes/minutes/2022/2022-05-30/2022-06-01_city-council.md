                     5% decrease in health insurance

He reminded those gathered that despite what many people believed, as house values increased,
the city' s tax rate went down, yielding the same amount of revenue. He presented an illustration
showing that with a 10%   property tax increase, the owner of a home valued by the county at
 340, 000 would pay an increase of$ 15. 44 per year. For a home valued at $461, 000, the proposed
increase would require an extra $ 20.95 per year. Mr. Harrington explained that the certified rate
would be shared by Salt Lake County the following week and the Council would be asked to adopt
the rate during the June 15 city council meeting. Utah law required a Truth in Taxation public
hearing which would most likely be held on August 9th( but possibly August 16th).

Another change to the budget was an additional police officer who would be assigned to a task
force at the attorney general' s office. That assignment would bring$ 115, 000 in revenue to the City
which was approximately $36,000 shy of the total annual cost of the new position. Additionally,
an officer was being added to the Victim Advocate program.

Chair Barbieri invited Lynn Handy to address the council as the Chair of the Budget Committee.

Mr. Handy explained that everything the city did had a direct effect on Taylorsville residents. He
stated that the committee supported the budget, including a 10% property tax increase. He then

outlined the top four priorities of the budget committee. They were:

1) Public Safety
2) Infrastructure
3) Human Resources
4) Economic Development

Mr. Taylor pointed out that although the City put safety first and included the SRO in its own

Lacy Parker asked if the storm drain budget included a filter at the end to keep trash and other
debris out of the rivers. She also asked if any money went towards maintaining the system. Chair
Barbieri asked Mr. Taylor to respond. He explained that some drains had grates, although not all
of them( due to clogging issues).He also indicated that the Jordan River was carefully monitored,
and the city was notified if/when there were any issues with outfalls and the city addressed them
as needed. He pointed out that there were several entities that monitored the river, including the
Jordan River Commission and Salt Lake County Flood Control. Mr. Cowdell added that the
Department of Environmental Quality and the Environmental Protection Agency required cities to
have a storm drain maintenance program in place, it was quite stringent, and Taylorsville was fully
compliant.




There was no one else who expressed a desire to speak so Chair Barbieri closed the public hearing.
