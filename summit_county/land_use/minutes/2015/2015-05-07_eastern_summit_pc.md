Director Putt explained that Staff has been working with the Planning Commission for a 
little over a year to make some amendments to the Development Code.  Through that 
process, they have determined it would be wise to consider some possible changes to 
the current zoning map.   
 
Tonight they will be focusing on the last piece of the puzzle, from Oakley to Woodland.  
They are looking for feedback from the Community particularly on this area, but they 
will take comments on the entire east side.  They are looking at establishing new zones 
in certain areas.  These are: 
 
1) The agricultural (AG-1) zone would replace the highway corridor (HC) zone.  
This zone would allow one-unit per-acre.  Both residential and agricultural uses 
would be allowed.    
 
2) The AG-6 zone would have a one-acre home site with five acres of agriculture.  
This configuration will allow the property to stay in greenbelt.   This zone has 
the potential to be a problem solver.  In Eastern Summit County, 60% to 75% of 
the AP-40 zone is non-conforming in size.  If the AG-6 zone is applied in the right 
locations, the problem of non-conformity will mostly be solved.  They think of 
this as a transitional zone between the AG-1 and the AG-40 zones.   
 
The Commission is talking about applying these zones to the base map, which would be 
agriculturally based.  They are also considering adding some new zones that people could 
choose to rezone to.  These are: 
 
1) The rural residential zone (RR) zone would be one-unit per one-acre.  The 
minimum lot size might be as small as .75 acre.  It might have horses and a barn, 
but probably not a mink shed. The difference between this zone and the AG-1 
zone is that it will have a smaller list of agricultural uses.   
2) The residential subdivision (RS) zone would be a single family development with 
little to no agricultural uses.  This could be three-units per acre.   
3) The recreation commercial (RC) zone.  Blue Sky Ranch is a recent example of this 
type of zoning.    
 
Director Putt said they are not here to sell the public on a zoning map, but to receive 
comments and input on how these zones should be mapped out.  They will go back and 
revise the maps with the feedback from the public.   
 
After the Planning Commission has refined the maps, the maps will be presented for 
another round of public input.  They are trying to get this project completed in a timely 
fashion while avoiding bad sloppy decisions.
