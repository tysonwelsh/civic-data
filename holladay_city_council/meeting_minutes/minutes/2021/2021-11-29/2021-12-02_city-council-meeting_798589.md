had been an $860,000 reduction over the past year. Mr. Wood explained that a lot of that came
from the sale of The Cotton Bottom. He added that the Debt Service had a positive Fund Balance
of $729,000.

On Page 17 of the Audit Report 2021, there was the Statement of Revenues, Expenditures, and
Changes in Fund Balances. Mr. Wood stated that the General Fund had an increase of $2,478,642.
Sales tax revenue, Transient Room Taxes, and monies from Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and
Economic Security (“CARES”) Act had not decreased. Last year, the expectation was that sales
tax would drop by approximately 10%. However, sales taxes increased by about 10%. This
allowed the City to fund different projects. Mr. Wood reported that in the past, the unassigned,
committed, and assigned Fund Balance in the General Fund could be 25% of overall revenues.
The State Legislature raised that to 35% in the past year so that cities could cover various needs.
The City of Holladay was currently at 28.2%. That indicated that the City has a healthy Fund
Balance and there were reserves to draw on.

The budget for the General Fund was overviewed on Page 19 of the Audit Report 2021. Mr. Wood
noted that there was a net savings of $2,538,451 in the Total Expenditures. He explained that
some of the savings were related to the difficulty of filling certain positions. Pages 33 and 34 were
discussed. Mr. Wood reported that the Long-Term Debt consisted of $6,095,111. He made note
of the Long-Term Debt summary, which compared the numbers from 2020 to 2021. There was
$8.4 million in total debt at the beginning of 2020, but the City paid down $2.4 million of that in
2021.

V.      Consideration of Ordinance 2021-29 Amending the Budget for the Fiscal Year
        Beginning July 1, 2021, and Ending June 30, 2022.
Mayor Dahle noted that the City Council discussed the Bailiff Services portion of the budget
during the last several meetings. City Manager, Gina Chamness looked into some of the options
further. There was also information included in the packet from the Unified Police Department’s
(“UPD”) Chief Justin Hoyal, which clarified the additional UPD needs.

Chief Hoyal looked into the need for a joint Detective/Bailiff and provided information related to
the demand for mental health services. Ms. Chamness reported that the need for mental health
services at a Detective level had increased dramatically since 2019. The number of mental health
cases increased from 11% to 25% of UPD total cases. Ms. Chamness noted that the increased need
for those services was anticipated as part of the American Rescue Plan Act (“ARPA”) funds. The
City could use ARPA funds to address the increase in mental health services caused by the
COVID-19 pandemic. As a result, the budget amendment proposed that the Fund Balance paid
for the addition of a UPD Detective/Bailiff, but ARPA funds were put towards an existing
Detective who focused on mental health response.

Council Member Durham asked how a case would become classified as a mental health case.
Chief Hoyal explained that when an officer responds to a call related to mental health, information
was added to the report, which was then submitted to a Detective for follow-up. Chief Hoyal
reported that UPD has a Mental Health Unit with a Sergeant and a Detective. Those were paid for
by Salt Lake County and provided County-wide regional services. There was also a licensed
Clinical Social Worker assigned to that unit. Each precinct had a Detective who followed up on
mental health cases as a secondary assignment. Initially, each precinct Detective followed up on
cases once a month. However, the increase in cases meant that the precinct Detective was now
spending approximately 50% of the time following up on mental health cases.

Ms. Chamness hoped the need for increased mental health services would be reduced by 2024 or
2025 and this would not necessarily be an ongoing cost. However, the City would have to wait
and see what happens over time. Mayor Dahle liked the suggestion shared by the UPD and City
Staff because it allowed the City of Holladay to maintain control of the Bailiff Services. It also
made it possible to assist with mental health services within the community. He believed that by
the time the ARPA funds ran out, there would be enough impact fees to cover any of the additional
costs.

Council Member Petersen moved to adopt Ordinance 2021-29. Council Member Quinn seconded
the motion. Vote on motion: Council Member Fotheringham-Aye; Council Member Durham-
Aye; Council Member Gibbons-Aye; Council Member Quinn-Aye; Council Member Petersen-

VIII. City Manager Report – Gina Chamness.
Ms. Chamness shared updates with the City Council and reported that information was received
from the Utah League of Cities and Towns (“ULCT”) related to the opioid settlement. The City
of Holladay would likely need to arrange a virtual meeting to adopt a Resolution within the desired
timeframe.

Community and Economic Development Director, LaNiece Davenport discussed the Holla Dollars
program. The City launched the program at the end of September 2021 with Federal stimulus
funding. Postcards were sent to all 12,000 households in the City. Ms. Davenport explained that
8% of residents signed up and 72 businesses signed up for the Holla Dollars program. There were
also 20 businesses still in the process of signing up. Residents had until December 17, 2021, to
register for the program and until December 30, 2021, to use the Holla Dollars vouchers. Ms.
Davenport noted that each household received a code for $40 in $10 increments to spend at
participating local businesses.

Council Member Fotheringham asked about the reimbursement process. Ms. Davenport explained
that a contractor was hired to run the program. At the end of each month, the participating business
would send their report to the contractor. The contractor would pay the business directly and send
a bill to the City. Ms. Davenport stated that 400 residents used their vouchers and nearly 1,000
residents signed up.

Public Services Director, Jared Bunch shared an update on the Skate Park. The construction
completion date was extended to December 15, 2021. That extension would allow for necessary
concrete pours as well as the installation of fencing and artificial turf. Construction crews were
working diligently on the project, and he hoped the park would be open to the public shortly.
