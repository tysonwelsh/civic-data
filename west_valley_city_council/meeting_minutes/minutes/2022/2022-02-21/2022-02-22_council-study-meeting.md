A.   ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING APPLICATION ZT-6-2022,
     FILED BY WEST VALLEY CITY, REQUESTING A ZONE TEXT
     CHANGE TO ALLOW INOPERABLE VEHICLE PARKING FOR AN
     AUTOMOBILE SERVICE UNDER CERTAIN CONDITIONS
     Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council
     Meeting scheduled March 1, 2022, in order for the City Council to hear and
     consider public comments regarding application ZT-6-2022, Filed by West Valley
     City, Requesting a Zone Text Change to Allow Inoperable Vehicle Parking for an
     Automobile Service Under Certain Conditions.

       Proposed Ordinance 22-08 related to the proposal to be considered by the City
       Council subsequent to the public hearing, was discussed as follows:

       ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 22-08, AMEND SECTION 7-7-104 OF THE
       WEST VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO AMEND CERTAIN
       REQUIREMENTS CONCERNING AUTOMOBILE SERVICE
       Jody Knapp, CED, discussed proposed Ordinance 22-08 that would amend Section
       7-7-104 of the West Valley City Municipal Code to Amend Certain Requirements
       Concerning Automobile Service.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

           City staff is proposing an ordinance amendment to allow inoperable vehicle
           parking for automobile service uses under certain conditions. Under the
           current ordinance, any parking of inoperable vehicles outside an automotive
           service use for more than 24 hours would be considered outside storage. The
           use of outside storage is prohibited in the C-2 zone, which is where many
           automotive service uses are located. Since some automotive service uses do
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – FEBRUARY 22, 2022
                             -3-


          need to wait occasionally for parts, staff is recommending this ordinance
          change to allow limited parking of inoperable vehicles in conjunction with an
          automotive service use under the following conditions:

                 Inoperable vehicles shall not be located within any minimum required
                  parking stalls and drive aisles and shall not block any traffic flow.
                 Inoperable vehicles shall be screened from any adjacent streets by a
                  building.
                 Inoperable vehicles shall have no visible exterior damage.
                 Inoperable vehicles must be located on asphalt or concrete.

       Councilmember Harmon noted that in one of the images shared by Jody, there are
       boats being stored. He asked if this was a violation. Jody replied no but added that
       the issue is items being stored on a property for years. Councilmember Fitisemanu
       asked how enforcement would apply the proposed regulations. Jody replied that all
       vehicles would have no exterior damage and would need to appear like they are
       simply parked and waiting for parts to arrive. She stated that staff felt this was the
       most clear, consistent way of enforcing.

       The City Council will consider Ordinances 22-08 at the Regular Council Meeting
       scheduled March 1, 2022 at 6:30 P.M.

       Steve Lehman, CED, discussed proposed Ordinance 22-09 that would approve the
       Vacation for a Portion of 4565 South Located at Approximately 4110 West.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
   MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – FEBRUARY 22, 2022

       information as follows:

              The West Valley City Engineering Division is requesting a street vacation
              for a portion of 4695 South at approximately 4110 West. The purpose for
              the street vacation is to remove from public record an old right-of-way that
              is no longer needed for public access.

              Staff has been unable to locate a dedication plat or subdivision plat wherein
              this portion of 4695 would have been dedicated. The street in question was
              likely built when the Academy Park No. 1 Subdivision was platted in 1960.
              Although this portion of 4695 South is not part of the subdivision plat, it
              would have made sense for this connection coming off of 4700 South.

              Because this portion of 4695 South would have been used by the public for
              access into the subdivision, it would qualify as a public street by virtue of
              its perpetual use and maintenance by West Valley City as a public right-of-
              way. The road only consisted of asphalt and some formal improvements on
              the south side of the right-of-way.

              When the City approved the Valley Cove PUD in 1999 which is located to
              the north, a new road was constructed with access being gained from 4695
              South. The PUD dedicated approximately ¾ of an acre for use as a City
              Park or trail head which is located adjacent to the portion of 4695 South to
              be vacated. The new road providing access to the PUD was dedicated and
              improved which effectively closed this portion of 4695 South for public use.

       The City Council will consider Ordinances 22-09 at the Regular Council Meeting
       scheduled March 1, 2022 at 6:30 P.M.

       Parkway Blvd. which is currently used as storm water storage, wetland area, and
       on which the Park Maintenance Facility is located. This property is ideal for a

RESOLUTION 22-45: APPROVE A FEDERAL AID AGREEMENT BETWEEN
THE CITY AND THE UTAH DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION FOR THE
RECONSTRUCTION AND WIDENING OF PARKWAY BOULEVARD
Coby Wilson, Public Works, presented proposed Resolution 22-45 that would approve a
Federal Aid Agreement Between the City and the Utah Department of Transportation for the
Reconstruction and Widening of Parkway Boulevard.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       West Valley City has received federal Surface Transportation Program funds to
       assist with the design and construction of a project on Parkway Blvd between 6400
       West and MVC. The amount of federal participation on this project is $5,300,000,
       which requires a local match of $467,800 from West Valley City, for a total
       programmed amount of $5,767,800.

       The proposed project will reconstruct and widen Parkway Blvd from two-lanes to
       five-lanes, including a center turn-lane. The project will include the addition of bike
       lanes, curb and gutter, sidewalk, street lighting, and drainage improvements. The
   MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – FEBRUARY 22, 2022
                           -6-


       environmental study was recently completed on this project and the project is ready
       to begin the design phase.

       This agreement will enable the project to access the federal funds and authorizes
       the expenditure of those funds throughout the entire project process, including
       design, right-of-way acquisition and construction. The matching funds will be paid
       during different phases of the project.

       Upon execution of the Federal Aid Agreement, UDOT and the City will contract
       with consulting engineering firms to design and manage the construction of the
       project. This resolution also authorizes the city to execute the design and
       construction management agreements at a future date.

       It is initially anticipated that construction will begin in the summer of 2023.

Councilmember Harmon stated that the City Council just approved a subdivision to the
South. He asked how this would work into the City’s plan. Coby replied that a Delay
Agreement will likely be involved so that improvements won’t be installed and then have
to be removed. Mayor Lang asked if sidewalk, curb, and gutter will be installed at the same
time. Coby replied yes and added that bike lanes will also be included.

The City Council will consider Resolution 22-45 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
March 1, 2022 at 6:30 P.M.

Funding for Bicycle Lanes on 3100 South.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as

       2020. As part of that plan preparation, ATP projects within the city were
       evaluated and prioritized. The results of that prioritization process identified 3100
       South Bike Lanes from 7200 West to 4000 West as the top priority ATP project
       within WVC. This information was used earlier this year when UDOT
       recommended several ATP projects to the Utah Transportation Commission for
       TIF funding.

CONSIDER APPLICATION SD-1-2022 FILED BY WEST VALLEY CITY,
REQUESTING FINAL PLAT APPROVAL TO DEDICATE A PORTION OF 4715
SOUTH BETWEEN 4130 AND 4730 WEST
Steve Lehman, CED, presented application SD-1-2022, Filed by West Valley City,
Requesting Final Plat Approval to Dedicate a Portion of 4715 South Between 4130 and 4730
West

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       4715 South was platted under Salt Lake County jurisdiction with County
       subdivisions being located south of 4715 South. It is not known why the entire
       right-of-way was not dedicated, but staff believes the dedication only occurred to
       what is known as the Section Line. There is often no deed, easement or dedication
       plat for these roads or portions of these roads as in this case for this situation.

       During an initial assessment for the 4715 South improvement project to be
       undertaken by West Valley City, Kearns Metro Township, and Salt Lake County,
       the Engineering Division, in conjunction with these other entities, noticed that full
       dedication did not take place when 4715 South was platted and subsequently
       constructed. It was also noted that dedication for those smaller stub connections
       (Arlington Park Drive, Dartmouth Street, and Vassar Street) did not occur. The purpose
       of this plat is to dedicate all remaining right-of-way located on or adjacent to the north side
       of 4715 South.
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – FEBRUARY 22, 2022
                             -8-


       The right-of-way increases in size from approximately 12-14 feet. Future
       improvements include curb, gutter and sidewalk in certain locations for bus stops
       and pedestrian connections. The east/west pedestrian walkway will continue to be
       used within the linear park.

Councilmember Christensen asked if the crosswalk in the area is still being used. Coby
Wilson, Public Works, replied yes and noted that it would be re-striped. Councilmember
Christensen asked if there is a sidewalk at this location. Steve replied not on the north side.
