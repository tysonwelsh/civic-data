1. Discussion regarding the LV 10-White Pine Canyon Village at Canyon Resort – 
Tiffanie Northrup-Robinson, Senior Planner  
 
Planner Northrup-Robinson said the White Canyon Village project is located at 
approximately 3975 Lower Village Road.  The parcel is approximately 4.20 acres in size.  
The parcel is divided by a golf cart path.  The applicant and his team introduced 
themselves: Brad Johnson is with Brad Johnson Consulting, Brett Kearney is with JVW 
architects, and Greg Barker is associated with White Pine Development Corp.   
 
Planner Northrup-Robinson said this is a multi-family project.  It is proposed be 80,000 
square feet and 2½ stories in height.  The applicant is proposing to build 30 multi-
family units.  These will be distributed between duplex, triplex, and fourplex units.  The 
proposed site plan was displayed.  Planner Northrup-Robinson showed the locations of 
the different units, along with the clubhouse and pool.   
 
The County Engineer’s Office had several areas of concern.  Some of the issues are close 
to being resolved, while others still need some work.  Driveways have been addressed 
by the applicants and the plans are being reviewed.  There are easement issues that 
need to be resolved, such as modifying the existing golf cart easement and an easement 
on the southern portion of the property with Rocky Mountain Power.   
 
Connectivity, both within and without the project, is being worked on.  Some of the 
sidewalks have ended in unusual locations.  Other issues are storm water and erosion 
control, but these will be addressed.  Staff wants to be sure the lighting is heading in the 
direction the Commission would like to see.   
 
Planner Northrup-Robinson said there are a multitude of easements on the Lower 
Village Master Plat.  Because the applicant wants to make minor modifications to some

Planner Sean Lewis said the applicant for this project is Brad Johnson.  Mr. Johnson 
introduced the owner, Jeffery Reslund.  Planner Lewis said this is a twofold application.  
The first part is a plat amendment to amend Silver Creek Estates Unit I along with the 
six lots identified in the zoning map, which are located in the Community Commercial 
zone.  The proposal is to combine the six lots into one for the purpose of applying for a 
conditional use permit for multi-family housing.   
 
Planner Lewis explained that the plat amendment and the conditional use permit (CUP) 
are two separate processes; however, the plat amendment must be approved before the 
CUP can be considered.  The applicant is looking for direction if this concept is 
acceptable to the Commission.  They are not looking for approval at this time.  The 
proposal will most likely change somewhat between this time and final approval; 
however, they wanted to start with the concept of possibly constructing eight multi-
family structures.   
 
Planner Lewis said he would like to review how density is calculated in the Community 
Commercial zone.  According to the Code, there is no set density, such as one unit per 20 
acres.  The allowed density is what can fit on the property when all requirements are 
met.  In this instance, 25% of the property is taken off the top for open space.  This 
leaves whatever can fit on the property as long as setbacks, height, parking restrictions, 
and the other requirements of Chapter 4 are met.    
 
Planner Lewis showed a sketch that indicated that if the parcels are not combined, 
there are approximately 3½ acres of potential developable land.  If the lots are 
combined into one, there is a net gain of about one acre of developable land totalling 
approximately 4½ acres.  He emphasized these are rough figures. 
 
Chair Peck asked what would be the total number of residential units.  Planner Lewis 
said a full analysis has not yet taken place.  For this application, the parking will be the 
limiting factor to the density.  Two parking stalls per unit are required.
