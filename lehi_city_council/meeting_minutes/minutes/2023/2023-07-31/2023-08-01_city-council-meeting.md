2. Swearing-In of Planning Commissioners
   Members of the Planning Commission were sworn in by Teisha Wilson, City Recorder.

3. Report on the Netherlands Transportation System.
   Kim Struthers and Mike West, Community Development Department, gave a report on their
   recent trip to Netherlands, describing their transportation system and how Lehi City could apply
   elements to improve transportation in Lehi.

   Mr. Struthers and Mr. West discussed topics related to urban planning and transportation. They
   focused on mobility, land use, and the overall quality of life. They referred to elements such as
   walking, biking, transit, and driving, highlighting how they all interact. They showed images
   and maps to illustrate the bike networks and road networks and pointed out areas of connectivity
   and ongoing improvements.

   the design of these networks to encourage usage.

   Mr. Struthers and Mr. West then discussed land use and livability. They provide examples from
   the Netherlands, showing how compact urban areas can be built with underground parking to
   prioritize open spaces and pedestrian-friendly environments. They presented images of urban
   centers with mixed-use developments, underground parking, and dedicated pedestrian and bike
   streets. They highlight the effectiveness of having parking structures that feed into shopping
   centers instead of having arterial roads that conflict with pedestrian spaces. The conversation
   touched on the cost implications of underground parking and the considerations of land use in
   urban planning.

   Discussion continued around the integration of transportation networks, land use, and urban
   design to create more sustainable and livable cities.

   There was discussion of European countries' higher tax rates to support infrastructure. It was
   suggested that Lehi City could incentivize developers without raising taxes.

   They discussed various projects that creatively use space, such as underground parking, mixed-
   use developments, shared parking agreements, and utilizing rooftops. They highlighted how
   proper design can lead to more efficient land use and reduced infrastructure costs.

   The conversation moved on to the importance of promoting homeownership alongside rentals
   for equity building. They discussed cooperative ownership models that could be implemented
   in Lehi. There was an emphasis on the need for diversity in housing types and the benefits of
   creating livable, pedestrian-friendly neighborhoods. They touched on concepts like biking
   infrastructure, reducing car dependence, and enhancing public spaces to promote happiness and
   well-being.


4. Discussion of Lehi City Flag Design
   Mayor Johnson informed the Council of the need for a Lehi City flag design, rather than just
   using the City logo on the flag. He then showed a video on flag design and how the City might
   apply the principles taught in the video to create a new Lehi City Flag.

    There was discussion regarding the complexity of designing flags and their symbolism. Mayor
    Johnson explained the significance of flags as representations of identity and pride, noting
    examples from other cities and states. He stated that various design attempts were made for a
    new Lehi flag, including incorporating historical elements, agricultural symbols like sugar
    beets and honeycombs, and elements representing technology and the future, such as
    semiconductors.

    Other ideas were also considered, including incorporating the city's crossroads status and
    utilizing simple shapes and colors. They recognized the challenge of balancing past and future
    symbolism in a simple and recognizable design. The importance of involving the public in the
