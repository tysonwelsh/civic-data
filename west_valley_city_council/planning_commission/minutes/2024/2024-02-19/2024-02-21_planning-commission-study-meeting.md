ZONE TEXT CHANGE APPLICATIONS
ZT-1-2024 Petition by WEST VALLEY CITY requesting amendments to Sections 7-1-109,
          7-2-117, 7-10-103, 7-13-517, and 7-14-102 of the zoning ordinance to increase
          the construction threshold that triggers certain improvements and to allow the
          option of payment in lieu of installation of off-site improvements. (Staff – Steve
          Pastorik at 801-963-3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov) Continued from the
          February 14, 2024, public hearing.

Discussion:   Steve Pastorik introduced Coby Wilson, City Engineer, and said he was here to
              answer any questions regarding the continued application ZT-1-2024. He
              reviewed the proposal, explaining that it is for two things. First is adjusting the
              current $50,000 trigger for improvements in place back in the 80s to $150,000
              tracking inflation.
Steve stated that the second change would give the City the option to allow a cash
payment instead of improvements. He noted that the Commission had questions
regarding this change, so he invited Coby to today's meeting.
Coby said that delay agreements become very challenging to collect. If we put a
delay agreement in place on a parcel now, and we don't get to those improvements
for 20 years, the improvement costs would be astronomically different. If there
isn't a storm drain system on the road and we put in curb and gutter, we wouldn't
have anywhere for the water to go. Therefore, adding the curb and gutter might
not be in our best interest. Coby noted that, in the past, this situation would be
addressed by a delay agreement. This text change would allow the developer to do
cash in lieu of that delay agreement. He explained that when the City has an
opportunity with a project in the area, we'll use that funding to make
improvements.
Commissioner McEwen said he doesn't feel this is a good option; He’s for putting
in curb and gutter whenever possible. He feels the City needs to be more
proactive in adding curbs, gutters, and sidewalks, especially when it is a safety
hazard for the community. I would like to see improvements added at the time of
construction. Coby stated that he agreed with that and that we would implement
improvements whenever possible. In those rare situations, it may create additional
problems.
Commissioner Winters said that Coby's example of not adding curb and gutter
because there aren't surrounding storm drains is reasonable. He asked if there
were other situations where they would wait to add improvements.
Coby had Steve pull up Google Earth to a property around 2700 South and
Redwood Road; he pointed out a home with no improvements to the east or west.
Coby stated that generally speaking, if we didn't have that catch basin in there on
the west side, this would be a prime example of where it wouldn't make sense to
add improvements at the same time the property is developed. He noted that once
a more extensive project can come through and add improvements on the south
side of the road, adding improvements to the east and west of this property would
not be beneficial.
Commissioner Winters said his explanation was helpful.
Commissioner McEwen asked how the money would be dispensed. Coby said we
would be collecting that money to provide improvements within the same vicinity
of where it is collected.
Commissioner Winters asked Coby for another example. Coby said that the City
will be doing a project on Parkway Blvd. He pointed out that Ken Milne is
developing a property in which they have a development agreement for
improvements. However, because of the upcoming City project, we would have
collected the monies from Mr. Milne for the City project. Coby explained that
most municipalities have moved to the cash instead of a delay agreement.
              Commissioner McEwen didn't feel those were good examples. He said that in his
              neighborhood, between 3800 South and 4100 South, a sidewalk is still missing on
              the east side, and those homes have been there for quite some time. Coby said, If
              I'm visualizing the area right, that wouldn't be an area where we would typically
              have drainage because improvements are nearby to tie into. So, I would expect
              that we would refrain from exercising a delay agreement or cash instead of a
              delay agreement. In that instance, the improvements would likely be constructed
              with new development.
              Commissioner McEwen thanked Coby for the information he provided.
              Chair Fuller said to clarify, a delay agreement is still an option. We're just adding
              one more option. Steve said that is correct; however, the Council has been
              reluctant to use them, and as Coby mentioned, they're difficult to administer and
              enforce later on.
              Commissioner Winter asked if residents would still have the option to pay for
              improvements in front of their property. Coby said that was correct, but we may
              put additional requirements on them regarding how to do the drainage upstream
              and downstream of the improvements.


ZT-2-2024     Petition by WEST VALLEY CITY requesting amendments to Sections 7-9-104
              and 7-9-105 of the zoning ordinance to add or amend parking requirements for
              several land uses. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545 or
              steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov).

Discussion:   Steve Pastorik introduced the next agenda item, an ordinance change proposed by
              the staff related to the City's parking code. Before delving into the details, he
              provided some context about the history of parking requirements in the City.
              Steve highlighted that the City has had minimum parking requirements since its
              incorporation in 1980. Over the years, the code has undergone modifications, with
              significant adjustments made in 1988, 2002, and 2014. The aim has always been
              to balance ensuring convenience for residents and businesses and avoiding
              unnecessary, expensive parking space allocation.
              Steve noted that the most recent substantial changes occurred in 2014 when the
              City utilized the "Parking Generation" publication from the Institute of
              Transportation Engineers. This national publication provides data on parking
              demand for various land uses by counting cars and considering building sizes.
              Additionally, Steve mentioned the 6th edition of the publication released last year,
              incorporating new survey data and land uses.
              Steve explained using another resource called "Shared Parking," published in
              2020. This resource analyzes peak times for different land uses, emphasizing the
              potential for sharing parking spaces among different uses within the same
              development. By considering other uses' varying peak parking demands, the City
              aims to reduce the required parking stalls.
              Steve briefly outlined the proposed changes, emphasizing a reduction in parking
              requirements for several commercial land uses. However, he clarified that
              residential parking ratios remain unchanged, as both the Commission and the
              Council have, in some cases, increased parking for residential areas.
              Steve invited questions and discussions from the attendees, offering to provide a
              detailed overview of the proposed changes upon request.
              Steve continued to discuss specific adjustments in the parking ratios for various
              land uses. He presented a parking table with the proposed changes, emphasizing
              that some modifications were aimed at improving clarity and categorization.
              Steve reviewed the changes with the Commission.
              Steve introduced the replacement table for shared parking, breaking down peak
              times into morning, afternoon, and evening. This new table includes additional
              land uses to accurately reflect parking needs during different periods.
              Commissioner Woodruff mentioned that Salt Lake City has a minimum and
              maximum parking permitted. He asked if there were any reasons West Valley
              City would need a maximum amount of permitted parking. Steve said the City did
              have a maximum at one time, but it no longer exists. He mentioned that some
              entities have asked for more parking than that required.
              Chair Fuller read a study recently where a city cut parking, pushing people to use
              public transit. Steve said reducing parking is a good method if you want to make a
              more urban environment. However, we are trying to keep more of a suburban
              environment.

CA-1-2024 Petition by SUBURBAN PROPANE requesting a conditional use amendment
          for a remodel and expansion. The property is located at 3245 West 2100 South
          on 1.13 acres and is zoned M (Manufacturing). (Staff – Jody Knapp at 801-
          963-3497 or jody.knapp@wvc-ut.gov)
Discussion:   Jody Knapp said Suburban Propane approached us, and they want to build a new
              office complex, replace all their old equipment, and reconfigure the whole site so
              it flows a little bit better for them. She stated that this business has been in the
              same location since 1970, and they want to redo the property.
              Jody noted that the two smaller buildings with gray roofs will stay, and then they
              will demolish this larger building. The new office building would be moved onto
              the south end of the site so it would flow better for their business.
              Jody said the concept plan shows a storage area in front of the building for their
              vehicles as well as propane tanks, and the whole site will be asphalt. She pointed
              out that they would need to meet all of our setback and high-image arterial
              standards for the landscaping. She noted that the requirement for landscaping in
              the M zone is five percent, and they propose around fifteen percent.
              Jody presented the proposed structure and noted that it's a two-story building, and
              it is primarily made up of office and conference rooms. It's about a 6000-square-
              foot building, including a 1200-square-foot second level. The north side would be
              the primary elevation, and then this would be the side you'd see from 3270, so
              they may have to add some additional relief, but you do meet the design criteria.
              She mentioned that they would be required to add a masonry wall. Jody said she
              would prefer them to wrap the wall around the sides, but it would not need to
              surround the property.
              Commissioner McEwen pointed out the unmaintained park strip depicted in an
              image Jody had presented and said he is trying to understand why businesses have
              stopped taking care of their property. Jody said this property would most likely
              have been an enforcement case to clean up their property, but thankfully, they are
              proposing to do that now.
              Jody pointed out the pole sign and said it is a freeway sign, an exception so that it
              can remain on the property.
              Commissioner McEwen asked if they are allowed to have bars on windows. Jody
              stated that there isn't anything prohibiting bars on windows; however, the building
              with bared windows will be demolished.
              Jody noted that the remaining structures would be cleaned, painted, and
              maintained.
