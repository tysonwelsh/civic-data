       G.      Public Hearing: Fratto Farms Zoning Map Amendment and Land Use Map
               Amendment Request.
               (Legislative Item)
               On the Request of Troy Dana of Westland Development, Representing Fratto
               Farms, LLC., a Land Use Map Amendment from the Residential Medium Density
               to Residential High-Density Land Use Designation, and a Zoning Map Amendment
               from the RA2 (Residential Agricultural, 20,000 ft² minimum lot size) to RM2
               (Multiple-Family Residential, 12 dwelling units per acre maximum) Zone for
               approximately 2.03 acres, located at approximately 791 East 12200 South. Known
               as Applications 2025-0013-MA and 2025-0014-MA. Staff Contact: Nick
               Whittaker, (801) 576-6522, Nick.Whittaker@draperutah.gov.

Mr. Whittaker presented the Staff Report and explained that the application is for a Zoning Map
Amendment and Land Use Map Amendment for property located at 791 East 12200 South. The
Vicinity, Aerial, Land Use, and Zoning Maps were displayed. The Land Use Map Amendment is
proposed to change the current land use designation from Residential Medium Density to
Residential High Density. He shared an overview of the different land uses. The current land use
accommodates larger lots with single-family homes while the proposed land use favors more dense
development.

The Zoning Map Amendment is proposed to change the current zoning designation from RA2
(Residential Agricultural) to RM2 (Multiple-Family Residential). According to the Draper City
Municipal Code 9-8-020(B)(1), the purpose of the RA2 Zone is to "foster low-density development
with little impact on its surroundings and municipal services; to generally preserve the character
of the City's semi-rural areas; and to promote and preserve conditions favorable to large-lot family
life, including the keeping of limited numbers of animals and fowl. The predominant use in these
zones is intended to be detached single-family dwellings, protected from encroachment by
commercial and industrial uses.” On the other hand, the proposed zone is RM2. Mr. Whittaker
explained that according to the Draper City Municipal Code 9-8-020(B)(5), the purpose of the
RM2 Zone is to "permit well-designed apartments, townhomes, twin homes, and condominiums
at relatively high densities that are appropriately buffered from and compatible with surrounding
land uses.”

The applicant, Troy Dana, introduced himself to the Commission and explained that he has been
a resident of Draper for 25 years. He is a developer and has developed projects in Draper in the
past. This is a piece of property that is owned and it is in an area that is starting to change. The
Land Use Map and Zone that the property is in currently is inconsistent. This property is adjacent
to some commercial uses and is 300 to 400 feet away from the townhomes to the north. Less than
a quarter mile to the east, there are some other townhomes, and there is the commercial corridor
at 700 East.

Mr. Dana has seen a lot of change occur in Draper over the years. He wants to be respectful to the
adjacent and surrounding property owners, which is the reason he has already spoken to some of
the residents about this property. When he originally met with Staff, the proposal looked a little
bit different with some office and warehouse mixed in with the residential. That has since changed,
as shown in the Concept Plan. The family that this property was purchased from has owned it for
a long time. As a developer, he respects that and wants to name it after them. Mr. Dana explained
that he is not asking for the highest mix of residential units, but wants there to be something that
will enhance the area. He reminded Commissioners that this property is across the street from
commercial and adjacent to some commercial. This development will enhance the area and result
in improvements. Mr. Dana reiterated that he has already spoken to some of the neighbors about
what is proposed. The intention is to focus on the best use of the property and consider what will
work in the area.

Commissioner Fowler asked if the applicant had considered something less drastic in terms of the
zone. Mr. Dana reported that the R4 Zone has one-quarter acre lots but they are unlikely to work
based on the land values. He pointed out that sometimes there is pushback against affordable
housing because of the density. There are a lot of different options for this site. He shared
information about the townhome project that is down the road and the backyard space available
there. It is not his intention to cram a lot of density into each development he does but to think
about what makes sense.

Planning Manager, Todd Draper, presented the Staff Report and explained that the request is for a
Conditional Use Permit for approximately 5.77 acres, located on the east side of Vista Boulevard
at approximately 12383 South Vista Station Boulevard. The property is currently zoned OS (Open
Space) and RA1 (Residential Agricultural). The applicant is requesting a Conditional Use Permit
to allow for construction of a new wireless telecommunications facility. The Vicinity, Aerial,
Land Use, and Zoning Maps were displayed. Mr. Draper explained that this item was last seen by

previously proposed.

Mr. Draper reported that the proposal is for a new monopole on City property. The Site Plan was
reviewed and he pointed out where this would come in and where the existing drive is located. It
would be used for most of the utility connections with the exception of power, as there is a separate
easement as part of the agreement with the City. Mr. Draper explained that this is a standard

applicant indicated that landscaping did not make a lot of sense given the location and surrounding

conditions photos were reviewed. Commissioner Fowler asked how many monopoles there are in
the City and how many are needed. Mr. Draper explained that the applicant can provide
information about the number of monopoles AT&T has. Commissioner Nixon asked why this
was not constructed two years ago. Mr. Draper stated that the applicant can likely address that
question. Commissioner Nixon understands monopoles can reach 100 feet and the City can allow
up to 22 feet for antenna widths, but asked if this will still work with a 15-foot width rather than a
22-foot width.

representing AT&T. The main reason this was not built two years ago was that AT&T started an
antenna swap project around that time. It takes a long time to make those changes. Multiple
towers were put on hold to prevent building towers with equipment that would need to be swapped
out in the next 18 months. There was no desire to build a new site with old equipment. As far as

       I.      Public Hearing: City-Initiated Tree Maintenance Ordinance Update Request.
               (Legislative Item)
               On the Request of Draper City, Text Amendments to Portions of Titles 7 and 9 of
               the Draper City Municipal Code (DCMC) in order to Modify the Standards Related
               to the Maintenance of Trees on Private Property and within Park Strips. Known as
               Application 2025-0054-TA. Staff Contact: Todd Taylor, 801-576-6510,
               todd.taylor@draperutah.gov.

Planner 3, Todd Taylor, presented the Staff Report and explained that the application is a request
for a Text Amendment to portions of Title 7 and Title 9 of the Draper City Municipal Code. The
purpose is to modify the standards related to the maintenance of trees on private property and
within park strips. City Staff was made aware of two different tree maintenance-related items
around the same time. The code amendments are intended to rectify those issues. The first came
from Code Enforcement. A standard is needed that can be cited for the removal of dead, diseased,
or other hazardous trees. The proposal for Title 7 is to add some text that Code Enforcement can
cite when those conditions occur. This would allow for the removal of those trees. The second
issue has to do with trees obstructing visibility from the public right-of-way. The proposal is to
add text within the Fences and Visual Obstructions section in Title 9 to allow the City Engineer to
deem trees to be unsafe as far as visibility. Those trees can then be removed by the property owner

affordable housing, one with single-family housing and one with multi-family housing. The City
must approve qualifying home ownership for single-family or multi-family density housing within
the density parameters set by the statute. This can be done through zoning or a Development
Agreement. From there, the City can:

   •   Require 60% of the units be deed restricted to owner occupancy;

   •   Restrict the size of the units (1,600 square feet for both single-family and multi-family).

Ms. Jastremsky spoke to the City Council about this to receive some feedback. Work is being
done on a rewrite for the zoning code, so the plan is to have this built into the new code. It will
either be an overlay zone or a new zoning designation. There is still some uncertainty about how
it will be put in, but the plan is to do so. Chair Adams likes the fact that there will be deed
restriction. It was clarified that the deed restriction is limited to 60%. There are also limits to how
long it can be deed-restricted. Chair Adams asked if the deed restriction is only for the first
purchase or first owner. Ms. Jastremsky believes it is for five or six years, so it is not long-term,
but it is a start. Commissioner Nixon asked about the 1,600-square-foot restriction. She wanted
to know if that includes garage space. It was noted that it was not well defined, but the code does
not consider a garage as part of the square footage. As a result, the assumption would be that in
Draper, the 1,600-square-foot restriction does not include garages. The assumption currently is
that it is 1,600 square feet of floor space.

HB-422 was discussed, which states that open houses cannot be regulated differently than a
residential use. There was some uncertainty about where this bill came from because an open
house is normally a permitted use in a residential zone. While there is an understanding that certain
signs might need to be regulated to ensure that a sign is not placed on private property or in an area
that obstructs sidewalks, what is proposed in HB-422 does not appear to address a need in the City.

Senate Bill (“SB”) 23 was reviewed. The City can create “first home investment zones” with a
minimum of 30 housing units per acre. 50% must be owner-occupied. It was clarified that while
this can be done, but it does not necessarily need to be done. However, there is some incentive to
do so. If the intention is to have a “first home investment zone,” then it might make more sense
to have a higher owner-occupied number. With the 50% requirement, the zone would likely have
half of the owners able to purchase their first home. The other 50% could be rented out. Chair
Adams asked if the City could take a lead on that and require a higher percentage. There was

S.B. 179 relates to new and unlisted business uses. The City needs to adopt a process for reviewing
and approving a new or unlisted business use and designate an appropriate zone for a use. For
example, if there is a business that wants to come into Draper and that business is not included in
the table, then there needs to be work done to find a zone that makes sense for that use. The City
also needs to amend the code to create a list of prohibited uses. It is necessary to amend the code
so there is a clear procedure in place, which is something the Commission could be involved in.
Ms. Jastremsky explained that currently, something that is not listed is not allowed. The City will
now need to have a list of prohibited uses. In the future, if a use is not listed and it is not on the
prohibited list, then it is allowed and the City will need to find a way to make it fit in somewhere.

S.B. 141 was discussed next. According to this bill, there are certain things a city cannot do:

     •   Require closed or covered parking to be more than 10 feet wide or 20 feet long;
     •   Require uncovered parking to be more than 9-feet wide or 20-feet long;
     •   Restrict an unobstructed tandem parking space from satisfying two parking spaces as a
         minimum parking space requirement;
     •   Require a garage for single-family attached or detached dwelling that is owner-occupied
         affordable housing.

If a garage is required, each parking space within the garage counts as part of the minimum parking
space requirement. Discussions were had about tandem parking. Ms. Jastremsky explained that
some zoning codes state that if there are two tandem parking spaces, it will count toward one
parking space. This is because there needs to be space to switch the cars around and move them.
It is restrictive in use, so some codes state that it only counts towards one. However, this bill states
that unobstructed tandem spaces count as two spaces. S.B. 340 was reviewed, which is for
protected persons. Escape tunnels are permitted for protected persons. The only review authority
the City has is a building inspection. Commissioner Nixon asked if there is a list of protected
persons, which was confirmed. There is a specific department that is tasked with determining
whether someone is a protected person. However, it is not believed that kind of information is
available to the public. “Protected persons” is defined in the statute as an individual who is
certified to be at risk of harm.
