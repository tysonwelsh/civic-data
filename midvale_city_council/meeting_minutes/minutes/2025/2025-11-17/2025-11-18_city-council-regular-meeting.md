           LOCATED AT 362 WEST CENTER STREET TO SALT LAKE COUNTY, AND
           AUTHORIZING THE MAYOR TO EXECUTE THE AGREEMENT FOR
           PURCHASE AND SALE OF REAL PROPERTY, MIDVALE PARK
           OPERATIONS AND MAINTENANCE INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT, AND
           RIGHT OF FIRST REFUSAL.
Matt Dahl said on October 28, 2025, the Salt Lake County Council authorized the
Administration to submit an offer to Midvale City for the purchase of approximately 2.561
acres of land at 362 West Center Street (Property), which is located at the southwest
corner of Midvale City Park. The County intends to use this property to construct a new
Salt Lake County library branch, replacing the aging Ruth Vine Tyler Library (Tyler
Library). The County has previously determined that the Tyler Library is no longer viable
to maintain and necessitates replacement. Seeing the need to ensure the success of the
new branch, Salt Lake County and Midvale City staff have worked to prepare a proposal
that would be acceptable to both the Salt Lake County Council and the Midvale City
Council to bring the new branch to the Midvale City Park.

The offer before the City Council consists of three documents:

Agreement for Purchase and Sale of Real Property:

Property: 2.561 acres at 362 West Center Street

November 18, 2025


Purchase Price: $1,215,786

Termination of Existing Lease: Midvale City is to ensure that the property is not
encumbered by existing leases. This term affects one existing lease, and the parties are
prepared to amend the document if the sale of the Property is approved.

Closing Date: December 19, 2025

Conveyance: The Property will be transferred by Special Warranty Deed

City Retained Assets: Midvale City will retain ownership of the playground equipment
and the electronic sign located on the Property

Midvale Park Operations and Maintenance Interlocal Agreement:

Public Access: The Property will remain open to the public for the duration of the
agreement.

Operations & Maintenance: Midvale City will continue to operate and maintain the
Property until Library construction begins or the agreement is terminated.

City Responsibilities at Termination: Prior to the agreement’s termination, the City
will remove and relocate the playground equipment, electronic sign, utilities, and
irrigation infrastructure as needed.

Insurance: The City will maintain appropriate insurance coverage for all activities
related to the operation and maintenance of the Property.

Design Input: The County will allow a designated City representative to provide input
on matters related to the design of the library property.

Right of First Refusal:

Right Granted: Salt Lake County will grant Midvale City the first option to purchase the
Property should the County decide to sell it in the future.

Purchase Terms: If exercised, the City would purchase the Property for $1 above any
bona fide third-party offer.

Timeline: The City has 30 days to exercise its right after receiving notice of the County’s
Intent to sell the Property. The County then has 15 days to provide a title abstract. The
City has 15 days to review the title, and closing will occur within 15 days after the review
period.

Closing Costs: All closing costs will be paid by Midvale City.

The County anticipates taking possession of the Property by the end of 2025. Throughout
2026, the County will focus on selecting an architect, conducting public outreach, and
completing the library design. During this period, Midvale City will continue to operate the
Property as part of Midvale City Park, maintaining full public access. The County will
involve Midvale City in the design process to ensure compatibility with the surrounding

The Midvale City Council is being asked to suspend the rules to accommodate the

Adam Olsen said Midvale City was awarded financial assistance through a Transportation
and Land Use Connection (TLC) grant for a Parks, Trails, and Open Space Master Plan.
This plan is the first of its kind for Midvale, providing comprehensive analysis and
recommendations for the City’s parks and open space network, including preparation of
an Impact Fee Facilities Plan (IFFP), a required step in the preparation and

implementation of a Parks Impact Fee Analysis for the collection of development impact
fees for the expansion of the City’s parks and open space network.

Throughout 2025, staff worked with MHTN and Zions Public Finance (consultant groups)

       C. RECEIVE PUBLIC COMMENT REGARDING AMENDMENTS TO SECTION
           17-7-10.12.5 OF THE MIDVALE CITY MUNICIPAL CODE ENTITLED
           DEVELOPMENT REQUIREMENTS OF THE JORDAN BLUFFS SUBAREAS
           1-3 ZONE RELATING TO PUBLIC ROAD STANDARDS.
Wendelin Knobloch said this code text amendment was triggered by an upgrade to the
road cross section for the Tertiary Road that traverses the Large-Scale Master Plan for
the View 78 Apartments Phases 1-2 (see Figure 1 Public Road Classification) for which
the Planning Commission recommended approval on February 26, 2025 pending this
amendment to the development code text, a Master Development Agreement for Jordan
Bluffs, and the project specific development agreement. The Master Development
Agreement was amended in June 2025, and the project specific development agreement
is placed on the November 18, 2025, City Council Regular Session agenda together with
the Large- Scale Master Plan and this code text amendment.

The developer proposes an 8-foot park strip or parallel parking, depending on the location
along the Tertiary Road (see Figure 2 below and Large-Scale Master Plan). This is an
upgrade to the road cross section that exceeds the minimum requirements in the
ordinance which are a 5-foot parks trip without any requirements for providing parallel
parking (see Figure 3).

Even though the city may be in favor of this upgrade the code language does not allow
this flexibility without minor modifications. To clarify baseline requirements and ensure
consistency, the term “at minimum” has been inserted where appropriate throughout the
Development Requirements section of the Jordan Bluffs 1-3 zone (MMC 17-7-10.12.5.).

Public notice has been sent to affected entities as required in 17-3-9. B of the Municipal
Code. No comments have been received by staff or the Planning Commission as of this
writing. The Planning Commission unanimously recommended approval of this code
text amendment.

Zoning Code Text Amendment Criteria

Midvale City Code 17-3-1(F) outlines the criteria necessary for amendments to the
zoning code. A proposal may only be approved if it demonstrates one or more of the
following:

   1. The proposed amendment promotes the objectives of the general plan and
      purposes of this title;
   2. The proposed amendment promotes the purposes outlined in Utah State Code
                                            29
Proceedings of City Council Meeting
November 18, 2025


      10-9a-102;
   3. The proposed amendment more clearly explains the intent of the original
      language or has been amended to make interpretation more straightforward; or
   4. Existing zoning code was the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact.

Staff finds that this proposal meets the third criterion listed above because it is the city’s
intent to provide optimal solutions for the transportation needs of its citizens and flexibility
in the code language encourages innovation in the private sector.

       H. CONSIDERATION OF MIDVALE CITY PLANNING WITH ORGANIZATIONS
          TO COMMEMORATE CULTURAL CELEBRATIONS ON MIDVALE CITY
          PROPERTY.
Garrett Wilcox said Midvale is and has historically been a diverse community. Some of
the community’s earliest homesteaders were Mormon pioneers from Norway, Sweden,
Denmark, England, Scotland, and the Northeastern United States. As Midvale’s industry
grew with the construction of the railroad, mills, and smelters, Midvale attracted a diverse
workforce of immigrants from Southern and Eastern Europe. Today, Midvale’s population
is 66% White, 18% Hispanic, 7% multiracial, 4% Asian, 3% Islander, 3% Black, and 1%
Native. Midvale’s Hispanic, Asian, Islander, and Black populations are higher as
percentages than Utah’s average.

                                            62
Proceedings of City Council Meeting
November 18, 2025


With its racial diversity, Midvale is and has historically been a religiously diverse
community. Over the years, Midvale has been home to congregations from Buddhist,
Baptist, Catholic, non-denominational Christian, The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day
Saints, Evangelical, Episcopal, Methodist, Serbian-Greek Orthodox, and United Church
of Christ faiths.

Midvale has recently been approached about an opportunity to partner with an
organization to have a menorah lighting celebration at City Hall next month. Currently, the
City does not have a formal policy about partnering with organizations to commemorate
cultural celebrations on Midvale City property. In the past, Midvale City coordinated with
a group to host an annual Cinco de Mayo celebration at Midvale City Park. Due to some
changes within the organization, the Cinco de Mayo celebration has not been held for
several years.

City staff would like to discuss with the City Council if the City Council has an interest in
exploring partnerships with organizations in commemorating cultural celebrations on
Midvale City property. If the City Council is interested in exploring these partnerships, City
staff is seeking Council direction on how the Council would like staff to engage in these
potential partnerships. These conversations with organizations are often fluid and ideas
rapidly change so that it makes it unfeasible to have the organizations directly negotiate
with the City Council at Council meetings. Staff is requesting that the City Council
establish some basic guidance on the direction of staff’s negotiations until a more
thorough policy regarding City partnerships can be adopted.

Until a formal policy is adopted, City staff recommend that the City Council consider the
implications of the First Amendment, the requirements for municipalities providing
nonmonetary support to nonprofit organizations under Utah Code Ann. § 10-8-2, and
budget amendment procedures under Utah Code Ann. § 10-6-128. These legal
considerations may inform some of the parameters that the City Council gives to staff with
its conversations with organizations.

The City Council may consider directing City staff that they may only partner with
organizations to commemorate cultural celebrations if:

   1. The organization has a demonstratable interest in or connection to the community
      and heritage of Midvale City including its people, businesses, and institutions. The
      organization is not divisive, contentious, or engaged in partisan advocacy or illegal
      activities, in the City’s sole determination.

   2. Cultural celebrations must meet the purposes identified in the Midvale City Harvest
      Days Parade Policies, established by the City Council in Resolution No. 2019-R-
      23. Cultural celebrations should:
             a.     Be safe and family-appropriate;
             b.     Build and strengthen the community of Midvale City;
             c.     Celebrate the community and heritage of Midvale City including its
                    people, businesses, and institutions;
                                             63
Proceedings of City Council Meeting
November 18, 2025


               d.    Celebrate the events, people, and cultural institutions of historic
                     importance to the greater Salt Lake County, the State of Utah, and
                     the Intermountain West;
             e.      Provide entertainment through music, performance, and special or
                     unique attractions; and
             f.      Not be inappropriate for the intended audience, divisive, degrading,
                     or obscene.
   3. If the cultural celebration is proposed to be held at a City property that is not
      otherwise open to the public, the City must have final say in the planning,
      scheduling, marketing, and other significant decisions regarding the cultural
      celebration. The potential partnering organization may participate in the planning,
      scheduling, and marketing of the cultural celebration, but the City has sole
      discretion and authority to make final decisions for the cultural celebration.

   4. If the cultural celebration is proposed to be held at a City property that is not
      otherwise open to the public, the City may invite, at its sole discretion, other
      organizations to participate in the cultural celebration. The City may revoke its
      invitation to participate at its sole discretion.

   5. The organization is willing to provide volunteers, equipment, and supplies to carry
      out the cultural celebration. The City may supplement the organization’s
      contribution with its own employees and volunteers, equipment, and supplies. All
      volunteers must pass a Midvale City volunteer background check.

   6. The City is not required to make a budget amendment to carry out the cultural
      celebration.

   7. If City staff are not clearly convinced that the above criteria are met, City staff will

   A. DISCUSS PROPOSED AMENDMENT TO MIDVALE CITY RETIREMENT
       BENEFITS.
Rori Andreason said Midvale City currently contracts with Group Benefit Services (GBS)
as the City’s insurance broker. As part of their comprehensive services, GBS offers
Retirement Plan Services and has proposed enhancements to the City’s retirement
program that would improve employee benefits, reduce fees, and strengthen fiduciary
oversight.

Staff recommends consideration of amending the City’s retirement benefit program to
transition plan administration from MissionSquare to GBS Retire, a division of GBS, to
achieve these improvements.

Key Points:
  1. Lower Fees, Enhanced Services
     Transitioning to GBS Retire will lower employee investment management fees
     from 1.50% to 1.00%, providing direct cost savings to participants. GBS Retire also
     offers enhanced service quality and plan management support for both employees
     and City staff, ensuring a smoother and more responsive participant experience.

      2. Fiduciary Oversight

       LOTS.
Daniel Van Beuge said Midvale has been dealing with issues related to commercial
vehicles parking on public streets and in public parking lots. In several instances, public
streets and public parking lots are being utilized as commercial vehicle storage.

Large commercial vehicles such as semi-trucks and trailers parked on public streets has
been an ongoing problem. These large vehicles block drivers’ visibility around them,
creating safety concerns for traffic. They also occupy significant sections of available
public parking for Midvale residents. The City Council previously adopted an ordinance
prohibiting these vehicles from parking overnight on public streets. This has helped
reduce the number of large commercial vehicles being parked on public streets.
Tragically, in March of this year, one of the members of the City’s Youth Council died after
colliding with a parked semi-truck during the day. After a number of discussions, the City
Council instructed staff to draft an ordinance prohibiting semi-trucks from parking on
public streets at any time with several small exceptions.

Since then, several other parking issues involving commercial vehicles have come to City
staff’s attention. First, owners of a fleet of vehicles being rented via the Turo app are using
public street parking to store their vehicles between rentals. As many as twelve vehicles
listed on Turo have been parked on a single section of a public street. This has frustrated
