1.3 Consideration of CU-18-013, Conditional Use Permit for an Electronic Message
Center on a Pole Sign Location: 3675 S. 900 E. Applicant: Granite Federal Credit
Union Community: Millcreek Planner: Erin O’Kelley
Erin O’ Kelley said code states that any ground electronic message sign in the C-2 Zone
within 300 feet of a residence requires a conditional use permit. There were 12 parcels
within the 300 feet; 3 zoned residential and 2 zoned commercial with residential on them.
The applicant is replacing the existing sign. There are regulations in code regarding light
control; it requires a sensor to reduce ambient lighting. The sign is 48 square feet and 17
feet in height. She said the Planning Commission may make alterations to the placement
of the sign based on the light it puts off. The Millcreek Community Council unanimously
approved the conditional use permit.

Chair Stephens asked if the code offered hours of operation. O’ Kelley said it does not,
but the applicant cannot increase the ambient lighting than what was there before.
Commissioner Mumford asked if the message could have motion. O’Kelley said code
requires a static image.

Eric Shinsato, Allied Electric Sign Company, pointed out that the electronic message sign
is rather small, so the impact will not be that great; it is 22 square feet. He said they can
program the sign to turn down the brightness during certain hours. Commissioner LaMar
asked if it based on the light outside. The applicant said he could program it based on
time. Commissioner Healey clarified that the message center is sequential with
messaging. Shinsato said yes, but it is not continuous. Chair Stephens asked Shinsato if
he recommended a dwell time for the image. Shinsato said it is generally between 5-8
seconds, but the customer determines that.

Chair Stephens asked if the software of the sign could be in conformance with ordinance.
O’ Kelley said the code says the sign should be programed to control ambient lighting,
which would need to be measured. Lilly said the code sets a performance standard. He
read from code section 19.82.135, “All electronic message centers shall be equipped with
a sensor or other device that automatically determines the ambient illumination and must
be programmed to automatically dim according to ambient light conditions.” He said the
other device mentioned in code could be the software the applicant referenced. He said he
could work it out through the technical review to have a solution that satisfies the intent
of the ordinance.

Chair Stephens opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. Chair
Stephens closed the public hearing.

Commissioner LaMar said it seems to be appropriate for the Commission to limit the
time on the electronic message portion. O’Kelley said the code says that hours of
operation could be imposed in the conditional use permit. Lilly said he would offer the
Commission offer the reasons why this condition may be imposed should they choose to
do so. Commissioner LaMar said the Commission should be consistent with other sign
lighting in the City. Lilly said this sign is near residential, so that could be a reason why
hours of operation may be imposed.

Commission needs to add additional requirements because it does not seem that this
project has any adverse impacts. Commissioner Allen said he does not like electronic

to say that during certain hours the message did not change. Commissioner Wilson asked

1.6 Consideration of SD-18-008, Approval of an Exemption from Plat Requirement
Pursuant to section 10-9a-605 of the Utah State Code, to Accommodate the Vacation
and Dedication of a Right-of-Way Location: 1612 E. 3300S. Applicant: Azner
Property Management Planner: Francis Lilly
Lilly said this is a new type of approval that the Commission will see more of. He said he
and the City Attorney reviewed state law and the Land Use Authority must approve any
subdivision action. He said in many cases, the dedication of right-of-way that is required
by new development can be a subdivision action. The Land Use Authority must approve
that dedication with a written certification. In the future for projects that do not require a
plat, staff will include a statement that they would be approving an exemption of plat
requirements pursuant to code to facilitate required right-of-way dedication. On May 16,
2018 the Planning Commission approved a conditional use permit for al 0-unit townhome
project at 1612 E. 3300 S. for Robert Renzo. During technical review, staff discovered a
portion of dedication was inaccurately dedicated to Salt Lake County in 1971. The
dedication was inside this property, so the way to get rid of it is through a formal right-of-
way vacation process. He said changing the dedication requires an adjustment to the
subdivided parcel. The Planning Commission must approve the substantive change to the
subdivision. Lilly read Utah Code Ann. 10-9a-605(1). Lilly went through the certification
requirements; public notice, streets, water and sewer approvals, zoning, and conformance
to land use ordinance. He recommended the Planning Commission approve the
exemption from plat requirements to accommodate the vacation and dedication of right-
of-way for property at 1612 E. 3300 S.
Commissioner Mumford asked if the City can supersede state code. Brems said state code
allows the Planning Commission to do this. Chair Stephens asked if this matter required
