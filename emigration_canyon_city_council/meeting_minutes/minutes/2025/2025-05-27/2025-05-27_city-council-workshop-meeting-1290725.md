2. COUNCIL DISCUSSION TOPICS
A. Discussion on MSD Services and Budget Processes
Marla Howard presented a detailed financial overview comparing Emigration Canyon's
finances to those of other municipalities in the Municipal Services Dishict (MSD). She reviewed
financial figures from January to June 2024, highlighting that the figures did not fully represent
the year's total expenditures since most road maintenance occurs from June through October.
For the first half oI 2024, Emigration Canyon generated approximately $325,000 in operating
income from sales tax, road funds, grants, and interest, along with an additional $326,000 from
bond-funded capital projects, totaling around $65'l ,000 in revenue. She explained that a
significant portion of Emigration Canyon's expenses stemmed from services provided by both
county entities and third parties, including administrative overhead, animal services, indigent
legal services, justice court, code enforcement, engineering, and Public Works operations. She

established to allow unincorporated areas to financially support smaller municipalities like
Emigration Canyon, and changing this arrangement would require legislative action. A
historical financial review showed that in 2023, Emigration Canyon's total income was nearly
$1.2 million with expenses around $1.1 million, resulting in a smaller deficit of about $700,000.
ln 2022, the Canyon incurred higher expenses due to extensive public works, including an
overlay project, resulting in $1.4 million in expenditures.
Council Member David Brems inquired about the breakdown and justification for various
expenses, particularly for Public Works operations.
Marla Howard explained the billing mechanisms and noted the complexity of accessing
detailed backup data.

Council Member Robert Pinon highlighted that Emigration Canyon serves not only residents
but also a large volume of external traffic, contributing to infrastructure wear and tear, which
Howard agreed was a valid justification for financial support.
Marla Howard also noted that Emigration Canyon is not currently utilizing the Municipal Energy
Tax (MET), which other municipalities have implemented. There was a discussion about the
differences between the MET and franchise taxes and how they are applied and collected.
Cameron Platt clarified the distinctions and processes for implementing each tax. The council
discussed the potential to implement these taxes to reduce reliance on MSD subsidies and
build a local fund for future projects.
Council members emphasized the need to strategize around tax implementation and possible
legislative outreach. Marla Howard suggested seeking funds through tourism and state
legislation, noting that other municipalities have successfully done so. Council members
requested a memo outlining steps to pursue such funding, and Marla Howard indicated she
had already communicated some of this to legal counsel. Toward the end of the meeting,
discussions turned to fund balances, with uncertainty about how current balances were
reflected in budget documents.
