      1. 1. A discussion regarding short-term rental properties in Provo. (23-043) (00:12:09)

            Melia Dayley, Policy Analyst, presenting. Ms. Dayley covered different types of rentals
            and what other cities are doing as well as some of the things Provo would need to
            consider should an ordinance be passed to allow these. Chair MacKay clarified that per
            the code right now individuals would only need to get a rental license. She also asked
            whether the short-term rental groups (like Air BnB) were collecting any tax for Provo
            and if they would be classified as a hospitality accommodation and capture additional
            sales tax revenue. Councilors also discussed if staffing and were told that it would
            probably require hiring more staffing. Vice Chair Rachel Whipple speculated on
            whether it would be better to have a business license for an Air BNB versus a rental
            dwelling license so they can treat them like hotels. Scott Johnson, Zoning Administrator
            responded that he thought it could be done. He pointed out that as he’s looked at other
            cities, and when it comes to those types of permits/licenses. Cities typically charge
            higher for licenses or short-term rental permits. Currently, RDL permits in the city are
            $30 to $60, other jurisdictions are much higher.



A motion was made by Chair Katrice MacKay and seconded by Councilor George Handley
proposing that Council Staff explore and bring back recommendations on the following: (1) a
short term rental (STR) license, (2) requiring STR companies to verify the existence of an STR
license in order to process credit card payments, and (3) allowing short term rentals of individual
rooms (rather than the entire home) for owner occupied properties.

   Motion passed 5-0, with Councilors Hoban and Fillmore excused. Following the vote, Chair
   MacKay asked that Council Staff report back within two months.

      2. A discussion regarding tenant/landlord governance and rental dwelling policies and
         regulations. (01:13:49)

         Michael Sanders, Policy Analyst, presenting. Mr. Sanders discussed how several items
         around this same topic have been brought up so the staff has chosen to group them
         together for this presentation. The initial request was how can the city enforce on
         landlords for health and safety violations, inform renters of their rights, and provide a
         landlord reporting option for tenants. He then covered some of the state and county
         preemptions that exist, many falling under the Utah Fit Premises Act. He then covered
         what Provo has tried to do in this area as well as Residential Dwelling License (RDLs)
         and Good Landlord Programs. Councilor George Handley asked about whether the Good
         Landlord Programs would only benefit the large, commercially ran complexes and how it
         might be a benefit to the renter to know who participates in that type of program.
         Discussion was had regarding what studies might be involved and what those studies
         should cover to start a program like this. Brian Jones, Provo City Attorney, stated that the
         last study conducted showed that the costs of services provided to rentals versus non-
         rentals was negligible, so a Good Landlord Program was not cost effective when
         discussed in the past. Chair Katrice MacKay wondered why large apartment complexes
         are being subsidized by only charging $60 for a whole building versus $20 for a single
         unit. Vice-Chair Rachel Whipple wondered if more inspections would need to be done
         for a multi-unit building.

A motion was made by Chair Katrice MacKay and seconded by George Handley requesting
administrative staff to review the pricing structure of Rental Dwelling License (RDL) permits
for single and multiple units and ensure it represents the true cost of business and make a
recommendation as to if the City should be charging for RDL permits per unit, per building,
or maintain the status quo.
Motion passed 6-0, with Councilor Fillmore excused.


 3.      A discussion on tiered water rates (23-055) (01:46:56)


Michael Sanders, Policy Analyst, presenting. Mr. Sanders stated that this presentation was to
follow-up on the same topic from the August 1st meeting where the council directed staff to
return with recommendations on expanding tiers of the current water rates. Public Works is
going to engage Bowen, Collins and Associates to do an updated study as the last one was
done in 2020. They were given five goals to guide their recommendations. Councilor George
Handley had a question regarding the representation of a “church” in the 2021 study and
whether that usage was a single building or a group. He also asked about large water users
like BYU and how a tiered water rate system would affect them. Gary Calder explained that
BYU has their own well and while they do get some water from Provo City. Mr. Calder also
noted that BYU has gone to great lengths to use water mindfully on their properties and
buildings and because of this, it’s not part of the data shown to the council.

A motion was made by Councilor George Handley and seconded by Councilor Dave Shipley to
specifically include industrial, commercial, and non-profit businesses in the consideration of
tiered water rates.

         Motion passed 6-0, with Councilor Fillmore excused.

A motion was made by Councilor Shannon Ellsworth and seconded by George Handley to
accept the recommendations as written by Staff, with the addition of the first motion.

         Motion passed 5-1, with Councilor Mackay voting no and Councilor Fillmore
         excused.

7.   A presentation on the 5-year public safety staffing plan (23-064) (04:12:22)

     Troy Beebe, Police Chief and Jeremy Headman, Fire Chief, Presenting. Chief Beebe
     explained that he had previously brought to the council a study regarding reactive and
     proactive policing and was grateful for the additional funding for three officers he
     received in the FY24 budget. Chief Beebe’s goal is that as revenues allow, seven more
     officers be added over the next four years. This allows him to manage the department in a
     productive way and keep the citizens safe. Councilor Ellsworth asked where his current
     staffing levels were at, and he responded that the department currently has116 full-time
     employee positions filled and funding for 122. Councilor Bill Fillmore asked if current
     staffing levels meet the needs of the airport and the Chief responded that it would.

     Chief Headman than presented his plans for the Fire Department. The top need is an
     engine at Station 1. The area around Station 21 is the busiest, where many calls are
     dispatched too. He is asking for an additional 9 positions over four years. Each platoon is
     made up of three firefighters, a captain, and an engineer and they rotate three shifts Chief
     Headman let the Council know that he has applied for a Safer Grant and feels good about
     the chances of being awarded the grant from FEMA. The grant would fund the nine
     positions for the next three years. He’s hoping the administration will budget for an
     additional three positions for the next three years, so after three years Fire payroll will
     have the funds for those nine positions and transition from the grant money to city funds
     without any problems. Fire engine delivery is two to three years out, which is a significant
     delay, but he wants to make sure funding is available for personnel before funds are spent
     on an engine. Chair Katrice MacKay asked if Chief Headman knew when he receive an
     answer from FEMA on the grant. Chief Headman responded that he had hoped to know in
     a month or two. Vice Chair Whipple asked if Station 4 still needed a new engine and the
     Chief confirmed it does and has been included in the current budget. Councilor George
     Handley asked if to get the grant the Chief had to provide any information that the city
     would be able to take over the salaries at the end of the three years and the Chief
     responded that was not a condition of the grant, just the current department budget and the
     ability to demonstrate current need.

8.   A discussion regarding expanding the tax increment finance agreement with the Noorda
     College of Osteopathic Medicine. (23-045)

     Keith Morey, Assistant Director of Development Services, Presenting. Mr. Morey is
     following up on an assignment from the last work meeting where the board directed staff
     to look at and propose benchmarks that must be met or a portion of the TIF would have to
     be repaid. Mr. Morey presented an updated resolution that showed if the agency failed to
     achieve the first milestone (parking garage) then in such an event the available tax
     increment funds would be reduced 20% or $1.4 million dollars to $35.6 million in total. If
     the agency fails to achieve the second milestone (housing) then in such an event the
     available tax increment funds will be reduced by the remaining penalty amount from $37
     million to $30 million.

     A motion was made by Board Member MacKay and seconded by Board Member
     Whipple that this matter be brought back for a vote at the next meeting.

     Motion passed unanimously 7-0.
