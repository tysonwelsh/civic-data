2. Planning          Matter       First      Reading:          ZT-23-011m             Repeal       of the Foothills             and Canyons
Overlay        Zone        and the Geologic             Hazards            Ordinance,           replacing       both     with     a Sensitive            Lands
Ordinance;           Francis       Lilly,     Planning           Director
Mr. Lilly       said this ordinance             has been reviewed                 by the contract           geologist,     all the Community
Councils,        and the Planning             Commission.                Staff would       like to repeal       the Foothills            and Canyons
Overlay       Zone (FCOZ).            It has been observed                  that some issues have come up that should                                  have
been regulated             in FCOZ        but were not. It is more                 appropriate       to apply the standards                   more like
perfori'nance        criteria.     Mayor        Silvestrini        said that the old ordinance                  was adopted             by Salt Lake
County       in 1995. Mayor            Silvestrini        said he fully           supports       a new ordinance           to address issues that
are not addressed             by the old ordinance                and overlay         zone. In the Millcreek               Community                 Council
area sensitive           lands include         fault    lines,    fault     setbacks,      and floodplains             associated         with    Mill
Creek,       and moderate         or high liquefaction                   areas. East Mill         Creek      area has fault            line areas and
setbacks       near Highland          Drive,       floodplains            along    Mill    Creek     and some ravines                  and gullies.
Canyon        Rim is probably               least impacted          by sensitive          lands. There        are a few areas with                   steep
slopes.      Mount       Olympus          has significant          and well-known               sensitive     lands including              debris      flow
areas, alluvial          fans, an avalanche             path, and floodplains.


Goal 1 is to preserve             and enhance            the physical          elements         that define      each neighborhood's
character.       Staff wants to protect                viewsheds,          strengthen        resilience      to natural         disasters,       and
prioritize      development          of effective         neighborhood               scale plans.      Staff proposes            to replace           the
overlay      zone with        a sensitive        lands map. There                 are a significant         number       of lots in Millcreek
subject      to liquefaction.         An important               change      is adding       language       to allow      for a dispute              resolution
process       and appeal process.              The current          language         does not have that written                  in.


The current        approach        of enhanced           requirements             has caused confusion                 among      applicants           and
resulted      in certain      properties        being     ineligible         for enhanced          protections         because         they were not in
FCOZ.        The engineering              and processes           standards        have largely        remained          the same with            minor
updates       and clarifications.            The new chapter               covers     properties       citywide        that lie within           sensitive
lands as determined              by the city's          sensitive         lands map. A new table shows                     the various           elements
required       for a geotechnical             report.    A major           organization         restructuring       of chapters           was made to
provide       consistency        and clarity.          There      will    be a new dispute           resolution         process.        All    the
community          councils       recommended              favor         of the code. The Planning               Commission               held a public
hearing      on Febniary          28, 2024,        and added a few proposed                      changes to protect              existing        vegetation,
adding       vegetation       along waterways,              and adding            definitions       for alluvial        fans and riparian              area.

            2.3 First      Reading         of an Ordinance                  Adding        Chapter           3.10 to Title       3 of the Millcreek
            Code       of Ordinances,              thereby        establishing           a tax on the Gross               Receipts       of
            Telecommunications                      Service       Providers,          Setting       the Rate         for Such Tax          and
            Establishing            the Effective            Date     of the Tax
            Ms. Dudley,             HR-Finance            Director      said a tax on telecommunication                         services      adds another
            revenue       stream to Millcreek                 to help pay for police              services.         This is collected          and
            distributed        by the Utah Tax Commission.                           Utah     cities and towns           may impose           a tax of up
            to 3.5% on the value               of telecom            service       provided     within        their boundaries          and the telecom
            service     provider        may pass the tax on to its customers.                           The annual           telecom     tax revenue            for
            Millcreek       is estimated            at $360,000           per year. Millcreek               would     receive      disbursements           from
            the state pool.          The same amount                 of revenue        would        be generated         by a 4.16%           property      tax
            increase.      Property       tax is generated              from       homes      and for-profit          businesses.       Telecom          taxes
            apply     to even nonprofits.                 Millcreek's        effective        date would        be April        1, 2025.


          Ms. Dudley             presented         the franchise          taxes adopted          by cities and towns               in Salt Lake
            County.       Only       the town       of Brighton,           which      is newly       incorporated,           has not adopted            any of
          these fees; 83% have telecom                           taxes, 94% have energy                     sales and use taxes, and 89% have
          cable taxes. Mr. Winder                     said it makes            sense to lower          the rate and broaden              the base of
          taxes. Everyone              pays this, even organizations                        that would        not pay property           taxes. There
          has been success from                    the energy         and sales use taxes to prevent                     raising    property          taxes in
          the last two years. By adopting                         a telecom         tax, it helps prevent              larger   property       tax
          InCreaSeS.



          Mayor         Silvestrini      explained           how the animal              services      contract       has gone up 20%, the
          public       works        contract       with     Salt Lake       County        has escalated.            Everything,        everywhere,
          costs more money.                People          may suggest          cutting       other services,          but people       want those
          services,       too. The city gets calls all the time from                            residents        who want more animal
          services.       People       clamored            for the pickleball          courts     and new playground                 equipment,           but
          people       clamor        for other items.            Once again, Millcreek                 Common            and the new city hall
          building        did not contribute               to tax increases.           The reason why the city is considering                            the
          new tax is because               the city must pay more to provide                           services.       This is equivalent             to a 4%
          property        tax increase         and the city is trying                to hold the line on property                  tax increases.          This
          does broaden              the revenue           that the city can rely             on, and it provides             a revenue        source that is
          reasonably         stable in terms               of inflation      and economic             pressure.
