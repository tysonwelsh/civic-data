1.       A presentation regarding Station Area Plans from the BYU Provo City Lab Class.
         (22-081) (0:10:00)

Brandon Larsen, Planning Supervisor, introduced the presentation being given by BYU students
from professor Jamin Rowan’s class. Mr. Larsen explained that the City is required under state
law to pass a Station Area Plan regarding transit hubs in the City. This BYU class has done some
preliminary work on Station Area Plans which they will present to the Council. There are seven
stations (one Frontrunner and six UVX stations) which will be addressed in four plans.

Addison Graham introduced the class presentation. State law requires the station area plans to
cover an area within a quarter mile of the UVX bus stops and a half mile of the Frontrunner

Laura Andersen addressed increasing availability and affordability of housing. Ms. Andersen
addressed several tools that could be used to address housing issues. Use of the Interim Transit-

higher-density housing developments. The use of mixed-use zones or low-density residential
zones are other options. Ms. Andersen highlighted specific station areas which would be best
suited for the use of these zones.

Councilor Rachel Whipple asked about the specific area which they recommend rezoning to low-
density residential. Ms. Whipple noted that the Joaquin Neighborhood Plan contemplates a
north-south division rather than east-west; Ms. Andersen clarified that the neighborhood plan
contemplates use of the LDR zone, not specifically that placement. Councilor George Handley
commented on residential zoning in Joaquin and some of the challenges. Another classmate
shared comments from residents in the area he spoke with; in response to a question from Ms.
Whipple, he indicated that he spoke with homeowners in the area of 300 East.

Addison Graham presented on promoting sustainable environmental conditions. He shared green
building standards used in other cities and highlighted some practices that would work well in
Provo, such as LEED standards and flipping park strips. Mr. Handley asked staff’s perspective
on whether expediting the permitting process as an incentive for builders to use green practices
would be useful. Bill Peperone, Development Services, indicated that he agreed with Mr.
Graham’s assessment that easing the process would encourage favorable outcomes. As far as
whether a faster process was preferred over a financial incentive, Mr. Peperone was hesitant to
speak on behalf of the development community. Mr. Graham shared another idea from the city
of Boise, where the city assigned project managers to developers to assist with the city process.

Ms. Whipple asked if flipping park strips could be done for properties that were not owner-
occupied. Mr. Graham noted that one of the major issues in Joaquin is that the property owners
are not necessarily residents there. He noted that the water cost savings and easier maintenance
of flipped park strips could be appealing to property owners. Ms. Whipple noted there is still
maintenance work to factor in such as weeding. Mr. Graham also noted the importance of
maintaining well-established trees, as placing only rock material could create heat islands.

Ms. Andersen presented on enhancing access to opportunities. She addressed the language
accessibility of the Frontrunner station. The signage there is exclusively in English. Data shows
that 1 in 7 Utah residents speak another language in the home than English and Provo has a
significant proportion of Spanish-speaking residents. Developing wayfinding signs that would be
accessible to non-English speakers would increase access for all visitors to Provo. The various
redevelopment projects going on in this area present an opportunity to implement new signage

4.      An ordinance amending Provo City Code to update references to local development
        standards. Citywide application. (PLOTA20220333) (1:39:03)

David Day, Assistant City Engineer – Development, presented. Each year they update
development standards to make clarifications based on feedback from staff and the public. The
ordinance would change the standards from 2022 to 2023 for two sets of standards: the standard
drawing details and the Public Works department development design standards. There are 26
pages total changing out of all the documents (from about 5000 pages total).

Councilor Rachel Whipple asked if the City could create its own standard that would allow art in
crosswalks. Mr. Day said that would be possible as long as it complies with the overarching
standards and safety requirements adopted by the State. Councilor Katrice MacKay asked about
sidewalk bonding in new developments. She noted that it seems very inefficient for developers to
build sidewalks twice. Mr. Day provided some background on different sidewalk requirements.
There is a lot of waste in the current process, but they have not identified a better way to handle
sidewalk installation due to the different needs of construction sites and other residents in an area
for use of sidewalks once a development goes in. Ms. Whipple asked about the change in
terminology from “back of walk” to “property line;” Mr. Day indicated that the term property
line is clearer for city staff to determine what sight lines are needed. Presentation only. This
item was already scheduled for the Council Meeting on November 15, 2022.

5.      A discussion regarding a proposed appropriation for an Information Systems (IS)
        Department external assessment. (22-080) (2:03:12)

John Borget, Administrative Services Director, presented. The demands for IT resources at the
City continues to grow. There have been some concerns about the need to add additional staff
members. They recommend engaging an external consultant to evaluate the city’s Information
Systems operations. The assessment will look at the strategies, budget, overall reporting
structure, and other factors. The study can be completed for about $32,000. Half of the funding
will come from the IS operating budget and they have proposed that the other come from
General Fund Balance. After hearing proposals from several vendors, they are recommending the
Hartman Executive Advisors group. They have in-depth public-sector experience and their staff
have impressive expertise in the industry. They are also going to be on-site for the assessment.

David Elges, Hartman Executive Advisors, presented. He gave background on their organization.
They are an independent executive advisory group that works with non-profits. They do not
profit from any technology solutions, so the recommendations they can provide are in the best
interest of the City. Their assessment will evaluate the City’s IS department and whether the
current staffing and strategies being employed will help the City to accomplish its organizational
goals. Mr. Elges reviewed their focus areas for the assessment as well as their methodologies. He
reviewed the timeline and process for their review, which is anticipated to take 60 days.

6.      An ordinance amending the previously adopted ordinance 2022-33 regarding the
        Neighborhood Program. (22-055) (2:17:36)

Justin Harrison, Council Executive Director, presented. Following the Council’s adoption of
Ordinance 2022-33 in August to make changes to the Neighborhood Program, staff have
prepared additional code amendments to clarify several of the changes. The first change is to line
169, which would change the time limit for when a Chair serves again after previously serving to
12 months (from 24 months as written in the original ordinance). The second change is in
Section 2.29.050 line 207. This subsection references the Open and Public Meetings Act—the
consensus of the Neighborhood Program Review Committee was that these meetings be noticed
on the city website, rather than to follow exactly what is spelled out in the OPMA. The intent is
for these meetings to be publicized on the city website to encourage participation by Provo
residents. Councilor Rachel Whipple asked if they would follow a specific schedule about when
meetings would be posted. Mr. Harrison indicated that the intent was that meetings would be
posted at least 24 hours beforehand as well as through social media. Ms. Whipple asked if that is
set out in the City Code. Mr. Harrison explained this would be spelled out through procedures in
the Neighborhood Handbook. The final change is line 279 to correct a typo. Presentation only.
This item was already scheduled for the Council Meeting on November 15, 2022.

7.      An ordinance amending Provo City Code regarding towing operations to clarify
        signage requirements and allowed fees. (22-076) (2:23:38)

Brian Jones, City Attorney, presented. He reviewed the proposed changes to the City code.
Several sections are being removed and will simply refer to the relevant state code provisions.
For fees, towing operators cannot charge any other than those explicitly listed. This ordinance
also makes some minor clarifications in subsection 9.

Councilor Rachel Whipple asked what possible recourse a driver would have based on the
language “if a tow or boot is unlawful no fee shall be payable.” Mr. Jones indicated violations of
the city code should be reported to the Provo Police Department. A driver would be able to take
the towing operator to small claims court to get their money back if it was an illegal tow.

Councilor Travis Hoban supported adding a grace period for delivery vehicles. Councilors
George Handley and Katrice MacKay also indicated their interest; there was general interest
