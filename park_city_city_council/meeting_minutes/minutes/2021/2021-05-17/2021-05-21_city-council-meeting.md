FACILITATED DISCUSSION

Social Equity and Affordability:
Ken Fisher, Recreation Manager, Jason Glidden, Housing Manager, Adriane Juarez,
Library Director, Officer Franco Libertini, Enrique Sanchez, Community Outreach
Specialist, and Police Chief Wade Carpenter presented this item. Fisher showed a
video promoting the MARC and indicated recreation was about building community and
enriching people’s lives.

Glidden discussed the housing solutions goal. He stated since 2020, his team added 79
affordable units to the inventory. His team looked at the affordability of HOA fees and
found other ways to create more affordability. A new section of the City code was
created to incentivize developers to build affordable housing. He also noted a bilingual
housing specialist was hired who helped 20 families apply and receive rental
assistance.

Glidden stated 300 new affordable units were in different stages of development and his
team was looking at affordable dwelling unit (ADU) requirements to further grow the
inventory. He also stated the department was looking at housing for seniors and the
neuro-diverse population.

Juarez discussed supporting the underserved in the community. She indicated the
library did a good job in helping the underserved by offering services to all. Library fines
were eliminated, new programs were implemented, including a “Reading to End
Racism” group that met in the coffee shop, and more. Some initiatives that were being
planned or expanded, included having a coffee shop run by people with all abilities,
making a sliding scale for fees based on income, starting an LGBTQ+ book club, and
more.

Officer Libertini spoke in Spanish and Sanchez translated to English as they discussed
the fear that immigrants faced as they arrived in the City with a lack of understanding of
the language and culture. They worked long hours for little pay and usually didn’t have
time to plan for the future. They also feared the police because the police were
oppressors in their countries of origin. With these circumstances, immigrants didn’t feel
represented in the community or in local government. Officer Libertini discussed the
goal of feeling connected in the community and stated it was helpful working with
nonprofits in town. This goal encompassed the LGBTQIA+ community as well. His team
was submitting an MEI card that scores the City on its efforts working with
underprivileged groups. Officer Libertini and Sanchez stated social equity was a lot of
work and they hoped everyone would have a voice in the community.

Chief Carpenter discussed the diversity and inclusion goal and stated the real goal was
to create trust. The Police Department wanted to make sure the resources were
available to help those who didn’t speak English. A Facebook group was set up and
Facebook Live implemented to get messaging out to the Latinx community. Efforts were
made to educate that segment of the community on the laws and how to interact with
officers. The department implemented a Spanish Citizens Academy. There was also an
effort to make City boards and commissions reflective of the community makeup. Chief
Carpenter reviewed future initiatives his team was working on, including hiring a
diversity liaison, renovating the City Park building to make it a hub for youth, and adding
amenities in the parks to build community.

Council Member Joyce indicated other critical priorities had measurable goals, but
Social Equity was harder to measure, and asked how the City would know it was
making progress. Fisher stated a lot of small actions had a big impact. There were
many examples of helping the underserved. Council Member Gerber stated if the rec
programs had all white people, then the City knew it was not meeting the goal.

Council member Gerber expressed frustration that Housing only got 10 minutes in this
presentation. She was not okay with Housing being meshed with Social Equity, and

David Everitt, Katrina Kmak, Gretchen Milliken, and Jenny Diersen presented this item.
Everitt stated this pillar was about the City’s identity although all the strategic pillars
contributed to the City’s authentic local culture. Diersen displayed a picture of the opera
house in the City and reviewed how it burned down, was rebuilt, then a snowstorm
caved in the roof, and it was again rebuilt, and now stood as the Egyptian Theatre. She
stated this was a symbol of how a City evolved. Everitt stated the history of mining
helped make Park City authentic. He was impressed with the civic engagement and the
community involvement, the businesses and nonprofits, and the access to outdoors.

Milliken stated 50% of Planning applications were Historic District reviews, which said
something about the attraction of the Historic District today. She was impressed with the
preservation ordinances and codes that helped to preserve the City’s history. She noted
there were preservation grants, work on the Daly West Headframe, and updating the
Historic Sites list. Kmak reviewed some accomplishments of the library and virtual
connections made during the pandemic. Diersen highlighted the arts in the community
and what the City did to support artists. Two public art projects were completed. The
Utility Box Art - Phase Two was an upcoming project. She stated events were very
important to the community and they were being brought back safely.

Heinrich Deters, Trails and Open Space Manager, stated the outdoors was part of the
community. He reviewed the properties that were bought and designated as open
space, indicated Nordic skiing was implemented on the golf course during winter
months, and stated trails were built, many with easy grades for all users. Fisher
indicated he submitted a budget request to have outdoor ice on the tennis courts during
the winter, and noted the courts were lit and people would be able to skate until 10:00
p.m. On warm days, the fences could be locked to ensure safety.

Diersen stated the businesses and nonprofits were high risk during the pandemic and
the City was essential in helping them open and serve the community. Council awarded
grants and implemented car-free Sundays, free parking, and other programs to help
businesses. Some goals moving forward were special service contracts, extending
library leases, adding a one percent incentive for private developers to be used for
public art, and implementing a local history appreciation month. Other goals included
providing a space for artists in affordable housing, implementing a trailhead congestion
management program, offering owners of ski era structures the opportunity to be on the
Historic Sites Inventory, focusing on sustainable tourism, and being an Olympics venue
in 2030 or 2034. Everitt also suggested having a tourism dividend or other form of direct
benefit for residents. Diersen referred to other goals such as constructing a community
reflection/memorial garden, having a destination and community impact management
program, and having a mining site tourism initiative to tell the story of the community.
Everitt stated a nightly rental management program was needed as well as expanding
the dark store ordinance to include the entire City. Everitt asked if staff was trying to do
too much. He suggested reprioritizing with the community and stakeholders so the focus
was on quality over quantity.

Environmental Leadership:
Luke Cartin, Clint McAffee and Heinrich Deters presented this item. McAffee indicated
there were several water conservation and water filtering projects in progress to
improve water quality. He displayed the water projects completed from the $75 million
bond. He noted that in finding and stopping water leaks, as well as conservation efforts,
water usage had declined by 50%. He reviewed the Spiro Mine Tunnel project and
those efforts to decrease water loss. The Public Utilities Department was currently
focused on the new water treatment plant, which was very advanced and would move
the City forward.

Deters stated most of Park City was open space and he reviewed the efforts for weed
and fire mitigation, conservation easements, agriculture leases, etc. Some future
projects included heightened weed mitigation with non-chemical methods.

Cartin reviewed some Sustainability accomplishments included a solar project in Tooele
that would benefit the City, electric vehicle infrastructure upgrades around town, and the
community renewable program in conjunction with 16 other cities and three counties.
He discussed land sequestration efforts and energy efficiency studies and
implementation. The goal was to rapidly become a self-sufficient, sustainable
community by 2030, which included zero waste. He stated sustainable efforts were
incorporated in events, planting trees, efforts with trails and open space such as the
transit to trails pilot program, funding for HOAs to mitigate weeds and wildfires, and
water conservation.

Council Member Joyce asked if the City could work with landscaping companies to offer
a service that would teach residents how to conserve water in landscaping. Mayor
Beerman suggested giving assistance to xeriscape and adding that cost to the
individual’s water bill over the next 10 years, using the City’s bonding capacity to fund
that project. McAffee stated he could look into those options.

Council Member Joyce asked if Rocky Mountain Power was ready to accommodate the
charging surge as electric vehicles became more common. Cartin stated there was a
program to balance the electricity in the area by reducing some areas while charging
electric vehicles.

Council Member Gerber supported the goal of planting a tree for every resident and
having the residents plant them on their own properties, and suggested getting a variety
of trees. Cartin indicated GIS staff had mapped out the City to see the best locations for
planting trees. Council Member Henney stated over the years, different organizations
had tried this project and he asked if the City would facilitate the tree distribution. Cartin
indicated Boise, Idaho had this program and he was learning from them. Council
Member Doilney stated the efforts to increase environmental leadership had been going
on for years and the City could see progress being made. It gave him confidence as
other pillars were beginning to be worked on.

Wrap Up, Inclusions, and Takeaways:
Dias summarized Council accepted the final Vision 2020 report, but work was needed
on the statement that “Everyone has a SEAT at the Table,” along with adding goals to
the pillars. He indicated staff would slow down with Authentic Local Culture goals to see
what would work best. There were minimal liaison assignment changes. Housing would
be a focus and in the next couple months there would be several work sessions on this
topic. Other measurements should be noted by the City to see progress and set goals.
Also, tools were needed to see progress with Social Equity. Mayor Beerman wanted to
know what it would take to move the needle in Social Equity and stated his focus was
social infrastructure to support equity. Dias stated the last takeaway was that in the next
60 days, taskforces would be created for each strategic pillar. The Council liaisons
could meet with staff and others and work on better ways to measure success or
implement projects. The Council agreed to the following liaison assignments: Social
