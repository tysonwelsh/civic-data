 1.    Resolution 2021-20 appropriating $112,162 in the Ice Sheet Fund from Ice Sheet Fund Balance
       for Turf Replacement at Peaks Ice Arena. (21-058) (0:15:53)

       Motion: An implied motion to approve Resolution 2021-20, as currently constituted, has been
               made by council rule.

Scott Henderson, Parks and Recreation Director, presented. The Peaks Ice Arena was built in 1998 for
the 2002 Olympics. The arena was a celebrated example of the transition from an Olympic venue to a
vibrant day-to-day operation. This was the only ice arena in Utah County and the only public indoor turf
field in Provo. The 200-foot by 80-foot turf field, installed approximately ten years ago, needed to be
replaced with a newer, safer surface. The arena generated close to $200,000 in annual revenues. Turf
diversification and annual revenues were key to the successful operation of the arena.

$112,162 of that fund balance to replace the indoor turf. The low bidder was chosen after extensive
research indicated they could provide quality turf for the arena.

 3.     Ordinance 2021-21 amending Provo City Code regarding Municipal Council Members'
        Compensation. (21-026) (0:22:59)

       Motion:      An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2021-21, as currently constituted, has been
                    made by council rule.

Cliff Strachan, Council Executive Director, presented. The commission reviewed mayoral and council
member compensation for Utah cities with a population greater than 85,000. Recognizing the
complexity of a leading city like Provo, which included a wide range of services, the commission
recommended that the mayor’s salary be raised to $130,000 per year and the council member salary be
raised to $21,000 per year. The committee also looked at council expense stipends and proposed an
increase in each kind. The general expense increased to $125 per month, communications expense rose
to $150 per month, and monthly stipends to council chair and vice chair increased to $125 and $85
respectively. If adopted, the new compensation levels would be effective beginning January 2022. The
council could reduce or refuse any changes.

Chair Sewell opened public comment. With no response, public comment was closed.

Chair Sewell invited council discussion. Councilor Handley thanked the commission for completing this
independent work for the city. He felt this was the only responsible way to consider compensation. He
referenced a statement made during work meeting that the increased council compensation could allow
more financially diverse members on the council. He felt the council compensation was adequate for
the hours worked.

Councilor Hoban noted it had been four years since a compensation increase for the mayor and council.

Councilor Ellsworth did not think anyone ran for office for the money. Everyone put in a lot of time,

John Borget, Provo City Administrative Services Director, presented. Mr. Borget said the proposed
budget for Fiscal Year 2022 (beginning July 1, 2021 and ending June 30, 2022) was $303,469,053. The

David Mortensen, Provo City Budget Officer, said the proposed resolution would tentatively adopt the
tentative budget. Public hearings would be set for June 1 and June 15, 2021. The total budget for Provo
City did not include Storm Water or Redevelopment. The budget number in the resolution included
total expenditures and transfers out. It should just include expenditures, so the number needed to be
changed from $331,746,989 to $303,469,053.

Councilor Harding asked what was meant by tentatively adopting a tentative budget. Mr. Jones said
that state code required the city to create a tentative budget and submit it to the council. The council
would then tentatively adopt it. It then became the official budget document for the citizens to review
during the public hearings.

In response to a question from Mr. Strachan, Mr. Jones replied that if the council held truth in taxation
hearings in August they would not adopt the final budget until the end of August. During the period
from July 1 until the final budget was adopted, the city could operate from the tentative budget or from
the previous year’s budget. The custom had been to operate from the tentative budget.

 7.        Ordinance 2021-22 enacting Provo City Code Chapter 14.20C. Citywide Application.
           (PLOTA20210026) (0:59:32)

       Motion: An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2021-22, as currently constituted, has been
               made by council rule.

Melissa McNally, Provo City Planner, presented. This item was presented to the council a few weeks
ago. During that meeting, council members expressed concern that the changes to the Freeway
Commercial Two (FC2) zone might affect the existing zone just off Center Street. Planning staff
discussed the concerns and decided to create a Freeway Commercial Three (FC3) zone that would just
apply to two parcels located north and south of Lakeview Parkway. Those parcels were currently in the
A1.20 zone.

Chair Sewell invited council discussion.

Councilor Harding was supportive of this change. He appreciated that planning staff took a second look
at the council concerns that were raised.

Mr. Jones explained that the original request was to amend the FC2 zone and then apply that zone to
these two parcels. Council would need to make a motion to have the ordinance enacting an FC3 zone
(as shown on the screen) be the subject of the implied motion.

Chair Sewell opened this item for public comment.

Leeanna Griffee, Lakewood Neighborhood Co-chair, said that, while the neighborhood approved the
proposal, they would like to make a recommendation. They would like to take out language that said
the building height could be changed at the discretion of the Development Service director. They did
not want any changes to the height of buildings in the zone. In addition, the buffer zone, consisting of
trees and grass, should be protected.

Chair Sewell closed public comment.

Councilor Shipley asked if someone from the Administration could talk about building heights being
changed at the discretion of Development Services.

Robert Mills, Planning Supervisor, said that discretion was put into text amendments so that the director
could make slight modifications relating to building heights or setbacks. This was helpful when there
had not been a specific development attached to the rezone. There were certain conditions that stated
any modification had to be visually compatible with the neighboring development and did not cause a



Provo City Council Meeting Minutes - May 18, 2021                                               Page 6 of 11
violation of building or fire code. This allowed a little flexibility but would not detract from the intent or
purpose of the zone.

Councilor Ellsworth said there were plenty of instances in the code where they left discretion to the
Development Services director. That was a process and person they could trust.

Councilor Harding said he was supportive of the change. The new zone created a 65-foot buffer,
between the commercial businesses and the existing residential neighborhood, that needed to be
landscaped. He did not see much value to the community to have this kind of dead space. He hoped
that the developer of the commercial property would find a way to make the buffer space an amenity

 9.        An ordinance amending Provo City Code regarding conditional uses in the Freeway
           Commercial (FC1) Zone. Citywide Application. (PLOTA20210000262) (1:28:33)

       Motion:      An implied motion to adopt the ordinance, as currently constituted, has been made by
                    council rule.

Aaron Ardmore, Provo City Planning Supervisor, presented. This item was continued from the last
meeting to give more time to research the request. The proposed ordinance would add a conditional
use in the Freeway Commercial (FC) zone, to allow a product to be produced when the establishment is
an accessory and adjacent to a cannabis distribution facility.

The current FC zone allowed the permitted use of a cannabis distribution facility or pharmacy. What
was up for discussion was whether the Council wanted to force a business in the FC zone to purchase a
separate property to produce a good, package that good, truck it down the road to this zone, unload it,
and then sell in the FC zone. On the other hand, did it make more sense to allow them to operate a
small production facility behind a business they were already operating?




Provo City Council Meeting Minutes - May 18, 2021                                              Page 8 of 11
The applicant, Brittany Johnson, was invited to present. Ms. Johnson said they were asking for an
amendment that would allow production as an ancillary at the back of their facility. Since this was a
new use in the state, it was very limited and highly regulated. This type of product was not allowed to
be seen by the public and they were not allowed to advertise. The business, which was nondescript,
was allowed to have a sign with a green medical cross. The facility was in the FC zone off the Center
Street freeway interchange. The location was isolated from any residential areas. The production
facility would be a small, enclosed pod. The distribution facility was not open to the public and a
medical card was required in order to enter the facility.

Councilor Ellsworth asked what conditions made this type of use a good neighbor and a better use for
the assigned land use. Ms. Johnson said the proposal would allow the product to be produced on site
rather than having to truck it in after being produced somewhere else.

Councilor Ellsworth asked staff what kind of conditions could be assigned to this type of conditional use
permit. Mr. Ardmore said there were several conditional use permits that included a set of conditions –
like dance halls. There were also several conditional use permits that did not have a standard set of
conditions. If concerns with a condition use were found, a review of the conditional use was made and
then a set of conditions might be applied. This conditional use might be one of those that needed to be
reviewed. We might not know what those conditions needed to be until we have approved other
requests.

Mr. Jones noted this industry was heavily regulated by the state. He asked the applicant if there was a
limit on the number of licenses that the state would issue for production facilities. Ms. Johnson said
that Deseret Wellness held one of the two distribution permits allowed in Utah County. She was not
sure about production permits. She said they were only requesting to be ancillary to an existing
dispensary. An additional dispensary would need another dispensary permit released, it would need to
be in the FC1 zone, and they would also need to request an ancillary production permit.

Chair Sewell invited public comment. With no response, public comment was closed.

Councilor Harding said that, while medical marijuana use was highly regulated in the state, it had
become less regulated and restricted in other states. We need to make our choices with that
understanding in mind. When the large green cross went up, it was very visible from the freeway, but it
was also very visible from the whole area around the dispensary.

During his research, he found there were three separate stages of medical cannabis – cultivation,
production, and distribution. Due to state deadlines, there had been quick decisions made about where
they would be allowed in Provo. Since that time, further discussion had been held about the best
locations for those to be permitted. He would like further discussion on this issue. We needed to set
limits and apply conditions to the conditional use before we opened up where this type of production
was allowed. If conditions were not applied to a conditional use, then it was basically a permitted use.
He wanted to have more clarity on if the state would issue more permits before he made a decision on
this request. He would not mind letting other communities lead out in the area of medical cannabis. If
this were approved, would we be making it easier for more companies to establish this type of business
in Provo?

Councilor Ellsworth said her biggest heartburn with this use was the large green sign that could be seen
from I-15. It did not signal that it was a good place to get off the freeway, to get gas, or to get lost.


Provo City Council Meeting Minutes - May 18, 2021                                               Page 9 of 11
There were connotations with that kind of sign at a gateway to our city. She felt we could do better by
that interchange if we had different, cleaner signage.

Councilor Fillmore noted that the applicant’s company only produced medical cannabis, authorized by
state law. It was a CDB based product and did not contain any of the chemical components of
marijuana. Ms. Johnson stated that it did contain those components – it was a medical marijuana
dispensary. She was not aware that it contained hallucinogenic properties or any elements of addictive
properties. However, she was not an expert on the subject. Since she was not the owner of the
company, she did not know if the company would favor opening up to offering non-medical cannabis, if
allowed to by the state.

Councilor Fillmore asked Mr. Jones if permitting this would open marijuana production and retail sales in
other zones. Mr. Jones said granting the conditional use in this zone created no requirement that you
would have to do so in other zones.

Councilor Hoban clarified that medical marijuana in Utah can, and did, contain THC, which was the
hallucinogenic aspect of marijuana.

Councilor Shipley said that distribution was already allowed in Provo and production was already
permitted in a different zone in Provo. We were telling the applicant they had to produce it in one zone
and then transport it to the distribution facility in a different zone. This simply increased community
exposure to the product, something that councilors were concerned about. He felt that production
adjacent to this property was the right answer. Would further discussion change the minds of those
councilors that would vote no on this issue. If not, we should just vote on this tonight.

Councilor Handley said that production was already an allowed use. We were spreading the location of
the whole industry throughout the city which went against the grain of what the council was concerned
about. He was inclined to support the proposal. We were probably creating more symbolism around
the issue than warranted, given the fact that it was already permitted in the city. He did not see any
compelling reason why production should not be adjacent to the dispensary.

Chair Sewell said the significance of allowing production next to the distribution facility lowered the cost
and increased attractiveness to a business locating there. His concern about medical marijuana was that
it could include both THC and CBD in varying amounts. The FDA has made a conscious decision to keep
cannabis as a schedule one drug, which is the same category as heroin and other drugs. He would
rather err on the side of being cautious about encouraging production of a drug here.

Councilor Fillmore said he supported the point of view of Councilors Sewell and Harding. There was no
rush to approve this particular issue.

Mr. Jones said that quotas and limits could change at any time. Even if councilors were uncomfortable
with those, they would not have any control over whether they changed or not. In addition, production
was allowed in industrial zones, per state statute. The FC1 zone was not an industrial zone. If
production, as a permitted use, was taken out of the FI zone and put into the FC zone, it would allow the
ancillary use. However, per state law, it would then automatically become a permitted use in all
industrial zones in the city, as well as the FC zone.
