about the gravel berms.

       b.       Discussion on Fire Code Amendments – Jon Teerlink.
Mayor Dahle reported that the Fire Code amendments would ensure that the Fire Code is in alignment
with State Code. He asked UFA Division Chief, Bradley Larson, to discuss the amendments. Chief
Larson expressed his appreciation for the City. He reported that periodically, the State looks at the
Building, Fire, Residential, and Planning Codes and makes amendments to fit local needs. The State
had determined that a local jurisdiction could require a fire sprinkler system for a residential home by
Ordinance if the following conditions exist:

   •   Located in an urban-wildland interface area and did not meet minimum standards for Wildland
       Fire Ordinance;
   •   No public water system or fire hydrants were present;
   •   Access road had a grade greater than 10% for more than 500 continual feet;
   •   Building was over 10,000 square feet or double the average square footage of the homes
       within a subdivision; and
   •   Exceptions – could not require a fire sprinkler system if the home was located outside
       wildland-urban interface, was a one-lot subdivision, or had 50-feet defensible space.

According to City Planner, Jonathan Teerlink, 24 homes were found to meet the 10,000 square-foot
requirement within the last five years. Chief Larson reported that fire sprinkler systems can be
monitored. That would not only make it possible to save a home in the event of a fire but also
minimize water damage. Ms. Chamness and Mr. Teerlink suggested that he bring the item to Council
to determine whether the amendment was something the City of Holladay would want to consider.

Mayor Dahle noted that this was not a State requirement, but the State would allow a City to add the
item to an Ordinance. Chief Larson confirmed this. Council Member Fotheringham stated that the
City cannot be more restrictive than the 10,000 square feet requirement. Council Member Gibbons

did not believe there was an Ordinance in the City that requires a fire suppression system in the home.
Chief Larson noted that there are homes in the City of Holladay that are fire sprinklered because they

        c.     Update on SB 34 Implementation – LaNiece Davenport.
Mayor Dahle reported that there would be an update on Senate Bill (“SB”) 34. There was a lot of
information included in the Meeting Materials Packet related to the item. His understanding was that
as part of SB-34, the City needed to report to the State every year to share the progress made on
affordable housing. Community and Economic Development Director, LaNiece Davenport explained
that SB-34 came out of the 2019 Legislative Session. The reason the State Legislature was focused
on housing was because there are significant population growth issues throughout the State.
Ms. Davenport noted that some of the key objectives of SB-34 were highlighted in the slide
presentation.

The State wanted the City to develop a Moderate-Income Housing Plan, look at the plan each year,
and consider how it would be implemented. Ms. Davenport stated that a copy of the 2019 Moderate-
Income Housing Plan was included in the packet. If the City does not comply with the requests from
the State, they will be ineligible for the Transportation Investment Fund (“TIF”) and the Transit
Transportation Investment Fund (“TTIF”). Ms. Davenport overviewed some of the main
requirements of the Moderate-Income Housing Plan. For instance, it needs to be reviewed and
updated every five years, the City needs to consider housing demographics, look at the five-year
housing needs, report on the implementation each year to the Department of Workforce Services
(“DWS”) and the Wasatch Front Regional Council (“WFRC”), and post the Plan on the City’s
website. The City of Holladay had done that and met planning requirements.

The reporting requirements were reviewed. Ms. Davenport explained that the reporting requirements
were underway, and the City was asked to review the Moderate-Income Housing Plan as well as the
Implementation Report to consider housing gaps. She discussed the Area Median Income (“AMI”)

Mayor Dahle wondered how the State viewed the actions from municipalities so far. Ms. Davenport

        d.      Discussion on Budget Amendments.
Mayor Dahle believed the Council was clear about the UPD budget amendment. The small
amendment for the Police Appreciation Week was also clear. As a result, further discussions were
had about the amendment related to bailiff services. Mayor Dahle referenced the fourth option of
hiring an additional UPD Detective and splitting the cost of that position between the Holladay Justice
Court and UPD duties.

Council Member Fotheringham noted that a Detective is a high-ranking position and a bailiff seemed
like a lower-ranking job in comparison. He asked why those two roles had been proposed as one split
job. Chief Hoyal explained that with the UPD, the Detective and Officer positions are the same in
terms of the pay scale. The decision for the split Detective and bailiff position was related to the
needs of the precinct. Detective caseloads were increasing, there were more longer-term
investigations, and there had been an increase in mental health cases. That was the reason he proposed
the split to Ms. Chamness. Mayor Dahle explained that one of the challenges facing the City was the
fact that they had lost contracted bailiff services in the past. The intention was to create a more
permanent solution.

Council Member Gibbons expressed his support for UPD providing the bailiff services. He felt it
would add stability to the position and offer flexibility to the precinct. Chief Hoyal noted that if the
bailiff was sick, on vacation, or training, there would be other Detectives available to fill the position
temporarily. Council Member Petersen asked about Cottonwood Heights. Ms. Smith reported that
Ms. Chamness had a meeting scheduled early next week. The idea was that if their revenue did not
cover their portion, Cottonwood Heights would reimburse the City of Holladay.

Mayor Dahle was concerned that adding another full-time employee at the UPD would create
difficulties with the budget. A lot of time and effort had been spent on the tax increase and promises
had been made about the capital improvements attached to that income stream. He suggested that the
City be mindful about adding another full-time UPD employee. It should only be done if it is the best
option for the City. Mayor Dahle suggested that the City may want to increase the bailiff rate from
$20 to $30 per hour. It could be a part-time job instead of there being a full-time UPD employee. No
decision needed to be made at the current City Council Meeting, but he wanted to make sure that the
Council considered all options before making a final decision. The Council agreed.
