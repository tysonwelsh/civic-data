       A.      Public Hearing: Carol Lee Subdivision Plat Amendment and Deviation
               Request.
               (Administrative Action).
               On the request of Troy Blundell, a Subdivision Plat Amendment and Deviation
               Request on 1.07 acres located at approximately 995 East 12600 South in the TC
               (Town Center) Zone. Known as Application 2024-0191-SUB. Staff Contact:
               Jennifer Jastremsky, (801)576-6328, jennifer.jastremsky@draperutah.gov.

Community Development Director, Jennifer Jastremsky reported that the Amendment and
Deviation Request was continued from the June 13, 2024, Planning Commission Meeting. The
subdivision is south of City Hall, accessed by 12600 South, and a private lane. It is in the Town
Center Land Use and Zoning designations. The existing subdivision has two lots. The original
application included a third lot. Upon review, the Planning Commission expressed concerns about
potential future uses of the third lot and requested that the subdivision amendment remain as two
lots. The applicant made that change. There will be two lots, one for each existing home, and the
previous third lot will be incorporated into the two lots. Lot 4 will be .79 acres and Lot 5 will be
.28 acres in size. The applicant has also requested a deviation to not install curb, gutter, and
sidewalk. Currently, 12600 South and 1015 East do not have those improvements. The adjacent

Bainbridge Subdivision will install a cul-de-sac at the end of 12600 South, which was also
approved without curb, gutter, and sidewalk.

Chair Adams asked for more information on why the application was continued at the last meeting.
Director Jastremsky stated that the subdivision has Town Center zoning, which does not allow for
single-family homes. The existing homes predate the zoning and are, therefore, legal non-
conforming. The Town Center zone allows for commercial, office, and mixed-use. There was a
concern that if a third lot was created, it could potentially open the residential street to commercial
development.

The applicant, Troy Blundell stated that Director Jastremsky covered the application thoroughly
and he had nothing to add but was available to answer any questions from the Commission.

Chair Adams opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing
was closed.

Commissioner Shah asked about the deviation request for curb, gutter, and sidewalk. Director
Jastremsky stated that the Bainbridge Subdivision being built on the Fitzgerald Farm east of the
subject subdivision will install a cul-de-sac at the end of 12600 South with no curb, gutter, or
sidewalk. The cul-de-sac was approved by the City Council in lieu of connection to 12600 South.
Currently, there is no curb, gutter, and sidewalk on this section of 12600 South or 1015 East.
Because it is an existing road, there is no real impact on the roads from not installing the
improvements, and Staff does not see a need for those improvements to be installed. Adding curb
and gutter to the middle of a road that doesn't have those improvements can create drainage issues,
so it makes sense to not require those improvements with this plat amendment.

The existing homes on 12600 South are zoned Town Center. If the area is redeveloped in the
future, at that point the developer would need to add improvements, provide parking, etc. The
trigger would be redevelopment. The deviation request is for existing residential properties that
will remain residential.

Commissioner Fowler saw no reason to deny the request. She expressed her gratitude for the
applicant’s willingness to change the design to better fit with the neighborhood.

              todd.taylor@draperutah.gov, (801) 576-6510.

City Planner, Todd Taylor, presented the 2024 Moderate-Income Housing Report. The General
Plan was adopted in 2019. In 2022, the Moderate-Income Housing Plan was amended into the

General Plan. Each year, the state requires each city to report on the plan. Beginning in 2024,
they are also required to report on the following for entitled units:

   •   Total units that are entitled through a development agreement, planned unit development,
       or other legal document that have not pulled a building permit.

   •   Total count of vacant parcels in an overlay zone and the number of residential units that

   •   Total count of vacant lots that are currently zoned residential and the number of residential

   •   Total count of entitled residential units that have received a will-serve letter and have all
       infrastructure in place.

Staff did not yet have data on the new reporting requirements but would obtain it prior to
submitting the report.

With regard to Accessory Dwelling Units ("ADU"), Planner Taylor indicated that there are 31 total
permitted ADUs in the City. In FY 2023-2024, five ADU Building Permits were issued and 10
ADU permits were granted. Director Jastremsky noted that there are approximately 60 ADUs in
the City, but only 31 had renewed their permits. Chair Adams asked if there is a benefit to residents
to register their ADU, and Director Jastremsky stated that the benefit is the ADUs will be legal.

Planner Taylor stated that Draper has adopted several strategies which are reported yearly.

Strategy F is to zone or rezone for higher-density or moderate-income residential development in
commercial or mixed-use zones near major transit investment corridors, commercial centers, or
employment centers. The City's efforts to create the Town Center Station Area Plan are underway,
and the current draft envisions approximately 560 new dwelling units between City and Utah
Transit Authority-owned sites. Staff has conducted individual meetings with Council Members
and stakeholders and is considering additional adjustments to the plan, as well as determining if
additional funding will be required. Once the plan is adopted, zoning changes will be presented
to the Planning Commission and City Council for consideration.

Strategy G is to amend Land Use regulations to allow for higher density or new moderate-income
residential development in commercial or mixed-use zones near major transit investment corridors.
The City has kicked off its comprehensive Zoning and Subdivision Code update. A joint workshop
was held earlier in 2024. A survey and open house were also conducted. Land Use regulations
will be modified to allow for additional housing types.

Strategy J is to implement zoning incentives for moderate-income units in new developments. In
2024, a joint Planning Commission and City Council workshop was held to identify potential
incentives. The comprehensive Zoning and Subdivision Code update will also address this

strategy. As part of the online survey and open house, the public was asked about housing type
preferences, location, style, etc. The next step is to research best practices of other municipalities.

Strategy L is to reduce, waive, or eliminate Impact Fees related to moderate-income housing. No
funding has been allocated for this study. Instead, the 2024-2025 budget prioritized funding for
other MIHP measures such as the Zoning and Subdivision Code update and the Station Area Plans.
Staff has identified that the City may want to pursue a different strategy. If another strategy is
chosen, a text amendment will be brought to the Planning Commission and City Council for
adoption.

Strategy V is to develop and adopt a Station Area Plan in accordance with Section 10-9a-403.1.
Efforts to create the Town Center Station Area Plan are underway. In 2023, Staff met with
Wasatch Front Regional Council staff regarding completing other station area plans. Staff have
begun drafting a request for proposals for consulting services on the Kimballs Lane Station Area
Plan and expect to begin that by the end of the year. Staff have also spoken with multiple interested
buyers for the Olabarri properties, which is the agricultural land near Kimballs Lane Station
between the station and Juan Diego High School.

Strategy O involves partnering with other jurisdictions to build affordable housing. Draper City
Council approved a resolution in 2023 to enter into an interlocal agreement with Salt Lake County
for Community Development Block Grant ("CDBG"), Emergency Solutions Grant ("ESG"), and
Home Investment Partnerships Program ("HOME") funds. The agreement runs through June 30,
2027.

Planner Taylor presented a list of alternate strategies to replace Strategy L. A potential
replacement is Strategy E, "Create or allow for, and reduce regulations related to, internal or
detached accessory dwelling units in residential zones."

The State asks for feedback and recommendations from each jurisdiction regarding the types of

Chair Adams asked what input would be helpful from the Planning Commission. Director
Jastremsky stated that no input is necessary for the report, but the Commissioners should consider
which strategies they believe the Council should consider for inclusion. Staff and Council had a
discussion regarding the Impact Fee reduction strategy, at which time the Council expressed
concerns about the strategy. Staff would prefer to adopt a more practical strategy.

Commissioner Shah stated that Impact Fees are used for fire, water, and other necessary services.
Chair Adams stated that he understood the reluctance to implement Strategy L as it provides no
benefit to the end user to make it affordable, moderate housing. It is a discount to the developer
that results in a loss of income to provide those services. Director Jastremsky stated that reducing
or waiving Impact Fees would be tied to the housing being in the moderate-income level, but the
strategy creates questions regarding how long the housing must stay at that price level and if deed
restrictions are applied.

Chair Adams asked about the income level that is considered moderate. Director Jastremsky
clarified that it is calculated as 80% of the County's Area Median Income ("AMI"), or
approximately $91,000. Chair Adams stated that ADU housing may be the only type of housing
in Draper that could work for people in that income range. Director Jastremsky stated that there
are some income-restricted apartments that meet the moderate-income housing standard, but there
are no single-family homes for sale that meet the criteria.

Chair Adams suggested requesting that the state provide examples of successful ADU
developments in Utah or elsewhere in the United States. The Ivory neighborhood has longer,
narrow lots that were created with the intention of building detached ADUs. There will be a
narrow, shotgun-style house with a two-car garage, then a second detached two-car garage with
an upstairs ADU at the rear of the property. His concern with that setup is the number of people
who will use the ADU as an office rather than moderate-income housing. Director Jastremsky
stated that Staff will research the topic. They have seen building permit applications for homes
with internal and external ADUs. If the ADU qualifies under City Code, the ADU permit is issued
with the building permit.

Chair Adams stated that the best examples he has seen of successful new ADU developments are
at Eagle Mountain. They have 5,000-square-foot homes with three-car garages. The third-car
garage is segmented off with an internal stairway to the basement ADU. The two-bedroom
apartment takes up approximately three-fourths of the basement, and the upstairs owner-occupant
has a family room and bathroom in the other one-fourth. That is a true ADU that benefits both
parties, but Eagle Mountain is very different from Draper. He would like to see examples of places
in Utah where ADUs have worked so the City has something to reference when speaking with
developers.

Commissioner Fowler asked for clarification on Strategy I. Director Jastremsky stated that single-
room occupancy developments are complexes with small, approximately 300-square-foot studio
apartments that are designed for one person. Planner Todd Draper stated that it could also be
dorm-style units with a shared kitchen.

Commissioner Shah stated that he would like to look at the Station Area Plan and be involved in
the discussion regarding the Impact Fee strategy. Perhaps a development agreement could be
utilized with the Station Area Plan to make up for the loss of Impact Fees. Chair Adams indicated
that the City may be able to charge more for developments with ADUs because it will be two

be charged for ADUs.

Commissioner Squire spoke to Strategy X. She has been learning about different types of housing
that better fit in single-family-home neighborhoods and does not detract from that lifestyle. She
grew up in an older part of New York where many of the larger homes were converted to multi-
family homes. You cannot tell that there are three units in a home. There are also twin homes or
home clusters that share a common courtyard. They are attractive developments that do not look
like low-income housing. They are smaller homes at a lower price point, which she believes is a
better option than large apartments or condominium complexes. If the City encourages that type
of development, it could be beneficial. She will send examples of these housing types to Director
Jastremsky.

Chair Adams asked if per-unit bonding is affected by the Impact Fee strategy, for example, if
bonding could be waived for additional units. Director Jastremsky stated that bonds are tied to the
development, not individual units. Chair Adams stated that his intention was to find an incentive
for the developer that will not negatively impact the City. Commissioner Shah stated that he does
not believe bonds are an issue. Transportation Impact Fees are a demotivating factor for
developers. Cities usually accept a bond letter in lieu of an actual cash bond.
