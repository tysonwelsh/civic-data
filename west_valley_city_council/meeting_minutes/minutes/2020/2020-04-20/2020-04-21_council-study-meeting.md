RESOLUTION 20-44: APPROVE AN AMENDMENT TO A SUBGRANT
AGREEMENT BETWEEN WEST VALLEY CITY AND SALT LAKE COUNTY
GOVERNING THE DISBURSEMENT AND USE OF HOME INVESTMENT
PARTNERSHIP FUNDING
Heather Royall, CPD, presented proposed Resolution 20-44 that would approve an
amendment to a Subgrant Agreement between West Valley City and Salt Lake County
governing the disbursement and use of Home Investment Partnership Funding.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

   Salt Lake County has notified West Valley City of an addition to their FY 2019-2020 HOME
   funds, in the amount of $100,000, to be used solely for the restoration of mobile homes knocked
   off their foundations by the earthquake, in the Kopper View and Western Estates Mobile Home
   Parks. West Valley City will administer an Emergency Critical Assistance Program using these
   funds.

      I.      Housing Rehabilitation Programs (HCD19115) Total Budget
                 a. Single-Family Housing Rehabilitation:             $ 174,800.00
                 b. Rehabilitation Project delivery costs:            $ 20,200.00
                 c. Mobile Home Restorations:                 $ 100,000.00
                                                     Total            $ 295,000.00

           Urban Development (HUD) for financial assistance to conduct a HOME Investment
           Partnership Program (HOME Program) pursuant to the HOME Investment Partnerships
           Act, Title II of the Cranston-Gonzales National Affordable Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. 12701-
           12839, as amended, and subject to the Rules and Regulations, promulgated by HUD
           governing the conduct of HOME Investment Partnership Programs including, but not
           limited to , Title 24, Part 92 of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) (the Rules and

           and Budget (OMB Circulars).

Kevin Bruder, Maverik Center, presented Resolution 20-45 that would authorize the City

RESOLUTION 20-46: AUTHORIZE CHANGE ORDER NO. 2 TO THE
CONTRACT WITH ACME CONSTRUCTION FOR THE 2700 WEST
IMPROVEMENTS PROJECT
Dan Johnson, Public Works, presented Resolution 20-46 that would authorize change order
No. 2 to the Contract with Acme Construction for the 2700 West Improvements Project.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as

follows:

       Acme Construction is currently improving 2700 West, between 3500 South and
       4100 South. This section of road is the primary access for the Valley Fair Mall,
       Fairbourne Station and City Hall, and is a very important transportation facility to
       the city.

       The existing pavement condition is very poor, and as part of the work on the project,
       Acme is contracted to rotomill 4-inches of existing asphalt and repave with 4-inches
       of new asphalt.

       Due to the high-profile nature of this arterial, the Public Works Department is
       proposing to change the pavement design and include fiber reinforced asphalt
       pavement in the top two inches of asphalt.

       The addition of aramid fiber reinforced asphalt will help to reduce reflective
       cracking through the asphalt surface by increasing the tensile strength in the upper
       pavement layer. This change will add pavement life to this important segment of
       roadway.

Councilmember Buhler asked if the change in product requires a different contractor for
installation. Dan replied no. He indicated that the product is stickier and flows a little
differently, but the installation crew will experience nothing different. Councilmember
Buhler verified that there is no additional price increase related to installation. Dan replied
no. Councilmember Fitisemanu asked if this product will be suggested for all roads in the
City moving forward. Dan replied that he believes this will likely be more widely used. He
indicated that the City is evaluating cost for 4100 South but will see how the product
performs on 2700 West. Mayor Bigelow asked if the product will be inspected to ensure
potholes or other issues are avoided. Dan replied that the City can observe the product at
the plant and view the distribution of the product on the road but there is no formal testing
available at this time.

The City Council will consider Resolution 20-46 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
April 28, 2020 at 6:30 P.M.

B.     COUNCIL REPORTS
       COUNCILMEMBER FITISEMANU- MOBILE COVID TESTING
       Councilmember Fitisemanu stated that a mobile COVID-19 Testing unit was set up
       at Centennial Park. He stated that the City promoted this well and he was
       appreciative of the efforts of staff to collaborate with the University of Utah. Mayor
       Bigelow asked if the hours and days of testing will become more frequent.
       Councilmember Fitisemanu stated that the mobile unit is stretched thin but the
       University is working hard to get more testing in the City. He noted the two closest
       locations to West Valley and added that Wayne Pyle, the City Manager, has been
       proactive and supportive of all efforts.

       MAYOR BIGELOW- COVID-19
       Mayor Bigelow stated he got a request from another City to allow hair salons to
       reopen. He indicated that support was based on Utah County never having closed
       these businesses and still maintaining a lower infection rate. Mayor Bigelow stated
       that West Valley City is doing a lot of analysis and preparation that receives little
       notice or scrutiny. He stated that the City has continued to place high priority on
       public responders to ensure they are kept safe with both protective equipment and
       with safe practices to mitigate them from contracting COVID-19. As far as
       reopening businesses, staff is monitoring the number of those who have been
       diagnosed in the City and how this could impact everyday safety and life. Mayor
       Bigelow stated that the City is not necessarily following the same trends as other
       places in the State. Staff is monitoring the situation closely to identify when it
       would be in the best interest of the safety of the citizens of West Valley and the
       economic well-being of the City to open businesses. Mayor Bigelow stated that the
       data isn’t always clear but it’s important to balance all issues. Councilmember
       Buhler stated that Utah County is behind Salt Lake County at the moment, but this
       will likely increase. He indicated it is important that, as a political body, the City
       operates on data and not need at this time as it relates to COVID-19 and re-opening
       parts of the City. Councilmember Huynh asked how many cases there are in the
       City. Councilmember Fitisemanu provided numbers related to zip code and stated
       that the City has a higher population density so it’s expected to be higher. Paul
       stated the latest numbers indicated around 263 infected in West Valley City.
       Councilmember Huynh stated he would like this broken down by zip code.
