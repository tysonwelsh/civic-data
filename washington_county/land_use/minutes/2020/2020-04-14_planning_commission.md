Washington County Land Use Authority Meeting
                                 April 14, 2020

The regular meeting of the Washington County Planning Commission was conducted entirely via
electronic means through Zoom on Tuesday, April 14, 2020 from the Commission Chambers in the
Washington County Administration Building, 197 East Tabernacle, St. George, Utah. Chairman Kevin
Jones opened the meeting at 1:30 p.m. He led the Pledge of Allegiance and then explained how the
meeting would be conducted.

Commissioners present by Zoom: Brandon Anderson, Dan Briggs (Alternate), Shayne Durrant, Slade
Hughes, Mark Owen, Reed Scow (Alternate acting as a voting member), Doug Solstad, and Jon Walter.

Staff present: Scott Messel, Community Development Director; Brian Graf, Deputy County Attorney;
Doreen Bowers-Irons, Administrative Assistant; Cheyenne Bentley, Deputy Clerk at host location.
Todd Edwards on Zoom.


1.   RESOURCE MANAGEMENT PLAN ELEMENT OF THE GENERAL PLAN
AMENDMENT REVIEW. Review and recommend approval of the proposed amendments to the
Resource Management Plan Element of the Washington County General Plan.

Mr. Messel explained the process of the Resource Management Plan that took place. He reported the
commission reviewed the plan and held the public hearing during the March 10, 2020 meeting. At that
time, the commission was not comfortable approving the changes until they had time to review them.
They then tabled the item until the next meeting. Staff has brought the amendment request back for
review and a recommendation to the County Commission to approve the amendments to the Resource
Management Plan Element of the General Plan.

A brief discussion for clarification ensued and some minor corrections made, after which, Chairman
Jones called for a motion.

MOTION:       Commissioner Durrant MOVED to recommend approval of the changes to the
              Washington County Resource Management Plan as amended. Commissioner
              Solstad SECONDED. The motion carried unanimously.


2.   PUBLIC HEARING FOR THE KOLOB RESERVOIR ROAD DEDICATION. Review
and hold a public hearing for a proposed road dedication of the Kolob Reservoir Road to Washington
County. Parcels 1016-B, 1017-A, 1017-B, 1038-A, and 1043-HV. Washington County is the applicant.

Mr. Messel referred this request to Todd Edwards, County Engineer.

Mr. Edwards reported this is a housekeeping item. Washington County has been maintaining the Kolob
Reservoir Road for several years. Currently, the road is a prescriptive easement. The adjacent property
owners have requested to move the road in order to discourage illegal camping and utilize their
property better. Once the road is moved, the prescriptive use will go away so the county has requested
the road be formally dedicated to them. Currently, the 24-foot wide road meets the county’s needs. The
county is asking the planning commission to recommend acceptance of the Kolob Reservoir Road.

3.    PUBLIC HEARING/ZONE CHANGE. Review and hold a public hearing for a zone change
of Parcel DVA-2-A-83-DA from RE-40 (Residential Estate 40,000 sq.ft. minimum lot size) to RE-20
(Residential Estate 20,000 sq.ft. minimum lot size). The parcel is located at 1383 W. Diamond Valley
Dr. in Diamond Valley. The applicant is Justin Wanlass.

Mr. Messel stated Items 3 and 4 are for the same project and both are public hearings so he will explain
the proposal as one but each item would require separate motions. If the commission prefers they can
hold a public hearing for each one separately. The commission opted to combine the two items for the
public hearing.

Mr. Messel reported that Cory Morgan had been working with county staff on possible options to
subdivide his two parcels located east of the Diamond Valley Elementary School in Diamond Valley.
Unfortunately, Cory passed away. His son in-law, Justin Wanlass is moving forward with the 3-lot
subdivision plans. Parcel DVA-2-A-83-DA, which is Lot 83 of Diamond Valley Acres Phase 2
Subdivision. There is an existing single family dwelling on Lot 83. Lot 83 is approximately 1 acre in
size. Access to the existing single family dwelling on Parcel 7236-A-6-DA is from a driveway across
Lot 83. The proposed amended and extended subdivision will divide Parcel 7236-A-6-DA into 2 flag
lots. County code permits two flag lots on one flag stem access. The two flag lots are identified as Lot
83A and 83B. They are each approximately 6 acres in size. A drainage study has been requested for
Parcel 7236-A-6-DA before the subdivision is approved which, has not been submitted at this time.

A zone change is required to bring the proposed 3-lot subdivision into zoning compliance. It is proposed
to rezone Lot 83 from RE-40 (Residential Estate 40,000 square foot minimum lot size) zone to RE-20
(Residential Estate 20,000 square foot minimum lot size) zone. The primary purpose for rezoning this
lot is to meet the street frontage requirement.




Lots 83A and 83B meets the zoning standards of the existing A-5 (Agricultural 5 acre minimum lot size)
zone and will not change.

Recommendation:

Staff reviewed the proposed zone change and recommends approval based on the following findings:
    1. The public hearing for the zone change was properly noticed.
    2. The proposed zone change is in harmony with the General Plan.
    3. The zone change must be approved for the proposed subdivision to meet the applicable county
        codes.

Commissioner Owens questioned the need to rezone Parcel DVA-2-A-83-DA. Mr. Messel explained
that the reason was to allow for the access road to the back parcels.

The commission had additional clarification questions regarding the existing properties and dwellings
currently on the parcels.

Chairman Jones opened the public hearing for both Items 3 and 4.

Justin Wanless, applicant and Dave Howard, Project Engineer, had questions for clarification regarding
the requested drainage study. They will work with Todd Edwards on that request.

There were no other comments for or against the project.

Chairman Jones closed the public hearing for both Items 3 and 4.

5.   CONCEPT PLAN DEVELOPMENT PRESENTATION. Presentation and review of Pine
Valley Legacy a proposed planned development to be located in Pine Valley. The applicant is Pine
Valley Legacy, LLC/Brian Thiriot.

Mr. Messel provided the background of the PDRRGF (Planned Development Rural Recreation
Grounds and Facilities) zone. He reported that Pine Valley Legacy, LLC is proposing a PD for a Rural
Recreation Ground and Facilities on Parcel 6199-D-1 in Pine Valley. The first step in the Planned
Development application process is a review of the concept plan for the proposed development. He
explained the phases of approval for this type of use. He pointed out that this is concept plan only and
no action from the planning commission is required. He provided a power point presentation of the
submitted documentation and asked for any feedback the commission would like to give.

Commissioner Owens stated in reviewing the current Washington County General Plan, which he
cited, this proposed plan conflicts with the Pine Valley General Plan. He opposed the proposal, as he
did not believe it to fit into the Pine Valley Community General Plan. He pointed out some of the issues
in conflict with the plan.

A discussion addressing the general plan updates, vacation rental areas, the different types of uses for
tourism, and appropriate locations for rural recreation grounds and facility areas within the county
ensued.

Commissioner Hughes pointed out that the property is located near the Forest Service Boundary.

Commissioner Walter commented on the density of these types of uses coming into the county. The
number of proposed units and their sizes along with the different types of amenities coming into the
county, in his opinion, did not meet the intent of the Rural Recreation Grounds and Facility ordinance.
The applicant and others with similar plans should consider ways to mitigate the impact to the area.
Commissioner Hughes pointed out that the county relies a lot on tourism. With these types of facilities
coming in, there is no other avenue for them except under that PD zone. He asked where else would
they belong.

The applicant was not in attendance to answer questions. The commission had several additional
comments and questions. After a lengthy discussion and clarification, they provided feedback to give
to the applicant. Mr. Messel explained some of the requirements the applicant will need to meet in
order to proceed. He reported the applicant might bring this project back in the future.
