         The purpose of the Joint Work Session was to review the items listed on the agenda for the City Council and
Redevelopment Agency meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.; receive an update regarding the Zone Ogden project; discuss
proposed amendments to Ogden Municipal Code Title 8; discuss Arts Grants Ordinance amendments; discuss Council/Board
business; and hear Council/Board comments.

Agenda Review
        Chair White and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council and
Redevelopment Agency meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Update – Zone Ogden
         Senior Planner Rypien explained that the purpose of the Zone Ogden project is to update the City’s development codes;
the project began in summer of 2023 and is currently in the drafting stage and Planning staff are aided by consultants from the
firm Logan Simpson. The Council previously received an update on Zone Ogden during the work sessions on March 4 and July 8,
2025. This is another opportunity for the Council to review the draft chapters of the new development code and to ask questions
and provide input as the code is drafted. Mr. Rypien used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide a review of the
following draft chapters:
              • Special Use Standards, including Residential Daycare, Accessory Dwelling Units, Telecommunications
                  Facilities, etc.
              • Development Standards, including standards related to landscaping, fencing, lighting, parking, etc.
         For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Throughout his presentation, Mr. Rypien discussed the proposed updates with the Council regarding form-based zoning
guidelines; design guidelines for commercial businesses on 25th Street; relaxing standards for accessory dwelling units; historic
preservation of properties and streamlining the Landmarks Commission application process; enforcement of home occupation
regulations; and regulations for temporary signs and permanent signs.

Proposed Ogden Municipal Code Title 8 Amendments
          Mayor Nadolski discussed his prioritization of the Ogden Airport since his election as Ogden’s Mayor; he noted
Administration is proposing to make significant changes to the City’s Title 8 Airport Code and to the City’s Administrative Code
related to airport operations. Ogden City has been working for years to make improvements to the Ogden-Hinckley Airport, to
bring it in line with industry standards, and to create a thriving airport environment that would attract aeronautical industry.
Recent efforts have included efforts to attract commercial air service, updating Title 8 to address lease terms and fees, adopting an
airport master plan, updating the City’s General Plan related to airport operations, and hiring professional, qualified airport
management. Partly spurred by an audit report requested by the Utah State Legislature, the Administration is proposing to take the
next steps in improving airport operations and regulations by rewriting the City Code and Administrative Code to better reflect
industry best practices and legal standards. This effort is proposed to take place over several phases as the code updates and
rewrites can be processed.
            Community and Economic Development Executive Director Johnson and Airport Director Condie introduced the topic
and also introduced Ryan Leick of Ascension Group Partners, who has helped to formulate the primary management documents
for the Airport. Mr. Johnson, Mr. Condie, and Mr. Leick used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to review Administration’s
strategic realignment of operations requirements proposal for the Ogden Airport; they discussed the findings of the 2024 State
Performance Audit pertaining to the Airport, which identified a need for the following:
                • Ensure clear governance – operational clarity
                • Improve operational efficiency
                • Align management practices with aviation industry best practices
                • Improve transparency and accountability
                • Define strategic focus to align with City vision/values
            The proposal is to realign the current structure of the Municipal Code over a six-month period; most of the regulations
dealing with the Airport are located in Title 8 and some are under the City’s Administrative Policy Title 11. If the proposal is
approved, the primary airport operational ordinance will be codified under Title 8. Ogden Airport’s economic impact is reported at
$328.1 million annually. This generates $12.9 million in State and local tax revenue, $118.6 million in annual payroll, and 2,626
jobs. The Legislative Audit of the Airport found a $0.89 million annual operating deficit since 2006. The Airport is not required to
be revenue neutral and may not be able to become revenue neutral as it is a primary non-hub airport. The Master Plan for the
Airport should be bolstered by best practices, specifically mentioned minimum standards, and rules and regulations. Mr. Leick
discussed the mission and vision statements for the Airport, the purpose of the original 2020 Airport Master Plan, and the layout
of the Airport identified in the Strategic Plan. He also summarized the history of the Ogden Airport dating back to 2005 and
emphasized adopting new minimum standards for the Airport is important prior to starting any airport development. He also
discussed grant funding assurances, Federal Aviation Authority (FAA) orders that apply to the Ogden Airport, and primary
management compliance documents. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
            Discussion among the Council, Mr. Leick, and other representatives of Ascension Group Partners centered on the reason
that it is necessary to remove the standards and requirements for the Airport from the City Code and into an administrative policy
document and the level of detail for the new proposed administrative policy; the Council concluded they need more time to
consider the matter and would like an opportunity for more discussion of the proposal. Council Assistant Executive Director
Symes indicated Council staff have added two agenda items to the calendar for August to give the Council time for additional
discussion before action is requested by Administration.
