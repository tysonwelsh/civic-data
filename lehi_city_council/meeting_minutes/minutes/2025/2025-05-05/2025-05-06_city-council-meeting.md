2. SHAR affordable Housing Overlay Discussion- Planning Department
   Brittney Harris presented an update on the SHAR Ordinance. The discussion explored various
   approaches to creating more accessible housing, ranging from engineering's proposal of
   allowing development everywhere with a 50% density increase to alternative models that could
   make housing more attainable. Presenters examined specific subdivision examples, such as
   Grandeur Estates and Longhorn Meadows, to illustrate potential implementation strategies. Key
   considerations included managing utility impacts, maintaining reasonable lot sizes, and creating
   financially viable options for developers. Notably, feedback from Flagship Homes revealed
   significant challenges in the current affordable housing landscape, suggesting that hitting a
   $400,000 price point might require innovative solutions like public-private partnerships or
   alternative ownership models. The presentation aimed to align council members' perspectives,
   emphasizing the need to balance developer interests, community needs, and practical economic
   constraints in creating an effective affordable housing strategy for Lehi City.

   Councilor Albrecht clarified that it is unlikely that the city will find a developer who wants to
   do an entire development under the SHAR zone, the lots will likely be mixed into larger
   developments, which will make them easier to manage. Councilor Newall mentioned that this
   is the method that Weber County used with JD ranch. She also thinks that deeds should be
   restricted indefinitely. The most important aspect in her opinion is creating new homeowners
   and allowing them that equity that they need to get into better homes later.

   developers get projects approved by city council, because he wants to ensure that the city’s
   goals are made. He admits that developers will have to work hard to find opportunities, but he
   thinks that that is an acceptable ask from the city. He likes the hybrid approach.

Chief Jeremy Craft spoke for the fire department. Councilor Newall asked how often Lehi fire
department is being called out to other cities. Chief Craft doesn’t have the numbers for this year,
but last year it happened 60 times. There has been an increased need in Saratoga Springs, and Lehi
has been covering their 911 calls while they are otherwise occupied. Most of the help Lehi City
receives is for larger fires. The city is helping more than they are receiving help currently.
Councilor Albrecht asked if other cities are getting revenue from ambulance calls while Lehi is
helping with other emergency calls. Councilor Hancock asked if it would be less of a burden on
the city if we handled other city’s interfacility transport rather than answering calls the way the
department currently is. Chief Craft said that it would likely be better for the city financially, but
that it likely would cause a steep drop in morale as personnel don’t enjoy them. Councilor Stallings
asked how the department would manage without an assistant fire chief. Chief Craft said he will
be asking for it again next year. The 25% increase with the opening of the new station is proving
to be more work than expected. The department can get by, it just makes it less progressive.

Council took a brief break at 6:05 to get refreshments.

presented a list of equipment the city would need to do it on their own, as contractors who take
care of the work are rare and hard to schedule. Councilor Stallings asked if the city could hire out
the equipment once it was purchased. Marchbanks said that they could, but that they would want
to outsource our labor with it to ensure that the equipment is used properly. He also put together
the costs for outsourcing the project. This is a much more expensive method. Councilor Hancock
asked what fire station would be responding to a phragmite fire. Station 84 would take the call and
would have a five minute response time. Access into the area is difficult for the fire department

price had increased by the time the lanes were put in, and the city had to put in pylons instead.

pedestrian bridges. Councilor Newall mentioned that the curbing should be the first priority. She

but Lehi City has placed posts around the corner. Mayor Johnson asked if the turning radius was
