 PC 22-050 Casa Cliffside-Short Term Rental [Conditional Use Permit] Adam KS Tripp/Steven
 & Jessica Lucero, authorized agent/owner are requesting a Conditional Use Permit to rent out a
 five-bedroom home as a short-term rental located at 93 South 1250 East in the Traditional
 Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) zone; TIN 07-160-0020 (Wilson Neighborhood).


STAFF: T. Rice reviewed the request for a Conditional Use Permit to rent out the entire five-bedroom
home as a short-term rental located at 93 South 1250 East in the Wilson Neighborhood. The
maximum occupancy for this rental is two per bedroom plus an additional two for a total of 12
occupants. The proposed parking plan shows five parking stalls, two in the garage and three in the
driveway. As proposed, the parking stalls comply with the requirements in the Land Development


Code. There have been eight comments received all against having a short-term rental in the
neighborhood.

PROPONENT: Adam Trip, real estate agent representing the current owner and future owner of the
home, said that the buyer contacted him to find a property that they could occupy in the summer and
use as a short-term rental in the winter while they are not here.

PUBLIC: Joy Gallagher Shaw, who lives in the cul-de-sac stated that six children live in the house
across the street. She talked about how the cul-de-sac sits on a blind corner and drivers who do not
live there tend to speed around the corner and into their cul-de-sac causing a safety issue for the
kids. She spoke about how they chose this location and her home as an investment in a community
and a safe place. Airbnb’s increase crime in communities and break down the fabric of a
neighborhood that watches out for each other. She spoke about the neighbors and how they are
invested in their community, and how renters are not. You are setting a precedence by allowing
short-term rentals and it will have long-term consequences on neighborhoods.

Shelia Raymond, who lives two houses from the proposed rental read a letter that she submitted to
the commission on September 2, 2022.

Tawni Hyde, a neighbor said they had a short-term rental in the neighborhood, and the neighbors
are the ones who had to report to the city if they were not following the rules. She does not want to
have to police the area again.

Karen Salinas, who lives across the street asked how this will benefit their neighborhood. She asked
who would want to purchase a home that is next to an Airbnb. She asked the commission to protect
their neighborhood, the residents, and their right to privacy, comfort, and safety.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Peterson stated that the challenge the commission faces is they are
responsible to follow what the Land Development Code allows and not preferences. There have not
been any issues presented by staff that would prevent them from approving this project even if it is
not liked.

Commissioner Doutre said that looking over the public notice that was mailed out to the neighbors
regarding this project, she wondered if it could be updated to educate more on what the code allows
and where residents can find more information.

Commissioner Peterson stated that the City Council has the authority to make changes to the code
and advised that any concerns should be brought up with the Council.

Commissioner Guth mentioned that the process to update the code on short-term rentals was the
time for the public to speak out about the changes.

MOTION: Commissioner Croshaw moved to conditionally approve a Conditional Use Permit for
PC 22-050 with the conditions of approval and findings for approval as listed below. Commissioner
Guth seconded the motion.

CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL
1. All standard conditions of approval will be recorded with the Conditional Use Permit and are
   available in the Community Development Department.
2. This Conditional Use Permit authorizes a five (5) bedroom short-term rental occupying the full
   single-family home.


3. This Conditional Use Permit authorizes two (2) people per bedroom with two (2) additional
    individuals for a maximum of twelve (12) occupants in the STR.
4. A minimum of five (5) parking stalls are required for the STR. All parking areas shall be located
    off-street, outside the front and corner yard setback and paved with an approved hard surface.
    The 5 total parking stalls shall meet minimum LDC dimensions (9x18) per stall and a minimum
    12-foot-wide driveway.
5. Parking of recreational vehicles, trailers, boats, ATVs or similar vehicles associated with the
    STR shall be legally parked on-site and are not permitted to be parked on-street.
6. Rental contracts for the STR shall be for 30 days or less.
7. Rental contracts shall not be offered to the same individual, group, or party for more than two
    consecutive rental contracts in a six (6) month period. The owner shall maintain rental records
    available for review and inspection by Logan City.
8. Quiet times shall comply with the City noise ordinance of 10 pm to 8am.
9. A Logan City building/fire inspection is required which may result in building remodeling and
    building permits prior to the issuance of a business license.
10. A Logan City business license is required before a rental contract can be issued.
11. The STR shall comply with local and state Building, Health, Fire, Safety, Maintenance and
    Nuisance Codes prior to operating.
12. Exterior lighting shall be concealed source, down-cast and reviewed and approved prior to the
    issuance of a building permit and shall comply with current LDC regulations.
13. No signs are approved with this Conditional Use Permit. All signage shall be approved and
    permitted by staff in accordance with the Land Development Code.
14. No fences are approved with this Conditional Use Permit. All fences shall be approved and
    permitted by staff in accordance with the Land Development Code.

FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL
1. The proposed project conforms to the requirements of the Land Development Code and is listed
   as a conditional use in the Use Table.
2. This STR meets the City’s STR density and location parameters as specified in Chapter
   17.37.130.
3. As conditioned the use is compatible with the surrounding land uses and will not interfere with
   the use and enjoyment of adjoining or area properties.
4. The streets providing access and other infrastructure to the subject property have adequate
   capacities and level of service for the conditional use.
5. The Planning Commission has substantiated the findings for a Conditional Use Permit in Chapter
   17.42.050.

Moved R. Croshaw                   Seconded: J. Guth                       Approved: 5-0

Yea: Heare, Croshaw, Doutre, Guth, Peterson            Nay:            Abstain:

PC 22-053 Black Rifle Coffee Company [Design Review Permit] Julianne McGee/Logan Main &
4th Pads LLC, authorized agent/owner are requesting a Design Review Permit to construct a new
2,718 square foot coffee shop with a drive-thru located at 398 North Main Street in the Town Center
One (TC-1) zone; TIN 06-043-0017; -0015. (Adams Neighborhood).


STAFF: T. Rice reviewed the request for a Design Review Permit to build a new coffee shop with a
drive-thru, outdoor dining patio, and dog park located at 398 North Main Street in the TC-1 Zone.
Drive-Thru restaurants are allowed in the TC-1 Zone; however, the zone is intended for more dense
urban development patterns, with minimal setbacks and tall buildings up to 80 feet in height, and
dense housing, up to 70 units per acre with up to 100% lot coverage.

The building frontage in the TC-1 Zone along Main Street and 400 North is required to be at 75% as
per the Land Development Code (LDC). As proposed the project is at 55% frontage along 400 North
and 23% frontage along Main Street. To meet the LDC requirements, the building length would have
to increase by 29 feet along 400 North, and approximately 90 feet along Main Street.

The LDC requires a setback of zero to five feet in the TC-1 Zone. The proposal shows a setback of
approximately 20 feet along 400 North and a setback of approximately 50 feet along Main Street.
The LDC establishes a maximum lot coverage of 100% in the TC-1 Zone with no minimum. As
proposed the 2,718-square-foot building on a .63-acre lot (27,442 square feet), has a lot coverage
of 10%, which complies with the LDC although is substantially less than what is intended for the TC-
1 Zone.

The two existing accesses to this property from Main Street and 400 North are proposed for closure
and new access is being proposed through a 12-foot shared access with the adjoining property to
the south. This will have to be approved by UDOT

To compensate for the lack of building frontage along Main Street, the applicant has proposed
installing a three-foot tall rock wall along the west side of the project to make up for the lack of building
frontage and massing. Staff does not feel that this meets the intent of the TC-1 Zone.


The LDC requires transparency on the ground floor of street facing facades to be at 60% in the TC-
1 Zone. As proposed the project is at 50% transparency on the west façade, adjacent to Main Street,
and approximately 35% on the north façade, adjacent to 400 North.

The Planning Commission can approve a 10% adjustment in the setbacks, frontage, and lot
coverage.

Commissioner Peterson asked if the adjustment on the frontage is more than 10%. T. Rice said that
what the applicant is proposing is more than a 10% reduction.

PROPONENT: Nicole Kastern, Architectural Design Manager with Black Rifle, spoke about how the
Black Rifle company was founded by a veteran who lives in Utah. She spoke about the adjustments
that need to be made to their design to meet code requirements and said that they are willing to work
with the city to make those changes. They would like to add large columns along the street-facing
sides of the building to meet the massing requirement. They are also planning on adding a
monument to honor veterans and first responders.

Joshua H., Architect for Black Rifle, said that one of the company’s key focuses is honoring local
veterans and first responders, and giving back to the community by finding charitable causes and
donations for local fire departments, police departments, and veterans. To increase the building’s
frontage, they can increase the size of canopies along the street facades and add a large shade
pergola that would extend out towards Main Street. He spoke about enlarging the rock wall so the
community can install plaques or add some type of memorial to honor local service workers or military
veterans.

PUBLIC: Chris Wilson, owner of Wilson properties to the south, said that their proposed access,
which is shared with his property is going to congest his property with traffic. This shared access
needs to remain open so traffic can flow to the east past the neighboring businesses to get out onto
400 North or 100 East.

Chad Ricks, an Employee of Wilson Motor, spoke about the traffic issue and how it is already difficult
to access Main Street, at times taking 11 minutes to turn right onto Main Street. Adding more traffic
to use this access will make it much worse.

Jeannie Simmonds said that the gas station that was previously at this location is approximately the
same size as this proposed building. This vacant corner has the opportunity to reflect the intention
of the TC-1 Zone, but the proposed project does not. It is difficult to approve a project based on
suggested changes to the proposed design. This is not what she hoped the TC-1 Zone would
become.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Heare said this project does not fit the vision of the TC-1 Zone. The
proposed access is not a good location, it will create a bigger traffic concern, and has not been
approved by UDOT. He does not feel comfortable approving this design

Commissioner Doutre said that this design does not fit on this corner, their proposed numbers show
that it does not fit in this zone. This is one of the few locations that could house what the TC-1 Zone
was intended for. It would be great to see something here that has commercial space on the ground
floor and housing units. She is okay with reviewing changes to this plan but doesn’t feel that it will
meet the intent of the zone without significant changes.


Commissioner Croshaw said that once they know what UDOT will allow they can then design the
building around it. He asked about the space between the proposed drive and the building to the
southeast and how traffic will flow between the buildings.

Commissioner Lucero asked about the UDOT process and where that is. T. Rice said as of today
the applicant did not know where they were in the process.

Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director said the access issue has to be worked out with
UDOT first then their design can be adjusted to meet the LDC.

Commissioner Guth said that the size of the proposed development is so far from what is required
in the TC-1 Zone. This is the most prominent site in Logan today and has more traffic flow than any
other site in the city. This site should have three or four businesses of this size and create a frontage,
mass, and height that supports and celebrates this corner.

Commissioner Peterson said many items need to be addressed by the applicant once they receive
approval for access from UDOT.

Commissioner Lucero asked if there would need to be a formal agreement in place for the shared
access.

M. DeSimone said that the 12-foot wide right-away access between the properties is not sufficient
for commercial access. If the access is going to extend onto Wilson’s property, they would need an
agreement in place.

Commissioner Lucero said this is an important and prominent corner in the city, it is a bridge from
the city to the university. The proposed project does not meet the intent of the TC-1 Zone with the
lack of massing, a half wall does not accomplish the mass that is needed. As conditioned the project
would meet the LDC requirements; however, it is not typical to approve a project that is proposed
this far off from what is required.
