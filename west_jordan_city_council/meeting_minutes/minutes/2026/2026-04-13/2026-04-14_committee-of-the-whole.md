 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
     a. Discussion on Future Land Uses at Rodeo Arena Site
        Council Member Shelton said he took James Brainard, former Mayor of Carmel, Indiana on
        a tour of the Rodeo Arena and surrounding area earlier in the year, and Mr. Brainard had
        been excited to see the amount of undeveloped land in what he perceived as the city center
        area. Council Member Shelton explained that the designated City Center area was intended
        to be east of Redwood Road, and Mr. Brainard had encouraged him to look wholistically.
        Mr. Brainard put the City in contact with Jeff Speck, a prominent urban planner/designer
        (designer of Utah City), who suggested moving the Rodeo Arena to a different location to
        put the current rodeo space to better, higher use than a few times each year.

         City Administrator Korban Lee noted that Mr. Speck pointed out the Rodeo Arena was a
         low-investment land use on high-investment land surrounded by many high-investment
         institutional anchors. Mr. Speck suggested starting the “downtown” around the existing
         institutional anchors on City-owned land, and eventually growing it across the street. Mr.
         Lee asked the Council if it was a concept they were interested in studying. He explained that
         such a study would be very different from the study previously discussed by the Council
         and staff concerning the Rodeo Arena.

         Council Member Whitelock suggested the land should first be checked for any kind of deed
         restriction or deed lock. She pointed out the City already had a Parks Master Plan. Council
         Member Whitelock felt the City would need to replace park space for the east side of the
         City as well as move the Rodeo Arena. She spoke of potential impact to residential
         neighbors, and believed there were many factors to consider with such an idea. Mayor

         surface parking that could be viewed as a blank canvas for development. He suggested a

     b. Discussion of the Upcoming 2026 Resident Survey (via Y2 Analytics)
        Council Office Director Alan Anderson said the Council had funded a survey of the
        community for the last three years. He introduced Kyrene Gibb and Ethan Meldrum with
        Y2 Analytics, and said there would be a 6–7-week process to prepare the 2026 survey. Mr.
        Anderson asked the Council to discuss the type of questions they wanted included in the
        survey. Based on the discussion of the previous agenda item, Ms. Gibb suggested including
        questions related to what residents would want to see in a gathering space, what types of
        events they would want, and what residents would envision to create a sense of downtown.
        She said timely questions specific to the 2025 survey had centered around City
        beautification and Code Enforcement.

         Ms. Gibbs answered questions about survey data gathered over time. Council Member
         Harris suggested questions to gather citizen opinion on water usage, perhaps focusing on
         willingness to change or opinion of how the City was addressing water conservation.
         Council Member Whitelock suggested adding the Rodeo Arena to the hundred-dollar
         question, or a different question that would gauge support for improvements to rodeo
         facilities. Chair Bedore said he would support a question specifically about the Rodeo Arena
         and including the Rodeo in the hundred-dollar question. Chair Bedore said he knew some
         residents were not aware that the annual rodeo and carnival were part of the same City
         celebration.

         Council Member Whitelock suggested removing questions specific to Code Enforcement
         that had been included in the 2025 survey. Ms. Gibb said she had a good sense of the
         direction the Council wanted. The Council and staff discussed willingness-to-pay questions.
         Ms. Gibb advised that a question including a dollar amount for an average household and a
         list of possible areas for investment would provide the most usable information. Chair
         Bedore said he liked follow-up questions that would help the Council better understand
         dissatisfied or negative responses. Ms. Gibb thanked the Council for the discussion.

     c. Discussion of Advice and Consent Process
        Council Office Director Alan Anderson presented proposed amendments to clean up and
        update the Advice and Consent Process in City Code and Council Rules. The proposed draft
        included the following language in Code Section 2-1-3: Appointments of Committee

         Members, “The City Council may elect to meet with appointees prior to providing advice
         and consent. Such meetings shall follow the procedures outlined in Council Rules.” The
         following new Section 3.9: Advice & Consent for Mayoral Appointments was proposed for
         Council Rules:

             Certain appointed positions within the City may only be filled with the consent of
             the City Council. The following procedures shall apply in such situations:
                 A. Council Members may meet with the appointee together during an arranged
                    appointment or as part of an official council meeting.
                 B. A vote on consent for the appointment shall be scheduled as soon as possible
                    after the arranged meeting.
                 C. The Council packet shall contain the information referenced in Council
                    Policies and Procedures Appendix A – Item 3(b)(iii).
                 D. Council Members may grant or withhold advice and consent to appointed
                    positions.

         Mayor Burton recommended removing the words “as soon as possible” from subsection B.
         Mr. Lee and Chair Bedore expressed agreement. Council Member Whitelock expressed
         concern that no language was included requiring that information be provided to the
         Council in their meeting packet on Friday prior to the appropriate Council meeting. Chair
         Bedore said he agreed with the concern. Mr. Anderson said staff had discussed arranging
         an opportunity for the Council to meet individuals before the meeting packets were even
         put together. He said language could be added.

         Council Member Shelton expressed concern with the potential for two weeks to pass
         between the Council being introduced to an individual, and providing advice and consent
         for appointment at the next Council meeting. Mr. Lee pointed out the initial meeting with
         the Council could take place on a Thursday or Friday prior to the Council meeting in which
         advice and consent would be granted. A majority of the Council indicated support. Mr. Lee
         said it was his preference for the initial meeting between the Council and a candidate to not
         take place in public meeting so the individual’s current position was not jeopardized. He
         said staff tried to be consistent with explaining the advice and consent process to
         candidates.

         Council Member Whitelock emphasized that expectations should be added to Code and
         Council Rules. Senior Assistant City Attorney Patrick Boice said the goal was to balance the
         flexibility aspect with some expectation of consistency, and said he would be happy to draft
         language. Council Member Whitelock said she remembered with the previous form of
         government the Council would meet in a closed session with a candidate for discussion and
         interview, with advice and consent two weeks later at the next Council meeting. She said
         she wanted the time to be able to consider and decide, and said she did not think two weeks
         was too long. Council Member Shelton repeated his concern for two weeks between the
         introduction and consent.

         Responding to a question from Council Member Harris, Mr. Anderson said the CAO,
         department heads, statutory officers, and Planning Commissioners were all appointed by
         advice and consent of the Council. Mr. Anderson said the two weeks had not been a problem

      d. Presentation of Proposed Changes to Wildland Urban Interface Map
         West Jordan Fire Chief Derek Maxfield presented proposed changes to the Wildland Urban
         Interface (WUI) Map to incorporate requirements of House Bill 41 (2026). Members of the
         Council expressed frustration with the pixelation of the map provided by the State. Council
         Member Jacob asked if future buildout would affect the lines on the map. Staff responded
         that buildout may affect the State’s formula. Chief Maxfield answered questions from the
         Council about WUI-related homeowner insurance costs.

          Council Member Jacob said the request from the State for the City Council to provide advice
          and consent for the WUI Map felt perfunctionary. Mr. Boice explained the State would use
          the State map to assess fees, and the State recommended that the City map reflect State map
          determinations. Chief Maxfield said he would forward a link to the Council for the WUI Map
          on the east side of the valley.
