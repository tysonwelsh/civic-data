      Residence at 5257 South 3600 West in Taylorsville, Utah; File No: 351- 24- SI-

      Planner




      Ms. Bergeson explained that the applicant represented Cold Spring Real Estate
Holding, LLC which owned the single- family residence in question. A rental license was
issued for the home in June 2023. At that time, the applicant                         was informed    that a single-

family home           could    house         one   family of related   individuals,   or   up to four ( 4) unrelated
adults. The applicant informed staff that she was working with the Department of Health

and Human Services to certify the home as a recovery residence in order to provide
support and housing to persons recovering from substance use disorder. Substance use
disorder was recognized as a disability and therefore was subject to standards for
residential facilities for persons with a disability in Taylorsville Municipal Code§ 13. 11. 070.
The applicant had submitted the required documents with a reasonable accommodation
request that the limit of unrelated individuals residing in the home be increased from four
 4) to nine ( 9). Staff recommended that number be reduced by one to eight ( 8).


The subject property was surrounded by single- family residence zoning R- 1- 8 on the
south and east sides, R- 1- 7 on the west side, and single- family rental R- 1- 8 on the north.
The application did not have a requirement for a public notice or public hearing.

Ms. Bergeson mentioned that there were some current code enforcement issues on the
property which would need to be addressed before a business license could be issued.

Chair Wilkey asked if the home had already been operating as a recovery residence. Ms.
Bergeson       indicated     the applicant    could address         that but that she was aware that at times

since the home was purchased in 2023, there had been more then four adults living there.

The Chair then asked if there had been a history of emergency services responding to
the property and Ms. Bergeson responded that there was a record of only one police call
 a fight) at the home.


Commissioner           Willardson    felt the neighbors       had the right to weigh    in on the application.
Ms.       Bergeson       pointed     out   that  public  hearings         weren' t typically   required   when
nondisabled          individuals   moved    into a home.


Commissioner Young wondered if the 8- 9 residents would each have a vehicle; he also
asked how the city would enforce the number of residents. Ms. Bergeson indicated that
residents could have a car as anyone could although they were likely to reside there on
a more short- term basis. Enforcement issues would be up to the code enforcement

division, but the state would be doing regular inspections as well.

Mr. Spung interjected that the City would have the option of revoking the business license
if the number         of occupants    was not adhered         to.


Chair Wilkey invited the applicant to step forward and address the Commission.

Cate Ockey introduced herself and said she would welcome regular inspections to satisfy
the city that no more than the approved number of residents were living in the home. She
explained that she had very strict guidelines for who was eligible to reside at this facility.
They had to have one year of sobriety and be employed. The residents received help
learninghow to handle things that theywere typically

Commissioner Russell: Asked if both males and females would reside in the home and if
there was an age requirement. Ms. Ockey responded that this was a male- only facility,
and all residents had to be 18 or over— able to legally enter into a rental contract.


Commissioner Wright inquired about the success rate for her facility. Commissioner
Willardson commented that he had heard that drug rehabilitation facilities only a less than
40% success rate. Ms. Ockey explained that it completely depended upon the individual

program. A sober living program would have a different rate than a transitional housing
program, for example, but both fell under the umbrella of a " recovery residence."

Commissioner       Willardson       asked   Ms.   Ockey if she wanted to allow more people to
increase cash flow or if it was for good will. She responded, " both." He expressed               concern
that many of the residents would need to park their cars on the street.

Commissioner Wendel wished to focus on what Ms. Ockey was attempting to accomplish.
She said, " We certainly want to elevate the lives of everyone in our community" and we' re

not doing that if they come out of a treatment program and end up on the Jordan River
Pkwy because there is nowhere else for them to go. Substance abuse existed in each
and every neighborhood, and for us to deny that or think that everyone is getting the help
they need is wrong. She also pointed out that we do not get to pick our neighbors. Housing
laws do not allow us to discriminate. She preferred to give people an opportunity to be
successful and felt that we as a community have an obligation to do that. Based on the
available square footage, she felt eight people was more reasonable than nine. She would
even be willing to talk about one person per bedroom in the home.

In response to the question about her success rate, Ms. Ockey said that of 24 people she
had helped in 2. 5 years ( including at other locations),         three had relapsed.


Commissioner Russell was interested in the cost to rent a single bedroom in this particular
home. She responded          that it was $ 675 for a shared       bedroom,   or $ 800   for a single.   The

State pays more if she were to accept vouchers but then she would have to accept
anyone/ everyone, and she prefers to be more selective.

Commissioner Willardson            asked    if there   were   windows   in each   bedroom.    Ms. Ockey
responded     in the affirmative.


Commissioner Wright asked Ms. Ockey to address how state and federal guidelines
influenced her program. She explained that she was regulated by both the Department of
Health and Human Services and Housing and Urban Development. She said her only
dealings with the federal government involved a stream of federal parolees and the rules
and regulations     at that level were much stricter.


Ms. Ockey explained that one of her requirements was that her residents give back to the
community. She therefore required that they did community service and had done so in
Taylorsville. They also had responsibilities at the home which rotated monthly.

Commissioner Wright asked Ms. Ockey about the inoperable vehicle reported by code
enforcement. She explained that although it ran, the owner needed to get some lug nuts
before he could replace the tires.


Commissioner Wright noted on Google Earth that it appeared there were young children
living in the neighborhood. He wondered how that worked with these residents. She
responded that some of her residents also had children and even though they could not
reside at the home, they were allowed to visit. The children of one resident played
regularly with children in the neighborhood. He was looking for other accommodations so
that his kids could live with him           since   he had   partial custody.   The residents looked rough
around the edges but had big hearts and loved their families very much.

Like    Commissioner          Willardson,    Chair Wilkey was concerned that there would not be
sufficient parking for all the residents. Ms. Ockey said there were plans for RV parking
which,         when    finished,   would allow an additional three vehicles to be parked on the
property. She also pointed out that at any given time, there were typically two residents
who did not have cars.


In response to an inquiry about men living in such close quarters, Ms. Ockey said she
had zero tolerance for fighting. It did not matter who started it or who finished it. If you
were in a fight, you could no longer live there. Regarding the police call mentioned earlier
by Ms. Bergeson, Ms. Ockey was the one who had called the police.

Commissioner Russell asked about the lease agreement and Ms. Ockey explained that
the minimum stay was a 90- day lease with a month- to- month option thereafter. One
exception is a gentleman who was 15 years sober, and he had been there quite a bit
longer. She had promised Ms. Bergeson that as residents moved out, she would not fill
their spots unless/ until she had approval from the city. But she was not willing to put
anyone out on the street.


Commissioner McElreath commented that it appeared that according to city code, they
were required to allow eight occupants unless they had good justification to decide
otherwise.            Commissioner Wendel said she believed that was based on the square
footage and the Fair Housing Act. Mr. Spung explained there was a great deal of case
law that put the number in the 6- 8- person range for a single- family dwelling but it all
depended on the specific circumstances. Staff recommended a maximum of eight. He
described some of the reasoning behind the FHA.

 Commissioner McElreath was comfortable with staff' s recommendation of eight residents,
 but he wished someone from the legal department was present to consult. Commissioner
Wendel agreed.


 Commissioner Willardson did not feel the property was suitable for that many people and
 he felt the request was based on a desire to increase profits.
