         The purpose of the Joint Work Session was to review the agenda for the City Council and Special Redevelopment
Agency meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.; discuss the proposed Ogden City Fiscal Year 2025-2026 Budget; discuss a
proposed partial street vacation at 331 Stewart Drive in Business Depot Ogden; discuss a proposed zone text amendment
pertaining to landscape requirements for new single-family dwellings; discuss Council/Board business; and hear Council/Board
comments.

Agenda Review
        Chair White and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Proposed Ogden City Fiscal Year 2025–2026 Budget
          Council Assistant Executive Director Symes reported the City has received the certified tax rate from Weber County;
Council staff are still analyzing the tax rate and any potential impacts on the proposed budget, but it is known that revenue
projections included in the tentative budget were higher than what will be yielded if the proposed certified tax rate is accepted.
The anticipated budget shortfall is approximately $900,000 and Mr. Symes discussed options for adjusting the budget prior to
adoption of the final budget or via a mid-year budget adjustment. Mr. Symes and Management Services Executive Director Stout
engaged in discussion with the Council regarding the manner in which the tax rate is calculated and the cause for the shortfall; Ms.
Stout explained the main cause of the shortfall is an over-projection for revenue related to new natural growth. She noted that she
will be prepared for a more detailed discussion regarding the certified tax rate and formulas used to calculate revenue projections
in a future work session.
          Mr. Symes then reviewed the budget public hearings and action items listed on tonight’s meeting agenda.
          Council member Graf expressed concern regarding specific Capital Improvement Plan projects for which no funding has
been included in the final budget; this included Lester Park and a splash pad project. Mr. Symes stated that he will investigate the
funding plans for those projects and noted that it is common to include a project in the approved Capital Improvement Plan, with
plans to allocate funding at a later date. Council member Graf stated that it appears that the American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA)
money that was to be set aside for Lester Park has been allocated to other projects and he is concerned there will not be sufficient
funding for the project in the future. Ms. Stout apologized for the error and indicated she will provide a full accounting of all
funding sources allocated to Lester Park and provide it to Council staff.

Proposed Partial Street Vacation – 331 Stewart Drive
       Assistant Planning Director Simpson explained the Boyer Company is requesting the vacation of a portion of public
right-of-way along Stewart Drive within Business Depot Ogden. The area proposed for vacation is a narrow strip, approximately
440 feet in length, with a maximum width of 8 feet, totaling 2,475 square feet. The petitioner is in the process of developing
property in Business Depot Ogden, anticipating a new industrial building at 398 Stewart Drive. The purpose of the request is to
adjust the right-of-way line adjacent to the property so that it follows the existing street improvements more closely and to allow
more space on the front of the building to meet the twenty-foot front setback requirement. The Planning Commission reviewed the
request at its February 5, 2025 meeting and recommended approval of the proposed vacation, subject to applicant providing a
clear legal description of the area right-of-way being vacated, which has been satisfied.
          There was discussion among the Council and Mr. Simpson regarding the site development plans for the subject property
and whether a tenant has already committed to occupy the new building.


Proposed Zone Text Amendment – Landscaping Requirements for New
Single–Family Dwellings
          Planning Director Brierley explained that Planning Division staff have proposed a zoning ordinance text amendment
concerning the landscaping standards for front and side yards of new single-family dwellings. This amendment seeks to restrict
turf grass coverage to a maximum of 35 percent of the total landscaped area within these yards. Furthermore, it mandates that no
less than 50 percent of the landscaping area facing a street be comprised of living plants. Lastly, landscaped mulch and rock areas
adjacent to driveways will be required to incorporate a physical barrier, such as vegetation or fencing, to prevent their use for
vehicular parking. This proposed ordinance is intended to satisfy a condition of the $10 million grant from the Utah Department of
Natural Resources for replacing the 36-inch water line through Ogden Canyon, which requires limiting turf grass in front and side
yards of new single-family dwellings to 35 percent. In addition to fulfilling the grant condition from the Utah Department of
Natural Resources, this amendment addresses a gap in the current ordinance by requiring living plant coverage in front and side
yards. Mr. Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss recent and ongoing drought conditions in the State of
Utah and past actions taken by the City in an effort to conserve water resources. The current proposal is an additional water
conservation effort. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and Mr. Brierley centered on the type of landscape conversion that will require a permit;
the fact that the ordinance will only apply to new homes; specific regulations included in the proposed ordinance; plans for
enforcement of the proposed ordinance; and the manner in which the City will educate residents about the change.

Council Business
        Council staff reviewed the Council calendar for the remainder of the month of June and for July.
        The Council engaged in discussion regarding activities underway in Ogden City related to enforcement of Federal
government immigration policies; there was a focus on the appropriateness of local law enforcement in engaging in illegal
immigration enforcement actions.
