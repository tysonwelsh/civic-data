          1. 2   Mayor' s Report


         Mayor Kristie Overson stated that everyone was heartbroken about the tragic event that
transpired on Halloween night when two young Taylorsville residents were hit by a car while trick
or treating. Her thoughts went particularly to the Finch family who lost their young son, Karl. She
understood that there was nothing anyone could say that would ease their pain as they grieved for
him, and the city respected the family' s desire for privacy and space to mourn. The police
investigation was ongoing, and the mayor asked that the community avoid speculation and let the
officers do their job. Speeding had been ruled out as a contributor to the accident and the driver of
the vehicle continued to cooperate with investigators.


The city was looking at all their crosswalks. Flashing and beacon lights were already in place at
most critical crosswalks in the city, including one just south of the intersection where this accident
took place. If there was more that needed to be done, the city wished to do it. There was nothing
more important to her than the health and safety of the community. The mayor explained how very
grateful she was for the input and care expressed by nearby residents. She then asked those
gathered to join her in a moment of silence, which they did.

The previous week, local schools had observed Red Ribbon/ Drug Free Week. She and Chief
Cottam had visited 3rd Graders at Calvin Smith Elementary and talked to them about the
importance of making good choices and being good citizens.

The Taylorsville Exchange Club had celebrated National Day of Service by providing service at
the Taylorsville Bennion Heritage Center in cooperation with the Taylorsville Youth Council. Both
groups worked hard and made a meaningful difference at the grounds of the museum. She also
wished to congratulate the Exchange Club for 25 years of service in the city. She pointed out that
the city had a robust citizen/ volunteer program and encouraged anyone present or possibly
watching the meeting online to sign up to serve on a committee.

The Youth Council was currently job shadowing city staff in an effort to learn about government,
leadership and service. She hoped they would get a birds- eye view of how the city operated.

       Mayor Overson explained that there were a couple of areas along the Jordan River where
improvements would be seen the following year. The Jordan River Commission was working on
a boat launch at Millrace Park. Additionally, at Brighton North Point south of 4700 South there
would be a portage built which would allow boaters to remove their boats from the water, walk a
short distance and then put their boats back in the river, continuing on their way.

Regarding re- districting, every ten years the legislative boundaries were redrawn based on updated
census data. The mayor recently saw a map which proposed breaking Taylorsville into two
separate districts. She was very opposed to that as Taylorsville was a single, whole community.
Therefore, she spoke up at a public hearing and asked that the city be kept whole. Fortunately,
when the maps were finalized and turned over to the state legislature for consideration, they now
depicted the entire city in one district. Still, only the legislature had the authority to make the final
decision.


Early in 2022, city council boundaries would also be reevaluated to ensure that each remained
roughly equal in population.

      Salt Lake Community College would be hosting an open house regarding plans for residential
units/dormitories to be built on campus. The event would be held on Wednesday, November 10th
at the Student Event Center from 5: 00 p.m. to 7: 00 p. m.

Taylorsville Police had recently organized a multi-agency directed enforcement operation plan. It
was focused on saturating high crime areas to conduct pro-active law enforcement, arrest suspects
involved in criminal activity, suppress gang activity, and gather intelligence. Other local police
departments had also participated in the event including West Valley City, Sandy, Unified Police,
Cottonwood Heights, Riverton, Utah Highway Patrol, the Metro Gang Unit and Unified Fire
SWAT Medics. She indicated how gratifying it was to see so many agencies working together for
the common good and thanked TVPD Sgt. Jake Hill for organizing this very effective event.

A VOCA ( Victims of Crime Act) grant had been awarded to the city for $ 141, 203which would
provide for the immediate needs of crime victims. She thanked the police department' s civilian
staff who were proactive in seeking out this funding.

Finally, she welcomed the newest members of Taylorsville Police. Miles Knapp brought four years
of law enforcement experience with him while Daniel Espinoza brought ten years of experience
and Spencer Stone had been a police officer for three years. The Oath of Office was administered
to all three before the police department' s K-9 handlers and dogs were introduced to those
gathered. Sgt. Jake Palmer was partnered with Koda. Officer C. J. Moore worked with Turbo and
Officer Hayden Hoffman was the handler for Cowboy. The teams were intended to be used both
for patrol and narcotics detection.

         7. 1    Fraud Risk Assessment— Scott Harrington


 El Scott Harrington described a Fraud Risk Assessment that had recently been conducted by the
state auditor. It represented a brief assessment of the financial controls the city had in place. The
cityreceived 355 out of 395points which was the same score received theprevious year. A link
to the state' s fraud reporting page had been put into place since the last assessment. However,
because one of the questions had been rephrased from its previous form, the city also lost 20 points
from the previous year. The verbiage on the financial disclosures collected from employees and
elected officials each year would be adjusted to agree with the state' s new recommendation
regarding ethics training.

Mr. Harrington pointed out that the Auditor had been very impressed with the depth of knowledge
on the city' s Audit Committee (which also served as the Budget Committee.)
