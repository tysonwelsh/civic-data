better communication from the city and a set time and place for meetings. Then there is a small
third group that feels disenfranchised by the program.

3.      A presentation on Envision Utah's Valley Visioning work. (21-080) (1:31:29)

Curtis Blair, President and CEO of Utah Chamber of Commerce, and Ryan Beck, Envision Utah,
presented. Mr. Blair reviewed the process for the Valley Visioning initiative, which involved
many organizations throughout Utah County. The Valley Visioning effort is intended to address
the future of growth in Utah County. Mr. Beck continued presenting, noting that the success of
Utah County is going to determine the success of the state. He shared results from their listening
sessions conducted over the course of about half a year. The group then presented respondents
with specific scenarios highlighting different patterns of growth to get feedback. Options
included current conditions, organized centers, westward growth, southern growth, and urban
infill. They conducted random sample surveys as well as public feedback surveys and results
were fairly consistent between them. There was a lot of support for organized centers, westward
growth, and urban infill. Many people acknowledged the importance of agricultural and open
space land uses in south part of the County. For housing scenarios, the results showed preference
for more of a mix of single-family, townhomes, and apartments/condos—this was driven by
affordability and a desire to be located close to urban centers and services. Cities and developers
need to develop more multifamily housing to allow for this to happen. The vision for the future
included wise water management, convenient transportation choices, clear air, a well-educated
population, housing options, local agriculture, open space, and recreation opportunities.

Mr. Beck highlighted important strategies for accomplishing this vision:
     Develop network of walkable mixed-use centers
     Variety of market-based neighborhood and housing types
     Well-maintained highway, transit, and active transportation infrastructure
     World-class education system with high-quality educators
     Efficiently use resources and minimize pollution
Mr. Beck also shared their 2050 vision map, which identified different types of centers: metro,
city, urban, neighborhood, and industrial. Mr. Curtis explained that the Utah Valley Chamber

5.      A Resolution Appropriating $1,250,175 in the Legacy CIP Fund for the Acquisition
        of Land near Slate Canyon (21-058) (2:07:30)

Tara Riddle, Property Manager, presented. The subject parcel is two parcels with combined
acreage of 29 acres. In recent years, the City has acquired properties around Slate Canyon and
Rock Canyon for parks and trails. There was interest around this property due to its proximity to
the Bonneville Shoreline Trail (BST). Normally discussions about property purchases and sales
are conducted with the Council in Closed Meetings; broad public knowledge of the city’s interest
could result in speculation which would drive up the price. Ms. Riddle clarified the process by
which this property was explored for City acquisition. This was the subject of a development
proposal several years ago; it was determined as not feasible since the property was outside the
water service zone. At the time, the developer decided that the cost of the needed improvements
in the area could not be recouped with the requisite fewer homes. The land was then deeded to
the current property owners. The City has had issues with this property over the years, including
transient camps, trash, and other nuisance issues. This has increased the City’s interest in the
property so they could control the activity happening on the property. Images of the property
show a cluster of trails running through property as well as the existing development to the west.
Among other reasons, the property is ideal for acquisition because it provides clear routing of the
BST that maintains the elevation and follows a comfortable contour; the property also provides
an opportunity to restore the disrupted hillside through revegetation and maintaining open space.

The appraised value of the property as determined by a City appraisal in November 2020 was
$930,000. In April 2021, the property owner’s appraisal was for $2,270,000. The property
owner’s appraisal was problematic because the comparable properties were flat farmland in
Mapleton and therefore not really comparable. The City worked with the seller to negotiate a fair
price of $1,250,000. The City has made several assurances to the developer, including that the
property would be zoned as Open Space Parks and Recreation, and that it is located within the
Critical Hillside Overlay Zone. In the future, if the City decides not to use this property for trails
or a park, the seller would have the right of first refusal to purchase the property back while
following the process outlined in City Code for the disposal of property. This request is for the
Council to adopt the resolution appropriating the necessary funds from the Legacy CIP fund.

Councilor Shannon Ellsworth expressed her appreciation to Ms. Riddle for her efforts in this
process. Councilor Bill Fillmore also thanked Ms. Riddle. He asked about Parks and
Recreation’s intentions for this property. Doug Robins, Assistant Parks and Recreation Director,
said that their priority first and foremost was to align the BST. There would likely be space for
sports courts, off-leash dog park area, and any other park amenities they would want to see. The
property could also incorporate natural areas and the hillside. Mr. Robins indicated that they
would want to start the committee park development process in that area before sharing more
specifics, but that is an overview of their broad intentions at this time. Councilor David Harding
hoped this would be part of the level of service calculation for parks in the City. Councilor
George Handley asked whether this property would be part of the revised master plan for Slate
Canyon. Mr. Robins indicated yes. Mr. Handley expressed that the City’s investment in Slate
Canyon was exciting to see. He thanked Ms. Riddle for her negotiations and the conversations

7.      A discussion regarding the Council’s priorities for future appropriations from the
        Legacy CIP Fund. (21-037) (2:26:34)

Cliff Strachan, Council Executive Director, presented. Mr. Strachan shared background on the
Legacy CIP, which was initially comprised of almost $8 million. The Foothills Protection
Committee recently gave guidance on some of the spending of the funds of the Legacy CIP.
George Handley, Foothills Protection Committee Chair, clarified the nature of the committee’s
request. Mr. Strachan highlighted several potential uses of Legacy CIP funds, including some of
the unfunded supplemental requests in the City budget. Requests had been received from several
city departments. Mr. Strachan outlined these requests, including sidewalk infrastructure on
several sections of Canyon Road, 600 South sidewalk, 2230 N at 250 East improvements, Center
Street improvements, and Slate Canyon Drive improvements were from Public Works. Parks and
Recreation has identified Bonneville Shoreline Trail improvements and development and updates
to the group event pavilion at Timpanogos Golf Course as unfunded supplemental requests.

Wayne Parker, CAO, shared details on the Fire Department’s request for the ProQA medical
interrogation system, which allowed dispatchers to utilize an interrogation software for
interacting with residents during a medical emergency. The Fire Department also requested an
upgraded fire alerting system, which would allow all fire stations to have alerts directly to
dispatch services much more quickly. Councilor David Shipley asked about the software referred
to in the Fire Master Plan—Mr. Parker indicated that ProQA had the requested functionality.

Councilor George Handley asked whether the Administration had any sense of the type of
guidance cities would receive about using ARPA funds for infrastructure. Kelsey Zarbock,
Accountant, explained that the Treasury Department was accepting comments on their guidelines
until July 16 this month, so it was still somewhat subject to change. For infrastructure, water and
wastewater projects were more likely to be eligible, with other projects being up in the air as to
whether or not they would be eligible for ARPA fund use. Isaac Paxman, Deputy Mayor, shared
his comments on the Administration’s approach to the uncertainty with ARPA.

Councilor Shannon Ellsworth said that her understanding was that the Administration had
decided on the use of what they designated as the Legacy CIP; the Council did not have a
specific policy statement or determination about legacy projects. Mr. Strachan cautioned the
Council against sing Legacy CIP funds for ongoing expenses.

Councilor Bill Fillmore asked for more information about qualifying infrastructure projects. Mr.
Strachan explained that most of the projects on this list of supplemental requests would not
qualify according to staff’s reading of ARPA. Mr. Parker noted that water, wastewater, and
stormwater projects were pretty clear permitted uses, but they were waiting for further and final
guidance from the Treasury department. In response to Mr. Handley’s earlier comment about
