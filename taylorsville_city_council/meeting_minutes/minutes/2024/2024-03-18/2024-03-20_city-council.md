Reading Across America took place the previous week and the Mayor had the opportunity to read

library here in Taylorsville. The event was very well-attended by many who provided input. The
Mayor pointed out this would be a years-long process.

The food pantry held a breakfast and boutique the previous Saturday. The pantry was manned on
Saturdays by very hard-working volunteers who distributed a large amount of food each week.

Mayor Overson expressed her appreciation to TVPD as they had experienced a challenging week.
In the last several days they had handled some very difficult incidents in a very professional

manner. She was very grateful for them.

3.2. Prosecutor and Defense Report — Brooke Larsen / Jarrod Barben

Chief Prosecutor Brooke Larsen explained that there were two other prosecutors who appeared in
court three days per week—one in person and the other online. Their case load involved a lot of
traffic matters, retail theft, drug offense, domestic violence, DUIs, etc.

There was a DUI program called 24/7 Sobriety which would be available to defendants in certain
DUI cases. It would allow participants to avoid jail time and would involve daily alcohol testing.
Davis County had already been using the program and had great success thus far. She was excited
that the program was becoming more popular in Salt Lake County as well.

Ms. Larsen pointed out that her office had a strong working relationship with the Stowell Crayk
firm who provided indigent defense work. She also spoke highly of the TVPD officers, explaining
that they were the best that she had worked with as far as how well-trained they were. She stated
they showed up for court regularly and were always prepared, etc.

Council Member Burgess asked if the 24/7 Sobriety program would reduce recidivism. Ms. Larsen
responded that was certainly the hope, and that time would tell.

6.1 Public Hearing — Receive Public Comment Regarding Funding Priorities for the
2024-2025 Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) and HOME Programs
— Ken Donarski, CDBG Consultant

=] Ken Donarski explained that this public hearing was for those entities who had submitted
funding applications to address the City Council about their funding needs.

Chair Cochran opened the public hearing.
Brandon Veihl, Grants Manager for South Valley Services, explained that SVS was a domestic
violence crisis shelter and service provider aimed at providing services for victims throughout
Utah. He pointed out that 1 in 3 women and | in seven men will experience domestic violence in
their lifetime. Approximately 40% of those victims would become homeless. SVS provided
assistance to 2600 people, about 5% of those were Taylorsville residents. The requested funding
would help SVS continue providing much needed assistance to domestic violence victims in
Taylorsville and beyond.
There was no one else who expressed a desire to speak, so Chair Cochran closed the public hearing.
