PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE:

   Nicole Douglas newly hired Office Specialist II led the pledge of allegiance and introduced herself to
the council and those present.


CONSENT AGENDA APPROVED:

  Councilor Ostler moved to approve the consent agenda which included the approval of the minutes for
the regular council meeting 2-15-22, work session held on 2-22-22, a list of claims on the warrant
register dated 3-1-22 and the sale of burial rights to Mark Robbins. Councilor Worwood seconded the
motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

BUSINESS LICENSE APPROVED:

   Councilor Ostler moved to approve the business license application for the following four businesses:
Price Check Auto, Red Cliffs Embroidery & Ink, Five Cent Enterprises, Bigman Outdoors. Councilor
Parady seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

NO PUBLIC COMMENT

ARBOR DAY PROCLAMATION APPROVED:

   Parks and Cemetery Superintendent Gerratt Bethers addressed the council about his plans to
celebrate Arbor Day on April 26, 2022. He stated that he would be planting 30-35 trees this spring.
There will be community events held to plant trees at the Hive and at the golf course. Mr. Bethers also
encourage the council to help educate the community about the danger of topping trees.
Councilor Callaway moved to authorize the Mayor to sign Arbor Day Proclamations for 2021 and 2022.
Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

OUT-OF-STATE TRAVEL APPROVED FOR GAS DEPARTMENT

  City Administrator Seth Atkinson informed the council that Public Works Director Kyle Marchant and
Gas Department Utility Tech Zach Larsen were requesting permission to travel to Colorado Springs on
April 6-8 202, for GASworks and GASCalc software training. Councilor Worwood moved to approve the
out-of-state travel for Mr. Marchant and Mr. Larsen. Councilor Callaway seconded the motion. The
motion passed on a unanimous vote.

ELECTRIC TRANSMISSION LINE PROJECT STUDY AND SERVICE LINE PURCHASE AGREEMENT APPROVED:

  Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council the need for a 138 kV electric transmission line. Rocky
Mountain Power (RMP) has been putting together a report detailing the cost of the upgrade needed for
a capacity increase. The city would be responsible for the direct cost in the report of $790,00 +/- 20%
The council had reviewed the details of the costs and scope of the project at the work session held on
February 22, 2022. The total cost of the projects is estimated at $8,000,000.
   The city had also received a cost from RMP for the purchase of the home service for the McPherson
property to the north of the city. The total cost was calculated at $18, 412. This was in exchange for the
easements granted to the city for the transmission line on the McPherson properties and an easement
for distribution to the east side of Main Street.
   The council agreed and directed staff to move forward with the transmission line extension project
and the purchase of the RMP service on the McPherson home. Councilor Worwood moved to approve
moving forward with the Rocky Mountain Power study. Councilor Parady seconded the motion. The
motion passed on a unanimous vote.
   Councilor Parady moved to authorize the Mayor to sign electrical service purchase agreement with
Rocky Mountain Power for the McPherson property. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The
motion passed on a unanimous vote.

CITY ADMINISTRATOR AND CITY COUNCIL UPDATES:

   Mr. Atkinson noted that there will not be a work session next Tuesday because of the political caucus
meetings. He also informed the council that he would be absent from the meeting on March 15, 2022.
   Councilor Parady said that he has been asking the public if they would prefer funding the drug task
force officer with surplus sales tax or with a property tax increase. He said that he has been having
some interesting conversations with people.
   Councilor Worwood stated that he had transferred the parade information over to Councilor
Callaway.
   Councilor Callaway asked the other councilors for names of people they thought might be interested
in serving on the Mammoth Parade Committee.

WORK SESSION:

    1.    Mr. Ben Wheat representing Roderick Enterprises addressed the council about a concept
         master plan for property on the south end of the city. Roderick Enterprises is looking to attract
         light industrial businesses with a need for warehouse and office space. He stated that their
         development would be an 8–10-year project with a business interested in the north end now.
         They will be submitting a petition for the north half annexation and would like to request that
         the Level 2 analysis be done on the south end of the property in preparation for it to be changed
         to a Level one. The council talked about creating a new light industrial zone to protect against
         some undesirable industries that are currently allowed in the industrial zone. The council
         directed staff to do the analysis for the annexation area map level change for the south end
         property.

    2.   Mr. Atkinson presented a draft of a policy dealing with property boundary disputes.

    3. The council discussed adding a new question to the Well-Being Survey.

    4. Councilor Worwood moved to convene into executive session to discuss the acquisition of real
       property. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion carried on a unanimous vote.
