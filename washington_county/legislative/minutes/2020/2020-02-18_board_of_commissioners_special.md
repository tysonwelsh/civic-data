WASHINGTON COUNTY COMMISSION
WORK MEETING MINUTES
FEBRUARY 18, 2020

The Work Meeting of the Board of the Washington County Commission was called to
order by Chairman Victor Iverson at 1:00 p.m. on February 18, 2020, in the Washington
County Commission Chambers, 197 East Tabernacle, St. George, Utah. Those in
attendance were Chairman Victor Iverson, Commissioner Gil Almquist, Commissioner
Dean Cox, County Attorney Eric Clarke, County Administrator Nicholle Felshaw, Sheriff
Cory Pulsipher, Undersheriff James Standley, Chief Nate Brooksby, Deputy Clerk
Cheyenne Bentley and Chairs and Fire Chiefs from the following Special Service Districts:
Dammeron Valley, Diamond Valley, Dixie Deer, Gunlock, New Harmony Valley, North
Central Fire, Northwestern, Pine Valley, Southwestern and Enterprise City.

REGULAR AGENDA ITEMS FOR CONSIDERATION:

DISCUSSION OF EMERGENCY SERVICES

Chairman Iverson explained that the purpose of the meeting was to discuss emergency
service operations within the various Special Service Districts (SSD) to determine the best
practices for what is currently working and areas that need improvement.

Commissioner Cox expressed gratitude to those that participate and give of their time
serving on SSDs. He understands the difficulty in training and retaining volunteers and
would like input on the potential of future consolidation along the SR-18 corridor. He also
desired to better understand the functionality of the districts and how they will progress

into the future.

Commissioner Cox asked a question of all the Districts regarding whether they had the
ability to provide the two-in two-out service. Wherein, if two (2) firefighters enter a
building, two (2) are to remain outside in order to rescue them should the need arise.
Commissioner Cox also asked which districts owned and operated Self Contained

Breathing Apparatuses (SCBA).

Pine Valley Fire Chief Steve Shakespeare stated that Pine Valley is unique in the sense that
the majority of the population is retired and often in need of younger and more able bodied
volunteers. The need of Pine Valley is mainly fire, as their medical service is well
established. In the event of a structure fire, mutual aid is their first option to be able to fight
those fires. Pine Valley’s annual budget it $100,000 which is collected by assessments of
one hundred-fifty dollars ($150) per home or fifty dollars ($50) per parcel as well as various
private donations. There are currently twenty-seven (27) volunteers for the district.

Diamond Valley SSD Chair John Cazier stated that their district’s challenge is adequate
funding. Diamond Valley contracts with a wildland fire crew in order to satisfy the fire and
safety needs within the District. Funds are collected through individual assessments on
homes at one hundred- fifty dollars ($150) each. A Community Investment Bond (CIB)
was acquired in order to purchase a fire engine for a second crew in the area. The District’s
fire chief would like to conduct an area wide training in order to certify and train any

interested individuals in the area. Consolidation of the Districts from a budget standpoint
makes sense in the long term as the funding could be used to hire a full time chief, however,
there are concerns that the individual Districts would like to maintain autonomy. A training
ground/facility in the Diamond Valley area would be beneficial to train all volunteers
within the SR-18 corridor to help unify the Districts.

North Western Special Service District (NWSSD) Chair Steve Haluska stated that their
District provides all services with the exception of medical. A standby fee of ninety-eight
dollars ($98) annually is charged per household in order to provide the services in the area.
Currently, the NWSSD’s budget is $100,000 annually and includes coverage of
approximately 900 properties. The Central Fire Department is able to provide the two-in,
two-out service in the event of a structure fire and do operate SCBAs. Most residents
understand that the cost of living in a more rural area includes paying for the services that
the SSD provides.

Gunlock Fire Chief Vic Welch stated that there are twelve (12) people on the department,
with about eight (8) of those active. Gunlock is a relatively new district and is not able to
provide the two-in, two-out service as they do not have the SCBA equipment.

John Cazier questioned whether there was a resource at the County that could assist with
grant writing for the Districts. Mr. Cazier stated that their District discarded several of their
old SCBA machines to another County as he was not aware that other neighboring districts
were in need.

Commissioner Cox explained that Washington County does not currently have a grant
writer, however, there are resources at Five County AOG as well with the County Fire
Warden, Adam Hyder, that could potentially help the district with any grant questions.

Brent Hafen stated that one of the advantages of unifying the districts is that larger areas
are more likely to receive grants that would benefit all of the districts as a whole.

Washington County Emergency Services Director Jason Whipple explained that several of
the grants obtained through the County are oftentimes large and cumbersome.

Brookside Fire SSD Representative Dan Tanaz, stated that retention of the volunteers is an
issue. Grant writing has also been challenging as it is a long and competitive process,
especially for apparatus grants.

Commissioner Almquist questioned how communication between the chiefs could be
improved between the Districts.

Commissioner Cox questioned the advantage of creating an interlocal agreement between
the districts in order to improve communication and resource issues.

Brent Hafen stated that unifying the districts makes the most sense moving forward.
Unifying the equipment would be beneficial as all volunteers would have the knowledge

to use it.

Commissioner Almquist questioned if anyone had trained at the Dixie Technical College
with the Utah Fire and Rescue Academy (UFRA).

Crystal Lake, from the Northwestern SSD stated that she has trained with UFRA and that
the course was very beneficial.

Enterprise Fire Chief Brenden Moody stated that they are contracted with North Central
Fire SSD. Difficulties that Enterprise faces are much the same as the other Districts with
retention, training opportunities and obtaining grants being their top challenges. Enterprise
Fire and EMS covers approximately 400 square miles.

North Central Fire District Chair Duncan Reed explained that their budget comes from the
taxing entity, Enterprise City, and have an annual budget of $11,000.

Southwestern Special Service District Fire Chief Don Christensen stated that the values of
homes have increased over time. This leads to older people moving into the homes and
often times they don’t have the knowledge of how volunteer fire departments operate or
are too old to volunteer. Taxing entity operates at $47,000 annual budget. SWSSD
currently has SCBAs, however, training volunteers proves to be costly for a volunteer.

Dammeron Valley SSD Mike Johnson explained that fees are $210 per tax ID regardless
of improvement on the lot. Most of the volunteers work full time jobs and can only respond
if they are able to. There are currently fourteen (14) firefighters on the roster.

Commissioner Cox mentioned that the State provides a general SSD online training course
and encouraged all to take the course.

Dammeron Valley resident Mack Sorenson explained that the Dammeron Valley SSD is
currently not functioning as it should and urged the citizens of Dammeron Valley to work
together in order to resolve the issues within the area.

Dammeron Valley resident Larry Carland agreed that the Dammeron Valley Board is
dysfunctional. He mentioned that there are several dozen fire hydrants that are not up to
code and will not pump adequate amounts of water if needed. Mr. Carland questioned the
ability to be funded through taxation rather than by annual dues.

Christina Dubois explained that she previously worked in multiple departments for
Phoenix City and has experience writing grants. She mentioned that it would be beneficial
for the districts to unify in order to qualify for larger, more impactful grants.

Chairman Iverson questioned having Zion Public Finance examine each of the Districts’
budgets to determine what unifying would look like moving forward. More information is
needed to implement best practices and mitigate challenges moving forward.

Crystal Lake from Northwestern SSD gave an overview of how a unified SSD would work
with the current Districts and how the resources would be greater for a larger more singular
district. She then outlined several options and provided a pro and con list of each.

Dammeron Valley resident Ryan Anderson stated that oftentimes citizens do not
understand the stress placed on the chief of a fire district. It is critical to have volunteers
and people who serve that are trained and have experience.

Chair of New Harmony Valley Special Service District Kendall Kelsey questioned how a
unified fire department would benefit the citizens of New Harmony as they are on the
opposite side of the County. He explained that impact fees are collected on new
construction and questioned whether those funds could be used to assist with the fire
district. Mr. Kelsey then introduced the new fire chief, Bruce McDonald.

There being nothing further to come before the Commission, the meeting was adjourned at
2:56 p.m.
