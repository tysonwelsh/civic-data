Determination 2020-02, pursuant to Utah Code Annotated 52-4-207(4)
I, Craig Hawker, Vice Chair of the Draper City Planning Commission, do hereby determine
conducting an electronic meeting of the Draper City Council with an anchor location presents a
substantial risk to the health and safety of those who may be present at the anchor location and

location.

   • Federal, state and local leaders, including the Draper City Mayor and City Council, have
       all recognized a global pandemic caused by the spread of the COVID-19 virus.
   • COVID-19 cases in Salt Lake County and Draper continue to increase at rates which pose

   1. Public Hearing: HBA Plaza at Draper Text Amendment, Zoning Map Amendment,
       and Land Use Map Amendment Request (Legislative Action)

On the request of Ross Ford, representing the Home Builders Association of Utah, for a Text
Amendment to the 136 Center Commercial Special District (CSD-136) code, and to rezone 2.55
acres of property located at 38 West 13775 South from the M1 zone to the CSD-136 zone, and to
amend the Land Use Map from Business/Light Manufacturing to the Commercial Special District
designation. Applications TEXTMAP-991-2020, TEXTMAP-992-2020, TEXTMAP-993-2020.
Staff contact is Jennifer Jastremsky, 801576-6328, jennifer.jastremsky@draper.ut.us.

Jennifer Jastremsky began the presentation by stating that the subject property is located just North
of the Prison on 13775 South. This property contains an office building, and was originally given
site plan approval in 2014. It was part of the Osmond Minor Subdivision. The Applicant has a new
tenant who wants the building for medical use. The building is currently designated for
manufacturing use, as it is located in the business and light manufacturing land use and our M1
light manufacturing zoning designation. That zoning designation does not allow for medical use.
The Applicant is proposing adding the property into the 136 Center CSD, directly North of the
site, as well as add it to the Commercial Special District Land Use designation.

The 136 Center, CSD is geared toward office uses, and this is an office building on the property.
As far as uses go it is compatible with that CSD. In addition to the rezone to the 136 Center, they
are proposing some text amendments to the CSD. Commercial Special Districts are a little different

than the standard zoning districts. They are more property specific; they have concept plans; they
are geared toward the proposed development where the site is already built.

They are proposing a couple of changes to the code to make this site fit in better with 136 Center.
The first section that will be changed is the Purpose Statement for the CSD, and that is just to show
the change in the acreage due to adding this property. The next change deals with architectural
features, specifically the fenestration, having to do with the windows. Office buildings in the 136
Center CSD are required to have 40 percent high efficiency glazing, that means 40 percent of the
structure needs to be in glass.

The Applicant is proposing to exempt elevator shafts and stairwells from this requirement. While
the existing building generally meets that 40 percent, there is an elevator they will be adding to the
structure that they are hoping will not need to have 40 percent windows on the elevator shaft
specifically. The next change in the code would be landscaping. The CSD requires a three-foot
berm along the perimeter of the project to help shield the parking lot. The Applicant is proposing
to exclude that berm along the East property line that abuts I-15. I-15 actually sits a couple feet
higher than this property, so that three-foot berm would probably not serve a very big screening
purpose to the parking lot.

Lastly is signage. The Applicant is proposing a pole sign on their property. The CSD currently
allows one pole sign. For the CSD they are proposing to change that allowance to two pole signs
so they can have a sign on their property as well. The exhibits within the CSD would be modified,
one to show the property within the boundary. The next one is to show the concept site plan for
the entire CSD. They will be adding an image to the concept elevations within the CSD code. The
proposed sign meets the height restrictions that are currently within the codes. They are not
proposing to change the specific standard of which type of pole sign that is allowed in the CSD,
but they are increasing the number that would be allowed.

   2. Public Hearing; City Initiated Commercial Buffer Zone – Zoning Text Amendment
       Request (Legislative Action)
       A city initiated text amendment to create a new zoning district called Commercial Buffer
       Zone (CBU). The proposed zone will be located in Chapter 9-11 (Commercial Zones) of
       the Draper City Municipal Code. Other related sections of Title 9 may also be amended as
       part of this proposal. Application: TEXTMAP-1022-2020). Staff contact: Maryann
       Pickering at (801) 576-6391 or maryann.pickering@draper.ut.us.

Maryann Pickering began the presentation by stating that this amendment was initiated at the
request of the City Council. There have been some concerns with recent requests for rezoning and
building commercial projects next to some residentially developed properties. The intention of this
zone is to create it so it can be lower intensity commercial that would fit with the existing
residential areas. It would be an option for applicants to choose. She emphasized that at this point
the City has no plans to rezone any properties with this new designation, it would simply be
adopted and placed in the zoning code as an option for applicants. The changes to this zone and
request to rezone would be applicant driven, and this new zone could be located anywhere in the
City. Because it is intended to be more of a buffer zone, it is lower intensity and it is very similar
to the current Town Center, or TC zone. Some of the highlights of the zone are that the uses are
similar but it will be less intensive than the TC zone.

We imagine that this will probably be used on smaller properties, so we would want those less
intensive uses. There would be permitted and conditional uses in the zone, and the development
standards are very similar to TC. The parking would have to be located behind the buildings, so it
would be pulling those buildings up to the street and having more of a street frontage as is required
in TC. Service elements would need to be screened, but lighting and signs would comply with the
standards that exist today in the Municipal Code.

The one difference with this one is the maximum height would be two stories, or limited to 30 feet
in height. One other item that has been included is design standards that would apply to this zone
based on the map. Maryann explained that the blue areas on the map showed the current boundaries
of the current Town Center Zone. The yellow areas surrounding the blue are the areas that if
redeveloped to the new commercial buffer zone, they would have to comply with the adopted
Town Center design guidelines. That would help ensure that the area in and around the Town
Center has a similar design.

Because this zone could be implemented anywhere in the City, if a property chose to rezone to this
commercial buffer on the West side of the freeway, they would be subject to the normal design
standards for the commercial projects that are found in the Municipal Code. A second item
included in this is the redevelopment of properties in historic review. Right now, within the Town
Center zoning district there is a provision for the Planning Commission to approve projects if there
are non-conformities if there is a historic structure to be preserved. That would be something like
setbacks or height.

One of the old historic buildings is 40 feet tall, but it would be appropriate to preserve that building
and have them be taller than what would currently be allowed in the zone. That would be something
the Planning Commission would have to decide, it would not be a staff level approval. Another
item that was included was anything that was listed on any of the three historic registries would
also need a review by the Historic Preservation Commission in order to move forward. Maryann
said she would be happy to answer any questions about the proposed zone, and reminded the
Commission that this would be a recommendation to the City Council for their next meeting in
August.

Vice Chairman Hawker asked if there was a reason why this was done? Is it just good planning to
be ahead of the curve, or are there specific buildings that will be looked at in the near future that
this could help?

Maryann replied that it was more good planning and getting ahead of the curve. She stated it would
be an option if someone wanted to redevelop to commercial, but weren’t residential, we
could say this is the zone we developed that we think fits well as a buffer next to those residential
properties. It is something that could be considered.

or based on citizen responses they have concerns. They can forward that negative recommendation
