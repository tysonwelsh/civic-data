COMBINED USE I & II ZONE ORDINANCE 05-17-2022 ADOPTED:

  City Administrator Seth Atkinson explained that Last year, the City Council enacted a temporary land
use ordinance for the Combined Use Zone (CU) in order to refine the zone to include more provisions

for parking and open space. The planning staff has examined options to include or exclude from the
Combined Use zone. While researching options for the CU zone, staff began to explore ways to also use
the Combined Use II zone.

   As the city has also considered ways to zone for future land development along the Main Street,
there may be a way to promote best practices for commercial areas, residential/commercial mixes, and
transportation routes. City staff has discussed the concept of commercial pods or areas and then
providing multiple transportation routes to these pods. In this way, the city can avoid using Main Street
as the sole transportation corridor to access commercial services. Surrounding these commercial pod
areas would be a zone of commercial/residential mixed-use development. The CU-I zone would consist
of more commercial options with some residential. The CU-II zone would consist of more residential
options and some commercial. In this way, the zones can promote a transition from a heavy commercial
area to a light commercial area. The attached changes to the CU-I and CU-II zones reflect this
philosophy. Placement of these zones will be a future discussion of the Planning Commission and City
Council.

  In addition to the use changes in CU-I, density was also changed to allow for 6 units per acre with a
maximum building footprint of 5,000 square feet. Frontage requirements were 70’ for residential
purposes, with an additional 10’ for each additional unit. Non-residential uses would require 70’ of
frontage. The zone also has a maximum building size limitation of 15,000 square feet.

  The CU-II zone has also had changes in uses that should be reviewed carefully by the City Council. This
zone would allow for more residential options and limit the number of commercial uses. Like the CU-I,
the CU-II zone would have a density limit of 6 units per acre and a frontage requirement of 70’ for
residential with an additional 10’ for each additional unit, and a 70’ requirement for non-residential.
One main difference is that the CU-II allows for a 75’ height, while the CU-I was limited to 45’.

  Open space and parking were addressed in other sections of the code. Parking is still being reviewed
and a proposal will be submitted soon. A new definition was proposed for open space that removed
setbacks from the area counted as usable open space. Setbacks would include front, side, and rear yard
setbacks since these are typically counted toward space associated with a residential unit. The new
definition then limits usable open space to space accessible to all residents of a residential subdivision.
The open space definition would apply to all zones in Title 10.

 The definitions section was updated to include this new definition of open space and a definition of
Gardening, Horticulture which would replace the “Agriculture” definition. Ordinance 05-17-2022
amended Title 10 Chapter 2 and Title 10 Chapter 7 with these changes. Councilor Worwood moved to
adopt the Ordinance and the Exhibits. Councilor Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed on
a unanimous vote.

RICHARD ANDERSON AGREEMENT APPROVED:

  Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council that property owner of a lot located at the corner of 300 N
and 600 E owned by Richard Anderson would like to construct a new home on the lot while living in a
manufactured home that is on the lot. The proposed agreement states that the manufactured home
must be moved or demolished within a certain amount of time after construction is completed. If this
provision is met; Mr. Anderson could construct the new dwelling while living in the old home.
   Councilor Parady moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the agreement. Councilor Ostler seconded
the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

JENSON REFRIGERATION SUBSTITUTE DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT AND IMPACT FEE AGREEMENT
APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council two agreements with Jenson Refrigeration. He explained
that the city has been in recent negotiations with Ken Jenson regarding a refrigeration plant
development on the south end of the city. The parties have reached a mutual agreement to make a
change to a development agreement that was entered into last year. This change adjusts language
regarding a buyback provision. The prior language allowed the city an option to buy back the property if
a certificate of occupancy had not been issued for the plant within a certain time frame. The language
adjustment now requires a completed and passed commercial four-way inspection on the building
before the buyback provision becomes no longer applicable.

    The second agreement allows for a multi-year payment for 50% of their impact fees. 50% will be due
up front at the time of building permit issuance. The city recently commissioned a study that calculated

commercial and industrial impact fees that more align with actual usage. Other cities have similar
calculations. The new calculation reduced their impact fees by approximately 30%.

Councilor Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.
