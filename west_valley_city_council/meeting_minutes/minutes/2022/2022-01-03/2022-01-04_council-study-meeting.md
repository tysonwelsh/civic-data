A.   ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING THE WEST VALLEY CITY
     POLICE DEPARTMENT'S ACCEPTANCE OF A JUSTICE ASSISTANCE
     GRANT (JAG) THROUGH THE U.S. DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE
     PROGRAMS
     Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council
     Meeting scheduled January 11, 2022, in order for the City Council to hear and
     consider public comments regarding the West Valley City Police Department's
     Acceptance of a Justice Assistance Grant (JAG) through the U.S. Department of Justice
     Programs.

       Proposed Resolution 22-01 related to the proposal to be considered by the City
       Council subsequent to the public hearing, was discussed as follows:

       ACTION: RESOLUTION NO. 22-01, AUTHORIZE WEST VALLEY CITY
       TO ACCEPT THE EDWARD BYRNE MEMORIAL JUSTICE
       ASSISTANCE GRANT
       Colleen Jacobs, Police Chief, discussed proposed Resolution 22-01 that would
       authorize West Valley City to Accept the Edward Byrne Memorial Justice Assistance
       Grant.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

              The U.S. Department of Justice Program is awarding the West Valley City
              Police Department the 2021 Edward Byrne Memorial Justice Assistance
              Grant (J.A.G.). The allowable expenses of the grant are the purchase of
              equipment, and funding training and programs they approve through the
              application process. The funds are allocated to West Valley City based on a
              formula that considers the population of West Valley City and our part one
              crime statistics. There is separate funding allocated to each state to ensure
              fairness. The J.A.G. Program allows states and local governments to
    MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JANUARY 4, 2022
                           -3-


              implement otherwise unfunded programs designed to improve the criminal
              justice system.

              The West Valley City Police Department Special Weapons and Tactics
              (SWAT) Team is in need of new equipment and updated training.
              Department funds are not sufficient to cover the additional expense to
              replace/repair the needed equipment or to provide the updated training to
              properly utilize the necessary equipment. Many of the SWAT team’s
              trainings consist of firing weapons on the range and running scenarios in
              abandoned buildings. To improve our trainings, the SWAT team is wanting
              to repair many of our weapons, purchase simunition weapons and
              simunition rounds to give the most up-to-date and sound training. The
              simunition equipment we currently have was a one-time purchase that has
              been handed down throughout the years and is near a point of failure.

       Councilmember Fitisemanu asked if the amount of the grant fluctuates. Chief
       Jacobs replied that it does fluctuate slightly each year but generally stays around
       the same overall. Councilmember Huynh asked how many officers are on the
       SWAT team. Chief Jacobs stated around 36 and noted it’s a collaborative effort.
       Mayor Lang asked if these funds are reflected in a previous budget. Wayne replied
       he is unsure but noted that there is a budget opening on the regular meeting tonight
       and would be noted there if it has been added. Councilmember Harmon asked if the
       night vision equipment is new. Chief Jacobs replied there is some equipment but
       not enough and everything is outdated.

       The City Council will consider Resolution 22-01 at the Regular Council Meeting
       scheduled January 11, 2022 at 6:30 P.M.

RESOLUTION 22-02: APPROVE A MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING
("MOU") BETWEEN WEST VALLEY CITY AND THE UTAH ATTORNEY
GENERALS OFFICE (UAGO) TO JOIN THE INTERNET CRIMES AGAINST
CHILDREN (ICAC) TASKFORCE
Colleen Jacobs, Police Chief, presented proposed Resolution 22-02 that would approve a
Memorandum of Understanding ("MOU") Between West Valley City and the Utah
Attorney Generals Office (UAGO) to Join the Internet Crimes Against Children (ICAC)
Taskforce.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The West Valley City Police Department has participated with the Utah Officer of
    MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JANUARY 4, 2022
                           -4-


       the Attorney General, Internet Crimes Against Children (ICAC) Task Force for
       many years. Detectives with our department work hand in hand with the ICAC Task
       Force to investigate internet crimes against children occurring in West Valley City.
       This collaboration has been very successful for many years. The detectives assigned
       to the task force work this as a collateral assignment and are compensated in
       overtime reimbursed by this MOU/grant.

       The memorandum of understanding between the Utah ICAC Task Force and West
       Valley City Police Department specifies that we are receiving $16,000 to use
       towards vigorously and properly investigating crimes against children that occur in
       some relationship with the Internet or other electronic media devices. Those
       investigations will be conducted only by sworn detectives and in a spirit of
       cooperation with other ICAC task force members. The funding is available from
       July 1, 2021 until June 30, 2022.

Mayor Lang asked if the same officer serves on this task force. Chief Jacobs replied yes.

The City Council will consider Resolution 22-02 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
January 11, 2022 at 6:30 P.M.

      REQUESTING FINAL PLAT APPROVAL FOR THE CYPRESS COVE
      PUD LOCATED AT 3540 WEST 4700 SOUTH
      Steve Lehman, CED, discussed Application PUD-4-2021, Filed by Adam Maher,
      Requesting Final Plat Approval for the Cypress Cove PUD Located at 3540 West 4700
      South

      Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
      information as follows:

             Mr. Adam Maher, is requesting final approval for a residential community
             to be known as the Cypress Cove Town homes. The City Council approved
             a zone change in August 2021 from the RB zone to the RM zone.

             The Cypress Cove PUD is a residential community consisting of 12 units
    MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JANUARY 4, 2022
                           -6-


              on 1.08 acres. This equates to an overall density of 11 units per acre.
              Residential units will be two stories in height. Per the development
              agreement, the minimum dwelling size will be 1750 square feet with an
              average of 1900 square feet. Building materials will be brick or stone,
              stucco and fiber cement siding.

              Access to the subdivision will be gained from 4700 South and from 3600
              West. Both access points are via a shared access agreement with the
              Cypress Credit Union. The developer will need to improve and replace any
              damaged improvements along 4700 South. Landscaping improvements to
              the right-of-way will also be made to this street through project approval.

              All units will have a two-car garage. In addition, all units will have a
              driveway with a depth of 18 feet per the development agreement. As such,
              this project is parked at 4 spaces per unit.

       Mayor Lang asked if the City can ensure the developer won’t raise the level of the
       elevation. Steve replied that this was discussed in the Planning Commission
       meeting and the applicant will be required to take all fill off site to preserve the
       integrity of the development to the north.
