       ORDINANCE, CHAPTERS 2, 3, 5, 6, AND 7 OF THE UTAH COUNTY LAND USE ORDINANCE,
       RELATED TO THE ESTABLISHMENT OF MINOR SUBDIVISIONS; THE DEFINITION AND
       NOTICING OF, AND THE PROCEDURES AND REQUIREMENTS FOR, LARGE SCALE
       DEVELOPMENTS; THE DEFINITION OF COUNTY COMMISSION; THE GENERAL
       PROVISIONS, APPLICATION REQUIREMENTS, STANDARDS, AND CONDITIONS FOR
       PLANNED SUBDIVISIONS; THE POWERS AND DUTIES OF THE PLANNING COMMISSION
       AND COUNTY COMMISSION; THE DEVELOPMENTAL IMPACT STATEMENT CHECK LIST;
       AND THE DEFINITIONS RELATIVE TO THE TRANSFER OF DEVELOPMENT RIGHTS
       SENDING OVERLAY ZONE AND TRANSFER OF DEVELOPMENT RIGHTS RECEIVING
       OVERLAY ZONE (Continued from the March 28 and April 18, 2017 Commission meetings)
(Track A, 08:05)
Brandon Larsen of the Utah County Community Development Office explained some updates have been made to
the proposed ordinance following feedback during previous Commission meetings. In summary, subdivisions for
one-lot and two-lot properties would not need to be reviewed by the Utah County Planning Commission for
approval, but would be presented to the County Commissioners. This ensures the decision-making power remains
within the Board while reducing application time by approximately one month. Noticing requirements will remain
the same as current ordinance: ten days mailed prior notice for adjacent properties, allowing the public to file any
objections if needed.

2.     DISCUSSION ON POTENTIAL AMENDMENT OF THE UTAH COUNTY LAND USE
       ORDINANCE TO ALLOW BUILDINGS AND FACILITIES FOR THE REPAIR OF
       AGRICULTURAL VEHICLES AND MACHINERY IN THE RESIDENTIAL AGRICULTURAL
       (RA-5) ZONE OF UTAH COUNTY
(Track A, 45:10)
Commissioner Ivie noted commercial agriculture repair shops are currently forbidden within Residential Agriculture
zones. There are gifted people able to repair their own agricultural machinery, but there are negative impacts to the
land in working directly in the field or on the soil. Bryce Armstrong of the Utah County Community Development
Office presented a list of talking points to receive feedback from the commissioners on how to address this issue.
Repair shops could be designated as permitted use, or perhaps create exceptions if there is a home on the lot. They
discussed aesthetics, weather-delayed repairs, reasonable time frames for storage, and parking. Commissioner Lee
noted he encourages business and individuals using their own property for their desires, but there is a need to
balance the needs of the neighbors and their property rights as well. They also discussed additional costs related to
meeting safety codes, and Commissioner Graves advised researching potential annexations and similar existing city
codes. Mr. Armstrong noted the suggestions would be presented to the Utah County Planning Commission.

3.      DISCUSS A PROPOSED ORDINANCE TO MODIFY UTAH COUNTY CODE SECTION 26-3-9
        REGARDING THE 20 YEAR REVIEW OF AGRICULTURAL PROTECTION AREAS, AS A
        RESULT OF THE PASSAGE OF SENATE BILL 15, FROM THE 2017 UTAH LEGISLATIVE
        SESSION
(Track A, 1:05:35)
Utah County Deputy Attorney Robert J. Moore mentioned Utah County has the most agricultural protection areas in
the state, totaling more than seventy throughout the county. Current statute requires agricultural protection areas be
reviewed during the twentieth calendar year after approval, but the recent passing of Senate Bill 15 during the 2017
Utah Legislative Session changed one word from “shall” to “may,” making that review optional for existing
agricultural protection areas. Mr. Moore is proposing an ordinance for the County Commissioners to be involved in
initial reviews of non-controversial areas, and allow the protection to continue for an additional twenty years if there
are no immediate issues to address. Mr. Moore reiterated this would only apply to existing areas; any concerns that
could result in a modification of the terms or a complete termination of the protection would go through the full
review process. Mr. Moore considers this a pro-agriculture move, benefitting and saving time for citizens and
county staff.

Commissioner Ivie asked about noticing residents, for them to be aware of upcoming reviews for nearby agricultural
protection areas. Robert Moore noted the Utah County Community Development Office, specifically Peggy Kelsey
at this time, maintains those records. Additionally, anyone can file a written complaint against an agricultural
protection area at any time, and those are presented to the Board during the initial review process. Commissioner
Lee emphasized the importance of giving the public opportunity to file complaints, and including requirements for
notifications in the proposed ordinance. Bryce Armstrong of the Utah County Community Development Office
explained how Notices of Creation are recorded directly onto properties.

Provo resident Robert Stevens asked about the county process for changing a regulation. Referring to the Work
Session agenda items on today’s calendar, he asked about the next steps. Commissioner Graves noted any member
of the public can approach a commissioner with a suggestion, and then be directed to the appropriate channels. Mr.
