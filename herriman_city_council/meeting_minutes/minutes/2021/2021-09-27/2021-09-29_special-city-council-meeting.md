Councilmember Shields acknowledged the exhausting process and observed the amount of time Council, staff and
the development team had spent. He was eager to have conversations and explore ideas with a certain reluctance
understanding it was better to engage in communications rather than opposition. He recognized the list of
problems identified in other master development agreements within Herriman City’s history and the desire to
correct those short comings in future agreements. Councilmember Shields relayed his belief the annexation would
be a benefit to Herriman. He offered excerpts from the Olympia development agreement he felt were important
additions which had been omitted from previous development agreements. As the development moves forward,
each plat or subdivision application would have a development report containing specific information including the
type of units, acres, amount of open space, number of units remaining in the MDA shall accompany the application.
As part of the development reports there is a requirement to submit a letter from a traffic engineer certifying the
development proposed by the application substantially complies with assumptions and projections of the traffic
impact study. This letter would certify the proposed infrastructure would supply a level of service of D or better. If
the engineers letter indicated the units or development phase does not comply with the study or would otherwise
cause greater impacts than indicated, the developer would be responsible to pay for the additional impact. The City
would have the right to withhold building permits and approvals until such remedies have been completed. Each
development application would be required to provide verification of continued availability of water, sewer, storm
water, fire, and other utilities. The City would not be obligated to issue any building permits in the absence of this
verification. Secondary water would be installed for all phases of development.

Councilmember Shields discussed the requirements for open space. The open space must be developed at the same
rate as residential units and at no time could this fall behind more than ten percent or building permit approvals
would cease. Every home would be located within one-half mile of a park or open space. Open space would
exclude many of the uses approved in previous master development agreements such as park strips or rooftop
gardens. The developer must construct, at their expense, numerous public parks and dedicate any park over two
acres in size or trail to Herriman City. A special agreement was also achieved to construct a 14-acre park in
conjunction with the Hidden Oaks subdivision directly south of Olympia with approximately seven acres on each
side. The Parks Department would work with local area sport organizations to help design and locate permanent
facilities and improvements for local sports to practice and hold competitions. This agreement would require a four-
year review where both parties sit down and discuss the development, general economy, and other issues related to
the plan as well as ways to remedy issues which may have arisen.

Councilmember Shields indicated two independent commercial real estate brokers had been consulted, one on
behalf of the City and one on behalf of the developer. Each source independently responded with a total feasible
amount of commercial space of 200,000 square feet inside the development. The parties negotiated to 300,000
square feet minimum commercial space and the holding of at least ten acres of land for commercial use for up to 25
years. The numbers negotiated have minimum requirements; however, no maximum amounts. The City would be
actively involved working with professional retail recruitment firms along with other economic development
resources to increase the commercial within and around the Olympia Development. Extra incentive was offered for
the development of an employment center with at least 150 on-site jobs and/or a performing arts center or other
September 29, 2021 – Special City Council Minutes                                                    Page 5 of 7
substantial civic use; however, police and fire services would be excluded from the calculation. The commercial
development would be distributed throughout the project.

Councilmember Shields reiterated the developer would be 100-percent responsible for the infrastructure inside the
development including parks, roads, strips, trees, landscaping, sidewalks, trails, water system, and stormwater
management systems. The City would only pay for any upsizing of water lines or certain amenities. The design
standards were increased substantially and more fully development as some open space would serve as stormwater
detention basins. To keep those open spaces usable, large facilities would be required to be built to hold stormwater
underground for most storms. Those which are in the 85th percentile or greater would be allowed to have any water
above ground on the fields, and then only for long enough for it to flow to the below ground infrastructure. This
would allow for landscaping to remain in place and could be utilized year-round.

Councilmember Shields observed the financial projections anticipating nearly $35 Million in one-time fees in terms
of building permit fees. The financial impacts suggest nearly $3 Million in revenues in excess of costs annually.
Olympia would be the first development in the history of Herriman City to completely pay for itself both in terms
of infrastructure and ongoing operations and maintenance.

Councilmember Ohrn indicated she was not intending to be argumentative; however, disagreed with the master
development agreement. She indicated she would have voted affirmatively on the annexation and the PID as they
were positive additions to the community. She thanked staff, the developer and the Council who worked on the
agreements for their time and effort working through the details. She indicated she had a differing view of the
development and recalled previous conversations with other colleagues that negotiations did not reduce the density.
Councilmember Ohrn felt it the level of density was irresponsible, and this would not help the affordability of
housing. She would have liked to have additional time to discuss the master development agreement.
Councilmember Ohrn observed the already high transportation costs and was saddened to not see plans to offer
solutions. She relayed her gratitude for the PID and how it had been designed and noted she could not support
additional costs for the residents. Councilmember Ohrn applauded everyone for their efforts; however, could not
offer support.

Councilmember Henderson thanked everyone for their hard work at the direction of the majority of Council. He
critiqued the process as he felt this was a gigantic opportunity missed to reduce the negative impacts the
development would have on current residents. He opined the negotiated MDA did have positive aspects and was a
better document that what Herriman had seen previously. Councilmember Henderson suggested it was an irrelevant
comparison as it really should only be compared to the agreement approved by Salt Lake County and the net results
on residents. He appreciated the infrastructure would be installed initially. Councilmember Henderson disagreed
that instead of focusing on proper civic planning and reducing the negative effects of the density awarded by the
County Council, the effort was based on false premise of a financial windfall for Herriman City and the timeframe
was driven by the municipal election. Consequentially, this effort set down the path for a political decision versus
one focused on proper planning. Councilmember Henderson indicated rather than an open public meeting and
having dialogue as promised, the majority of the meetings and discussions were held behind closed doors and only
the information in favor of the annexation was communicated to the public. Councilmember Henderson opined
this was a huge step backwards, not just a mistake, as it was a permanent mistake which would irreparably harm not
only Herriman residents, but the residents of surrounding cities. He hoped the Council could move on to other
business and work in a better light. He recalled a statement made previously which stated if a resident did not like
decisions made by the Council, those residents could vote them out of office and hoped residents would remember
this statement come election time.

Mayor Watts thanked the developer for their willingness to entertain the invitation to annex into Herriman City. He
thanked staff for their efforts to try to find a solution which could work for all involved and opined this would
make the community better. He relayed his excitement to have helped the City to become more financially stable.
