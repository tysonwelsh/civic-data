Chair Stephens opened the meeting.
1. Business Matters
1.1 On-Premises Sign Ordinance Discussion
Francis Lilly said staff has been working on revisions to the sign ordinance and fence
ordinance. Chair Stephens said the City needs a fence ordinance that is enforceable.
John Jansen said there are a lot of signs that are defined in ordinance. The code provides
for height, setback, size, number, type, and they require building permits. He said there is |
not a temporary sign ordinance, and this has become an enforcement problem. He |
highlighted some issues that he sees with signs around the City; size, design,
location/setbacks, separation between signs, type, height, maintenance, and temporary
signs. He said Millcreek needs to balance the City’s perspective with the business
perspective on what makes for a good sign. He illustrated examples of signs around the
City: pole signs at 3300 S. 2300 E.; roof, a-frames, bench, and wall signs on 2300 E.;
pennants/streamers, light pole signs, canopy signs at 2300 E. and 3900 S.; setbacks,
height, size, design on 2300 E. and 3900 S.; center identification/design monument and
real estate signs on 4500 S.; center id signs and electronic signs on 3300 S.; banners, |

mobile signs, and maintenance issues at 3900 S. 900 E.; murals, semi-permanent banners
throughout the City; car sales sign on street poles on State Street; manufacturing areas
and multifamily are relatively clean with monument signs or sign on the building; and

He said the Commission needs to discuss the appropriate signs in each zone. He said
there are a lot of opportunities for change. Chair Stephens asked if staff is going to
present a draft ordinance to the Commission. Lilly said yes and asked if the Commission
had strong feelings about anything. Chair Stephens said he would not mind emulating
West Valley’s standards. Jansen said West Valley started with County ordinances too and
amended them in the first four years of incorporation. Lilly said it is easier to change
signs when a change of use happens. Commissioner Allen said he wants Millcreek’s
businesses to succeed and having a standard will reduce advertising competition.
Commissioner Wilson said she wants to make sure the health, safety, and well-being
priorities are met first, and also making clear delineations on permanent and temporary
signs. Commissioner Allen said there needs to be clear definitions on what signs are.
Lilly said West Valley requires a sticker for permitted signs regulating when they need to
be removed.

Commissioner Wilson asked about general appearance standards for corridors being
addressed in the general plan. Jansen said it talks about appearance standards in the
corridors, but the general plan consultant’s contract does not include ordinance work
other than on Main St. Lilly said they did material pallets in the general plan. Jansen said
there is a transportation master plan that does have cross sections for streets.
Commissioner Carlson said the key is what kind of nonconforming status does the City
give to on-premise signs, what triggers the requirement to change the sign, and what does
that look like. Jansen said that has changed over time and it is now included in the other
nonconformities section, so that gives businesses extra rights but those can be modified
somewhat with changes in the use. Commissioner Healey said on-premise signs should
be on-premise and not used as billboards. Lilly said the State’s nonconforming statute
does give some context to what it means when a sign is non-conforming. He said the City
could define what abandonment is. He said abandoned signs are in state statute and he
wants legal tools to force the changeover. Commissioner LaMar asked about a piece in
code for historic signs, for example the bowling sign on 3300 S. Lilly said most of the
historic signs are in the Millcreek Town Center, so there will be special conditions for
those.

Chair Stephens would like to see an ordinance as soon as possible. Lilly is anticipating on
bringing an ordinance to the Commission in October. Commissioner Wilson said the
ordinance should make sure that on-premise signs are wholly encompassed on-premises,
especially light. Jansen asked Kurt Hansen if there has been any enforcement on
maintenance of signs. Hansen said no. Lilly said the City needs the right code and an
abatement process. Commissioner LaMar asked if yard or event signs would be included
in the ordinance. Lilly said the City cannot regulate the sign by reading the sign. Blaine
Gehring said the County allows for four community event signs throughout the
community for a certain number of days. Lilly said they could be treated as temporary
signs.

1.2 Fence Ordinance Discussion

Jansen said the City started looking at this ordinance then hit the pause button. He said he
walked around his neighborhood and took pictures. He showed the pictures to the
Commission. He said issues to address are: no real standards or maintenance
requirements, height, locations/clear view, materials, front yards, back and side yards,
back facing lots, unique characteristics like sports courts. Fred Lutze said he does not
allow fences on corners that obstruct views. He said he does not think tall fences should
be allowed in front yards. Commissioner Allen asked when vegetation starts to become a
fence. Commissioner Wilson said there could be standards on quantity, visibility and
such with vegetation. Commissioner Healey said the City should split the issue in two,
focus on fences first then vegetative fences. Jansen said staff would avoid the vegetative
issues for now. Commissioner Healey said the City would need to come back to that issue
though because of health and safety.

Lilly said most cities have a reduced front yard fence standard. Lilly said the City could
define the front yard as part of this ordinance. Commissioner Wilson said the materials
list should include anything out of disrepair. The list of materials to avoid are: corrugated
metal without contrasting poles or trim, pallets, laminated wood products, barb wire, and
razor wire. Commissioner Wilson said the City should also list what a fence is. Lilly said
there will be a fence definition in the ordinance. Commissioner Carlson said with fences
already in place, what is the mechanism to replace them. Jansen said the issues would be
complaint-driven.

Commissioner Allen said the type and height should be regulated in the front yard. He
thinks taller fences are okay, but they should be open/see-through. Commissioner Wilson
said see-through fences are also a component of crime prevention. Commissioner Healey
said some backyards front on sidewalks so there should be definitions distinguishing
yards. Lilly said the fencing could be based on use instead of zone. Commissioner Wilson
asked if there were incentives to maintain a design standard. Lilly said new development
could have provisions in development agreements on design standards.
Jansen said there are issues with double fences. Commissioner Wilson mentioned
offering an inducement with double fencing. A discussion regarding the space in between
the double fences took place about it being a child’s play area and a haven for nuisance
animals. Lilly mentioned that the code only requires a building permit for fences taller
than seven feet in height. Jansen brought up fencing for sports courts, pools, and tennis
courts. They need greater fence heights, security, and lighting control.

Lilly said except for residences in Foothills and Canyons Overlay Zone (FCOZ), there are
no residential regulations for retaining walls. There is no definition for it and they can be
used to extend yards. Lilly said he thinks people have used retaining walls to change the
existing grade. Commissioner Wilson expressed concern about erosion for large projects.
Robert May said contractors and landscapers know that after four feet, a permit is
required, so they install walls up to that point. Gehring said North Salt Lake and
Bountiful have good ordinances on slopes. He distributed copies of North Salt Lake’s
ordinance on fences for the Commissioners to highlight what they like.

Commissioner LaMar showed the Commission pictures that he took of fences illustrating
use of various materials. The Commissioners looked at fences that are right next to the
sidewalk and in the right-of-way. Commissioner Wilson said the purpose of the park strip
is to protect pedestrians from vehicular traffic and she is not concerned with what is
planted there. Lilly asked how the Commission felt about chain-link fences.
Commissioner Wilson said it is an affordable option that the City should not regulate if
they are reasonably maintained. She also brought up looking at grant programs to assist
residents, in specifically defined neighborhoods where the City wants to improve
character, to create uniform fence lines or offer an opportunity to buy into a certain type
of fence. Chair Stephens asked what enforcement issues come up that cannot be resolved
by lack of a fence ordinance. May said ninety percent of cases is safety issues with line of
site. Chair Stephens said the ordinance should focus on code enforcement issues first then
appearance issues. Commissioner Carlson said there should be part of the ordinance that
addresses when things must be determined as non-conforming. Chair Stephens said if the
ordinance was focused on safety issues, it would get approved faster. Jansen summarized
that staff will draft something simple that addresses all these concerns.
