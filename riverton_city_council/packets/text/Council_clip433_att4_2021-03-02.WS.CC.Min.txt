34          Suburban Land Reserve Presentation – Benson Whitney, Vice President
35
36   Mayor Staggs introduced Benson Whitney who is the Vice President of Suburban Land Reserve
37   and turned the time over to him for his presentation.

38   Benson Whitney explained that the Planning Commission approved their site with the exception
39   of what to do with Sunday Drive. Mr. Whitney explained that the Planning Commission
40   recommended that Sunday Drive should not go through. He talked about options for their site
41   plan.

42   Councilmember Stewart said that he thought the entire development would have all private
43   roads and expressed concern about having public roads in this development. He said that he
44   would fully support Sunday Drive not going through if this was a private road.

45   Mr. Whitney said that the Planning Commission had debated whether the roads should be
46   public or private. He said that because each lot would have its own tax identification number
47   similar to other neighborhoods then these roads should remain public.

 3   Mr. Whitney said that the pool and park were only for the residents of this development.

 4   Councilmember McDougal said that he supported Councilmember Stewart because the facilities
 5   in the development were only for the residents and that road should be a private road.

12   Councilmember Stewart said he was concerned that this would set a precedence for future
13   developments if the development had public roads. He also said he would like the current MDA
14   issues regarding park space to be addressed.

24            Recommendation for Ordinance Regarding Farm Animals - Ryan Carter, City
25            Attorney
26
27   Councilmember Stewart said that the ordinance was re-written to add language for setbacks,
28   number of animals, and size of the animals in regards to pygmy goats.
29
30   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, said that Riverton City is the pioneer in creating an ordinance with
31   pygmy goats. This type of ordinance is generally found in rural areas. Mr. Carter said that the
32   basic concept for this ordinance would be a minimum lot size of 20,000 square feet and then
33   twice the distance of the minimum setback from the neighboring structure. This
34   recommendation will go to the Planning Commission and then it will come back to the City
35   Council.
36
37   Councilmember McCay says that residents have actually gotten rid of the pygmy goats and
38   supports a change to the ordinance.
39
40   Councilmember Stewart MOVED that the Riverton City Council proceed forward with the
41   recommended changes on farm animal ordinance. Councilmember McDougal SECONDED
42   the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion; there being none, he called for a
43   roll call vote and the vote was as follows: Councilmembers Buroker-Yes, McCay-Yes,
44   McDougal-Yes, Stewart-Yes, and Wells-Yes. The motion passed unanimously.

10            Riverton Personnel Policies and Procedures Manual - David Brickey, City Manager
11            and Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director
12
13   David Brickey said that he asked Kevin Hicks to prepare slides addressing this issue.
14
15   Mayor Staggs said that he was surprised that we were the only city who offered the 10% salary
16   benefit and was worried that there was no year requirement to receive this benefit.
17
18   Mr. Hicks clarified that the current policy is only available to employees who retire from URS
19   and retire from Riverton. Employees cannot receive the benefit if they have previously retired
20   from URS and are now retiring from Riverton.
21
22   Councilmember Stewart said that the salary benefit should start at 5 years and the benefit could
23   change at 7 years and 10 years.
24
25   Councilmember Buroker said that there should be a length of years of service requirement but
26   was neutral as to how many years it should be.
27
28   Councilmember McDougal said he thought there should be a tier vesting system.
29
30   Councilmember McCay said that the benefit should be 5% of pay at 5 years, 6% of pay at 6
31   years, 7% of pay at 7 years, and so on until the benefit capped at 10% of pay at 10 years.
32
33   All of the councilmembers agreed with Councilmember McCay’s suggestion.
34
35   Mr. Hicks asked the council to clarify if the employee had to hit the year mark to get the
36   percentage.
37
38   The council all agreed that the employee must hit the mark to get the percentage.
39
40   Mr. Hicks said that he would prepare the paperwork for this change to the manual.
