RESOLUTION 21-08: ADOPT THE LEGISLATIVE AGENDA OF WEST
VALLEY CITY FOR THE 2021 UTAH STATE LEGISLATIVE SESSION
Nicole Cottle, Assistant City Manager, presented proposed Resolution 21-08 that would
adopt the Legislative Agenda of West Valley City for the 2021 Utah State Legislative
Session.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       As the second largest city in the state it is imperative that the City has a significant
       presence at the state legislature. The City enjoys a particularly strong presence and
       maintains that presence each year through the process of setting guidelines for
       representation and executing on those guidelines.

       Due to the city’s unique position, it has been essential to maintain this presence and
       to proactively work on legislative issues throughout the year. Each January, the
       Council sets forth the guidelines for this work. These guidelines are found in the
       attached resolution. The guidelines allow staff and contractors to adequately
       represent the City on any topic in a constantly changing environment and provide
       protection to the City from any misunderstanding of the City’s position.

       Due to the extremely dynamic nature of the legislative session, staff must be clear
       about the direction of the City Council. This resolution assists in that effort.

       Of the various issues that will be discussed during this legislative session, of
       particular interest are policing, land use, emergency operations, taxation, funding,
       local control, economic development matters, and transportation funding.

The City Council will consider Resolution 21-08 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
January 19, 2021 at 6:30 P.M.

CONSIDER APPLICATION SMI-11-2020, FILED BY STEVEN FLORES,
REQUESTING FINAL PLAT APPROVAL FOR THE STEVEN FLORES
SUBDIVISION LOCATED AT 2283 WEST 3800 SOUTH
Steve Lehman, CED, presented application SMI-11-2020, filed by Steven Flores,
requesting Final Plat Approval for the Steven Flores Subdivision located at 2283 West
3800 South.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

         Steven Flores, is requesting final plat approval for a two lot subdivision in the R-
         1-8 Zone. The subject property is located at 2283 West 3800 South. An existing
         dwelling occupies the front portion of the property and will remain as part of the
         subdivision.

       The applicant is proposing a subdivision consisting of 2 lots. Lot 1 will be
       approximately 10,900 square feet while lot 2 will be approximately 30,900 square
       feet which excludes the stem portion to the new lot. All frontage and area
       requirements relating to both lots will be satisfied as part of the R-1-8 zone.

       Access to the subdivision will be gained from 3800 South. All public
       improvements such as curb, gutter, sidewalk and stamped colored parkstrip are
       currently in place. The applicant is proposing to use a single drive approach to the
       new lot. The driveway for the existing single family dwelling will remain. A new
       water and sewer line will need to be installed for lot 2. The applicant and/or builder
       will need to coordinate with the City Engineering Division the replacement of
       existing infrastructure when the water and sewer line is installed.

       The existing dwelling will remain on what will become lot 1. The existing free
       standing outbuildings will be removed to make way for the new lot. It is anticipated
       that the new home would be located behind the existing dwelling. The remainder
       of lot 2 could be used for a detached garage or garden space as it is rather large.
       Another option could be that the far south portion of lot 2 could be incorporated
       into a future subdivision should property to the south ever develop.

       As with all new subdivision development, there is a concern with the potential of
       ground water impacts. A soils report is needed to determine water table depth. For
       the Northridge Subdivision to the east, the basement depth was approximately 4
       feet below existing top back of curb. If Engineering and the Building Official are
       comfortable using the report that was prepared for that subdivision, a new report
       would not be required. However, the applicant would be relegated to follow the
       recommendations outlined in that report.

Councilmember Buhler asked if there is a flag lot to the east of the property in question.
Steve replied yes. Councilmember Buhler clarified the configuration of neighboring
parcels.

The City Council will consider application SMI-11-2020 at the Regular Council Meeting
scheduled January 19, 2020 at 6:30 P.M.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as

       Tyler Bodrero, representing the property owner, is requesting final subdivision
       approval for the Wallace Stegner Academy Subdivision. The subject property is
       bordered on the north and west by agriculturally zoned property, the east by Rocky

       The proposed subdivision has been submitted to divide an existing 42 acre site into
       one 6 acre lot and a remainder parcel of approximately 36 acres. The 6 acre lot or
       lot 1 as noted on the plat, will be used for a new Charter School. The remainder
       parcel will be developed at a later date.

       As a requirement of the subdivision, the property owner will be dedicating right-
       of-way along Parkway Boulevard. Improvements will be installed along this right-
       of-way consisting of curb, gutter, sidewalk and asphalt. The right-of-way
       improvements along this section of Parkway Boulevard will be constructed by the
       City as a City project.

       To provide access to the school itself, a new public road will be installed along the
       west boundary of the subdivision running north from Parkway Boulevard. This
       road will be a 54-foot right-of-way and will also be constructed with curb, gutter,
       and sidewalk. This road will be built by the applicant. The new road will stub to
       the north providing a connection to the remainder parcel for when it develops.
       Access to the school will be via the new road with two points of ingress/egress.
       Staff has included a preliminary site plan for the School and will include that in the
       power point presentation.

       The final plat identifies two parcels along the west boundary of the plat. The
       alignment of the new road is taking into consideration improvements from the
       adjacent land owner that encroach the proposed subdivision. Anticipating a
       resolution with the property owner to the west, the road has been designed to
       accommodate those improvements along with a new drive access to the single
       family dwelling.

       A permitted use application will be reviewed for the school itself. Site

C.     OPEN SPACE REQUIREMENTS FOLLOW-UP
       Steve Pastorik, CED, provided a summary of discussions the Council has
       previously held. Councilmember Buhler stated that the proposed changes would
       fall in line with other Cities. He asked if staff would be supportive of the changes.
       Steve replied the topic of open space was originally brought up by the Council. He
       indicated staff feels comfortable with the proposed changes if the Council requests.
       Steve explained that most landscaping is negotiated through development
       agreements. Councilmember Buhler suggested adding language to the code that is
       normally negotiated through development agreements. Councilmember Fitisemanu
       agreed and asked why other cities don’t include trees in their calculations. Steve
       replied he is unsure but noted that the City’s current code doesn’t calculate trees
       internal to a project either. Councilmember Lang asked if the changes would apply
       to multi-unit residential. Steve replied yes. Councilmember Lang stated that she
       likes the proposal. She encouraged trees to be planted and indicated that she feels
       the proposed changes are a good start. Mayor Bigelow stated that there has only
       been a couple developments that would need to meet these requirements. Mayor
       Bigelow suggested adopting the changes recommended by staff. Councilmember
       Buhler agreed and encouraged language be added to incorporate what is normally
       negotiated in Development Agreements.
