37          Budget Study Session
38
39   Nick Geer, Administrative Services Director, discussed budgeting practices including the general
40   fund. Some Councilmembers expressed concern about reducing general fund reserves before fully
41   understanding how funds would be allocated. There was also discussion about past practices of
42   shifting funds between accounts, particularly subsidizing utility services.
43
44   Mr. Geer discussed a proposed change to remove the Dutchman to Old Liberty Road project from
45   the road impact fee budget due to it not being included in the current impact fee plan. He explained
46   that the project could be added back once the updated impact fee study is completed and formally
47   includes it. Several Councilmembers expressed concern that removing the project entirely could
48   signal to residents that it was no longer a priority, particularly given prior public expectations. In
49   response, alternatives were discussed to maintain transparency, including listing the project in the
50   budget without an assigned dollar amount and noting it as “under study” or pending the updated
51   impact fee plan.

 1   Mr. Geer discussed the general capital improvement fund and explained that some projects,
 2   including the rodeo grounds building addition, were deferred. The Council approved a split that
 3   reduced skate park funding and restored funding for the rodeo grounds building addition.
 4
 5   The Council discussed water funding and clarified that the current budget did not include bonding,
 6   but that future discussions would be needed to address long-term water capital needs. Staff
 7   explained the budget adjustments were intended to support near-term projects while deferring some
 8   costs, though Councilmembers expressed concern that this could lead to future pressure to raise
 9   fees or consider bonding if funding does not keep pace with needs.
10
11   Mr. Geer reviewed the water impact fee and secondary water capital funds, noting that no major
12   changes were proposed aside from minor adjustments tied to project timing and ongoing impact fee
13   study. He discussed the culinary water system and explained the complexity and cost of bringing
14   other wells online, including testing and regulatory requirements, and emphasized the need for a
15   broader long-term water strategy.
16
17   The Council discussed whether the City should continue funding memberships in two chambers of
18   commerce. Some members expressed concern about the overall value of chamber participation
19   while others noted networking benefits and support for local businesses. After discussion, the
20   majority of the Council agreed to discontinue participation in the Mountain West Chamber and
21   remain with the South Valley Chamber.
22

24             Water Rates and Conservation Discussion
25
26   Mr. Geer introduced a discussion on water rate communication and conservation messaging,
27   seeking Council input on how best to educate the public about new water rates. Councilmembers
28   suggested using multiple communication channels, including social media, a water bill insert,
29   newsletters, and updates on the City’s website.

31
32             General Plan Discussion
33
34   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, presented updates to the City’s general plan
35   priorities, including continued focus on improving trails, active transportation, and land use planning
36   along major transportation corridors. Councilmembers discussed expanding pedestrian
37   infrastructure details such as sidewalks and bike routes. The discussion also focused on land use
38   conflicts along major corridors such as Redwood Road and 12600 South, particularly the tension
39   between state-driven density expectations and UDOT’s transportation and access constraints.
40
41   Mr. Lethbridge outlined next steps for the general plan update process, including upcoming
42   individual meetings with Councilmembers to discuss district-specific land use and planning
43   concerns. He also indicated that a joint work session with the Planning Commission was being
44   scheduled, likely in May, to review proposed updates and gather additional input. He noted past
45   low turnout at standalone open houses and expressed support for leveraging existing community
46   events to increase participation.
47
48             Fencing Ordinance
49
50   Kevin Hicks, City Manager, discussed the proposed updates to the City’s fencing ordinance as part
51   of an effort to simplify code language and provide greater flexibility in development standards. He

 1   explained that the current requirement for solid masonry fencing in non-compatible land use
 2   situations had created challenges, particularly in infill areas where conditions vary widely.

 4   Mr. Hicks noted that upcoming ordinance revisions would also remove burdensome requirements
 5   such as notarized affidavits and instead provide clearer standards for Planning Commission

 4   Discussion/Action Items
 5
 6             Resolution No. 26-15 – Approving the Riverton City Water Shortage Plan
 7
 8   Cary Necaise, Public Works Director, discussed and reviewed Riverton’s Water Shortage Plan,
 9   which outlines tiered response levels for drought conditions affecting both culinary and secondary
10   water, aligning with Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District’s current drought level two
11   designation. He clarified that while the City can adopt its own internal response structure and
12   enforcement measures, it cannot avoid District-imposed penalties for exceeding water allocations.
13
14   Councilmembers emphasized the need for flexibility in managing escalating drought levels,
15   particularly in how parks are irrigated and potentially used, with staff noting parks are being
16   categorized by service level to prioritize limited watering resources. Concerns were raised about
17   long-term impacts on park conditions, maintenance costs, and the potential need to adjust
18   recreational usage during higher drought stages.
19
20   This item was continued to a future meeting to amend the language so that drought level decisions
21   are clearly made through elected leadership.
