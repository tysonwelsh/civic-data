27   Community and Development Director, Michael Johnson, presented the Staff Report and stated
28   that Project CUP-22-011 involves a request for a wall height extension to construct an eight-foot
29   wall along the north property line at 7275 South Caladora Cove. This matter was noticed and
30   heard at the October 5, 2022, Meeting. It is a Conditional Use Application for a wall height
31   extension that allows the construction of fencing, walls, and combinations of the two for a height
32   beyond six feet. The wall was previously built without proper approval and has gone through a
33   tenuous history of applications, withdrawals, illegal construction, and appeals that have led to this
34   point. The City’s Appeals Officer has instructed Staff to accept these types of applications and
35   process them as a potential remedy for the violation.
36
37   Mr. Johnson reported that only a portion of the fencing extends beyond six feet in height. A
38   property rendering was displayed. The property slopes from the front yard to the back yard where
39   the total height at its highest point at exactly eight feet. The request is allowed with a Conditional
40   Use Permit without requiring consent from the neighbor. Staff received a comment from the
41   adjacent neighbor stating that they do not believe the fence should be defined as a fence or wall
42   but rather as part of the structure of the home and, therefore, subject to residential setbacks. Based
43   on the definition of fencing as listed in the Ordinance, Staff considers it to be a fence or wall and
44   the rationale for fence height increases includes the following:

11   Mr. Johnson reported that the rationale submitted by the applicant included providing the family’s
12   pets with unassisted access to the backyard, screening utilities that serve their home, mitigating
13   noise from their animals to neighboring properties, and providing increased privacy. Since 2019,
14   approximately 15 of these types of applications have been reviewed and approved using privacy
15   as the primary rationale. He explained the portion of the wall greater than six feet is located along
16   the natural slope portion of the property and the extended portion is consistent in high point
17   elevation with the rest of the wall. Although the fence has already been constructed, the applicant
18   must complete all necessary steps to acquire a Building Permit, including potentially opening up
19   a portion for inspection. The permit application must be submitted to the City within 30 days of
20   any decision. Width issues were discussed.
21
22   Commissioner Ebbeler referenced City Code Chapter 19.84.080 Subsection B, Paragraph 2 which
23   states that the use will not under the circumstances of the particular case be detrimental to the
24   health, safety, comfort, order, or general welfare of persons residing or working in the vicinity.
25   He also noted that the same section specifies that appropriate buffering will be provided to protect
26   adjacent land uses from light, noise, and visual impacts. He was underwhelmed by the neighbor’s
27   legal argument regarding the structure but noted that there were issues raised in terms of additional
28   noise that should be addressed. He understood the precedent and emphasized the importance of
29   evaluating the criteria and determining whether it is met or not.
30
31   Mr. Johnson referenced the Staff’s finding that determined that dogs were present prior to the
32   fence. As a result, use of the side yard as a dog run was not being reviewed but rather the
33   construction of a portion of the wall over eight feet. The fence itself does not affect the noise
34   impacts that occurred prior to its construction.
35
36   Senior City Planner, Samantha DeSeelhorst stressed the importance of addressing the precedent
37   of other wall height applications where privacy has been a significant factor. Although rationale
38   was provided, she did not feel that everyone needs to agree with the determination or the Staff
39   recommendation. Mr. Johnson confirmed that Staff reviews and analyzes each application the
40   same way and referenced the Conditional Use Permit criteria.
41
42   Commissioner Anderson considered the neighbor’s statement regarding noise to be irrelevant to
43   the evaluation of the eight-foot fence request. She felt there were different issues present that need
44   to be resolved. Ms. DeSeelhorst explained that issues with odor, sight, and sound may be dealt
45   with by Code Enforcement. She questioned whether noise should be considered as part of the
46   fence application.

15   height for one-third of the fence and why the entire length is not limited to six feet.
16   Ms. DeSeelhorst explained that the additional rationale that has been provided for the application
17   and determined to hold up by Staff was the increased need for privacy based on slope conditions
18   that exist between the two properties. Staff found that there are other factors present that merit
19   recommendation and approval.

21   Commissioner Anderson did not believe that the eight-foot portion of the fence has created a
22   negative impact. The Commission discussed the relevancy of the additional impact of the eight-
23   foot section. Commissioner Shelton stated that the fence has nothing to do with dogs or the use of
24   that section of the yard. The question should focus on determining the impact. City Attorney,
25   Shane Topham, agreed and believed that the visual impact helps determine the impact of the eight-
26   foot section of fencing.

35          3.1       Project CUP-22-011 – A Public Hearing and Possible Action on a Request by
36                    Katherine Pasker, on Behalf of Robert Bryne, for a Wall Height Extension to
37                    Construct an Eight-Foot Wall Along the North Property Line at 7275 South
38                    Caladora Cove.
39
40   Chair Allen reported that this is the second time the matter has appeared on the agenda and was
41   discussed during the Work Session. Staff had nothing to present.
42
43   Dan McDonald identified himself as the legal counsel for Mr. and Mrs. Yaganti who live adjacent
44   to the subject property. The proposed is technically a building structure; however, if considered
45   to be only a fence, they believe it does not meet the rigorous clear, and convincing evidence
46   standards under the law. There are no unique or special circumstances of a natural material or



     Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 10/19/2022                                    4
 1   adverse condition that are minimized or eliminated by granting a Conditional Use Permit. He
 2   stated that the applicant is facing a self-imposed hardship. Mr. McDonald believed that placing
 3   the dogs closer to his clients’ home is not the most reasonable solution. He stated that the applicant
 4   violated the law, was cited criminally for building the fence without a Building Permit or
 5   Conditional Use Permit and represented that they were withdrawing their prior application. There
 6   are no equitable or mitigating circumstances that would justify granting the permit and his clients
 7   would like to see it torn down. He encouraged the City to uphold the rule of law.
 8
 9   Jim Wright identified himself as the legal counsel representing the applicant, Robert Bryne. He
10   believed the issue is not whether the fence should be torn down but the small portion of the fence
11   that exceeds the six-foot height allowance. What is being considered is whether Mr. Bryne should
12   remove the portion of the fence that exceeds six feet. The question of legal standard is set forth in
13   Utah State Law and is found in Utah Code Section 10-9a-507. The Statute states that a Land Use
14   Authority shall approve a Conditional Use if reasonable conditions are proposed or can be imposed
15   to mitigate the reasonably anticipated detrimental effects of the proposed use in accordance with
16   the applicable standards. He emphasized that it is their belief that the Ordinance is in conflict with
17   the State law and the City cannot change the State law standard. They were unaware of any
18   detrimental facts with the strip that exceeds six feet and it must be shown that it cannot be mitigated
19   by a reasonable condition. They were aware of no detrimental impact and based on Utah Law, the
20   application must be approved.
21
22   Mr. Wright reported that there was an honest misunderstanding on the part of Mr. Bryne when the
23   fence was originally constructed in terms of why he retracted the original Conditional Use Permit.
24   This is an attempt to correct that misunderstanding and go through the required process to ensure
25   compliance and obtain approval under the appropriate applicable standard.
26
27   Commissioner Ebbeler reported he reviewed the public comment and had difficulty rationalizing
28   the issues. The Planning Commission is bound by law through determinations based on Section
29   19.84 of the City Code and the 16 criteria listed. He believed that considering other Conditional
30   Use applications has no bearing on this case. He was frustrated by the questions raised by the
31   adjacent neighbor that seem to conflict with the determination that includes nuisance and noise.
32   The criteria were reviewed.
33
34   Mr. Wright stated that based on the Utah Statute previously referenced, the standards shared by
35   Commissioner Ebbeler are unlawful. He struggled to understand the detrimental impact that the
36   presence of the small strip of fence would have on the health, safety, and welfare of anyone.
37
38   Robert Bryne reported that it was a misconception that the primary reason for constructing the
39   fence was to serve as a dog run. He stated that the fence was built to ensure the safety and security
40   of his family. He described the activity of a former friend who entered his backyard, threatened
41   his life and that of his family threw a rock through their window and was charged criminally. He
42   emphasized the need for security and to protect his home and family. Mr. Wright reiterated that
43   they are focusing on the small portion that exceeds the eight-foot section, which has no bearing on
44   the location of the dogs.
45

 1   Community and Economic Development Director, Michael Johnson, stated that the City has all of
 2   the facts that are relevant to the case and recommended that the Commission deliberate.
 3
 4   Commissioner Ebbeler did not feel that Section 19.84.080 has been met. The difficulty comes
 5   with determining the compliance of the fence when there are complaints from the neighbor
 6   regarding noise and nuisance issues. He believed there is a gate that boxes in the area for a specific
 7   purpose. Mr. Johnson reported that Staff’s perspective is that a Conditional Use is allowed if
 8   permitted by City Code and tied to the use of the property as single-family residential. Regardless
 9   of whether there is a fence, the homeowner is still allowed to use that portion of the property for
10   residential purposes as they see fit.
11
12   Commissioner Steinman wanted to better understand Commissioner Ebbeler’s perspective. He
13   believed that whatever is located inside the six-foot section is outside the Planning Commission’s
14   purview. Whether it is a gated-off garden or a dog run is not a deciding factor. He understood
15   that the six-foot section is built by right. The eight-foot section is the focus and the Commission
16   should look at the process of bringing it into compliance with the Code.
17
18   Commissioner Anderson did not see the detrimental impact of the additional portion of fencing
19   that exceeds six feet. The criteria include the evaluation of whether it is detrimental or not. She
20   considered the noise issue to be irrelevant when considering the current request. Those types of
21   issues are to be managed by Code Enforcement and not the Planning Commission.
22
23   Commissioner Ebbeler argued that the fence is built and the gate is located where the applicant
24   has focused on placing the dogs in a small area of a large yard. He did not consider it to be an
25   arbitrary and capricious standard to assume that a wall should exist but opposed the rectangular
26   area created for a very specific reason.
27
