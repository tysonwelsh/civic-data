Council Business

Jordan School District (Presentation)

             Mayor Clayton turned the time over to Jordan District:             Superintendent Dr.
Anthony Godfrey.

               Dr. Anthony Godfrey, Jordan School District, introduced Nicki George.

              Nicki George stated she has represented this area since Janice Voorhies left.
With the new redistricting, she will lose this area.

               Dr. Godfrey introduced Scott Festin from the Planning and Enrollment, Michael
Anderson, Associate Superintendent, Scott Thomas, Administrator of Auxiliary Servies, and Tami
Pyfer, Community Engagement Specialist. They want to continue to strengthen relationships with
municipalities. They have the same goals; just different roles. They have been focused on
promoting literacy, particularly for students in Kindergarten through 3rd Grade.

               Scott Festin, Planning and Enrollment, Jordan School District, reviewed growth
projections for transportation. He handed out a map showing the western part of the district.
Jordan District no longer has any schools in Copperton. The students are bussed to other
locations. There are 105 students in Copperton. Growth in areas outside of Copperton could
affect where Copperton students attend school.

               Council Member Severson asked if enrollment struggles are due to too many
students.

               Mr. Festin stated there are a lot of schools that have too many students.

              Council Member Bailey asked if we could request a survey to the elementary
school parents to see if they can attend a school that is closer.

              Dr. Godfrey stated that can be done. They have been cautious and do not want
Copperton to move to different schools as growth continues. The district will not make changes

              Council Member Bailey stated if the children go to a new elementary, it feeds into

               Mayor Clayton asked how hiring issues are going.

Jordan School District has been a leader in a lot of ways and she wants to be helpful. She

Public Hearing of Annexation Policy Plan


    Public Comment

               Mayor Clayton opened the public hearing.

                Council Member Stitzer stated Kayla Mauldin, Planner, Municipal Services
District, has done a lot of work ensuring that everything with the policy is up to the state code.
She has also included a lot of information on studies and research and growth for Copperton.
They have met several times to come up with different ideas, maps, and what they see for the

future.

                Kayla Mauldin, Planner, Municipal Services District, delivered a PowerPoint
presentation entitled Copperton Annexation Policy Plan and Ordinance. The reviewed what they
are; policy plan and ordinance purposes; background; state code requirements; content; the main
ideas: expansion areas; future land use; annexation policies; important considerations; what’s
next; staff recommendations; planning commission recommendations; and then took questions.


     Discussion/Motion to approve Annexation Policy Plan

               Mayor Clayton opened the public hearing.

              Ben Scholle thanked the Council for their had work. He asked if we are going to
keep the bridge open for wildlife to pass through. He also asked what will be planted in the green
zone.

               Council Member Stitzer stated it will stay open. The landscaping will be up to the
landowner.

               Ryan Perry, Rio Tinto, stated the maps are not set in stone. They want to work
with the town council and create a pathway to establish priorities.

               Mayor Clayton closed the public comment

              Council Member Severson, seconded by Council Member Olsen, motioned to
adopt the annexation policy plan as written. The motion passed unanimously.

                                 −−−−−−−−−−−−−−

Public Hearing of Annexation Ordinance

               Mayor Clayton opened the public hearing.

                Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, stated when there is an annexation policy plan,
it is used for people to make decisions to approve or deny annexation petitions. But there still
must be something in the code to govern the process. Copperton inherited Salt Lake County’s
code, but it needs to be reevaluated. It should contain principles that will be used to review an
annexation petition.

     Public Comment

               There was no public comment.

               Mayor Clayton closed the public hearing.

Committee/Board Updates

    Legislative Research Committee

               Mayor Clayton stated the legislative session has closed.

                Nathan Bracken stated there were a lot of water bills this session and this may
come into play with talks of annexation. He spoke about HB303 and HB146 which take away
cities’ incentives to have affordable housing. He also spoke about HB151 which restricts the
ability of municipalities to us redevelopment agencies for retail development.
