Agenda Review

scheduled to begin at 6:00p.m. There was a brief focus on the "Idle-Free Week" proclamation relative to efforts underway to

Council/Mayor/Community & Economic Development Discussion
          Mayor Caldwell stated he received a few questions from Council staff in advance of this discussion; first was a request
for information on the 2021 Utah Legislative Session and Administration's areas of focus. He noted this has been a difficult year

Administration is closely monitoring 163 bills that stand to impact Ogden City if they are adopted and there are 47 bills that the
City supports. He noted Management Services Director Brown has been intimately involved in the Session and he thanked her for
her efforts aimed at protecting the City from negative legislation. It appears that local government is under attach this session as

Love, Listen, and Lead project sponsored by the Utah League of Cities and Towns (ULCT). Early in the pandemic, the League
became aware that there are so many cities and towns in the State that do not have the resources that larger cities have, and this led
to a 'nasty' public dialogue. The ULCT decided to lead out in setting a different tone to counteract the negative national dialogue

leadership and he applauded the ULCT and its membership for pulling together to provide accurate information and being a
calming influence. The League has also worked to address collective police reform and Ogden's own police chief was involved in
those efforts. He then noted he is happy to answer additional questions, or the Council can direct questions at Community and
Economic Development staff that is present tonight.

 Proposed Honorary Street Name Designation - Bingham Fort Lane                                                                             ,
           Council Deputy Director Symes explained Anna Keogh has submitted a petition to assign an honorary street name for a                 )
portion of 2nd Street between the Union Pacific railroad tracks and Washington Boulevard. The proposed honorary street name is
"Bingham Fort Lane" in honor of Bingham Fort, which was located in that area. The proposal was reviewed by the Planning
Commission and a recommendation of approval for a shorter distance was forwarded to the Council with a vote of 7-0. As
recommended by the Planning Commission, the honorary street name would apply to the portion of 2nd Street between Century
Drive and Wall A venue, which the Planning Commission found to align with the fort's historic location more closely. Upon initial
review by the City Council, the Council requested that the proposal be reviewed by the City's Diversity Commission. This review
was completed, and the Diversity Commission forwarded a recommendation of denial to the Council with a vote of 9-1. The
Diversity Commission's recommendation was based on the belief that the proposal did not accurately represent and honor the
perspectives of all the peoples of the area at the time. The Council's February 2, 2021 work session will allow the Council to
review all information provided by the Diversity Commission, as well as additional information from the petitioner, and provide
direction for staff to continue the consideration process.
          Council member Hyer stated he would like to hear from Ms. Keogh.
          Ms. Keogh indicated the request is for 2nd Street to have the honorary name of Bingham Fort Lane, which was the name
of the street until1890 when streets were numbered. Residents in the area that are interested in preserving the history are trying to
merge the past and present; they believe the placement of a street sign with the honorary street name would be a quick and easy
way to inform those passing by that Bingham Fort existed. Council member Hyer asked Ms. Keogh to explain the nature of
Bingham Fort. Ms. Keogh stated the Shoshone Indians lived in Weber County when settlers arrived; from the onset, the two
groups were friendly to one another, though there were cultural differences. The fort was formed responsive to the Black Hawk
War in Central Utah; lives were lost in battles fought in the war and all communities along the Wasatch Front were advised to
build a fort for security purposes. The fort was not really needed in Weber County and the fort doors remained open for Indians
and settlers to go in and out at will. During the winter of 1854, the Shoshones ran out offood, so they surrendered their arms and
moved into Bingham Fort and Ogden Fort and lived with the settlers until spring at which time they moved out of the forts to
resume their nomadic lifestyle. The friendly relationships between the Indians and the settlers continued until the time that the
railroad came to the community.
          Council member Choberka thanked Ms. Keogh for the information she provided; she also appreciates the
recommendation to honor both cultures and she aske Ms. Keogh for more information about that. Ms. Keogh stated that one point
that seems to have been overlooked by the Diversity Commission is that there is already a road in the Bingham Fort area called
Indian Camp Road; however, the group wants to pursue other monuments that would honor the history of the Indians in the area.
They are working with the developer of the residential project at the corner of2nd Street and Wall A venue to include a statue of
Chief Little Soldier. Council member Choberka noted the Diversity Commission has suggested that someone reach out to
representatives of the Shoshone Nation to see what they feel would be appropriate in terms of honoring them. Ms. Keogh stated
she would also like that outreach to take place and she will communicate any feedback from the Shoshone Nation in the letter she
will send to the developer of the project regarding what type of monument they would like. Mr. Symes stated the City does not
have a defmed process for that type of outreach and that is something that may be addressed in the rewrite of the ordinance that
governs honorary street name designations. Council member Choberka stated she would like to have input from the Shoshone
Nation before the monument is fmalized. Council member Nadolski agreed and stated he feels the Diversity Commission can be
relied upon to provide input regarding those types of issues.
          Discussion among the Council and staff centered on the ability of the City to influence the fmal decision to be made by
the developer regarding the type of historical monument he will include in his project.
          Council member Nadolski then referenced the Diversity Commission's recommendation and noted he feels that the
Council should deny the petition based upon their recommendation. Council member Hyer stated he does not see the conflict with
recognizing the past and the history of this area; some will say he is biased, but there is precedent in the City in terms ofthe streets
