 2.     Planning Commission Review and Discussion of the Taylorsville General Plan
        Update – Chapter 3: Land Use (Mark McGrath, AICP / Long-Range Planner)

Long-Range Planner Mark McGrath explained that the Land Use chapter was arguably
the most important chapter of the General Plan. At least 90% of the issues that go
before the Planning Commission stemmed from this chapter, so it was important to get
it right. He reminded the Commissioners of the General Plan outline, its Guiding
Principles and Fundamental Strategies:

General Plan Chapters-
  1. Introduction
  2. Community Character
  3. Land Use
  4. Mobility
  5. Economic Development
  6. Parks and Recreation
  7. Neighborhoods
  8. Moderate Income Housing
  9. Environmental

Guiding Principles:
   ▪ Balance, Diversity and Social Equity
   ▪ Stewardship
   ▪ Resilience
   ▪ Civil Beauty and Character
   ▪ Health and Mental Wellbeing

Fundamental Strategies:
   o Strengthen Community Identity and Character
   o Create Vibrant Economic Centers and Mixed-Use Neighborhoods
   o Maintain and Create Distinguishable, Stable, and Desirable Neighborhoods
   o Create a Multi-Modal Community

Mr. McGrath sought the Commission’s input before he would present them with the final
draft in May. The last portion of the discussion that evening would involve the
Taylorsville Expressway Station Area Plan which would ultimately be adopted as an
addendum to the Land Use chapter. There would be a public hearing involving that plan
on April 23rd.

Some relatively recent changes to the land use market included a reduction in brick-
and-mortar retail as well as an increase in remote work, resulting in a diminished need
for office space.

City’s primary objectives would be to plan to accommodate another 15,000 people

Mr. McGrath explained that rather than focusing on the number of units per acre as has
been done in the past, the updated classifications would focus more on the building
type.


                                            3
According to multiple community surveys, residents had made it clear that they wanted
the identity of their neighborhoods protected. While they understood growth was
necessary, they did not wish it to happen in their own neighborhoods. In what Mr.
McGrath referred to as the 3% Strategy, ninety percent of new growth should be
accommodated on 3% of the City’s land. To that end, he presented five principles for
achieving the 3% Strategy. They were:
   1) Focus growth in centers along major transportation corridors and near transit
      stations.
   2) Create areas of high-density mixed use in targeted areas throughout the City.
   3) Encourage infill and redevelopment.
   4) Limit change in existing neighborhoods.
   5) Preserve open space and sensitive lands.

After much discussion, staff had settled on nine “Site Specific Planning Areas” where
they hoped to focus future growth. Those areas were:
   ▪ 2700 West/I-215/4700 South
   ▪ 4800 South Redwood Road
   ▪ 4800 South Historic District
   ▪ Center Point: 5400 South Redwood Road
   ▪ Redwood Road south of I-215
   ▪ West Point: 4000 West/Bangerter/5400 South
   ▪ Westbrook Elementary
   ▪ Taylorsville Expressway Station Area Plan

Commissioner Young said he loved the concepts and would love to talk about how to
steer the development. Senior Planner Jim Spung responded that the General Plan was
a vision of what Taylorsville could become. Then the Zoning Map could be amended to
allow for that vision to take place. However, all the Planning Areas were privately owned
and the most the City could do was help guide any interested developers.

Mr. McGrath asked the Commissioners what their vision was for the Crossroads
Shopping Area and the general district. Commissioner Quigley responded that he would
love to see it more architecturally attractive, become mixed-use, perhaps 3-4 stories tall.
He found Farmington Station very appealing. Commissioner Young agreed.

Commissioner Wright felt the business community as well as city residents would likely
support the idea of building to a specific pre-defined vision, particularly if their input was
considered.

After moving on to discuss the area of Redwood Road immediately south of I-215, Mr.
McGrath pointed out that transportation and traffic congestion were major issues for the
area. He wondered what the Planning Commissioners envisioned there.

Commissioner Wright commented regarding the lack of access to many of the area
properties and the fact that UDOT was unlikely to allow that to change. Mr. McGrath
responded that it was possible a parking lot aisle could become a collector road of sorts

which would connect various properties which were quire valuable. Commissioner
Wright clarified that they did not hold much value individually but did so collectively.

A brief discussion regarding the status of the Volta project followed.

Regarding the former Westbrook Elementary area, Commissioner Young asked what
Mr. McGrath proposed for that area. He responded that it would likely be medium- or
low-density residential. Commissioner Young pointed out there would be pressure to
create open green space there.

Mr. McGrath explained that flexibility was one theme of this chapter of the updated
General Plan. He stated that the goal was to get away from being a community where
commercial, residential and office development were all segregated. A more cohesive
and dynamic community was the objective.

Commissioner Wright asked in what ways he believed the update accomplished that.
Mr. McGrath responded that one way was in the new employment district that would
allow other types of use within that zone to provide those working in the area with
necessities such as places to eat lunch, to secure daycare, etc. He said he also sought
much more mixed-use development in the community to complement the transit
corridors where there were a variety of land use types all within the same area. He
mentioned that the most profitable land use a city could have from a tax base
perspective was high density mixed use because it combined retail dollars with high-
value buildings so that the city received both property tax and sales tax.

Taylorsville Expressway Station Area Plan
Mr. McGrath reiterated that there would be a public hearing on this matter at the next
Commission meeting. State law required that any community with a fixed guideway
transit system must have a station area plan that would facilitate affordable housing and
other housing. There were three such stations in Taylorsville. The study area was quite
large, and the City was required by law to notify everyone within it and within 300’ of it of
the public hearing. Therefore, notices were sent to the owners of 1,387 properties. No
one would be forced to redevelop their property, but if they ever chose to, the City would
have a vision established for that transition. He provided the Commissioners with a brief
overview of the plan.

Commissioner Young pointed out that Fore Lakes was adjacent to a historic area, and
he wondered if anyone had considered tying the two together. Mr. McGrath said that
had not been discussed but was an intriguing idea.

Mr. McGrath wrapped up his presentation by explaining the four objectives provided by
the State of Utah regarding Station Area Plans:
   1) Must increase the availability and affordability of housing;
   2) Must promote sustainable, environmental practices such as water conservation,
       air quality, etc.;
   3) Must enhance access to opportunity—jobs, shopping, education, etc.;
   4) Must increase transportation choices and connectivity;

He pointed out that the City’s goals actually went beyond those objectives. The hope
was to create a place of lasting value—a place with a very high quality of life. There
would also be an additional step in the adoption process in that once the final plan was
adopted by the City Council, it must be certified by a committee made up of
representatives of the Wasatch Front Regional Council and the Utah Transit Authority.
That was required before Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) could begin operations.

The Planning Commission and staff discussed procedural issues for the April 23 rd public
