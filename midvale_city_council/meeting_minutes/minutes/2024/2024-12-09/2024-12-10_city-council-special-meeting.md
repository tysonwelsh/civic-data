       C.     Mayoral Interviews

Matt Dahl said this is the first time at Midvale City that a mayor has left mid-term and
there was a need to replace the position. Matt explained the origin of the term Mayor
and when the position of Mayor first was filled, he also explained the rules and
responsibilities of the mayor position in Utah and Midvale City.

Matt explained the “weak” mayor form of government that Midvale City has, the city has
a 6-member council, the council holds both executive and legislative authority, meaning
that the powers that the mayor holds are granted to the position by the council
members. He talked about what characteristics he believes make a good mayor and
expressed his appreciation for the willingness of all the candidates to want to lead
Midvale City.

Matt reviewed the process of selecting a City Mayor as follows.

   1. Candidate Interviews
The City Council will evaluate the submitted applications and letters of interest from the
candidates to determine their qualifications and suitability for the position.
   • Each qualified candidate will be interviewed by the City Council.

   •   Candidates will speak in order that their applications were submitted.

   •   Since there are 11 candidates, each will have 2 minutes to speak.

   •   Up to an additional 5 minutes per candidate is allotted for questions from the
       Council. Any Council Member that is also a candidate for mayor cannot ask
       questions during those 5 minutes.

   2. Voting Process for Mayoral Selection
   •   A candidate must be approved by the majority of the City Council to be selected.

   •   City Council members cannot vote for themselves.

   •   Voting will be conducted by ballot. In each round, council members will receive a
       personalized ballot to select their chosen candidate. The City Recorder and City
       Attorney will collect and count the ballots, announce the results, and place the
       ballots in an envelope labeled with the voting round. This process ensures that
       candidates do not vote for themselves, the vote count is verified by two officials,
       and the ballots from each round are kept separate for future reference.

   •   If the first round of voting does not result in a candidate receiving the majority vote
       of the Council, a second round of voting will be conducted between the two
       candidates with the most votes in the first round of voting.

   •   As provided for in State law, the City Recorder will conduct a coin toss to break a
       tie in the event of a tie between candidates. The City Recorder will assign each
       candidate a side of the coin before each coin toss. If, after the first round of voting,
       a tie occurs between three or more candidates, the City Recorder will conduct a
       round-robin coin process to determine which candidate(s) will advance to the
       second round of voting.

ground to be a successful flip.

   •   Approve the interview process for city council candidates.

State law requires that every qualified applicant for a city council vacancy receive an
interview before the City Council. This typically involves candidates speaking to the City
Council and the public during a City Council meeting. The city council must determine the
process for candidate hearings, including the speaking order and time limits for each
candidate.

Staff recommended allowing candidates to speak in the order their applications were
submitted. If there are 10 or fewer candidates, each would have 3 minutes to speak; if
there are 11 or more, each would have 2 minutes. Each candidate would have up to 5
minutes to answer questions from council members.

   •   Approve the process for addressing tie votes.

State law allows for up to two rounds of voting by the City Council to select a candidate
to fill the city council vacancy. If no candidate receives a majority in the initial vote, a coin
toss may be required to narrow the field to two candidates. The second vote then selects
the new city council member from the two remaining candidates.

State law requires that the municipal clerk to use a coin toss to reduce the field of
candidates to two candidates in the event of an initial vote which generates two or more
tied candidates (e.g., vote distributions like 2-1-1 or 1-1-1-1). Staff suggests that each of
the tied candidates be paired off with another tied candidate. The City Recorder will assign
each a side of the coin prior to each coin toss. The City Recorder will then flip a coin. The
candidate whose assigned side of the coin is facing up will advance while the other
candidate will be eliminated.
