Administrative Services Director, Bruce Kartchner reviewed the Public Hearing Items and
reported that Items 8.1 and 8.2 concern adoption of the Certified Tax Rate for the General Fund.
They are separate items because they address Bluffdale taxes for Salt Lake and Utah Counties.
Most of the other funds being considered tonight do not have a property tax that can be levied so
the Certified Tax Rate from 8.1 and 8.2 is just for the General Fund. Each year, the County and
the State prepare the calculations to determine what the new rate should be. This year's rate is
.000976. This is based on the amount collected last year and the anticipated dollars with growth
to be collected this year. This rate is lower than last year's rate of.001161 because as property
values have increased in the City, the rate has decreased accordingly. He recommended adopting
the rate of.000976.


Resolution 8.3 pertains to the Final Operating Budget for the General Fund and all other funds
with the exception of the Fire and Police Protection Fund, the Local Building Authority("LBA")
Fund, and the Redevelopment Agency ("RDA") Fund. They will be handled in separate
resolutions.

Mr. Kartchner gave a broader perspective so citizens would understand the breadth of the work
they do in preparing the budget. They look at a very broad group of budget strategic drivers, one
of which is trying to maintain conservative fiscal responsibility for the City. They have a strong
desire to maintain current service levels. One driver this year is they need to increase the service
levels in the Fire Department. They also want to make sure infrastructure is maintained
responsibly.


The City owns millions of dollars worth of infrastructure that deteriorates if not cared for, so
making sure that infrastructure is being cared for and does not deteriorate is a huge responsibility.
1300 West at one point became a gravel road because it had not been maintained for years. The
City increased revenues to the point that that could be taken care of. The City now uses roads that
have a specific cycle to make sure every road is touched within a certain timeframe to make sure
the usable life remains above a certain level. It is less expensive to maintain than to replace and
repair.


They want to remain on stable financial footing as a community. Some things can be done in a
single year that may provide long-term sustainability.

Two full-time front-line positions are in this year's budget as well as a full-time Assistant Public
Works Director. As infrastructure has grown,this has become a necessity. The construction ofa
Public Works Building is included to house the personnel and equipment necessary to service
public infrastructure. They want to make sure to maintain exceptional service levels by retaining
well-trained and skilled employees and providing for the increasing costs of building and
maintaining infrastructure. Inflation has been high but the costs of building and construction-
related equipment and products are at a greater rate than inflation as a whole. The budget results
keep a healthy, appropriate financial reserve.

Mr. Kartchner reiterated that there are different types of budgets with different purposes. The
General Fund is primarily the City's operating fund. The newly created Fire and Police Protection
Fund was discussed. The three funds collectively are where services are generated and personnel
is paid, so will include wages, salaries, and benefits.

The General Fund is the least restrictive. As money comes in, it can be used however the City
deems necessary. At times a restricted amount will be deposited, such as a grant, and it will
immediately be transferred into a fund that provides for the restrictions necessary on those specific
items.


The Water Operating Fund is a business-like enterprise fund. It uses full accrual accounting rather
than modified accrual accounting like other funds.


The Fire and Police Protection Fund is a restricted fund that includes fire and police costs such as
salaries and equipment. The Fund has the power to levy a tax under State statute. Any funds
generated or transferred into the Fund may not be used for any other purpose than fire and police
protection.


Capital Projects Funds are generated in a variety of places. Most recently, the bulk of the money
that is in the Capital Projects Fund comes from specific grants for specific items,such as the trestle
to be replaced on 14600 South beneath the railroad. Several million in state-allocated funds have
been received for that and the City will receive more funds over the next several years as those
allocations come due to be sent to them. There is leftover COVID money that has not been spent.
These funds are not being used for operations, per Council direction, as one-time costs are
appropriate for one-time revenue. The City had a sudden need for a firetruck and was able to locate
one, and American Rescue Plan Act("ARPA")funds were used to acquire it. An ambulance also
died this year and fortunately, they were able to find an ambulance to purchase with ARPA funds.

There are a variety ofgrants specific to the projects occurring there. Some ofthe money there will
be surplus fi-om the General Fund at the end of any given year, which surplus funds may or may
not be able to transfer.


The Roads Fund specifically provides money for road maintenance. It has two primary sources.
One is the gas tax that everyone pays when purchasing fuel. The other is fi*om a few years back
where the State, in conjunction with the County, designated some ofthe sales tax increment to be
provided specifically for roads funds. Cities were finding that as cars become more efficient and

as electric cars appear that do not use fuel, that fuel tax revenue was decreasing. This created a
need to provide maintenance funding from some other source. This was one of the alternatives
provided.


It includes a variety of impact fees. As development occurs, to meet the needs of the new
development, developers, and contractors pay an impact fee for the additional impact to the
community. That money is collected in the funds and can be spent only on things related to growth.

The final item is the Economic Development Administration ("EDA")funds that collect the tax
increment on specific project areas and can be used for very specific items or projects that occur
in and benefit those areas. Some of the principles they use in developing a budget include
prioritizing cost. Those priorities generally come fi*om different discussions they've had with the
Council where they have directed specifically or staff has recommended and Council's approved
that these are the important things, and they prioritize costs accordingly. They next try to match
revenue to expenses.


Restricted funds, including Capital Projects, the Road Maintenance Fund, the RDA, LEA, and
impact fees, the Water Fund, and the new Fire and Police Protection Fund cannot be used to pay
for General Fund operating expenses. They cannot simply pull money out ofthe Water Fund and
use it to pay for police officers or firefighters. Money cannot be taken out ofthe Capital Projects
fund to pay for other operations or given to the Public Works Department to maintain parks and
roads.


Another basic principle is to maintain stable reserves. They do this for a variety ofreasons. First,
the reserves are important in case there is an actual rainy day, and rainy day funds are only used
when it is raining, which is not the current situation. During the Great Recession, rainy day funds
were used as it was notjust raining, but pouring. Mr. Reid pointed out that using these funds would
be like paying your mortgage out of your savings account, which is a bad situation. Reserves
should be spent on specific, extraordinary expenses, not ongoing costs.

Mr. Kartchner mentioned that the City will occasionally need to take out debt to fund projects and
the primary reviewer offinancials with respect to debt is Moody's. They rate cities on the quality
of their financial stability and Bluffdale has an extremely high rating, which bumped up a couple
of positions in January. The ability to borrow money cost-effectively is dependent upon those
