NON-CONFORMING USE DISCUSSED:

   City Administrator Seth Atkinson explained to the council that In the last couple of months, the city
has been involved with a code enforcement issue regarding the conversion of a carport to a garage on a
property located at 15 W. 600 S. that does not conform to the current CU1 zoning setback requirements.
It had been a legal, non-conforming use and structure, with an existing carport, but has recently been
converted, into a garage, without proper building permit, or zoning permission. When this was reported
to City staff they had concerns that it appeared the homeowner had possibly expanded further into the
front yard, and side yard setback, which could make this now an illegal, non-conforming structure and
use, as well as having concerns that a required building permit had not been pulled, and required
inspections completed.
    In the past, staff has interpreted the zoning code to allow for carport to garage conversions in non-
conforming structures if the footprint is not expanded, and adequate fire protection has been
addressed. To establish if setback encroachment had occurred, and in an effort to work with the
homeowner to get a building permit for proper inspections to happen, the city staff and building official
attempted to contact the homeowner with those goals in mind. Contact with the homeowner was
successful, but disregarded, and staff ended up red-tagging, or placing a Stop Work Order on this
project. The homeowner subsequently continued construction following the red tag, which led to a
citation ultimately being issued to the homeowner. The city staff worked with the homeowner to get a
building permit issued for the garage, so that it could be retroactively inspected to the degree it could
be, having missed seven initial required inspections, and looking to establish if zoning code was indeed
violated. Upon inspection it was found that the garage was extended two (2) feet to the north which
illegally encroaches into the thirty (30) feet setback on the frontage of the property, which also made
the originally non-conforming side yard encroachment expand as well. However, the inspection to the
improvements on the garage conversion were found to enhance the fireproofing of the structure, vastly
improving the fire hazard. As the building permit was issued, a fine was assessed because work
continued after a red tag, and the homeowner has paid this fine at the time of building permit
issuance.

The homeowner was advised that the garage created a larger footprint but that there was a section of
the Nephi City Code that could be used by the homeowner to potentially allow for this expansion on a
non-conforming use. This code section is Nephi City Code 10.21.4 (B)(4) which states:

         An application for substitution or extension of a nonconforming use may be made to the
         legislative body by the property owner or certified agent. The legislative body shall give
         consideration to the nature and condition of adjacent uses and structures and their interaction

Another difficulty has arisen with the inspection of the garage. It was observed, during the inspection of
the garage, that a connection has been made between the garage and the existing house that essentially
creates an addition on the home. Within this addition, plumbing, electrical, and load bearing
construction has occurred without a building permit or an inspection. To receive a proper inspection,
some sheetrock on the walls would need to be torn out, and an additional building permit obtained.
    Mr. Atkinson suggestion three different option that the council could consider. The four following
options were suggested:

    1.  To deny the homeowner the extended garage. If the Council chooses this option, the
       homeowner will need to remove two feet of the north facing section of the garage but could be
       allowed to enclose the previous footprint of the carport. This could be covered under the
       existing carport to garage conversion building permit, but would require another inspection
       once completed
    2. To grant the substitution or extension as is. This would allow the homeowner to keep the
       extension on the garage.
    3. To grant a permit subject to such requirements and conditions with respect to location,
       construction, maintenance, operation, and duration of the proposed use as it may deem
       necessary for the protection of adjacent properties. To date, adjacent property owners have
       not issued any complaints, formal or otherwise, regarding the improvements to this property.
    4. To grant the substitution or extension as is with the condition upon obtaining a building permit
       for the additional and remodel between the home and garage, and following through on what is
       necessary to responsibly receive inspections, including possible removal of sheetrock from walls,
       or other modifications the building official deems necessary for code compliance. This would
       allow the homeowner to keep the extension on the garage.

   Trevor Jones the property owner addressed the council and said that the structure still meets the
required 30-foot setback. Mr. Jones stated that there were some structural issues with the carport and
as he has had time, he has fixed the structural issues by adding a foundation wall, concrete siding, sheet
rock and asphalt shingles. These improvements have made the structure safer. He thought that if he
was making improvements to an existing structure that he didn’t need a building permit. He showed
pictures of the improvements.
    After some discussion, Mayor Pro Tempore Skip Worwood opened a regular council meeting.
  Councilor Parady asked why when he had personally had a similar project, he was told that he needed
to stay within the same footprint of his existing structure. City Planner Shauna Talbot answered that she
was new to the job at the time of his project and his expansion was much larger. City Attorney Kasey
Wright said that we simply did not know about this provision in the code at the time.
   Councilor Parady moved to accept the option of permitting the extension as is but requiring Mr. Jones
to obtain a building permit for the addition and remodel and to have all necessary inspections.
Councilor Shari Cowan seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

TRUTH IN TAXATION PROCESS DISCUSSED:

   Mr. Atkinson presented a power point prepared by the Central Utah Recorders Association that went
through the Truth in Taxation process. The Utah State Code specifies that the municipality must contact
the County by March 1 to inform them that they want to raise property taxes. The council appreciated
the presentation and directed staff to discuss this issue early next year in order to meet the March 1st
deadline.

CENTRAL UTAH FOOD BANK REQUESTED $2500.00 ONGOING CONTRIBUTION:

   The council discussed a request presented to them by Juab County for Nephi City to contribute $2500
annually to the Central Utah Food Bank. The council wanted more information of who was requesting
the money and maybe add this to next year's budget requests.
