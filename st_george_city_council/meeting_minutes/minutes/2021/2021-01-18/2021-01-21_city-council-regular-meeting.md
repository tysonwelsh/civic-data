SWEARING IN OF NEW MAYOR:
    City Recorder Christina Fernandez administered the Oath of Office.

      Mayor Randall thanked the Council for their support and the City staff for taking her
      under their wings. She noted that all of her family is in attendance except for her
      husband, Tony, who was attending via Zoom because he was at home recovering
      from a recent heart attack due to Covid-19. During his heart attack, while be carried
      away by ambulance, he made her promise not to withdraw her name, no matter
      what happened as he believed the City needed her. She thanked him for his support
      and his love – no one can do this job or be on the Council without the support of
      their family. She thanked her mom and dad for raising her and her siblings to be
      strong and independent and taught them to can do whatever they want in life if they
      worked hard. She thanked her children for being supportive and her grandchildren,
      especially her granddaughters. They looked at the pictures of the previous Mayors,
      all of which were strong men; however, now there is a picture of a woman on the
      wall. She spoke about her favorite interview; it was with an 8-year old little girl who
      dreams of being a zebra trainer. She knows this little girl will follow her dreams of
      being a zebra trainer. She will work so hard for the City that she and so many other
      people love. Finally, she thanked her sisters and sister in law for their texts of
      encouragement.

      Councilmember Larkin congratulated Mayor Randall.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard congratulated and welcomed Mayor Randall to her new
      role on behalf of all City staff.

CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT:
     Consider approval of a Conditional Use Permit to develop building 1100, a
     single-story building, on Lot 1 in the Commerce Point Phase 1 commercial
     subdivision located on South Bluff at the intersection of Black Ridge Drive
     and the I-15 interchange.

      Planner Mike Hadley presented the request for a conditional to develop building
      1100, a single-story building, on Lot 1 in the Commerce Point Phase 1 commercial
      subdivision located on South Bluff at the intersection of Black Ridge Drive and the I-
      15 interchange. The Planning Commission recommended approval. He provided a
      PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics: Overview; Vicinity map;
      Zoning map; Map of proposed building; Site plan; Elevations; and Materials board.
      He noted the parking being provided meets City code.

      MOTION:
          A motion was made by Councilmember Hughes to approve the
                    conditional use permit to develop building 1100, a single-story
                    building, on Lot 1 in the Commerce Point Phase 1 commercial
                    subdivision for exceeding 20,000 sq. feet.

      Councilmember Hughes asked why this needs a conditional use permit.

      Community Development Director John Willis explained a conditional use permit is
      required due to the size of the building. The project exceeds 20,000 sq. ft.
      aggregate for all of the buildings triggering the need for a conditional use permit.

      Councilmember Hughes added “for exceeding 20,000 sq. feet” to his motion.

      SECOND:
          The motion was seconded by Councilmember Larkin.
      VOTE:
          Mayor Randall called for a vote, as follows:

                    Councilmember Hughes – aye
                    Councilmember Smethurst – aye
                    Councilmember McArthur – aye
                    Councilmember Larkin – aye

                    The vote was unanimous and the motion carried.

IMPACT FEE FACILITIES PLAN/IMPACT FEE ANALYSES/ORDINANCE:
     Consider approval and adoption of the proposed Impact Fee Facilities Plans
     (IFFPs), the Impact Fee Analyses (IFAs), and the Impact Fee Ordinance for
     Storm Drain and Parks & Recreation.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard noted the Council is familiar with this item as there
      have been quite few discussions regarding these Impact Fees. He explained the
      Council has already approved the other Impact Fees that the City assesses on new
      growth. Tonight, the Council is specifically considering Storm Drain and Parks &
      Recreation Impact Fees. The Council discussed these fees at a work meeting last
      Thursday. The Homebuilders Association was present at the meeting. Staff has
      answered all of the questions Council had. Staff recommends the Fees be adopted.

      Mayor Randall noted there has been a lot of discussion regarding the proposed fees
      and the Southern Utah Homebuilders Association (SUHBA) was involved.

      Councilmember McArthur noted the City has hired the most qualified group to assess
      what the Impact Fees should be. The numbers provide are the best possible
      numbers that can come from the best possible source. He commented that if the
      City put out a survey and asked if new growth should pay for itself, he believes the
      answer would be yes, it should. He ran for office saying that he would try to
      preserve the quality of life in St. George. He believes the parks and recreation is
      what provides that quality of life; therefore, he does not want the service level to
      diminish on these items. Prices are going up and St. George is the second fastest
      metropolitan area that is growing in the state, and Utah is the fastest growing state
      in the nation. The City has significant fees for new growth. In his opinion, although
      he loves what the builders and SUHBA are doing for the community, he does not
      know where else to get the money for these services.

      Councilmember Larkin commented this is really challenging for the Council. The
      builders have been great partners. SUHBA was heavily involved in the Master Plan
      for which this assessment was made. Impact Fees cover 28% of the impact, not
      fully paying for the impact. An impact fee is a one-time fee that has to be
      maintained in perpetuity. She believes there needs to be discussions in the near
      future to evaluate what the City already has and if the City can keep up the level of
      investment. Trails mean a lot to her; however, does the City need to evaluate which
      ones are being utilized and which aren’t. She noted that attainable housing is
      needed.

      Mayor Randall stated maintenance for these types of facilities is paid out of the
      general fund, Impact Fees cannot be used.

      Councilmember Smethurst noted when building additional parks and trails, more
      staff is needed. Currently, the City is understaffed in a number of areas. He
      commented the City needs to look at how it will keep up and how to fund the
      maintenance of them.

      Councilmember Hughes commented there will be in an increase in the general fund
      revenues with new growth. The previous City Manager warned him about taking on
      a number of parks, medians, etc. He agrees with most of what has been said. An
      issue for him is the cost of ongoing maintenance. New growth is not paid only by
      Impact Fees, there are other things the developers pay for such as roads and
      infrastructure. The building industry in the community is an important part of the
      economy. When Impact Fees are charged, it does not come out of the builder’s
      pocket per se, the homeowner will pay that. If the market softens, there will be
      some pressure on the price of homes and Impact Fees can some impact on that.
      Historically, the City has never adopted 100% if the proposed fees.

      MOTION:
          A motion was made by Councilmember Hughes to approve the Parks &
                    Recreation Impact Fees at 95% of the recommendation.
      SECOND:
          The motion was seconded by Councilmember Larkin.
      VOTE:
          Mayor Randall called for a roll vote, as follows:
