BACKGROUND:

      Rik Dunham is requesting a plat amendment of lot 4 in the CR England Subdivision. The
      purpose for the amended plat is to create a new lot for the expansion of an existing
      Dominion Energy facility.

ISSUES:

      The CR England Subdivision was recorded in December 2013. The subdivision consisted
      of 4 lots south of the 2100 South frontage road at approximately 4525. A small parcel
      owned by Dominion Energy was not part of the subdivision but is located immediately
      adjacent to lot 4 in the northeast corner of the subdivision.

      Due to modifications that Dominion Energy is making on these small regulator stations,
      they are in need of expanding their existing site. Since the small parcel was not part of the
      original CR England Subdivision plat, they are proposing the amend lot 4 by creating a
      new lot of approximately 9,800 square feet.

      Access to the new lot will continue to be gained from the existing access along the 2100
      South Frontage Road. Although access could also be gained from the existing right-of-
      way easement serving the other lots in the subdivision, staff recommends that they receive
      approval from the City Traffic Engineer regarding the point of ingress/egress to ensure
      proper distance from the intersection.

      Future expansion of the facility would be subject to a permitted use review by staff.

STAFF ALTERNATIVES:

   1. Approve the CR England Lot 4 Amended Subdivision subject to a resolution of staff
      concerns.

   2. Continue the application for reasons determined in the Planning Commission meeting.

C-21-2021
Western Storage (Outside Storage)
6551 West 2100 South
M Zone (10 acres total, 6 acres for outside storage)

The applicant, Western Storage, is requesting conditional use approval for outside storage at
6551 West 2100 South. This property is zoned M, manufacturing and the West Valley City
General Plan designates this area as light manufacturing. This parcel borders Salt Lake City to
the north and the zoning there is OS, Open Space. Surrounding land uses include vacant
undeveloped land to the north, Bland Recycling to the east, and the Western Auto Wrecking
salvage yards to the west.

This site was originally part of a larger salvage yard (C-60-76). In 2019, the southern 4 acres
were improved for a Tow Yard (PR-12-19). The remaining 6 acres have since been cleared and
cleaned up with the intent of improving the property for outside storage. The exact tenant and
storage use has not been determined at this time but the attached Letter of Intent outlines the type
of storage that may occupy the property, including the following:

      Farm equipment, tracked vehicles, construction equipment and related tools.
      Cars, trucks and other wheeled vehicles.
      Recreational vehicles including trailers, ATV’s and UTV’s
      Raw construction materials/supplies
      Household Goods
      Con-ex boxes and similar storage containers

The applicant has indicated they will not permit boats, combustible materials, perishables,
animals, explosives, sewage, garbage, refuse or any toxic or hazardous waste to be stored on the
property.
The surfacing requirements for all driving, maneuvering areas and storage for wheeled vehicles
is hard surfacing. Areas that utilize tracked equipment may be on a gravel surface. The plans that
have currently been submitted indicate both surfaces will be used with the access and Fire lane
being paved. Public Works has discussed the drainage plan with the applicant and they have
provided drainage calcs as if the entire area was paved. This will allow for some flexibility in
improvements without having to modify the drainage plans based off the surfacing. The exact
requirements and specifications for surfacing, access, drainage and fire protection, including
hydrants, will be determined at a later date once a tenant submits a business license and more
details are provided on what will be stored where on the site.

There are currently no buildings proposed for the storage yard with the intent that the use would
be an unmanned facility. If it is determined by the Building Department that a restroom is
required those facilities must be located within a permanent structure that is connected to water
and sewer. Portable/temporary restrooms are not permitted for permanent uses.


The southern boundary of this site is located along the UT-Hwy 201 North Frontage Road which
is a High Image Arterial. The landscaping was updated when the towing business located on site
however they were not required to fully comply with all of the standards due to the valuation of
the improvements at that time. There are also plans of widening this frontage road in the next 2-4
years. Therefore, WVC Public Works will be recommending a Delay Agreement for this
frontage and the required improvements.

The frontage along the north side, along 2100 South, will also need to be improved. There must
be a minimum 20’ landscaped area installed along with the required pavement cross section and
streetlights. Public Works will also be preparing a Delay Agreement for the curb, gutter and
sidewalk improvements along this frontage.

The outside storage will be screened from public view with a six-foot chainlink fence with vinyl
slats. The fence will be setback a minimum of 20’ from both frontages.

At this time no dumpster enclosure locations have been shown and no signage has been
proposed. If these are proposed at a later date they will need to comply with the standards in the
West Valley City Code.

Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any concerns raised at the public hearing as well as the
following:

   1. The approved use for this application is outside storage.

   2. All vehicles, trucks and trailers shall be stored on a hard surface. Tracked equipment,
      heavy equipment or materials may be stored on gravel.

   3. Outside storage is only permitted in areas behind the fencing and must be screened from
      public view. The fencing will be chain-link with vinyl slats.
   4. If restrooms are required by the Building Department they shall be located within a
      permanent structure that is connected to water and sewer. No temporary or portable
      restrooms will be permitted.

   5. The existing landscaping on the south side of the site must be maintained and improved
      per the original plans (PR-12-19).

   6. The landscaping on the north side of the site shall be a minimum of 20’ wide, include a
      berm, 1 tree every 30’ and a minimum on 50% live plant material not including tree
      canopies.

   7. Must meet the requirements of all affected departments and agencies.

   8. Subject to review upon valid complaint.

Continuance, to allow for the resolutions of any issues raised at the public hearing.

M-2-2021
Hunter Park Plaza Landscaping
Exception 3550/3556 S 5600 W
C-2 Zone (1.7 Acres)

The property owner is requesting modifications to the requirements of Section 7-10-103 of
Title 7, Landscaping Standards Applicable to All High-Image Arterials. This property is one
of many that was impacted recently by UDOT’s widening of the intersection at 3500 South
and 5600 West, in connection with the Mountain View Corridor project. Both streets are
subject to the regulations outlined in section 7-10 of the West Valley City Municipal Code,
which defines landscaping standards for properties that have frontage on high-image arterial
streets. The right-of-way improvements eliminated landscaping next to the sidewalk. The code
requires a minimum of a 20’ landscape buffer adjacent to the sidewalk, but there is currently
only a three to eight foot area remaining. The Planning Commission can grant a modification
to the 20’ landscaped buffer requirement if the property owner can demonstrate that
compliance would necessitate “elimination of improvements required by other ordinances
which could not be relocated elsewhere on the site” (7-10-104(1)(b)).

The three existing buildings and parking would be impacted by requiring the full buffer. The
property owner is requesting an exception to the 20’ requirement. Due to the narrowness of the
remaining landscaped area, staff also recommends that lawn and trees should not be required,
but there shall be at least 50% live plant material. The applicant provided a landscape proposal
for the frontage. Besides the existing shrubs that are in front of the middle building, the
applicant is proposing to fill the remaining areas with Red Japanese Barberry. Staff doesn’t
typically get too involved in plant types being proposed, but we recommend that the plan be
modified. Barberry shrubs have a lot of thorns, so having them line the entire sidewalk may be
problematic. Also, these shrubs are known for collecting a lot of blowing garbage and debris.
Aesthetically, it would also be better to have more variety.

Staff recommends that a new plan be prepared that includes at least three different shrub/plant
varieties. Each separate planting area shall have an equal mix of plant types, with at least 50%
live plant material. Taller plant varieties shall be closer to the building with a maximum height of
3’ for all plant varieties.

Staff Alternatives

Approval. Twenty feet of landscaping is not required. The landscaped areas along the frontage
can remain in their current configurations and not include lawn or trees, but shall also meet the
following conditions:

     1. There shall be at least 50% coverage of live plant material in all landscaped areas.
     2. Each separate landscaped area shall include at least three plant varieties, with an
        equal mix of plant types.
     3. A planting plan shall be provided to the West Valley City Planning Division and
        approved by staff before any work is done.
     4. Installation shall be completed by July 1, 2022.

Continuance, for reasons determined at the hearing.

of many that was impacted recently by UDOT’s widening of the intersection at 3500 South and
5600 West, in connection with the Mountain View Corridor project. Both streets are subject to
the regulations outlined in section 7-10 of the West Valley City Municipal Code, which defines
landscaping standards for properties that have frontage on high-image arterial streets. The
right- of-way improvements eliminated landscaping next to the sidewalk. The code requires a

the property owner can demonstrate that compliance would necessitate “elimination of
improvements required by other ordinances which could not be relocated elsewhere on the site”

50% lawn and providing two additional trees, selected from 7-10-103(3). The area west of the
drive approach is not included in this request. The property owner will be installing the full 20’
landscaped buffer and meeting all other provisions of the code in that section.

M-4-2021
Speed Queen Laundry Landscaping
Exception 5670 W 3500 S
C-2 Zone (0.36 Acres)

The property owner is requesting modifications to the requirements of Section 7-10-103 of Title
7, Landscaping Standards Applicable to All High-Image Arterials. This property is one of many
that was impacted recently by UDOT’s widening of the intersection at 3500 South and 5600
West, in connection with the Mountain View Corridor project. Both streets are subject to the
regulations outlined in section 7-10 of the West Valley City Municipal Code, which defines
landscaping standards for properties that have frontage on high-image arterial streets. The right-
of-way improvements eliminated landscaping next to the sidewalk. The code requires a
minimum of a 20’ landscape buffer adjacent to the sidewalk, but there is currently only about
an 18’ buffer on the east end, and then it narrows significantly on the west end of the property.
The Planning Commission can grant a modification to the 20’ landscaped buffer requirement if
the property owner can demonstrate that compliance would necessitate “elimination of
improvements required by other ordinances which could not be relocated elsewhere on the site”
(7-10-104(1)(b)).

The building would be impacted if the 20’ landscaped buffer is required. The site currently has
23 parking stalls, where only 12 are required per the code. However, the applicant has
indicated that most of the stalls are used throughout the day. On the east side of the building
only about 2’ of landscaping would need to be added to meet the 20’ requirement. However,
that would eliminate three parking stalls. On the west end of the building one parking stall
would have to be removed to meet the buffer requirement. The property owner requested an
exception for the full 20’ buffer along the entire frontage. In exchange for this, he offered to re-
landscape the area west of the building. The issue with this is that area is part of another parcel,
and that property owner should be responsible for maintaining the landscaping there.

There are also other issues on the site to consider when evaluating the landscaping exception
request. The drive access on the west was impacted by the road widening, and the curb and
gutter were recently re-installed. Requiring the 20’ buffer here would either make for an
awkward connection to the new improvements or would require that they be removed and
reinstalled. However, offering a full exception to the 20’ buffer and leaving things as they are
also looks unfinished. There is sidewalk in front of the building that now leads to nowhere on the
west edge, and the new curb and gutter that was installed along the parking lot access ends
abruptly and does not look finished. There are also two sidewalks coming towards the building
from the new sidewalk along 3500 South, which is a bit unusual. There were two sidewalks
coming into the site, but in a different configuration, prior to the road being widened, so staff
guesses that UDOT installed two sidewalks again just because that is what was there before.
Staff checked with the Building Inspection Division, and only one sidewalk is needed to meet
ADA requirements.

There are several different ways to approach the landscaping exception request for this
property. Staff has provided five different options below for the Planning Commission to
consider and make a recommendation.
Staff Alternatives

Option 1

Approval. Twenty feet of landscaping is not required, but the following modifications shall
be made to the site as described below and shown in Figure 1.

     1. The sidewalk coming into the site that is farthest west shall be removed and that area
        shall be landscaped. Only one sidewalk is needed to meet ADA access requirements.
     2. The sidewalk that runs in front of the building shall stop at the edge of the parking on
        the west side. The extra concrete shall be removed, and asphalt shall be added to this
        section to match the parking lot.
     3. New curb and gutter shall be added to match what was recently installed for the new
        drive access along the north edge of the landscaped buffer. These improvements can
        end at the edge of the sidewalk that runs along the west side of the building.
     4. Landscaping shall include at least 50% lawn, except for in areas narrower than 8’ wide.
     5. There shall be at least 50% coverage of live plant material in all landscaped areas.
     6. Five trees shall be selected from 7-10-103(3) and be installed.
     7. A planting plan shall be provided to the West Valley City Planning Division and
        approved by staff before any work is done.
