    2.2. Discussion regarding the Impact Fee Accounting - Alan Rae, Finance Director and Blake
          Thomas, Community Development Director
Community Development Director Blake Thomas gave an introduction to impact fees presentation. Impact
fees were a form of development exaction. They were a one-time charge for a new development to raise
revenue for new or expanded public facilities necessitated by the development. The improvements have a
useful life of ten-years and be owned or operated by a local government to be eligible. They were also governed
by state statute. They were paid for by developers and builders and were set to be used specifically for qualifying
projects according to fund type.

Impact fees were not a tax, building permit, utility hook-up, or an application fee. They were not recurring
costs, a savings account for the City, funds used to do operation, or maintenance of infrastructure, free to be
used on whatever project the City Council wanted to do. The project had to be on the Impact Fee Facilities
Plan (IFFP).

Impact fees were determined if imposed by the City Council. Growing communities needed impact fees to
fund public infrastructure costs. Director Thomas offered a brief overview of the steps to determine what the
fee should be set at. He reviewed the Master Plan requirements, IFFP, Impact Fee Analysis (IFA) and the
enactment.

The adoption process started with the determination if an impact fee was necessary. Public notice was
published notating the City planned to enact or change the IFFP or IFA. The City hired a firm to prepare
July 8, 2020 – City Council Minutes                                              Page 3 of 14
plans for the analysis and offer a time to hold a public hearing prior to City Council consideration which
ultimately had a ninety-day post adoption effective date. The impact fee funds included: transportation, water,
storm drains, and parks.

Director Thomas presented the Herriman transportation impact fee facilities plan. This represented the next
ten-year plan of projects which included twenty-nine total projects. Not all projects were eligible to be paid
one hundred percent by impact fees. Water facilities plan had twenty-eight future projects. Storm drain
facilities plan had some new developments. This were lower than other fees because there were less projects.
The Parks impact fee facilities plan had many projects planned but was more flexible.

Finance Director Alan Rae reviewed financial aspects of the impact fees in detail, giving examples and
hypotheticals that were identified in the packet.
