Proposal 
 
The Summit County Community Development Department has received an application for a 
Conditional Use Permit for Parcel WMS-5 (15.75 acres), located at 388 East Boulderville Road, 
Summit County, UT. The proposal includes a ~24,500 sq. ft. indoor riding arena with view areas, 
storage, stall, an office, and staging room. The proposal includes a ~6,900 sq. ft. stable with 
stalls, showers, tack rooms, tie-ups, restrooms, and a laundry room. The stable includes a 1,000 
sq. ft. dwelling on the upper floor. The proposal also includes a ~2,400 sq. ft. Quonset 
Hoop/Shop and ~1,600 sq. ft. Quonset Hay and Horse storage structure. Lastly, the proposal 
includes an outdoor riding arena, several pasture areas, runs, paddocks and horse sheds.

• Onsite riding and training activities (not intended for public use)

The applicant/owner (Kimbrough & Timothy Raduenz) propose the development of a 
single-family residence with accessory agricultural equestrian/livestock improvements on the 
property located at 388 East Boulderville Road, Oakley, Utah, within East Summit County.  
The proposed use of the property is agricultural livestock boarding and care, specifically for 
horses and potentially smaller livestock (mini horses, goats, etc.). Horses are classified as 
livestock, and the proposed improvements are customary and necessary for their care, shelter, 
conditioning, and management. 
Use will include:  
● Onsite riding and training activities (not intended for public use) 
● Use of the arenas by private boarders, owners and limited riders associated with the 
property. 
● Horse/livestock boarding 
● Long-term housing and care for retired or non-working horses 
The proposal is consistent with the rural, agricultural, and low-density character encouraged by 
the East Summit County Development Code and is designed to preserve open space, support 
ongoing agricultural productivity, and minimize impacts to surrounding properties. 
 
Residential Components 
A single-family dwelling will be constructed on the front portion of the property, oriented toward 
Boulderville Road and designed to be compatible in scale, massing, and placement with existing 
residences in the area. The residence will comply with all applicable zoning standards, including 
setbacks, height, and lot coverage requirements. 
An accessory caretaker residential unit will be incorporated within the barn structure located on 
the west side of the property. This unit will provide on-site housing for a full-time caretaker or 
agricultural employee whose responsibilities include livestock care, feeding, health monitoring, 
facility oversight, and property maintenance. The caretaker unit is necessary to ensure proper 
animal welfare and day-to-day management of the operation. 
 
Agricultural Equestrian Use and Improvements 
The large, open area of the property (approximately 15.75 acres) will remain dedicated to active 
agricultural use associated with livestock (horses). Improvements will include: 
● One Indoor Riding Arena (not to exceed 30,000 sq ft)

Water Use 
Water use on the property will support residential occupancy, livestock watering, and routine

20 shares of Boulderville Ditch irrigation water are associated with the property and owned by 
Kimbrough & Timothy Raduenz.  We are in the process of converting 3 shares (roughly 6.8

Manure, Waste, Fly and Snow Management 
Manure Management, Storage Location and Design 
Manure will be collected daily from stalls, paddocks, and holding areas and transported to a 
designated manure storage area located on the north side of the indoor arena.   
The storage area will be constructed on a concrete base. The area will include perimeter 
containment via concrete berm and will be graded to prevent runoff from leaving the storage 
area.   
The storage area will be located with appropriate setbacks from ditches and drainage features 
and designed to allow routine cleaning and equipment access.  
The manure storage area will be covered to reduce precipitation contact, likely using a tarp 
system. 
 
Manure Disposal and Land Application 
Manure will be managed through a combination of on-site land application and off-site removal. 
On-site land application will occur on the south ~six acres using a manure spreader and will only 
be conducted under suitable conditions and in appropriate amounts. Manure will not be applied 
during frozen ground conditions, saturated soils, active runoff, snowmelt conditions, or when 
application could result in runoff. 
Off-site removal will occur using a dump trailer and/or dumpster to an approved disposal facility, 
(most likely Henefer Landfill) when land application is not appropriate and when storage

Winter Operations and Snow Management 
Winter operations will include continued daily manure collection and transfer to the storage

Fly and Pest Control

frequency. 
Manure will be removed daily from animal areas and maintained in a managed storage pile. Fly

If necessary, a labeled larvicide may be applied in accordance with manufacturer instructions as 
a supplemental control measure. 
 
Dust Control 
Dust will be managed as needed during dry conditions. 
Control measures will include watering high-traffic areas such as arenas and equipment routes, 
maintaining appropriate surface conditions, using arena footing that is designed to minimize 
dust, managing vehicle speeds, and maintaining vegetative cover where feasible outside of 
traffic areas.

The applicant is requesting approval to amend an existing Conditional Use Permit (CUP) to 
allow additional uses on the property while maintaining the currently approved operations 
under CUP file #17 -165. The proposed amendment includes a site fill anticipated to occur over

accepted. The fill area will be graded to match existing natural  grade with topsoil and

designated area will be used for recycling activities, including the screening and crushing of 
earth materials and recycled construction products such as concrete and asphalt. Hours of 
operations would comply with applicable Summit County noise regulations, including restricted 
operating hours between 9:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m., Monday through Saturday, and before 9:00

Chapter 7: Open Space  
 
The Open Space Element establishes policy direction for preserving, managing, and 
strategically using open space throughout Eastern Summit County. Open space is a deﬁning 
feature of the region and plays a vital role in protecting natural systems, supporti ng 
agricultural operations, safeguarding water resources, maintaining wildlife habitat, and 
preserving the small-town rural character valued by residents. 
 
This Element supports the County’s broader land use strategy by encouraging compact 
development in appropriate locations while preserving signiﬁcant natural landscapes, 
working agricultural lands, and scenic resources. Maintaining open space between 
communities helps preserve community identity, prevent continuous low- density 
development, and guide growth toward established community areas. 
 
Open space also supports recreation and quality of life by providing opportunities for 
walking, biking, horseback riding, hunting, and other outdoor activities. Long -term 
stewardship of open space requires coordination among public agencies, private 
landowners, municipalities, and conservation partners to ensure that growth and 
conservation occur together in a balanced and sustainable manner. 
 
Context  
 
Open space in Eastern Summit County includes a network of public lands, working 
agricultural lands, and natural landscapes that together deﬁne the region’s identity and 
environmental health. Publicly owned lands, including national forest, state parks, and 
reservoir areas, provide important watershed protection, wildlife habitat, and access to 
regional recreation. 
 
Privately owned lands also play a signiﬁcant role in the open space system. Large areas of 
agricultural land function as working open space that preserves scenic views, supports local 
agriculture, and maintains wildlife movement corridors. These lands als o help maintain 
separation between communities and reinforce the rural landscape. 
 
Open space performs essential environmental functions across the region. Watersheds, 
wetlands, riparian areas, and groundwater recharge zones support water quality and long -
term water availability for agricultural, ecological, and community needs. Protecti ng these 
resources is fundamental to sustaining both the natural environment and rural 
communities. 
 
Wildlife habitat extends across both public and private lands, making coordinated planning 
essential to maintaining connected landscapes and reducing habitat fragmentation. 
Recreation opportunities are also closely tied to open space, with trails and publi c lands 
providing access to outdoor activities that support community health and quality of life. 
71
Long-term preservation of open space relies on a combination of conservation tools, land 
use planning, and partnerships that help protect resources while accommodating 
appropriate growth. 
 
Deﬁnitions  
 
Open space within Eastern Summit County serves a variety of functions, including 
agriculture, conservation, recreation, scenic preservation, and environmental protection. 
Open space may be publicly owned or privately protected through conservation easements, 
deed restrictions, development agreements, or subdivision requirements. 
 
Open Space Types 
Working Agricultural Open Space : Lands actively used for agricultural purposes such as 
grazing, hay production, crop production, or other agricultural operations. These lands 
function as open space while supporting local food systems, preserving rural landscapes, 
maintaining wildlife habitat, and sustaining the agricultural economy. 
 
Community Separation Open Space : Open lands located between towns, neighborhoods, 
or along major transportation corridors that preserve visual separation between 
communities, maintain rural character, and discourage continuous low- density 
development patterns. 
 
Watershed Protection Open Space: Lands that protect surface water, groundwater recharge 
areas, wetlands, riparian corridors, and drinking water sources. These areas provide 
essential ecological functions that support water quality, water supply reliability, and long -
term environmental health. 
 
Conservation Lands : Lands preserved through legal protections such as conservation 
easements, deed restrictions, land use covenants, development agreements, or subdivision 
dedications. Conservation lands are protected to preserve ecological, agricultural, scenic, 
cultural, or  recreational values. These values, often referred to as Conservation Values, 
describe the importance of the land based on features such as biodiversity, habitat, 
ecosystem services, scenic character, or cultural signiﬁcance. 
 
Qualifying Open Space for Development : Land within a development project that meets 
established criteria for preservation as open space. Qualifying open space may include 
environmentally sensitive lands, wildlife habitat, agricultural lands, scenic areas, or 
recreational resources and may be us ed to satisfy open space requirements in the 
Development Code. 
 
Recreation Types 
 
Passive Recreation: Recreational activities that require minimal constructed facilities and 
place limited stress on natural resources. Passive recreation activities are typically

wildlife viewing, hunting, horseback riding, picnicking, and nature observation. 
 
Active Recreation : Recreational activities that require developed facilities or structured 
spaces. These activities may include sports ﬁelds, playgrounds, courts, community parks, 
or other constructed recreational amenities. 
 
Non-Motorized Recreation: Recreation activities that do not rely on engines or motors for 
movement. These activities include walking, hiking, bicycling, horseback riding, and cross -
country skiing.

Eastern Summit County contains extensive open space resources that shape land use 
patterns and support the region’s rural character and quality of life. Open space includes 
publicly owned lands, conservation easements, working agricultural lands, and 
undeveloped natural areas. 
 
Federally managed lands account for approximately 44% of the planning area, while lands 
managed by the State of Utah account for approximately 2%. These lands provide important 
beneﬁts, including watershed protection, wildlife habitat preservation, and re creation 
access. 
 
In addition to public lands, approximately 9% of land within Eastern Summit County is 
protected through conservation easements. Many of these easements support agricultural 
operations and preserve scenic landscapes and wildlife habitat. Map X illustrates conserved 
open spaces and county-owned lands throughout the planning area. 
 
Working agricultural lands represent one of the most signiﬁcant forms of open space in 
Eastern Summit County. These lands support grazing, hay production, and other agricultural 
uses while maintaining open viewsheds and providing natural buffers between 
communities. 
 
Environmental features such as steep slopes, wetlands, riparian corridors, and ﬂoodplains 
further inﬂuence development patterns. These areas provide important ecological 
functions, including water ﬁltration, groundwater recharge, and habitat protection.  Map X 
identiﬁes critical environmental areas within the planning area.

Trail systems and recreational corridors are also an important part of the open space 
network. Regional destinations and locally developed trails provide access to outdoor 
recreation and connect communities to surrounding landscapes. 
 
Preservation of open space does not require the elimination of growth. Thoughtful planning 
strategies—such as directing development to appropriate locations and preserving large, 
connected open areas —allow Eastern Summit County to accommodate growth while 
protecting the resources that deﬁne the region’s character and long-term sustainability. 
 
Community Engagement 
 
Community engagement conducted through the Our Summit Vision and Strategic Plan and 
subsequent Eastern Summit County outreach consistently identiﬁed open space 
preservation as one of the community’s highest priorities. Residents emphasized the 
importance of maintaining scenic landscapes, protecting agricultural lands, preserving 
wildlife habitat, and ensuring continued access to outdoor recreation opportunities. 
 
Survey responses demonstrated strong support for protecting open space to  maintain 
ecological health and community character. Approximately 79% of respondents indicated 
that open space is important for protecting native plants, wildlife habitat, and water 
resources. Many residents also emphasized the importance of preserving wo rking 
agricultural lands, with 65% of respondents identifying agriculture as an important form of 
open space and a valued part of the local economy. 
 
Access to open space and proximity to natural areas were also important themes. 
Approximately 59% of respondents identiﬁed favorite open spaces as valuable because they 
support plant and animal habitat, while 53% indicated that nearby open space contribut es 
to neighborhood quality of life. These responses highlight the importance of maintaining 
accessible open spaces that serve both environmental and community needs. 
 
Water protection emerged as a key concern during engagement efforts. Approximately 45% 
of respondents identiﬁed water as the resource requiring the greatest attention to maintain 
quality of life in Eastern Summit County. Protecting watersheds, wetlands, and riparian 
corridors was frequently identiﬁed as essential to long-term community sustainability. 
 
Overall, public input reinforced the importance of preserving large, connected landscapes, 
maintaining open space between communities, and balancing growth with conservation to 
sustain Eastern Summit County’s small-town rural character.

Open Space Goals, Objectives, Policies & Strategies 
 
GOAL: Preserve and manage open space to protect agricultural lands, water resources, 
wildlife habitat, scenic landscapes, and recreation opportunities while maintaining 
separation between communities and supporting well-planned growth. 
 
OBJECTIVE A: Preserve working agricultural lands and large open landscapes. 
 
Policy 7.1: Protect working agricultural lands as a primary form of open space that 
supports rural character, and the local economy. 
 
Strategy 7.1.1:  Prioritize conservation easements and voluntary land 
protection programs that preserve productive agricultural lands. 
 
Strategy 7.1.2: Encourage clustered development and conservation design 
that maintains large, contiguous agricultural areas. 
 
Policy 7.2:  Maintain open space between communities to preserve community 
identity and discourage continuous low-density development patterns. 
 
Strategy 7.2.1: Identify priority community separation areas and incorporate 
them into future open space and land use planning efforts. 
 
OBJECTIVE B: Protect water resources, watersheds, and environmentally sensitive lands. 
 
Policy 7.3: Protect watershed areas, wetlands, riparian corridors, and groundwater 
recharge areas through coordinated land use and open space planning. 
 
Strategy 7.3.1: Prioritize open space acquisition and conservation efforts in 
areas that protect drinking water sources and irrigation systems. 
 
Strategy 7.3.2:  Coordinate open space planning with watershed protection 
strategies identiﬁed in the Water Element. 
 
Policy 7.4:  Use open space planning to reduce environmental risks and 
maintain ecological function.

Strategy 7.4.1: Encourage preservation of environmentally sensitive lands as part of 
development review and subdivision planning. 
 
OBJECTIVE C: Maintain connected open space systems that support wildlife habitat and 
migration. 
 
Policy 7.5: Preserve large, connected open space areas that support wildlife habitat 
and seasonal migration corridors. 
 
Strategy 7.5.1:  Coordinate with the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources to 
identify and map important wildlife habitat and migration corridors. 
 
Strategy 7.5.2: Incorporate wildlife connectivity considerations into land use 
planning and open space preservation efforts. 
 
OBJECTIVE D: Expand and connect recreational open space and trail systems. 
 
Policy 7.6:  Support development of interconnected trail systems that link 
communities, recreation areas, and public lands. 
 
Strategy 7.6.1:  Coordinate with municipalities, recreation districts, and 
organizations such as local trail foundations to expand regional trail 
connections. 
 
Strategy 7.6.2: Encourage trail easements and connections as part of new 
development where appropriate. 
 
OBJECTIVE E: Use conservation tools and partnerships to preserve open space. 
 
Policy 7.7: Maintain cooperative partnerships with land trusts, municipalities, and 
nonproﬁt organizations to support open space preservation. 
 
Strategy 7.7.1: Actively pursue grant funding and partnership opportunities to 
support open space acquisition and management. 
 
Policy 7.8: Use strategic funding tools to preserve priority open space lands.

Strategy 7.8.1:  Consider the use of bond funding, conservation funding 
programs, and other ﬁnancial tools to protect open space and watershed

OBJECTIVE F: Ensure open space is effectively managed and supports long -term 
community goals. 
 
Policy 7.9: Require open space preserved through development to be appropriately

Strategy 7.9.1:  Require open space management plans that address

Policy 7.10: Coordinate open space preservation with land use planning to support

Strategy 7.10.1: Encourage development patterns that concentrate growth in 
appropriate locations while preserving large open landscapes. 
 
OBJECTIVE G: Protect open space systems, scenic landscapes, watersheds, and rural 
community character from high-intensity or utility-intensive development patterns that are 
incompatible with Eastern Summit County’s long -term environmental, infrastructure, and 
land use goals. 
 
Policy 7.11: Discourage large-scale industrial or utility -intensive land uses in areas 
identiﬁed for open space preservation, agricultural use, wildlife habitat, watershed 
protection, or community separation. 
 
Strategy 7. 11.1: Evaluate proposed high-intensity land uses based on 
demonstrated impacts to water resources, energy demand, emergency 
services, scenic quality, wildlife habitat, transportation systems, and 
compatibility with surrounding rural land uses. 
 
Strategy 7.11.2: Encourage development patterns that preserve undeveloped 
landscapes and maintain visual and physical separation between existing 
communities. 
 
Strategy 7.11.3: Coordinate land use decisions with infrastructure capacity, 
long-term resource availability, and adopted open space preservation 
priorities.
