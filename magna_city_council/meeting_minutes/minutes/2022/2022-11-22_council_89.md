Allocation of ARPA Funds for an Intersection Realignment Project

               Steven Kuhlmeier, Engineering, reviewed the 2700 S 8000 W intersection
realignment project. Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC) has allocated federal funds for this
project. The allocation of funds requires an Environmental Impact Study to be completed at the
cost of Magna Metro Township before a Federal Aid Agreement can be entered into for the WFRC
grant funds. He reviewed the crash data from 2010 – 2022 and design concept.

              Council Member Pierce, seconded by Council Member Prokopis, moved to
approve allocation of $100,000 of ARPA funds to move forward with the intersection realignment
project. The motion passed unanimously.

Unified Police Department Funding

               Chief Del Craig stated he is proposing a conversion of one police officer allocation
to a sergeant supervisor position to be able to better manage investigations and community
service functions in Magna. There will be a $32,000 a year budget increase. He would like
approval from the Council to propose the sergeant supervisor position to the Salt Lake Valley Law
Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA) Board.

                  Council Member Hull asked if the sergeant is going to be specifically targeting
the schools. The SRO’s are finished with their day when school is over and are unable to follow-
up with parents or other resources. If there is a supervisor that knows what is going on, they can
follow-up with the parents and provide additional resources. The school districts are overburden,
and it is spilling over into the community.

                Council Member Barney stated the ideal situation would be to create an
additional position for a supervisor. With the conversion of an officer, he is concerned about losing
an officer that would normally be on the street.

                Chief Craig stated Magna will not lose an officer, an investigator position will be
converted to a sergeant supervisor position. Many of the issues are arising out of the schools and
then spilling out into the community. The supervisor will be available after school hours, will be
responsible for coordinating police resources and make them available to families.

DATE           TUESDAY                                   NOVEMBER                        22, 2022

                Council Member Hull stated funds for the position need to be built into the budget,
so it does not go away.

              Council Member Barney, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to direct Chief
Craig to propose the conversion of an investigator position to a sergeant/supervisor, to the
SLVLESA Board. The motion passed unanimously.

Emergency Management Division Hazard Mitigation Plan

                Council Member Pierce stated every five years the mitigation plan is updated.
Salt Lake County would like a letter committing to work with them on a mitigation plan. The
mitigation plan goes through a different process than other emergency plans. It will go through
the county, state, and then to FEMA for approval and then the Council for adoption. The process
takes about a year to a year and half to complete. The letter formalizes Magna Metro Townships
participation in the process.

              Council Member Hull, seconded by Council Member Barney, moved to approve
the letter formalizing its participation in the mitigation plan update. The motion passed
unanimously.

Emergency Management Issues

                Council Member Hull stated during the earthquake three years ago,
communication was a big issue. When another emergency happens, the Council needs to be
prepared. She would like the Council to consider purchasing satellite phones. In addition,
Basecamp is software where files can be uploaded, stored, and everyone will have access.
CARES funds could be used to purchase the satellite phones and software. The Council could
participate in quarterly trainings so it can be better prepared during an incident.

              Council Member Pierce stated there needs to be a backup plan for
communication after an emergency. If a satellite phone is purchase, it needs to be kept at the
Webster Center. One satellite phone will not be beneficial. The cost is not cheap and there are
monthly service fees. Amateur radio users in the area need to be considered and it would be a
less expensive option.

                                              10
DATE            TUESDAY                                   NOVEMBER                          22, 2022

                Chief Del Craig stated as UPD has been conducting school exercises and has
been working with Salt Lake County and have talked about these very issues. The problem with
satellite phones is that they fall into disrepair and get discharged. UPD is looking for alternatives
to satellite phones and new services. The Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD) is
also looking into these issues. It would be beneficial to bring all these parties together and discuss
these issues.

              Counci Member Barney stated it would be a good idea to have a plan in place
before funds are spent.

              Council Member Pierce stated Salt Lake County has instructors that the Council
should work with to provide training.

                Chief Craig suggested calling Clint Mecham, Unified Fire Authority (UFA), and
have him put together a tabletop exercise. This type of exercise will show how an emergency will
work in real time. Decisions on where individuals need to be to access communications need to
be determined. It is important to have key people in the right locations.

                Council Member Barney stated a plan needs to be created. The plan will then
dictate what infrastructure and equipment is needed to execute the plan.

Planning Meetings

                Council Member Barney stated he attended a UDOT long range transportation
planning meeting. UDOT plans to construct overpasses at the intersections on Mountain View
Corridor. They also want to widen 8400 South which would wipe out a bunch homes along that
road. He suggested waiting until the overpasses are finished before widening 8400 South to see
what the traffic impact will be.

The legislature held a long term planning meeting and the main take away is Magna is one of the
biggest growth areas. The demographic has shifted, as many Caucasians that have moved out,
that many Hispanics have moved in. Magna’s identify has been tied into Kennecott, Rio Tinto and
mining, when its past is actually tied to Latter Day Saints pioneers, Greek and Italian immigrants.
He would like the Council to consider how to lean into the diversity.
