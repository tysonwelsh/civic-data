(Track A, 06:40)
Brandon Larsen of the Utah County Community Development Office explained this updated ordinance is to give the
responsibility of technical decisions regarding small subdivisions to the County Engineer and staff. A more stream-
lined process that does not require Commission approval would best serve any applicants, providing a clear outline
of steps to follow and receiving the information more quickly.

(Track A, 12:15)
Commissioner Lee stated he loves the concept, but has many questions; the first being ‘How will the people have
someone on their side as their representatives in government if the Commission is removed from the process?’
Brandon Larsen pointed out this is an administrative, and not legislative, process. If an applicant meets all of the
requirements for a subdivision, they must be approved. Commissioner Lee and Mr. Larsen discussed the options to
appeal a denial, with Commissioner Graves mentioning the ‘Box Elder Subdivision’ which was previously brought
before the Board.

(Track A, 16:45)
Utah County Deputy Attorney Robert Moore joined Brandon Larsen to note subdivision applications cannot be
legally denied if the requirements are met. Mr. Moore reiterated subdivision approval is an administrative process,
and this is an attempt to simplify it. He and Commissioner Graves discussed how omitting Commission review and
approval would reduce the application time by sixty days. Commissioner Ivie expressed concern that adjacent
landowners would not have enough time to protest proposed subdivisions if too many shortcuts are made. When
Commissioner Ivie expressed he does not want the elected officials to be hobbled from giving input, Mr. Larsen and
Mr. Moore addressed the ‘plain language of the law’ and provided court examples warning against bringing
legislative action within an administrative process. Mr. Moore noted the legislative action has already taken place
by defining different county zones. The commissioners discussed the differences between large-scale and minor
developments.

1.    UTAH COUNTY FRUIT GROWERS PRESENTATION BY CURTIS ROLLY ROWLEY
(Track A, 45:10)

Santaquin resident Curtis Rowley came forward to talk about his family farm and present a slide show (a copy of
which can be found attached to these minutes). The presentation covered tart cherry, apple, peach, and sweet cherry
production in Utah County, which is the second largest agriculture producer in the state. Mr. Rowley expressed
concern over a potential mining pit being considered in the Genola area, and the severely negative impact such an
operation would have on local agriculture. Mr. Rowley’s cousin, and fellow farmer, Robert Rowley, also came
forward to talk about the problems associated with mining and farming in the same area. The commissioners
thanked Curtis Rowley for the presentation, and talked about the importance of protecting natural resources.


                                         PUBLIC COMMENTS
(Track A, 1:13:05)
Utah County resident Lynn Fowers lives on the west side of the proposed Genola mining pit. He has been in the
fruit-growing business his entire life, just as his parents and their parents. He reaffirmed the issues presented by
Curtis Rowley during today’s Work Session. Dale Rowley, Ryan Rowley, and Sheryl Fowers also came forward to
comment on the plight of Utah County fruit growers. Ms. Fowers specifically mentioned a task force organized in
the late 1990’s to address problems associated with gravel pits; the task force spent years putting together a
resolution which was ultimately dismissed by the Commission at that time. Commissioners Lee, Graves, and Ivie
all indicated they would be very interested in revisiting the solutions which had been proposed in 2002, and asked
Ms. Fowers to stay in contact with their office.
