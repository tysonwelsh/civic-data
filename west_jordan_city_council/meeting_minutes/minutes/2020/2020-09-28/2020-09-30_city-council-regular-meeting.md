6.    PUBLIC HEARINGS
       a. Ordinance No. 20-35 regarding the Master Development Agreement and Master
          Development Plan for Jones Ranch, 592 acres of property located at approximately 8800 S U-
          111 as outlined in the request for Council action report

Community Development Director Scott Langford oriented the Council with Ordinance No. 20-35
regarding the Master Development Agreement and Master Development Plan for Jones Ranch, 592 acres
of property located at approximately 8800 South U-111. He explained that they would discuss this item
and the next together and let the applicant respond to questions. Mr. Langford suggested the Council allow
public comment, but not take action on the item.

Bryon Prince with Ivory Development presented an overview of the major elements planned for the Jones
Ranch Development. The plan included seven major land uses, with 35 acres of commercial on the corners
of 90th South and U-111. He showed proposed residential unit renderings, explained planned open space
(17% of overall site) and trail network, and showed a rendering of the planned clubhouse. Mr. Prince
clarified that the 17% open space calculation included open space within individual villages.

Responding to Council Member Whitelock, Mr. Prince said he would be amenable to adding the 35 acres
of commercial to the Master Development Agreement. He explained that the trail along Barney’s Creek
would be completed as each phase of development along the path was completed.

Chair McConnehey opened the public hearing at 7:56 p.m.

7.    BUSINESS ITEMS
       a. Ordinance No. 20-30 regarding the Master Development Agreement and Preliminary
          Development Plan for Jordan Fields

On August 26, 2020, the Jordan Fields Preliminary Development Plan was presented to and discussed by
the City Council. A public hearing was held, and concerns were expressed. Assistant Planner Mark
Forsythe updated the Council regarding changes made in response to concerns expressed at the previous
meeting. Eleven parking stalls were added to the Preliminary Development Plan, for a total of 360 parking
stalls. The detention basin and landscaping plan were also updated.

Randy Smith with Fieldstone Homes, applicant, introduced Bryan Kniep with Goodwin Knight and Brent
Bateman, attorney with Durham Jones & Pinegar. Mr. Smith said they were excited about providing a
long-lasting, pedestrian-friendly development. He said they worked with Staff to meet City Code and
place buildings in a way that would slow traffic.
City Council Meeting APPROVED Minutes – September 30, 2020                                             Page 5


Responding to a question from Council Member Green, City Attorney Rob Wall stated the Council, acting
as a legislative body, needed to follow established standards. In a court situation, interpretation of Code
would favor a developer. Council Member Green expressed the opinion that the developer did not want
to work with the City Council, and had adopted a take-it-or-leave-it stance with the plan presented, with
a threat of filing a law suit if the plan was not approved. Council Member Green emphasized that the
proposed infill development did not meet City Code, specifically regarding placement of parking,
landscaping associated with parking, requirement to provide lighting along Jordan Valley Way, and open
space requirements. He stated the applicant needed to give better evidence of compliance with
requirements, and said he would not vote in favor of the application as it was presented. He said he did
not believe the application met the intent of a TOD.

Council Member Whitelock said she did not believe the application met City Code. She commented that
the proposed development was not connected, and did not include commercial or retail. She stated she did
not feel the proposed development was “transit oriented” as claimed, and pointed out with the nearest
grocery 0.8 miles away, driving would be necessary for residents. Council Member Whitelock said there
had not been time to review the letter sent by the applicant’s attorney.

Council Member Jacob questioned how Staff could say the application met Code if Council Member
Green found many areas where it did not. He said he did not have a problem with the proposed
development, and said he would vote to approve, adding that he believed it would be up to the developer,
owner, and landlord to make the development successful long-term.

Council Member Pack said Council Member Green made a compelling argument that the application did
not meet Code, but the Staff Report indicated the application did meet Code. He said he was interested in
coming together with the Planning Commission and listening to them vet these types of issues. He
questioned where the disconnect was, and said he would like a workable solution for the application that
would fit and make sense to the City Council. He suggested the conversation continue after the Council
members had an opportunity to study and discuss the information newly received.

Council Member Lamb said he agreed with Council Member Jacob, and said it was unclear to him why
the Council was discussing what should and should not be developed at the subject location. He agreed
there was not a grocery store nearby, but said he did not think one would be built closer in the future.
Council Member Lamb said he did not believe postponing would change anyone’s mind, and said he was
in favor of moving forward with a motion.

Council Member Green said he tended to agree overall with Council Members Jacob and Lamb. He stated
the proposed development probably fit the area, but did not fit within City Code. Council Member Green
said he did not understand the willingness to wave ordinances for the project. He said he had a problem
with the idea that although the proposal did not precisely meet the letter of the law, it met the spirit of the
law. Council Member Green emphasized the need to apply Code consistently to avoid the appearance of
favoring one developer over another. Either an application met Code or it did not. He stated Staff did not
have the authority to wave Code provisions. Council Member Green stated he would vote against a motion
to approve.

Council Member Pack asked if approval of the MDA could include conditions to address concerns
expressed. City Attorney Rob Wall responded that the Development Agreement could contain any
condition the Council and applicant agreed on together. He said the applicant was taking the position that
the issues raised were not clear in Code, with the possibility that the issues would be challenged legally.

Responding to a question from Council Member Jacob, Mr. Langford and Assistant City Attorney Duncan
Murray explained that density in the TSOD must be ratified by the City Council according to City Code.
City Council Meeting APPROVED Minutes – September 30, 2020                                        Page 6


Brent Bateman explained that his letter was sent to the City early Monday morning, and said he wished it
had been forwarded to the Council prior to the evening of the Council meeting. He said the letter was not
intended to come across as hostile, and stated the developer did wish to work with the City. Mr. Bateman
stated the developer was willing to accept conditions of approval, and was willing to comply with City
Code.

Randy Smith emphasized his desire to work with the City and create a good, solid development. He
showed elevation renderings of the proposed development. Mr. Smith said he would be happy to provide
street lights on Jordan Valley Way. He said the proposed development would be close to several
employers, schools, and public transportation, and said he believed people would want to live in the
development because of that proximity. He said in his experience 1.5 parking stalls per unit was a good
ratio for multi-family development.

MOTION:        Council Member Jacob moved to approve Ordinance No. 20-30 approving the Master
               Development Agreement and Preliminary Development Plan for Jordan Fields.
               Council Member Lamb seconded the motion.

Council Member Pack said he would like to see the application move forward, and said he believed
specific conditions for approval would be prudent. Council Member Green said he did not believe the
applicant would be able to comply with some of his concerns (e.g. buildings not facing parking lots) given
the proposed layout.

Council Member Jacob said he was leaning on the expertise of Staff in making the motion, and said he
hoped his action did not come across as arbitrary or capricious.

Chair McConnehey spoke in opposition to the motion. He expressed a desire to have time to review the
letter from Mr. Bateman, and said he wanted to be able to talk through other options.

Council Member Jacob withdrew his motion.

Council Member Green asked Mr. Langford how long Staff would need to draft language for TOD infill
that would give some ability to not be quite as strict with the Ordinance. Mr. Langford responded that the
draft and approval process would take at least a couple of months. Amendments to Title 13 would need to
be vetted by the Planning Commission.

Council Member Green said he believed a motion to approve would fail at that point. He asked the
applicant if they would be willing to delay a couple months to work through a TSOD amendment and get
it right.

City Attorney Rob Wall said he could see two issues: the question relative to Code allowances and
requirements; and the question of whether or not the Council was satisfied with the development if Code
requirements were resolved. He commented that the Council had not had time to review and discuss the
letter sent by Mr. Bateman, and added that the developer sounded willing to work with the Council.
Speaking to the idea of “spirit of the law”, Mr. Wall stated Code provisions needed to be met and should
not be ignored. He recommended the Council table action and discuss with Staff in closed session the
reasons for their recommendation, considering the threat of legal action by the applicant. Mr. Wall
suggested it would be good to engage with the developer to see what they were willing to do, and stated
any conditions agreed on should be memorialized in the Development Agreement. He agreed with Council
Member Green that Staff did not have authority to wave Code provisions.

Chair McConnehey suggested the Council meet with the developer in a work session prior to the next
Council meeting.

Brent Bateman stated he had received extensive land-use training. He agreed that developers needed to
meet City Code, and clarified that the disagreement was whether or not that was happening. He said he
knew his client had some time constraints.

Randy Smith said he was grateful for the discussion. He emphasized that time was a concern for the
