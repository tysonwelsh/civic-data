         E. Mayor and Council Reports
Council Member McGuire discussed the Arts Council’s latest mural project and showed pictures
of him and his son at the unveiling. He met with Representative Teuscher to discuss upcoming
legislative issues. He met with a developer regarding some land on the west side of the city. He
wishes that could be preserved as open space, but that’s not an option. He noted that sometimes
residents feel like their voices aren’t heard in decisions that are made, but he had a resident reach
out regarding tree pruning on their street and he was able to contact City Manager Whatcott and
come up with a more efficient way to inform residents ahead of time. He thanked the resident for
reaching out.
Council Member Zander visited with Laurie Stringham from the County Council at Large, and
she is pleased with her service there. A resident submitted a permit for changes to her yard, and
asked Council Member Zander to try and rush the process along. Staff was so fast and efficient,
they showed up to help her before Council Member Zander was able to contact anyone. The
building permit department did a wonderful job and it was very much appreciated by that
resident.
Council Member Harris met with our seniors and had lunch with them for the patriot luncheon;
he thanked everyone for their service with the seniors in our city. Along with others, he met with
Senator Lincoln Fillmore and discussed his agenda. He also had dinner with our firefighters at
the station where there was a dunk booth, and his wife and kids got dunked. It was a lot of fun
with a BBQ contest, and he noted that we have a wonderful crew of firefighters. He got a view of
the land out west and discussed some of the potential ideas. He met with the South Valley Sewer
Board, he had a tour and a board meeting. There is a proposal to change boundaries for the sewer
board service area. That’s a big deal for us, so there will be a presentation on that in the near
future to understand those changes.
Council Member Shelton also met with Representatives Teuscher and Fillmore, he appreciated
those conversations. He is hopeful we can persuade them to vote our way when it comes down to
decision time. He visited with the Senior Advisory Committee last week, they are making lots of
plans. He went to Herriman’s community partner’s lunch and it was fun to visit with folks from
around the southwest part of the valley. He attended the Jordan Education Foundation, where the
mayor presided and represented the city well. There were some interesting presentations, and
three of the five presenters were residents of South Jordan and experts in their fields. One
gentleman from the Kem C. Gardner Institute shared that he thinks interest rates will continue to

         I. Public Hearing Items

                  I.1.   Ordinance 2022-15, Replacing Chapter 5.62 of the South Jordan
                  Municipal Code with a new Title 14 regulating Residential Facilities for the
                  Disabled (By City Attorney Ryan Loose). RCV

Attorney Loose explained where this was currently in the code, and why they would like to
move it to a new title. This updates us with current law, and is not a huge change from what
we already had.

Council Member McGuire motioned to approve Ordinance 2022-15, Replacing
Chapter 5.62 with a new Title 14. Council Member Shelton seconded the motion.

Council Member Shelton asked to clarify that this is just cleaning up language and giving it
a space of its own, instead of being lumped in with other sections.

Attorney Loose clarified that nothing significant changed, only a few tweaks. There is one
current application for a place off 11400 South and they’ve had a few residents call about it.
It will be processed under the old rules, and if they want it processed under the new rules
they would just have to re-file it after the change.

Roll Call Vote was 4-0, unanimous in favor. Council Member Marlor was absent from
the vote.

         J. Staff Reports and Calendaring Items

Deputy City Manager Dustin Lewis discussed the strategic planning meeting next week,
and how staff has been meeting to work towards that. That will all be reviewed at the
meeting to make sure things line up with the council’s plans. As a staff, they have identified
eight strategic areas they would like to have questions answered on, so they can better
prepare their budgets. He noted there will be some emails sent out with topics that need to
be discussed. The first section will be spent working through those priorities, then talk
about a possible city tagline, some tax policy, the compensation policy, as well as service
levels. In the November meeting they will discuss their IT strategic plan, potential
annexations, parking policies and Summerfest.

Mayor Ramsey is looking forward to a potential tagline to being discussed.

Attorney Loose asked the council if they like the legislative luncheon format, and most of
the council responded yes. Congressmen Owens’ visit yesterday was solely for the youth
council, but he really wants to see the council and mayor when he comes next week and is
giving them 90 minutes. They will meet him at tank 5B, out by 11800 South and 5600
