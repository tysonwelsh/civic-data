GENERAL PLAN/ZONE TEXT APPLICATIONS
GPZ-3-2022 Petition by MARIA ELENA DUBON requesting a General Plan change from
           Non-Retail Commercial to General Commercial and a zone change from RB
           (Residential Business) to C-2 (General Commercial). The property is located
           at 4543 and 4547 South 4000 West on 0.73 acres. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at
           801-963-3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov)

Discussion:      Steve Pastorik explained that the subject property comprises two lots owned
                 by the same property owner, with an existing daycare business. He stated that
                 there is one building on the southern lot and two structures on the lot to the
                 north. He noted that this would be the second location for this business.
                 Steve said that the applicant proposes demolishing the existing structures to
                 build a restaurant with a drive-thru.

                 Steve stated that the concept plan revealed some issues needing to be
                 addressed. He pointed out that the curb cut along the west side of 4000 West
                 would be too close to the curb cut for the adjacent Sonic restaurant.
                 Steve said that Public Works suggested that the applicant work with Sonic on
                 a shared access, and if they are unable to reach an agreement, the subject
                 property would have the one access to the north. Another issue, Steve said, is
                 the landscaping along the north side. He explained that the code requires 20
                 feet of landscaping, and they are showing 10 feet. Steve stated that a
                 provision in the code allows for a reduction in landscaping if specific criteria
                 are met. However, this property is next to a residential use, so they do not
                 meet the requirements and request an exception. Steve mentioned that the
                 overall percentage of landscaping along the north side would be higher than
                 required, even with the exception.

C-4-2022      Petition by CIR ENGINEERING requesting a conditional use approval for a
              262-unit apartment development. The property is located at 2450 West 3800
              South on 3.65 acres and is zoned RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling
              Residential). (Staff – Brock Anderson at 801-963-3361 or
              brock.anderson@wvc-ut.gov)

Discussion:   Brock Anderson stated that this proposal is for a multi-unit development. He
              pointed out the four access points in the submitted site plan. Brock noted that
              Public Works evaluated the proposed plans and said right-of-way dedication
              would be required, and they will need to provide an extra 10 feet of land. He
              noted that they might be able to shift the building to the north.

              Brock explained that the development agreement includes a parking
              requirement of 424 stalls based on the number and mix of units within the
              development outlined in their original plans. He said the revised plans have a
              different number and combination of units, the parking requirement would
              now be 414 stalls, and they are increasing the number of garage stalls. Brock
              noted that the Commission could change the provision if it is appropriate.

              Next, Brock addressed the landscaping. He explained that the development
              agreement states the amount and location of landscaping should be
              substantially like the concept plan in exhibit C and presented the exhibit to the
              Commission. Brock noted the changes made from their original plans. He
              explained that they had moved some amenities, lessened the amount of
              amenity space, and added parking stalls. Brock feels that the area removed
              should be added somewhere else on the site. He pointed out that they propose
              placing trees inside the park strip, which are typically placed between the
              sidewalk and the building. Brock showed images of the neighboring Costco,
              and they do have trees within the park strip, so if the Commission is okay with
              the tree placement, we can leave them as proposed.

              Brock explained that the building would be three stories high along 3800
South, and the remaining sides would be five stories in height. He will need
to clarify the location of the amenities with the applicant.

Brock noted that the development agreement states that the building should be
constructed substantially like the rendering. All windows should be finished
with the same materials and level of detail as portrayed on the east side of the
renderings. He explained that they are proposing different materials, and the
renderings do not have the same level of details as those submitted initially.
Brock would like the Commission's opinion regarding the proposed materials
and the renderings.

Brock asked what the Commission thought about the parking proposal.
Commissioner Lovato said that the development agreement requires 424
stalls, and they are now proposing 414 stalls. He asked where the applicant
removed the ten stalls on their site. Brock pointed out that the surface parking
on the west side of the subject property was initially proposed to be ninety-
degree parking, and they are now proposing parallel parking. He stated that
they have added stalls within the parking garage and where the original space
for the amenities was located. Commissioner Lovato said changing the
parking orientation provides less surface parking and opens up the adjacent
road. Brock stated that he didn't think there would be enough room for the
ninety-degree parking, and that's why they made the change.

Commissioner Lovato asked how well the parking would work being against
the wall. Brock clarified that the parking would be on the same side as the
building next to the sidewalk. Commissioner Lovato thanked Brock for his
explanation.

Commissioner McEwen asked if the applicant had approached Costco about
sharing their parking lot on the southwest side. Steve Pastorik stated that
Costco is not interested in sharing that parking.

Commissioner Woodruff feels the reduced stall requirement is reasonable
based on unit change.

Commissioner Lovato asked if they would be charging for any parking.
Brock stated that all parking would be free.

The Planning Commission did not express any concerns with the trees located
in the park strip.

Brock then asked the Commission's thoughts on the proposed materials and
elevations. Commissioner Woodruff asked what they were offering in place
of the tile and the steel. Brock explained they are no longer proposing tile so
that the brick would be the replacement, and the steel would be replaced with
metal panels. Commissioner Woodruff is comfortable with the brick but isn't
              sure about the metal panels. Brock asked if they were okay with aluminum
              wood siding, which was not in the applicant's original proposal.
              Commissioner Woodruff said that there is minimal use of that siding, so he is
              okay with it.

              Commissioner McEwen stated that the original plans are much different from
              those submitted as part of this proposal. He feels that applicants tend to come
              back before the Commission multiple times, and each time, the projects are of
              less quality than what was proposed initially. He would like the applicant to
              be held to their original plans. Commissioner Fuller agrees with
              Commissioner McEwen. Brock stated that he feels it is appropriate to tell the
              applicant that the elevations should be modified.


C-5-2022      Petition by BRETT CARTER requesting conditional use approval for ARK
              Plaza, a retail warehouse center. The property is located at 1927 West 3600
              South on 1.03 acres and is zoned C-2 (General Commercial). (Staff – Jody
              Knapp at 801-963-3497 or jody.knapp@wvc-ut.gov)

Discussion:   Jody Knapp explained that the original plat was approved in 1998, and every
              use after that would be a conditional use. She said that the applicant will
              maintain the existing landscaping and could need to add a few trees to the site.

              Jody presented the proposed floor plan and pointed out the two smaller units
              in the structure's bottom-left and right-hand corners. She explained that the
              space on the right would be a maintenance/equipment space and not for lease.
              The unit on the left side would be a leasable space. Jody said that the ladder
              accessing the roof would be in the unit on the right.

              Jody stated that the applicant would need to modify the front doors, awnings,
              and materials to meet the commercial design standards. She noted that similar
              previous applications from this developer stated the overhead doors would be
              removed if the building was leased to mainly retail businesses. Jody said the
              applicant wants to keep the doors to provide larger warehouse space and
              access for oversized products. The applicant also mentioned that tenants come
              and go, so leaving the doors would allow more business types to lease the
              units. She stated that the units are narrow and suggested widening the
              divisions to create space for adding glass to the building.

              Jody mentioned that Homestead Farms is adjacent to the rear of the subject
              property, and they want their fence to remain even though it is damaged. She
              said the two property owners would discuss the wall.

              Commissioner Woodruff asked if all of the overhead doors meet our
              commercial design standards. Jody said that the doors are viewed the same as
              walls, so there isn't anything prohibiting them. She explained that one option
                  would be adding a requirement stating the door color needs to match the
                  building behind it. Commissioner Woodruff feels that could help.


C-6-2022          Petition by JOE SALISBURY requesting conditional use approval for a 282-
                  unit apartment development. The property is located at 2892 S Decker Lake
                  Drive on 16.05 acres and is zoned RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential).
                  (Staff – Brock Anderson at 801-963-3361 or brock.anderson@wvc-ut.gov)

Discussion:       Brock explained that this proposal is for a 286 multi-unit development. The
                  development agreement states that they will have a unit mix of 98 three-
                  bedroom townhomes, 94 two-bedroom units, and 94 one-bedroom units. Once
                  the applicant received comments from the Public Works Department, they
                  modified their site plan, causing a slightly different unit mix. Brock said they
                  are now proposing 94 three-bedroom units, 96 two-bedroom units, and 96
                  one-bedroom units, and they would like the Commission to approve this
                  application with the new unit mix.

                  Brock noted that each unit would have a two-car garage except for fifty-two
                  FlexGen units that will not have a garage but will have assigned parking stalls.
                  He said that the applicant proposes adding two feet to the fence adjacent to the
                  Youth Detention Center, making it eight feet tall. Brock pointed out that they
                  have a shared recreational easement, and that area has a fence around it with
                  one access. Staff suggests that two gates are added, giving the residents more
                  access to that area.

                  Brock stated that the applicant is still requesting the same amenities as their
                  original proposal. He then reviewed the different unit types and floor plans.
                  Brock noted that additional window space would need to be added to the six-
                  plex town home building.

                  Brock said that the applicant had submitted a different sign plan from the
                  standard five-foot-tall monument sign. They propose an eight-foot-tall sign as
                  an alternative for the Planning Commission to review and approve.

                  Brock asked the Commission if they had any thoughts on the unit mix,
                  parking, or monument sign. Chair Meaders said that the assigned parking
                  stalls should mitigate not having an attached garage. However, it is something
                  different, and she would like to think about it more. There were no further
                  comments.
