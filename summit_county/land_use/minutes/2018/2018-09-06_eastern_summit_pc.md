3. Public hearing to discuss and possible action regarding State of Utah Roadless 
Rule Petition which may include possible amendments to the adopted Resource 
Management Plan included as a Technical Appendix to the Eastern Summit 
County General Plan– Sean Lewis, County Planner 
 
Planner Lewis said that about one year ago, Staff came to the Commission to adopt the 
Resource Management Plan.  That was under the direction of the State of Utah Public 
Lands Policy Coordinating Office.  The State and the Governor’s Office are requesting a 
change to the Roadless Rule.  He said this rule specifies what can be done on National 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission

Forest lands.  The State believes this will allow Forest Service Lands to be better 
managed than Washington D.C. officials would be able to.  This ordinance change has to 
go through the Planning Commission and the County Council.   
 
Planner Lewis said Staff is not proposing the Resource Manage Plan be amended.  Staff 
believes management practices were significantly addressed in that ordinance, which 
was adopted last year.  Staff believes the proposal is not in conflict; therefore, no editing 
is needed.  He said this was noticed as a public hearing to allow someone from the 
public to present information which might indicate that edits are needed. 
 
Planner Lewis said that Summit County has two national forest areas.  There is the 
Wasatch-Cache National Forest and the Ashley National Forest.  These were shown on 
the vicinity map.  He said that most of the areas have lots of roads in them.  These roads 
are named or are given a number.  The purpose of the proposed petition is to prevent 
new roads from going in.      
 
Planner Lewis said the State has asked Summit County to identify areas either as Forest 
Stewardship or Forest Restoration.  These were defined by the State of Utah.  Staff is 
recommending that everything is identified as one of those two areas.  Staff has come 
up with factors to help identify the areas:  
1. Much of the existing roadless area is accessible via existing roads.  No new roads 
are proposed.   
2. Existing regulations prevent the effective management of the forest by limiting 
the amount of dead/dying timber that may be removed.  Summit County 
supports the effective management of the forest by allowing timber industries 
appropriate access to remove dead/dying timber.   
3. Appropriate forest management includes many methods of thinning timber 
stands, including responsible logging, prescribed burns, natural controlled 
burns, among others.

4. The forest areas in Summit County represent a major draw to the area from 
tourists and locals alike.  This represents a significant portion of Summit 
County’s economic viability.  The forest areas should be protected, and managed 
effectively to preserve this valuable resource utilizing all elements enumerated 
in factor three.   
 
Planner Lewis said based on these factors Staff recommends these areas be identified as 
Forest Stewardship Areas or Forest Restoration Areas.  Chair Clyde said he is familiar 
with some of these areas.  They are covered with roads.  Planner Lewis reiterated the 
roadless rule allows no new roads.   
 
Planner Lewis said three work sessions are scheduled with the County Council.  The 
Council will hold a public hearing at the end of the month and at that time, will make a 
recommendation.  He added that Summit County was informed about the State’s 
petition the first of August.  The State asked that the process is completed by the first of 
October.  He asked if the Commission is okay with the direction that Staff is going.   
 
Commissioner Hanson said the Resource Management Plan is already in play.  Does it 
specify that no new roads are being proposed?  Planner Lewis responded that it does.  
He added that plan came before this Commission several times.   
 
Planner Lewis said there are many dead trees that have beetle kill.  These trees have 
become hazards for people and for forest fires.  There needs to be proper management 
of the forest.  That may mean removing them by manmade means or by fire.  Fires can 
help to keep the forests healthy.  Summit County is in support of all these methods, but 
not the clear cutting of the forest and desecration of these areas.   
Commissioner Hanson said her concern is balancing recreation with forest

the rule is saying there will be no new roads.

consider.  He said in February of 2008, the Planning Commission issued a Conditional 
Use Permit (CUP) for “commercial open storage of rock/stone veneer and related 
equipment.”

applicant, Mr. Trunnell, clean up the project site.  A mandatory review was required

conditions of the site.  Since that time, Staff has worked with and cited the applicant for

what he is going to do to clean up the parcel.

FINDINGS OF FACT 
1. On February 6, 2008 the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission issued a 
Conditional Use Permit for “commercial open storage of rock/stone veneer and 
related equipment” at 5520 North High View Road (off of Brown’s Canyon Road.)  
2. Attached to the CUP approval were 14 conditions of approval. 
3. On August 20, 2008, the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
conducted a six month review of the project. 
4. On August 20, 2008, the Planning Commission approved the Conditional Use 
Permit with two additional conditions of approval bringing the total to 16. 
5. On July 25, 2018 the Summit County Code Enforcement Officer received a 
complaint that the applicant may be running an illegal land fill on the property.  
6. On August 14, 2018, the County Code Enforcement Officer, the County Engineer, 
and a representative from the State Department of Environmental Quality 
inspected the property.    
7. At the August 14, 2018 site inspection, the following violations were found: 
 Not having the boundaries of the two five acre parcels covered in the CUP 
clearly marked.

 Stone was being processed on site.  There was a large stone saw in operation

 There were what the Code Enforcement Officer considered as “junk” vehicles

Management and Radiation Control issued a letter ordering the applicant to 
immediately cease disposing of these wastes on his property and to remove all 
solid waste from his property.

11. On February 6, 2008 and again on August 20, 2008, the Planning Commission

the basis of erroneous or misleading information. 
2. The Park City Stone commercial Conditional Use Permit violated the terms and 
conditions of approval of the permit relating to establishment or operation of 
the use.

and regulations of the State, Federal, or Regional agencies applicable to the 
development.
