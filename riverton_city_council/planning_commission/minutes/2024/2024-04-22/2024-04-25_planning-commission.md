       A.     BREE PLACE CUP, PLZ-24-2015, A CONDITIONAL USE APPLICATION
              FOR A PRIVATE LANE TO SERVE TWO (2) ADDITIONAL LOTS,
              LOCATED AT 12191 SOUTH 3600 WEST. APPLICANT - GARY CANNON.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich presented the Staff Report and stated that the request is a
Conditional Use for a private lane. A review of the application reveals two adjacent
properties owned by Gary Cannon. The application includes both the Ownership Affidavit
and an Affidavit authorizing Mr. Cannon to pursue the application on behalf of the property
owner. The New Ownership Declaration grants Mr. Cannon the authority to pursue a
subdivision in addition to the private lane. The subdivision will be further explored if the
Planning Commission approves the private lane. The property location was identified on a
map displayed.

The Autumnwood Subdivision is zoned R-3 and features a concrete wall along its border. It
effectively isolates and restricts access to the 1.56-acre property. There are currently no
planned access points or driveways, nor does the lot align with any existing infrastructure
on the western side of the road. Furthermore, no future development plans appear to
necessitate access. These factors combine to make this lot an ideal candidate for a private
lane application. Public notice was sent out to the neighboring properties within a 300-foot
radius and no public comments were received.

The oblique aerial image with the zoning overlay reveals the R-3 zone to the north, followed
by the RR-22 zone to the west across 3600 West. The southwest corner features the RR-2
zone, and the R-1 zone is located directly to the south. The subject property itself falls within
the RR-22 zone and consists of 1.56 acres, designated for one-half-acre lots. Ensign
Engineering was able to design a Concept Plan that preserves the existing house,

incorporates two new lots, and utilizes a private lane while remaining within the designated
lot size. The plan is considered to be of exceptional quality and significantly simplifies the
approval process. It has been determined that a private lane would be most effectively
situated on the north side, where an existing driveway approach is already located, slightly

Mr. Prestwich reported that the next step will be for the applicant to come back with a
preliminary subdivision plat and apply for final approval. When considering private lanes,
the determination should be made as to whether it qualifies for a private lane. The
preference is for public streets. This request meets the requirements to justify a private lane
as there is no public infrastructure planned in the area and no one else will utilize the access.
It also maintains the goals of the City and keeps the existing zoning in place. Two users will
utilize the 20-foot drivable surface. A fire turnaround will also be required.

In response to a question raised by Commissioner Rushton, Mr. Prestwich stated that the
driveway width increases from 20 to 25 feet for three lots. Vice-Chair Matheson noted that
the site is very close to one-half acre in size. Mr. Prestwich stated that the geometry on the
turnaround is unusual and will be reviewed by the Fire Department. He explained that the
turnaround will not count against the one-half acre requirement although the step portion
does.

Gary Cannon gave his address as 1397 West 13400 South and stated that they went back
and forth with various renderings to come up with the current layout. He anticipated the
need for further adjustments. The proposed layout was intended to increase usability. He
noted that they could have placed a road down the north property line and pursued three
lots facing north. What is proposed adds seclusion to the back two lots while preserving as
many existing trees as possible.

Commissioner Rushton asked if the intent was to keep the existing home and level the
outbuildings in the back. Mr. Cannon confirmed that was the case and stated that there is
a two-car garage on the south property line that is in poor condition.

Vice-Chair Matheson opened the public hearing.

Ingo Heaton reported that he lives directly east of the subject property and there are
stormwater retention structures mentioned in the application. He acknowledged a lack of
understanding regarding the purpose of the structures and whether they were relevant to
the current discussion. However, due to concerns about potential water runoff impacting his
property, he expressed a desire to learn more. He asked about the fate of the trees on the
southeast corner of the property. They were described as being in a state of decline, with
branches falling onto Mr. Heaton’s yard.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the engineer successfully addressed the private lane and lot size
issues and laid out the stormwater, which is not typically done for a Conditional Use Permit.
It is usually done as part of the subdivision review process. Staff had not yet reviewed any

Commissioner Rushton moved that the Planning Commission APPROVE PLZ-24-
2015 “Bree Place Private Lane” at 12191 South 3600 West, subject to the following
conditions:

      1.     Private lane shall follow Riverton City Code for development on private
             lanes. No construction may begin on the private lane until a subdivision
             plan is approved.

      2.     Approval is for a private lane to service two (2) lots.

      3.     Private lane shall be at least 20 ft wide drivable surface.

      4.     A private lane shall be named and a street sign shall be installed with the
             subdivision.

      5.     Private lane shall be paved in either asphalt or concrete according to the
             requirements of the Unified Fire Authority and the Riverton City
             Standards and Specifications.

      6.     The private lane shall include a turnaround designed and installed to the
             requirements of the Unified Fire Authority and Riverton City Standards
             and Specifications.

      7.     The subject parcel’s driveways, including the private lane entrance, shall
             conform to Riverton City Standards for driveways, including but not
             limited to width, number of driveways, and separation distance between
             driveways.

      8.     The private lane and private utility stubs shall be maintained by the
             homeowners. No City maintenance will be performed on the private lane
             or on private utility laterals on private property.

      9.     Written CC&Rs or an agreement for shared access and maintenance of
             the private lane must be submitted with the subdivision application.

The motion was seconded by Commissioner Cluff. The motion passed with the
unanimous consent of the Commission. Commissioner Cannon did not participate
in the vote.

       B.     HOMESTEAD 2 REZONE, PLZ-24-4001, DAVID WEEKLEY HOMES IS
              SEEKING APPROVAL FOR A REZONE OF APPROXIMATELY 9.26 ACRES
              AT 12701 SOUTH 3600 WEST.

Mr. Prestwich presented the Staff Report and stated that the property is situated off 3600
West on the south side of 12600 South. The property was identified on an aerial map
displayed. The surrounding lots were identified as having been previously reviewed by the
Commission in 2021. The property was subject to multiple rezoning requests and was
ultimately rezoned CPO with an Elderly Housing Overlay and an SD designation. The SD
zoning designation only lasted for 11 months after which the City Council amended it. Under
that zoning, David Weekly Homes acquired the property, initiated a rezoning process, and
submitted an application for the Homestead Subdivision. It was ultimately approved by the
Planning Commission and resulted in the construction of plotted homes. Following various
discussions, David Weekly Homes contacted the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints
(“LDS”) church about the vacant property. The vacant parcel remained undeveloped and
was overgrown with weeds.

The developer formulated a plan to address this neglected property. Their proposal involves
acquiring access through one of the existing lots and constructing a road leading to the
vacant area. A fire turnaround would then be established at the end of the new road. The
proposed development would allow for the creation of nine new lots in the vacant area, with
the trade-off being the reduction of one lot within the existing development. Additionally,
some adjustments within another section of the existing development may be necessary.
The elements represent future considerations and will be presented in detail at a later date,
likely accompanied by a site plan and proposals for subdivision amendments or a complete
subdivision application. Mr. Prestwich emphasized the value of understanding the overall
goal of the project while having a clear vision for the proposed development of the vacant
property. He noted that the specifics will be addressed at a future date.

If the Commission is satisfied with the current project, approving the rezone request would
allow for a slightly larger development and facilitate the utilization of a property that might
otherwise remain undeveloped. It was noted that the zoning designation for this specific
property is isolated.

Mr. Prestwich clarified that the Planning Commission is acting in a legislative capacity and
functioning as a recommending body. The ultimate decision regarding this rezoning request
rests with the City Council. Following standard protocol for rezoning applications, notices
were distributed to residents within a 1,000-foot radius, exceeding the usual 300-foot
notification range. No public comments were received.

The developer did a lot of work to identify every property owner and prove to the City that
they had permission from all involved. Affidavits from the involved parties were displayed.
Additionally, a number of Power of Attorney documents were included in the packet. The
documents pertain to lots within a pre-existing subdivision that have already been sold.

It was further clarified that David Weekley Homes disclosed the potential rezoning to
potential buyers before any of the lots were sold. As previously mentioned, discussions
regarding the rezoning effort had been ongoing for some time. A delay was encountered
while finalizing a Purchase Agreement with the LDS Church.

The rezoning map was displayed and the portion owned by David Weekley Homes was
identified. It was noted that the zoning designation is a combination of CPO, Elderly Housing
Overlay, and SD. This was acknowledged as confusing and has caused difficulties for
potential buyers and lenders.

David Weekley Homes could have opted to rezone only a specific portion of the property,
however, there was discussion regarding potential confusion. In the interest of clarity, a
recommendation was made to rezone the entire area RM-6. This zoning designation would
come with the same requirements currently applied to the existing development, essentially
resulting in the same type of development. The rezoning was described as simplifying the
zoning map and making things less confusing for everyone involved. Mr. Prestwich stated
that when the rezone was approved, the General Plan was not updated. Staff recommended
approval.

In response to a question raised by Commissioner Rushton regarding the parcel adjacent to
the parking lot, Mr. Prestwich clarified that the area is designated for stormwater
management. It was described as a detention pond with pipes that were exposed as a result
of flooding. The developer and/or the LDS Church will need to modify the reroute of the
water runoff.

The Staff Report included an explanation regarding the mid-process change to the Elderly
Housing Overlay. It changed in the middle of the rezone and no longer applies to commercial
properties. It builds in an implied bonus density. The developer is not seeking additional
units and aims to achieve the base RM-6 density.

Commissioner Rushton asked about the potential for future conversions to general housing
as the demographics shift. Mr. Prestwich explained that the project will not be age-restricted.
The project is currently marketed as an active adult community. Future resident turnover or
changes in target demographics could lead to a shift toward general housing without any
zoning restrictions. Mr. Prestwich stated that the project is not age-restricted. Should an
age restriction be implemented, staff recommended following the prior approach of utilizing
an SD zoning to designate a minimum age of 55 or older. The proposal represents the most
recent iteration of the plan. While density was addressed, the inclusion of the age restriction
and the restriction on units facing Water Street remain open for consideration. It is important
to note that regardless of the chosen path, the zoning designation will ultimately result in
units with a similar appearance. The key decision before the Planning Commission lies in
determining whether to incorporate these additional restrictions.

Vice-Chair Matheson stated that in the absence of the proposed SD establishing a 55+ age
requirement, a potential scenario could arise where the homes would become unexpectedly

requirement. The issue would not require further discussion in 10 to 15 years.

Commissioner Cluff asked for clarification regarding the Elderly Housing Overlay and if the
designation primarily dictates how properties are developed, such as mandating the
inclusion of elderly-friendly features. She further questioned whether the elderly housing
overlay would impose age restrictions on properties. It was clarified that the elderly housing
overlay might be linked to an 80% figure.

A hypothetical scenario was presented. The scenario involved a large family with eight
young children purchasing one of the houses with the total occupancy still falling under the
speculated 80% threshold. A question was raised regarding the possibility of such a
purchase being blocked by existing residents who might object to the noise associated with
young children. Vice-Chair Matheson acknowledged a similar situation that had caused
contention in his own neighborhood in the past.

The applicant, Rulon Dutson, was present representing David Weekley Homes and gave
their business address as 392 East 6400 South in Murray. He thanked staff for their efforts.
He expressed satisfaction with the lack of public opposition to the proposal. This was
interpreted as a positive reflection on the information previously presented to the
Commission over one year ago. He emphasized David Weekley Homes' reputation as a
responsible developer and reported that this is their first project in Riverton. The well-
maintained work site and the positive feedback received from neighboring residents to the

From a planning perspective, he considered the rezoning as a positive step toward aligning
land uses and establishing a designation that reflects the intended purpose and current use
of the property. This approach was presented as a solution to the concerns regarding office
and commercial zoning that had previously drawn opposition from neighbors. The proposed

when the original zoning was approved. This caution stemmed from the recognition that the
relevant ordinance was undergoing a period of transition. The applicant emphasized their
priority of ensuring compliance regardless of the final form of the ordinance.

the Planning Commission's zoning recommendation. He acknowledged the possibility of
the Commission recommending a full rezoning of the commercial area. He questioned the
necessity of such a recommendation if the Commission opted for silence on the matter.

The inconsistency between the current General Plan Map, which depicts the entire area in
blue, and the Commission's preferred zoning designation for a specific section was
highlighted.   While the Commission initially perceived staff as encouraging a
recommendation regarding the commercial area, clarification was sought.

Mr. Prestwich explained that the General Plan serves as a representation of the City's
desired future land use for a particular property. Ideally, upon development, the zoning
designation aligns with the General Plan. The current inconsistency was acknowledged.
However, staff emphasized the ongoing General Plan Amendment process scheduled for
this year. The amendment process was presented as an alternative avenue to address the
inconsistency, eliminating the urgency for a specific recommendation regarding the
commercial area from the commission. In conclusion, staff confirmed that the Commission
addressing the General Plan inconsistency would not pose any problems.

Commissioner Rushton offered a historical perspective on the property, acknowledging that
it was contentious for a while and the various development concepts considered. The
ultimate decision by the City Council to rezone the property and apply the Elderly Housing
Overlay was mentioned. He speculated that the Elderly Housing designation was chosen
due to its potential appeal to nearby residents. The perception of elderly residents being
less disruptive, such as having fewer children and staying home more frequently, was
highlighted. However, it was noted that the density allowed under the elderly housing
overlay still facilitated a viable and profitable development.

The success of the current development, managed by a reputable developer and featuring
a well-selling product, was commended. Commissioner Rushton expressed their belief that
the existing density and location were suitable and advocated against the inclusion of the
elderly housing overlay, aligning with the staff recommendation.

The established Homeowners Association (“HOA”) and their marketing efforts promoting the
community as being for active seniors were acknowledged. He proposed leaving the
decision regarding the future balance of residents to the HOA's discretion. This would allow
them to determine the appropriate time for any potential adjustments.

The growing number of small parcels suitable for similar home development was presented
as a trend likely to become more prevalent in Riverton, given the current housing shortage.
Commissioner Rushton advocated for the rezoning to R6 without the mandatory Elderly
Housing requirement. The ultimate authority of the City Council, as the land-use authority,
to reinstate the Elderly Housing Overlay if deemed necessary was acknowledged. The
complexity of the initial process to allow development on the property was contrasted with
the current success of the project, demonstrated by strong sales figures and the apparent
lack of opposition from nearby residents.

A contrasting perspective was then presented. Vice-Chair Matheson acknowledged his own
age, envisioned himself as a resident, and expressed concern about a sudden influx of

Commissioner Cluff was open to either approach regarding the elderly housing requirement.
Attention was drawn to the applicant's previous statement specifying HOA rules that

A question was raised to clarify whether the 80% occupancy threshold was based on the
Elderly Housing Overlay or the HOA's independent regulations. It was clarified that the HOA
rules incorporate the 80% threshold in addition to any zoning requirements. In essence, the
HOA has established its own self-enforcing rule. Commissioner Cluff emphasized the ability
of the community to modify this rule if they desired.

A suggestion was made to solicit the applicant's preference on the matter of the Elderly
Housing requirement. Commissioner Rushton, however, expressed reservations about the
fairness of such a question. The zoning designation was presented as an external factor
influencing the situation. He complimented the developer on their transparency, citing the
unprecedented practice of obtaining affidavits from all existing residents. This effort was
viewed as a strong indicator of the applicant's commitment to open communication.

Reference was made to the Staff Report, which included a section addressing concerns
raised about building within a commercial zone. Commissioner Rushton acknowledged the
efforts undertaken by Riverton to facilitate the project's development. He mentioned that the
HOA and the governing documents, including covenants and established community
regulations have been established. He was against the need for continued involvement from
the commission on this matter. The successful completion and strong sales figures of the
development were highlighted. He expressed a belief in the self-determining nature of the
community and advocated against unnecessary government intervention in such projects.

Commissioner Cluff raised concerns about potential legal issues for the HOA if they attempt
to alter the 80% elderly occupancy rule, given that buyers had purchased their homes with
the understanding of this restriction. Commissioner Rushton acknowledged the potential for
negativity but argued against allowing such worst-case scenarios to dictate the decision.

A request for clarification was made regarding any potential complications for staff if the
Commission recommended including the same elderly housing requirements previously
established by the overlay designation and if was easy to incorporate such a requirement.
Mr. Prestwich confirmed that the inclusion would be a straightforward process. It was
confirmed that including the requirement would streamline future tasks for staff.
