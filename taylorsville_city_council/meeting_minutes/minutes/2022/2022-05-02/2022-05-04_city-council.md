Councilmember Harker inquired about the status of the fountains at Centennial Plaza. Mayor

a completely separate entity, and the District’s board was authorized to govern by state statute.

        7.1      Public Hearing and Resolution No. 22-11 – A Resolution of the City of
                 Taylorsville adopting Final Redistricting Maps Pursuant to the Requirements
                 of Utah Code Annotated §10-3-205.5(2)(b)(ii) – Tracy Cowdell

       City Attorney Tracy Cowdell reminded the council of the background behind the “One
Person, One Vote” doctrine and the legal process of redistricting to meet that doctrine. The latest
census had been completed and it had been determined that Taylorsville had 60,448 residents
which meant that each council district needed to be made up of approximately 12,100 people. The
city was prohibited from creating district lines that dissected voting precincts. He explained that
the council had adopted tentative maps and notified the public that a public hearing would be held
this evening. The Council could either formally adopt those tentative maps or they could choose
to adopt alternative maps. He pointed out that it was important to ensure that each council district
was as close to the required population as possible. He also reminded the Council that it had to
adopt maps by May 10. If maps were not adopted that evening, a special meeting could be called
with 24 hours’ notice.

Councilmember Cochran wished to confirm that the maps would take effect upon adoption. Mr.
Cowdell confirmed that was correct.

Chair Barbieri asked City Administrator John Taylor to display the tentatively adopted maps. She
then mentioned that Councilmembers Knudsen and Cochran had alternative maps they wished to
present. She asked Councilmember Knudsen to explain the reasoning behind his proposed map
which was then displayed for both the City Council and the public to view.

Councilmember Knudsen explained that he sought to change the districts as little as possible and
thought it made sense to wait to account for future development at Summit Vista until the 2030
census since the growth might not actually happen for several years.

Chair Barbieri then invited Councilmember Cochran to explain his proposed map.
Councilmember Cochran stated he had focused on balancing the population numbers and reducing
the deviation figures. He recommended exchanging voting precincts 11 and 12 between himself
and Councilmember Burgess. He explained that minor change would bring the deviation to under
1%.

      Mayor Overson reiterated her thought process behind creating the maps she recommended.
She wanted to do not only what would work today but also what would work well in the future.
She felt it was important to keep Summit Vista and Volta development areas separate so that all
the expected growth was not concentrated in the same district. She also believed it was important
for elected officials to remove themselves from the equation and simply look at the population
numbers and the anticipated growth which was not theoretical. Summit Vista and Volta, for
example, were “in the books” and could be counted on to increase population figures. She indicated
that all three sets of maps were worthy of consideration.

        Councilmember Knudsen mentioned that voting precinct #TAY001 was primarily
commercial, so moving it to a different council district as was done in the tentatively adopted map
did not change the numbers significantly.

Councilmember Cochran appreciated the thought Councilmember Knudsen had put into his
proposal. His (Councilmember Cochran’s) concern with it was that he felt District No. 1 had too

Chair Barbieri opened the public hearing.

Dennis Sanok indicated he did not have a comment but asked that the various proposals be
displayed again for the public’s benefit. Mr. Taylor displayed each of the three proposals again
and reiterated the deviation of each.

Mr. Cowdell pointed out that all three proposals complied with constitutional requirements and
were defensible.

Marc McElreath expressed appreciation for the presentation but suggested that if two completely
new proposals were now under consideration, he asked that the council table the item and publish
the maps so that the public would have the opportunity to review them. He also asked that in the
future, such items be attached to staff reports so that the public could view them prior to the
meeting.

There was no one else who expressed a desire to speak so Chair Barbieri closed the public hearing.

The various councilmembers discussed the pros and cons of each proposed map.

For clarity, Mr. Taylor stated that moving precinct TAY001 from Council District No. 5 to District
No. 1 would take the deviation from 3.4% to 5%. That was still within legal limits although it was
not taking confirmed growth into consideration.

Councilmember Burgess stated that although he was pleased with all three options, he most liked
the maps that Mayor Overson had proposed. He felt they made the most sense.

Mr. Taylor mentioned that his one caveat was that there were no permits issued for the Ream’s
center, meaning that any development figures in that area were purely theoretical. However, some
development was anticipated. Conversely, there was a confirmed number of units anticipated for
Summit Vista and Volta (1800 and 650+ units respectively).
