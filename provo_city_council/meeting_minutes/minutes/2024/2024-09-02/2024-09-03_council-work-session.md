Item 1: A discussion regarding converting a part-time Laboratory Intern
position into a full-time Laboratory Technician in the Wastewater Fund (24-075)
0:06:09

Gordon Haight, Public Works Director, presented a request to convert a part-time laboratory
technician position into a full-time role. He explained that the change is necessitated by the
upcoming opening of a second water reclamation facility in January and a new water treatment
plant. The additional staffing would ensure proper training and coverage across the three
facilities.

Mr. Haight noted that this proposal represents a modification from the department's original
budget submission. Initially, the department considered adding more positions but opted for a
scaled-back approach after conducting a thorough analysis. The cost increase associated with this
transition is estimated to range from $61,000 to $66,000 annually.

Item 2: A discussion regarding a request to rescind an Ordinance that created a
Franchise Agreement with the Union Pacific Railroad (24-076) 0:12:17

Vern Keeslar, Traffic Manager, presented on the history of franchise agreements between Provo
City and various railroad companies, dating back to 1880. He explained that the most recent
franchise agreement with Union Pacific Railroad expired in 2009.

Mr. Keeslar outlined several current issues with the railroad, including:

       Difficulty obtaining permits for projects near railroad property.
       Lack of maintenance on railroad crossings.
       Noise complaints from train horns.
       Annual fees charged to the city for utility crossings.
       The railroad's claim of ownership over certain city property.

He requested that the Council consider revoking the 1880 ordinance and passing a resolution
recognizing the expiration of the 1909 franchise agreement. This action would enable the city to
negotiate a new agreement and potentially reclaim some property rights.

Councilors asked clarifying questions regarding property ownership, the historic depot
demolition, and the potential impacts of revoking the old ordinance. They expressed support for
initiating negotiations to establish a new, more beneficial agreement with the railroad.

Item 3: A discussion regarding consolidation of forestry services (24-077) 0:37:09

Scott Henderson, Parks & Recreation Director, presented a proposal to consolidate forestry
services under the Energy Department. Currently, these services are divided between Parks &
Recreation and the Energy Department.

Mr. Henderson outlined the benefits of consolidation, which include:

       Placing all forestry services under the expertise of the City Forester.
       Enhancing emergency preparedness with fully trained crews.
       Improving communication with residents regarding tree-related issues.
       Creating salary and training equity among forestry staff.
       Generating general fund savings of approximately $250,000 annually.

John Borgett, Administrative Services Director, elaborated on the budget implications. He stated
that approximately $205,000 would need to be appropriated from the Energy fund balance to

Councilors asked clarifying questions about the budget process and long-term financial impacts.

Item 4: An ordinance amending the Zone Map classification of approx 1.53 acres
of real property, generally located at 484 N 2760 W, from the Agricultural (A1.5)
zone to the One-Family Residential (R1.8) Zone. Lakeview South Neighborhood.
(PLRZ20240184) 1:02:57

Mary Barnes, Planner, presented a request to rezone approximately 1.53 acres of property from
the Agricultural (A1.5) zone to the One-Family Residential (R1.8) zone. The property owner
sought the rezoning to subdivide the lot into two parcels, which was not permissible under the
current zoning.

Ms. Barnes explained that the proposed rezoning:

       Aligns with the surrounding zoning.
       Supports the goals outlined in the General Plan.
       Enables the creation of a new building lot in the area.

Councilor Bogdin raised concerns about potential future development if a cul-de-sac were added
to adjacent property. Ms. Barnes and Bill Peperone, Development Services Director, clarified
that even with a cul-de-sac, the property would likely only accommodate 2-4 lots due to width
and frontage requirements.

Councilors discussed the existing easement on the property and the implications for future
development. They expressed general support for the rezoning request.

Item 5: A discussion regarding Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs): Special Use
Permit (24-016) 1:11:10

Melia Dayley, Policy Analyst, provided an update on previous council motions concerning
Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) and initiated a discussion regarding the special use permit
process for ADUs.

Ms. Dayley presented two staff recommendations:

       Repeal the signature pathway process.
       Amend the minimum neighborhood approval requirement from 66% to 50%.

She explained the current pathways for obtaining ADU approval and highlighted the differences
between them.

Councilor Handley moved to maintain the status quo for the signature pathway special use
permit, and Councilor Hoban seconded the motion.

Extensive discussion followed, with Councilors expressing differing viewpoints:

       Some supported retaining the pathway, emphasizing the economic benefits for
        homeowners and the housing demand for students.
       Others favored its repeal, citing neighborhood conflicts and the low number of permits
        issued through the process.

Councilor MacKay made a substitute motion to direct staff to draft language to repeal the single
property Special Use Permit, which was seconded by Councilor Christensen. Further
deliberations addressed enforcement challenges, the impact on illegal ADUs, and the broader
consequences of repealing the pathway.

Councilor MacKay’s motion to repeal the signature pathway for ADU approval was ultimately
passed with a vote of 4-3, with councilors Handley, Hoban, and Whipple opposed.

Brian Jones, City Attorney, requested consent to include clarifying language in the remaining
pathway to set expectations regarding signature collection not guaranteeing approval. No
objections were raised.
