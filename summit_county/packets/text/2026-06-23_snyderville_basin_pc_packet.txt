Proposal 
 
The applicant is requesting a Conditional Use Permit to construct a ~750,000-gallon 
underground water tank and associated facilities. The purpose of the project is to replace an 
existing above-ground leaking water tank (Parcel SL-D-215-X) which is beyond repair. The 
replacement water tank will accommodate existing and future water needs for Snyderville 
Basin’s Silver Creek Service Area 3.  
 
The project also includes ~5,000-ft of additional water line improvement. The water line is 10 
inches in diameter and does not require a land use permit. 
 
All improvements are taking place within recorded easements, public utility easements, and 
road easements.

 Summit County Service Area 3 - Primary Water Storage Tank and District Well 
 Conditional Use Permit Application 
 Summit County Service Area 3 is seeking approval to construct and replace a new district 
 primary water storage tank on lot SS-21-A-X, owned by Park City. 
 The water system of Service Area 3 was initially built in the early 1960s with minor 
 improvements through the years. With this aging system and the rapid growth of SIlver Creek, 
 Service Area 3 needs critical improvements and upgrades to meet current and future growth. 
 Currently Service Area 3 is approximately 85% built out. 
 In 2024, Service Area 3 applied for funding with the Utah Division of Drinking Water for a major 
 capital improvement to upgrade and improve the aging water system. During that application 
 process, Service Area 3’s primary above ground water storage tank began leaking and was 
 classified as beyond repair and in dire need of replacement. 
 In May 2024, emergency funding was approved through the Division of Drinking Water to 
 expedite its relocation and replacement. Additionally, the Division of Drinking Water approved 
 funding for the greater upgrade and improvement plan which includes the replacement of the 
 primary district well, arsenic zone isolation of the back-up district well, and water main upgrades 
 to meet current fire flow requirements. 
 The requested CUP is for a replacement in-ground concrete water tank which will hold 750,000 
 gallons. This major upgrade will meet critical storage and fire flow to the residents of Silver 
 Creek and meet the expected demands at full build out of Service Area 3. The expected life of 
 this type of concrete storage tank will exceed 75 years. 
 Service Area 3 identified the new location on SS-21-A-X as the ideal relocation site. The 
 elevation allows for optimal flow and head pressure, thus enhancing safety and redundancy. 
 The site has minimal to no impact on the visual landscape to the residents of Service Area 3. 
 Plans do not entail landscaping, fencing, exterior lighting, or additional structures as the tank will 
 be in-ground. 
 The site of the current above ground water tank is SL-D-215-X and will be abandoned and we 
 will repurpose the location to drill a new district well to replace the existing well which is more 
 than 60 years old. 
 Service Area 3 is currently in the final process of purchasing a perpetual easement from Park 
 City for SS-21-A-X. The lot is adjacent to Service Area 3’s boundaries and considered 
 unincorporated Summit County. Additionally, at a later date, Park City may request the 
 annexation of this lot into Service Area 3.

Proposal 
The applicant, on behalf of the owner, is requesting Conditional Use approval for an Accessory 
Dwelling Unit (ADU) in Unit 47 of the Park East III Business Community Condominiums. The 
applicant states that the main level will include workshop/storage, bathroom, and mechanical 
room; the mezzanine floor will remain unchanged; and the second (top) level will include a 2-
bedroom ADU as well as a work room, office, and restroom.

Background 
On February 18, 2026, the applicant submitted a Conditional Use Permit (CUP) application to 
construct an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) within the second level of Park East III, Unit 47, 
located at 4518 North Forestdale Drive, Unit #47. Pool Party Place LLC, C/O Mountain West 
Small Business Finance, is listed as the property owner. The subject unit is located within a shell 
building that was originally approved via a Low Impact Permit on December 27, 2021. The Park 
East III Business Community Condominium plat was subsequently recorded on March 22, 2022 
(Entry #01185685). These condominiums are located within the Park City Business Center 
(PCBC) and are subject to the PCBC Development Agreement. The property is also governed by 
the Combined Parking Management Plan Park East II and Park East III (Exhibit B). 
 
The project is located within the Service Commercial (SC) zone. Because the current application 
is limited to the review of a proposed ADU, the parking analysis focuses on ensuring the new 
residential use does not conflict with established site parking. The building’s primary 
commercial and warehouse uses are governed by the existing Parking Management Plan, which 
allocates two dedicated spaces to this unit: one overhead door space and one reserved space. 
The existing commercial configuration remains unchanged and compliant with the SC zone. 
Development Code Section 10-8-5 requires a minimum of one off-street parking space for an 
ADU. To satisfy this requirement, Staff has included Condition of Approval #3, which mandates 
that one of the two allocated parking spaces for this unit be reserved solely for the use of the 
ADU tenant(s). 
 
 
25
3 
 
The original shell building approval created a top level that exceeds the maximum allowable 
area for an ADU, which is limited to 1,000 square feet. Due to this square footage restriction, 
the applicant is proposing an ADU consisting of approximately 999.12 square feet, accessed via 
a staircase from the mezzanine level. The remaining space on the second level will be utilized as 
a work room, office, and restroom. Proposed floor plans detailing this layout are included in 
Exhibit A. 
 
Section 10-2-10 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code lists Accessory Dwelling Units as a 
Conditional Use within the Service Commercial Zone. Only one (1) ADU may be permitted per 
commercial unit within the zone. Staff and service providers have reviewed the project and 
found that it complies, as conditioned, with all requirements of the Code and the Park City 
Business Center Development Agreement.

PEIIIBCC-47 of the Park East III Business Community Condominiums.

Business Finance.

STAFF REPORT 
 
To:   Snyderville Basin Planning Commission   
From:   Jennifer Strader, Principal Planner 
Date of Meeting: June 23, 2026 
Type of Item:  Development Code Amendments - Public Hearing, Possible Action 
Process:  Legislative Review 
 
 
Proposal 
Staff is proposing to amend certain sections of the Snyderville Basin Development Code (Code) 
to ensure consistency with Senate Bill 284 (SB 284), which was introduced in the 2026 
legislative session. 
 
Background 
In the 2026 legislative session, SB 284 was adopted, which modified provisions related to land 
use. The amendments to the Code are necessary to ensure compliance with the applicable 
provisions. SB284 does the following in regard to the necessary Code amendments: 
 
• clarifies standards for county land use regulations and requirements; 
• addresses requirements relating to an appeal or variance hearing;  
• prohibits a legislative body from acting as an appeal authority;  
• modifies the standard of review of a land use authority's decision to deny or 
approve a land use application;  
• modifies appeal requirements; and  
• makes technical and conforming changes. 
 
Proposed Amendments 
1. Section 10-2-10: USE TABLE 
 a. All defined terms have been capitalized. 
c. Added a section for a “business use” that allows the County Council to determine 
if a new or unlisted “business use” should be allowed in the Code without 
receiving a recommendation from the Planning Commission. 
 
2. Section 10-3-2: GENERAL PROVISIONS 
 a. All defined terms have been capitalized. 
c. Replaced County Council with Administrative Law Judge as the appeal authority.

2 
 
3. Section 10-3-9: SUBDIVISIONS 
 a. All defined terms have been capitalized. 
b. Replaced County Council with Administrative Law Judge as the appeal authority. 
 
4. Section 10-3-18: AMENDMENT TO RECORDED SUBDIVISION PLAT 
 a. All defined terms have been capitalized. 
 b. Replaced County Council with Administrative Law Judge as the appeal authority. 
 
5. Section 10-9-19: ENFORCEMENT 
 a. All defined terms have been capitalized. 
b. Replaced County Council with Administrative Law Judge as the appeal authority. 
 
6. Section 10-9-22: APPEAL PROCEDURES 
 a. All defined terms have been capitalized. 
b. Replaced County Council with Administrative Law Judge as the appeal authority. 
 
7. Section 10-9-10: DECISION MAKING AND ADMINISTRATIVE BODIES 
 a. All defined terms have been capitalized. 
b. Updated and reorganized Planning Commission powers and duties, including the 
addition of training requirements and bylaws. 
  
Analysis and Findings 
Section 10-7-3 of the Code states that whenever there is initiated an amendment to the Code, 
it must be reviewed by the Planning Commission who will deliver a recommendation to the 
County Council. The County Council, after holding a public hearing, shall approve, approve with 
modifications, or deny the amendment according to the following criteria. 
 
Criteria 1: The amendment shall be consistent with the goals, objectives, and policies of the 
general plan.  
 
Analysis: The Snyderville Basin General Plan emphasizes clear, predictable, and 
measurable development regulations (Policy 2.5) and encourages clarity in Code 
provisions (Objective B). The proposed amendments are procedural in nature and do 
not alter land use designations or development standards. The amendments improve 
clarity, consistency, and internal organization of the Development Code, and therefore 
do not conflict with, and are consistent with, the General Plan. 
 
Criteria 2: The amendment shall not permit the use of land that is not consistent with the uses 
of properties nearby.  
 
Analysis: The proposed amendments do not modify permitted uses, zoning districts, or 
development standards, and do not authorize any new land uses. 
 
75
3 
 
Criteria 3: The amendment will not permit suitability of the properties affected by the proposed 
amendment for the uses to which they have been restricted.  
 
Analysis: The proposed amendments establish procedural requirements for the 
classification of uses and review of new or unlisted business uses as required by SB 284. 
The amendments do not approve or authorize any new land use, nor do they modify 
existing zoning regulations, permitted uses, or development standards.  
 
Criteria 4: The amendment will not permit the removal of the then existing restrictions which 
will unduly affect nearby property.  
 
Analysis: The proposed amendments are administrative in nature and are intended to 
establish procedures required by state law. The amendments do not remove existing 
development restrictions or alter the standards applicable to specific properties.  
 
Criteria 5: The amendment will not grant special favors or circumstances solely for one 
property owner or developer.  
 
 Analysis: The amendments will affect the entire Snyderville Basin Planning District. 
 
Criteria 6: The amendment will promote the public health, safety and welfare better than the 
existing regulations for which the amendment is intended to change. 
 
Analysis: While the proposed amendments are primarily procedural in nature and are 
not expected to directly affect public health or safety, they promote the public welfare 
by providing clear standards and processes for land use classification decisions, reducing 
ambiguity in Code administration, and improving predictability for applicants, staff, and 
the public. 
 
Recommendation 
Staff recommends that the Planning Commission review the proposed amendments, conduct a 
public hearing, and taking into account any public input, forward a positive recommendation to 
the County Council based on the following Findings of Fact and Conclusions of Law. 
 
FINDINGS OF FACT 
1. The Snyderville Basin Development Code is adopted under authority granted to the 
County to regulate land use within the Snyderville Basin Planning District.  
2. The Utah Legislature adopted Senate Bill 284 (SB 284) during the 2026 legislative 
session, modifying provisions related to land use regulation, including classification of 
uses, appeal authority structure, and related procedural requirements.  
3. The proposed amendments revise portions of the Code to ensure consistency with SB 
284, including updates to use classification procedures, appeal authority assignments, 
and related administrative provisions.  
76
4 
 
4. The proposed amendments include, but are not limited to, capitalization and 
clarification of defined terms, updates to statutory and internal Code references, and 
revisions to appeal authority provisions to comply with state law requirements.  
5. The proposed amendments establish a process for classification of unlisted or new 
business uses and provide for review by the County Council consistent with SB 284 
requirements.  
6. The proposed amendments do not modify zoning districts, land use designations, or 
development standards applicable to property within the Snyderville Basin Planning 
District.  
7. The proposed amendments are procedural and administrative in nature and are 
intended to improve clarity, consistency, and compliance with state law. 
 
CONCLUSIONS OF LAW 
1. Pursuant to Section 10-7-3 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code, the Planning 
Commission and County Council are authorized to review and act upon proposed 
amendments to the Code.  
2. The proposed amendments are consistent with the goals, objectives, and policies of the 
Snyderville Basin General Plan, including provisions promoting clear, predictable, and 
measurable development regulations.  
3. The proposed amendments do not permit land uses that are inconsistent with 
surrounding properties.  
4. The proposed amendments do not adversely affect the suitability of properties for their 
currently permitted uses.  
5. The proposed amendments do not remove existing development restrictions in a 
manner that would unduly affect nearby properties.  
6. The proposed amendments do not grant preferential treatment or special benefits to 
any individual property owner or developer and apply uniformly throughout the 
Snyderville Basin Planning District.  
7. The proposed amendments promote the public health, safety, and welfare by improving 
clarity, consistency, and compliance with state law in the administration of the Code.  
8. The proposed amendments comply with applicable provisions of Utah Code, including 
the requirements of SB 284, and are consistent with the County’s authority to regulate 
land use.

for a determination regarding the classification of a proposed Use that is not

B. In cases where a proposed Use is not listed in the table, the Director shall compare
the nature and characteristics of the proposed Use with those of the Uses
specifically listed and make a determination if the proposed Use is similar in nature
and logically fits into any of the categories listed.
1. Where it is determined that the proposed Use is consistent with an existing
category or Use, the proposed Use shall be permitted, conditional, or
prohibited as the existing Use with which it has been associated.

more specific category shall apply.

Uses listed, the Use shall be prohibited unless and until this title is amended
to specifically include the Use.
