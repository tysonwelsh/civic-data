         2.3. Discussion regarding a reimbursement agreement with Parkwest Real Estate
              Partners, LLC for infrastructure improvements associated with the Axia Business
              Park Development – Blake Thomas, Community Development Director.
      *This item was discussed first*

      Community Development Director Thomas stated this was a reimbursement agreement for
      the Axia development off Porter Rockwell and this location was in between another
      neighboring property and an existing development. He stated to be eligible to be reimbursed,
      it had to be upsized because of a neighboring development. He added it also needed to be in
      alignment with the Master Plan, and there needed to be funds available.

      Community Development Director Thomas showed the site plan and the costs for each of
      the reimbursable items. He stated a part of the site would be deeded to the City for a park
      and ride location, and he highlighted that 22-percent of the road was impact fee eligible, but
      since it was fronting a piece of property that they were developing they were ultimately
      responsible for the whole section of the road. Therefore, he proposed in this reimbursement
      agreement they pay for the 22-percent that was impact fee eligible up front, and then they
      would put the $117,000 from Wasatch Front into the project.

      Community Development Director Thomas stated the next piece was the section of the road
      that fronted property, which he noted was basically the upsize cost. He explained there was
      a piece of storm drain crossing and so they would be required to reroute the storm drain
      around the building. He estimated there was about 84 feet of storm drain crossing they would
      have to reimburse in the project cost of $211,007, and there were also water lines under that
      road as well, with a cost of about $218,000. Community Development Director Thomas
      stated they were expecting a revenue of about $325,000 in eligible reimbursements, and the
      plan would be to offset the developer instead of reimbursing the costs directly.

      Councilmember Shields asked why they had to pay the difference if the impact fees were
      insufficient to reimburse the whole cost. Community Development Director Thomas
May 24, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                                   Page 3 of 19


      explained it was a mostly a timing thing. Councilmember Shields stated if they continued to
      perform projects based on future impact fees they would fall behind and never catch up.

      Councilmember Henderson asked why they were agreeing to pay more than they were

          2.2. Discussion of Proposed Amendments to the Fiscal Year 2023 Budget – Kyle
               Maurer, Finance Director
      Finance Director Maurer offered an overview of the proposed amendments. He reviewed
      the vehicle purchases and stated there were some vehicle leases that had not been budgeted
      correctly. He stated he wanted to eliminate the police fund subsidies and get that fund back
      to zero. He also expressed their external auditors had suggested making sure all of the impact
      fees were in the correct funds. Finance Director Maurer stated they had had some
      reimbursement agreements which they had done some catchups on and reconciled and added
      they had received some sizable grants that he wanted to get into the budget, although they
      would not start spending on them until next year.

      Finance Director Maurer stated one of his goals with the adjustments was to not have to pull
      from the fund balance to accomplish everything. He said he had gone through every single
      personnel line item and recalculated it through the end of the year to see what kind of savings
      he could come up with and had found approximately $780,000. He stated some of the vehicle
      leases had not been budgeted correctly, which was a $45,000 adjustment and the vehicles
      he had missed were approximately $191,000. He clarified the vehicles he was referring to
      were engineering vehicles and were all replacement vehicles which were scheduled for 2024
      but had been ordered a year early.

      Finance Director Maurer stated they had purchased a pothole patching machine for the
      streets, and he indicated they had received more fourth quarter county sales taxes which
