2. Planning Matters
2.1 First Reading: ZT-19-003 Adoption of City Center Overlay Zone; Francis Lilly,
Planning and Zoning Director
Mayor Silvestrini said the Council would have a first reading on the matter and would
make a decision on it at the next meeting. Francis Lilly said the City Center Overlay
Zone (CCOZ) would replace the Town Center Overlay Zone which would be compliant
with the adopted City Center Master Plan. The overlay zone accomplished significant
things, it was more detailed and prescriptive than any other ordinance Millcreek had.
Each application would be required to go through the conditional use criteria of
identifying and mitigating detrimental effects, even if it was not a conditional use. The
overlay zone would protect the rights of existing businesses. Applicability of the
standards would be triggered either when 50 percent or more of a building was
remodeled, or when a building was added to by 15 percent. There would be
neighborhood-based standards that vary by district; Neighborhood, Mill Center, and
Marketplace. There would be a new process for Planning Commission review and public
input, triggered by the intensity of the building. Conditional uses would be based on an
intensity threshold; total square footage greater than 25,000 square feet, a ground floor
footprint greater than 15,000 square feet, or a height greater than 36 feet. There would
also be a detailed approach to building height that reflects sensitivity to views, mass, and
neighborhood input. Proposals would go through a preapplication meeting, site plan
review application, staff analysis of detrimental effects, conditional use permit
application and Planning Commission review if required, staff site plan
approval/preliminary conditional use permit, final development plan/final conditional use
permit, then a building permit. There would be a high standard for building design in
building form and materials, streets, signs, open space, and resiliency and broadband
service.

There had been community input on the CCOZ through the General Plan process with
city-wide outreach; City Center Master Plan process throughout the City, especially with
the Mountair neighborhood; Town Center Overlay Zone as a temporary overlay; and
hearings, workshops, open houses, studies, designers, and meetings on the City Center

Overlay Zone. There had been input from residents on trees and rooftop features, from
park designers on mixed use components and setbacks, from developers on window
offsets and building separation, and the Planning Commission on height, conditional
uses, safety, and transitions and fagade variation. All the community councils
recommended approval of the overlay zone. The Millcreek Community Council’s input
provided an addition to the fee-in-lieu to trade the 20% open space requirement for
pedestrian improvements and concerns about height. The Canyon Rim Citizens
Association’s input resulted in height adjustments to the southside of 3300 South to
preserve views. The East Mill Creek Community Council’s input resulted in changes to
the street tree list for Neighborhood streets. The Mount Olympus Community Council’s
input resulted in more non-residential use requirements for corner buildings on the
northeast and northwest corners of 3300 S.

Lilly said the City would need to continue working on other items after the CCOZ
adoption, including: Highland Drive corridor improvements, Mountair streetscape, Villa
Vista Avenue redevelopment, Millcreek Common design, parking district/parking
structures, residential parking restrictions, Millcreek Common construction, initial
development around Millcreek Common and along 3300 S. and Highland Drive,
implementation of a business improvement district, potential code revisions, and
pedestrian improvements. He added the annexation concept with the boundary line
adjustment with Salt Lake City that would take effect in July 2020 to the list. The
General Plan addresses building heights that range from 2-6 stories and that development
abutting lower intensity in established neighborhoods should provide height transitions in
massing and height. This ordinance would accomplish what the General Plan wanted to
accomplish. The City Center Master Plan adopted guiding principles to guide future
ordinance development, public-private partnerships, and land use decisions. The City
Center should emphasize walkability, minimize internal traffic and parking demand, and
include municipal property owned by the City and designated for public use. The design
of the City Center should represent the City in ways unique to Millcreek and emphasize
culture, art, ideas and interaction between citizens. Lilly highlighted the proposed
building height map from the ordinance to the Council with the greatest allowable height,
48-74 feet, around the open space. Staff recommended approval of the ordinance as
recommended by the Planning Commission. Lilly thanked John Janson for his work on
the ordinance.

Mayor Silvestrini asked about the conditional use process. Lilly said the Planning

projection was based on. He said it depended on the timing of the increment being
absorbed.

3. Financial Matters
3.1 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 19-39, Authorizing the Issuance and
Sale of Not More Than $4,350,000 Tax and Revenue Anticipation Notes, Series
2020A Of Millcreek, Utah; and Related Matters
Mayor Silvestrini said the resolution was for the borrowing Millcreek inherited from the
Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area each year to fund the Unified Police
Department (UPD) contract price. The borrowed amount enabled Millcreek to fund
police services through the rest of the fiscal year. Millcreek has been able to reduce the
borrowing amount by a couple million dollars. The process authorizes the Mayor to sign
a note with a lender to borrow the amount to pay by the end of the year. Jason
Burningham said they had solicited lenders and received two bids from entities with
proposals for tax exempt and taxable rates. He provided a handout to the Council with
those details. He said both entities, Zions Bank and Bank of Utah, had purchased these
notes in the past. There were two issuances a year based on the fiscal and calendar years.
The amount in the resolution was not to exceed $4,350,00 and Burningham was
anticipating the notes would be closer to $4,335,000. The resolution would put
parameters in place and authorize the Mayor the ability to execute and finalize the
agreement with the selected lender. Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek had been receiving
property tax money early that year. Ideally the City would borrow all on a tax-exempt
basis but there were parameters qualifying for tax-exempt borrowing and due to
Millcreek’s high General Fund balance, Millcreek was not able to borrow as much on a
tax-exempt basis. Laurie Johnson said she did a cash flow model with UPD to keep
Millcreek whole between property tax collection and the cost of UPD. She said she
validated the amount Millcreek was borrowing. Council Member Marchant asked how
the Council could reduce the debt the coming year. Burningham said the Council could
use fund balance though Millcreek’s bond rating was based on a high fund balance so the
Council should not dip that too much. Johnson said a property tax increase would have
reduced the amount borrowed. Burningham said the Council was going in the right
direction. Council Member Marchant reiterated that Millcreek did not create the debt but
inherited it and have since reduced it. Burningham said the borrowing was not debt on the
books but a short-term obligation. John Brems said the Council should adopt the current
version since an updated one was distributed that morning.

Council Member Marchant moved to approve Resolution 19-39, which includes the latest
modifications made mentioned by Mr. Brems, authorizing the issuance and sale of not
more than $4,350,000 tax and revenue anticipation notes, Series 2020A of Millcreek, Utah;
and related matters. Council Member Jackson seconded. The Recorder called for the vote.
All Council Members voted yes. The motion passed unanimously.
