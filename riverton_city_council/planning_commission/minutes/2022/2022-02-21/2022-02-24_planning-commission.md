       A.     CONDITIONAL USE HOME OCCUPATION, “SJ METAL” TO BE LOCATED
              AT 4541 WEST PARK BEND COURT. APPLICANT, SAM AND STACI
              COMPAGNO.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich, presented the staff report and stated that the application is for
a home occupation for SJ Metal Designs located on the northwest corner of the City just
south of 11800 South. The property is located in the Parkview Subdivision. Mr. Prestwich
identified the location of the subject property on a map displayed and reported that the notice
was sent to property owners within 300 feet. The lot is a .26-acre corner lot that is zoned R-
3. There was a time in City planning when developers could dedicate open space and
deduct that from the required lot sizes. For that reason, the lot is smaller than the required
one-third-acre for R-3 zoning. Mr. Prestwich stated that that is no longer a practice in
Riverton.

SJ Metal uses a CNC plasma cutting table to make custom parts and artwork. The staff
report erroneously refers to it as a waterjet. There are no non-resident employees and they
have no other home occupation businesses. They do not anticipate any customer visits and
they are requesting permission to use about 25% of the attached garage for their business.
The ordinance allows for up to 50% usage.

Home Occupations are allowed in Riverton City and the ordinance allows for a variety of
home occupation uses. The key is that the Planning Commission evaluates the applications.
It was noted that there are three levels of home occupations. There has been an increased
level of scrutiny each time a person goes from one level to another. A simple home
occupation is the most common and there are probably thousands in the City. This level is
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                                 2
February 24, 2022

where a homeowner may have an LLC for a rental home that they own elsewhere or they
run an online business where no customers or employees come to the home. In this case,
a Business License is required. The strict rules for a simple home occupation include all of
the fixed standards and qualifications set forth in the ordinance. The second level is a staff-
approved home occupation, and the third involves an applicant with at least one variation
from the fixed standards or qualifications and needs approval from the Planning
Commission.

Because the applicants are requesting use of the garage, the application automatically
comes before the Planning Commission. There is a two-car attached garage with additional
parking on the side of the driveway. The applicant is required to provide a layout plan of the
proposed usage, which they have done.

The CNC machine uses electricity and air to cut the metal, and underneath the wireframe is
water to catch sparks and by-products. The Fire Department found that there are other
machines like this in use throughout the valley and that they perform an inspection, however,
it does not require an Operational Permit, so there should be no further issues. The sparks
generated from this type of machine are minimal and do not produce enough energy to
present a fire hazard.

Mr. Prestwich explained that the ordinance states that a workshop, including carpentry,
small-scale sheet metal work, furniture making, upholstery, and other similar activities must
come to the Planning Commission as a Conditional Use. Additionally, if any portion of the
garage space is proposed it must receive approval from the Planning Commission.

Mr. Prestwich described how the machine works and the level of noise it produces. He
stated that it is no louder than what is typical in a neighborhood such as lawnmowers,
motorcycles, stereos, etc. He stated that there should be little impact on the neighborhood
from the business.

Significant public comment was received on the application, which was included in the
packet. Staff recommended approval subject to the conditions set forth in the staff report.
It was noted that the Building and Fire Departments inspected and approved the application.

The applicant, Staci Compagno, reported that they do sheet metal design artwork. She
showed an example of their work and stated that they have been doing it as a hobby. There
has been a great deal of interest so they decided to do it as a business and take the steps
necessary to obtain a Business License. Their business consists entirely of custom orders
and they do not expect any customers to come to the home. They also do not expect any
delivery of materials. The machine used allows for a 4’ x 4’ piece of material and is easy to
pick up and manage. Some of the concerns expressed by the neighbors included the
number of cars currently at the residence. Mrs. Compagno explained that they have four
teenage and/or young adult children living with them who come and go in their vehicles.
They also have family visit on occasion. All of their vehicles can be parked in the driveway.
The machine is quieter than an air compressor. Additionally, both applicants have full-time
jobs and will only be using the CNC machine at night and on weekends.

       B.     CONDITIONAL USE, “SCOOTERS COFFEE”, A COMMERCIAL DRIVE-
              THRU BUSINESS, TO BE LOCATED AT 3479 WEST 12600 SOUTH.
              APPLICANT, KIM WILLIFORD REPRESENTING SCOOTERS COFFEE.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the request was reviewed by the
Planning Commission as a rezone in January. The subject property was identified on a map
displayed. The entire corner is undeveloped but zoned for commercial development. The
subject parcel is 0.51 acres in size and has had numerous inquiries over the years about
potential development. It has been difficult to develop because of its small size. The
previous zoning was C-N and Staff recommended a rezone to C-G (Commercial Gateway),
which was approved by the City Council. Scooters Coffee recently applied for a Conditional
Use Permit for a drive-thru to serve food, which is a Conditional Use in that zone.

The public notice was sent to property owners within 1,000 feet. Mr. Prestwich reported that
notices are sent to neighboring property owners whenever development is proposed. He
appreciated the patience of the residents throughout the process. Mr. Prestwich displayed
a rendering showing the parcel and the surrounding zoning. He explained that the General
Plan called for office-type use and the Commercial Gateway zoning allows for more uses
than the CPO zone. When a property is commercially zoned, it is because different uses
work better in different places. The City has a table of several land uses and depending on
the zone, a use is listed as permitted, not permitted, or conditional. If it is permitted, it does
not come before the Planning Commission until there is a site plan. If it is conditional, it
needs to comply with the Code and receive Planning Commission approval.

This particular application is a drive-thru for foodservice and is shown on the table as a
conditional use. The site plan shows the driveway coming off 12600 South and will require
the Utah Department of Transportation (“UDOT”) to permit the access. Parking was
provided for a walk-up window and Americans with Disabilities Act (“ADA”) parking. No
fencing was shown on the plan but Staff recommended fencing along the border of the
residential property to block car lights from shining into the windows of the residences.

Staff recommended approval of the application subject to the conditions set forth in the staff
report.

Commissioner Gilchrist asked about the traffic anticipated for the proposed business. He
expressed concern with the left turn from 12600 South into the property. Mr. Prestwich
stated that a Traffic Study was not conducted. The parking requirements include four cars
stacking at the window. Some car washes in the area allow for the stacking of four to five
cars, which is not enough. The applicant has maximized the stacking to the extent possible

on the plan. If a Traffic Study is needed, the Commission can request one. The left-hand
turn may be restricted as UDOT will be reviewing it and making final recommendations.

Commissioner Park asked about the potential to add an island to direct traffic to the right
and prohibit left turns. Mr. Prestwich explained that because it is a UDOT decision, they
could add the requirement that the applicant comply with all UDOT requirements.

The applicant, Katherine Butae, explained that the right turn only was proposed after working
with the Civil Engineer and UDOT. There will only be right turns into and out of the property.
A concrete barrier will limit turning movements. It was noted that the applicant continues to
work with UDOT. The Traffic Study was drafted and ready to submit with the plans. The

can accommodate the stacking of eight cars with the proposed configuration. It was
anticipated that in a three-hour period there will likely be 80 to 90 cars going through the
drive-thru, which is the maximum that they can handle. They plan to have a double window
to avoid stacking extending into the street.

       C.     CONDITIONAL USE, “STELLING PRIVATE LANE”, TO BE LOCATED AT
              1611 WEST 13400 SOUTH. APPLICANT, KEVIN GUST WITH MAJESTIC
              HOMES.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the application is for a private lane.
The location of the subject property was identified on a map displayed. He explained that
everything west of Redwood Road and 13400 South gets wider and everything east of
Redwood Road and 13400 South has a soft shoulder with a swale and an asphalt path
behind it. He recognized that the area has unique characteristics that the City wants to
maintain. There are a number of homes in the area that are served by private lanes. The
zoning is RR-22, which is one-half acre residential with animal rights. This area is closely
protected by the Planning Commission and City Council to maintain the current zoning. The
General Plan calls for a more rural feel.

City Attorney, Ryan Carter, stated that this area of Redwood Road has historically been an
organized community and the City is dedicated to keeping it as rural as possible.
Mr. Prestwich stated that the subject property has an existing private lane along the property
line that serves another lot and homes. The parcel is approximately five acres in size with
one home that is served by the private lane. There are two homes on the private lane and
the applicant is asking for approval of three additional homes. There is enough frontage for
the third home to face the street but the configuration gives the applicant more flexibility.
The setbacks are not affected by either configuration. The proposed layout shows the lane
on the east side of the property with a 30-foot right-of-way, 26 feet of pavement, and three
lots rather than one. The applicant was prepared to move forward with the site plan as soon
as the private lane issue is resolved.

Mr. Prestwich stated that private lanes are evaluated in City Code and allowed in any
residential zone where at least two of the following conditions exist:

       1.     A Lot of Record that is pre-existing and has no frontage or it can be
              demonstrated by the applicant that the property cannot physically be
              subdivided with public streets.

       2.     The proposed development on a private lane will not impede the extension of
              any adjacent public right-of-way either now or in the foreseeable future.

       3.     The proposed development does not impede the necessary access from
              adjoining properties as required by the Master Transportation Plan.

It was noted that the adjoining properties already use the property through a right-of-way.
The main concern is with the property in the rear if the owners decide to develop it. It
appeared that because the other lane does not connect through to provide access, the City’s
preference was made known.

The Master Transportation Plan does not show a road that needs a connection in this area,
so a private lane can be used. When evaluating a private lane, if it meets the criteria, it
should include 20 feet of paved surface for one or two lots and 25 feet of paved surface for

set regarding the requirements and the share of the cost of maintenance.

Commissioner Gilchrist asked if the area of the private lane was included in the lot size,

has been a greater need for housing and families have wanted to develop the back one-half
acre of their lots. This is a way the City has chosen to accommodate that and it was
considered very carefully.

shared the history of the area and stated that the ultimate conclusion was that the residents
decided to pay the price of not upgrading the roads to keep the rural feel and the existing

it to remain.
