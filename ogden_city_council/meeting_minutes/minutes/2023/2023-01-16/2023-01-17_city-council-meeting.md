A memo from the City Comptroller regarding a proposed amendment to the Fiscal Year (FY) 2023-2027 Capital
Improvement Plan (CIP) and a proposed amendment to the Fiscal Year (FY) 2022-2023 budget came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated current proposal is to add project PD001 to the plan with funding proposed for FY23. The
Planning Commission reviewed the proposed amendment at the November 16, 2022 meeting and forwarded a recommendation of
approval. The Commission found that the proposal was consistent with the general plan. Administration is proposing to fund the
VirTra relocation and Public Service building roof repairs with fund balance from the General Fund and the Business Depot
Ogden (BDO) Fund. The General Facilities Improvements project #FL007 will receive $907,000 and anew VirTra CIP #PD001
will receive $437,000 for a total of $1,344,000. The Administration is also proposing to transfer $300,000 from the BDO Fund to
the Redevelopment Agency for the down payment and due diligence activities related to the purchase of the Union Station
property. To allow Fleet and Facilities to order needed equipment that traditionally have long lead times, the Administration is
proposing that $1,000,000 in retained earnings be appropriated to an equipment reserve fund. In addition, Fleet and Facilities is
proposing to use funds from the sale of assets to acquire new vehicles for Public Services, Police, Community and Economic
Development, and Management Services.

Police Chief Young summarized the memo and explained he is proposing the addition of project PD001, Francom Public
Safety Building VirTra Expansion, to the FY2023-2027 Capital Improvement Plan (CIP). The project would relocate the Police ,
Department’s 360-degree interactive training platform from its current location at the Weber County Sports Shooting Complex to ‘
the Francom Building Safety Building. An existing bay that is currently used as gym space will be converted to house the
platform. The estimated cost for the upgrades to the building are $437,000. A separate but related budget amendment item will
appropriate the funding for the project; funding is anticipated to come from the use of retained earnings in the BDO operations
fund.

Comptroller Stout then presented a spreadsheet detailing the adjustments to revenue and appropriations included in the
FY22-23 budget if the current proposal is approved.

Council member Hyer stated that he has used the VirTra program and can speak to the value of the training and
experience that it provides to users. He is supportive of this proposed CIP amendment and is grateful that the Police Department is
putting the training tool to good use. Chief Young stated he appreciates the support of the Council, and he invited other members
to visit the Police Department to experience the training program. This led to discussion of the training scenarios that are offered
through VirTra.

Council member White asked if the City is currently leasing the facility where the VirTra is located from Weber County.
Chief Young answered no, the City has been allowed to use the Weber complex free of charge, but the ownership of that facility is
changing, and the City needs to find a new location for the training platform. Council member White then referenced involvement
of the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) and amendments to their budget associated with the Union Station; she noted that the Union
Station is not located within an RDA project area, and she asked Administration to address that issue. Ms. Stout stated that the
RDA has the ability to purchase land that is adjacent to land in an RDA area. The Union Station property is near the Kiesel RDA
area, and the plan is to ask the County and School District to participate with the City in creating a new RDA area to help fund
some of the improvements at the Union Station. The manner in which this budget amendment is structured will make it easier to
track tax increment funding (TIF) associated with the project.

Vice Chair Richey asked Ms. Stout to address the reduction of the general fund balance as part of this budget
amendment. Ms. Stout stated that the City Council set a goal years ago to get the fund balance to 10 percent. The fund balance is
considered to be the City’s ‘rainy day fund’ and the City’s fund is very healthy and can be used for this type of project.
