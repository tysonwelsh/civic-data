Proposed Ordinance 2023-54 amending the Municipal Code to revise use
and development standards for junk and salvage yards, rebuildable car lots,
recycling collection centers, auto repair shops, entertainment and fitness
centers, RV storage lots, personal storage, and design and development
standards for new development in the M-1 and M-2 zones

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed zone text amendment
regarding use and development standards in the M-1 and M-2 zones came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated
Administration is proposing several amendments to the allowable uses and development standards of certain uses in the M-1 and
M-2 zones. The amendments would do the following:

e Prohibit new junk and salvage yards, rebuildable car lots, recycling collection centers, and limit the area
vehicles could be stored in new auto repair shops.
, e Limit new entertainment and fitness facilities, except motor vehicle related uses.
e Allow manufactured home sales displays and RV storage lots with offices.
e Limit new personal storage facilities.
e Modify setbacks, height, and lot coverage standards.
e Establish design standards for new industrial buildings.
e Establish landscape buffer standards between industrial and residential uses.

The proposal focuses almost entirely on the M-1 (Limited Manufacturing) and M-2 (Manufacturing and Industrial) zones
and would not generally be applicable to the City’s other manufacturing zones, which include the MRD and DDR zonesatBDO,
the OCIP zone in west Ogden, or the BEI and BEH zones within the Trackline Economic Development Project Area in west
Ogden. Administration posted a notice of pending ordinance on April 20, 2023, which limited the new applications for most
permitted and conditional uses in the M-1 and M-2 zones, prohibited new junk and salvage yards in the M-2 zone and prohibited
new recycling and collection centers and towing and impound lots in the M-1 and M-2 zones. The pending ordinance restrictions
expire on October 17, 2023.

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
proposed text amendments; his first slide included a graph that charted the growth industrial land construction in Weber and Davis
Counties from 2010 to 2023, the net absorption of the industrial square footage, total vacancy rates, and lot term average vacancy
rates. The conclusion that can be drawn from this data is that vacancy rates have decreased, which is an indicator of aneed that is
not being met and more construction should be anticipated. He noted the Economic Development Corporation of Utah (EDCUtah)
works to attract projects to the State of Utah and most of the projects they see are manufacturing-type projects. Job growth in the
manufacturing sector has outpaced growth in other sectors by more than eight percent; Business Depot Ogden (BDO) is a prime
example of these trends; over the past four years, the acres available or development has decreased from nearly 400 to under 100.
The supply in Weber County is dwindling. He added that the average annual wages in manufacturing are $74,201, compared to
$54,154 in other sectors. The manufacturing industry has a significant impact on the State’s economy, including travel and
tourism, real estate, and services, all of which contribute to the gross domestic product (GDP). The manufacturing sector is also a
‘job multiplier’; each manufacturing job creates .92 jobs in other sectors. Key industries the City has focused on in the
manufacturing sector include aerospace and defense, outdoor products, life sciences, software and technology, and advanced
materials and manufacturing. He called out a random M-2 zoned property in the community to be considered as a case study; it is
2.8 acres and is very near another property for which a salvage yard has recently been proposed. He offered a comparison of the
benefits of a salvage yard versus a common manufacturing use. The annual property and sales tax generated by a salvage yard
would be $7,500 and $30,000, respectively. A yard on a property of this size would have approximately four to five employees.
However, a salvage yard use can result in a significant amount of environmental contamination. If the property were used as a
personal storage facility, the annual property and sales tax revenue would be $27,000 and $11,000, respectively and there would
be approximately three employees. He noted there are currently 15 personal storage facilities in Ogden, equating to 11 square feet
of storage space per capita, which is commensurate with the market rate of 10 to 13 square feet per capita. If the property were
used for a manufacturing purpose, the annual property tax would be $25,000, but the use would employ 50 employees directly and | i
46 indirectly and would have an impact on the GDP of $15 million. He noted the proposed ordinance would prohibit new junk and
salvage yards, used and rebuildable car lots. The basis for this prohibition is that the uses are associated with low employment,

Communications Plan for Proposed Union Station Campus Master Plan
Development Framework. Update and review the communications plan for
the proposed Union Station Campus Master Plan Development Framework
extended from the September 26, 2023, meeting

Council Deputy Director Symes explained that as a follow-up to the MAKE Ogden Plan and the downtown MAKE
Ogden zoning ordinance rewrite and rezones, the Administration is proposing the Council adopt the Ogden Union Station Campus
Master Plan Development Framework (Union Station Framework Plan) to help guide development of the Union Station campus.
As part of the MAKE Ogden downtown zoning ordinances adopted in 2023, language in the zoning code allowed more strict
zoning regulations for an area of the downtown if the area had an adopted master plan. The Union Station Framework Plan would
allow more localized and specific standards to be applied to development of the Union Station campus beyond the base standards
in the zoning ordinance. As per the Council’s Rules and Norms, the item was extended. An extension, unlike a tabling, does not
require a date certain for when the item will be brought back for consideration. The Council’s Rules and Norms do require that the
Council staff provide an update and present a communications plan for the item at the Council’s next regular meeting. Mr. Symes
noted later this week, Council staff will be meeting with Legal staff and Administration to discuss the timeline for bringing the
matter back before the Council. On Wednesday, November 15, Administration will be holding a Union Station Advisory
Committee meeting and public open house from 5:30 to 7:30 p.m. and concept plans will be presented to the public for feedback.
This feedback will be used to narrow down the concepts that will be presented to the Council in February of 2024.

Chair Choberka asked if the advisory committee meeting will be open to the public, to which Mr. Symes answered yes.

Framework Plan, but noted that nothing has changed; she has suggested meetings be held where the people are located, or during
times when more people can attend, but that is not being done and Administration continues to hold meetings on days and times
when it is difficult for most blue-collar workers to attend. She suggested that attendance in the meeting be connected to census
tract data to ensure that the entire City has a voice. A comprehensive study was conducted when the City was considering being
involved in the Community Renewable Energy Act (CREA) and she suggested that type of study for the Union Station matter as
well. Recommendations are only as good as the data used to inform them and it is necessary to represent the entire City, not just
one specific group.
