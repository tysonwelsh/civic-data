         4.1     Presenting Highlights of Mark Reid's 17-Year Career as City Manager.

Mayor Hall stated that City Manager, Mark Reid was in his 17^ year as Bluffdale City Manager.
It is not common for a city manager to stay in one city for so long. Mr. Reid came to Bluffdale
when the City transitioned its form of government and he was vital in that transition. Bluffdale's
form ofgovernment is a six-member council with legislative and administrative bodies. The City
Council adopts annual budgets and ordinances, creates long-range plans for City services and
utilities, establishes City fees, passes resolutions, regulates zoning and land development, and
oversees the provision of numerous other services. However, the Council relies on the City
Manager for day-to-day management.


Mr. Reid worked with three different mayors over his 17 years. Over that time,Bluffdale grew by
250% with a population increase from 7,900 in 2008 to almost 20,000 in 2024. Property values
soared by approximately 400%,and City assets grew by 1,635%. The City has a new fire station

Ms. Thayer reviewed changes to the Budget since it was last presented. She reported that the FY
2026 CTR of0.000866 was provided to the City the previous day. The City Council must adopt
a final or proposed property tax rate on or before June 26 each year. Because Bluffdale is in both
Salt Lake and Utah Counties, the rate is usually received late because it must be equalized across
both counties. City Staff has no involvement in that calculation as it is provided by the State
Auditor. State property tax law requires that when property values rise, property tax rates must
fall. The adjusted rate each year is called the Certified Tax Rate, and it maintains the same property
tax level as the previous year.


Ms. Thayer reviewed a sample Notice ofProperty Valuation and noted that many different taxing
entities are included on the notice. Bluffdale City property taxes are a separate line item and
account for approximately 10% oftotal property taxes paid. If compared to bills from other cities
in Salt Lake County,all other taxing entities would have the same rate, but individual city amounts
and public safety taxes will vary.


Bluffdale's historical CTR was also reviewed, which indicates that the rate has declined over the
past several years as property values increased. The City went through the Truth in Taxation
process to enact rate increases in 2017 and 2019 to cover public safety costs. Mr. Reid explained
that in 2017,the City increased its firefighters from four to eight. That required a 47% tax increase,
but it did not increase individual property taxes significantly because it only applied to Bluffdale's
portion of taxes. The 2019 increase was necessary to accommodate increased Fire Department
costs.

The Budget process:

      • The Tentative Budget that is presented to the City Council in April is referred to as the
           City Manager's Budget.
      • Staff works to refine the Tentative Budget until the Final Budget is ready in June.
      • State law requires that a Final Budget be passed by June 20 each year.
      • The Budget includes conservative revenue estimates and maximum expenditures.
      • Budget adjustments are a normal part ofthe Budget year.
      • A public hearing is held for each Budget and Budget Adjustment to provide the public
           with the opportunity to provide their feedback.
      • Once the Final Budget is passed, it becomes the City Council's Budget.
