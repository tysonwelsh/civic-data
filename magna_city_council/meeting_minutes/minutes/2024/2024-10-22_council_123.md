Mr. Sanderson provided a detailed quarterly financial report to the council. He highlighted the
robust sales tax revenues for Magna City, which continue to show strong performance.
Notably, Magna ranks in the top 10 cities for sales tax growth, demonstrating a positive
economic trajectory for the city. He presented figures confirming a solid financial standing on
the revenue side, with sales taxes performing very well. On the expense side, Mr. Sanderson
reported that there had been no significant changes beyond the usual categories such as

Communities that Care fund, informing the council that they had received a $25,000 State

component of the fund's financial inflow for the year. Overall, the report outlined a clear view of
Magna City's financial health across various funds, emphasizing strong sales tax growth and
steady management of other financial elements.

D. Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA) Proposed 2025 Tax
Increase — Rachel Anderson, District Administrator and Legal Counsel, and
Rick Moon, Treasurer
Rachel Anderson explained the need for a tax increase to reach the maximum allowed
property tax cap of 0.0023. She discussed the financial challenges facing SLVLESA and the
need to increase revenue to pay for law enforcement services. She elaborated that SLVLESA
was surprised to find they had not hit the tax cap as anticipated, due to property values
increasing more than expected, which lowered the effective percentage collected. This
situation presented an opportunity for SLVLESA to attempt reaching the tax cap again, thereby
allowing them to better cover their financial obligations. She pointed out that this step is critical
because the SLVLESA finances are getting very tight, and they need to secure enough
revenue to continue providing essential law enforcement services. She acknowledged last
year’s approval of a tax increase and explained that the strategy was to move closer to the tax
cap's maximum to stabilize SLVLESA's fiscal health. She underlined the importance of
reaching or approaching the maximum tax cap to adequately fund necessary services,
especially given the financial strains from having two separate service providers after Salt Lake
County Sheriff split from UPD.
Rick Moon presented financial details, explaining that without a tax increase, SLVLESA would
have a negative balance by the end of next year, with a projected shortfall of $800,000 and
falling $2,100,000 under the 5% balance minimum mandated by state statute. This financial
shortfall emphasizes the urgency of the situation and the necessity for a tax increase to
maintain compliance with statutory requirements and maintain essential services. He outlined
several options for phased tax increases over the next few years to ensure the organization
can cover its bills and sustain operations. The proposed tax increase would amount to
approximately $65 per year for the average residence valued at $453,000, translating to an
additional monthly cost of approximately $5.45.
Ms. Anderson explained the approval process, emphasizing the need for either unanimous
approval from member cities or a majority approval from the cities plus a two-thirds vote from
the Salt Lake County Council if unanimity isn't achieved. She highlighted that this process
involves stakeholder engagement and must be balanced with public transparency and
accountability, noting that parcel-specific notices have been sent out to inform residents about
the potential financial impact on their properties.
Council members discussed concerns about the notification process and coordination with
other emergency management agencies, emphasizing the need for comprehensive
communication strategies. Ms. Anderson agreed to provide written details of the proposed
increases to the council to facilitate an informed decision-making process, and committed to
presenting this information to the Local Emergency Planning Committee (LEPC) to ensure
alignment and cooperative efforts in addressing community safety and fiscal sustainability
concerns.

C. Global Industry Standard for Tailing Management and RTK Tailing Storage Facilities

- Laura Ingersoll, Senior Advisor/Community and Social Performance and
Paula Doughty, Tailings and Hydrology Manager
Representatives from Rio Tinto Kennecott presented information on their tailings management
practices and emergency response plans during the city meeting. Paula Daugherty, the
manager of tailings and water services, and Laura Ingersoll, a senior advisor, briefed the
council on the current status and management strategies concerning the tailings storage
facilities. Rio Tinto Kennecott has implemented a global industry standard on tailings
management in adherence to the requirements set forth by the International Council of Mining
and Metals, of which they are a member, ensuring tailings storage is managed safely and
efficiently. During their presentation, the representatives explained the extensive geotechnical
monitoring in place at their facilities that includes over 500 piezometers, inclinometers,
accelerometers, and satellite radar imaging used to regularly assess and convey the stability of
the impoundments. They highlighted how the seismic risk assessment and past earthquake
experiences have informed their planning and response actions. Concerns addressed included
the potential impact on nearby roads, specifically regarding a hypothetical seismic event that
could affect parts of State Route 201. This informed the council about possible runout
scenarios that would solely impact 201’s westbound lane without reaching residential
structures.
