Pre-Council, 4:00 p.m.
1. Welcome and Invocation
   Mayor Johnson welcomed everyone and noted that all Councilmembers were present.
   Councilor Southwick gave the invocation.

2. Presentations and Reports
2.1 Update on Willow Park
    Steve Marchbanks, Parks Manager, stated that they will begin taking reservations soon and
    believes the transition from Utah County is going well. He showed the concept plans for
    Willow Park and noted that they will have designated camping and large group areas with
    plans for improvements. He said they are going to develop a nice dog park as well. They hope
    to better utilize most of the space by removing some trees and planting new trees and regrowing
    some vegetation. They will have a canoe boat launch and create more fishing options. There
    was discussion regarding the details about their plans for the dog park area and how they will
    increase the park’s maintenance facilities. Mr. Marchbanks stated that they’ll construct the
    improvements in phases as they have the materials and the budget to do so. The irrigation
    system will be the most expensive to install, but there are grants available to assist with funding
    the park.

2.2 Update on the PEC Contract for Parks Design
    Steve Marchbanks presented on the current Family Park concept plan. He said that the most
    recent design is not final, but was just a beginning point for the contractor. He said that the
    City will design what they feel is appropriate, involve the Parks, Trails and Trees Committee,
    and then final approval will be given by the Council. He said the concept that was submitted
    as part of the RFP processes was inaccurate and needs to be changed.

    baseball and other park amenities which are still to be determined. He said it will be constructed
    in phases and the designer will determine how to best phase the project. Parks impact fees can

    selection for the baseball facilities.

2.3 Discussion of the Baum Advertising Contract
    Mayor Johnson stated that they want to discuss this again at a future meeting. There was
    concern with naming rights of parks and baseball fields regarding the contract. He said they
    may need to reevaluate the contract because of some legal challenges.


3. Agenda Questions
   There was discussion about when they may resume in-person meetings for City Council. Mr.
   Walker stated that the Governor’s order that allows for remote meetings expires on June 30th.


4. Administrative Report

    and they are going to educate the public on the risks of wildland fires in Lehi. He stated that
    they will contact residents in Traverse Mountain and provide education on the risks and how

6. Consideration of Resolution #2020-40, approval of a street name change for the southern
   portion of Holbrook Way to Miller Campus Drive.
   Dan Kohler was representing the applicant. He stated that the change would be to recognize
   the significant contributions the Miller family made to Intermountain Healthcare and the
   community.

    Councilor Koivisto inquired if the residents were notified of the change to the road name. Mr.
    Kohler stated that they are the property owner and will be the first to develop along this road.
    He said they discussed the name change with the Holbrook family and there was no objection
    there.

    Motion:     Councilor Koivisto moved to approve Resolution #2020-40, a street name change
                for the southern portion of Holbrook Way to Miller Campus Drive; with the
                condition that the applicant reach out to the residents in Holbrook Farms and
                publish the road name change; subject to the completion of all Development
                Review Committee and Planning Commission comments. Councilor Hancock
                seconded the motion.

    Councilor Condie suggested that the word “condition” in the motion be changed to
    “recommend” because a condition may imply that the residents can approve or deny the name
    change. Councilor Hancock agreed.

    Councilor Koivisto stated that she expects the applicant to reach out to the residents and would
    prefer to keep the word “condition”. She stated that there have been a lot of changes in the area
    and believes the notice should come from Intermountain Healthcare.

    There was discussion about how and when to notify the residents. Councilor Hancock
    questioned the necessity of notifying residents because no one lives on that road so residents
    will not be impacted by the change.

    Councilor Hancock rescinded his second of the motion. Mayor Johnson asked for a second on
    the motion.

    The motion failed for lack of a second.

    Motion:     Councilor Hancock moved to approve Resolution #2020-40, a street name change
                for the southern portion of Holbrook Way to Miller Campus Drive; with the
                recommendation that the applicant ensure there is notice to residents through their
                standard public relations process; subject to the completion of all Development
                Review Committee and Planning Commission comments. Councilor Condie
                seconded the motion.

9. Consideration of Ordinance #34-2020, a Development Code Amendment to Chapters 12
   and 26, adding requirements for storage containers on residential property.
   There was a comment submitted online by Steve Moulton: I appreciate the Planning
   Commission and City Council finding a solution to this amendment. I’d like to propose that
   the additional recommendation from the Planning Commission to limit “container shed
   conversions” to larger lots be removed as there is already adequate code specifying setbacks,
   open yard space, etc. for accessory buildings.

    Using my own situation as an example, I live on a 1/4 acre lot, but have a small home and
    large backyard. My lot is large enough to meet current accessory building codes for the
    “container shed conversion” I have in mind. However, under the proposed lot size change I
    would not qualify to do so. Current city code would, however, allow me to install a larger,
    taller, wooden, Home Depot shed instead.

    Some of the reasons I prefer to convert a container into a shed are: o A container retains the
    majority of its value for many years. Home Depot sheds lose the majority of their value the day
    they are installed. o The “container conversion shed” I have in mind will require much less
    up keep and will look good for many years to come. Much longer than a Home Depot shed. o

    A “container conversion shed” is more secure than your average wooden Home Depot shed.
    o The “container conversion shed” I have in mind will actually cost less money than a wooden
    Home Depot shed of similar size.

    In summary, I feel the original amendment proposal, which includes an application process to
    ensure existing accessory building codes are met, is sufficient written as is.

    Thank you for considering this proposal.

    Councilor Condie was inclined to agree with the public comment, so he asked about the
    concerns of the Planning Commission. Mr. Struthers stated that the discussion was that these
    would be large shipping containers that may not fit well on smaller lots. They felt it may cause
    more problems then what we would want to allow, so that’s why they only wanted them on
    large lots. He said there is a maximum cover requirement in any zone that you can’t cover
    more then 30% of your backyard with buildings or accessory structures, so it may not be an
    issue.

    Councilor Hancock suggested that they allow a maximum container size based on lot size.
    Councilor Condie researched the containers and found that there are various sizes and that
    there are options to have them look nice. He said the standard sizes seem be to 20, 40, and 53
    feet in length, and they are all 8 feet wide.

    Motion:     Councilor Hancock moved to approve Ordinance #34-2020, a Development Code
                Amendment to Chapters 12 and 26, adding requirements for storage containers on
                residential property; and include that 20 foot containers be allowed on ¼ acre lots,
                and that the traditional size or larger containers be allowed based of the proposed
                amendment. Councilor Southwick seconded the motion.

    Mr. Struthers asked that the Council specify by zone or a specific .25 acre lot. There was
    discussion about which zones this would include.

    Amended Motion: Council Hancock amended his motion to include a minimum 10,000
            square foot lot size for the containers. Councilor Southwick seconded the
            amendment to the motion.

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Yes; Councilor Southwick, Yes;
    Councilor Hancock, Yes; and Councilor Koivisto, Yes. The motion passed unanimously.
