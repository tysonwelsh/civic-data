     2.1. Discussion relating to a proposed vacation of open space for the Herriman Cove Phase 3 Trail
          - Blake Thomas, Community Development Director
Community Development Director Blake Thomas informed the Council of the Planning Commission
requirement to have a minimum of three trail access points were required as part of the Fort Herriman Cove
Subdivision. He proposed a vacation to eliminate one of the locations intended for the Bonneville Shoreline
trail access for the subdivision.

He observed the topography and the impacts on the property owners along with other trail access options and
Recommended the requirements for the trail access between lot 310 and 311 be revisited. It was suggested to
relocate the trail access to another location or eliminate this trail access since there are three other access areas
already in existence. Director Thomas requested to avoid placing trails along narrow corridors between
residential lots as vandalism tends to happen. He requested direction from the City Council as to which avenue
they would like to have staff pursue. Councilmember Shields observed the subdivision map and questioned
how access point “D” connected to a trail system. Direction Thomas responded that initially 14300 South had
been a dirt road at the time of construction which essentially satisfied the requirement of a trail access at the
time.

Councilmember Smith asked what the Parks Department would recommend. Assistant City Manager Wendy
Thomas highlighted the grade associated with the specific access and recommended either vacating the trail
access or moving it to the opposite side of lot 311.

Mayor Watts opined to vacate the trail access as there was no value to having the trail connection in the area.
Councilmember Shields and Smith agreed.

    2.2. 7300 West Project Update - Jonathan Bowers, City Engineer
City Engineer Jonathan Bowers presented a timeline of the 7300 West project. Initially, the project was
conceptualized as a storm drain project and was put on hold to allow for gas and communication utility
relocations. Through the budget process, the project was proposed as a roadway and utility project. Currently,
the design and bidding process is complete and is awaiting a contract to be executed with the anticipated
completion of fall of 2020. There are four properties along the roadway that would require acquisition in order
to complete portions of sidewalk, curb and gutter.

The first property would need to have 1,467 square feet acquired to allow for the sidewalk, curb and gutter
improvements. Staff visited the resident on February 21, 2020 to which the property owner indicated they
would consider the proposal. Since then, they have reached out to an attorney to represent them through the
acquisition process. The City has been awaiting to hear from either the property owner or their representative.
If the City installed the curb and gutter without the property acquisition, the curb and gutter could be installed;
however, a potential flooding risk would be evident and would result in minor additional runoff into their
driveway. Currently, the current condition included the flooding risk as depicted in the picture presented to
the Council. City Engineer Bowers reported the runoff risk could be mitigated with the installation of a drive
approach, but it would require either acquisition or consent.
April 8, 2020 – City Council Minutes                                                Page 3 of 14

The second property acquisition would require 1,801 square feet for the sidewalk, curb and gutter
improvements. Staff visited the resident on February 25, 2020 and followed up with a phone call on March 31,
2020. The property owner asked for more time to consider the request and relayed their pleasure of the overall
roadway project. Curb and gutter could be installed without acquisition or additional runoff risk.

The third property would require an acquisition of 827 square feet for a sidewalk to be installed. The property
owner indicated they would not like to have the sidewalk improvements installed and were not amenable to
dedicate property at this time; however, were positive about the overall project. City Engineer Bowers notated
curb and gutter could be installed without property acquisition or additional runoff risk.

The fourth property that would need right-of-way acquisition would require a total of 2,609 square feet to
install sidewalk, curb, gutter and utility stub improvements. Staff has been unable to contact this property
owner despite several attempts. City Engineer Bowers relayed an alternative to proceed could include the
installation of asphalt to the right-of-way or move the boundary of the project to the north side of this specific
property.

    2.4. Presentation of the 2020-2021 Herriman City Budget - Alan Rae, Finance Director
Finance Director Alan Rae offered a synopsis of the proposed 2020-2021 Herriman City Budget. He started
with the majority of proposed new hires are only budgeted for six months. These potential new employees
would not be anticipated prior to January 1, 2021. Many of the expenditures on the proposed budget are being
delayed. All in-state travel and training would be disallowed through the 2020 calendar year and any out of
state training would not be allowed for a full calendar year.

Several vehicles had been anticipated in this proposed fiscal year; however, no vehicles would be purchased
prior to January 1, 2021. The City has anticipated leasing vehicles, all of the purchase price and lease revenue
would be recorded in this year and would not change based on the time of year they would be purchased.
Executive Staff would review the economy as of January 1, 2021 to see if the vehicles should be acquired.

Many capital projects had been included in the proposed budget; however, none of the projects would be
allowed to commence without first being reviewed by the Budget Committee.
