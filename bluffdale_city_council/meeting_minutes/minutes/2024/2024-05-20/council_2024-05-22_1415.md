       4,2     Presentation and Discussion Regarding the SLCo Animal Services 2023 Annual
               Report for the City of Bluffdale. (Presenter Carrie Sibert, Community Liaison
               Coordinator.SLCO AnimalServices^


Carrie Sibert, Community Liaison Coordinator, presented the SLCo ("SLCo")Animal Services'
Annual Report and reported that 2023 was their 10^'' year as a no-kill shelter. To celebrate, they
awarded 10 organizations and individuals at their "Spayghetti" Gala. They also launched the Hang
Ten Shelter Club with the motto "Riding the wave of no-kill for a decade" to challenge other shelters
around the nation to join them in the no-kill philosophy. They mentor other municipalities to become
no-kill.


In the previous year. Animal Control Officers responded to over 18,000 calls for service. The team
offosters and volunteers provided over 43,000 hours helping animals in need either in their homes or
at the shelter. Over 2,600 pets were adopted or sent to rescue organizations, and 3,500 pets were
sterilized at the in-house clinic.


In 2023, SLCo received two National Association of Counties Achievement Awards. One was for
the Utah Friends for Animal Control and Effective Solutions("FACES")Endowment. FACES was a
501(c)(3) non-profit that supported them in maintaining their no-kill status by funding many oftheir
programs. When FACES was dismantled, they left an endowment to help continue funding those
programs. The second award is for the Advisory Committee. Every community they serve has a seat
on the committee and has a voice in how they operate and the programs they run. They also won Best
in State for the 15th time in 2023,and they were just informed that they won for 2024 as well.

Ms. Sibert gave an overview ofthe 2023 Asilomar statistics for all the jurisdictions they serve:

    • They brought in over 6,200 animals.
    • They were able to find homes for over 2,200 animals.

       4.3     Brmghurst Station Special District Expansion - Phase 4. (Presenter, Brandon
               Watson),


Assistant City Planner, Ellen Oakman, introduced Brandon Watson of EDGEhomes. Mr. Watson
stated that they are in the process of deciding on a name for the development and are currently
referring to it as Bringhurst 2.0. It is an approximately 12-acre property southeast of Bringhurst
Station between Redwood Road and the Jordan River Railroad. The project is near the Jordan River
Trail. The developer wants to preserve and enhance the trail as much as possible,and they are working
closely with Bluffdale and SLCo on the best options for doing so.

Access to the development will be from Bringhurst Boulevard and will require crossing both the
Jordan River Trail and the Utah Lake Distribution Canal. It will require culverts and bridges, and
they will need to find a way to keep the trail open during construction. To the south ofthe project is
a County-owned parcel that would be an ideal location for a trailhead or small park, and they are
working with Staff and the county to determine if that is possible. Mr. Watson indicated that the
project is in an interesting geographic location due to the current and planned roadways in the area.

The developer has met with the South Valley Sewer District Board and discussed the need for a
regional sewer lift station. They have also been in communication with the Apostolic United Brethren
to discuss necessary easements and permission for the lift station.


EDGEhomes is proposing 1,600 square-foot attached homes with approximately 700-square-foot
unfinished basements. They have been working with Staff to determine the best way to incorporate
open space, either onsite or by reaching an agreement with SLCo for a regional park. There is a large
grove ofscrub oak trees on the parcel that they would like to preserve so they are considering installing
hammock parks, which are more passive than traditional open space.

In response to a question fi*om Council Member Crockett, Mr. Watson stated that their proposal is for
eight to nine units per acre. He expects no more than 100 homes.

Ms. Oakman provided context on the legislative changes that would be necessary. In addition to
amending the Special District, the development would require both a zoning change and a General
Plan Amendment. The current zoning is A5 Agricultural, and the proposed zoning would be SD-X
Bringhurst Station. The current Land Use Designation is Very Low Density Residential, and the
proposed Designation is Mixed Use. In response to a question from Mayor Hall, Ms. Oakman
confirmed that the project has not gone before the Planning Commission. The first step in amending
the Special District is to present it to the City Council, which is done prior to application.

Council Member Wilding asked for clarification on the proposed lift station location. Mr. Watson
stated that the South Valley Sewer District's Master Plan does not specify a location. Alignment and
location will be discussed and fine-tuned later in the design process. Existing homes in the area are
on septic systems, so this would allow them to connect to the sewer system. With Staffs assistance,
they met onsite with SLCo to discuss the county's willingness to let them make use ofthe adjoining
parcel. He understands there are other parcels owned by Bluffdale City that could potentially be
traded for that parcel, but he did not know the specifics.

Community and Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, added that they are in the process
of determining ifit is possible to secure the parcel. The parcel in question is too small for the county

Mr. Crowell presented the Staff Report and stated that the amendment covers changes to Title 11 and
Title 12, which represent the majority of Bluffdale's Land Use Ordinances. They contain various
sections that address the design standards for developing accessways to subdivisions. In 2015, the
City adopted the current provisions for developing off of preexisting private rights-of-way. At that
time,two lots in a subdivision could be accessed via a shared 25-foot driveway. In 2023,that number
was increased to three lots. After three, a standard City street is currently required. In most cases,
that is 52 feet wide with curb, gutter, and sidewalk on both sides. The same standard applies to both
public and private streets. In 2015, Staff did site visits in Holladay, which has a lot of developed
property off long, private rights-of-way. Bluffdale has similar characteristics in its land use pattern.
In 2022, the City Council held a work session on private rights-of-way. In 2023, the number oflots
that can share a driveway was increased from two to three. Mr. Crowell presented a working draft of
the new Ordinance to both the City Council and Planning Commission earlier in 2024.

Existing private rights-of-way include paved, gravel, and dirt roads. Other types of private rights-of-
way are used in commercial developments. Mr. Crowell gave an example of a private right-of-way
in the Highlands at Bluffdale subdivision at Redwood Road and 14200 South. It has a narrow, 20-

foot road. The current Ordinance would require it to be 52 feet wide, which is not practical for many
four-lot subdivisions.


The amendment will create the following tiers:

   • One to three lots: 25-foot shared driveway. It does not have to be paved.
   • Four to six lots: 26-foot paved road. Drainage is required, but sidewalks are not.
   • Seven to ten lots: 35-foot paved road. A sidewalk is required on one side.
   • Eleven or more lots: 52-foot paved road with curb, gutter, and sidewalk. The road can be
        either private or public.
   • A pre-existing private right-of-way can be used for a new subdivision as long as it meets
       Intemational Fire Code standards, which specifies access, water supply, and turnaround
       requirements.


The amended Ordinance will create opportunities for new subdivisions in areas where a full 52-foot
street is not practical or affordable.

Key points ofthe proposal include:

   • The current 30-lot limit for private rights-of-way will not change.
   • Rights-of-way are not included in lot sizes.
   • Terms and references have been updated where appropriate.
   • A new Streets Standards illustration has been created.

   • Full residential street standards will no longer apply to subdivisions with four to ten lots if
        using a private right-of-way.
