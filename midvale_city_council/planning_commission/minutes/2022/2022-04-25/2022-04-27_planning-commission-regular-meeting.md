GENERAL SESSION

Chair Snow called the Planning & Zoning Commission meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. He
explained how the meeting would proceed. First, the Planning Department would brief the
Commission; then the applicant would speak to the Commission; the item will be opened to the
public for their comments on public hearing agenda items; the Midvale Deputy City Attorney
will then brief the Commission on their role on the given item; and then the Commission would
deliberate and decide.

     2. Michael Edwards requests an amendment to Section 17-7-1.4 of the Midvale Municipal Code
        that proposes to decrease the rear setback from 25 feet to 15 feet.


Mr. Knobloch stated the applicant proposes to decrease the rear setback in the Single Family
Residential (SF-1) zone from 25 to 15 feet and introduces a well-reasoned argument regarding
why this reduction may be desirable.

Mr. Knobloch stated that the Planning Department does recommend approval of motion. The
setback request is not completely new to Midvale being that the SF-2 zone already allows it. The
planners do not see an issue with the requested amendment.

APPLICANT:

Mr. Edwards stated that he is wanting to cover his back patio and attach it to his residence.
Comparing this set up to his neighbors’, he stated that he doesn’t see it being an issue for
easements or utilities. Mr. Edwards noted that he has a sketch for reference to show the
Commissioners upon request. Mr. Edwards stated that he has completed extensive research on
other City’s zoning requirements and the effects on residents and Midvale does already have
similar setback requirements in the SF-2 zones for larger lots.


Mr. Edwards asked the Commission had any questions.

Vice Chair Anderson voiced her concern with the neighbors taking issue with the patio cover and
setbacks. She added that making the amendment to the Code would not only be a special
exception for Mr. Edwards but would open the door to other issues regarding zoning in SF-1
zones.

Mr. Edwards stated that he understands the possible repercussions and generally hopes that it is
not affecting the neighborhood too much.

Vice Chair Anderson referred to past issues regarding the setbacks and the stir that was caused in
the neighborhood. The consideration will not specifically refer to patio covers but other additions
of all types regarding the zoning ordinance. She stated that everyone needs to be protected by the
ordinances.

Chair Snow mentioned the difference of 2,000 square feet between the SF-1 and SF-2 zones. He
added that the proposed amendment will make a larger impact than just allowing patio covers.

Mr. Edwards stated that he was aware that he can still build a cover on in his backyard with an
air gap, but that is undesirable.

Commissioner Liedtke stated that Mr. Edwards is following the proper channels to build his
desired addition. However, there may be more ramifications compared to community benefits.

DISCUSSION:

Mr. Wilcox stated that this is a legislative item that will be proposed to the City Council. It is not
an obligation to vote for or against but a recommendation of what the Planning Commission
feels is best for the community.

Commissioner Liedtke stated that there should be options available to residents to build out in
the future. He added that this would allow residents to continue living here in Midvale with the
ability to provide upgrades or expansions and be satisfied with their homes.

Mr. Olsen stated that there is no separate zoning for cul-de-sacs or corner lots. Typically, a new
build can have setbacks discussed and possibly altered.

Chair Snow and Commissioner Liedtke ask Mr. Wilcox about the requirements for requesting a
variance.

Mr. Wilcox states that a variance requires a hardship that would not allow a resident to live
comfortably. It would have to show that the setbacks make a resident’s lot completely
undevelopable. If a property owner cannot meet requirements for new zoning ordinances, then a
variance can be requested.

  DISCUSSION:

          1. Discussion regarding Planning Commission bylaws and current practice of the Planning
             Commission Chair voting/not voting.

  Mr. Olsen stated that the Commission has not been allowing the Chairman to vote. In the
  Municipal Code it is stated that each member shall vote. In 4-4. B it defines a quorum as having
  three members of the Planning Commission present. Mr. Olsen stated that it would be helpful for
  the Chair to vote in these instances. In moving into the bylaws, he added that a roll call vote will
  be held on all actions by the Commission except closing public hearings, recessing a meeting,
  approval of meeting minutes, and adjourning a meeting. It is not stated that the Chairman is not
  to vote. These findings stated that the Chair can in fact vote. In discussion with the Midvale City
  legal team, it is an allowed action.

  Commissioner Liedtke stated that in the past it was more of an issue of a smaller number of
  commissioners not an issue of the Chair voting or not.

  Vice Chair Anderson stated that she is in favor of the Chair voting.

  Mr. Olsen stated that the Code does not allow the Chair to start or second a motion, but the
  Chairman may vote. He added that no vote is necessary due to make this decision as the Code
  already allows the action.

  Mr. Wilcox agreed with Mr. Olsen’s earlier statement regarding a quorum stating that a
  minimum requirement of three votes in one direction must occur or a motion will fail.

PUBLIC HEARINGS

   1. Darren Nate, representing Ryker Development, LLC requests a partial right-of-way vacation,
      Preliminary Subdivision, and Conditional Use approval for a project located at approximately
      100 W. Center, in the TOD (Transit Oriented Development) zone, to allow for a 26-unit
      townhome project.


Mr. Olsen stated that the commission will consider three applications: right-of-way vacation,
preliminary subdivision plan, and conditional use permit. The preliminary subdivision and
conditional use portions below will review the project as if the right-of-way vacation is granted.
The concept plan for the project illustrates a 26-unit townhome project. Included with the staff
report are renderings and landscape plans for the project.

He added that the right of way vacation along Center Street totals 0.09 acres. Reasoning is to
vacate part of old parking lot for developer to follow new TOD guidelines more closely. The
current right of way does not allow compliance for the build for proximity to curbs. Applicant
has gone through earlier items required for request with no issues.

Mr. Olsen stated that the preliminary subdivision plan has been completed and all criteria have
been discussed.

He added that the Conditional Use Permit (CUP) is a requirement due to size of project. This
project is largely similar to another Townhome development to the South of this location. The
Planning & Zoning Depart feels that the project is in alignment with the General Plan. The
development is near major transit and transportation opportunity areas which is desirable for City
growth and urbanization.

Mr. Olsen stated that no comments have been sent opposing the project thus far.

There were no questions from the Commission.
