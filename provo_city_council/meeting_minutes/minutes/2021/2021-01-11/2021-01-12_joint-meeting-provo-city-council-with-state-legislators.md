1.       A discussion regarding upcoming state and local issues. (21-009) (0:13:05)

Council Chair David Sewell invited each legislator to spend several minutes introducing topics
of interest to them.

Representative Norm Thurston was following discussions about affordable housing with interest.
He was aware that the legislature was considering some blanket restrictions or requirements, and
he wanted to ensure that cities had appropriate resources to enforce such provisions. Senator Curt
Bramble expressed concerns about the state’s overinvolvement in land use planning. He felt the
state did have a vested interest in economic development in the state.

Councilor Shannon Ellsworth shared her concerns about the draft bill which would limit cities’
ability to regulate accessory dwelling units (ADUs) within their own jurisdictions. She felt that
ADUs were a phenomenal tool for affordable housing, but that Provo needed more flexibility
than the bill would afford. Provo was already home to a significant amount of the County’s
affordable housing and she was concerned about some of the problematic elements of this draft.
Councilor David Harding spoke to ADUs and also commented on a desire to revisit the sales tax
formula; he thought shifting toward population would help to encourage housing growth.

Senator Bramble responded to Mr. Harding’s comment on the sales tax formula. He noted that
with the exponential growth of online sales, the legislature issued a resolution in recent years
asking for an analysis from the Utah League of Cities and Towns regarding sales tax shifts.

Representative Marsha Judkins asked about transportation planning and development on Provo’s
west side. Wayne Parker, CAO, shared background information in response to Rep. Judkin’s
question. Councilor George Handley commented on the review process for the Transportation
Master Plan and suggested that identifying stronger guiding principles in the master plan around
the city and community’s goals would be helpful in relieving tensions.

(0:36:50) Senator Mike McKell highlighted several issues of importance or relevance to the city
of Provo, including Rep. Brady Brammer’s bill to create a service district for Utah Lake,
doubletracking Frontrunner, the inland port, strategic opportunities for areas such as Ironton
(with the introduction of the inland port and the location of several rail spurs in Provo), and
opportunity zones. Several Councilors emphasized the importance of double-tracking
Frontrunner as a priority for communities on the Wasatch Front. Several legislators shared
thoughts on double-tracking and the possibility of allocating some one-time funds toward the
initiative. Ms. Ellsworth and Mr. Handley also highlighted ecological protection of Utah Lake as
an important issue. Councilor Bill Fillmore asked about requiring Tier 3 gasoline. Senator
Bramble commented on diesel testing effectiveness measures.

Representative Keven Stratton thanked the Council for the opportunity to meet and hoped more
such meetings would happen in the future. He highlighted several issues, including how to
incentivize and promote Tier 3 gasoline, Utah Lake algal bloom, and conservation of Bridal Veil
Falls. He also chairs the Water, Federalism, and Natural Resources Committees and would be
focused on issues relating to those committees. Mr. Handley appreciated Rep. Stratton and Sen.
Bramble’s support on the conservation of Bridal Veil Falls and he thought there was broad
community support to designate Bridal Veil Falls as a state monument. Rep. Stratton noted that a
resolution of support from the Council regarding such a designation of Bridal Veil Falls would
be helpful; garnering local support would help strengthen the proposal.

(1:14:16) Representative Adam Robertson noted that he and many constituents were interested in
having a north-south connection through the area near 1450 East. Rep. Robertson also expressed
concern about reports from some constituents about the city’s regulatory environment and that
the City seemed to be overly focused on green issues.

Councilors pushed back on this assertion, highlighting the importance of sustainability as a prime
consideration in planning for Provo’s future. Mr. Fillmore highlighted the Council’s recent
efforts with foothills preservation and the Parks and Recreation Department’s plans for the
regional sports park. Mr. Fillmore also noted recent advances for significant public safety needs
in the City. Mr. Harding said the Council looks to govern in the ways that best support quality of
life for Provo residents. He noted that the Council was committed to sustainability because they
did not wish to make choices today at the expense of the future. Councilor David Shipley invited
any Provo residents with such concerns to contact the Council directly. He did not sense that the
Council would prioritize ‘green issues’ over basic needs in the City and he was hyper-sensitive
to the financial expenditures of the City and how funds are prioritized. He welcomed a visit with
