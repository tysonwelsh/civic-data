36          Budget Study Session on Operational Budget and Debt
37
38   Nick Geer, Administrative Services Director, introduced a discussion of the City’s debt and
39   operational budget, outlining existing bond obligations, vehicle leases, and other reportable debt
40   items before explaining that departments build operational budgets from the ground up each year
41   and highlighting structural changes such as moving animal control to police operations, shifting
42   street lighting costs to road funding, reorganizing debt funds, and directly charging internal utility
43   costs rather than using accounting offsets.
44
45   Mr. Geer clarified that the City previously adopted the “non-reciprocal” accounting method for
46   internal utility but now recommends directly charging City departments for water and sanitation
47   services to simplify reporting, eliminate required public notices and hearings, and better reflect
48   actual usage. He noted that this approach is common among other cities, aligns with accounting
49   standards, and will improve transparency and accountability as the City continues reviewing
50   operational budgets, including culinary water costs and projected increases such as the anticipated
51   rise in Jordan Valley Water purchases.

 1   Kevin Hicks, City Manager, discussed the implications of House Bill 501, explaining that earlier
 2   versions of the bill would have required cities to set water rates at a level tied to 1.5% of local
 3   median income, which initially suggested Riverton’s total water revenue would need to increase
 4   dramatically. After revisions, the bill no longer mandates those rates but instead makes that
 5   threshold a requirement for cities that want to qualify for state funding for water infrastructure
 6   projects. Based on updated calculations using per-capita income, Riverton would still need to
 7   significantly increase its water revenue, potentially approaching about $15 million annually
 8   compared to the current roughly $7 million, to qualify for such funding by the 2031 timeframe. This
 9   could affect future councils’ ability to access state assistance unless the City focuses on long-term
10   rate planning.
11
12   Mr. Geer reviewed several budget areas and proposed adjustments across multiple areas of the
13   general fund, including council expenses, city management, utilities, public works, recreation, fire,
14   code enforcement and parks. Increases were noted for necessary litigation, insurance, utility, and IT
15   expenses, while some areas saw minor decreases due to reduced activity or reallocation.
16
17   Mr. Geer discussed the City’s newsletter and recreation calendar distribution methods.
18   Councilmembers expressed the importance of maintaining physical copies for residents who do not
19   use digital platforms, particularly older residents. Council also requested a cost breakdown of
20   printing versus postage for the next meeting to determine a recommended approach.

33            Ordinance No. 26-03 – “Quinn Rezone,” General Plan Amendment and Rezone of
34            approximately 0.97-acres located at 1980 West 13400 South. Changing the General
35            Plan Designation to Medium Density Residential and the zoning to R-4, a single-
36            family residential zone with up to 4 dwelling units per acre.
37
38   Mr. Lethbridge explained that the proposed rezone concerns the property at 1980 West, 13400
39   South, currently under 1 acre and containing an existing home and outbuildings. The parcel is
40   uniquely split between R3 and R4 zoning, a remnant of prior actions when multiple lots were
41   combined, resulting in a mix that doesn’t practically allow additional subdivision. The surrounding
42   area includes a variety of zoning types, including quarter-acre lots created prior to City
43   regulations. The applicant requests R4 zoning across the entire parcel to align with the southern
44   portion and provide consistency along the block. However, the Planning Commission reviewed the
45   request and recommended denial, noting the unique circumstances and limited practical effect of
46   the existing R4 portion. Options presented to the Council include extending R4 for consistency or
47   leaving the rear as R3, with staff providing sample motion language for either approach.
48
49   Jeff Quinn, Applicant, explained that the intent was to subdivide the property into quarter-acre lots,
50   retain the existing home on the front portion, and allow their son, Dallas, to build on the rear lot
51   accessed via a private lane. He clarified that only one additional home was planned, that one

39   Mr. Lethbridge addressed a question regarding noticing, clarifying that all notices are generated
40   through the Salt Lake County recorder’s office system. He explained that the property in question
41   is listed as a protected, private lot in the County records, which prevents an address from being
42   produced for mailing. He emphasized that this designation is beyond the City’s control, and while
43   the City has a legal obligation to provide notice, in this situation the County-generated mailing list
44   could not deliver a notice to that property.
