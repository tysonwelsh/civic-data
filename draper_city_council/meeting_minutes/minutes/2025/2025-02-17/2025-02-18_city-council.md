Study Session

      Report: Fuels Mitigation
      Fire Chief Clint Smith and Parker Hansen provided updates on fuels mitigation
      efforts, participation in the State’s Community Wildfire Protection Program,
      grant funding sources, FY2025 deployments, and specific bills currently under
      consideration by the State Legislature. They spoke of equipment that would
      improve efficiency in the Fire Department, emphasized the desire to be
      proactive, and answered questions from the Council.

      Discussion: Boundary Adjustment with Highland City
      City Manager Mike Barker explained a request from Highland City to adjust the
      common boundary in the southeast corner of Draper. He said the next step
      would be to pass a resolution of intent. Councilmember T. Lowery said she
      understood the request, and expressed that Highland City had never
      contributed to trail maintenance, yet Highland residents wanted trail access.
      She said it would be nice if neighboring cities partnered with Draper City in
      making the trail systems successful, and emphasized that Draper was bearing
      the entire financial burden. Councilmember F. Lowry said he was not
      interested in going forward with a boundary adjustment unless Highland City
      came to the table with a plan for helping contribute. A majority of the Council
      indicated agreement.

6.a   Public Hearing: Ordinance #1634 approving a one-time incentive payment to
      the Mayor in recognition for his outstanding service and extensive
      involvement on various boards and commissions during the 2024-2025 period

      Mayor Walker opened a public hearing, and closed the public hearing seeing
      no one come forward.

      City Manager Mike Barker said the Council would need to decide on an amount
      for the one-time incentive payment if they decided to approve the ordinance.
      Mr. Barker spoke of Mayor Walker’s time commitment and contribution on
      various boards and commissions, giving voice to Draper City businesses and
      residents. He said the proposed ordinance, if approved, would recognize the
      Mayor’s time spent making sure the interests of Draper were well protected.

      Councilmember T. Lowery asked how the situation was handled in other cities.
      Mr. Barker said staff put together a spreadsheet of mayoral salaries around
      the State, including incentive programs and additional compensations. Mr.
      Barker said he believed the proposed one-time incentive payment to be well
      in line with what other cities did to compensate their mayors for participation
      with external boards and commissions. Mayor Walker emphasized the
      incentive payment was not his idea, but said the recognition of his time spent
      was appreciated.

      Referring to the Mayor’s work with the Utah League of Cities and Towns (ULCT)
      and other external groups, Councilmember T. Lowery said she believed the
      Council could not underestimate the power of having Mayor Walker’s voice at
      the table. She recognized that time spent representing the interests of Draper
      was time the Mayor did not spend in his professional career, and expressed
      support for the one-time incentive payment. Councilmember Green said he
      believed Mayor Walker was definitely deserving of the incentive payment.
      Councilmember Johnson said Mayor Walker had been as effective as he was
      because of the relationships he had created. Councilmember Vawdrey said
      Mayor Walker was great for the City, and said the Council appreciated Mayor
      Walker.

6.b   Action Item: Resolution #25-10 adopting the Capitalization Threshold for
      Draper City
      Finance Director John Vuyk presented a proposal to change Draper City
      capitalization thresholds. He explained that “capital asset” was defined by the
      Government Finance Officers Association (GFOA) as an asset used in
      operations with initial useful life extending beyond a single reporting period,
      and currently defined by Draper City as an asset with an initial individual cost
      of more than $5,000 and an estimated useful life in excess of one year. Mr.
      Vuyk said the Draper City standard was established in 1999. He said staff
      reviewed all Draper City assets and determined City assets fell into eight
      categories. He proposed the following asset classifications, with updated
      capitalization thresholds and useful life. Mr. Vuyk said the City would save time
      in capitalizing assets with the proposed changes.

                                                         Capitalization   Useful Life
       Asset Classification
                                                          Threshold       (In Years)
       Office Equipment                                         $10,000       3-7
       Furniture and Equipment                                  $10,000      5-15
       Machinery and Equipment                                  $10,000      5-15
       Improvements (Other than buildings)                      $10,000      7-40
       Vehicles                                                 $20,000      5-15
       Infrastructure                                           $20,000     10-20
       Buildings (Including improvements)                       $20,000      7-40
       Water Tanks                                              $50,000      100
