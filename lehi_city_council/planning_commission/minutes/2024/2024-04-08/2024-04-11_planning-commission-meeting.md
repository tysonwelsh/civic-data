3.2) Public hearing and recommendation of Mark Skousen’s request for review of Light
Industrial zoning for the Hadco-Pacific Annexation, approximately 10.63 acres located at
approximately 2100 North 1450 West.
Brittney Harris presented information on the item.

Commissioner Everett requested clarification on the adjacent property and its zoning. Brittney
pulled up the parcel map to show property ownership.

The applicant was Mark Skousen. Commissioner Everett inquired if this would always be a right
of way. The applicant stated that there had not been any discussions about it. Brad Kenison
mentioned that much of this property had been supplied back to Hadco from UDOT.
Commissioner Kunze asked if the usage would be changed. The applicant replied that it would
not be changed.

Commission Chair Jackson opened the public hearing.

Ross Carter was opposed. Thirty years ago, the intent for the property had been for storage and
maintenance. The noise and dirt were nuisances. Despite police involvement and provided
documentation, legal enforcement had not been carried out. Noise studies had been provided, and
he felt like they had changed. He is highly concerned about the noise and the impact that it has
had over the years on his community.

Brad Sunderland was opposed. The city had put a Park and Ride near his home. There was an
access route near the Park and Ride to Hadco, and one of the most dangerous intersections in
Lehi was State Street and Trinnaman Lane. He stated it made him anxious with vehicles coming
off 2100 North meeting at State Street and going onto Trinnaman Lane.
Mike Bentley was opposed. He stated years ago Hadco had been instructed to stay on the
Southwest lot. Now, they closely bordered his and other neighbors' homes. This was supposed to
be Light Industrial, but when he read City code, it did not meet Light Industrial standards. He
wrote a letter to the City Council about one year ago to address the issue. He logged a week's
worth of Hadco nuisances. The application requirements are needed to apply to the Hadco
annexation. He was nervous about what was going to happen to his house. The dust and the noise
were unprecedented and negatively affecting their livelihood.

Chad Perry is opposed. He echoed Mike Bentley's comments.

Bradley Doornor was opposed. If he had known about the situation of the noise and dust, he
would not have purchased the house. He felt like Hadco was doing the bare minimum to meet the
code standard.

Suzanne Perry was opposed. She wanted to know if it could have been annexed but not with the
potential noise and dust. If the UDOT property had been annexed, she wondered if there was any
way it could have been arranged so that it couldn't be sold.

Kathy Sunderland was opposed. She wanted to know who owned or was taking care of the park
and ride. She had concerns about the property and how unsightly it was. If it were annexed, she
wondered who would oversee it.

Peter Rehm inquired about a sound wall, asking how high it would be and how effective it would
be.

Brittney Harris acknowledged the online public comments which will be included in the public
record below.

Heidi Cook I am unsure if my original comment was submitted. I would like to give input on not
allowing the expansion of Hadco and or allowing them zoning for light industrial use. We have
enough noise with 2100 N, Hadco, State Street, I-15 etc. Please keep our homes, park, church as
peaceful as can be. Please add sound barrier walls.

Colton Cook Please do not allow Hadco/Mark Skousen additional light industrial approval of
this area. We already have enough noise from them in their current location, but also from 2100
N, State St, I-15, etc. We also have enough dust and dirt from their current location as well as the
surrounding building. Please allow us to enjoy our neighborhood and recreation in our
backyards, park, and church pavilion. A sound dampening wall is already needed with the
number of noise/dust/debris that is around our neighborhood that has built, added onto, and
encroached around us, please do not allow further noise, dust, and dirt to be allowed and cause
more problems with our neighborhood.

Amanda Pierce, I live in the neighborhood sandwiched between the Hadco property, 2100 N, and
State Street. With these 3 factors combined (not to mention the nearby freeway) the noise level
both day and night is already too high. We simply cannot allow the noise level to increase. About
a year ago I was speaking with a Lehi City Police officer. I asked him if there were any
neighborhoods he would avoid living in within Lehi City (referring to crime). He answered,
"Honestly I would only avoid living in your neighborhood, just because of the noise level".

Commission Chair Jackson closed the public hearing.

Commissioner Eyre asked about the annexation because it was an existing use. Commissioner
Everett questioned what was considered an existing use, noting that what was being done didn't
fit any of the zoning, and it would have to be conditional, especially where it concerned County
property. Commissioner Everett did not feel like it was a conforming use. Commissioner Eyre
stated that the request made sense before it was confirmed. Kim Struthers stated that with
annexation, there was leverage because it still had to go to the Council, and there would have to
be an annexation agreement. Kim mentioned that recommendations could be made to the
Council for that annexation agreement. Kim further stated that unless the property was annexed,
the use was usually annexed with whatever use was already in place, but the annexation
agreement could stipulate the usage. Brad Kenison mentioned that sound walls could be installed
at different heights to prevent noise penetration at different levels. Kim mentioned that a lot of
the noise came from within Lehi City, and Lehi Police dealt with that.
Commissioner Kunze asked the applicant if some of the louder operations would be moved
closer to the freeway where the annexation would have taken place, which was further from
residential areas. Josh Moffit stated he believed some of the dust would have been mitigated by
the buildings and some of the trucking would have been distributed by paved roads and the
relocation of trucking operations.

Commissioner Everett stated that some of the problems stemmed from the existing pieces in the
City. The annexation agreement would have only pertained to the item discussed that night. He
inquired why things had not been put into code that could have been enforceable that night.

Commissioner Everett wanted to make a summary comment. He understood the consistency of
an annexation agreement to help alleviate the negativities that were currently being experienced.
If it were not granted, the use would have been continued, and Commissioner Everett would
have liked to help fix that. He felt the route to fix this was to make recommendations through an
annexation agreement. Commissioner Jackson agreed with Commissioner Everett.

        Motion: Commissioner Eyre recommend approval of Mark Skousen’s request for
        review of Light Industrial zoning for the Hadco-Pacific Annexation, approximately
        10.63 acres located at approximately 2100 North 1450 West; noting it allows to have
        more controls with development should it be developed in the future. It is recommended
        that the City Council include an annexation agreement outlining the legalities that can be
        established for sound and dust mitigation on both this property and the south property
        owned by Hadco. Including all DRC comments and findings stated in the report.
        Commissioner Kunze seconded the motion.

        Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes;
        Commissioner Jones, yes; Commissioner Kunze, yes.
