COMMENT PERIOD
A.  PUBLIC COMMENTS
    Dennis Tenney stated that he and his neighbors are concerned about nuisance in
    their neighborhood. He indicated that there is a resident who has large overgrown
    trees infested with bugs. He indicated that he would like Code Enforcement to visit
    this neighbor and encourage the resident to voluntarily cut down the trees. Mr.
    Tenney also requested that the City revise and review the nuisance ordinance to
    address this issue. He indicated that he would provide suggested language to the
    City Recorder.

       Mark Chadwick stated that he also has a nuisance issue. He indicated that there is
       significant loud vehicle noise from his neighbor’s muffler. He stated that he has
       confronted him but the issue has not stopped. Mr. Chadwick indicated that he would
       like to look at possible solutions or changes to the noise ordinance to prevent this
       type of behavior.

       Mike Rigdon expressed concern about speeding vehicles on 4100 South and a lack
       of people being ticketed.

       Jeff Farnsworth stated he is a neighbor of Mark Chadwik and is in support of the
       noise concerns in his area with loud car engines.

       Lindie Beaudoin stated that she lives in Newton Farms and is concerned that a park
       that was promised has not been built. She indicated that she and her neighbors were
       told the park would be constructed in 2021-2022 but the City has stated that there
       is no funding for it.

       Darrell Curtis state that he is also concerned about noise issues. He indicated that
       he lives near the Mountain View Corridor and he would like to see signs restricting
       engine brakes. He indicated that it is very loud all hours of the day or night.

B.     CITY MANAGER COMMENTS
       Wayne Pyle, City Manager, had no comments.

PUBLIC HEARINGS
A.   ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING THE BUDGET OF WEST
     VALLEY CITY FOR THE FISCAL YEAR 2021-2022
     Mayor Bigelow informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular
     Council Meeting scheduled August 10, 2021, in order for the City Council to hear
     and consider public comments regarding the Budget of West Valley City for the
     Fiscal Year 2021-2022.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

              This resolution adopts the final budget for West Valley City for the 2021-
              2022 fiscal year and sets a date for public comment at a hearing to be held
              on August 10, 2021.

              West Valley City will adopt a final budget that will be made available for
              public inspection during regular office hours in the City’s Recorder’s office
              and gave notice of a hearing to receive public comment before the final
              adoption of this budget for FY 2021-2022.

       Jim Welch, Finance Director, presented a PowerPoint Presentation summarized as
       follows:

              -   Reflects Council direction and objectives
              -   Promotes efficiency in government
              -   Public disclosure/transparency
              -   Balanced budget required by state law
              -   Prepared to accepted national standards
              -   Budgetary Drivers
                     o Fiscal Responsibility
                     o Governmental and Regulatory Requirements
                     o Increasing Costs of Services and Operations
                     o Aging Infrastructure and Maintenance Costs
                     o Economic Conditions
                              COVID-19 Impacts
                              Federal Stimulus
                              Changing Market Conditions
                     o Revenues and expenses for 2021-2022

  Wayne Pyle, City Manager, verified that the taxes were not lowered. Jim replied
  yes. Wayne stated that the revenue is level for the City as a whole. Jim replied yes.
  Wayne verified that this is a small increase by the City based on a judgment levy
  but any other increases on a tax bill, such as the Granite School District increase,
  does not come from the City. Jim replied yes.

  Councilmember Huynh asked if there was a sales tax increase. Jim stated that
  property tax and sales tax are separate and it varies. Councilmember Huynh asked
  if sales tax can be used to offset property taxes being raised. Jim replied that this
  would depend on many factors.

  Mayor Bigelow opened the Public Hearing.

  Michael Espinosa stated that he is 30 and came to West Valley City for
  affordability. He indicated that the taxable value of his home has increased
  significantly and he is seeing an 8.4% increase for his taxes overall. He indicated
  that he understands other entities are responsible for many of them but noted he
  would like to see the City trim some of its budget. He indicated that everyone is
  aware of and feels the pressure of rising costs and inflation.

  Harold Burris stated that he is facing a significant increase in property tax. He
  indicated that the market value of his home has increased significantly and he is on
  a fixed income. Mayor Bigelow clarified that the City does not have any say or
  ability in establishing the market value of a home. He indicated that this falls under
  the County jurisdiction. Councilmember Buhler noted that the tax bill provides
  information on how to submit a rebuttal.

  Jim Vesock stated that he has owned his home for 6 years and home values are
  increasing everywhere. He expressed concern about the estimated parking ticket
  fines decreasing, funding for and from Code Enforcement and Ace, and increases
  in the City Manager’s office. He stated he feels the budget is inaccurate and stated
  that he believes the budget should not be re-opened after approved.

  Darrell Curtis stated that the budget numbers are unclear and he doesn’t know what
  is represented where. He stated he would like more detail on the report so he knows
  where his tax dollars are going.

  Mayor Bigelow closed the Public Hearing.

    Mayor Bigelow discussed proposed Resolution 21-140 that would authorize the
    City to enter into a Real Estate Purchase Agreement and accept a Public Utility
    Easement, a Perpetual Easement and a Temporary Construction Easement with and
    from Daniel D’Ewart for property located at 4693 South Lemonwood Circle.

      Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
      information as follows:

             The Daniel D’Ewart parcel located at 4693 S. Lemonwood Circle is one of
             several parcels affected by the 4700 South Reconstruction Project (4000
             West to 5600 West). This project is being constructed in cooperation with
             Kearns Metro Township. This project will reconstruct the pavement, curb,
             gutter, sidewalk and decorative street lighting on both sides of 4700 South
             and 4715 South to improve safety and ride quality of 4700 South and 4715
             South. Compensation for the purchase of the Public Utility Easement,
             Perpetual Easement and a Temporary Construction Easement and
             improvements is $11,600.00 based upon the appraisal report prepared by
             Van Drimmellen Associates, Inc.

             The project is being funded under the Surface Transportation Program
             (STP) with federal funds. Kearns Metro Township executed the Federal
             Aid Agreement with UDOT. West Valley City has signed an Interlocal
             Agreement with Kearns Metro Township. The city is responsible 6.77% of
             all project costs, including right of way, within West Valley City
             jurisdictional limits. The total value for the Public Utility Easement,
             Perpetual Easement and a Temporary Construction Easement is $11,600.00,
             making the West Valley City share of the acquisition $785.32.
