      Mr. Gwilliam noted he sent an email to the Mayor and Council outlining why he would

      his opinion, the arguments he is making make a lot of sense to consider. He asked

      Mr. Teepen explained PA11 is even closer to PA12 than PA17 is to Sentieri Canyon.
      PA12 has single family homes being built now.

      He noted that it will be at a collector and major roadway. Putting all of the high
      density in one area makes the area not as livable for those living in apartments, it is
      better to have them integrated into neighborhoods. He does not believe a wall

      application that has been submitted.

DISCUSSION REGARDING DOWNTOWN IRRIGATION:
     Water Services Director Scott Taylor provided a PowerPoint presentation covering the
     following topics: Open ditch irrigation system – inefficiency; Annual Water Use – 5
     random irrigation users vs. neighbors. He noted that he has found records showing
     the City bought the water and leased it back to the residents since the 1930s;
     however, the users claim they own the water. He has asked users to provide
     documents showing they own the shares; however, they often bring in a deed from a
     property transfer that states they are purchasing the property with any interest in
     the irrigation company, but nothing ever states the users own the shares. He
     explained that the West City Springs irrigation water was culinary quality water
     which the shares were owned by the users. The City either bought their shares or
     provided the option to provide culinary water at a discounted rate. He proposes to
     do the same for the Cottonwood and East City Springs irrigation users for a specific
     period of time.

      City Attorney Shawn Guzman commented that only a few still would qualify to
      receive the irrigation water with the terms currently in place.

      Mr. Taylor added if the users want to keep their gardens or lawns, they would need
      to install sprinkler systems. He is not sure how many would want to do that.

      Councilmember Hughes commented that most users use the irrigation water for
      gardens; therefore, they would most likely use a drip system.

      Mr. Taylor noted the irrigation users are aware that the open ditch system is going
      away.

      Councilmember Hughes stated the users are aware of the situation as there have
      been meetings. They will want to know what has been decided. He understands the
      inefficiency, however; the open ditch system is a big deal to some people.

      After being asked by City Manager Adam Lenhard, Mr. Taylor explained inefficiency is
      the biggest issue as this is the worst use of the water. It would take infrastructure
      to capture the unused water. The estimate of lost irrigation water is approximately
      15 million gallons per year.

      Leisure Services Director Shane Moore stated it will cost a couple thousand dollars to
      install a sprinkler system.

      A discussion took place regarding a reduced rate versus a buy-out option.

      After being asked by Councilmember McArthur, City Recorder Christina Fernandez
      explained with Cottonwood, the irrigation water stays with the owner – if the user
      passes or sells the home, the water shares are forfeited back to the City. With East
      City Springs, the irrigation water stays with the home.

      Councilmember McArthur commented the number one argument he hears is that
      these users have built this town, were here a long time and now have to give up
      irrigation because the City has grown so much.

DISCUSSION REGARDING THE BRIGHTER SIDE FOUNDATION:
     Leisure Services Director Shane Moore provided an overview of the Brighter Side
     Foundation. The Foundation will be used to raise grant money for different programs
     throughout the City.

      Assistant City Attorney Bryan Pack explained he would like to hear input from the
      Council what level of control they would like the City to have over the Foundation.
      As a government entity, the City can accept donations currently. The Foundation is
      not necessarily the mechanism needed to accept donations; however, it will send a
      message to donors that it is not quite so much the government because it is a
      separate entity. Staff has drafted documents allowing the Mayor to appoint the five
      Foundation Members; but can be amended to say the board members are specific
      positions – elected officials or staff of the City. Having elected officials or staff be
      Foundation Members starts to merge the Foundation with the City and kind of
      eliminates the point of the Foundation of separating it from the City. Another option
      is to have the Foundation Members be appointed by other board or commissions of
      the City.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard noted donations could be significant.

      A discussion took place regarding what the funds would be used for. The funds will
      be used for projects throughout the City, not just Leisure Services projects. After
      being asked by City Manager Adam Lenhard, Assistant Attorney Bryan Pack
      explained donors can err mark a donation for a specific project.

      Assistant Attorney Bryan Pack stated his recommendation is to have the Mayor
      appoint the Foundation Members.

      Deputy Director of Arts and Events Michelle Graves explained foundations like this
      opens the door for the City to receive donations for larger projects.

      Councilmember McArthur believes Foundation Members should be high net worth
      individuals, but there should also be a steering committee who meets as needed.

      Assistant Attorney Bryan Pack proposed the City Council serve as the steering
      committee.

      The consensus of the council to move forward, with the City Council serving as the
      steering committee. The Foundation Members will be required to accept the
      recommendations of the steering committee.
