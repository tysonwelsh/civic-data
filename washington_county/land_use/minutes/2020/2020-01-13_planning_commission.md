Washington County Planning Commission
                                    Land Use Appeal Hearing
                                            Minutes
                                        January 13, 2020

The Washington County Land Use Authority Appeal Hearing was held on Monday January 13, 2020 in
the Council Chambers of the Washington County Administration Building at 197 E. Tabernacle, St.
George, UT. Chairman Slade Hughes opened the meeting at 1:30 p.m. He led the Pledge of Allegiance.

Commissioners present: Brandon Anderson, Dan Briggs (Alternate acting as a voting member), Kevin
Jones, Mark Owens. Excused: Shane Durrant, Reed Scow (Alternate), and Doug Solstad.

Others in attendance: Bruce Baird, counsel for MSH Investments dba Zions Gate Management Company
LLC; Eric Clarke, Washington County Deputy Attorney, counsel for County Staff; Brian Graf,
Washington County Deputy Attorney, counsel for Planning Commission. Staff: Scott Messel, Community
Development Director; Doreen Bowers-Irons, Administrative Assistant.


APPEAL HEARING OF CONDITIONAL USE DENIAL FOR A RURAL RECREATION
REQUEST ON KOLOB MOUNTAIN.

Bruce Baird, counsel representing the appellants introduced himself.

Eric Clarke, counsel representing Washington County Community Development Staff introduced himself.

Mr. Baird referenced his letter of November 27, 2019 and the response to that letter from Mr. Clarke. Mr.
Clarke’s stance makes sense; however, the problem is that it is fundamentally wrong. The response
indicated that if the argument is correct, it would nullify the entire moratorium provisions in CLUDMA.
He argued the problem with that is it misreads 17-27a-504 of Utah State Code which, is the moratorium
section. He cited the code and provided copies to the commissioners. He also referenced 17-27a-508,
which he cited. As noted in the referenced letter, it is impossible to find that the moratorium the county
adopted on April 2, 2019 is anything other than a trigger of a pending ordinance.

Mr. Baird reported he served on the Land Use Task Force that is a coalition of several entities. In 2005,
they rewrote the county land use management code to ensure that counties could not “stack” a moratorium
and try to have it exceed the 180-day limit, which, this appeared to be. He provided audio minutes from
the county commission meetings of April 02, 2019 when the moratorium was adopted and the September
10 and 24, 2019 planning commission meetings. He pointed out that instead of starting with the planning
commission as required by county code; the ordinance had been started by the county commission in their
April meeting. He explained the evidence of that allegation and the information he attained from the
planning commission meeting recordings. He conveyed that the project should not have been denied, as
the planning commission had not made a recommendation to the county commission. Therefore, by
passing the ordinance amendment of the Planned Development Zone procedures and creating a PD Zone
for Rural Recreational Grounds and Facilities on September 17, 2019, that was an illegal act. His final
statement was that the based on the recording of the planning commission meetings, Mr. Crowe’s
application should have been processed. The law is stoned cold clear and the county knew what the law
was and was trying to change the rules of the game after it had been played. He asked if there were any
questions. There were none and he turned the time over to Mr. Clarke.

Mr. Clarke provided a packet for the commission and staff. In the packet was a CD-ROM that included
all applications and documents submitted by the appellant.

Mr. Clarke stated the planning commission wears several hats. There are days when they come in and
they have to wear a policymaking hat i.e. legislation. Other times they are wearing an administrator hat
and only applying the laws as written for a conditional use permit and so forth. Today they wore a judicial
hat because it is an appeal on the record. The appeal is coming to this body for a determination if the
county’s interpretation is right or wrong.

Mr. Clarke pointed out that the record was before the commission and that the commission will listen to
any evidence and then have the opportunity to ask any questions. They would then have deliberations
outside of the hearing or if they were comfortable, they could have an open discussion for a determination.
Once the planning commission has made their decision, the parties will be notified of the decision within
15 days. Then either party has the right to appeal the final decision to the district court.

Mr. Clarke stated the issue before them was did the county illegally enforce the moratorium? The county
rarely imposes moratoriums, and there may be some procedural errors. The process has not been flawless
with this one. Moratoriums are restrictions on property rights. What matters is when the county sent a
letter saying they are not going to process the request because of the pending ordinance doctrine. The
planning commission will have to decide if that was an illegal stacking of moratoriums. He argued that it
is not. He referred to the State Code Sections 504 and 508 of the CLUDMA act, which he cited. In 504
the key trigger is that this is a temporary land use regulation.

Mr. Baird argued that this would apply to different types of situations than what is contemplated under
508. He cited Section 508 of the Utah State Code and pointed out that under the temporary land use
regulation it just calls it a land use regulation.

Mr. Clarke stated the question is can they be stacked, not should they be stacked. The county tried hard
not to have these stacked because the longer it takes; the longer a property right is restricted. The county
is not in the business to try to restrict property rights. The issue isn’t is that good policy or not. The issue
is, is it illegal to have a pending ordinance that is not adopted before a moratorium time ends to apply that
doctrine. If the legislature had written a sentence in the codes that a pending ordinance does not apply if
the county had enacted a temporary land use regulation, then we would not be here. There is nothing in
the code that indicates they cannot be stacked. The appellants are arguing that essentially, the county
started a pending ordinance by the language of the moratorium without the county commission consulting
with the planning commission and that triggered the provisions of the separate act. There is nothing in the
code that one trumps the other. They are two separate tools counties can utilize. He spoke of the role of
the legislature and how the law was written. Perhaps they should have specified in the law whether they
could or could not be stacked so that it was not inferred. The appellant’s position is a wrong interpretation
of the statutes.

Mr. Clarke concluded that the planning commission is tasked with deciding if staff erred in its denial of
the conditional use permit request for the Rural Recreation Grounds application for MSH Investment dba
Zions Gate. He asked if there were any questions for him.

Mr. Baird gave a rebuttal indicating he had been in Mr. Clarke’s position when he was Salt Lake City’s
Land Use attorney. He was also involved in a case that had no hope, which is the most frustrating thing
as your client wants you to say something but there is nothing to say. Therefore, you make the best

argument possible. He complimented Mr. Clarke on trying to come up with something. As previously

    a) Read the words.
    b) Read all of the words together not just things out of context.
    c) Read them to make sense.

Mr. Baird argued that it is impossible to read if you start it by the manner provided by local ordinances to
amend the land use regulations. Land use regulations is a defined term in Utah Code. He provided the
Code Section then cited it. He alleged that the county did not do this moratorium because of water, fire,
or sewer. They did this moratorium because one particular county commissioner, who does not seem to
believe in private property rights, did not want people to develop their private property in a way that the
code allowed them to do. As Mr. Messel indicated, we opened the door and not knowing what would walk
through the door and now seeing what walked through the door, we need to change it. The county did not
change the ordinance in time. They gave themselves a window in which to change it but did not do so. It
is clear the county had acknowledged that and pulled the trigger on April 2, 2019. He concluded by stating
if there is any doubt this was done erroneously, then the commission needs to find in favor of the
development rights of the private property owner. He explained case notes referenced by Mr. Clarke in
his brief.

Mr. Baird stated two final things, in terms of the completeness of the record, which Mr. Clarke stipulated
to, that the minutes of the record include all of the agendas, minutes and recordings for both the County
Commission and Planning Commission in the event there is an appeal. He averred that they would appeal
because of the amount of money at stake and the private property rights. He thanked the commission for
their time.

Commissioner Hughes had questions for the county. It was mentioned there were some procedural error
on the County’s side. Does the county interpret procedural error as something that they accept or
something that they apply only when it applies to the county and not to the applicant.

Mr. Clarke clarified the reason that with the recently passed ordinance, it took the rural recreation grounds
and facilities from a conditional use permit and put it in a zoning district. He spoke of the timelines of the
ordinance being passed noting it went to the planning commission on September 10, 2019 but they did
not make a recommendation to the county commission. The county commission pulled the rural recreation
use from that larger proposed ordinance changes so they would not go beyond the 6-month moratorium.
Mr. Baird reached out to us and correctly pointed out that when the ordinance was passed there had not
been a recommendation from the planning commission and there was a relative provision in State Law
that talked about the process the county commission could have if they wanted to do something without
getting permission from the planning commission but we had not yet put those provisions in the ordinance.
The planning commission did review those changes and recommended approval to the county commission
and they are now in place. That was the first procedural error.

Mr. Clarke stated that the second thing Mr. Baird found in the County Code that had been there forever
and was missed was that if a land use change goes to the county commission they were required to hold a
public hearing. The county has been holding the public hearings in the planning commission meetings
only. The county has since amended the ordinance to hold the public hearings at the planning commission
level. We did not apply the ordinance as passed in that initial instance because it was not passed correctly.

That is why we are here talking about the pending ordinance doctrine. There clearly was a pending
ordinance that had been initiated by the planning commission.

Commissioner Owens addressed Mr. Clarke clarifying his understanding of Utah Code Section 508. He
referenced Mr. Clarkes response related to the initiation of the county code amendment to the Rural
Recreation Grounds and Facilities. He pointed out that during the September 10, 2019 meeting the
planning commission formally initiated the process by holding a public hearing regarding the proposed
land use code amendments. He asked if he was missing something. Mr. Clarke answered that he was
correct. At the time of the application, the ordinance changes had been initiated.

Discussion and clarification on the initiation of the ordinance ensued. Mr. Clarke explained the process
and reiterated that the amendment was formally initiated at the time of the public hearing in the September
10, 2019 planning commission meeting.

Mr. Baird responded by stating his position was simple. The county formally initiated the pending
ordinance on April 2, 2019 when they said, “Shall remain in effect until Washington County adopts a
permanent ordinance amending the facilities”. That is how counties start processes. They say they are
starting and that is the starting gun.

Commissioner Owens advised Mr. Baird that he had lived in the area for approximately 32 years and took
exception to his comment that one county commissioner took it upon himself to stop the rights of property
owners. The statement was contrary to his knowledge. Mr. Baird and Commissioner Owens briefly
discussed the comment.

Chairman Hughes asked if there were any further questions, there being none he conferred with Brian
Graf, Deputy Attorney who explained how the hearing should proceed. Chairman Hughes consulted with
the commissioners on how they would like deliberate. The commissioners concluded they would do so in
a private setting.

MOTION: Commissioner Jones MOVED to adjourn the appeal hearing and move into private
         deliberation. Commissioner Anderson SECONDED. Chairman Hughes asked if there
         were any further comments. There being none, Chairman Hughes called for a vote.
         The motion carried unanimously.

Mr. Graf informed Mr. Baird and Mr. Clarke that a written decision would be available within 15 days.
The commissioners then left the podium and the meeting adjourned.
