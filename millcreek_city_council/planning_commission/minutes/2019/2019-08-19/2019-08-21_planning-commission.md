1.1 Consideration of ZM-19-014, Rezone from R-1-8 to R-2-8 for the Development of a
Duplex Location: 3419 S. 1300 E. Applicant: Jake Wright Planner: Blaine Gehring

property located at 3419 S. 1300 E. from R-1-8 to R-2-8 to facilitate construction of a new
duplex. The subject property is a .38-acre parcel with a single-family home on it and it is
adjacent on the south to a new planned unit development (PUD) by Brad Reynolds
Construction. There is an existing single-family home between the subject parcel and the
PUD. Currently the subject property is located adjacent to R-2-8 zoning to the south and
across the street on 1300 E. and adjacent to R-1-8 zoning on the east and west. The property
has an existing single-family dwelling located on the premises which will be demolished.
Brad Reynolds Construction would like to build a duplex on this parcel similar to those in the

1.2 Consideration of ZM-19-015, Rezone from R-1-21 to R-1-10 and Adjustment of the
Foothills and Canyons Overlay Zone Boundary Location: 3898 E. Thousand Oaks
Drive Applicant: Midway Hospitality Partners Planner: Robert May
Robert May said the applicant was proposing a multi-request application; rezone of two
parcels from R-1-21 to R-1-10, one of those parcels will have a zone map amendment to
adjust out of the Foothills and Canyon Overlay Zone (FCOZ) boundary, and a reduced front
yard setback from 30 feet to 18 feet. He said six parcels currently share the same address and
are owned by the same property owner. Three parcels are part of a plat recorded in the Mt.
Olympus Hills Subdivision in 1958 and the other three parcels are not part of a plat, possibly
created in the late 70’s. He said the existing FCOZ zone encumbers part of lot 2 and all of
parcel four, so it will be removed from parcel four and replaced with the Residential
Compatibility Overlay Zone (RCOZ). Parcels five and four would be rezoned from R-1-21 to
R-1-10. He said there was not public opposition to the FCOZ boundary adjustment. Staff
found that the proposed rezone for the two lots was compatible with the General Plan and
existing lot configuration and density of the neighborhood. The boundary adjustment of the
FCOZ removal and RCOZ addition would produce a better outcome for the neighborhood in
terms of building capacity and arrangement. Staff felt that the front yard setback reduction
should be done through the subdivision plat process following a successful rezone. May said
there was concern in the neighborhood meeting that lot 2 had the possibility of a through
street. He said code prevents lots from having double frontage. The Mt. Olympus
Community Council recommended approval of the rezone but expressed concern about a
potential drive through on lot 2 and to reduce the setback at a later time. He said staff
recommended granting only one access.
May said the development agreement should limit the density to what was being proposed
and not grant through access on lot 2. Francis Lilly said the Commission could impose a zone
condition to limit the density to not more than 5 units. Commissioner LaMar asked about
access to the property being limited from one cul-de-sac. Lilly said the access would be
limited to vehicular access. May said there is an existing structure on the property.
Commissioner Reid asked about Millcreek’s preference for cul-de-sacs. Lilly said he was not
a fan of cul-de-sacs, but they are part of the character of the neighborhood. He said if lot two
had a through street, the existing structure would have to be demolished and it was not
feasible. May said the subdivisions were old and new cul-de-sacs have different
requirements. Commissioner LaMar requested that lot 2 be allowed to have access from both
cul-de-sacs. May said Title 14 limits single family homes to one access, or two if the access
is circular in nature, and the subdivision ordinance prohibits double frontage lots. Lilly said
the concern about access was driven by two things; not wanting a through street and keeping
density lower. He said the access issue could be resolved by limiting the density.
Mt. Olympus Community Council Chair - David Baird, 4538 Thousand Oaks Drive, said
there was general council support for the application. He said the applicant is cleaning up
clerical inconsistencies and it would be a received premium to have six parcels consolidated
to five. He said there would not be a character change to the neighborhood by doing the
rezone and FCOZ adjustment. He pointed out that the plat was recorded in 1978, not 1958.

Development Consisting of 8 Townhomes Location: 4115 S. 300 E. Applicant: Mark

Erin O’ Kelley said the applicant was requesting a conditional use permit for an 8-unit
townhome project in the RM zone. The 8 townhomes would be two-bedroom with a garage
and private patio. The total height of the buildings is 35 feet. There will be one driveway that
accesses all the garages, 4 guest parking stalls per Millcreek Code, and one amenity for the
residents. There will be added public improvements along 300 E. including, curb, gutter, and
sidewalk, with enhanced landscaping. The surrounding property to the vacant parcel is zoned
RM with multi-family developments to the south and single-family residential to the north.
The subject property shares an access easement with the apartment complex to the southeast
as well as some additional land the apartment complex is using as partial open space and
pedestrian pathways. In this open space there are two mature trees as part of the apartment’s
open space. O’Kelley said the applicant was requesting a 5-foot front yard setback reduction.
The project proposal is for long-term residential rental units with access off 300 E. and a
shared driveway with the apartments. She said there would be perimeter fencing on the north
property line and the amenity had not been decided.
O’Kelley showed the site plan, building elevations, and landscaping plan to the Commission.
She said any development in the RM zone is held to the landscaping code though developers
can request exceptions. O’Kelley said the Commission should consider 8 factors per code for
allowing the setback reduction exception, which were:

1. The granting of the setback reduction will not be materially detrimental to the public welfare or
injurious to the property or improvements in the vicinity in which the property is located;

.2. The setback reduction is consistent with the policies and provision of the General Plan and this
Code.

3. Where applicable, the setback reduction results in a building site that accommodates a wider
buffer between the use and adjacent R-1 or R-2 zones than what is required by this Title.

4. The setback reduction does not create a situation where the development will result in an increase
in the number of residential dwelling units than would occur if the development was built without
the reduction.

5. The setback reduction does not create a situation where the proposed development will cause
objectionable noise, odors, trespass lighting, or similar adverse impacts on adjacent properties.

6. The setback reduction does not create a situation where the proposed development substantially
reduces the amount of privacy that would be enjoyed by nearby residents any more than would be
available if the development was built without the reduction.

7. The setback reduction does not create a situation where the proposed development will create an
obstruction of view in excess of two feet in height located within a triangular area bounded by
lines drawn from a point on the centerline of any driveway, set back fifteen feet from the front
property line to points on the property line at the street in front of the property line teen feet either
side of both edges of the driveway.

8. The setback reduction does not create a situation where it is reasonably anticipated that the
proposed development will result in vehicle stacking on a public right-of-way where that right-of-
way intersects with the driveway of the proposed development. Landscaping Required.
