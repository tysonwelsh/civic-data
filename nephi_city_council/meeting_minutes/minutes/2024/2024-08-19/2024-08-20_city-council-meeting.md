SHERIFF DOUGLAS ANDERSON HONORED OFFICER KYLE BELL:

    Nephi City Police Officer Kyle Bell was honored and awarded the Juab County Sheriff’s Office
Lifesaving Medal by Sheriff Douglas Anderson. Officer Bell was credited with saving the life of Tiffany
Hueglly two years ago. Sheriff Anderson said that he wanted to present the award earlier but said that
it is never too late to recognize heroism. He said that Ms. Huegly’s horse had accidentally rolled over
with her still in the saddle. The horse fell and the sharp point of the saddle horn impaled her leg.
Officer Bell offered lifesaving treatment using a dog leash to make a tourniquet. Mayor Seely also
recognized the fine work that Officer Bell performs with the youth of our community as the School
Resource Officer. Officer Bell stated that he enjoys working for the community and expressed his
appreciation for the city and county’s support.

R6 REPRESENTATIVE SHAY MORRISON UPDATED COUNCIL:

   R6 representative Shay Morrison updated the council about the R6 community advisor program. He
stated that the original idea was to share a city administrator with the small rural communities that
couldn’t afford a full-time city administrator. The program morphed into having five community
advisors’ mentor the smaller communities. He said that he has enjoyed learning from Mr. Atkinson who
is one of the R6 community advisors.
    R6 offers many training opportunities. They hold the annual Regional Summit and now they have
added mini trainings throughout the year. The next training is scheduled for September 26 th that will
cover training of staff. He said that the monthly newsletter has a Tip of the Month and this month’s tip
is that it is illegal to edit recordings of meetings.
   Mr. Morrison said that he has been able to aide Nephi City with short-term rental code, permitting
software analysis, and public comment policy. He stated that he enjoys working with the Nephi City
staff members. R6 Executive Director Travis Kyhl thanked the mayor and council for allowing Mr.
Atkinson to help other rural communities.

DR. COURTNEY FLINT REPORTED ON THE UTAH WELLBEING SURVEY:

     Dr. Courtney Flint reported to the council that Nephi was one of 49 cities participating in the Utah
Wellbeing Survey Project in 2024. This project is designed to assess the wellbeing and local perspectives
of city residents and to provide information to city leaders to inform general planning processes.
   This report described findings from the 2024 Nephi survey and comparative information with other
project cities. In March 2024, Nephi City advertised the survey for residents through the utility bill
newsletter, social media, city council announcements, and flyers in public buildings. All city residents age
18+ were encouraged to take the online Qualtrics survey.
    Dr. Flint reported that 450 viable surveys were recorded in this 2024 survey effort. The data showed
that the average personal wellbeing score in Nephi was 3.92 with 71% of respondents indicating their
wellbeing at a 4 or 5 on the 5-point scale. The average score for community wellbeing in Nephi was
3.43 with 48% of respondents indicating community wellbeing at a 4 or 5 on the 5-point scale.

ORDINANCE 08-20-2024 -CU-1 AND CU-2 ZONES ADOPTED:

  Ordinance 08-20-2024: AN ORDINANCE AMENDING THE ZONE MAP OF NEPHI CITY was presented for
the council’s approval. The ordinance changed the described parcels in the ordinance from CU-1 zone
and R1-8 zone to CU-II zone. This change in the zoning map is trying to meet the following goals of the
General Plan: Under Land Use it states:
        Goal 2.1. Establish orderly, planned growth and development that enhances the
        City’s distinctive environment and small-town character, and provides consistent
        services and infrastructure.
Under Community Design it states:
        Goal 3.4. Maintain the rural feel through appropriate street design.
Under the Transportation section of the General Plan, it also states:
        Goal 5.1. Provide and maintain a transportation system that promotes the orderly
        and safe transport of people, goods and services while preserving the residential
        character of the city.
  City Administrator Seth Atkinson had prepared a narrative along with the ordinance to help future
councils understand why these changes in zoning along Main Street were made.
   Councilor Parady moved to adopt Ordinance 08-20-2024. Councilor Shari Cowan seconded the
motion. The motion carried with the following vote:

        Yes: JD Parady, Jeramie Callaway, Shari Cowan
        Absent: Travis Worwood, Skip Worwood
        No: None

ROCKY MOUNTAIN POWER ASSET PURCHASE AGREEMENT APPROVED:

  Mr. Atkinson updated the council that an asset purchase agreement was recently sent to the city staff
for review regarding the purchase of electrical assets owned by Rocky Mountain Power servicing two
areas, one of which is now in the city.

  When the Biglow Annexation was annexed into the city, one parcel, owned by Alex Johnson,
contained electrical services for some freeway signs. These services should have been acquired by
Nephi City at the time, but it was overlooked at the time. The parcel directly across the street, owned
by The Wright Direction, has expressed interest in annexing and being served by Nephi Power. For

Work Session 9:25 p.m.

1.    Mr. Atkinson reported that for some years now, there have been some funds set aside for bridge
     expansion, replacement, and repair but have been insufficient to cover some of the rising costs of
     construction. In addition, new development has been a moving target that may influence where
     bridge replacements should occur. With the surplus from the last fiscal year, there should be
     enough to complete the improvements on at least one bridge in town. Staff from our Streets and
     Public Works Department had met and determined that the following three bridges are the ones
     recommended for improvements:
             a. 200 West on Salt Creek
             b. 600 East on the Hollow
             c. 800 East in Salt Creek

     After some discussion, the council agreed on the 800 East Salt Creek Bridge because of school
     traffic, development in the area, and the activity at Orgill Fields.
