5. Discussion Items

         5.a. Downtown Strategic Priorities, CRA Creation and Development – Mayor
              Trent Staggs

Mayor Staggs said that given the data, the demand for a multi-family product is great. He said
he wanted to know what the Council’s vision was going forward. He presented the steps that
the City has taken towards enhancing the downtown area including smart zoning, Psomas
study, Utah State study, sign and beautification projects, improvements to the Sandra Lloyd
Community Center, market study, Community Reinvestment Area (CRA) designation and
community partner projects with Salt Lake County and Riverbend clubhouse. He said that there
are developers interested in this area. He said that the next steps include approving the
Community Redevelopment Area and creating a vision for zones 1 and 2.

Mayor Staggs said that the City invested $40 million into the Western Commercial District. He
asked the Council to discuss their vision for the downtown area.

Councilmember Buroker said she is one who advocated for a historic designation downtown.
She said that she had changed her mind about “historic” downtown but would still like to have a
unique designation that represents Riverton. She said that she would like to put some money
into this area because it is closer for herself and the residents in her district then the Western
District.

Councilmember Wells said that he does not want to give up on this area and said he would like
to get some restaurants in the area. He said that he spends a lot of time on the west side and
supports Riverton businesses.

Councilmember McDougal said that in the 25 years he has been in Riverton, they have been
trying to figure out what to do with this downtown area. He said that he believes multi-family
homes are a good use for this area and this would not require a financial commitment from the
City. He said that the City should not fight the market and building town homes would support
the businesses that are already in this area.

Councilmember Stewart said he can see both sides of this argument. He said the area has two
major changes which are that Redwood Road is a large highway and there have been
numerous subdivisions built near the downtown area. He said that he has watched Scottsdale,
Arizona grow and be able to preserve their downtown area. He said that Scottsdale’s model
had retail and diners in the lower end and rentals and office space in the upper end. He said if
Riverton comes up with a good product then it will attract investment companies. He said he is
supportive of this idea.

Councilmember McCay said that she would like to see upscale commercial and office space.
She said she would rather not subsidize developers. She said she does not want to move the
skate park unless the developer is willing to pay the cost.

Mayor Staggs said that the majority of the Councilmembers agree that they should start talking
to developers. He said that the CRA is a key part of this discussion and is on the agenda later
during City Council. He said he has had developers reach out to him and thinks a developer
may be more willing to put up commercial with the tax increment.

Councilmember Wells said that he does not want multi-family housing in the downtown area.
He said there will be a parking issue and there is not enough space for parking. He said there is
also a high turnover in multi-family housing and he does not want this for the downtown area.

Councilmember McDougal said he is not supportive of all multi-family housing for this area. He
said he is mostly concerned about the City investing and he does not want the City to finance
most of these costs.

Mayor Staggs said that the bulk of the CDA tax dollars went to development on the west side.
He said if something was structured similar to this development then he believes the Council
would be supportive. He said this type of structure would not be a City investment but a

Councilmember Buroker said that if the City does not want high density housing then the City
has to be proactive.

Mayor said this may require more money than the tax increment will provide. He said he does
not want to go with the path of least resistance because this will result in high density housing.


         5.b.      Park/Field Programming and Utilization - Mayor Staggs

Mayor Staggs said that one of the Councilmembers had asked the Council to consider bringing
more programs to the main park. He said that an ordinance passed in 2015 prohibited league
affiliated practices or games at the main park. He asked the Council if the main park should be
open to league games and practices.

Councilmember McDougal said he would like to repeal that ordinance so that the main park
could get more use. He said that most of the leagues practice and play in city neighborhood
parks. He said that he likes the idea of designating this park space for a particular sport.

Councilmember Wells said that he goes by the main park often. He said that he is surprised at
how full the park is and people come from all over to play pickleball and play in the park. He
said that the big grass space is the only place that could be utilized more.

Councilmember McCay would like to expand the uses of the main park. She said that she
supports anything the City can do to increase physical activity. She said she would like to hear
from Parks and Recreation about the needs that they anticipate.

Councilmember Buroker said that she is very split on this. She said that Provo City has some
very passive parks, along with Logan City and their parks are always full. She said that there is
a lot of green space at the park and believes that this issue should be brought back and
analyzed.

Councilmember Stewart said he was on the Council when the ordinance prohibiting organized
sports at the main park was passed. He said he believes the park has matured and would be
ready to explore other uses but does not want them to interfere with City events. He said he
would like to defer to the Councilmembers whose districts are near the main park.

6. Mayor/Council/Manager Reports

David Brickey, City Manager, said that on June 22 there was a major rain storm that caused
flooding and significantly impacted the Midas Creek Park. He said the City has removed
$20,000 worth of mud in the park and the creek.

Trace Robinson, Public Works Director, said that radar data indicated a 185 to 200 year rainfall
intensity occurred over Herriman. He said that debris was a significant factor. He said the
culvert had the capacity for the water but the debris made it come up on the road. He said that
the City had hauled approximately 60 tons of material. He said the City will need to fix the
sprinklers and replant and sod in September 2021.

Councilmember Buroker said there was a concrete roadway covered with mud near the
detention in the Ivory Homes development east of 2700 West and asked if this needed to be
cleared off. She also asked why there was a concrete roadway.

Mr. Robinson said this was required by Salt Lake County to provide them access. He said they
took the sweeper in Midas Creek Park and that the City could do the same thing in this pathing
on this roadway. He said that he will come back with recommendations for improvement.

Councilmember Stewart said that anytime there is a heavy storm, he gets nervous. He said the
City has done a lot to make sure that flooding does not occur during these heavy storms.

Councilmember McDougal said that he appreciated the speed that Riverton responded and
complimented City staff for their efforts. He said he would like to communicate better with
residents about dumping in the Midas Creek area.

Mayor Staggs suggested that the “Keep Riverton Beautiful” could focus on this problem on
Midas Creek and Rose Creek.

         15.b. Ordinance No. 21-19 - Amending and Updating Riverton City’s Ordinance
         Regulating Noticing of Public Hearings, Amendments Proposed by Riverton City.

Mr. Lethbridge explained that this is a public hearing for Public Notice to announce Public
Hearings for Land-Use applications and is required by state and city code. He said the recent
changes to the state code eliminated the need to announce public hearings by publishing
notices in the newspaper and the requirement was replaced with requirements to post the notice
on the City web-page. He said the proposed text change harmonizes the City’s land-use code
and brings it into conformity with the state code regarding method and timing of making
announcements. He said it eliminates the newspaper requirement, and better addresses the
posting to the City’s website and it also sets the date range for noticing consistent with State
Code and makes all section of the City Code standardized. He said the Planning Commission
recommended approval of this amendment, and staff concurs with that recommendation.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Comment
period.

Councilmember Wells MOVED that the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 21-19 - Amending
and Updating Riverton City’s Ordinance Regulating Noticing of Public Hearings.

motion; there being none, he called for a roll call vote and the vote was as follows:
Councilmembers Buroker-Yes, McCay-Yes, McDougal-Yes, Stewart-Yes, and Wells-Yes. The

         15.c. Ordinance No. 21-20 – Proposed Changes to 18.225 Accessory Structures,
         Addressing Building Heights and Size, Setback Regulations and Other
         Amendments, Amendments Proposed by Riverton City.
Page | 9 Minutes – City Council Meeting
July 20, 2021




Jason Lethbridge said this is a public hearing for a Zoning Text Change proposed by Riverton
City and the zone text change involves Section 18.225 Accessory Structures. He said the
proposed amendments are primarily what staff would refer to as ‘housekeeping’ items, clarifying
or correcting existing language and standards. He said one example of this in the proposed
amendment is the current ordinance includes a prohibition on the use of storage or freight
containers as accessory structures, but that language is located in the section relating to
carports. He said this amendment relocates the language on storage containers to the General
Requirements section.
He said in addition to minor housekeeping changes the amendments consist of the following:
• The ordinance includes allowance for structures below 200 square feet to be located 1 foot
from property line. The amendment adds a limitation that the structure must also be single level
and below 16 feet in height, to minimize impacts to surrounding properties.
• Increase of side and rear setbacks to 15 feet where a second story is included.
• Clarification of requirements for unfinished storage areas under trusses, including limitation
to ladder access only.
• Clarification of height requirement and setback for agricultural structures, including minimum
15 foot setback where structure exceeds 20 feet
• Clarification of fencing and setback requirements for above ground pools, adding where such
pools have a capacity above 5,000 gallons fencing and setback standards apply.
• Addition of setback requirements for pool slides, diving boards, etc where such equipment
exceeds 36 inches in height.
• Deletion of maximum square footage limitation for internal accessory dwelling units, as Utah
State Code no prohibits such limitations.

Councilmember Stewart asked if the setback is from the base because he worries about runoff
going onto neighboring property. Mr. Lethbridge said the City could add that language to the
ordinance.

Councilmember McCay asked whether existing swimming pools would be grandfathered in. Mr.
Lethbridge said that existing pools will be grandfathered in and this new language will only apply
to new builds.
