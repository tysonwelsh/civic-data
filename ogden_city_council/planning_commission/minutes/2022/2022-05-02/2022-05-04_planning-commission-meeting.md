   b. Amended Conditional Use Permit/Public Facility Site Plan, for Weber Basin Water Pump Station at
    4826 Old Post Road. (Attachment 2b)
    Mr. Rodriguez stated this is a request to amend an existing conditional use permit to allow the
    relocation of the existing pump station to the west side of Old Post Road. He stated the proposed
    location meets all requirements of the zone in terms of setbacks and height and is 32’ from the south
    property line.
    Mr. Joseph Bauman stated he had emailed the Planning Commission a list of concerns as he owns
    property to the south and desires to build a home in the future. His concern is that there are existing
    wetlands on the property and there may be potential of water draining onto his property. He asked
    consideration be tabled and that there be further review to address his concerns. He also stated the
    potential of noise also is a concern. Commissioners noted they had received the email.
    Mr. Rodriguez stated the proposed building will be a single story building constructed of CMU block
    and finished with solid brick. A screening fence is proposed to surround the site. He stated the
    property slopes to the west and views should not be obscured He stated the plan has been reviewed
    by Engineering in order to address the wetlands and drainage, but the Commission could request a
    more intense review if they desire.
    Commissioner Safsten commented the new building would require new pipes which would be a
    benefit to the surrounding community. Chair Graf indicated the site plan shows chain link fencing
    with barbed wire which is not allowed in residential zones and should be revised.
    Mr. Sam Frankhauser, engineer for the project, stated the design of the building is engineered so that
    storm water is addressed. He stated both he and the owner are aware of challenges on the new
    property, and a system is in place to capture water to be routed to the storm sewer system. A new
    20” PVC pipe also will be installed. The building will resemble; the existing building, and will be
    constructed of CMU with a brick façade, both of which will serve to contain noise within the building.
    Commissioner Schade noted the homeowners would likely hear more noise from traffic on Harrison
    Boulevard at this location than from the proposed pump house, but there is a need for water to be
    adequately handled. Commissioner Safsten stated the newer building would have newer equipment
    and will likely be quieter than the existing building and the proposed 20” water line would improve
    water service.
    MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to approve the conditional use permit
            amendment based on the findings the proposed use will comply with regulations and

e. Special Use Permit, to allow duplex at 2020 Adams Avenue. (Attachment 2e)
  Mr. Rodriguez reported the applicant desires to use the existing home as a duplex and has applied
  for a special use permit as the nonconforming regulations allow reuse of historic buildings as multi-
  family when the underlying zone allows the number of dwelling units proposed. The property is
  zoned R-2EC and the building is identified as being eligible for nomination as a historic resource as
  part of the East Central Bench Historic District research when designated as a National Historic
  District. The Commission is to consider whether the change in use will be detrimental to the
  community, whether it is consistent with the General Plan and is conducive to the preservation of the
  historic building. He reported the lot area exceeds the standards of the R-2EC zone for a duplex,
  providing needed housing is consistent with the goals of the General Plan, and records indicate the
  building was likely originally constructed as a duplex. The layout of the interior would require
  extensive renovation to create a single-family home which could damage the historic fabric of the
  building. Staff recommendation is for approval.
  MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to approve the special use permit based
               on the findings it is not injurious or detrimental to community health, safety and welfare, is
               in keeping with the General Plan and will preserve an existing historic structure. Motion
               was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously with Commissioners
               Blaisdell, Safsten, Sandau, Schade and Graf voting aye.

4. Expansion of Nonconforming Use, to add additional floor to dwelling at 702 W. Ellis Street. (Attachment 4)
  Mr. Isaac Akers stated there is an existing nonconforming right and there are three existing units on the
  site. He occupies a single-family home and desires to construct an upper story to accommodate the
  needs of his family, creating a kitchen and more bedrooms. He agreed the existing second tenant
  space is to be eliminated. The footprint of the building will not be changed, but the intent is to build a
  second level of living space.
  Ms. Patrick indicated there is an existing nonconforming use certificate for this home, allowing two
  single-family homes on one lot. The current structure contains a basement level consisting of a

                                                                                             Page 4 of 14
  kitchen, living room, two bedrooms and a bathroom, and the main level contains a kitchen, living room,
  three bedrooms and a bathroom. The application is to add a second level which would contain a
  kitchen, laundry, family room and three bedrooms as well as an office. Because the lower unit is to be
  not legal, it is to be eliminated, and an interior stairway should connect the three levels to assure it
  continues to be used as a single-family home. She stated while most homes in the nearby area are
  single-family, the second story would not be out of character with the community. The home should
  have the appearance and use only as a single-family home and all building and zoning code concerns
  must be addressed. She felt the second stairwell and exterior entrance gives the appearance of a
  multi-family structure, stating the stairwells should be within the structure. Staff recommendation is for
  approval subject to the proposed floor plans being modified to better reflect the appearance and
  function as a single-family home and all departmental staff corrections be satisfied.
  Commissioner Safsten asked about how the home would fit with the neighborhood as most homes are
  single story. Ms. Patrick answered Ellis is an unimproved street, and the home is set back from the
  right-of-way so that its height would not be noticeable to neighbors. Chair Graf felt the second
  stairway does give the appearance as a multi-family home, and suggested the exterior entrance should
  be eliminated and any stairs connecting floors should be inside the structure.
  Mr. Travis Pate, 2546 Jefferson Avenue stated he felt the proposed plan appears practical, stating he
  felt it would be costly to re-engineer the design to have an interior stairway and felt it is not warranted.

  MOTION:       A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to approve the expansion of the

                is compliant with the definition of a single-family home and will improve the appearance
                and safety of the neighborhood and not be adversely affected by the expansion. Approval
                is subject to the floor plan being modified to assure better connectivity between floors and
                create the appearance as a single-family home, and all departmental staff requirements

8. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment, to reduce parking requirements for low to moderate
   income housing in all zones. (Attachment 8)
  Mr. Montgomery stated Ogden had conducted a parking study a few years ago which determined much
  parking was being under-utilized and that the City standard were too high. At that time, standards for

11.Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendments, for landscaping requirements and to clarify
   requirements for waterwise landscaping. (Attachment 11)
  Mr. Montgomery explained Utah is the second driest state, and while there have been drought cycles
  over time, these seem to be increasing in frequency. He reviewed water usage statistics from Ogden
  City, stating there are many needs for the use of water to sustain life and while residential use is
  minimal in comparison with other types of use, due to the increased number of residents, the amount of
  water used is a concern. While some types of water usage cannot be controlled, landscaping is one of
  the most visible and controllable parts of water management, with residential use increasing during the
  summer months. He presented information relating to anticipated growth of the area and State
  directives relating to existing drought conditions. He stated Ogden has been proactive in conserving
  water by building new storage facilities, replacing old pipes and meters. This action is an effort to
  update existing ordinances relating to water usage by limiting turf grass, identifying waterwise plants
  and create incentives to reduce wasted water. There are some misconceptions relating to xeriscaping
  as it is often viewed as being little living plant materials with no maintenance, but it is choosing the
  correct plant materials and reducing water usage for the particular soil. He stated while there is an
  initial cost for installation of weed barriers and plants, the cost in water usage is recovered within a few
  years. He reviewed each of the sections of the Code to be amended, stating particular attention is paid
  to park strips as they are often difficult to manage due to hard surface on two or more sides, and the
  limited amount of turf is often either over-watered or neglected. The City is partnering with Weber
  Basin Water in providing financial incentives to remove turf in park strips to be replaced with more
  waterwise materials. After further reviewing the proposed amendments, he recommended approval.
  Commissioner Schade asked about water usage for swimming pools as they are listed as a bonus in
  the group dwelling chapter. Mr. Montgomery stated these are classified the same as other amenities
  such as volleyball, playgrounds, etc. but stated there should be a requirement for pools to have a cover
  to reduce evaporation and loss of water. Commission asked about the usage of artificial turf. Mr.
  Montgomery answered it is already allowed so long as certain standards are satisfied, and this
  regulation is not proposing to change any of these. Chair Graf noted the Commission had held a work
  session and discussed these details in depth at that time, and he felt comfortable with the proposal
  based on that discussion.
