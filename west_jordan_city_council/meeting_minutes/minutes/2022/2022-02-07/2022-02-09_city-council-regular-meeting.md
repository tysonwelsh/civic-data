CALL TO ORDER
Chair Whitelock called the work session to order at 5:30 pm and read a declaration regarding
electronic meetings due to COVID-19.

A.     Veteran’s Memorial Park Master Plan
Council Member McConnehey explained the purpose of the discussion was to provide staff with
Council perspective regarding updates to the Veteran’s Memorial Park Master Plan and discuss
what the Council would like to see at the rodeo grounds and the park. He presented a proposed
timeline for the Veteran’s Memorial Park Master Plan update and showed the Master Plan for
Veteran’s Memorial Park that was approved in 2005.

Council Member McConnehey listed current event uses at Veteran’s Memorial Park: Western
Stampede; Memorial Day; Veterans Day; third-party special events; Demolition Derby; Monster
Truck Event; Small and Tall Ball. Chair Whitelock requested the Linda Butters Memorial 5k and the
4th of July parade be added to the list. Council Member Bloom spoke in support of continuing the
annual Memorial Day event, including a breakfast. Chair Whitelock asked if the Memorial Day
breakfast was something the Council would be willing to support with Council budget and staffing.
Council Member Pack spoke in favor of Council sponsorship with volunteer support. Council
Member Worthen said she felt the Council should take ownership of the Memorial Day breakfast.
Vice Chair Green spoke of plans for the 2022 Memorial Day event. Council Member McConnehey
suggested Council Office Director Alan Anderson coordinate with events staff regarding including a
breakfast with the Memorial Day event. Public Services Director Isaac Astill commented that events
staff were spread pretty thin but would do their best to work with the Council. Council Member
McConnehey requested details regarding what was planned and budget impacts for each event.

Vice Chair Green suggested adding a concert in the park to 4th of July/Western Stampede activities,
especially when the 4th of July fell on a weekday rather than a weekend. Council Member
McConnehey asked if the Council would be interested in adding a flag retirement ceremony to the
City Council Work Session Minutes – February 9, 2022                                             Page 2

list of annual events. Vice Chair Green, Chair Whitelock, and Council Member Jacob expressed
support.

Council Member McConnehey expressed a desire to get more and better use out of the rodeo arena.
Council Member Pack said he agreed with the idea of renting out the facility for events. City
Administrator Korban Lee said he would love to get more use out of the rodeo arena but commented
that renting the facility to third parties was a big drain on City staff and resources. He asked if the
Council wanted staff to create a budget to facilitate third-party events moving forward. Council
Member Jacob expressed support for a separate budget and said he would want rental fees to be
commensurate.

Council Member Worthen suggested reaching out to find year-round corporate sponsors for the
venue if year-round use was planned. Responding to a question from Council Member Bloom,
Council Member McConnehey said the Council had conversations in the past with sound technicians
who had indicated ways to reduce noise impact on surrounding neighborhoods. He suggested
incorporating plans for the rodeo arena into the Veteran’s Memorial Park Master Plan to facilitate
moving forward.

Mr. Astill commented that a small-scale concert in the arena would involve a $60,000 - $100,000
investment. Vice Chair Green said he knew Draper City encouraged corporate sponsorship to offset
event costs, with small benefits provided to the sponsors.

Council Member McConnehey suggested improving electrical infrastructure designed for food
trucks and other uses to reduce noise from generators. Council Member Worthen agreed. Vice Chair
Green suggested identifying grass areas at the park that could be replaced with something requiring
less water and maintenance.

Current recreational uses at Veteran’s Memorial Park were outlined: multiple playgrounds; baseball
fields; pavilion; tennis courts; museum; rodeo/4H activities; art facility (coming soon); volleyball;
and soccer. Council Member McConnehey asked the Council to consider whether the plan for
Veteran’s Memorial Park should include the rodeo area, or if the rodeo arena should have a separate
plan. He wanted to keep plenty of open-turf and passive recreational space at the park, but also
recognized the need to focus on water conservation. He suggested adding multi-use amenities that
would reduce the resources required.

Chair Whitelock asked if there were any recreational uses the Council wanted to remove from the
list, and there was no response from Council Members. Council Member Pack suggested adding an
animal rescue facility to the list. Council Member Worthen suggested adding a small skate park. She
spoke of the possibility of a local arts event and a splash pad. Vice Chair Green liked the idea of a
splash pad and suggested a small band stand or amphitheater. Council Member Jacob liked the stage
and splash pad ideas as well. He said he believed the museum, as it sat, was a waste of space at the
park. Council Member Bloom suggested the museum was incorporated more with the Western
Stampede event.

Council Member McConnehey requested a permanent set of flag poles at the rodeo arena before the
next rodeo and was willing to pay for the flag poles out of Council Contingency if needed. Council
Member Worthen suggested finding a sponsor for the flag poles. A majority of the Council indicated
approval for directing staff to obtain cost estimates. Vice Chair Green suggested flag poles should be
added at the Veteran’s Memorial for the U.S. Space Force and POW-MIA flags. A majority of the

   Council expressed agreement. Chair Whitelock said she had received requests from residents for
   both pickleball courts and a dog park.

   Council Member McConnehey suggested the Council consider how much active versus passive space
   should be included at Veteran’s Memorial Park. He asked the Council to consider water wise
   planning, maintenance requirements, ongoing reinvestment, and staffing as guiding principles for
   the Master Plan update. Council Member Jacob suggested that perhaps the Council could consider
   reinstituting a parks fee to protect City budget for parks.

   Council Member McConnehey asked if the Council would be in favor of staff looking into options to
   open up and integrate some of the separate areas of the park for improved connectivity, and no
   disagreement was expressed. All Council Members expressed agreement with a suggestion for the
   rodeo arena to be included as a subunit of the Veteran’s Memorial Park Master Plan.

   Council Member McConnehey gave a brief history of rodeo events in West Jordan. He emphasized
   that infrastructure plans needed to be considered and updated with the Master Plan update. Council
   Member McConnehey showed photographs of the current rodeo arena and facilities and pointed
   out areas for suggested improvement. He showed images of the Spanish Fork rodeo arena and
   facilities as a comparison.

   Council Member McConnehey asked the Council to discuss their vision for the rodeo arena moving
   forward. Chair Whitelock knew the equestrian park in South Jordan had experienced financial
   difficulties and she would want to see financial plans and ideas. Council Member Jacob said with
   creative financing he would love to see the arena become something better. Council Member Pack
   agreed, he felt the facility was underutilized and expressed support for enhancing the venue. Council
   Member Bloom was in favor of expansion. Vice Chair Green was not in favor of expanding the rodeo
   arena itself but would want it to be more useful. He said a good financing plan would be needed. He
   commented that some type of multi-use facility would be nice, perhaps made of steel or concrete.
   Council Member McConnehey wanted to see the facility grow. Chair Whitelock asked staff to bring
   back information about possible expansion/improvement.

   City Administrator Korban Lee commented that the City was currently moving forward with the
   arts facility. He said the request regarding the rodeo arena would require significant staff time and
   resources and asked for some prioritization from the Council. Chair Whitelock responded that the
   purpose of the discussion was to help staff understand the Council’s vision for purposes of setting a
   plan. She said she would want staff to provide an idea of how much it would cost to achieve the plan
   desired and feedback regarding what would not be feasible. Council Member McConnehey
   commented that it would be difficult to get sponsors without a plan. Mr. Astill said staff would work
   to put a plan together and provide numbers to the Council.

   Chair Whitelock adjourned the work session at 6:54 pm

4.    EXECUTIVE REPORTS TO COUNCIL
      a. Mayor’s Report
Mayor Dirk Burton spoke of the current Legislative Session, and invited the Council to join him as
he conducted a tour of the Big Bend project with mayors of surrounding cities. He spoke of a recent
opportunity to serve as a judge for the We the People Program at West Hills Middle School, and said
he was very impressed with the youth. The Mayor expressed appreciation for the teachers who had
helped get the youth where they were today.

       b. City Administrator’s Report
Public Services Director Isaac Astill reported work spaces for most personnel had been relocated in
preparation for the planned City Hall renovation. He said staff would share progress photos
throughout the project.

Public Works Director Brian Clegg provided an update regarding water line breaks and a pressure
regulator problem. He also reported on the 8600 South bridge project with a planned 18-month
construction schedule.

Community Engagement & Government Affairs Director Tauni Barker reported the City had
received 95% of ARPA matching grant funds for a project in the southwest quadrant. The remaining
5% would be received when the project was completed. She provided an update regarding the
current Legislative Session, and said an important issue in her opinion was local control versus state
control. Ms. Barker highlighted bills that would have an impact on West Jordan and asked for
feedback from the Council. Vice Chair Green said he would spend political capital on developer-
driven zoning issues, and suggested partnering with surrounding communities. Council Members
indicated agreement with the City remaining neutral regarding retail incentives. Ms. Barker said she
agreed with Vice Chair Green’s comments.

 5.    CITY COUNCIL STRATEGIC PLANNING (WORK SESSION)
Chair Whitelock said the objective of the strategic planning discussion was to put together a plan or
road map of where the Council would like to see the City in the future and where the Council wanted
to spend funds. Prior to the meeting, Council Members provided the following suggested goals:

        Airport Development                         Infrastructure
        Amenities                                   Leader
        Business Development                        Net Zero
        Commercial Mix                              Robust City Center
        Destination City                            Safe Community
        East/West Corridor                          Secondary Downtown
        Enhance Community through Fiber             Strong Sense of Community
        Environmentally Green Building              Transportation
        Inclusive City

Council Office Director Alan Anderson asked the Council to identify items they believed were
priorities for discussion first. When questioned, Council Member Jacob explained that his
“secondary downtown” suggestion meant that he would like to see West Jordan as a secondary
downtown area next to downtown Salt Lake City, and mentioned Seattle and Bellevue, Washington
as an example.

Council Member McConnehey asked for further clarification regarding the purposed of the meeting.
He commented that the City already had a General Plan and a Transportation Master Plan and asked
if the discussion was meant to define a new vision. Chair Whitelock responded that she wanted the
Council to lead with a plan to indicated Council desires for the City, with clear direction for staff. Mr.
Anderson referenced the request for goals from Council Members and noted the suggestion that
West Jordan become a destination city. He said the idea was to identify what elements of existing
plans needed to be enhanced or updated for Council goals to become reality.

Vice Chair Green said several ideas on the list appeared to be more overarching philosophies rather
than what the Council wanted for the City. Council Member Bloom said several of the suggested
City Council Minutes – February 9, 2022                                                        Page 3

ideas seemed to complement each other and could snowball together. She said for her, business
development was crucial because it impacted almost everything else.

The suggested goals were divided into two categories:

        Primary/General Philosophies/General Plan (Policy and Budget)
              Business Development             Destination City
              Transportation (study/money)     Strong Sense of Community/Safe/Inclusive
              Community                        Infrastructure
              Leader in the community          Robust City Center

        Secondary/Specific Goals
              Airport Development                 Amenities (including fiber)
              Commercial Mix                      East/West Corridor
              Energy Efficiency/Green

Council Members discussed a desire to improve business development processes in the City. Council
Member McConnehey pointed out much of what the Council had discussed was already stated in the
General Plan. Vice Chair Green said he believed there were things the Council could do to support
the General Plan policy-wise and budget-wise in terms of business development. Council Members
Bloom and Jacob expressed agreement.

Chair Whitelock said she believed becoming a destination city would require investment. She would
like to see an amusement park in West Jordan. Vice Chair Green suggested a policy stating that West
Jordan was a destination city, with the Council doing everything they could to make the City a
destination city. Chair Whitelock defined “destination city” as a place someone would go to take
advantage of the multiple amenities/businesses available. Council Member Pack said he believed it
took just as much energy to dream big as to dream small, and said he agreed with the goal for West
Jordan to be a destination city. He spoke of requests from residents in the last several years for a
skate park. He suggested highlighting and focusing on elements of existing plans to achieve the goals
discussed.

Chair Whitelock said she hoped the Council could set goals and determine where to spend money
to be proactive rather than reactive. Vice Chair Green said he agreed with establishing Council
priority goals and ideas to guide decisions going forward. Council Member McConnehey said he
would be supportive of adding something specifically about West Jordan being a destination city to
Chapter 3 of the General Plan.

Mr. Anderson shared responses from a citizen survey conducted in early 2021 regarding
transportation. He asked if the Council was interested in a policy requiring standardized concrete
fencing on arterial streets and standardized streetlights throughout the City. Chair Whitelock said
she was interested in such a policy. Council Member McConnehey commented that standardized
elements could be emphasized in the urban design portion of the General Plan.

Chair Whitelock suggested the Council schedule a separate work session to discuss the City Center.
A majority of the Council indicated approval. The Council discussed transportation goals. Council
Member Pack suggested networking with different entities and employing transportation
professionals. Council Member McConnehey suggested including money in the next budget to fund
an east/west transportation study. Council Members indicated agreement.

   Chair Whitelock said she believed the Council had indicated a desire for a strong sense of community
   and a safe community through funding for police and fire services. Council Member McConnehey
   mentioned the concept of crime prevention through environmental design and suggested asking
   staff to recommend changes to City Code pertaining to safety.

   Council Member McConnehey noted that the Council had already asked questions and the Mayor’s
   Office had already shared information regarding infrastructure. He suggested there was not more
   that needed to be said other than the request that the Mayor’s Office and staff continue to
   communicate with the Council.

   The Council and Mr. Anderson discussed what was involved with being a leader in the community.
   Council Member Jacob said he believed West Jordan had come leaps and bounds in the last four
   years in terms of relationships and reputation. Council Member McConnehey said he credited much
   of the change to administrative staff. Chair Whitelock suggested keeping a great team was a
   budgeting responsibility of the Council.

   A majority of the Council indicated a desire for an east/west transportation study to be included in
   the next budget. Council Member McConnehey suggested requesting a proposal from the Economic
   Development Director regarding what could be accomplished with existing funding. A majority of
   the Council indicated agreement. Vice Chair Green suggested requesting a cost estimate for an
   update of the Business License Code and application process. Council Member McConnehey said he
   would be interested to know what opportunities the City Council and staff would have with the new
   system in terms of connecting with businesses and residents.

   Mr. Anderson provided an update on the General Plan Committee. He said he anticipated presenting
   a draft of the General Plan to the Council before the end of the year. Responding to a question from
   staff, Council Member McConnehey expressed support for adding a Commercial Recreation Zone to
   the portion of the General Plan that addressed land use. A majority of the Council indicated support.

   Lists of new and unfinished business items were included with the agenda for review. Chair
   Whitelock commented that the City was working on negotiations with a fiber provider for West
   Jordan. Chair Whitelock reminded the Council of the upcoming Utah League of Cities and Towns
   Conference. The Council was scheduled to meet next on February 23, 2022.

    6.    ADJOURN
