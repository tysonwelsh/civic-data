                                              WORK SESSION
1.     REVIEW PROPOSED AMENDMENTS TO THE UTAH COUNTY CODE, CHAPTER 11:
       ‘LICENSING AND BUSINESS REGULATIONS’ RELATED TO SENATE BILL 81: ‘LOCAL
       GOVERNMENT LICENSING AMENDMENTS’ WHICH BECAME EFFECTIVE ON MAY 9, 2017
       (Continued from the May 30, 2017 Commission meeting)
(Track A, 34:10)
Peggy Kelsey of the Utah County Community Development Office explained State Legislature recently passed
Senate Bill 81, which has essentially made all licensing fees regulatory. Utah County fess have been regulatory
since 2005. The new bill also allows occasional businesses, and those belonging to owners under 18 years of age, to
be exempt from licenses and permits. Lastly, home offices will not be charged licensing fees if clients do not come
to the home. Ms. Kelsey suggested Utah County home businesses also be exempted from needing a business
license. For the approximate 115 licenses that would have been required to pay the annual $50 fee, the county
would lose $6,000 overall revenue. Ms. Kelsey noted home offices with visiting clients such as hair or dance
studios would still be charged, and the commissioners discussed setting a specific number of visitors per day – three,
for example – before a home business could no longer be exempt. They talked about required inspections, and Ms.
Kelsey mentioned a proposed ordinance would be submitted for adoption during the next meeting of the Board.


                                          PUBLIC COMMENTS
(Track A, 43:20)
Judy Salazar is a Lehi resident near the Murdock Canal Trail extension. Ms. Salazar noted the Board had considered
and denied the approval of a fence on her property at a previous meeting; she is here today to present additional
information for review. Ms. Salazar read an email from the previous owner of her house (a copy of which can be
found attached to these minutes) which stated “the trail representatives promised they would work with us on almost
any kind of buffer between the trail and the property.” She also read a letter from Scott Finlayson, the Thanksgiving
Village Home Owners Association (HOA) President at the time of negotiations, supporting Ms. Salazar’s comments
that Utah County assured a barrier would be placed between the homes and the trail (a copy of which can be found
attached to these minutes). Commissioner Lee asked Ms. Salazar whether she could provide a written agreement
regarding the fence, and Utah County Public Works Director Richard Nielson commented on being involved in
those negotiation conversations. When Ms. Salazar offered to provide copies of HOA meeting minutes reflecting
those discussions, Commissioner Graves expressed minutes can be written by anyone and do not necessarily assert
fact; he would need to see a contract obligating the county to provide a fence. Ms. Salazar stated the county should
not back out of business promises at their convenience, and Commissioner Lee explained at least two of the three
commissioners must agree in order for any action to be taken by the Board.
