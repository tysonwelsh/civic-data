                                  SPECIAL CITY COUNCIL
                                 WORK MEETING MINUTES
                                  Wednesday, October 19, 2022
                                  Approved December 14, 2022

The following are the minutes of the City Council meeting of the Herriman City Council. The meeting
was held on Wednesday, October 19, 2022, at 10:00 a.m. in the Herriman City Hall Fort Herriman
Conference Room, 5355 West Herriman Main Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting,
as required by law, was posted in the City Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members of the
Council, media, and interested citizens.

Presiding: Mayor Lorin Palmer

Councilmembers Present: Jared Henderson, Teddy Hodges, Sherrie Ohrn, Steven Shields

Staff Present: City Manager Nathan Cherpeski, Assistant City Manager Tami Moody, Assistant City
Manager Wendy Thomas, City Recorder Jackie Nostrom, Finance Director Kyle Maurer, Director of
Operations Monte Johnson, Public Works Director Justun Edwards, Communications Manager Jonathan
LaFollette, City Planner Michael Maloy, Deputy Director of Parks, Recreation and Events Anthony
Teuscher, Finance Director Alan Rae, Police Chief Troy Carr, Community Development Director Blake
Thomas, and Planning Manager Clint Spencer.

10:00 AM:
    1. Call to Order
Mayor Lorin Palmer called the meeting to order at 10:08 a.m.

    2. Administrative Reports
         2.1. Strategic planning discussion
City Manager Nathan Cherpeski led the discussion pertaining to the strategic plan and outlined the goals
for the meeting would be to identify the top strategies for The City. The goals would be formulated from
the process discussed to determine what success would look like. After goals have been formulated, staff
would return with the strategic plan and develop a tactical plan for accomplishments.

City Manager Cherpeski outlined the underlying assumptions and notated it needed to be financially
sustainable. The plan would help with the decision-making process and outline the level of service for the

community. He defined short-term goals would consist of a time frame from one-to-two years, mid-term
goals would have a life expectancy of two-to-five years and long-term goals would be anything over five
years.

City Manager Cherpeski offered a short anecdote of the strategic plan being a 20-mile march rather than
a sprint and wanted to focus initially on effectiveness then look to efficiency. Council and staff
participated in an interactive activity to help determine the top “whys” of what the city should be doing.
The top outcomes for goals included:
    • Basic Services - 16 votes
           o Water
           o Sewer
           o Public Safety
           o Power
           o Garbage
           o Roads
           o Parks/Open Space
    • Quality of Life - 10 votes
    • Fiscal – 7 votes
    • Community – 5 votes

City Manager Cherpeski asked what success looked like with basic services. The Council deliberated on
how to adequately measure strategies and concluded reliable services included being responsible,
sustainable, innovative, forward thinking, exceptional, communication/engagement for short-term vs.
long-term, and trust. They opined the quality of life included basic level of service before any
enhancements. The Council wanted to plan to budget for basic level of service and pay for projects if
additional funds were available.

The Council recessed the meeting temporarily.

                                 Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn was excused.

The Council reconvened the meeting at 12:04 p.m.

The quality-of-life discussion continued surrounding:
   • Services – provide reliable quality services to citizens; the City worries about it so the residents
      don’t have to
   • Streets – maintain and function appropriately
   • Public safety –adequate staff to do the job
   • Utilities-Basic services
   • Parks/Open Space – balancing the amount of open space and amenities
   • Events – focus on fewer exceptional events
