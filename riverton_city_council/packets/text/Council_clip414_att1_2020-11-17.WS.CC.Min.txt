33      Sanitation Presentation
34
35   Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, presented a Sanitation Comparison explaining
36   Strategic Priority #8, which is to promote fiscally responsible governance. He said the priority
37   could be accomplished by long-range planning and by establishing appropriate fee levels.
38
39   Mr. Hicks explained that on June 16, 2020 the City Council requested a high-level analysis on
40   various options regarding the sanitation fund, including an option for costs to self-provide. He
41   also said that direction given from the Auditor was to either make sure the Sanitation fund was
42   correctly treated as an enterprise fund (by eliminating the subsidy) or to no longer treat it as an
43   enterprise fund.
44
45   As part of their Review Process, Mr. Hicks explained that he had met with department heads to
46   project what departmental needs might be over the next ten years such as projects, staffing, etc.
47   He explained that, based on the FY 2020 budget, sales tax revenue had been updated and
48   projected based on current experience and the past five years’ trend. He reviewed the long-
49   range plan process, along with the sales tax budget and projections to be conservative, but not
50   to conservative.

29       1300 West Withers Lane - Newman Construction, Applicant
30
31   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that Newman Construction

33   acres located on 1300 West between Silcox Drive (12700 South) and Withers Lane (12800
34   South). He said the property is currently zoned RR-22, which is a single-family zone with animal
35   rights. He said the area has a variety of zoning designations; the golf-course to the south is
36   zoned Parks-Open Space (P-OS), as is the rodeo arena and City Park to the west. To the north
37   is Commercial Gateway for the small office buildings; to the southwest is more RR-22 zoning,
38   and to the east is The Vistas at Riverbend, a 65-unit development zoned RM-8, which is a multi-
39   family zone allowing up to 8 units per acre. He said the General Plan for the subject properties,
40   as set in the recent General Plan update, is Medium High Density Residential and Mixed Use,
41   which carries with it a 5 to 8 Dwelling Unit per acre designation. The Medium High Density
42   Designation corresponds to the City’s RM-6 and RM-8 Zones, so the requested RM-8 zone
43   matches the General Plan.
44
45   Mr. Lethbridge said that on October 8, 2020 the Riverton City Planning Commission held a
46   public hearing to review the proposed application from Newman Construction. The Planning
47   Commission voted to recommend approval of the proposed rezone and recommended the
48   following motion: I move the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 20-29 - Rezoning the subject
49   properties located near Withers Lane and 1300 West to RM-8-D-SD (RM-8-Downtown-Specific
50   Designation) with the following provisions:

 1       1. Single-family detached units only.
 2       2. Single-story units only.

43       Ordinance No. 20-26 - Riverton City is proposing changes to Sections 17.15.030(2)
44       and 18.215.020(10) allowing payment in lieu of dedication of water shares for new
45       development
46
47   Trace Robinson, Public Works Director, said that the public hearing was for a Zoning Text
48   Change proposed by Riverton City. He then explained that for many years the City required
49   dedication of secondary water shares from residential and commercial developments. He said
50   the proposed change would allow the City to accept a payment of a fee in lieu of the dedication
51   so that the City could purchase water shares from which ever canal that would balance the
     Page | 8 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     November 17, 2020



 1   City’s secondary water system supply and demand. Presently, and for many years, he
 2   explained that the City had accepted water shares from any of the four canals running through
 3   the city. The location of the canals and the city pumps create pressure zones. Water can be
 4   transferred from an upper zone (on the west side of the city) to a lower zone (on the east side)
 5   by gravity, but he said that moving water from east to west was more difficult. He said the
 6   proposed Zone Text Change would allow the City to accept a fee in lieu of water share
 7   dedication, so the City could then purchase shares to meet specific needs and system design
 8   objectives. He said that Staff recommended the Planning Commission forward a
 9   recommendation to approve the proposed changes.
10
11   Mr. Lethbridge said that on October 8, 2020, the Planning Commission held a public hearing to
12   review the proposed text amendment; and, following the hearing, the Commission voted to
13   recommend approval of the text amendment.
14
15   In response to Councilmember Stewart, City Attorney Ryan Carter said the City would always
16   take water shares unless they were at capacity and then the City would take a fee in lieu, which
17   would be based upon an engineer’s assessment as to how the demand was in that particular
18   zone, so it is very zone specific. He said that if it was already decided that there was the
19   capacity to meet all of the water needs in a particular zone, a fee in lieu would be accepted that
20   would be based upon the market value of the share at the time of dedication. That money could
21   be used to purchase water shares in other zones where the water was needed. He further
22   explained that when a fee in lieu was accepted, it would be specific to the zone where the
23   development was occurring.
24
25   Mayor Staggs opened a Public Hearing and called for public comment; there being none, he
26   declared the Public Hearing closed.
27
28   Councilmember Stewart MOVED that the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 20-26 -
29   Approving changes to Sections 17.15.030(2) and 18.215.020(10) allowing payment in lieu
30   of dedication of water shares for new development and adding language to the annual
31   review or more frequently as determined by City Staff or the Council. Councilmember
32   McCay SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion; there being
33   none, he called for a roll call vote and the vote was as follows: Councilmembers Buroker-Yes,
34   McCay-Yes, McDougal-Yes, Stewart-Yes, and Wells-Yes. The motion passed unanimously.

22       Ordinance No. 20-28 - Riverton City is proposing changes to Chapter 18.135 regarding
23       the required distance separating agricultural animals from other land uses
24
25   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, said that the public hearing for a Zoning Text
26   Change proposed by Riverton City. He explained that the Zone Text Change was designed to
27   eliminate an ambiguity and potential ordinance conflict in the existing code. He said the conflict
28   was identified when the Legal Department was asked to investigate a complaint about animals
29   in an RR-22 zone. The RR-22 Zone comes with animal rights and includes requirements for
30   where animals may be kept in relation to residential structures. General Regulations 18.135.090
31   Animals and Fowl RR-22 Zone 18.25.110 (2) Farm Animals: No agricultural animals shall be
32   kept or maintained within 75 feet of any dwelling nor shall any such animal be kept or
33   maintained within 40 feet of any zone district boundary that prohibits such use. No coop or
34   shelter for keeping of agricultural fowl shall be constructed or maintained within 75 feet of a
35   dwelling nor 40 feet of a zone district boundary which prohibits such use. Keeping of Farm
36   Animals: No agricultural animals shall be kept within 40 feet of any residential dwelling. No
37   structure for the keeping of agricultural animals or fowl shall be constructed or maintained within
38   40 feet of a residential dwelling. He said that Staff recommended adjusting the ordinance to
39   make all set-backs a consistent 40-ft from a residential dwelling to agriculture animals or animal
40   structures.
41
42   Mr. Lethbridge said that on September 10, 2020, the Planning Commission held a public
43   hearing to review the proposed text change; and, at that meeting, the Commission voted to
44   recommend approval of the text amendment.
