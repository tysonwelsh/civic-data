outlined what those changes were.  He said these changes need to come before 
the Planning Commission.

Planner Strader said this is a request to divide Lots 6 and 7 of the Silver Creek Village 
Center into 55 lots.  The lots are intended to be used for both single family dwellings 
and townhomes.  The Silver Creek Village Center was approved in 2015 through a 
Development Agreement (DA).  It contains 244 acres.  It was approved for 1,290 
residential units as well as 50,000 square feet of commercial through the Specially 
Planned Area (SPA) process.  The DA lays out the standards of approval.  There are 
some lots on the plat that will be further subdivided in the future.     
 
A copy of the proposed subdivision plat was displayed.  The townhome lots were 
identified.  Planner Strader said there will be 29 lots for detached single family 
dwellings.  She said that Staff evaluated the workforce housing requirements.  There is a 
requirement that 35% of the first 271 units shall be workforce units.  After that, each 
development shall provide 25% as workforce units.   
 
The first part (of the 35% of the 271 units) was satisfied through the Lot 9 subdivision, 
which is the Mountainlands Development.  Taking this subdivision into account, there 
are a total of 282 lots that will be created, equaling 11 lots greater than the 271 target.  
That means this subdivision will need to provide 25% of the additional units as 
workforce housing units.   
 
Planner Strader said the DA allows accessory dwelling units to count as workforce 
units, if the lots are intended to be used for detached single family dwellings and have a 
square footage greater than 4,500 square feet.  The applicant is proposing these will be 
built on Lots 9, 14, and 55.  That will satisfy the workforce housing requirement.

Director Putt said the last time the Development Code was substantially reworked was 
in 2004.  The tools are no longer current.  He added that the land should be zoned for

Director Putt said there are 15 general neighborhood areas within the General Plan.  
Within those areas are six locations that have been identified as mixed-use areas.  He 
pointed out where those areas are.  An option would be to incrementally rezone some 
of the areas to community commercial.  As another option, Staff created two mixed use 
zones.  These are the neighborhood mixed use (NMU) I and II.  At this meeting, they will 
only talk about the NMU-I.   
 
Director Putt said they are looking at smaller pieces of property in some areas.  This 
may range from 2 to 20 acres and may be inside or very close to an established area 
such as Kimball Junction.  In those areas, the objective would not be to acquire large 
amounts of open space.  Consideration would be given to how well these properties 
would fit into the neighborhood.  In those cases, connectivity and the management of 
traffic would be valued.   
 
Director Putt said the purpose of the NMU-I zone is to create new development areas 
that are compact, higher density, mixed use, pedestrian friendly, transit oriented, and 
performance based.  Density shouldn’t be feared, if it is in the right place and in the 
right form.  It can make affordability work.  Green infrastructure would work well. 
Mixed use areas will have residential, commercial, civic, and recreation areas.  This type 
of planning considers the human aspect first and not the automobile.  The areas will be 
transit oriented.  He said that transit is more than just big buses.

The NMU zone will have the ability to create incentives.  It could provide opportunities 
for people to get a little more in exchange for something else.  An example may be more 
building height for a workforce housing project in exchange for underground parking.  
He said the NMU-I zone puts more focus on public areas.     
 
Director Putt outlined who would be eligible for the NMU-I zone.  In order to be 
rezoned, the property would need to be identified in the land use map as being in a 
mixed use area.  Those areas have been identified on the map.  These zones are RR, CC, 
Neighborhood Commercial, or the Town Center.  The property would have to be 
adjacent to an existing commercial mixed use, institutional, or civic area.   
 
Director Putt explained the process.  Rezoning would go through the existing legislative 
process or it could also through a Master Planned Development process.  This would 
assign the density of the project.  This could be figured through site coverage and 
setbacks.  Another way to determine density would be to use the floor area ratio (FAR).  
He explained what that is to the Commission.  There will be an open space requirement.   
 
The NMU-I zone would have performance standards.  There would have to be a 
minimum of three uses within the project.  The uses could be residential, commercial, 
office, recreation, or civic.  A limitation on direct retail would be established.  It would 
be capped at 45,000 square feet.  He said about 18 months ago, the FAR was tested with 
five local design groups.  The Gilmor and the Lincoln Station properties were used as 
the test parcels.   
 
Director Putt said Staff is suggesting language that would prohibit single story

Director Putt said the NMU-I would most likely use the Master Plan Development 
(MPD) process.  Staff is proposing that SPAs are no longer created.  This was a zoning 
tool that was negotiation based, not measurement based.  With a SPA, the property 
began with a very low threshold of development and went to a significant amount of 
development.  The density was determined through the process.   
 
The NMU process has two parts:   
 First, it is a development entitlement process.  After an application is filed, a 
series of steps must be taken.  After that, there is an outcome or a decision.  A 
CUP and an LIP is an entitlement process.   
 Secondly, it is a design review and flexibility tool.  It allows the design of the 
project to be reviewed.  Under certain conditions, some flexibility could exist in 
the standards.  There could be a measured exchange for community benefit.     
 
Director Putt said the Master Plan process will be required if someone requests to 
rezone to a NMU-I, or any commercial project that is 10,000 SF or more of building area.  
It would also be used for residential subdivisions of more than four lots.  He said a pre-
application process occurs at Staff level, including a review of the project.  The Planning 
Commission will make a recommendation.  The County Council takes the final action.   
 
The density allowed in the existing zone is all that would be permitted; however, there 
may be an incentive for something like workforce housing.  Setbacks and building 
heights are established by the zone, but could be altered by the County Council or the 
Planning Commission.  The minimum lot size could be reduced.  Open space and 
parking would be discussed.
