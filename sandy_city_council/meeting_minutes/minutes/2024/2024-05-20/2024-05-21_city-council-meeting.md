General Citizen Comment Period (No earlier than 6:00 PM)
Council Chair Ryan Mecham invited the public to participate in General Citizen Comment.
Mr. James Longstaff expressed concerns with the proposed NHL practice facility that is
planned for the Sandy mall area. He was concerned about the zoning of the area and the
impact on traffic and parking.
Ms. Jenica Wilcox expressed support for the Sandy Club and the importance of the club
to the families and community who use the club. On behalf of several parents who have
children at the club and could not attend this evening's meeting, she mentioned that the
parents have created a community and a village that support each other. That support
helped her and her children leave an abusive relationship.
Ms. Sharon Jensen expressed support for continued funding of the Sandy Club. She grew
up in Historic Sandy and works in the Jordan School district. Money spent on prevention
pays back again and again. The money spent on these children gets them through
school, teaches them skills and has a positive impact on the kids and their families. My
grandkids go there now and the club also provides amazing counseling services for the
kids and their families.
Mr. Dave Egelund expressed gratitude for the presentation on renewable energy. He
agreed with Council Member Cyndi Sharkey. He is not in favor of a city-wide fee to pay for
the program and suggested that the voters should decide. He also expressed support for
and additional staff person for the Animal Services department. It is a need.
Council moved to Item 3 on the Agenda.

City Council Meeting Minutes May 21, 2024
1. 24-190 Special Recognition of the Sandy Youth Council 2023-24 Program Year
Attachments: SYC 2024 End of Year
Liz Theriault and Chris Edwards, Program Advisors for the Sandy Youth Council
presented the year in review of the 2023-24 Sandy Youth Council Program. The Sandy
Youth Council has 24 members and represents 10 different high schools in the valley.
They provided over 600 volunteer hours of community service to the city. Through the
program, the SYC learned about the Sandy Justice Court, Economic Development,
Council policy, and the importance of civic engagement. They participated in the
state-wide youth council day at the Capitol and took tours of the America First Field and
the Hale Centre Theatre. The graduating seniors were acknowledged by the Council and
the Council congratulated the SYC members for participating in the program.
Informational Items
2. 24-189 Council Member Dekeyzer requesting the Council receive a presentation
on the Utah Community Renewable Energy Program
Attachments: Overview — Utah Renewable Communities Program (Sandy)
Council Member Dekeyzer introduced Christopher Thomas with Salt Lake City who
provided an overview of Utah Renewable Communities. Utah Renewable Communities is a
coalition of 18 communities partnering with Rocky Mountain Power to design a new
renewable energy program. He reviewed details related to the program, participating
communities, financial impact, potential pathway for new communities, program timeline,
and goals. Council questions and comments followed. Council thanked Mr. Thomas for
his presentation and information.
Council moved to General Citizen Comment.
3. 24-192 The Sandy Club presenting the Council with their Annual Update
Attachments: Sandy Club Council Presentation
Shalome Orton and Luke Bradley provided a year in review of the Sandy Club, A Safe
Place for Boys and Girls. They reviewed the importance of providing after school care for
school aged children and the positive and life long impact the Sandy Club has on its
members. Research shows the impact of providing after school care has the long term
benefits of increasing kids future earnings, improving their performance in school, and
reducing crime and juvenile delinquency. The Sandy Club provides many important
services to students and their families: supervised activities, tutoring, counseling, food
support, clothing and school supplies, summer programming, STEM learning
opportunities, family events, and holiday assistance. They also provided the Council with
a breakdown of the club's funding and expenses and expressed gratitude to the Council
and Administration for their continued and long-term support of the Sandy Club and
community. Council thanked Ms. Orton and Mr. Bradley for their service and commitment
to the community.
Council moved to Item 4 on the Agenda.

4. 24-186 Noal Bateman Committee requesting Council consideration of a
Resolution both honoring the Award and establishing the City Council as
the body overseeing the Award Program.
Attachments: Resolution 24-19C - Noal Bateman Resolution.pdf
John Winder and Tracy Cowdell, the Chair and Vice Chair of the Noal Bateman
Committee, presented Resolution 24-19C to the Council. The resolution establishes the
Sandy City Council as the body overseeing the award program and provides oversight and
staff assistance to the Noal Bateman Committee. The Noal Bateman Award is the
highest honor bestowed on a Sandy resident and was first established in 1986. Mayor
Zoltanski is supportive of moving the oversight of the program to the City Council and
made the request to the committee. Council questions and comments followed. Council
thanked Mr. Winder and Mr. Cowdell for their presentation and expressed support of the
resolution. This was an information item. The Council will vote on the resolution at a future
council meeting.

City Council Meeting Minutes May 21, 2024

5. 24-191 Administration, City Council Office, Justice Court, City Attorney's Office,
Community Development Department, and Non-Departmental presenting
an overview of their FY 2024-25 tentative department budgets.

Attachments: May 21 Department Budget Presentations

Budget presentations:
Melisse Stiglich, Justice Court Administrator, provided an overview of the Sandy Justice
Court budget. She reviewed the significant savings that resulted from holding virtual
hearings. Those savings were reallocated and utilized for upgrades to court technology
and increased signage. She reviewed the financial impact of new state-wide mandates
that will increase costs to the court budget in FY25. As a result, Ms. Stiglich requested a
reallocation of funds from travel to professional services to hire interpreters and pro temp
judges and also requested to add a position for an assistant court administrator. The
associated costs for these requests will be absorbed internally and no increased funding
was requested. Council Member Sharkey acknowledged the Justice Courts
accomplishments and efficiencies with respect to the budget. Council questions followed.
Martin Jensen, Deputy CAO provided an overview of the following departments and budget
requests: Mayor, Administration, Non-Departmental. He spoke about the
accomplishments of the Communications and Community Arts and Emergency
Management Departments. The Sandy Arts Guild and Sandy Amphitheater received the
Best of State Award. Mr. Jensen also spoke about the city's commitment to maintaining
and providing the highest level of service while operating on the same level of funding. The
FY2025 budgets have been adjusted only for inflationary costs of services and supplies.
Council questions followed.
Lynn Pace, City Attorney presented the department budget requests for the following
divisions: Legal, City Recorder, and Risk Management. Budget requests included
adjustments for inflationary line items and increased insurance costs for liability
insurance and claims, property insurance, and worker's comp. He also noted that there
was currently no allocation in the FY 25 budget for an Administrative Law Judge.
Dustin Fratto, Council Executive Director, provided an overview of the Council Department
budget for FY 25. Budget requests included a reclassification, inflationary adjustments,
and increases related to professional services.
James Sorensen and Brian McCuiston with Community Development provided an
overview of the department's Fiscal Year 2025 budget requests which included costs for
employee compensation, training, uniforms, and software upgrades. They requested
$125,000 for the implementation of the General Plan through the rewrite of the
development code. They also reviewed department savings and efficiencies in the past
year. Council questions followed.
Shane Pace, Chief Administrative Officer thanked the Council for hearing all the
department budget presentations and for their feedback and discussion this evening.

Sandy. The President of Macey's praised Sandy City and spoke about how great

Beautification Day and helped plant trees in Willow Creek Park. About 30 volunteers

Sandy Service Ambassadors. She thanked Police for hosting their annual police banquet

and the emergency management meeting with Amy DeNeff. She worked with the Sandy
Youth Council planting flowers during Sandy Beautification Day and she acknowledged
Parks staff for holding the Family Skate Night at Lone Peak Park and for giving out free
safety helmets to the kids. Jazz in the Park was also a great event.
Council Member Mecham thanked Police for hosting their annual police banquet and
congratulated Police on their many awards. The CDBG Committee is on break for the

was one of the smoothest openings in Sandy. He spoke about the Healthy Communities
Road Show sponsored by the Utah Foundation - a great organization. He recently toured
the Purple Air Facility located in Draper with the Deputy CAO, Martin Jensen. He will
bring more information to the Council on a potential partnership. The police banquet was
a wonderful event. He provided updates from PUAB committee meeting and the Sego Lily

Council Member D'Sousa on an Accessory Dwelling Units proposal and will bring it to the
Council at a future meeting.

recently attended with the South Valley Chamber in Washington DC. It is her second
year attending this conference and she recommended the council members consider
participating in the conference next year. The conference offers a great opportunity to
work with our entire Utah congressional delegation as well as advocate for our small and
large businesses in the city. She participated in Sandy Beautification Day and worked
with volunteers at Storm Mountain Park. June 6th is the 80th Anniversary of D Day. She
mentioned that her father is buried in Arlington Cemetery. She recommended the
documentary, The Girl Who Wore Freedom, which tells the story of D Day through the
lens of the people set free from German occupation. It is a story of gratitude and honors
the bravery and service of our World War II veterans.
