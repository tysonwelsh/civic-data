                              2020 Magna Budget Presentation

            Mr. Greg Schulz read the following statement:

      “Council Members and to the public here today, welcome to the presentation of
     the 2020 Magna budget. Per directive, the budget will be posted to the Magna
     Metro Township website under budgets for public view on or before October 25,
     2019. Copies of the budget spreadsheet sheets will be posted to the website and
     will be made available to the public during the budget workshops and during the
     final budget adoption process. The budget hearing and adoption date has yet to
     be determined and is dependent upon the adoption of the MSD budget by the
     MSD. Pursuant to state law, this budget is structurally balanced – a structurally
     balanced budget for 2020. By directive of the state auditor, the budget shows all
     revenues and expenses to include additional years forecasted to determine
     possible budget shortfalls. As our budget is primarily an administrative budget for
     administrative costs of the metro township, the budget shows the estimated pass
     through funding that will be sent to the MSD to pay for services being rendered by
     that organization, primarily public works operations, public works engineering,
     municipal parks operation and maintenance, planning and development services,
     justice courts, and animal control. Of the six services listed, five are performed for
     Magna through interlocal agreements between the MSD, Salt Lake County, and
     Magna. As of October 1, 2019, planning and development services became an in-
     house service of the MSD. This budget does not request a property tax or fee
     increase. As a metro township and as a member of a municipal type services
     district, Magna cannot impose a property tax per state law. Also, the MSD is not

       imposing a property tax for 2020. It is also important to note, Magna property
       owners have not paid a property tax to fund municipal type services since 2016.”

He also delivered a presentation showing a quick overview for 2019. Some of the 2019 strategic
highlights included the creation of a long-range strategic planning committee, the Community
Reinvestment Agency of Magna and the possible transfer of Magna’s redevelopment areas to
Magna, and the continued attempt to get a seat on the Inland Port Authority. Financial highlights
looking ahead to 2020, included local option sales tax and gross taxable sale notifications from
the State. Operating highlights included the transition of planning and development to the Greater
Salt Lake Municipal Services District (GSLMSD), as of October 1. The GSLMSD is working on
improvements to reduce costs and processing time, and it has started the code enforcement
process improvements. Looking ahead, it needs to look for cost effective and efficient delivery of
some municipal services, but it is seeing better detail on invoices from Salt Lake County. Another
challenge will be the outdated municipal infrastructure that needs repair or replacement.
Research also needs to be done to determine the economics of where things are now and where
they will be in 2020, considering economists are predicting a recession. This budget is very
conservative, so the Council should be able to work within the constraints. Going forward, he
hoped to have the budget to the Council on Friday and post it after that to the website. Budget
workshops will be held November 12th and 26th. The final budget hearing will be done in
December, once the GSLMSD adopts its budget creating the appropriation to the Magna Metro
Township.

Unified Fire Authority (UFA)

                Council Member Hull stated the UFA is working on its revised and restated
interlocal agreement with the agencies of the UFA. In order to vote on a fiscal year tentative or
final budget or to adopt any budget amendment, any two board members representing separate
parties can call for a weighted vote. The weighted vote would be based on two things, each
getting 50 percent of the weight: 1) a member fee percentage calculated by computing the
percentage each entity is paid for the services in comparison to the total member fees, and 2) a
population percentage calculated by amenities population compared to the total population.

Letter to the Redevelopment Agency of Salt Lake County requesting the transfer of
redevelopment areas located in Magna to the Community Reinvestment Agency of Magna

               Paul Aston reviewed the letter, which starts the formal process to transfer the
redevelopment areas located within the Magna Metro Township to the Community Reinvestment
Agency of Magna, pursuant to Utah Code § 17C-1-205. This is the first step that needs to be
taken, just as was done with the transition of the Planning and Development Division to the
Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (GSLMSD). He has discussed this with Salt Lake
County Mayor Jennifer Wilson and others from the County.

              Council Member Pierce stated when Mr. Ashton met with the County, he said the
Magna Metro Township would like to take over new projects, but the old projects would remain
with the County.

                Mr. Ashton stated some redevelopment areas were established under the
Redevelopment Agency of Salt Lake County (RDA), such as Magna Main Street and Arbor Park.
Ideally, he would like those transitioned to the Magna Metro Townships’ agency as well; however,
the Council would then have to contract with someone to manage them. He recommended
starting the request for proposal (RFP) process, and if the County’s RDA is the best agency to do
the job, that will be who the Magna Metro Township will contract with.

              Council Member Pierce stated if the Magna Metro Township Council is the
Community Reinvestment Agency of Magna, she did not think it had the expertise to run that
aside from what it is doing now.

               Mr. Ashton stated the Council can do an RFP to get the expertise it needed to
take care of things. It would contract with that agency just as it contracts with the GSLMSD for
planning and development, and public works services. Sending the letter is the first step; the next
step would be to have a meeting of the Community Reinvestment Agency of Magna to talk about
more details. The question has been raised what the County’s RDA has done with the funds
raised for Magna Metro Township projects. He was committed to finding that out and making
sure everything has been properly accounted for.

                Council Member Pierce stated it was her understanding the letter today is
essentially to put the County on notice so it will negotiate with the Magna Metro Township.

              Mr. Ashton stated two weeks ago, the Council sent the County a letter saying it
was interested in this matter. The letter was based on the interlocal agreement between the
GSLMSD and the County, which was a 90-day process. This process is better, in that it will give
the Magna Metro Township more control over what is being done in Magna.

              Council Member Pierce asked if the Magna Metro Township would contract with
the County’s RDA to manage the projects it is already doing.

                  Mr. Ashton stated yes. Right now, the Magna Metro Township does not have the
ability or staffing to take over everything by itself.

                 Council Member Pierce stated she was concerned with the Council going down
this route for that reason. If it could not find someone capable of fulfilling what it needed done, all
it would have done is mess up the relationship with the County. Then, if nothing worked out, it
would have to default back to the County anyway.

                Council Member Peel stated he disagreed. He thought the County would have
accountability to the Magna Metro Township if it contracted with it.

             Council Member Hull stated at this point, the County is so large and has so many
redevelopment areas that the Magna Metro Township is getting lost in the shuffle. For example,

there has been a redevelopment area on Magna Main Street for years, but very little has been
done.

               Mr. Schulz stated that redevelopment area began in the late 1980s. This allows
the Council to have oversight if someone is not doing the job the Council feels it should be doing.
Right now, the Council does not have that ability.

              Council Member Peel stated the County is getting 5.5 percent annually from the
Magna Metro Township for administrative costs to manage redevelopment areas. He wondered
if the Magna Metro Township got that percentage in return from the County.

               Mr. Schulz stated the management costs that were built into the redevelopment
area projects in the Magna Metro Township was 5.5 percent of the total increment.

              Mr. Ashton stated it might cost the Magna Metro Township that much anyway, but
it would be making the decisions.

               Council Member Peel stated the Magna Metro Township will have to pay an
administrator, but it will have accountability and transparency.

               Mr. Schulz stated the 2019 budget adopted by Salt Lake County for its
redevelopment agency shows some increment coming in, but it also shows expenses that exceed
the amount of that tax increment. If the funds went directly to the Community Reinvestment
Agency, the Magna Metro Township Council could see where the money went and whether the
5.5 percent for administration costs was enough or not.

               Council Member Peel stated he wanted it on record that the Magna Metro
Township Council was not trying to burn bridges with the County’s RDA, but wanted to hold it
accountable, and he wanted the Magna Metro Township Council to have a say in what was
happening in its township so it could be accountable to its citizens.

               Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to approve
sending the letter to the Redevelopment Agency of Salt Lake County. The motion passed 3 to 1,
with Council Member Pierce voting in opposition to the motion.
