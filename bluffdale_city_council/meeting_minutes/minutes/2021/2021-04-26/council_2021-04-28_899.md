Society, the County Planning Department, and County Legal Department to update the ordinances
that are currently vague. Mr. Knight presented a draft of the new ordinance showing the proposed
changes.

Mr. Knight presented various specific updates and additions. He explained that the minimum
qualifications for a Professional Engineer or Geologist are lacking in the current ordinance and

Mr. Kartchner explained that many businesses in Bluffdale do not participate in unemployment
insurance and are not included in the statistics presented. The gross number of businesses has
grown each year for the past three years. The growth was attributed to the Council's economic
development efforts over the past eight years. It was noted that the City's commercial districts are
growmg.

Data from 2018 was presented. There are currently a total of 263 businesses within the City that
report to the Department of Workforce Services ("DWFS"). The types of businesses were
described with the largest being construction-related. That was believed to be indicative of the
lack of service-oriented businesses. Data from September 2020 showed overall growth from 263
to 311 businesses. Construction-related businesses rose at a lower rate than other businesses.

Tools and ways to provide incentives for more service-oriented businesses were introduced. The
following three general tools were identified:

        1.     There are two areas of the City consisting of the Eastern Bluffdale Economic
               Development Area ("EDA") and the Jordan Narrows EDA, which collect tax
               increment that can be used for incentives.

       2.      The General Fund can also be used to provide incentives.

       3.      Reduce impact fees.

Mr. Kartchner explained that the sole purpose of the EDA is to create incentives. In the past,
Bluffdale has built infrastructure with development. EDA funds can be used for roads, water
systems, fire stations, and more to enhance an area's ability to bring business in. Past projects have
been successful in the areas where it has been applied. Several current project examples were
provided such as the storm water project and reimbursement for completion of new pavement.
Another option for an EDA is to pay fees on behalf of a company or business. It can also provide
direct incentives.

There are several restrictions for EDA incentives. It is geographical in nature and must be within
the boundaries or directly benefit the EDA area and the Redevelopment Agency ("RDA") Board
must approve it. These particular EDAs are also tied to base real estate value increases and jobs.
Their focus cannot be on sales tax generation.

Mr. Kartchner explained that many entities across the nation are using General Fund money to
provide incentives for businesses. Examples include abatement or a reduction in property taxes,
and abatement or rebate of sales tax. The benefit of such General Fund incentives is that they are
flexible. Things that can affect the General Fund include zoning changes that impact revenue. A

Administrative Services Director, Bruce Kaiichner, addressed the Council's inquiry regarding the
compensation of city council members and mayors from other cities in Salt Lake and Utah
Counties. He found that the minimum annual compensation for council members is $6,000 and
the maximum is $36,650. Larger cities tend to pay more but there is a more significant range in
compensation in smaller to mid-sized cities.

Mr. Kartchner reported that the Bluffdale City Council currently participates in a 401(k) plan, with
the ability to contribute individually. The Utah Retirement System ("URS") level for elected
officials is $13,152. Therefore, if the Council wishes to be eligible to participate in URS, salaries
would need to be raised to exceed $13,152.

In terms of mayoral compensation, the broad range between $12,485 and $146,578. Part-time
mayoral compensation ranges from $ 12,485 to $115,394. Mr. Kartchner conunented that in
several cities, mayoral responsibilities include those of the Chief Administrator/City Manager,
running the day-to-day operations of the city. He noted that mayoral responsibilities in small cities
compared to large cities are not very different, but smaller cities tend to pay less.

In response to an inquiry regarding how Bluffdale compares to surrounding cities, Mr. Kartchner
reported that council members in Riverton are paid $14,548 annually. In Draper they are paid
$23,400, in Midvale City, they are paid $16,000, and in Murray City, they are paid $15,000.
Bluffdale currently pays its Council Members $7,200 annually. It was noted by Mr. Reid that
Riverton wanted to ensure that their council members and mayor were eligible for URS and set
their pay accordingly.

Mr. Kartchner provided an overview of the budget process and explained that they are required to
go through the process as mandated by State law. The City is required to pass a Tentative Budget
by early May. The Tentative Budget is the staiiing point for fu1iher discussions and changes are
included in the Final Budget, which must be passed in June. He noted that the Tentative Budget
does not include increases in compensation for the Council or the Mayor.

If the Council decides to implement a property tax increase, the timeline would change. The Final
Budget must be passed by June 20; however, if a tax increase is requested, the Council would pass
a Tentative Final Budget on June 20 and then hold a Truth in Taxation hearing in August to allow
citizens to comment on the proposed tax increase.

Once the Tentative Budget is passed, changes will be brought back to the Council for further
discussion. They will also prepare a Final Budget Adjustment in the last meeting in June before
the final vote.
