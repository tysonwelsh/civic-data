Section 04.060. Criteria for Approval.
No amendment to this Code, or the Zoning District Map(s), may be recommended for approval by the Commission
nor approved by the City Council unless such amendment or conditions thereto are consistent with the General Plan.
In considering a Code amendment, or Zoning District Map(s) amendment, the applicant shall identify, and the City
Staff, DRC, Commission, and the City Council may consider the following factors, among others:

    1.   The effect of the proposed amendment on the character of the surrounding area;
    2.   Consistency with the goals and policies of the General Plan.
    3.   Consistency and Compatibility of the proposed zone with the General Plan land uses of nearby and adjoining
         properties.
    4.   The suitability of the properties for the uses requested and their suitability for the existing uses identified by
         the General Plan;
    5.   Whether a change in the uses allowed for the affected properties will unduly affect the uses, or proposed uses
         for nearby and adjoining properties;
    6.   The gain to the public health, safety and welfare from the existing classification to the proposed amendment;
         and
    7.   The overall community benefit of the proposed amendment.


  REPORT ANALYSIS

Lehi City requests review and recommendation of proposed amendments to Chapters 12A and 12B related to grading
and landscaping. Two separate concerns led to this proposal.

Drainage and Landslide Concerns
The first concern relates to grading taking place for homes or landscaping after the mass grading. Several areas in
Traverse Mountain have had issues with land movement from a higher elevation lot to a lower elevation lot. This often
happens after rainstorms or other water events do not drain properly and compromise the soil or retaining walls.
Planning, engineering, parks and building staff have discussed potential solutions for a few months. A local developer
that serves on the Traverse Mountain HOA board also provided some input and direction. The proposed amendment
reflects this combined effort to solve this problem.

The proposed amendment includes defining what engineering measures are required if someone wants to build on a
30 percent or greater slope. This includes a geotechnical report and comprehensive retaining wall plan.

The amendment also includes additional requirements for any grading permit. It requires drainage areas on each lot




Grading Development Code Amendments
that indicate the flow of water. It also requires the elevation of the lot on each corner. The proposal includes language
that if homeowners are doing landscaping or construction that will change the drainage of the lot or will change the
elevation of the corners, they must submit a new grading permit. Again, these requirements are to provide the least
impactful way to mitigate against more problems with drainage and landslides.

City Property Concerns
The second concern relates to an increasing number of people grading and/or landscaping onto City owned property.
This includes into the City owned hillside and park spaces. The proposed amendment clearly states that this is not
allowed and is considered a Class C Misdemeanor.

The two DRC comments from Parks relate to this issue. One concern is that landowners grade or landscape onto City
property with the thought or intent that the City will then sell the property to them. The parks department recommends
adding language that would preclude landowners from being able to purchase property from the City if they encroach
first, ask later. Parks also recommended changing a violation to a Class B Misdemeanor which is a little more serious.

The Planning Commission should consider if they would like these changes to be made to the proposed Development
Code Amendment.


  SCOPE OF APPROVAL

Development Code amendments are legislative items. The Planning Commission and ultimately the City Council have
full discretion on whether or not they approve them.

The public may speak to the proposed amendments. Comments focused around specific concerns and changes that
would improve the code requirements are the most beneficial.


  POSSIBLE MOTIONS AND FINDINGS

The Planning Commission may move to recommend approval or denial of the proposed amendments to the Lehi
City Development Code. Any motion should include findings. Possible findings include:

    1.   The proposed Development Code amendments will help prevent future landslide and grading issues.
    2.   The proposed Development Code amendments includes language to discourage encroaching onto City prop-
         erty.
    3.   The proposed Development Code amendment is/is not in conformance with the purposes, intent, and provi-
         sions of the General Plan and its various elements.
    4.   Additional findings based upon information presented during the public hearing.

Section 12-B.090 Penalties
(Amended 11/12/13)

A.      If grading occurs without a valid grading per-
mit or outside the approved surveyed area the grading
project may be stop work ordered and all work will
be stopped. The stop work order will stay in place un-
til the area graded outside of the approved area is re-
turned to its original elevation and re-vegetated. If
grading is conducted outside the surveyed area a sec-
ond time no grading will be allowed for 30 days as
well as the unauthorized graded area will be returned
to original elevation and re-vegetated. If grading oc-
curs a third time outside the approved area the grading
permit will be revoked and the landowner or his/her
designee must apply for a new grading permit paying
the permit fee again as well as a new bond, if applica-
ble and the disturbed ground outside the approved
area must be restored before the new grading permit
will be considered.
