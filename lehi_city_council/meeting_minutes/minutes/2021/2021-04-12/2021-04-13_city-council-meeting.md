2. Presentations and Reports
2.1 Ranked Choice Voting Presentation- Utah County Clerk's Office
Amelia Powers Gardner, Utah County Clerk, and Josh Daniels, Clerks Office, was available to
answer questions about RCV. Ms. Gardner stated that they are advocates for the State’s pilot
program. She said it helps to engage more people and better serves the voter. It also produces the
best representation. She said that candidates run on issues rather than personalities and it creates
a better campaign environment.

Mr. Daniels stated that the winners are calculated using instant runoff voting. He also stated that
a person’s vote will count for as many opportunities as that candidate is still in the running. He
explained how your choices ride through each round in an election. He said your number one
vote will always count until that candidate is either elected or eliminated and then it moves to the
second choice and so on. Mr. Daniels stated that in a multi seat election it’s like holding three
separate elections and every voter will get one vote for each round. It’s possible for a voter to
vote for the same person in multiple rounds if their number 1 candidate isn’t elected first or isn’t
eliminated. Ms. Gardner stated that the courts have ruled that this is constitutional. She also said
that all of their tabulation equipment is certified. She stated that all of the candidates in the two
cities that used RCV liked it and they did not hold a Primary. Of all the cities considering RCV,
Lehi is the only one considering holding a Primary, with the exception of Provo.

Councilor Albrecht inquired as to why the Legislature hasn’t adopted it and why they passed it to
cities. Ms. Gardner stated that she has found that incumbents are often hesitant to change a

2.2 North Pointe Solid Waste Special Service District Discussion - Neil Schwendiman
Neil Schwendiman, District Manager, informed the Council of some potential programs that can
assist residents with recycling materials and schedules. He stated that the program cost is about
34,000 per year, so that cost would need to be split by each service city. The district may also
contribute to the cost. The Council expressed interest in the program and asked Mr.
Schwendiman to follow up with Jason Walker, City Administrator.

2.3 Jordan River Sewer Line and Sewer Rate Discussion - Finance and Engineering
Dean Lundell, Finance Director, stated that the sewer fund is operating at a loss and they can use
cash reserves for now, but expenses are outpacing revenue so he suggested that it may be time
for a rate increase. He said most of the expense goes to TSSD, which is out of our control, but
there are some other expenses that the city incurs as well for operations. The Council agreed to
propose a 3% rate increase. Mr. Lundell stated that they will move forward with the public
noticing process. Lorin Powell, City Engineer, informed the Council that the City also needs to
construct the Jordan River sewer line which will cost 5 million. He noted that the system can be
paid for with impact fees.

2.4 Lehi Archery Range Discussion
This item was moved to the next Council meeting.

2.5 Transit Oriented Development Presentation - Planning Department
Mike West, with the Planning Department, presented on the benefits and options for a Transit
Oriented Development (TOD). He stated that traffic is getting worse and it will continue to get
worse, but a TOD can lower amounts of vehicular traffic if it’s designed correctly. He said that it
needs to be efficient and well used to get the best benefit. Reasons that facilitate a lower amount
of traffic in a TOD include pedestrian safety, easy to access transit, making it difficult for people
to drive and less convenient to park. It should also have a good mix of uses. Mr. West showed
videos of walkable areas in a TOD. He noted that they should be comfortable, safe, and
convenient. He also showed examples of successful TODs around the country.

    Mayor Johnson asked for public comments.

    Michelle Stallings stated that it would be difficult for voters to get to know the candidates
    without holding a Primary election. She said that the current voting system gives equal voting
    power to each voter, if it changes then a portion of voters will get one vote for two open seats.
    Mrs. Stallings also questioned the constitutionality of RCV in a multi seat election.

    Wayne Woodfield stated that he wants to explain the reasons why RCV is beneficial. He said
    a candidate doesn’t want to ostracize voters because they want 2nd and 3rd place votes too, so
    the campaign is more civil. It encourages sincere voting. In traditional City Council elections
    there’s a big problem with bullet voting, which is when someone only votes for 1 person when
    they could vote for 2 or 3. If you vote for more people, then you harm your first choice. RCV
    eliminates that flaw. RCV also eliminates vote splitting with candidates that have similar
    ideologies.

    Councilor Albrecht stated that there are concerns with trusting an algorithm. Mr. Condie stated
    that the algorithm for calculating the results is not complicated and that there should be a
    second verification software in place.

    Councilor Albrecht stated that she wants to be innovative and is willing to give it shot as long
    as the city can choose to opt out of the program in the future.

    Ms. Stallings stated that it’s not fair to some voters because it’s possible that only one of their
    votes will count.

    Councilor Condie stated that the way he has seen it work and the way it was explained to him
    for a multi seat race is that you have to look at it as 2 different elections (for 2 open at-large
    seats), so every voter will get a vote counted in each election.

    conduct a Primary Election using Instant Runoff Voting.
    Councilor Hancock stated that he felt it was now okay to move forward without a Primary. He
    said that even though there was a large number of people that filed two years ago, it was clear
    to him there only a few that were serious and that understood the issues. He also said that
    implementing the elections communications plan that Mr. Boyle suggested eliminates the need
    for a Primary.

    Councilor Koivisto stated that she believes not holding a Primary will favors the incumbents.
    She felt that a Primary was good in order to narrow the choices of candidates for the General
    election.

    Councilor Albrecht stated that a lot of choices can muddy the waters. She was concerned with
    the cost; however, she doesn’t want to change everything all at once, so she would be in favor
    of a Primary.

    Councilor Condie stated that if they do RCV, then they should be all in and that includes not
    holding a Primary.

                Lehi City Administration to conduct a Primary Election using Instant Runoff
                Voting. Councilor Albrecht seconded the motion.
