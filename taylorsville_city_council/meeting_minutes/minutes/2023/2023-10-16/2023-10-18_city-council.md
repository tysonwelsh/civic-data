3.       REPORTS

         3.1     UTA Presentation Regarding Updates on Funding, Project Bid, Construction and
                 Status of the Mid-Valley Bus Rapid Transit System – Carlton Christensen/Andrea
                 Pullos

              UTA Executive Director Jay Fox, Board Chair Carlton Christensen and Project Manager
Andrea Pullos updated the City Council on various aspects of the BRT project. Ms. Pullos explained that
the BRT system was seven miles long and would take riders from West Valley City Hall to the Murray
Central Transit Station. It would go down 4700 South and would have twenty-five stops along the
alignment. They anticipated approximately 1,900 riders on the new system per day with 15 minutes between
electric buses during peak times. Due to some grant funding, the fare would be free for the first five years
of service. A large part of the funding for the project (64%) was from federal funding, with 34% coming
from state funds and 2% from donations of land made by West Valley, Salt Lake Community College and
UDOT. Ms. Pullos went on to describe the process that had taken place since an initial public open house
in 2018 to the construction bid which was currently in process. She anticipated that the successful company
would get a notice to proceed in January. UTA had been working with city staff to determine what types of
meetings would be necessary for stakeholders and more information about those meetings would be
available at some point in the future.

5.          PLANNING MATTERS

            5.1     Discussion Regarding Review of the Taylorsville Land Development Code Update
                    Project; Chapter 13.04: Development Standards – Jim Spung

          Senior Planner Jim Spung reminded the City Council of the five goals of the project. They were:
     1.     Streamline development review procedures and increase predictability of land use decisions;
     2.     Consider the needs of the City both now and in the future;
     3.     Align the Land Development Code with the General Plan and state statutes;
     4.     Enable great design and a diverse mix of land uses;
     5.     Make the LDC more user-friendly through logical structure, graphics, tables, and illustrations.

Detailed information about the project could be found at taylorsvilleut.gov/government/land-development-
code-update.

Mr. Spung explained that tonight’s presentation involved proposed updates to the city code regarding
signage and advertising. He began by discussing the United States Supreme Court’s 2015 decision in Reed
v. Town of Gilbert as an example of what can happen when a city attempts to regulate signage based on its
content. He explained that a good rule of thumb was that if one had to read a sign in order to understand
the regulations, then it violated the content-based standards. Mr. Spung indicated that the LDC would
eliminate references to the content of a sign other than a few exceptions noted in the Gilbert decision. In
short, the code could regulate the number, sign, location, construction and amount of time a sign could be
displayed, but it could not regulate the subject matter of those signs.

7.      OTHER MATTERS

        7.1      Discussion Regarding Taylorsville City Ordinance Chapter 11.20 (Parking) – John
                 Taylor

      City Administrator John Taylor explained that the number of complaints regarding parking problems
in the city had substantially increased, and what worked 30 years ago did not necessarily work today. He
then turned the time over to City Engineer Ben White to lead the discussion.

Mr. White pointed out that unlike popular belief, homeowners did not own the parking spaces in front of
their houses. It was not unlawful to park in front of someone else’s home, so that was not something the
city was going to regulate.

Services that could be negatively impacted by on-street parking issues included:
    o Snow plowing
    o Garbage collection
    o Street sweeping
    o Mail delivery
    o Emergency services
    o School busses

Mr. White reviewed current parking restrictions instituted by state code as well as city code and then
presented information regarding on-street parking regulations in other nearby municipalities.

Some options to address the problems were:
    Prohibiting on-street parking
    Prohibiting all overnight parking
    Restricting overnight parking in the winter
    Limit on-street parking to one side of the street only
    Paint and sign any “known” parking problem areas
    Overnight parking in front of registered addresses only
    Parking by permit on a large scale

Councilmember Harker spoke in favor of finding the simplest solution and thought perhaps that was to
merely enforce the parking regulations that were already on the books. She also did not feel one solution
would necessarily be right for the entire city.

Mayor Overson agreed that simple was best, but pointed out that the problem itself wasn’t simple at all. It
was important to educate the public about the various requirements regarding parking near intersections,
whether a crosswalk was present, etc.
