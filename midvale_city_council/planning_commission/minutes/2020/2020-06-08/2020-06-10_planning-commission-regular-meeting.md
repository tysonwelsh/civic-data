a: TXT-20-04; ZONING ORDINANCE TEXT AMENDMENT; MODIFYING
DEVELOPMENT STANDARDS IN JORDAN BLUFFS SUBAREAS 1-3 ZONE
DISTRICT; BEN SEASTRAND/GARDNER JORDAN BLUFFS '- LC
(APPLICANT)

Mr. Alex Murphy stated in 2019, the City adopted a zoning ordinance for Subareas 1-3 of the
Jordan Bluffs zone. This ordinance established goals for the district, review process requirements,
and standards for commercial, residential, and mixed-use development based on a preliminary
“pod” concept put together by the property owner, Gardner Company. To date, one project has
been approved under this ordinance (Holmes Homes’ Jordan Bluffs Condo project).
Following an agreement between Zions and Gardner to develop the northern pods, D&E, Gardner
developed a new concept for the Jordan Bluffs area that would, over time and subject to market
conditions, develop an urbanized core of the project area with Zions serving as the northern anchor
and future development incrementally contributing to the vision.
To implement the new concept, certain requirements of the zoning ordinance require amendment
and Gardner has submitted this request to update the text. Pursuant to the text amendment process
described in §17-3-1 of the Midvale City Municipal Code, Staff and Gardner have been discussing
the required changes and the result is now available for Planning Commission consideration.
This text amendment proposes the changes to the following sections:
17-7-10.12.1 Purpose.
The new concept requires a new layout of the overall pod map. The changes results in a reduction
in the overall number of pods (14 to 12) and new boundaries for the pods. This change also includes
a revision to the central linear park location and will require additional modifications to the Master
Development Agreement for Jordan Bluffs, which will be reviewed separately by the City Council.
17-7-10.12.5 Development requirements.
Proposed changes to this section include a new road layout, new road standards, revised landscape,
building, and parking lot setbacks for the core, and increased building heights in certain pods.
The new concept proposes a new street system on the west side of Bingham Junction Boulevard.
The new street system provides a central boulevard-type street as a primary street serviced by
secondary and tertiary streets with standards designed based on expected traffic volumes. On-street
parking is required for the primary boulevard and an expanded bike network is provided on the
primary and secondary streets. Staff and the applicant have provided updated figures for this
section and recommend one additional change to the Public Road Classification figure to add the
bike network prior to adoption.

PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION JUNE 107, 2020

In conjunction with the new road layout, building and landscape setback along streets in the new
core have been modified. For Pods E-K, which include all pods west of Bingham Junction
Boulevard, minimum and maximum setbacks are determined by street type (primary, secondary,
or tertiary) and priority. Outside of those pods, landscape setbacks for minor roads have been
decreased from 15’ to 10’. Major roads (Bingham Junction, East Ivy, Center Street) remain as
originally adopted.

Following some forays into marketing the site, Gardner is also requesting the building height east
of Bingham Junction Boulevard be increased from 80’ to 95’ and that it be measured from finished
floor elevation to top of exterior wall (excluding parapets) instead of to the parapet. This results in
a maximum building height increase of approximately 20’.

17-7-10.12.6 Parking and access standards.

The proposed changes to parking standards include a reduction in the minimum requirement for
Office uses, a modification to access requirements, and the addition of on-street loading areas.
The current language requires a minimum of 3 parking stalls per 1000 SF of net leasable floor area
for general office uses or 5 stalls per 1000 SF for intensive office uses. This standard does not
account for proximity to TRAX stations or shared parking opportunities that may eventually
become in the project area, so a modification to establish a minimum of 3 stalls per 1000 SF and
maximum of 6 stalls per 1000 SF is proposed.

The City Engineer expressed some concern about mandating minimum access separation
requirements in the zoning ordinance, specifically calling out that it limited his ability to consider
traffic patterns and street layout when reviewing development proposals. The proposed language
changes the requirements for separation to recommendations and vests approval authority in the
City Engineer.

In response to concerns from the Bingham Junction area, new language for on-street loading areas
is proposed. On-street loading would be permitted within existing rights-of-way with requirements
regarding placement, frequency of use, and minimum improvement standards.

17-7-10.12.8 Fencing and screening standards.

As part of the on-street loading option added in §17-7-10.12.6, this section is modified to apply
screening requirements to off-street loading areas only instead of all loading areas. All other
standards remain as currently adopted.

17-7-10.12.10 Residential developments.

Proposed changes to this section include removing the duplication of building height requirements
and modifying the parking garage design requirements.

Because overall building height requirements are defined in §17-7-10.12.5, this text amendment
proposes removing them from this section.

PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION JUNE 107, 2020

New parking garage screening requirements are provided in this text amendment, based on a newly
defined “within clear sight of” standard. Parking garage elevations within clear sight of major
roads (Bingham Junction, Center, new primary, or new secondary roads) or of a public park are
required to cover at least 30% of the elevation with materials that match the primary building on
the site. Elevations that are not within clear sight of major roads or a public park are required to
be screened with landscape material. For the purposes of these requirements, elevations are
reviewed individually and considered “within clear sight of” if they are (1) approximately parallel

to a street or park, (2) within 300’ of one of a street or park, and (3) not otherwise obstructed by a

building or elevation change. Parking garages in Pods E-K are also required to comply with the

landscape setback requirements of §17-7-10.12.5(L).

17-7-10.12.11 Commercial developments.

Proposed changes to this section match the building height and parking garage changed proposed

in §17-7-10.12.10 above.

17-7-10.12.13 Signage.

The proposed change within this section would adjust the standards for a project area monument

sign, increasing maximum allowable height from 9’ to 10’ and maximum allowable area from 150

SF to 180 SF. No other changes are proposed.

Staff recommended forwarding a positive recommendation to the City Council to adopt the

proposed text amendment with a condition to update the Public Road Classification graphic to

include the required bike lane network.

Mr. Alex Murphy stated Ben Seastrand, representing Zions Bank and Gardner Jordan Bluffs LC,
is requesting approval of a Large Scale Master Plan (LSMP) for Pod D of the Jordan Bluffs
Subarea 1-3 area. The project occupies all of Pod D, approximately 20 acres at the northern end of
the Jordan Bluffs area.
The project includes a 400,000 SF office building, 800 stall parking garage, surface parking lots,
and various amenities and landscape areas. Access to the project would be provided from a new
access point on Bingham Junction Boulevard, which runs along the east side of the Pod, and Binary
Way, which is proposed to be relocated as part of an amended subdivision plat. Interior vehicular
traffic will be managed by parking lot drive aisles. Various pedestrian paths and outdoor amenity-
type spaces are provided throughout the project, including both public and private areas such as a
perimeter path with fitness equipment, new dog park, native landscaping on the cap slope, and a
community garden. The project is proposed to be completed in a single phase with construction
and move-in finishing in 2022.

The Midvale City General Plan 2016 identifies the Jordan Bluffs area as an “Opportunity Area,”
or an area where changes in the types and/or intensities of current land uses are anticipated.
Applicable land use goals from the General Plan for the Jordan Bluffs area include:
e Promoting consistency and compatibility with the Bingham Junction project to the north.
e Providing connectivity to the Main Street neighborhood, either physically (crosswalks) or

e Supporting redevelopment of the adjacent area to the east to blend and enhance any
redevelopment of the Jordan Bluffs Area.

e Extending Bingham Junction Boulevard.

e Improving pedestrian and bicycle travel through the area.

e Improving community connections to the Jordan River.

e Encouraging higher density residential in appropriate locations in Opportunity Areas.

e Expanding the variety of housing opportunities to allow for more choices in types and
locations of residences, including providing for a mixture of housing sizes, densities,
types and affordability in each area of the City.

e Providing an appropriate mix of quality housing choices to support commercial

General Development
Wetlands
The zoning ordinance requires the wetland/drainage area at the south end of Jordan Bluffs be
protected and enhanced. This project does not affect that wetland area, so no consideration of this
requirement is necessary.
Public Road Improvements
To improve connectivity within the project and to the rest of Midvale, the applicant is required to
complete any unfinished public right-of-way improvements in the project area. Bingham Junction
Boulevard and Center Street have already been completed by the Master Developer. The sidewalk
along Center Street was not reviewed by Planning Staff prior to installation and the applicant has
agreed to revise portions of it to improve the walkability and account for future power line removal.

be completed according to the new street standards in the ordinance as part of the construction
process. No street design has been submitted with the LSMP; however, the new street section is
defined by the zoning ordinance and Staff recommends requiring the applicant additional details
on the proposed street construction as part of the Development Agreement and Site Plan review
processes.
The applicant obtained a Traffic Impact Study from Hales Engineering that reviewed projected
traffic patterns before and after the project is completed as part of the overall Jordan Bluffs
development area. Hales recommended the following improvements be made to streets adjacent
to the project as part of the overall Jordan Bluffs development by 2025:

e Install northbound dual left-turn lanes and increase left-turn storage at Bingham Junction

and Center Street.
e Install left-turn acceleration lane for eastbound-to-northbound left-turn vehicles to turn
into directly at the Binary Way and Bingham Junction intersection.

e Coordinate timing of the traffic signals on Center Street to reduce delays and queueing.

Parking and Access

The parking requirement for an office development has a minimum of 3 and maximum calculation
of 6 stalls per 1000 SF of net leasable floor area. With a proposed floor area of 400,000 SF, this
results in a minimum requirement of 1200 stalls and a maximum of 2400. Between the proposed
parking garage and surface parking lots, a total of 1600 stalls are proposed, satisfying this
requirement.

At least 15% of the total parking area is required to be landscaped. The LSMP drawings do not
provide a calculation indicating this requirement is satisfied but do show a substantial amount of
landscaping is provided around the parking lot. Staff recommends requiring this calculation be
provided as part of the Development Agreement and Site Plan processes.

The surface parking lots provide the minimum dimensions for driveways and stall widths at 26’
and 9°x18’, respectively. The parking garage also provides 9’x18” stalls; however, it does not
provide the extra width required adjacent to columns nor does it provide a layout that considers
safe walking routes to elevators, stairways, doors, and to the sidewalk system. Staff recommends
requiring revisions to the parking garage to address these requirements be completed as part of the
Development Agreement and Site Plan processes. These changes may result in relatively minor
changes to the size of the garage but would not affect the layout or configuration of the overall
project.

Landscaping and Amenities

For commercial projects, the applicant is also required to improve 10% of the overall site as
landscaping, courtyards, plazas and walkways, excluding the required landscape setbacks along
streets. The LSMP drawings do not provide a calculation indicating this requirement is satisfied
but do show a substantial amount of landscaping is provided around the site. Staff recommends
requiring this calculation be provided as part of the Development Agreement and Site Plan
processes.

In addition to the required landscape area, the zone also establishes landscape setback requirements
along public and private rights-of-way. The only proposed rights-of-way affecting this project are
Bingham Junction Boulevard, with a landscape setback of 30’, and Binary Way, with a landscape
setback of 10’. At least 30’ is provide along all of Bingham Junction but the required 10’ is not
provided along Binary. Staff recommends requiring revisions to the landscape setbacks to address
this requirement as part of the Development Agreement and Site Plan processes.

In addition to general landscaping, the applicant has proposed several amenities available for use
by the public, employees, or both. These amenities include a one of the three required connections
from the top of Jordan Bluffs to the Jordan River Parkway Trail, a perimeter multiuse path with
fitness equipment, a new dog park at the Center Street Trailhead, community garden boxes, an
outdoor plaza, and a large group gathering area. From the LSMP drawings, it is unclear which of
these are intended to be publicly accessible nor who will be responsible for maintenance. Staff
recommends requiring this information be included in the Development Agreement.

Architecture

Building requirements relating to height, orientation, scale, proximity, and design have largely all
been addressed. At 6 stories tall for the tallest portion, the proposed building does not approach
the 180° maximum height limit. The proposed building is serviced by private access drives,
entrances are oriented towards plazas, and entrances and pathways minimize potential conflict
between pedestrians and vehicles. Changes in building plane are provided with this design as well.
Using brick, metal, and glass, the proposed exterior finishes satisfy the material requirements but
do not include calculations indicating the coverage or opening amounts. This can be addressed
during the Site Plan review process. At least 75% of each building face must be a durable material
or glass and at least 25% of building elevations facing public streets or parks are required to be
window, balcony, or door areas (15% otherwise, unless obscured from public view by another
building within 20’.)

As part of the architecture review, the applicant provided a solar reflectivity study assessing
potential impacts on the public realm caused by the amount of glass proposed for the building. The
study assumes a reflectivity rating of 0.19 and finds reflections on Bingham Junction Boulevard
and the Jordan River Parkway Trail are generally limited to fewer than 25 hours per year. These
reflections occur at very low sun angles (early morning or late evening) throughout the year. Areas
receiving reflections for a high number of hours per year (300+) occur typically around the
foundation of the building or on the roof of the shorter portion of the building. Most of these areas
are within the property boundary and are occupied by landscaping or parking. The one notable
exception is the sidewalk and street in the Binary Way right-of-way underneath the 6-story portion
of the new building, which has some areas receiving reflections for 1500+ hours per year. To avoid
creating an unsafe condition for drivers and pedestrians, particularly around the Binary Way
crosswalks, Staff recommends requiring revisions to the architecture or site plan to reduce the
number of hours or intensity of reflections as part of the Development Agreement and Site Plan
processes.

Parking garages also have architectural standards, including a requirement to enhance screening
on all facades within clear sight of significant public streets or the park using finishes that match
the primary building. This requirement affects the north elevation of the structure and has not been
addressed in the LSMP. The remaining elevations are not considered “within clear sight of” the
necessary areas because they are outside the minimum distance or obscured by other buildings.
Staff recommends updated parking garage elevations to address this requirement as part of the
Development Agreement and Site Plan processes.

larger than 180 SF in area and located at the project entrance on Bingham Junction Boulevard.
