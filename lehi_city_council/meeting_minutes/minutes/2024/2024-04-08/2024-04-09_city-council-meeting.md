Pre Council, 6:00 p.m.
1. Welcome and Opening Comment
Mayor Pro Tempore Condie welcomed everyone and noted that all Councilmembers were present.
Mayor Johnson was excused. Councilor Stallings gave the opening comment.

2. Presentations and Reports
2.1 Discussion of the North Utah County Equestrian Park
Cameron Boyle, Assistant City Administrator presented on the North Utah County Equestrian
Park agreement with Utah County, noting that the maintenance responsibilities will be shared
between Lehi, Highland, and American Fork cities. Questions were raised about the condition of
the park and potential revenue. Brad Kenison noted that Lehi City is interested in obtaining an
easement for a water tank at the park. The Council would like to better understand the details of
the agreement.

3. Agenda Questions
None.

4. Administrative Report
There was discussion about trees removed near 1900 South as part of TSSD’s improvement
project.

5. Mayor and Council Reports
The Council reported on their assigned committees.

9. Consideration of Ordinance #21-2024, the Lakeview Land and Rock General Plan
   amendment on 12.81-acres of property located at approximately 600 East 600 South,
   changing the land use designation from LDR (low density residential) to Heavy
   Commercial and Commercial.
   Tony Trane was representing Cole Peck. He stated that if the Council is not comfortable with
   the proposed amendment that they instead enter into a development agreement that would
   define specific land uses and establish requirements for buffering based on recommendations.
   The owners would like to see the property used for office warehouse with some outdoor
   storage, but they are willing to negotiate exceptions, terms and conditions in a development
   agreement that would make the development more amenable.

    Liz Anderson gave a history of her family’s purchase and use of the property.

    Mr. Trane stated that they want the Council’s recommendations for terms of a development
    agreement and that they are willing to make accommodations for the neighbors.

    There was discussion about the city’s connectivity standards which would require a road
    connection through the neighborhood. Mr. Trane stated that the owner does not need the road
    to connect so they are willing to discuss other options.

    Mayor Pro Tempore Condie asked for public comments.

    Liz Anderson explained that her family purchased this property before nearby homes were
    built and highlighted their long-standing connection to Lehi. She emphasized the need for
    light industrial space in Lehi. She stressed the importance of finding a solution that
    accommodates their business's need for outside storage, which is crucial for their livelihood.

    Alexa Jarrett, a neighboring resident, opposed a road connecting commercial areas to their
    residential neighborhood, citing safety concerns for children walking to school. She pointed
    out that the Planning Division staff recommended denial in 2021 due to insufficient buffering
    and inconsistency with the city's general plan. Alexa emphasized that the neighborhood roads
    aren't equipped for increased commercial traffic and urged the council to vote against the
    proposal for the neighborhood's well-being.

    Camillia Vetti, another resident from the same neighborhood, expressed concern about the
    potential decrease in property values if heavy industrial zoning is approved. She highlighted

    Mayor Pro Tempore Condie stated that he believes there’s potential for a development
    agreement. He also noted the city's role in setting zoning and the occasional need for

    Mr. Trane stated that if they suggest a development agreement then they need Council

    The Council discussed possible terms for a development agreement including road

    Attorney, explained that a Development Agreement can override or modify existing city code

    purchased it. She feels like making a change from residential would go against the
    expectations of residents who bought their homes with the understanding that the Peck
    property would be residential.

    Councilor Stallings emphasized the need to protect neighborhoods and children. She believes
    that the city's current general plan has caused problems with incompatible land uses. She
    noted that a residential zoning decision for this property would still leave potential for
    conflict with the adjacent light industrial area. She stated that she’s not comfortable with a
    general plan amendment either, preferring a development agreement that carefully considers
    the transition between residential and industrial areas. Her goal with the development
    agreement would be to ensure a smooth transition between land uses, protect residents from
    potential negative impacts, and address the needs of businesses requiring industrial zoning.

    Councilor Hancock stated that he tends to agree with Council Stallings on this, and agrees
    with Councilor Albrecht in that the residents should have been more involved in offering
    suggestions for terms to include in a development agreement. He also made suggestions for
    road connections and a land use designations and layout.

    Mayor Pro Tempore Condie expressed support for pursuing a development agreement as a
    potential solution. He believes they could address the concerns raised by residents while also
    considering the needs of businesses and the broader community. He emphasized the
    importance of collaboration and compromise to find a balanced solution that benefits
    everyone involved.
