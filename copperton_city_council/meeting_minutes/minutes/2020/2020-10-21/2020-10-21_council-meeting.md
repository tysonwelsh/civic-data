Unified Fire Authority (UFA)

              Ed Walden, UFA, stated there were a record number of red flag days this summer,
but things are now cooling down. The 50-plus firefighters deployed to fires in California and
Oregon have returned home. He thanked citizens for supporting the “Light the Night” event.
Captains exams are being administered and new captains will start in the middle of next month.
A UFA boot camp will likely start in February, 2021.

              Mayor Clayton noted he received over 1,000 face masks from Salt Lake County
Mayor Jennifer Wilson and has distributed them to businesses in town.

Unified Police Department (UPD)

                Detective Harry Holt, UPD, stated Copperton had 21 calls for service last month,
resulting in four cases. Precincts will be closing to walk-ins due to the spike in Covid cases.
Citizens can make an appointment and needs can be addressed by telephone.

               Council Member Bailey asked if there were curfews for the park or the town.

               Detective Holt stated the curfew for parks is 11:00 PM. He is not aware of any
town curfew.

               Mayor Clayton thanked Detective Holt for getting a van moved that was
obstructing the view of pedestrians trying to cross the street.

Fiscal Items – Approval of Expenditures

              Council Member Bailey, seconded by Council Member Stitzer, moved to approve
the $86.50 balance for legal fees. The motion passed unanimously.

              Mayor Clayton suggested that some CARES Act funding be used to buy
technology that could broadcast meetings from an anchor location or be mobile enough to move
to other remote locations. He obtained two quotes and would recommend Intermountain Media
Systems for $12,466.06 because it is more mobile than the other system. He also suggested
purchasing disinfecting wipes, face masks and hand sanitizer.

                Nathan Bracken suggested buying the supplies sooner rather than later because
projections are that Covid will spread dramatically during the winter. Supplies may not be
available at a later date.

                Mayor Clayton, seconded by Council Member Severson, moved to purchase the
Intermountain Media Systems technology, as well as 2000 cloth face masks with logos in various
sizes, 350 large size hand sanitizers, 350 cleaning wipes, bags to hold the supplies, and approved
a fee to deliver them. The motion passed unanimously.

Apollo Pazell Vacancy

               Mayor Clayton stated that Council Member Apollo Pazell has resigned due to his
job being too demanding, but he will remain active in community affairs.

               Nathan Bracken explained the process to fill the vacant Council seat. The Council
needs to give notice at least two weeks before the next meeting asking people to submit a resume
or application. Candidates then must be interviewed in an open meeting. Once selected, the
person would serve the remainder of Mr. Pazell’s term. He reviewed possible locations to post
the notice, which he will prepare. The notice must be issued by October 28, 2020. Applications
and resumes will be due one day before the November meeting.

              Council Member Severson noted that the applicant must be a registered voter.

            Council Member Bailey stated there are applications for the Planning
Commission and Cemetery Board which can be amended for Council applicants.

               The Council agreed that each Council Member will think of one question to ask
each candidate and send the questions to Mayor Clayton. Questions will be due by October 28,
2020. Mayor Clayton will then send the list of questions to all Council Members. Each Council
Member will give the candidates a score from one to four. Applicants can text or email Mayor
Clayton for applications by November 11, 2020.

               Mr. Bracken stated the decision on which candidate to select must be done in an
open meeting. He liked the idea of ranking the questions from one to four. Swearing in of the
new Council Member will be November 18, 2020. He will also compose a resolution of
appreciation for the Council to present to Mr. Pazell.

Tentative 2021 Budget

               Mayor Clayton described details of the proposed 2021 Copperton budget, which
was tentatively approved by the Municipal Services District. Funds can be moved around within
the budget, but the bottom line cannot change.

              Council Member Stitzer stated the Community Council has not received its 2020
allotment. She was hoping for a budget increase to no more than $3,000. A lot of fundraising
events have been cancelled.

              Mayor Clayton stated he was concerned that some Community Council funding
was used to pay for an injury received during the Town Days.

               Council Member Stitzer noted that the Town Days funds are all donations; it does
not receive taxpayer money.

               Mayor Clayton stated he would adjust the Community Council budget to include
an additional $500 for 2021.

               Council Member Bailey, seconded by Council Member Stitzer, moved to approve
the tentative budget. The motion passed unanimously.

               Mayor Clayton, seconded by Council Member Stitzer, moved to set the public
hearing for the budget on December 16, 2020 at 6:30 PM. The motion passed unanimously.

General Plan Review & Adoption

              Mikala Jordan, Long Range Planner, Municipal Services District, delivered a
PowerPoint presentation explaining the general plan and the process used to create it. She
reviewed the plan’s vision statement, Copperton’s five character areas, future land use map, five
key recommendations, the planning staff analysis, and next steps.

              Nathan Bracken stated the first change in the code will be to include limits on
density.

             Mayor Clayton, seconded by Council Member Severson, moved to approve the
General Plan as presented with recommended changes from the Planning Commission and
Nathan Bracken. The motion passed unanimously.

Committee/Board Updates

        Legislative Research Committee

               Nathan Bracken updated the Council on his efforts to obtain municipal energy
and telecommunication tax authority for Copperton. He reported on his meeting with the Utah
League of Cities and Towns, and his presentation to the Political Subdivisions Interim Committee.
Legislators outside of Salt Lake County still have misconceptions about the metro townships.

               Mayor Clayton stated he would help with meeting the legislators.

        Bingham Cemetery Board

               Council Member Stitzer stated a headstone was stolen from the Bingham
Cemetery in 2008. It is now housed in the Magna museum. The board is working to return the
headstone to its appropriate spot in the Bingham Cemetery. She also requested $90.00 from the
cemetery fund to renew the post office box, no later than Friday.

               Council Member Bailey stated she would get the money for her.

        Copperton Community Council

                 Council Member Stitzer stated the Community Council met tonight with Rio Tinto.
The crusher and conveyor belt projects are still ongoing but had been subject to some delays.
They will likely be completed by the second quarter of 2021. If there is excessive dust, please
report it to Rio Tinto. Most of the dust is from the construction.

They also discussed fire breaks, the 2021 budget, and adding a Halloween decorating contest to
their list of events.

She also asked Council Member Bailey to forward event permits to the new Council secretary,
Rachel Homer.
