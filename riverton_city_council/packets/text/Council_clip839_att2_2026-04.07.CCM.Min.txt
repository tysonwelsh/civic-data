45          Budget Session on CIP and Long Range Plan
46
47   Nick Geer, Administrative Services Director, introduced the Capital Improvement Plan (CIP),
48   emphasizing that capital projects are large-scale, often multi-year investments such as
49   infrastructure, parks, and facilities. He reminded the Council to distinguish between one-time and
50   ongoing costs, as some projects may require multi-year financial commitments.
51
     Page | 2 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   Mr. Geer discussed impact fees which are a restricted funding source that can only be used for new
 2   capital projects or new amenities, not for maintenance, repairs, or simply replacing existing
 3   features. This limitation means that projects like relocating an existing skate park may not qualify,
 4   since it does not create a net new amenity for the community.
 5
 6   Mr. Geer also discussed the General Capital Improvement Fund, which is primarily supported by
 7   sales tax and general fund transfers, along with supplemental funding from grants such as CDBG
 8   and other external sources. He noted that the fund is currently projected to be in a deficit position,
 9   as no cuts have yet been made. He clarified that when referring to “cuts,” he generally means
10   delaying projects rather than eliminating them.
11
12   Mr. Geer discussed the proposed utility rate structures, beginning with secondary water. He
13   explained that the current proposal phases in rate increases over a three-year period, with a
14   required rate study update expected around the 2029–2030 budget cycle to incorporate updated
15   consumption-based and tiered pricing models. At this time, there are no general fund subsidies
16   supporting secondary water in the proposed budget and the fund is currently projected to remain
17   budget-neutral.
18
19   Mr. Geer discussed sanitation rates, where a modest increase was proposed, 25 cents per first
20   garbage container, 25 cents per first recycling container, and 25 cents for each additional container
21   with no general fund subsidy.
22
23   Lastly, Mr. Geer explained that sales tax performance was down compared to the previous year.
24   He indicated the City is currently projected to slightly exceed budgeted sales tax revenue, though
25   several months remain in the fiscal cycle.
26
27
28             Water Rates Discussion
29
30   The Council discussion on culinary water rates focused on selecting among several proposed rate
31   structures designed to generate equivalent revenue but distribute costs differently between base
32   rates and usage tiers. Councilmember Smith, Johnson and Pierucci supported a lower base rate
33   (Option 1, $8 increase), emphasizing affordability, behavioral incentives for water conservation, and
34   uncertainty around future consumption changes and revenue from PUDs. Councilmember
35   McDougal favored a higher base rate (up to $15), arguing that the City has historically underpriced
36   water service, that regional wholesale water costs are rising, and that a stronger base rate would
37   provide more stability and reduce the likelihood of repeated future increases.
38
39   Councilmember Smith MOVED to adopt Option 1 of the water rates. Councilmember Johnson
40   SECONDED the motion. Mayor Buroker called for a vote; the vote was as follows: Haymond-No,
41   Johnson-Yes, McDougal-No, Pierucci-Yes, and Smith-Yes. The motion passed with a 3 to 2 vote.

36             Ordinance No. 26-05 – Moore Rezone, Amending the General Plan to Medium-High
37             Density Residential and Rezoning to RM-6 on the Approximately 1.45 Acres Located
38             at the NE Corner of 13200 S & Redwood Road, Taylor Moore, TMoore Real Estate
39
40   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the subject property, previously
41   the site of the Berrett’s Blossoms commercial building, is within an area of mixed zoning, including
42   commercial, neighborhood commercial, parks/open space, and RR-22 residential zoning. He
43   noted that no residential homes directly adjoined the property, with adjacent uses including vacant
44   commercially zoned land and nearby institutional and commercial uses. He explained that the
45   applicant had originally submitted a broader proposal involving higher density development, a
46   development agreement, and architectural standards, but later revised the request to a more
47   straightforward rezone application without additional development conditions. The request was to
48   rezone the property to RM-6, which allows detached single-family lots with a minimum lot size of
49   5,000 square feet and a maximum density of approximately six units per acre. A concept plan was
50   provided for illustrative purposes only, and final site design, including architecture and fencing,
51   would be addressed through the standard site plan process.

35   voluntary water use reductions of approximately 5–10%. He also noted that the drought committee
36   that developed the recommendation was divided, with some members supporting a more severe
37   Drought Level 2 classification that would have required mandatory reductions. He emphasized that
38   this was the first time in his tenure on the Board that such a split recommendation had occurred and
39   expressed concern about regional water supply conditions, indicating that without significant
40   changes, the region and state could face serious water supply challenges.
