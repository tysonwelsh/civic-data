3.      REPORTS

        3.1      Unified Fire Authority – Captain Rich

      Captain Rich presented information on the previous three months of UFA activity. The
number of incidents reported was down just slightly, despite the pandemic. It was believed this
was because some patients opted not to call 9-1-1 at a time when they otherwise would have, if
not for the current pandemic. It appeared that call volume was beginning to return to normal.
Captain Rich also spoke briefly about trending response times for both fire and medical
Taylorsville City Council Minutes

emergencies before discussing several deployments to assist with wildfires in both California and
Oregon. Additionally, Urban Search and Rescue had responded to Baton Rouge, LA to assist with
Hurricane Laura. There were 12 firefighters and/or paramedics from Taylorsville involved in that
deployment. Participating in these events allowed the UFA to not only assist other communities
but it also meant that it could potentially be a recipient of similar aid in the event it was needed in
the future. Unfortunately, there currently was not an effective mechanism in place for a Utah
agency to request this type of assistance from other states. It was a legislative issue and he
mentioned that at some point, his department might ask for the City’s support as they attempted to
ensure that such a mechanism was put into place.

Regarding staff recruitment, it was anticipated that there would be conditional employment offers
extended prior to Thanksgiving. Recruit school would begin early February.

A new Computer-Aided Dispatch (CAD) system would soon be implemented which would allow
all public safety entities in the Salt Lake Valley to be on the same system. This would be huge for
residents and it was a change that had been more than ten years in the making. The “go live” date
was set for September 29 and there would be a six-month evaluation period, after which any noted
inequities would be addressed.

Light the Night for Fallen Firefighters would take place September 27 through October 4. This
would be the fourth year that UFA had participated in the event.

        3.2      Communications/Website/Social Media Report – Kim Horiuchi

          Ms. Horiuchi explained the main messaging tools the City was using to get information
out to residents. These were the city’s website, e-notifications, social media and the city
newsletter.

Granicus, the city’s website provider reviewed the website since it had been in operation for a year.
The review provided some valuable insight as to how the public was using the site. The most
visited pages were the home page, the Taylorsville Dayzz page and the Taylorsville News page.

The “call to action” buttons on the home page were working well and it appeared that users were
finding the information they needed. Granicus’ final report indicated that the website design
looked “terrific” and that the city’s “digital content strategy appeared to be strong.”

Taylorsville currently had 5187 followers on Facebook and 3600 on Twitter. The number of
followers of the city’s Instagram account had more than doubled in the last two years.

Chair Harker expressed her enthusiasm for the consistent layout of the city’s newsletter. She was
very pleased with Ms. Horiuchi’s efforts.

5.      PLANNING MATTERS

        5.1    Ordinance No. 20-18: Public comment and consideration for a Zoning Text
        Amendment to the Taylorsville Municipal Code Sections 13.20.020: R-1 and R-2 Districts
        Table of Setbacks, Building Heights, Off Street Parking; and Section 13-26-080: Standards
        for Permanent Signs Allowed Without a Permit – Wayne Harper

        Mr. Harper pointed out how instrumental Ms. Horiuchi had been in moving business
licensing and building permitting online. Because of the changes she had made to the website,
there was no longer a need for paper applications.

He then went on to explain that there were two parts to the proposed ordinance. One portion
addressed the size of setbacks. There had been an unintended consequence of a 2012 ordinance
change which resulted in many residents being prohibited from adding porches, etc. to their homes.
The proposed ordinance would change that.

The second portion of the ordinance addressed flagpole heights as some had mentioned during the
citizen comment period. Staff had recommended an 80-foot limit in commercial/industrial zones,
but the planning commission had unanimously recommended that the limit be extended to 100
feet. Therefore, the Council was now being asked to consider a height of 100 feet although the
draft in the agenda packet still listed 80 feet.

Mr. Cowdell reminded the Council that the agenda had indicated that public comment would be
accepted on this matter.

6.      FINANCIAL MATTERS

        6.1   Budget Update Regarding Sales Tax and other City Revenue – Scott
        Harrington

      Mr. Harrington explained that he had structured his report to resemble the City’s usual
financial report. Overall, unaudited figures showed that 93.5% of the budgeted revenue had been
Taylorsville City Council Minutes
September 16, 2020
Page 8



collected thus far. Sales tax was up in comparison to the same point during the previous fiscal
year. He went through the various revenue streams in more detail and answered questions posed
by councilmembers. Although the City had anticipated needing to use $720,000 from the general
fund, it no longer appeared that would be necessary.

Councilmember Armstrong wondered if there was anything that had been cut from the budget that
could now be reinstated. Mr. Taylor responded that police overtime had been reinstated and that
there had been some discussion regarding funding sidewalk repair. Mr. Harrington pointed out
that there would be some additional costs for the new police department that would need to be
added to the budget around January.

Mayor Overson agreed that this was a good time for the council to consider any projects they
wished to address in anticipation of a mid-year adjustment and that perhaps a budget workshop
was in order.

Councilmember Armstrong felt it would be helpful for the Council to receive a budget update
every two months. Mr. Harrington pointed out that he would have the September sales tax figures
shortly.

Chair Harker was in favor of having a budget workshop wherein administration brought a list of
items it wished to return to the budget and the councilmembers would do the same.

Mr. Cowdell pointed out that increasing the budget would require a public noticing process so
administration would need a little more lead time than was typical at the mid-year point. It was
determined that the workshop would take place at 5:00 p.m. on November 4th immediately prior
to the regular meeting and at that point, they would determine if an additional workshop was
necessary.
