TIP Modification: SR92 Pedestrian Bridge – Bob Allen & Jim Price, MAG 2014 – Lehi applied for and received $2,983,550 to build a cycle track along 700 South. 2017- UTA, in coordination with other state and local agencies including MAG, applied for and received a federal TIGER grant to construct multiple bike/pedestrian facilities that included two pedestrian bridges in Utah County. One over SR92 in Lehi and one over the railroad tracks at the Provo Frontrunner Station. 2018 – UTA applied for and received $888,503 MAG exchange funds to pay the local match for the federal funds on the SR92 bridge. UDOT also contributed $1.2m to the bridge. 2019 – In anticipation of the Technology Corridor project in Lehi, Triumph Blvd was extended to I-15, severing the Historic Rail Trail next to the SR92 bridge which would hamper the effectiveness of the bridge. 2020 – The SR92 bridge needs an additional $1,045,549 for construction. Solution – Lehi would like to modify the scope of the 700 S cycle track project by removing the concrete barrier included in that scope. The limits of the project would remain the same. That scope reduction, in conjunction with the efforts of Lehi to have development acquire and build many portions of the cycle track project, would significantly reduce the costs of the project. The remaining funds would be allocated to complete the SR92 bridge and build a smaller pedestrian bridge to fill in the gap at Triumph Blvd. UTA will contribute an additional $95,000 to the SR92 bridge and MAG would add the 10% contingency funds from the 2018 UTA request equaling $88,850. With this solution, no new funds eligible to be allocated through the current TIP selection cycle would be used. Approve FY21 Unified Planning Work Program (UPWP) Executive Summary - Shawn Seager, MAG The July 1, 2020 to June 30, 2021 (FY21) Unified Planning Work Program (Work Program) describes the transportation planning work undertaken by Mountainland Metropolitan Planning Organization (MPO) Staff, Utah Department of Transportation Staff and the Utah Transit Authority Staff within the Mountainland MPO planning area. The Work Program is an annual budget. It illustrates to the members, Federal Highway Administration, Federal Transit Administration, and the general public where the planning resources are allocated. It describes the planned work, budget, and funding sources. This FY21 UPWP $ $2,571,482 budget is about the same when compared to last year’s $2,569,982 budget with a $1,500 increase from FY20. This is due to an increase in new studies and contracts. This budget proposes to spend $553,500 or 22% on new special studies including: 1. MAG Utah County Regional Highway Grid Network Study Approval $100,000 Planning Contingencies Funds – Tim Hereth, MAG MAG will begin a study of an urban area highway grid network based off ITE highway spacing guidelines and MAG RTP work. This work will include researching the Institute of Transportation Engineers (ITE) guidelines and create narrative. The study will be parsed into a quadrant level analysis with focus on network problem areas and demonstrate benefits and best practices of a connected regional highway grid network. A range of products for different urban types (greenfield vs. built-up environment) will be developed. Modeling will help identify facility size, road cross sections, highway spacing requirements, access management, the characteristics of different types of facilities and access types, and changes at the neighborhood level with grid connections. The study will also identify tiers in the regional system for potential ownership (local, county, state), funding options, cost by functional type by jurisdiction, and funding opportunities/funding gap. MPO Regional Planning Committee April 23, 2020 Page 5 The end product should include example codes/ordinances to aid cities in development process in creating the grid and demonstrate benefits of grid to the major highway (UDOT) system. Mayor Brunst moved that MPO Regional Planning Committee approve $100k to fund the Utah County Regional Highway Grid Network Study. Mayor Fugal seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.
