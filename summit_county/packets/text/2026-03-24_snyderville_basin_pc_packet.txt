Proposal 
The purpose of this work session is to  gather input from the Planning Commission on the overall 
direction, clarity, and intent of Chapters 5 and 6 of the draft General Plan.  
 
Background 
The Planning Commission has begun its review of the Draft Snyderville Basin General Plan. The 
purpose of this work session is to  continue that discussion and receive feedback on Chapter 5 
(Economic Development), and Chapter 6 (Natural and Cultural Resources).  
 
Chapter 5 creates a framework to support a diverse local economy in the Snyderville Basin while 
reinforcing the land use vision. It recognizes the Basin’s reliance on tourism and service -based 
industries, and encourages economic diversification, support for local -serving commercial areas, 
and improved coordination between employment, housing, and transportation planning. Policies 
emphasize reinvestment in established commercial centers, workforce housing as an economic 
strategy, support for entrepreneurship , and alignment of infrastructure investment with long -term 
economic opportunities.  
 
Chapter 6 provides direction to protect, conserve, and enhance the natural systems, scenic 
landscapes, and cultural resources of the Snyderville Basin. The chapter addresses critical  lands, 
wildlife habitat, water resources, and open space, while also recognizing the importance of historic 
features, agricultural heritage, and cultural programming in shaping community character. 
Additional policies support hazard resilience, climate -responsive planning, and scenic resource 
protection. Together, these strategies guide land use decisions to ensure growth occurs in a manner 
that sustains ecological function, preserves cultural legacy, and maintains the high quality of life 
valued by residents. 
 
Recommendation 
Staff recommend the Planning Commission review the proposed Amendments to Chapters 5 and 6 
of the Snyderville Basin General Plan and provide staff with direction.

Chapter 5: Economic Development 
 
The Economic Development Element establishes a policy framework to support a resilient 
and diverse local economy that complements the Snyderville Basin’s community character, 
environmental resources, and land use vision. While tourism and recreation will continue to 
play a central role in the Basin’s economic identity, this Element encourages economic 
opportunities that enhance long-term stability, support local employment, and contribute to 
overall quality of life. 
 
Implementation of this Element is closely coordinated with the County’s Economic 
Development Plan and other adopted planning efforts. Together, these documents guide 
decision-making related to business development, land use patterns, infrastructure 
investment, and workforce needs. 
 
Context 
 
Since the transition away from its historic mining economy, the Snyderville Basin has evolved 
into a community strongly inﬂuenced by tourism and recreation. Economic activity is 
shaped by regional visitation patterns, proximity to major transportation cor ridors, and 
access to outdoor amenities that attract both residents and visitors. The Basin’s economic 
vitality is closely tied to the availability of infrastructure and services, including utilities, 
transportation systems, educational facilities, housing options, and access to parks, trails, 
and open space. 
 
Kimball Junction functions as the Basin’s primary commercial and mixed-use center . 
Located near the intersection of Interstate 80 and State Route 224, it serves as a regional 
gateway and supports a concentration of retail, service, and employment uses that  attract 
both residents and visitors. While some neighborhood-scale commercial areas exist 
throughout the Basin, these areas are often limited in scope, resulting in a concentration of 
daily trips to Kimball Junction for goods, services, and employment. 
 
As the Snyderville Basin continues to evolve, Kimball Junction is expected to remain the 
primary commercial and mixed-use center serving both residents and the broader region. 
Future planning efforts will focus on supporting its role as an economic hub while improving 
connectivity, enhancing the pedestrian environment, and encouraging development 
patterns that better integrate employment, services, and housing. Strengthening Kimball 
Junction’s function as a cohesive community center will be important to reducing regional 
travel demand and supporting a more resilient local economy. 
 
Economic conditions in the Basin are also inﬂuenced by housing availability, workforce 
commuting patterns, and the seasonal nature of tourism. These dynamics create both 
opportunities and challenges for supporting a balanced local economy that can meet the 
needs of residents while maintaining the community’s character and environmental values. 
20
 
Existing Conditions 
 
Economic activity in the Snyderville Basin is strongly inﬂuenced by tourism, recreation, and 
regional service industries. Summit County currently supports approximately 35,800 jobs, a 
ﬁgure that exceeds the number of resident workers when secondary and s elf-employment 
positions are considered. Regional economic forecasts project continued employment 
growth, with an estimated 6,600 additional jobs anticipated countywide over the next 
decade. 
 
Within the Snyderville Basin, employment is concentrated in accommodation and food 
services, arts and recreation, construction, retail trade, and government sectors. Leisure 
and hospitality remain the dominant industries, reﬂecting the Basin’s role as a r egional 
recreation destination. Since the early 2000s, reliance on tourism -related employment has 
increased, with these sectors representing a substantial share of the local workforce. 
 
Wage levels in the Basin vary signiﬁcantly by industry. Service -oriented sectors such as 
leisure, hospitality, and retail provide a large share of available jobs but typically offer lower 
average wages than professional or regional employment centers. This dynamic contributes 
to workforce commuting patterns and inﬂuences housing demand, transportation needs, 
and economic resilience. 
 
Commuting patterns further illustrate the Basin’s role within the regional economy. Each 
day, a signiﬁcant number of workers travel into Summit County for employment, while many 
residents commute to higher -paying jobs outside the County. These patterns highlight the 
importance of coordinated economic, housing, and transportation planning to support long-
term economic stability. 
 
Access to infrastructure, including transportation systems, utilities, broadband connectivity, 
educational facilities, and recreational amenities, continues to shape economic 
opportunities in the Snyderville Basin. Maintaining a balanced mix of employment 
opportunities, supporting local -serving commercial areas, and strengthening connections 
between jobs and housing will be important considerations as the Basin continues to grow.

Chapter 6: Natural and Cultural Resources 
 
The Natural and Cultural Resources Element establishes policy direction to protect, 
conserve, and enhance the environmental systems and cultural assets that deﬁne the 
Snyderville Basin’s character and quality of life. Natural landscapes, open space, water  
resources, and historic features are foundational to the Basin’s identity and continue to 
shape land use decisions and community values. 
 
This Element works in coordination with the County’s Resource Management Plan (RMP), 
which addresses public lands and resource management strategies as required by state law. 
While the RMP focuses primarily on federally managed lands and statutory resource  
coordination, this Element provides guidance for the protection and stewardship of natural 
and cultural resources within the context of local land use, development, and community 
planning. 
 
Together, these planning efforts support long-term environmental resilience, preservation of 
scenic and ecological systems, and protection of cultural and historic resources important 
to the Basin’s heritage. 
 
Context 
 
The Snyderville Basin is characterized by a diverse natural landscape that includes forested 
mountain slopes, sagebrush uplands, riparian corridors, and valley -bottom wetlands. The 
Wasatch Mountains form a deﬁning backdrop and create signiﬁcant topographic variation, 
inﬂuencing settlement patterns, transportation networks, and ecological systems. 
 
Much of the Basin lies within the Lower Weber River watershed, where surface and 
groundwater systems provide essential drinking water, irrigation supply, wildlife habitat, and 
recreational opportunities. Key water features such as East Canyon Creek and Sil ver Creek 
are subject to seasonal variability, including drought conditions and spring runoff. Historic 
mining activities have also inﬂuenced water quality and landscape conditions in portions of 
the Basin, including areas associated with legacy contamination. 
 
Wildlife habitat and migration corridors remain important ecological features, supporting 
species such as mule deer, elk, and a variety of bird populations. As development pressures 
increase and climate conditions change, concerns related to wildﬁre risk,  habitat 
fragmentation, and water resource sustainability have become more prominent. 
 
Open space and recreation lands play a central role in shaping community identity and 
quality of life. Residents consistently identify access to natural landscapes, trails, and 
scenic resources as deﬁning characteristics of the Basin. Stewardship of these resources is 
supported through a combination of public land management, private land conservation 
efforts, and local partnerships. 
25
 
Cultural resources, including historic agricultural landscapes, mining -era features, and 
community gathering spaces, contribute to the Basin’s sense of place. While many historic 
economic activities have declined, their physical and cultural legacy continues to inﬂuence 
community identity and land use priorities. 
 
Existing Conditions 
 
Natural constraints signiﬁcantly inﬂuence development patterns in the Snyderville Basin. 
Areas classiﬁed as Critical Lands — including steep slopes, wetlands, ﬂoodplains, 
ridgelines, avalanche zones, and geologic hazard areas — represent a substantial portion of 
the Basin’s land area. These areas are subject to development limitations intended to 
protect public safety, environmental systems, and scenic resources. Where development 
has previously been entitled or avoidance is not feasible, regulations re quire sensitive site 
design and mitigation measures. 
 
Publicly managed lands also comprise a meaningful share of the Basin’s planning area, 
contributing to regional recreation opportunities, habitat protection, and scenic character. 
These lands play an important role in shaping growth patterns and long -term r esource 
management considerations. 
 
The Basin contains several historically signiﬁcant structures and sites, along with public art 
installations and community gathering spaces that reﬂect local heritage and cultural values. 
Preservation and interpretation of these resources contribute to community identity and 
tourism appeal. 
 
Community engagement efforts indicate strong support for environmental stewardship and 
conservation. Residents consistently identify protection of open space, natural ecosystems, 
and water resources as priorities for maintaining quality of life. Volunteeri sm and 
participation in stewardship activities further demonstrate community commitment to 
resource protection, while interest in preserving agricultural heritage and cultural 
landscapes remains an ongoing theme.

Background 
The subject parcel (SL-E-304) is located within the Silver Creek Estates Unit E Subdivision, which 
currently contains 64 lots. Lot 304 is a 2.53 -acre parcel located in the Rural Residential (RR) 
Zone. The property currently contains a single -family dwelling and an attached garage 
constructed in 1996. The primary dwelling has an approximate living area of 1,484 square feet, 
and the attached garage is approximately 547 square feet.  The applicant is proposing to 
construct a detached accessory building with a total area of approximately 3,240 square feet. 
The proposed structure will function as a detached garage for personal storage and use. 
 
Pursuant to Section 10 -2-10 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code, a major accessory 
building within the Rural Residential (RR) Zone requires approval of a Conditional Use Permit 
(CUP). A major accessory building is defined as a building or structure with a footprint greater 
than 2,000 square feet but not exceeding 12,500 square feet. This provision exists because 
major accessory buildings, depending on their use and design, can have impacts on the 
surrounding neighborhood. The CUP process ensures that such proposals receive special review 
and that potential impacts are considered before approval. 
 
Community Development staff, in coordination with applicable service providers, has reviewed 
the application and determined that the project, as conditioned, complies with the applicable 
requirements of the Snyderville Basin Development Code.

Findings of Fact: 
1.  On December 22, 2025, the Summit County Community Development Department 
received a complete application for a Conditional Use Permit (CUP) for a Major 
Accessory Building at 547 E Parleys Rd, Park City. 
2. The property is identified as Parcel SL-E-304 and it is 2.53 acres. 
3. Parcel SL-E-304 is a lot of record located within the Silver Creek Estates Unit E 
subdivision.  
4.  Parcel SL-E-304 is located within the Rural Residential zone. 
5. According to Summit County records, the property is owned by Tyler Living Trust C/O 
Katherine Tyler Trustee. Katherine Tyler signed the CUP application.  
6. The property is currently developed with a SFD and an attached garage.  
7. The purpose of the application is to request approval for the construction of a 3,240-
square-foot major accessory building. 
8. The proposed accessory building is for owner personal use and no commercial activity is 
proposed.  
9. The height of the proposed Major Accessory Building is ~25 ft.  
10. The setbacks of the proposed Major Accessory Building are as follows: Front ~ 361 ft 
Side ~ 172 ft (northeast side), ~15 ft (southwest side) Rear ~ 15 ft.  
11. Snow removal and storage locations are shown on the site plan. There is sufficient 
space. 
12. Service Area 3 is the water provider for this area. The applicant submitted a 
commitment of service letter dated March 5, 2026. 
13. The proposal will not dramatically affect solid waste or recycling systems. The property 
has multiple locations to fully enclose garbage and recycling containers, including the 
existing garage attached to the Single-Family Dwelling and also within the proposed 
Accessory Building. 
14. There are no known wetlands on the property. Slopes do not exceed 30 percent, and 
the site is not located on a ridgeline or within an avalanche zone. No geologic hazards 
have been identified. 
15. The proposed development is not anticipated to negatively impact public roads or 
services. 
16. No impacts to police or security services are anticipated. The property provides 
adequate access for law enforcement. 
17. The application has been reviewed by staff and applicable service providers and has 
been found to comply with all relevant standards and requirements.
