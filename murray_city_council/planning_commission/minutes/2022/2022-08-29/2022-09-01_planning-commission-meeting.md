STEP ABOVE DAYCARE – 404 East 4500 South – Project 22-133
Mr. Rios said the applicant is wanting to open a daycare at the address above, in a commercial
office building that is already existing in the General Office (G-O) zone. North of 4500 South
Street is Millcreek, to the south is residential R-1.8, single family, and to the east is multi-family.
Mr. Rios reviewed the building and proposed layout and floorplan. They will be taking up most
of the first floor. Most regulations for daycares come from the state, and there is a whole chapter
of state code dedicated to that. The applicant is working with someone from the state to meet all
those requirements before they open the daycare; that has been listed as one of the conditions
of approval for this permit. Staff recommends approval with the listed conditions, and the
additional requirement that they must comply with State Code Title R430.
Hajie Gal, applicant, stated he has reviewed the packet and staff conditions of approval and
they will be in full compliance. As Mr. Rios informed the commissioners, they are actually in a
working relationship with the childcare licensing from the state. The state came and reviewed
the property and had no issues with the property. The only thing the daycare has to do is put in
a fence, and that is something they will be working on. They were also told to schedule for the
fire marshal to come in and do a fire inspection, but other than that everything is good.
Ms. Milkavich asked about the number of employees, hours of operation and estimated number
of children they are planning for. Mr. Gal said many of their kids live in Murray and they will be
doing a lot of pick up and drop off for the local schools. When childcare licensing came and saw
the square footage, they said that based on that they could have anywhere from 75-100 kids.
There will be a daycare facility center director and between 10-15 staff. The hours of operation
will be from 6:00 a.m. to about 9:00 p.m., because some parents come home late.

MURRAY THEATER RENOVATION – 4949 South State Street – Project #22-122
Mr. Smallwood said this is a request from Murray City Parks & Recreation to renovate the
existing Murray Theater, so this is a design review application. He showed aerial pictures from
the staff report and explained what is in the surrounding area. This is in the Murray City Center
Zoning District, which is largely the downtown area. City Code states that any modifications to
buildings listed on the historic register, which this building is, does require a design review
approval after a recommendation from the MCCD Committee. There are lots of interior
renovations going on here, they are going to accommodate approximately 300 seats; the interior
is not up for debate tonight, as they can do whatever they want inside the building. The seating
was sunken down originally, but the previous owners built it up, and it is being put back as it
was originally. The existing marquis will come down, but will be built back up as it was originally
in 1938. He showed renderings from the proposed inside, as well as the outside as shown in
the staff report. The MCCD Review Committee voted 3-0 to forward a positive recommendation
to the planning commission. They did discuss planters in lieu of street trees that would normally
be required in the zone, due to the historic nature of the marquis; they don’t want to block the
marquis with trees. The MCCD Review Committee and Planning Division Staff recommend the
planning commission approve the design review application for the Murray Theater at the
address above, subject to conditions one through three.
Mr. Nay asked why renovation plans are being built for an older population versus someone 25
years old. He noted that many people don’t want to have to sit during a concert, along with a
large number of performers that aren’t willing to play to a seated audience.

a show.

ORDINANCE TEXT AMENDMENT – Chapter 17.6 Appeal Authority – Project #22-129
Mr. Smallwood presented a request from Murray City Planning Division Staff, in conjunction with
the attorney’s office. They have been discussion a text amendment to appeals and variances in
the Title 17 Land Use Ordinances. Specifically, most of the changes are in section 17.16, and it
is being changed to Land Use Appeals and Variances. This also affects the definition section,
and various other sections throughout the code where there were specific timeframes for appeal
dates and deadlines; those timelines will be repealed from those sections to bring everything
Planning Commission Meeting
September 1, 2022
Page 9

into one easy to read and understand language. A few of the larger changes are the definition
changes and unclear descriptions regarding appeal instructions. As Senior Planner, one of his
objectives and goals is to go through the land use ordinance and do some clean up, including
moving every definition to the Greater Definition section, instead of each chapter having its own
definition section. Next, there were unclear directions as to who directly a grieved person was in
the code; without a hard definition, anyone could apply to appeal a decision, regardless. The
state made a change recently to their code stating that “an adversely affected party is a person
who owns real property adjoining the property that is subject to the land use application; or will
suffer damage different in kind or an injury distinct from that of the general community as a
result of the decision.” That definition helped to narrow the field to people having a direct impact
from a project.
Mr. Lowry appreciates what’s being done and wonders if in the language given by the state
above it opens things up and makes it broader for someone to claim emotional/psychological
damage. They have had someone in the past claim potential psychological/emotional damage
from walking past a building that used to be a cherished memory for them.
Mr. Smallwood that could be a case, but they would have to submit that appeal and staff, the
attorney’s office and the hearing officer would decide further steps together.
Mr. Lowry noted that maybe there should not be a semicolon after “or land use decision,” just a
period and it stops there.
Mr. Smallwood said they are just using the state definition, and it has been copied exactly from
them.
Ms. Milkavich asked if the state could trump the city if there was an issue, and if so this is a
smart practice to follow their lead.
Mr. Smallwood said it is easier to follow them, then to work against them. They also moved the
definitions to the appropriate section. They will have clearer text, and he gave examples from a
previous section where things were all in one section, but not specific. Hardship tests need to be
specific to the appeal, so those were broken out into an appeal process and variance process,
to make things more clear. As discussed in the pre-meeting, there were many locations in the
code that gave different timelines for appealing a land use decision. Everything was narrowed
down into one section, where the timeline is 10 calendar days from the written land use
decision; this is the same timeline the state has adopted.
Mr. Nay asked if staff feels like that’s giving an aggrieved party enough to time to get everything
together and make an appeal.
Mr. Smallwood said the 10 days doesn’t start until a written decision is given. For a conditional
use permit, that would mean the countdown doesn’t start until the commission approves the
findings of fact. In theory, that should give them plenty of time to get their appeal completed.
