  • Greater reliance on Mutual Aid from outside agencies.
  • Loss of revenue from EMS transports and IFT transports.
  • Increased employee burnout and decreased job satisfaction.
  • Decreased time for community engagement and education.

   •   FTE additions were spread over three budget years.

   • Ongoing maintenance and operational costs.

• Increase in fleet procurement and operational costs.
   • Additional vehicles were added to fleet.
   • Inflation, fuel, maintenance cost increases.

• Increased cost for equipment.
   • Inflation, technological advances, supply shortages.
   • Additional requisite safety equipment provided to personnel.
Results of Increased Funding
• Fire Department response capability has increased by 33%.

• Increased capacity to handle calls for service within the City.

• Overall response times have decreased throughout the City.

• Better Fire and EMS coverage and decreased response times
  to ~30% the City’s west side landmass.

• Increased revenue from EMS and IFT transports has been
  realized due to better balance in transport unit availability.
Results of Increased Funding
• Increased service levels in Fire, EMS, Hazmat, and Technical
  Rescue response.

• Preparing to launch Critical Care Paramedic Program.
  • Increased capabilities and service to the community.


• A more balanced capacity for the Department to handle calls
  for service generated in South Jordan.

• Decrease in reported employee burnout and increase in
  overall employee satisfaction.
Current Operational Balance
• The Fire Department is currently able to achieve an appropriate
  balance between demands for service and ability to provide service.

• As a result, Fire and EMS units are available more often to respond
  to calls within South Jordan, thereby reducing reliance on Mutual
  Aid agencies.

• Having an appropriate number of Medic Ambulances has resulted in
  decreased ambulance response times, more ambulances available
  to respond, resulting in increased hospital transports.

• Transport revenue to the City has increased, with less revenue being
  lost to outside ambulance providers. This funding is used to offset
  Fire Department operational costs and capital expenses.
Budget Savings and Efficiencies
• Fire Engines have been redesigned to better serve the
  community and respond more efficiently.
  • These design changes have decreased the vehicle size and
    reduced procurement, maintenance, and operational costs.
  • Advanced fleet planning resulted in hundreds of thousands of
    dollars in savings through chassis prepayments and by avoiding
    inflationary price increases.

• Ambulance boxes have been designed to be remounted on a
  new chassis at the end of vehicle service life.
  • Remounting and refurbishing ambulance boxes on a new chassis
    has and will continue to save tens of thousands of dollars per
    vehicle and will extend the usable lifespan of our highest demand
    fleet vehicles.
Budget Savings and Efficiencies
• Continued evaluation of supply sources to find most
  economical procurement opportunities.
  • Examples:
     • Medical supply purchasing (e.g. medical glove costs were reduced by
       ~$20/box.)
     • Firefighter PPE costs were reduced by ~$200/person.
     • Training/Certification cooperatives with Jordan Valley Medical Center
       and other fire agencies, which have significantly reduced annual
       certification and training costs.

• Aggressive Lateral Firefighter Hiring Program
  • Has reduced or eliminated initial certification training for these
    employees.
  • Has significantly reduced on the job training hours.
  • Has rapidly increased service level capability to our community.

• Addition of Fire Station 65.
  • Station 65 response area is currently being developed.
  • Emergency response times will increase as development grows
    away from existing Fire Department facilities.
  • Call growth will eventually overcome current service capabilities.
  • Adding additional FTEs will be the largest financial hurdle.
  • 18-21 FTEs will be needed depending on timing of the addition.

• Mid to Long-term plan to add second fire crew to Fire Station
  64. (Dependent on speed of development)
  • As this area grows in population, roof tops, and infrastructure,
    calls for service will outpace a single fire company’s capabilities.
  • 15 additional FTEs will be needed to add one Ladder Truck.
  • 6 additional FTEs will be needed to add one Medic Ambulance.
