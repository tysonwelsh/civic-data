Discussion Items

       Reimagining Employee Skills

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, presented a spreadsheet showing the
employment positions that require a college degree. There are 22 positions that require a
degree which equates to 13.63% of the overall positions in the City.

David Brickey, City Manager, said a number of positions could be changed from degree
required to experience matched. Mr. Hicks agreed that some positions could be changed,
however, there are other positions that eliminating the degree requirement could lower the City’s
fraud risk assessment score.

         Municipal Waste Hauling Discussion

Craig Calvert, Contract Administrator and Purchasing Manager, said that Waste Management
was the lowest bid for the RFP and has been awarded the contract. He displayed the quote
from Waste Management with comparisons to the current costs.

Mayor Staggs commented that he thought the price would be lower since the City owns the
cans. In response, Mr. Calvert said that recycling has increased which accounts for the overall
increase in this bid. He also explained that Waste Management’s quote was $700,000 lower
than the next bid.

Councilmember Buroker commented that she was surprised that the waste part of the contract
decreased and asked if this bid will be affected by TransJordan’s move to their new location.
Mr. Hicks said the City pays TransJordan directly so this change will not impact this bid.

Councilmember McDougal expressed his concern that the City will not get the benefit from
recycling because there are no foreign markets for recycling and it is very expensive to recycle
locally.

Mayor Staggs asked if the City needed to determine at this time whether to continue recycling
Mr. Calvert said that the bid shows the worst-case scenario for recycling and explained that the
City does not need to decide now whether to continue the recycling program. Either party could
terminate the recycling portion of the contract with adequate notice.

Mr. Calvert explained that Waste Management wanted to include contract language for
residents that continually contaminate the recycling or overfill the waste cans. Councilmembers
and Mayor discussed this idea and asked for a tour of the facility to see how the Smart Truck
technology works.

Mr. Calvert said he will draft a contract with the language discussed and bring this back to
Council.

residents will know how to contact Google, where they are working, etc.

Casey Saxton, Communications Director, said the website will be rivertonutah.gov/googlefiber,

the items discussed was the southwest Salt Lake County vested housing units. The goal was to
provide a tool for legislators to see the reality of housing in the southwest portion of the County.

Mayor Staggs said at the state level the southwest mayors want to focus on the completion of
Mountain View Corridor and Bangerter Highway, UTA transit, and highway U-111. The
southwest mayors try to meet every few months to coordinate projects and initiatives.

Mayor Staggs reiterated his concern with UTA transit and the fact that southwest cities are
subsidizing other areas in Salt Lake County. He said that UTA’s five-year plan shows $1.2
billion on the capital budget side which doesn’t include a single project in the southwest portion
of the County. He encouraged residents to complete the UTA survey because if UTA doesn’t
receive feedback, they will not change their five year plan. He would like Councilmembers and
staff to participate on November 7 at West Jordan between 4-6:30 pm to talk to Wasatch
Regional about the regional transportation plan.

Councilmember Wells commented that he agreed with the conversations about Waste
Management.

Councilmember Buroker asked when the dog ordinance will be updated and brought to the
Council. Mr. Lethbridge said it should be ready at the next council meeting.

Councilmember Buroker commented the Mosquito Abatement hires a consultant every 5-8
years to review their buildings and equipment to determine a plan for when things need to be
repaired or replaced. She would like the same system in effect for the fire stations owned by
Riverton City.

Councilmember McDougal commented on the transportation data that was presented by Mayor
Staggs. He would like to shine a light on this information and continue to talk about these
issues. He thanked Mayor Staggs and said he was behind him one-hundred percent.

         Resolution No. 22-65 – Amending the Municipal Fee Schedule for the 2022-2023
         Fiscal Year

Mr. Hicks explained the City has a handful of fire hydrant meters available for rent so entities
can utilize and pay for water from the City’s fire hydrants. As part of the hydrant rental process,
the renter must pay a $1,000 refundable security deposit which is intended to cover the full cost
of replacing a meter in the event the meter is broken or is not returned. Staff recommends that
the deposit fee be changed from $1,000 to $2,000 because the current replacement cost for a
meter is $1,850.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the public hearing.

Councilmember Buroker MOVED that the City Council approve Resolution No. 22-65
amending the municipal fee schedule for the 2022-2023 fiscal year. Councilmember Wells
SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs
called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: Buroker-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes,
and Wells-yes. The motion passed unanimously.
