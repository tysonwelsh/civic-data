Commissioner DeFord asked how they determine that the road can no longer handle 
the traffic.  County Engineer Horton responded the purpose of a traffic study is to 
gather the existing traffic information of the area.  It is assigned a level of service (LOS) 
from an “A” to an “F.”  “A” means the traffic is flowing well.  “F” means the road is failing.  
When it falls to a LOS “E”, they start looking at making improvements.   
 
Engineer Horton said if someone can’t get through an intersection within two or three 
lights, that is a failing condition.  Waiting for one light is either an “A” or a “B.”  A LOS “C” 
would be when someone has to wait for a second light.  The studies are concerned with 
the peak times.  Typically, these hours are 4:00 p.m. to 6:00 p.m.  If congestion occurs, 
the problem starts creeping into the a.m. hours.  The worst hour during the peak time is 
identified.   
 
Engineer Horton said there are times on Highway 224 during peak hours that it is 
failing.  He said the road by the Weilenmann school has a 15 minute peak rush time.  
Typically, they don’t analyze or make improvements for special situations.  When they 
start seeing these on a repeat basis, the County will make recommendations.   
 
Engineer Horton said the developers recently completed another traffic study because 
he wanted to know if a separate turning lane was needed.  They discovered that will not 
be required even taking into consideration this project.  Commissioner DeFord asked 
Attorney Thomas what is the LOS grade before the County starts to consider taking 
action.  Attorney Thomas said that isn’t identified in the Code.
