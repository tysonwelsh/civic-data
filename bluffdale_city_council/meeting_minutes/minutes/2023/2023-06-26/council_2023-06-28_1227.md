       8.1     Ordinance No.2023-13-Impact Fee Plan and Analysis(StaffPresenter, Michael
               Fazio),


City Engineer, Michael Fazio reported that the hnpact Fee Plan and Analysis are reviewed as needed.
He introduced City Consultant, Cody Deters who was instrumental in preparing the new update. The
City is growing so updates to the Impact Fee Plan are needed regularly. The last update was in
2020/2021 as a result of pressure from commercial entities.


Mr. Deters thanked staff who have been wonderful to work with. He reported that the statute specifies
that an impact fee is the payment of money imposed upon new development activity as a condition of
development approval to mitigate the impact of new development on public infrastructure. When
someone connects to a City system there is a financial impact and they are using capacity that is either
already built or will need to be built. They need to pay their proportionate share of that impact. It
applies to new homes and new businesses but does not apply to someone who is remodeling their
home or finishing their basement.


Mr. Reid reported that for those who may be tearing down an existing home and rebuilding a new
one,the impact fee has already been paid on the old home and does not apply. The impact fee applies
only to new builds.


Mr. Deters explained that the impact fee methodology that they utilized was based on statute, which
requires that impact fee money be spent within six years ofbeing collected. The planning horizon is
between zero and 10 years. The first step is to look at demand and determine what is going to happen
in the City over the next 10 years in terms of residential and commercial growth. They also look at
the current level of service being provided to constituents. They need to perpetuate that forward and
continue to provide that level of service. The first place they will look to satisfy that demand on the
system is excess capacity in the current facilities that can be utilized. In the event there is no excess
capacity, they will need to build new facilities to meet that demand.


Mr. Deters commented that they next look at funding and how to pay for new infrastructure or
infrastructure that is already in place with excess capacity. Grant money is something that cannot be

used for impact fees. If the City goes into debt and issues bonds, the interest-carrying cost and the
cost to put the bonds in place can be included in the impact fee.

Mr. Deters reported that in September 2020,the City adopted impact fees requiring residential to pay
$17,426, multi-family pays 75% ofthat total. It was proposed that the impact fee be increased slightly
to $17,643 or $13,232 for multi-family. Park and stormwater impact fees increased while public
safety, transportation, and water decreased. Retail entities will now pay $10,964 per 1,000 square
feet for one Equivalent Residential Unit("ERU").


Mr. Reid pointed out that with commercial, the City does not collect an impact fee for Parks.
Mr. Deters addressed demand and the various methodology components. Staff and the City
Consultants reviewed all ofthe land use in the City and determined the likely development pattern in
the next 10 years. As of last year, there were 5,857 ERUs. Over the next 10 years, they anticipate
adding 1,769 including 1,535 new residential units and 234 commercial units. Transportation is
measured based on the number of new trips that are on the road. What is estimated is 36,208 new
daily trips, which is significant.


Level of service issues were discussed. For Parks, the measurement for the level of service is how
much money the City has invested into park facilities per residential ERU. Currently, the City has
invested $7,597. The desire was to continue to perpetuate that level ofservice into the future. Some
have contested how the measurement is calculated but it is the best for the City in Mr. Deter's opinion
because the money can be invested any way the Council sees fit as long as it is Parks and Recreation
Services.


The level ofservice for Public Safety is the square footage of building space per ERU, which is 4.39
square feet per ERU for residential and commercial. For commercial, a component can also be
charged for equipment that is over $500,000. The commercial equipment expense per ERU is $2,519.
The level of service for Transportation is known as Service Level D, which is a term of art in
transportation planning. The level of service for Stormwater storage is a 100-year storm event over
a 24-hour period. It will drain fiilly in 48 hours. For conveyance, a 10-year storm event over 24
hours.

Mr, Deters reported that the level of service for Culinary and Pressured Water is driven by State
standards that deal with source,storage, and pressure during peak flow events. With regard to existing
capacity, when new demand comes on the system they are assuming that for Parks,Public Safety, and
Stormwater there is no excess capacity. For water, the analysis shows there is approximately $6
million in excess capacity. The Zone 1 West Tank,Zone 1 West Transmission,the South Pressurized
Irrigation ("PI")Pump Station, and the proportionate share ofthe bonds that were issued in 2020. In
Transportation, there is $7.18 million in excess capacity in the seven-road segments. Every time
someone connects, it is analyzed. Each time they perform an impact fee analysis they see with
individual roads have had their capacity reduced. At some point, all ofthe capacity will be gone and
the City will have repaid the General Fund for the costs.
