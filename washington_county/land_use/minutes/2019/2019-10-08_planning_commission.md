Washington County Planning Commission Meeting
                                     October 08, 2019

The Washington County Land Use Authority Meeting was held on Tuesday, October 08, 2019 in the
Council Chambers of the Washington County Administration Building at 197 E. Tabernacle, St.
George, UT. Chairman Slade Hughes opened the meeting at 1:30 p. m. He led the Pledge of Allegiance
and then explained meeting protocol.

Commissioners present: Brandon Anderson, Shayne Durrant, Kevin Jones, Reed Scow (Alternate),
Doug Solstad, and Jon Walter.

Excused: Dan Briggs (Alternate).

Staff present: Scott Messel, Community Development Director; Natalie Nelson, Deputy County
Attorney; Doreen Bowers-Irons, Administrative Assistant; Kurt Gardner, Building Official; Todd
Edwards, County Engineer; Kim Hafen, County Clerk.


1.      AGRICULTURAL PROTECTION AREA. Review request for an Agricultural Protection
Area Application of 288 acres on multiple parcels within Pine Valley. The parcels are located mainly
on the north side of Pine Valley. The applicants are DeMar LLC, 3U Ranch LLC, SJ and Mary Ann
Atkin, and RL and Janece Gardner.

Mr. Messel provided maps and documentation of the proposal. He explained that the State of Utah
enacted Agricultural Protection Areas in the mid 1990’s. Properties in an Agricultural Protection Area
receive tax benefits, protection from nuisance lawsuits, and enforcement if the uses are part of normal
farming practices. Several property owners of real property are requesting approval to place their
parcels located in the meadow area of Pine Valley into the Agriculture Protection Area. The parcels
are generally located north of 100 North. Additionally, several parcels located south of 100 North and
Main Street are included in this request. He presented a map of the requested areas. There are a large
number of parcels included in this request. The total acreage is 288.38 acres. Please see attachment 2
for a list of parcels. Code requires that both the Agricultural Advisory Board and the Planning
Commission review the Agricultural Protection Area request and make a recommendation to the
County Commission. The County Commission holds a public hearing and approves or denies the
request. The Agricultural Advisory Board has reviewed the request and has recommended that the
County Commission approve the Agriculture Protection Area.

Recommendation:

The Planning Commission can recommend approval based on the following findings:

 1. The Agricultural Protection Area was properly noticed.
 2. The request is appropriate for the subject parcels.
 3. The Agricultural Advisory Board recommends approval.

Commissioner Walter clarified and confirmed the number of parcels and impact this may have on the
applicants. He asked if there were any significant easements or right-of-ways that other individuals
would be adversely affected by this use.

2.      PUBLIC HEARING /ZONE CHANGE. Review of a zone change request for Parcel 7006-
A-7-NC, from the existing A-20 (Agricultural 20 acre minimum lot size) zone to the A-10 (Agricultural
10 acre minimum lot size) zone. The parcel is 10 acres in size and generally located at approximately
2700 East and 1100 North in Enterprise. The applicants are Augustin and Hermelinda Lares.

Mr. Messel stated the applicants are requesting to change the zoning designation of Parcel 7006-A-7-
NC, which is located at 2700 East 1100 North in Enterprise, from the current A-20 (Agricultural 20
acre minimum lot size) zone to the A-10 (Agricultural 10 acre minimum lot size) zone. The applicants
would like to build single family dwelling on the lot. However, because the existing zoning is A-20
(Agricultural 20 acre minimum lot size) and the parcel is only 10 acres in size, the parcel does not meet
the current zoning requirement which, prohibits the applicants from building the home. The applicants
have submitted the request to rezone the parcel to the A-10 (Agricultural 10 acre minimum lot size)
zone in order to move forward to construct the home.

Recommendation:

Staff reviewed the requested zone change from the A-20 (Agricultural 20 acre minimum lot size) zone
to the A-10 (Agricultural 10 acre minimum lot size) zone and recommends that the commission hold
the public hearing and if they are comfortable with the project moving forward, may recommend the
County Commission approve the zone change request, based on the following findings and conditions:

  1. The zone change and public hearing were properly noticed.

3.      PUBLIC HEARING/ZONE CHANGE. Review of a zone change request of Parcels NCR-
2-2-A-1-NS and NCR-2-2-NS from the existing RA-10 (Residential Agricultural 10 acre minimum lot
size) zone to the RA-5 (Residential Agricultural 5 acre minimum lot size) zone. The parcels total
approximately 6.88 acres in size and generally located at approximately 658 N. Harmony Drive in New
Harmony. The applicants are Washington Wheat and Live Stock/Marc Brown, Agent.

Mr. Messel advised the commission that Staff is requesting the Planning Commission table this item
until more information can be provided. He explained that over time, there have been several lot line
adjustments, subdivisions, amended plats, additional lot line adjustments, and what could be considered

illegal lot splits. In looking at it, staff thought there should be some cleanup and make sure everything
is in compliance. The public hearing was noticed and there are people who wish to speak on the item.
We also received several letters of concern for the zone change. The planning commission is welcome
to take public comments and then take those comments into consideration or we can re-notice it for a
future meeting when all the concerns are addressed.

Commissioner Walter stated he would be interested in hearing from the public. The commissioners

Chairman Hughes opened the floor to the public hearing.

Tamara Taylor asked the commission to give consideration to the reasoning the request was tabled
relating to the plats, subdivisions, etc. Also, she was concerned with unpermitted buildings, bridges
blocking water flow, and flooding. Her home is below the proposed development and has been flooded
several times. She stated she objected to this project as it will impact the area in a negative way.

Bob Gowdy voiced his concerns of lack of infrastructure, water, and ingress and egress to the lot. He
objected to the project.

Theresa Larson claimed to be a 6th generation New Harmony resident. She pointed out that there is
great heritage to the historic town. She urged the commission to look at this several times before
allowing this development to move forward. She objected to the project and asked the commission to
consider the impact to the town it would create.

Commissioner Owens asked Mr. Messel if these issues had been presented in the past meetings. Mr.
Messel answered no, this goes back to there being several amended plats, lot line adjustments, zone
changes and the things that had been done in the past. Staff just wants to make sure that they’re
presenting what was actually approved that can now be moved forward. It appears some of the concerns
are related to utilities. They will need to meet the subdivision standards.

Commissioner Jones asked if all the parcels were owned by the same entity. Mr. Messel disclosed that
at one time they were but now there are several different owners. Mr. Hal Torgerson, who is now
deceased, was the developer of the majority of the properties.

Keith Harmon pointed out his property on the map noting that his property borders the 6.8 acres parcel.
He and his wife moved there for the rural area and would like it to remain so. He was concerned with
the water system and the fire hydrants which, are questionable. They are on a well and he is not positive
of the lines to feed the project. He was opposed to the project and its impacts to the community.

The water service to the North Valley Ranch Subdivision was briefly discussed. It was pointed out a
private well supplies water to the 7 residential lots.

Lisa Beck, stated she and her husband own lot NCR-2-1-A-NS. She had a power point presentation
she wanted to present but due to technical difficulties was not able to. She summarized the information
that was on the power point. She provided the history of the subdivision and the lot size changes made
over time. She claimed that the surrounding property owners had never been notified of any of the
changes. She suggested that the county require notification signs on the property being changed for the
surrounding neighbors to be aware of said change. She explained the questions and feedback for the
