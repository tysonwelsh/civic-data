Tim Metler of Metler Real Estate presented the project considerations and provided answers to
Council’s questions regarding cost, parking, and rental contracts.

Chair Hoban asked for Eric Chase’s opinion on the matter since he is the neighborhood chair of the area.

Eric Chase, neighborhood chair of the North Park neighborhood, discussed some of the changes he
would make if he were the developer. He said there is high turnover in the area and almost all residents
are students. He shared his belief that more housing, even with less parking, would be a greater benefit
to the area and City.

Councilor MacKay asked if Mr. Chase had received any negative feedback regarding parking.

Councilor Ellsworth asked if the loss of historic character and detached single family homes were a
concern for the neighborhood.

Mr. Chase said part of living in an evolving city is to adapt to the changes. He explained that the area
needs more density housing.

Chair Hoban opened public comment.

Jeff Whitlock of Provo agreed that there is a need for more affordable married housing. He expressed
his concern for the limited parking in the area. He asked if Council has authority to enforce conditions
discussed.

Zeke Peters of Provo shared his experience of finding an apartment as a newly married couple. On the
other hand, he described the minimal effort it has taken to sell his current apartment due to the high
demand of married housing. He also mentioned that high density does not always equate to parking
issues.

Chris West of Provo echoed that there is a short supply of housing, and he does not think parking will be
as big of an issue as others presume.

Chair Hoban closed public comment.

Councilor Handley shared his support for this ordinance.

Brian Jones mentioned transportation demand management (TDM) plans and explained how city code is
set up to handle them. He also provided information on the process planning commission takes
regarding development agreements and denials.

Chair Hoban asked what developer plans to limit within the contracts.

Mr. Metler responded.

Brian Jones restated and clarified response. He also mentioned the inability to enforce after phase two is
complete based on the development agreement.

Councilor Whipple asked how the advertising would be for the number of cars.

Chair Hoban asked for clarification on which line on the map they are referencing.

Todd Provost of UTA clarified that the darkest color is the road they are referring to. He also clarified
that the other lines are routes that were being reviewed by UTA.

Councilor Fillmore asked where on the map the other stations can be seen.

Dixon Holmes responded that the only station identified on the map is the one in Provo and the others
have not been determined yet.

Councilor Ellsworth shared her concern with proposed plan regarding access for residents who live on
the other side of the railroad.

Mary De La Mare-Schaefer of UTA replied that this project was not intended to solve all transportation
issues but to focus on high-capacity transit in a major corridor.

Mr. Holmes added that there are over 3,500 rooftops currently built or will be built that fall within a half
mile radius of the current station.

Chair Hoban opened item for public comment.

Mike Fugal of Provo asked what the resolution obligates the city to do.

Chair Hoban responded.

Councilor Fillmore stated he is in favor of this resolution.

Mayor Kaufusi said the administration in the southern cities are in support and shared her support as
well.

Chair Hoban called for a vote.

      Vote:     The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Hoban,
                Mackay, Shipley, and Whipple in favor.
