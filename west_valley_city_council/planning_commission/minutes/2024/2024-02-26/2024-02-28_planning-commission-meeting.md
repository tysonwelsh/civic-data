West Valley City
A zone text change to amend Sections 7-1-109, 7-2-117, 7-10-103, 7-13-517, and 7-14-102 to
increase the construction threshold that triggers certain improvements and to allow the
option of payment in lieu of installation of off-site improvements.

In conjunction with Public Works staff, Community Development staff is proposing an
ordinance change to amend Sections 7-1-109, 7-2-117, 7-10-103, 7-13-517, and 7-14-102 to
increase the construction threshold that triggers certain improvements and to allow the option of
payment in lieu of installation of off-site improvements.
In September of 1984, the City adopted an ordinance (Ordinance 84-38) that required 20 feet of
landscaping along high-image arterials with “any substantial modification to an existing site or
structure in which the estimated construction cost exceeds $50,000”. Later, in March of 1988, the
City adopted an ordinance (Ordinance 88-08) that required curb, gutter, and sidewalk
improvements when “estimated construction costs exceed $50,000 for substantial modifications
to an existing site or structure”. While other improvements like streetlights and street paving
have since been added to the ordinance, the $50,000 construction threshold has remained in place
since the adoption of these ordinances.

Using the CPI (Consumer Price Index) Inflation Calculator from the Bureau of Labor Statistics,
$50,000 in September of 1984 would be worth $146,070 today. Using the same calculator,
$50,000 in March of 1988 would be worth $131,651 today. Hence, staff is recommending an
increase in this threshold from $50,000 to $150,000 to account for inflation.

Staff is also recommending adding the option of payment in lieu of installation of off-site
improvements. Currently, whenever off-site improvements like streetlights, street paving, curb,
gutter, and sidewalk are required, the ordinance allows: 1) the installation of the improvements,
2) a bond guaranteeing the completion of the improvements, or 3) at the discretion of the City, a
delay agreement. The City Council has generally chosen not to approval delay agreements over
the last few years. In addition, Public Works has found that exercising delay agreements has
proven to be difficult. As such, staff is proposing the addition of a payment in lieu of the
installation of off-site improvements as another alternative to installation, particularly where
there are no improvements on adjoining properties to connect to. The use of this new option
would be at the City Engineer’s sole discretion.

This application was continued by the Planning Commission during their regular meeting on
February 14th. The Commission asked that the City Engineer, Coby Wilson, provide additional
information on the cash in lieu of improvements option proposed in this application. Mr. Wilson
attended the Commission’s study meeting on February 21st to help explain when and why this
option would be used.

Staff Alternatives:

ZT-2-2024
West Valley City
A zone text change to amend Sections 7-9-104 and 7-9-105 to add or
Amend parking requirements for several land uses

City staff is proposing an ordinance change to amend Sections 7-9-104 and 7-9-105 to add or
amend parking requirements for several land uses. Staff is proposing these changes to keep the
City’s parking ordinances up to date with the latest empirical data as described in this report.

Background

The City has had minimum parking requirements since incorporation in 1980. These
requirements are intended to offer residents, employees, and business patrons sufficient parking
opportunities. Providing too little parking can reduce convenience, affect lifestyle, and impact
adjoining uses and streets. Providing too much parking can be expensive, limit the economic
potential of land, and simply be unnecessary.

Over the years, adjustments have been made to these standards in an effort to strike the right
balance. The first major adjustments were made in 1988 as Ordinance 88-16 when the City
repealed its off-street parking requirements inherited from the County and adopted its own
ordinance.

Other major adjustments were made in 2002 as Ordinance 02-59. This ordinance generally
reduced parking requirements for several land uses and provided parking reductions for
developments with multiple land uses that are able to share parking due to different peak parking
demand times. These shared parking standards were patterned after an ordinance from
Montgomery County, Maryland.
The most recent, major adjustments were made in 2014 as Ordinance 14-24 where the City
reduced parking requirements for several land uses. These adjustments were based on the 4th
edition of Parking Generation from the Institute of Transportation Engineers (ITE) published in
2010. This publication provides empirical data on parking demand by various land uses. While
helpful, this publication included some survey data from before 1990.

The ordinance changes proposed in this application are based on two books. The first is the 6th
edition of Parking Generation from ITE that was published in 2023. This latest edition provides
newer survey data and additional land uses. The second book is the 3rd edition of Shared
Parking that was published in 2020 by the International Council of Shopping Centers (ICSC),
the National Parking Association, and Urban Land Institute (ULI). This book provides empirical
data on parking demand by time of day.


Proposed Changes

The changes proposed to Section 7-9-104 (Computation of Off-Street Parking Spaces) are
described below:

      Change 1: Churches and Mortuaries were moved from the existing category of Health
       Services to a new category called Institutional. Churches and Mortuaries aren’t really a
       Health Service.
      Change 2: Day Care/Preschool Centers and Nursery Schools were moved from the
       existing category of Health Services to a new category called Institutional. Day
       Care/Preschool Centers and Nursery Schools aren’t really a Health Service.
      Change 3: The parking ratio for Medical and Dental Office and Clinics was reduced from
       1 space per 250 square feet to 3 spaces per 1,000 square feet (1 space per 333 square
       feet). ITE data supports a lower parking ratio (2.63 spaces per 1,000 square feet).
      Change 4: The parking ratio for Veterinarians and Veterinary Hospitals was reduced
       from 1 space per 200 square feet to 3 spaces per 1,000 square feet (1 space per 333
       square feet). ITE data supports a lower parking ratio (3.08 spaces per 1,000 square feet).
      Change 5: The parking ratio for Day Care/Preschool Center and Nursery Schools was
       reduced from 1 space per 300 square feet to 1 space per 400 square feet. ITE data
       supports a lower parking ratio (2.27 spaces per 1,000 square feet or 1 space per 440
       square feet).
      Change 6: A parking ratio was added for Private Schools (K-12) under the new category
       of Institutional. Staff has reviewed Private Schools in the past without a parking ratio.
      Change 7: The parking ratio for Bowling Alleys was reduced from 5 spaces per alley to 4
       spaces per alley. ITE data supports a lower parking ratio (3.69 spaces per alley).
      Change 8: The parking ratio for Movie Theaters was reduced from 1 space per 4 seats to
       1 space per 5 seats. ITE data supports a lower parking ratio (0.18 spaces per seat or 1
       space per 5.6 seats)
      Change 9: The parking ratio for Automobile Parts and Supply Stores was reduced from 1
       space per 300 square feet to 3 spaces per 1,000 square feet (1 space per 333 square feet).
       ITE data for retail uses ranges from 2 to 3 spaces per 1,000 square feet.
      Change 10: Fast Food Establishments were separated from Restaurants and the parking
       ratio for this use was modified to eliminate the provision for employee parking. ITE data
       shows that our current requirement of 1 space per 100 square feet is sufficient to address
       all parking needs including employee parking.
      Change 11: Financial Institutions were separated from Professional Offices and the
       parking ratio for this use was reduced from 1 space per 250 square feet to 1 space per 300
       square feet. ITE data supports a lower parking ratio (3.4 space per 1,000 square feet or 1
       space per 294 square feet).
      Change 12: Grocery Stores, Neighborhood Grocery, and Convenience Stores were
       separated from Retail – Intensive. While ITE data supports a lower parking ratio for other
       retail uses, it does not for these uses.
      Change 13: The parking ratio for Professional Offices was reduced from 1 space per 250
       square feet (a reduction is allowed for space over 20,000 square feet) to 3 spaces per
       1,000 square feet (1 space per 333 square feet). ITE data for various office uses range
       from roughly 2 to 3 spaces per 1,000 square feet.
      Change 14: The parking ratio for Restaurants was increased from 1 space per 100 square
       feet (plus employee parking) to 12 spaces per 1,000 square feet (1 space per 83 square
       feet). This was the one use where ITE data supported a higher parking ratio.
      Change 15: The parking ratio for Retail – Intensive was reduced from 1 space per 250
       square feet (a reduction is allowed for space over 20,000 square feet) to 3 spaces per
       1,000 square feet (1 space per 333 square feet). ITE data for various retail uses range
       from roughly 2 to 3 spaces per 1,000 square feet.
      Change 16: The parking ratio for Retail – Less Intensive was reduced from 1 space per
       600 square feet to 1 space per 1,000 square feet. ITE ratios for carpet and furniture stores
       are slightly below 1 space per 1,000 square feet.

The changes proposed to Section 7-9-105 (Shared Parking) are summarized below:

      Change 1: The list of land uses was expanded to include uses listed in Shared Parking
       that are common in the City.
      Change 2: The parking demand percentages by time of day were updated for existing
       uses and added for new uses based on data from Shared Parking.
      Change 3: The weekday and weekend time period from 6 AM to 6 PM was divided into
       two time periods – 6 AM to 12 PM (noon) and 12 PM (noon) to 6 PM.

Staff Alternatives:

   4. Continuance.

   5. Denial. The ordinance should remain unchanged.

 CA-1-2024
Suburban Propane
3245 West 2100 South
M zone, 1.13 acres

The applicant, Suburban Propane, is requesting a conditional use amendment for a remodel and
redevelopment of their existing facility at 3245 West 2100 South. The zoning for this property is M,
Manufacturing and the General Plan designation is Light Manufacturing. The surrounding zone is M,
Manufacturing on the east, west and south. The surrounding uses include a retail/restaurant center to
the east (El Paisa), Zions Bank offices to the south, Rasmussen Equipment to the west and Utah Hwy
201 to the North.

This property has been licensed at this location since approximately 1970 and was developed under
Salt Lake County jurisdiction. At this time they would like to reorganize the site and update their
facilities with a remodel and redevelopment of this location. This includes removal of several
structures and construction of a new two-story 6,000 sqft office building, which includes a 1180 sqft
roof deck. The design is subject to the Commercial Design Standards. The building will be
constructed of honed CMU with Engineered Cedar Siding accents and a red composite panel
entrance feature. The existing buildings that will remain on site are in need of some maintenance and
will have their exteriors repainted a neutral color or a design to compliment the new office building.
There will also be a small propane filing station structure that will have the same neutral finish or
complimentary color scheme to the office. The large propane tank on site will also be replaced and
relocated for better site access and circulation.

Landscaping is required along the frontage of the property in compliance with the standards for High
Image Arterials (7-10) and the site is required to have a minimum of 5% landscaping. The west
frontage shall also be landscaped with trees at a ratio of 1 for every 30’ of frontage as well as 50%
live plant material coverage. All required parking and maneuvering areas for vehicles will be located
on hard surfacing with the proper lighting and landscaping per 7-9 of the WVC Municipal Code.
There will be right-of-way improvements required which will be part of the Public Works review of
this application including curb, gutter, sidewalk and street lights.

There will be outside storage on the northeast section of the property. This area is for vehicle storage
as well as propane tanks and supplies. The entire area will be surfaced with asphalt. The storage will
not exceed 6 feet in height. The area is directly adjacent to 2100 South, which is a High Image
Arterial, so the masonry screening per 7-7-119(4) will be required along that frontage. Staff also
recommends that masonry screening be extended along the west side landscaping to the gate and also
along the east side to the building on the adjacent lot. The remaining storage area is proposed as
chain link with slats and is screened by the adjacent properties or the propane filing area and parking.

There is an existing non-conforming pole sign on site. It may remain since it is freeway oriented
however, the owner is still evaluating if that sign will stay or possibly be replaced with a new
monument sign. If it does remain it will be updated and refinished. There will also be signage on the
building. All signage is required to meet the Title 11 standards.

There will be a dumpster provided for this building that will be located within a 6’ masonry
enclosure that is constructed to match the building.

Staff Alternatives

1. The building must be completed per the approved plans and be designed in accordance with the

   design to the new office building.
3. The site shall be landscaped per the approved plans, including the 2100 South frontage per the 7-
   10 standards in the City Code. The west frontage shall be landscaped with trees at a ratio of 1 for

4. Outside storage is only permitted in the designated areas on the northeast side of the site. The
   outside storage area shall be screened with a masonry wall per 7-7-119(4) along the north
   frontage as well as along the west side landscaping to the gate and along the east side to the
   building on the adjacent lot. The remaining storage area must also be screened from public view
   and may be chain link with slats.
5. The dumpster shall be completely screened and secured with a 6’ masonry enclosure that
   matches the building.
6. All signage must comply with the West Valley City Sign Ordinance. If the existing freeway-
   oriented pole sign remains it shall be repainted and repaired.
7. Must meet requirements of all affected departments and agencies, including Public Works and
   the right-of-way requirements.
8. Subject to review upon valid complaint.

C-2-2024
Godfrey West Valley Phase II
2466 S 5900 W
M Zone (16.54 acres)

Sansone Group is requesting conditional use approval for a warehouse building at 2466 S
5900 W. This property is zoned M, Manufacturing, and the West Valley City General Plan
designates the area as Light Manufacturing. The zoning is also M for adjacent parcels to the
north, east and west. The zoning to the south is Agriculture. The surrounding area is
undeveloped to the north and south. There is a Rocky Mountain Power Corridor to the east,
and warehouse uses to the west.

The proposed development is the second phase of a larger Sansone project. Phase I was
approved in February of 2021 and the building was completed in September of 2022 (ZC
20210004). The Phase II proposal includes a 309,750 sq. ft. warehouse building, which will
accommodate up to four tenants. The building will be constructed primarily of tilt-up concrete.
This facility is not required to meet the Commercial Design Standards due to the use being
limited to a manufacturing zone, however, there are three design-related requirements outlined
in the development agreement for the property (see attached), which the proposed building must
meet.

The proposed site layout orients the building towards 5900 West. Customer and employee
parking will be on the east side of the building and the truck and trailer loading and parking
will be on the west side of the building, which mirrors the first phase. There will be a new
public road constructed on the north side of the site. 2400 South will be extended as part of the
Godfrey Industrial Park No. 3 Subdivision, which the Planning Commission has already
reviewed (ZS 20230003). The applicant is proposing two access points on the north side of the
parcel from the new road, and two access points from Beagley Rd. The two access points on
the east end will be for customers and employees and the two on the west end will be for truck
access and parking. The applicant is proposing 194 customer and employee parking stalls on
the site. The exact number of parking stalls required is based on the proposed uses and
building square footage, which will be monitored as Tenant Improvement permits are
reviewed.

Curb and gutter improvements were installed with the construction of Beagley Road and 5900
West, however the park strip and sidewalk portion of the right-of-way improvements need to
be installed in conjunction with this development. Along the east edge of the parcel there is
also the Lee Creek Channel. This area must remain as an open drainage ditch. The code
typically requires 20’ of formal landscaping next to the right-of-way and the area must also
have a 3’ landscape berm to screen the parking lot. The location of the drainage channel makes
this difficult to accomplish. 7-9-112(2) allows for the Planning Commission to allow
alternatives to the berm requirement for M-Zoned properties over 10 acres in size. The
applicant is proposing to leave the Lee Creek Channel area with native vegetation, which is
about 40’ wide in the narrowest section, and then do 10’ of formal landscaping next to the
parking lot. Staff suggests that in lieu of the berm requirement and 20’ of formal landscaping,
the applicant provides shrubs that will grow 3’ tall within the 10’ landscaped area so that they
will create a hedge row to screen the parking lot. Street trees shall also be provided along the
entire frontage, with one tree provided every 30’.

The M zone requires 5% of the site to be landscaped. The current proposal provides 23%
landscaping on the site. On the conditional use approval for Phase I, the Planning Commission
required that the south side of the truck court area be screened with large evergreen trees. In
the current proposal for Phase II, it shows a retention basing going in this area, with minimal
plantings. Per the code there needs to be 20’ of formal landscaping. Some of the area can be
used for retention, but the code requirement still needs to be met and large evergreen trees
should still be used for screening. The frontage should have street trees planted once every 30’
and the formally landscaped area should have 50% coverage of live plant material. The south
side of the building along the frontage shall also have trees spaced in the same manner.

On the north side of the property, the west side of the site, the site plan shows a retention basin
extending all the way up to the new right-of-way line for 2400 South. Most of the basin is on
the Phase I portion of the site. When that phase was approved, there wasn’t going to be a road
on the north side. With the addition of the road, the code requires that the 20’ required setback
from the street be landscaped. The north edge of the basin area should be redesigned to
accommodate for 20’ of formal landscaping with street trees placed every thirty feet and 50%
coverage of live plant material, or they could potentially do 10’ of formal landscaping and
trees, with 100% coverage of live plant material within that 10’ if the Planning Commission
finds that reasonable. Street trees should be continued on the north side of the building as well
to create a cohesive streetscape.

No signage is being proposed currently. Any future signage on the property or building will
need to comply with Title 11.

Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any issues raised at the public hearing and the following
conditions:

 1.      The building and site shall be constructed per the approval plans.
 2.      All requirements of the Development Agreement on the property shall be met.
 3.      There shall be adequate parking on site for the future tenant mix.
 4.      In lieu of 20’ of formal landscaping and a berm between 5900 West and the parking,
         the applicant shall provide a 10’ wide formal landscaped area next to the Lee Creek
         Channel that includes shrubs 3’ in height, spaced closely enough that they will grow to
         create a hedge row for screening. One street tree shall also be provided every 30’ along
         the frontage.
 5.       The area south of the truck court shall include 20’ of formal landscaping that can be
          broken up into two sections. There should be evergreen trees for screening, like what
          was installed for Phase I. All formally landscaped areas should include at least 50%
          coverage of live plant material.
 6.       On the north end of the site, north of the retention basin, there shall be either 20’ of
          formal landscaping with 50% coverage of live plant material and street trees provided
          for every 30’ of frontage, or there can be 10’ of formal landscaping with 100%
          coverage of live plant material and the required street trees. Trees shall also be
          provided on the north side of the building, spaced once every 30’.
 7.       All other landscaping shall be completed in accordance with applicable standards in the West
          Valley City Municipal Code.
 8.       Signage shall comply with Title l1 of the West Valley City Code.
 9.       All requirements of effected departments and agencies shall be met.
 10.      Subject to review upon valid complaint.
