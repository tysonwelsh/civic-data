                                            ANNEXATION

GOAL:           1.0      PROVIDE FOR RESPONSIBLE GROWTH MANAGEMENT
                         THROUGH LOGICAL GEOGRAPHICAL BOUNDARY EXTENSIONS
                         TO ENHANCE COMMUNITY IDENTITY AND EFFICIENCY OF
                         RENDERING MUNICIPAL SERVICES.

POLICIES:
                1.1      In order to match the City boundary to various service areas, properties
                         proposed for development which require city services (including water) are
                         required to be annexed except for the following:

                         a.       Properties which are contiguous to existing City boundaries may be
                                  granted a waiver by the City Council after recommendation from
                                  the Mayor.

                         b.       Properties which are not contiguous to existing City boundaries
                                  must sign an annexation agreement.

                1.2      The territory to be annexed must conform to all requirements of the State,
                         including:

                         a.       That a petition be submitted requesting annexation signed by a
                                  majority of the owners of property in the area and comprising at
                                  least one-third of the assessed valuation, according to the last
                                  assessment rolls.

                         b.       That the proposed area for annexation must be contiguous to
                                  corporate boundaries of the municipality.

                         c.       That a plat or map of the area be drawn as required by State Law.

                         d.       That the approval be made by a resolution passed by a 2/3 majority
                                  of the governing body.

                         e.       That a map or legal description of an unincorporated territory into
                                  which Sandy City will favor or not favor a petition for annexation
                                  be adopted.

                   f.      That a policy statement of specific criteria be prepared describing
                           projected municipal extension.

                   g.      That no islands within this municipality can be created except where
                           existing islands or peninsulas may be annexed in portions, as may be
                           allowed by State Law.

             1.3   The policy declaration for Sandy City is:

                   a.      New municipal boundaries should conform, wherever possible, with
                           natural topographic features, e.g., ridge lines, streams, creeks, etc.
                           Care should be taken not to create topographically isolated areas or
                           areas which would require costly and difficult provision of services.

                   b.      Unincorporated islands should be encouraged to annex to the City.

                   c.      An interlocal cooperation agreement with the County, and with
                           Midvale, Draper, South Jordan, West Jordan, and Murray should
                           be adopted delineating ultimate Sandy City boundaries or perhaps
                           to round off existing municipal boundaries.

                   d.      The proposed area should be contiguous to Sandy City and should
                           be of substantial width to avoid narrow "cherry stem" connections
                           in order to be contiguous (e.g., avoid annexation along railroad and
                           canal lines, streets, etc.). Where possible, the connection should be
                           of substantial width to promote access to the annexed area by a
                           public street entirely within the City.

                   e.      To ensure efficient street and utility maintenance, where possible,
                           the annexed area should include the entire right-of-way of a street
                           to avoid two units of government maintaining the streets and
                           utilities.

                   f.      Arterial and collector roads should remain within one unit of
                           government for as great a distance as possible to ensure efficient
                           street and utility maintenance.

                   g.      Sandy City supports the concept of wall-to-wall cities through
                           annexation and/or incorporation with urban services being provided
                           by an incorporated city.
                   h.      Sandy City supports the Neighborhood Council concept. Where
                           there are existing communities inside of the City (i.e. Granite,

                                     COMMUNITY IDENTITY

GOAL:           1.0      ESTABLISH A STRONG COMMUNITY IDENTITY IN SANDY.

POLICIES:
                1.1      Develop and implement a community Streetscape plan, particularly
                         emphasizing the entrances to the city, the major arterial roads, and the
                         major intersections.

                1.2      Encourage the development of urban plazas as richly usable, multi-
                         functional public spaces, particularly in areas of pedestrian concentration.

                1.3      Where possible, cluster public buildings to draw attention and locate them
                         where they will be visible for as great a distance as possible.

                1.4      Implement a strong central focus for the City, either by a central park,
                         government center or another major city-wide focus.

                1.5      Design and erect a city "Welcome" sign or "Gateway" feature to be placed
                         at the entrances to the city.

                1.6      As a function of government, develop public relations and promotional
                         activities such as Sandy Pride, for the City to emphasize the positive
                         aspects of living, working and locating businesses in Sandy; and develop
                         other activities which will generally improve the image and community
                         identity of Sandy City.

                         Sandy Community Parks.

                2.3      Endeavor to include unique facilities in each community that will attract
                         residents not only from that community, but other areas of the city as well.

Urban Design

PURPOSE:
     The purpose of the Urban Design Element is to establish a process of giving physical
     design direction to urban growth, conservation and change. To be meaningful, it must
     define the urban design objectives for the City and illustrate a process for making decisions
     regarding the City's future, character and image. The urban design element must determine
     how individual parts of the City interact to create its image. The feature image of an
     identity of Sandy City is the outcome of myriad of public and private decisions and
     actions. Defining Urban Design Policies and Goals will help developers and City officials
     recognize the issues that their developments must address. One vitally important aspect of
     the Urban Design Element is the establishment of district character, that highlight certain
     characteristics and features that distinguish each Land use group, district or neighborhood
     from others, giving it a sense of identity of individualism that the citizens and community
     can identify with.

                    SAFE AND EFFECTIVE MOVEMENT OF PEOPLE AND GOODS.
POLICIES:
              1.1   Street Improvements – Minimize the negative impacts of traffic through

              1.2   Design Standards – Require all roads and roadway features to meet
                    minimum design standards established by the American Association of
                    State Highway and Transportation Officials (AASHTO); require all signs,
                    pavement markings, and traffic control signals to meet standards
                    established by the Manual of Uniform Traffic Control Devices (MUTCD),
                    and related roadway standards established by State, Federal, or local law.
                    Exceptions to applicable standards may be granted by the City Traffic
                    Engineer on a case-by-case basis and shall demonstrate innovative
                    superiority of other advantages over existing standards.

              1.3   Multi-Modal Streets – Design roadways to maximize use for all modes
                    of transportation including: personal vehicles, trucks, transit, bicycles,

                    while realizing that private passenger vehicles will continue to be an
                    important means of transportation.

              1.4   Coordination – Coordinate the development and improvement of streets

                    promote safety and improve mobility on major roadways while providing an

                    access directly onto arterials.

              1.6   Cut-Through Traffic – Discourage cut through and high speed travel on
                    local roads by maintaining a high level of mobility on regional (collector

                streamlining the delivery of both public and private services, and new opportunities

        b.      Placement of Utility Lines - In balancing the goals and interests of the community,
                the City Council adopted the following as part of Ordinance #96-9

                1.1      Overhead power lines pose a health risk to the community, but the full
                         scope of such risks cannot be accurately measured. Overhead lines also
                         decrease the value of adjacent residential property and detract from
                         community aesthetics. Such factors justify the imposition of a prudent
                         avoidance standard.

                1.2      The desire of the public to participate in decisions regarding power line
                         placement is supported by public policy. Further, the installation of
                         overhead transmission lines, unannounced and without prior public review,
                         violates the City’s General Plan.

                1.3      Municipal government has the authority and has traditionally played a
                         principal role as a forum for community dispute resolution, in regulating
                         development, and in providing public safety services. Among all levels of
                         government, municipalities have the authority and administrative structure
                         best able to balance interests for specific sites and projects.

                1.4      New transmission lines are not necessary to serve Sandy City. Existing
                         substations can be used or developed without the magnitude of adverse
                         impact imposed by new overhead transmission lines and without lowering
                         service delivery or raising general power rates.

                1.5      Persons residing, traveling, or working in the community should be free
                         from risks associated with overhead lines. Rather than imposing undue

Chapter II - Goals and Policies                                                             Page 2 - 60
              Sandy City General Plan




                    risks on the public, greater safety can be encouraged through making any
                    costs of risk avoidance a charge against profits.

              1.6   The potential adverse effects of allowing new overhead transmission lines
                    in Sandy is not in the best interests of the City; further, amendments are
                    needed to the presently enacted Sandy City Development Code (containing
                    the zoning regulations for the City) to better deal with the location and
                    installation of transmission lines in order to provide for the health, safety
                    and welfare, and promote the prosperity, improve the good order, comfort,
                    convenience, and aesthetics of the municipality and its present and future
                    inhabitants and businesses, to protect the tax base, protect both urban and
                    non-urban development, and protect property values. The City Council
                    finds that amendments are particularly critical relating to transmission lines
                    associated with the proposed Dimple Dell Substation.

              1.7   This ordinance appropriately balances all interests in consonance with
                    constitutional principles, is in the public interest, and services the public
                    good.

              1.8   This ordinance achieves all legitimate goals by reasonable means in that any
                    adverse impact on power system operations has been carefully balanced
                    against the corresponding gain to the public; and the regulations have been
                    calculated, on recommendation of the Power Study Committee, to permit
                    all property owners to beneficially use their properties for the practical
                    purpose to which the property is reasonably adapted.

              1.9   Procedures have been established where appeals can be heard and decided
                    if it is alleged that there is an administrative error or where a variance to
                    the ordinance is required. A procedure has also been established whereby
                    special exceptions may be granted under standard of “prudent avoidance,”
                    which standard assures a fair and reasonable accommodation of competing
                    interests in specific instances.
