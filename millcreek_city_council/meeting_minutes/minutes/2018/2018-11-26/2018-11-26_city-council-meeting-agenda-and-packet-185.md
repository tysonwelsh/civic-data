3. First Reading Planning Item
a. Consideration of ZT-18-010, Amendments to the Millcreek Code Regulating
Massage Establishments

Francis Lilly said earlier this year staff received requests from a number of concerned
licensed massage therapists (LMTs) about the restrictions the City’s current zoning
regulations imposes on the operation of their businesses. Among the issues they raised is
a concern that “massage” is not defined in the land use code (although ‘massage’ is
defined in the Business License Code), although these uses are called out and regulated in
the commercial and multifamily zones. Staff is proposing clarifying and modifying the
separation requirements found in the commercial zones for both massage businesses and
tattooing and body art establishments. After two hearings and considerable deliberation,
the Planning Commission forwarded a recommendation to the City Council on a vote of
five in favor to four against to approve the code amendments.

The current Millcreek code limitations are: massage as a practice is not allowed in the C-
1 zone, each massage therapist must possess a business license, businesses must not be
within 500 feet of a substantially similar business, massage businesses in the RM zone
are limited to hours of operation from 7:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m., massage businesses are
not allowed within 300 feet of an arterial or major intersection in the C-2 or C-3 zones,
and there is no definition of massage. The proposed changes include: massage practices
are allowed in the C-1 zone, there is no separation distance relative to other massage
establishments, no limit to hours of operation, massage businesses that are within a multi-
tenant office building are to be exempt from the intersection distance requirement now
proposed to be 200 feet (the Millcreek Community Council recommended a 200 foot
intersection distance for standalone massage establishments), and the proposed definition
of Licensed Massage Therapy Practice be added. The State Code definition of practice of
massage therapy means, “the examination, assessment, and evaluation of the soft tissue
structures of the body for the purpose of devising a treatment plan to promote
homeostasis”; and “the systematic manual or mechanical manipulation of the soft tissue
of the body for the purpose of:

Millcreek City Council Meeting Minutes 26 November 2018 Page 4 of 13

(i) promoting the therapeutic health and well-being of a client; (1i) enhancing the
circulation of the blood and lymph; (iii) relaxing and lengthening muscles; (iv)
relieving pain; (v) restoring metabolic balance; (vi) achieving homeostasis; or
(vii) other purposes.”

There is no city definition of massage referred to in code though there are 60 active
commercial massage business licenses in Millcreek. In recent years, the State Division of
Occupational and Professional Licensing (DOPL) has aggressively pursued massage
therapists that violate terms of their state licenses, or that are operating without a state
license. In 2018, DOPL took 24 disciplinary actions against massage therapists, 9 of
which were for people practicing massage without a license. Lilly said bad actors are
pretending to be licensed massage therapists when they are not. Between September
2015-September 2018, UPD reported 15 cases of prostitution in Millcreek and 6 incidents
occurred in massage establishments. DOPL’s disciplinary actions are not all related to
alleged sex crimes. The issue is the balance between the rights of property owners and
legitimate business holders.

Planning staffs recommendation is to add the proposed definitions: “Licensed Massage
Therapy Practice,” means any place of business in which massage therapy is practiced by
one or more massage therapists, as defined, licensed, and regulated under the Massage
Therapy Practice Act, under Utah Code Annotated Section 58-47b, each licensed
therapist possessing a Millcreek Business License in good standing; and, “Office
Building, Multi-Tenant,” means a building occupied by multiple tenants used primarily
for the conduct of business relating to administration, clerical services, consulting, or
other services not related to retail sales. A multi-tenant office building features a common
pedestrian entrance for all tenants and interior common area from which offices are
accessed. Another recommendation is to add licensed massage therapy practices to the
definition of office uses. Licensed massage therapy practice would be a permitted use in
the C-1, C-2, C-3, and RM zones subject to the restriction that they are not allowed
within 200 feet of an arterial/major intersection, unless the licensed massage therapy
practice is located in a multi-tenant office building. The Planning Commission
recommended a 200-foot separation, on the basis that they would rather not see a
proliferation of massage businesses in prominent intersections due to potential adverse
impacts for economic redevelopment. Some Commissioners preferred a 300-foot
separation. These separation requirements have precedent in the City’s ordinance and in
State law: separation requirements apply to signs, alcohol licensees, check cashing
businesses, smoke shops, restaurant and bank drive-throughs, commercial storage
facilities, auto sales and repair businesses, among other uses. Mayor Silvestrini thanked
Lilly for including the Planning Commission minutes in the staff report. Lilly said the
community councils recommended changes to the definitions and removal of separation
distance, though Millcreek Community Council said a separation distance was
appropriate.

Lilly showed the proposed text changes to amend the code. He said it is very difficult to
revoke a business license without very clear standards and these changes help staff
enforce the code. The massage industry advocates also recommended clarifying that reiki
be prohibited as a principal use. Staff recommends adoption of ZT-18-010, subject to the
minor changes that staff proposed in addition to the planning commission’s

recommendation; namely, updates to the definition of multi-tenant office building,
reference to licensed massage therapy practice as a defining term for the business
activity, and the addition of reiki as a prohibited use. Staff believes these
recommendations represent significant concessions on the part of Millcreek City to the
massage industry and are a reasonable compromise that balances the rights of property
owners and practitioners with the community’s interest to ensure safe and productive
businesses, and economic development, particularly at key intersections.

Winder-Newton each granted $1,000 to the Promise Program. This program is going to
help the elementary school kids at Millcreek Elementary be able to read better and
perform better at school. Millcreek Elementary is a Title 1 School, which means that they
meet certain poverty requirements where more than half of the kids that attend the school
qualify for free school lunch. The City is instituting through mostly volunteer and grant
money a program to help these kids succeed, which will improve the City by reducing
crime and kids getting in trouble and to help them become productive members of society
that will benefit everyone. Mayor Silvestrini thanked the County Council Members for
their generosity. The City also received federal grant money for an after-school program.

Council Member Catten moved to approve Resolution 18-57, Approving an Interlocal
Cooperative Agreement with Salt Lake County for Donation for Millcreek Elementary
through the Promise Program. Council Member Marchant seconded. The City Recorder
called for the vote. The motion passed unanimously.
