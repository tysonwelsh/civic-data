    2.3. Juniper Crest Road Crosswalk Discussion - Jonathan Bowers, City Engineer
Engineer Bowers indicated Juniper Crest Road was a major roadway in the City and contained two types of
cross-sections: Major Arterial and Major Collector. There were two existing crosswalks, and residents have
requested more along the road. However, installing any new crosswalks along the road was a safety concern
due to several factor, including:

   ·   Extremely wide road, requiring pedestrians to cross 5 lanes plus shoulders
   ·   No stop signs or traffic signal to control vehicle movement at potential crosswalk locations
   ·   Higher traffic and higher speeds on Juniper Crest
   ·   Lower visibility due to hills along the road

These concerns exist with the current crosswalk at Mt. Ogden Peak/Patriot Ridge Dr. Intersection, a school
crosswalk for Black Ridge Elementary and Providence Hall Elementary. Improvements to this crosswalk
would create a safer environment for pedestrians in the area. Possible improvements included a flash beacon
system, pedestrian refuge island, and restriping the intersection. It was recommended that these improvements
be installed and was estimated to cost approximately $100,000. New crosswalks had been considered by the
November 18, 2020 – City Council Minutes                                            Page 5 of 14
Traffic Committee at Ambermont Drive toward the east end of the road, but after review, they decided against
the installation due to safety concerns. It was recommended that a crosswalk be installed with visibility
enhancement at Tilton Drive, including rectangular rapid flashing beacons, which would cost approximately
$15,000. This would be budgeted in the upcoming fiscal year.

The Council discussed potential areas for crosswalks, safety concerns, and alternate option.

    2.5. Rosecrest Business Center CDA Analysis/Parking Structure and Impact Fee Obligation
         Overview - Blake Thomas, Community Development Director and Alan Rae, Finance Director
Community Development Director Blake Thomas explained The Herriman Business Center Community
Development Area (CDA) was created to promote and provide for the development of a 308-acre mixed use
development. The tax increment funds generated by the CDA were to be used for the public infrastructure
improvements needed to support development of the CDA. The Herriman Business Center CDA Project
Area Plan, prepared by Zions Bank in January of 2015, identified the CDA boundary and an overall
infrastructure plan was prepared by Rosecrest Communities. The Project Area Plan and Infrastructure Plan
were attached to the staff report. The Redevelopment Agency of Herriman City approved a tax participation
agreement between the City and Rosecrest Communities on September 14, 2016. The agreement stated that
the total cost of all improvements in the Herriman Business Center Community Development Area (CDA)
could be $67M. The participation agreement identified several projects that Rosecrest Communities
anticipated funding that total $30,545,692. The agreement obligated the City to reimburse Rosecrest
Communities annually from tax increment generated in the CDA and in the event that these funds are not
November 18, 2020 – City Council Minutes                                           Page 6 of 14
sufficient to reimburse for the improvements, impact fees must be used to reimburse for the improvements.
To date, no reimbursements of tax increment funds had been issued to Rosecrest Communities. Rosecrest
Communities has designed and constructed much of the infrastructure contemplated in the participation
agreement. They have submitted documentation including contracts, invoices, and check stubs which had
been reviewed and verified by the Herriman City Engineering Department. The summary of infrastructure
costs incurred by Rosecrest Communities was presented to the City Council and included in the staff report.

Finance Director Alan Rae noted they have been working with the data provided and consultants to determine
the amount of funds that will be available to reimburse Rosecrest Communities for the infrastructure they had
installed and how much tax increment fund would be available for other infrastructure needs. There were
$42.3 million in potential fund for the development, and the projected income was $39,663,670.

The Council discussed this item at length, and ultimately they were concerned that the City would owe more
in reimbursements than they would be able to fund. The amount they owned to developers was still
inconclusive. They requested more information so they could make a more accurate estimation. Staff noted
they were continually looking for other funding sources to free up impact fees to use for reimbursement.

         Thomas, Interim City Manager & Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi said there was a request to review current Council rules and procedures regarding
non-public meetings with developers and other third parties. The current rule states:

       The Council may regularly convene Work Meetings to openly discuss upcoming Council
       business, receive updates on items already before the Council, or to address those items on
       the Work Meeting agenda. The Council takes no action at Work Meetings and public comment is generally
       not allowed. Instead, after discussion, the Council will refer items to a future Regular Meeting
       agenda for consideration under the Consent Agenda, or to another work meeting for
       additional work and discussion by the Council. Notice of Work Meetings shall be provided

    7.4. Presentation relating to a horse trail easement vacation - Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi explained in 2002, the plat for Hamilton Farm Estates was recorded. The plat
included a 10’ public easement for use as a “horse trail.” The Easement was located on the private property
of 14 parcels within the subdivision. It ran east to west through Belmont Park Avenue on the southern
boundary of two home, and north to south on the western boundary of 13 properties in the subdivision. A
resident, whose property was affected by this easement, has asked the City to consider abandoning its interest.
The easement was perpetual and recorded by plat. As such, the City’s interest could only reasonably be
“abandoned” through means of a release or an official abandonment. A release would involve the City willfully
releasing its interest in the property by means of executing a recordable instrument. An abandonment would
involve the affected property owners petitioning the court to remove the easement for lack of use or for some
related reason. There are more homes adjacent to the Trail that also potentially benefit from its use. In some
November 18, 2020 – City Council Minutes                                               Page 11 of 14
instances, owners on the west side of the trail have direct access to the trail from their properties. To date,
staff have only heard from one resident regarding a request to abandon the City’s interest in the easement. The
Council expressed their dissent to consider the easement vacation.

residential land uses. Due to the topography of the site and future development of Mountain View Corridor
(MVC) by the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT), Mr. Messenger proposed a new “Overlay Zone”
that would regulate commercial signage within the project. Following the public hearing, the Planning
Commission voted 5-1 to recommend approval of the proposal. The dissenting member recommended the
proposal limit the number of tenants listed on each monument sign to six to reduce visual clutter and ensure
legibility for passing motorists. However, this recommendation was not included in the motion.

During the October 14, 2020, work meeting, the City Council requested the following additional information:

 ·   Proximity to Existing Signs. Council requested a map that depicting the existing signs for Zions Bank
     Stadium on Mountain View Corridor and the proposed signs for Academy Village (see map on page two
     of this report). The distance between the two Zions Bank Stadium signs was approximately 2,400 feet.
     The distance between the northernmost Zions Bank Stadium sign and the proposed Academy Village
     was approximately 1,000 feet. Based on the applicant's materials, the proposed “planned center sign”
     would likely be located further north, which would increase this distance.
 ·   UDOT Sign Regulations. UDOT regulated off-premise signs (i.e., billboards) along State routes, such
     as Mountain View Corridor (MVC). However, Herriman City Code would require all signs within the
     VMU Overlay to be “on-premise” signs. (Herriman prohibits off-premise signs.) Furthermore, the
     proposed VMU Overlay states the “planned center signs”—which would be within 100 feet of and
     oriented toward MVC—would comply with “all applicable Federal and State law(s).”
 ·   Compatibility. Staff reviewed sign regulations in Riverton and South Jordan and found the dimensional
     standards of the planned center signs in the proposed VMU Overlay Zone to be similar in scale with
     neighboring cities.

During the October 28, 2020, City Council meeting, Council directed staff to amend the draft ordinance and
include the following modifications to (1) limit signage to on-premise advertisement only, (2) increase the
minimum distance between a planned center sign and all residential dwellings—whether planned or existing—
and (3) clarify the review process of a comprehensive sign plan within the proposed overlay. Those changes
were made.

Councilmember Ohrn moved to approve Ordinance No. 2020-33 to amend Chapter 15, Overlay Zones of Title 10, Land
Development Code to create a Village Mixed Use Overlay Zone. Councilmember Smith seconded the motion.
