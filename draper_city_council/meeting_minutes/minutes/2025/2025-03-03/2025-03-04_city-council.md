Study Session

Presentation: Utah Risk Management Agency
David Petersen, Executive Director of Utah Risk Management Agency (URMA),
presented a brief history of URMA, explained services and support provided by
URMA, and answered questions from the Council.

Report: Tree Committee
David Cloward, Draper Tree Committee Chair, shared the Tree Committee’s vision
with the Council, reported results of a survey conducted during 2024 Draper Days,
spoke of optimizing the City’s return on investment in trees, and recommended the
City fund a full-time arborist. Mayor Walker thanked Mr. Cloward for the
presentation.

Discussion: Draper Days
Parks and Recreation Director Rhett Ogden and Special Event Coordinator David
Wilks provided an update on entertainment and event plans for 2025 Draper Days,
and answered questions from the Council.

5.    Items for Council Consideration
5.a   Public Hearing: Ordinance #1635 amending the text of the Draper City
      General Plan to add the Draper Town Center Station Area Plan
      Todd Taylor, Planner, explained required components of station area plans
      (2022 House Bill 462), explained the process utilized to put together the Draper
      Town Center Station Area Plan (SAP), and presented the preferred Draper
      Town Center SAP concept. Mr. Taylor reported the Planning Commission
      reviewed the proposed SAP and forwarded a positive recommendation.

      Mayor Walker opened a public hearing.



                                                                            2|Page
Paul Skene, Draper resident, said he loved the idea of redevelopment and
creating a walkable area and gathering spot. He said he was struggling with
transportation, and did not see anything in the plan pertaining to
transportation except light rail. Mr. Skene said some of the existing roads in
the area were already failing, and asked how transportation would be
addressed. He said he could not imagine bringing in the proposed density
without addressing the roads.

Vivian Gubler, Draper resident in the Parkstone neighborhood, said she
wanted minimum density, and said she was concerned about traffic. Ms.
Gubler asked if the four-story apartment buildings proposed to be adjacent to
Parkstone could be replaced with condos and twin homes, because the four-
story structures would block western views for Parkstone residents. Ms.
Gubler expressed the opinion that putting in the tall structures would not be
respectful of the surrounding community.

Shane Vogelsberg, South Jordan resident and future resident of the Bainbridge
neighborhood, said his family was attracted to Draper and the Bainbridge
neighborhood by how clean, peaceful, and family friendly it was. Mr.
Vogelsberg expressed concern with plans to increase traffic in the
neighborhood.

Charlene Pineda, Draper resident in the Parkstone neighborhood, said she did
not see the existing fence between Parkstone and the UTA property in the
renderings for the proposed SAP. Ms. Pineda said Parkstone was a private
community, and emphasized the residents did not want that end of the
community open to the public. She said she would prefer the proposed
apartment buildings next to Parkstone to be three stories maximum.

Mark Clifford, Draper resident in the Parkstone neighborhood, said he had no
idea the City was planning a massive development in the area when he bought
his home. He said the plans were upsetting, and said the property values of
existing residences would go down because of the SAP. Mr. Clifford asked if
the City had similar plans for the Kimball Station area.

Ron Smith, Draper resident in the Parkstone neighborhood, expressed
concern about parking in the proposed SAP. Mr. Smith said he developed
Parkstone 25 years ago. He expressed the opinion that a row of owner-
occupied townhomes should be developed adjacent to the Parkstone

                                                                     3|Page
boundary, not tall apartment buildings, and especially not rentals. Mr. Smith
spoke of neighborhood integrity.

Steve Taylor, Draper resident in the Masters subdivision, said he echoed
concerns expressed about traffic. Mr. Taylor expressed concern about the
height of proposed buildings, and said the Draper Town Center Station had
the lowest ridership of all Trax stations. He said there was no consistency in
the proposed SAP with the rest of the community. Mr. Taylor recommended
buildings should not be allowed to exceed three-stories.

Louise Craig, Draper resident, said she moved to Draper because it was a rural
community, and was not thrilled when Trax was added. She questioned why
the whole community should change for a transportation option that was not
used very well. Ms. Craig said she would prefer a maximum of three-story
buildings. She asked if it would be possible for Draper to have the law requiring
the SAPs changed.

Reed Gibby, Draper resident, said he was philosophically concerned about the
State making laws and regulations that were imposed on cities. He said he
appreciated the Council and was glad he did not have to make the decision.

Mayor Walker closed the public hearing.

Mayor Walker said the State Legislature insisted the current housing crisis was
caused by the city governments. He said he disagreed. Mayor Walker
explained some of the background related to the State requiring the SAP, and
emphasized the proposed densities were preferable to those initially
proposed by developers. Mayor Walker spoke of a bill currently being
discussed by the State Legislature related to local versus state land use
authority. He said the proposed density around the Trax Station was designed
to increase ridership, and said the City did not have control over what was
developed on the UTA property. The Mayor emphasized all SAPs needed to be
adopted by October 25, 2025, and emphasized that adoption of the SAP would
not guarantee the proposed buildings would be developed. Traci Gunderson
spoke of financial consequences that would be imposed by the State if the City
failed to meet SAP requirements.

Councilmember T. Lowery stated the threat to local control was very real.
Councilmember Vawdrey said the City had pushed back where they could.

      Councilmember Johnson reiterated that the requirements were forced on the
      City, and said the City would continue to work to find solutions for traffic.

      Councilmember Green thanked the Mayor for his hard work, and said the City
      needed to comply because consequences of not meeting SAP requirements
      were too bad. Councilmember F. Lowry stated the situation was unfortunate.
      Councilmember F. Lowry thanked staff for their work, and said the Council was
      aware of traffic and would do their best to make sure traffic was mitigated.

      Councilmember T. Lowery asked if the Council could change the proposed
      plan to reduce building height maximum to three stories adjacent to
      Parkstone. Community Development Director Jen Jastremsky said the Council
      could make modifications, but cautioned against reducing the allowed
      building height lower than three stories. Mayor Walked asked if the Council
      would be comfortable increasing building height in a different location in
      exchange for reduced height adjacent to Parkstone, and Councilmember T.
      Lowery responded only if absolutely necessary.

      Councilmember T. Lowery moved to approve Ordinance #1635, lowering the
      heights adjacent to Parkstone from four to three stories, giving staff latitude
      to make adjustments to meet needed densities, with fencing and
      encouragement for owner occupancy. Councilmember Vawdrey seconded the
      motion.

      A roll call vote was taken. The motion passed unanimously.

5.c   Action Item: Ordinance #1638 approving the Hidden Canyon Estates - Mercer
      Mountain Estates Development Agreement 11th Amendment for
      approximately 25.9 acres of property located at approximately 2635 East
      15040 South within Draper City
      Community Development Director Jennifer Jastremsky summarized previous
      amendments to the Mercer Mountain Estates Development Agreement. She
      said the applicant proposed to add additional fill on City property, with the
      applicant to provide $200,000 to the City. The area would be hydro-seeded
      after the fill was placed, with a two-season warranty period, and any impacted
      trails would be restored or realigned. The proposed amendment also
      addressed easements. Staff answered questions from the Council about trail
      access.

      Councilmember T. Lowery moved to approve                 Ordinance     #1638.
      Councilmember Johnson seconded the motion.
