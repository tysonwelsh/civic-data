TIP Modification Request: Utah Lakeshore Trail Additional Funding - Bob Allen, MAG The city of Saratoga Springs was awarded $996k of MPO funding in 2016 to complete two sections of the Utah Lakeshore Trail. These federal funding were made available in FY 2018-19. The project has been designed and is ready to bid with construction starting this winter. Three issues have occurred that have increased the cost of the project to $1,296,000 or an additional $300k. 1. The Army Corp of Engineers is requiring a boardwalk over wetlands be constructed for 80 feet of the project. 2. Mitigation areas for the wetlands disturbed were not priced into the project. 3. The state of Utah is requiring that a drainage system be constructed that will enter Utah Lake in select locations. The city has requested MAG cover these costs. Being over 10% of the total project cost, MPO RPC must approve the additional funds. There is a balance in the MPO funds account to cover this cost. MPO TAC recommends approval of an additional $300k to fund the cost overruns for the Utah Lakeshore Trail Project. Jim Evans moved to modify the TIP and approve an additional $300k to fund the cost overruns for the Utah Lakeshore Trail Project. Mayor Christensen seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee March 7, 2019 Page 3 TIP Modification Request: Utah County Disadvantaged Transit Pass Pilot Program - Bob Allen, MAG For the last year and a half, MAG, through a grant from the Veteran’s Administration has paid for monthly UTA passes for disabled Veterans that has been very successful. In cooperation with Utah County, UTA, The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, other faith-based organizations and MAG, MAG is looking to expand the program to other disadvantaged groups who are transportation challenged. Specifically, graduates of the Utah County Jail’s RISE program and unsheltered victims of domestic violence. MAG is proposing to use CMAQ funding for a pilot program to reduce vehicle trips by switching transportation challenged individuals from private vehicles to public transit. Transportation through this program could be accomplished with two transit trips, significantly reducing the number of trips in comparison to relying on private vehicles. Funding for the pilot program would come from CMAQ funds already designated to UTA for a van pool program. A maximum of $135,000 in CMAQ funds is being requested, including local match, to be spent over a five-year period. The pilot program will help determine the actual cost to run the program. Mayor Brusnt moved that the TIP be modified to relocate $135,000 for the UTA van pool program and fund the Utah County Disadvantaged Transit Pass Pilot Program. Mayor Frost seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Utah Transit Authority Ridership Update - Trustee Kent Millington, UTA An update was given on the recently collected Utah Transportation Authority Ridership data. UVX has had one million riders since August 2018, 80% of those being students. UTA will track the decline when school is out. UVX results are being used to promote BRT in Ogden. Utah County has transit police for the first time providing security on buses and education to pedestrians regarding jaywalking. Kent Millington asked that mayors let UTA know what transit needs their cities and towns have so they can be addressed. UTA is soliciting input on regional priorities for bus services through the Service Choices project. In collaboration with UDOT, WFRC and MAG, UTA will educate the community regarding transit planning, strengthen relationships with the public and stakeholders, and develop a bus network plan and vision in line with regional input. Meetings will be scheduled in each city for local input. For additional information got to ride.utah.com/service-choices. Other Business Air Quality Update - Susan Hardy, MAG Utah County currently carries 4 air quality non-attainment areas: PM10 (moderate designation since 1987), PM2.5 (serious designation since 2017), Ozone (marginal designation since 2017), and CO (Provo City since 1990). PM10: A Maintenance plan is now submitted to EPA – with the intension of Designation to Attainment and eventually re classified to “unclassifiable/attainment” which will eliminate the classification. PM2.5: February 12, 2019, EPA published in the Federal Register the finding that Utah County now meets Clean Data Requirements and shows that we maintained the standard and are eligible to submit a maintenance plan and the reclassification back to Moderate PM2.5 Area. Ozone: The monitoring system will continue monitoring data and hopefully will also show maintenance of low readings, ultimately allowing re-classification to “unclassifiable/attainment”. Provo CO: In 2005 EPA approved a maintenance plan. Continuous efforts are being done to maintain the standard, and in 2025 will be eligible to reclassification to “unclassifiable/attainment” The trends are showing a healthy direction of continuous lower readings with the real possibility of full attainment of all monitored criteria pollutants in the future.
