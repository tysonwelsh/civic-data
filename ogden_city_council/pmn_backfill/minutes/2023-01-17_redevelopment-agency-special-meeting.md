Proposed Resolution 2023-2 amending the budget for the Fiscal Year July
1, 2022 to June 30, 2023 by increasing the anticipated revenues and
appropriations for a gross increase of $300,000 to facilitate the purchase of
the Union station property
         A memo from the City Comptroller came before the Board to consider a resolution amending the budget for Fiscal Year
(FY) 2022-2023. The memo stated Administration is proposing a budget amendment to increase the FY2023 Redevelopment
Agency budget to facilitate the purchase of the Union Station property. The proposed amendment would transfer funds from
Business Depot Ogden (BDO) Lease Revenue ($300,000) to the Redevelopment Agency for the down payment ($100,000) and
due diligence activities ($200,000) related to the purchase of the Union Station Property.
         Comptroller Stout summarized the memo and noted this is the companion item to the budget amendment that was
approved by the City Council earlier this evening. The funding that is involved in the budget amendment will be appropriated to
the Kiesel Avenue Community Reinvestment Area (CRA) to be used for the downpayment, closing costs, and due diligence
expenses related to the purchase of the land under the Union Station building. Administration will be making a recommendation
for annual payments for the property purchase; annual payments are estimated at $750,000 per year. She concluded the
Redevelopment Agency is allowed to purchase property and the subject property is close enough to the Kiesel Avenue CRA that
the City Attorney felt it was appropriate to budget the property purchase from that fund.
         Chair Choberka called for a public hearing on proposed Resolution 2023-2, entitled:
                  “A resolution of the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency amending the budget for the Fiscal Year July 1, 2022
                  to June 30, 2023 by increasing the anticipated revenues for a gross increase of $300,000 from sources as
                  detailed in the body of this resolution; and increasing the appropriations for a gross increase of $300,000 as
                  detailed in the body of this resolution.”
         The Deputy City Recorder presented written notice of said hearing and affidavit of posting on the Utah State Public
Notice website on the 6th day of January 2023, specifying the time and place of this meeting as the time and place when and
where the proposed resolution would be given a public hearing and be considered for final passage.
         Al Jones stated that he fully supports this proposal, and he hopes this is a step forward in the right direction for Ogden
City.
         Sebastian Benitez stated that he also supports the proposal.

           A memo from Community and Economic Development came before the Board to consider resolutions approving a Real
Estate Purchase Contract (REPC) for parking for the Second District Court, an amended and restated Operating Agreement for the
WonderBlock project, and a Land Transfer and Development Agreement (LTDA) for the WonderBlock project. The memo stated
the proposed REPC would allow the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency to purchase seven (7) properties owned by the state of
Utah related to the Second District Court parking west of the court building at 2525 Grant Avenue. The REPC details the
agreement for the City to secure a portion of the existing parking as well as providing 100 stalls with the option of providing an
additional 100 monthly parking passes for use by the court in the East Parking Garage to be built with the WonderBlock project in
exchange for the subject properties owned by the state. Relative to the Operating agreement, the document outlines the joint
venture relationship between the Redevelopment Agency (Agency) and JF WonderBlock Partners, QOF, LLC (JF) a single-
purpose entity associated with J. Fisher Companies. This joint venture is created to allow the Agency and JF to pool resources for
the purpose of developing the WonderBlock project—a project the Administration asserts is not only crucial to implementation of
the Make Ogden Plan and the redevelopment of the Central Business District, but also to the financial sustainability of the City.
Key elements of the Agreement identify the capital contributions of the parties, establish how any profits from the development
will be shared, name JF as the manager and partnership representative, and provide specifics on how the property and accounts
must be managed and reported. The Agreement also outlines the circumstances under which a dissolution of the joint venture may
occur and how dissolution will be completed. Finally, the proposed LTDA replaces the original LTDA authorized in December
2020. It removes guarantees and more particularly addresses the conditions under which the property will be transferred by the
Agency and accepted by the joint venture. It recognizes the important relationship of the parking garages to the WonderBlock
project and addresses construction, payments, and general management issues.
           Community and Economic Development Director Cooper summarized the memos and used the aid of a PowerPoint
presentation to highlight the terms of each of the three agreements. He also presented historical photographs of downtown Ogden
to illustrate the development patterns and changes in zoning ordinances; he highlighted the lack of parking areas and past efforts
to transition to compact, dense, valuable development patterns that can create a sense of place in Ogden. He concluded his
presentation by providing a formula to illustrate expected outcomes of economic development and the impact that economic
development has on historical and cultural assets, transportation and mobility, open space and parks, and social equity/services.
This led to discussion among the Board and staff regarding the prioritization of each of the deliverables included in the pyramid
graphic contained on the slide discussing expected outcomes of economic development.

Executive Director Comments
         Executive Director Caldwell acknowledged the City’s Community and Economic Development Staff for the thousands of
hours that have gone into negotiations pertaining to the WonderBlock project; this project is ‘years in the making’ and he believes
both the City and the development partner are equally invested in ensuring the success of the project and what is best for the entire
community. He thanked the Board for their support for this truly visionary project.

Board member Comments
          Board member Lopez stated that when the Board voted on the WonderBlock project last week, he asked himself if he
would invest his own personal money in the project based on the information that had been presented to that point and the answer
was no. Tonight, he asked himself the same question and the answer was yes and that is why he voted in support. He stated this
has been a very difficult process and a difficult decision to make, but he wished to recognize the efforts of City Administration to
be transparent and open about the project; he believes there are enough bright and smart people who are working on this project
and there is a strong desire to do what is best. He does not fault the developer for wanting to make money on this project, that is
why they are in business. One of his concerns in the past related to ownership of residential units in the project, but that concern
has been addressed as City staff has provided additional information about the reasons that the project will contain market rental
units.
          Board member Hyer stated that the Board was criticized last week for having faith in the project and voting in support for
that reason; however, it is important to note that the Board has asked many hard questions about the project and the lengthy
process has given them time to collect more information to get comfortable with the proposal. He has lived in Ogden his entire life
and he is very excited about this ‘episodic event’ in Ogden’s history. He is ready to move forward, and he strongly believes it will
not be necessary to raise taxes because of the project. He has also reviewed the Constitution and cannot find any section of the
Constitution that is being violated due to this project.
          Board member Nadolski stated that one could view the discontent surrounding this project as dysfunctional on the part of
the City, but he views it as an opportunity for growth and he hopes that everyone understands that he will get behind the project to
make it succeed. He is exhausted by the points of contention relating to specific issues in the City and he wants to avoid future
‘flash points’ and get everyone to work collectively to make the future of the City better than it is today. Energy must be invested
in the best way and in the vein of solving problems for the citizenry.
          Vice Chair Richey stated last week’s meeting was difficult and he has tried very hard to absorb all of the feedback
provided that evening; he hopes that everyone understands that the Board is performing their due diligence and is working to
address the concerns that are voiced by constituents. He truly feels he has poured the appropriate amount of time into evaluating
this project over the past year and he wholeheartedly supports the project. He feels everyone has had a voice and he extended an
open invitation to anyone to reach out to him to continue to express their opinions or ask questions.
          Board member Blair thanked everyone for their assistance in getting the project to this point; he thanked Administration
for being open and honest throughout the entire process. He also thanked Board staff for providing support and conducting
exhaustive research to make sure the Board members could get comfortable enough to make a decision. He thanked Mr. Cooper
and the legal counsel for spending many hours with Board members to make sure they had enough information and trust in the
information. He was also able to meet with the developers, who have answered questions and have always expressed their love for
Ogden and a desire to help it expand and become even greater. He is excited about the partnership that has been established and he
feels that it will be successful.
          Chair Choberka stated she agrees with everything that has been said by her fellow Board members and added that she
wants to be sure to continue to include and support the local businesses that may suffer during the construction phase of this
project. She is willing to consider increased investment to support outreach and support for those businesses if necessary.

     There being no further business to come before the Agency, BOARD MEMBER HYER MOVED THE MEETING
ADJOURN AT 9:08 P.M. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY BOARD MEMBER NADOLSKI, ALL VOTING AYE.
