Council Member Harker inquired about the fountain lights at Centennial Plaza. City Administrator
John Taylor responded that they were being replaced. She also mentioned a damaged sign at the
Salt Lake County Library. City Engineer Ben White stated he would let the County' s public works
department know.


On the subject of repairs, Council Member Burgess pointed out that the lights on the monument
sign on 4700 South were out once again.


Mr. Taylor explained that there were two projects in the city whose developers were interested in
a rezone to an SSD ( Site Specific Development) zone. The first of the two was the horse property
off of 6200 South and nearly 4000 West. With current zoning there could be 25- 30 lots, but the
developer was looking to build 40- 50 lots which was more dense. He wondered if the Council was
interested in considering an application for a rezone to SSD. If they already knew they were not,
it was best to share that with the developer now, before he did more work.


Council Member Harker asked if he was looking to build townhomes. Mr. Taylor responded that
he wished to build a mix of townhomes and single- family homes. He said one benefit of an SSD
zone for the city would be that an HOA would be responsible for maintaining the roads within it.
Another benefit was that they would be considered moderate- income housing and would therefore
be eligible for the down- payment assistance program. Additionally, there was a shortage of that
type of housing in the city.

Council Members Knudsen and Harker both indicated they were not opposed to the possibility.
Council Member Burgess acknowledged the need for more housing. Knowing that, Mr. Taylor
asked them to think about what their priorities and interests would be. They would then set up a
formal meeting with the developer to walk through the SSD process.

Chair Cochran indicated that he, too, would be open to considering it. He stressed the importance
of ensuring that funding was in place up front.

Mr. Taylor said the second property he wished to mention was the 24- Hour Fitness building near
the Old Spaghetti Factory Restaurant. There was some interest in developing it as multi- family
housing. He wondered if the City Council was open to discussing the possibility of an SSD at that
location as well.


Council Member Knudsen pointed out it might be a good place for such housing. However, he
expressed       concern   about   the increased   traffic   on   1900   West and around   Target.   Mr. Taylor
responded that might be one of the biggest benefits to the city— finding            a resolution to the traffic
nightmare near Target. He said that again, if they were open to the possibility of an SSD there, he
asked that they consider       priorities for such a project.

          1. 2         Mayor' s Report


 IE      Mayor Kristie Overson reported that Salt Lake Community College had hosted a ground-
breaking ceremony earlier in the month for the Larry H. Miller and Gail Miller Business Building
at the Taylorsville Campus. She explained this marked a transformative step towards modernizing
the existing structure into a state- of-the- art facility which would empower future leaders for
generations to come. Among other things, the building would house the Gail Miller Business
School, one of only a few such schools to be named for a woman, and the first one in Utah.

Eisenhower Jr. High had recently set a new world record for the fastest human conveyor belt. The
students transferred a 12. 5- pound mattress 180' in 1 minute 36 seconds. Mayor Overson said it
was a" wild and crazy" event, and she appreciated the invitation to witness it.

On Veteran' s Day she had the opportunity to meet with sixteen veterans at Ivybrook Assisted
Living Center and speak to them about their service. She was grateful that she was able to do this
each year and looked forward to continuing the tradition.

The ChamberWest Fall Conference had recently taken place, with keynote speakers Boyd
Matheson         and    Robt   Spendlove.   Mayor    Overson      said there   were    many   networking   and
collaboration opportunities, and she greatly appreciated the city' s partnership with ChamberWest.

A workshop for the Central Jordan River Reconnection project was held the previous week at the
library in Taylorsville. The goal was to help shape a new regional park along the Jordan River, and
this workshop was an opportunity to express opinions about what was important along that stretch
of the river which crossed five different cities


She attended a quarterly Council of Governments( COG) meeting the previous week and as usual,
it was focused on homelessness and housing as they geared up for the coming legislative session.
She appreciated being able to meet with fellow mayors to discuss the multitude of important things
happening around the valley.

Utah' s Department of Transportation had hosted a walking event the previous week to celebrate
the new route from the 4700 South offramp to the state campus on 2700 West. It was a great
opportunity      to walk the route, and she   was   curious   to see the difference   it would make.

Mayor Overson was pleased to announce that the 50/50 sidewalk program was caught up. This
meant that work had been completed for all citizens who had paid for repairs/ improvements to
their sidewalks,        with the city covering 50% of the cost.


There were a handful of leaf bags left for residents who wished to collect the leaves at their homes.
Each Taylorsville home was entitled to a roll of these bags, and once full, they could be left for
pick up at either Swensen Valley Regional Park or Vista Park.

TVPD was fully staffed, and she looked forward to Chief Cottan' s report and the swearing- in of
two new officers



Unified Fire responded to a structure fire at a local business over the weekend. She greatly
appreciated that they were always willing to respond when needed and thanked them very much
for their efforts.


The city' s annual audit would be presented later in the meeting, and she explained that staff had
worked with auditor Rob Wood for many months in preparation. She pointed out that the final
audit had previously been presented to the budget committee as well.

The Mayor explained that the city was in search of a new code enforcement officer. She
encouraged anyone interested to apply, and pointed out that the exiting officer had not left the city,
but simply transferred to a different department.

Earlier in the day two of the new Youth Council members had led a discussion and some activities
from Sean Covey' s 7 Habits of Highly Effective Teens. She enjoyed watching them teach one
another to be successful in their lives. Each student had also signed up to shadow a city
representative later in the year.

           Rob Wood of HBME, LLC presented Taylorsville' s Annual Comprehensive Financial
Report. He wished to thank Scott, Stephanie and Shelley for doing such a great job on the city' s
finances. He had met with the Budget Committee earlier in the week and pointed out what a
sophisticated committee it was, explaining that it involved the most robust citizen participation of
any of the sixteen cities his firm worked with.

The purpose of the annual audit was to gain an opinion on the financial status of the city. He
pointed out that it was a " clean" audit, meaning HBME did not feel there were any material
misstatements          within    the financial    statements.   If any errors had been noticed, they had been
immediately corrected. He explained that the Management' s Discussion and Analysis on pages 5-
12 was a summary of the audit.

He pointed out that there had been an increase of close to $ 11 million due to an influx of funds
from other        entities.   There   was $   10. 89 million dollar in revenues over expenses. Because of all the
construction and development projects, he anticipated seeing that amount go down as the cash was
expended on those projects.

Mr. Wood stated that             overall, Taylorsville    had a strong   net position   with a growth of over $ 10

million. None of the funds had an overage. In fact, they showed significant savings. State law
required that the general fund remain between 5% -                35%    of the current year' s operating revenues.
It was currently at 28. 1%.

 WA   City Attorney Tracy Cowdell presented information regarding the separation of powers
between the three branches of government.

He explained that the council-mayor form of government in Taylorsville differed from what was
present in the majority of Utah cities. Instead, the majority had a manager- council form. In that
form, the city manager was the CEO rather than the mayor, and the mayor acted as the chair of the
city council.
