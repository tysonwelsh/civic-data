1.   Mr. Spung          said he would welcome any questions Commissioners had during his presentation. He advised
     this was first presented back in November 2020, when Staff started working on this project. They are working on
     comprehensive updates to the Code to modernize it and make it work better. The report being presented this
     evening is the Assessment Report Staff prepared to provide an outline or road map for actually drafting the Code.
     It includes specific recommendations Staff has and things that should be improved. He added that the document
     captures a lot of the big ideas and principles so that when Staff begins drafting the final document it will not be a
     surprise to anyone what is contained therein.         He gave a quick overview of the project, reviewing the goals
     established early on which are: Streamline development review procedures and increase predictability of land use
     decisions; Consider the needs of the City both now and in the future; Align the Code with General Plan and Utah
     State Statutes; (4) Enable great design and a diverse mix of land uses; (5) Improve user-friendliness through logical
     structure, graphics, tables, and illustrations.

     He reviewed the project time-line to be: Fall 2020 – Project Orientation; Winter 2020 – LDC assessment and
     outline; Presently it is at the Administration & Procedures segment, zoning districts and uses, and development
     and design standards. Winter 2020 through Summer 2021 – Prepare the draft LDC; Fall 2021 – LDC Adoption. Mr.
     Spung said the City’s website contains many important details to anyone wishing to give input.

     Mr. Spung outlined some of the more finely defined goals as being: Create a more user-friendly code; fine-tune
     the zoning districts; reorganize and strengthen use regulations; improve and tailor development standards; and
     simplify the development review procedures.

          2.   The next topic for discussion was “Fine Tuning the Zoning Districts”. The zoning districts are displayed
               on the City Zoning Map and lay the ground-work for what can and cannot be done on a specific property.
                     That is the list of land uses allowed to have in specific areas and the number of zoning districts is
               important because that is how the City will develop. If everything is zoned residential, everything will
               develop residential. The “toolbox” is a little outdated and doesn’t really reflect the General Plan in a
               vision for what Taylorsville wants to become.

                       •   The big part of this project is going to be aligning the menu of zoning districts available, with the
                           General Plan. The General Plan has different land use categories, some of which include high
                           density residential, medium density residential, mixed use, office, business park, etc. There are
                           many different types of land uses and he did not feel the current code presents a great tool for
                           the development community to really build the types of places that the City wants.

                       •   Staff is not proposing any substantial changes to existing zoning as part of this project, it is more
                           or less a clean-up effort to remove unnecessary districts that perhaps are not really used, adjust
                           current district standards and introduce new zoning districts that maybe there are not currently
                           that would help implement some of the types of development which are not acceptable.

                       •   He gave examples: Some of the districts may be re-named to something that makes more sense,
                           i.e., “professional office” and “research and development” are two separate districts right now
                           and might be consolidated and renamed to “employment district”, so that it is broader and not
                           having to have a specific district for two separate uses that could fall under one.

                       •   Again, to consolidate similar districts such as “neighborhood commercial” and “limited
                           commercial” districts. Those in essence are very similar and could be consolidated into a
                           “commercial neighborhood” type district.

                       •   The report also talks about creating new zoning districts that are not currently being used. There
                           was a “mixed-use” district, but it is very one-dimensional, and Staff proposes breaking that up
                           into different scales of mixed use, more of a neighborhood mixed use district, a transitional,
                           which would be more moderate to medium density or scale, and then a regional mixed use
                           which would be more of a transit-oriented development type district.

                       •   The menu of zoning districts should be aligned to accommodate General Plan identified unique
                           place types and fine tune the zoning districts.

          5.   Mr. Spung introduced another slide he felt the Commissioners would be very involved with, that being
               “Reconciling the New Land Development Code with the Zoning Map”. Currently there is an adopted
               zoning map in the City and if district names are changed or consolidation of districts, then that needs to
               be reflected on the map that the City enforces. That is an essential way to keep the citizens informed.

                       5.1 With updating the zoning districts, that will eventually have to translate to the map, which
                           involves two different steps in so doing: The first step is conversion of map, which is basically
                           taking the current zoning today and converting that map to the most closely resembling district
                           in the new Code. For example, if Professional Office and Research and Development Districts are
                           consolidated into an Employment District, everything that is Professional Office and Research,
                           and Development would just change to Employment on the new map. It would just be a name
                           change, not redrawing boundaries or changing colors, just renaming and converting. This first
                           step must be completed prior to the effective date of the new Code.

                       5.2 The second step is for substantive changes, which gets a little more challenging because this is
                           where the City can step back and figure out if the zoning area designations should be changed
                           for the City. An example would be if a new mixed-use zoning district that is not currently in use,
                           did the City want to apply those on the map. That would be changing the zoning from whatever
                           it is now to something different which requires more analysis and discussion. That is the reason
                           why Staff is recommending doing this in two stages – the conversion and then consulting the
                           General Plan in making recommendations for re-zonings.

          7.   On Affordable Housing, Commissioner Burggraaf           asked for clarification reference the term “work
               force housing”. He considered himself part of the work force and wondered how that was distinguished
               in this definition. Mr. Spung said that everyone uses a different term to be politically acceptable now.
               Some say work force housing, and some say affordable housing. Generally, the idea is the City annually
               has to create a moderate-income housing report to be sent to the State of Utah. Taylorsville’s report was
               forwarded in January 2021. It basically summarizes the number of dwelling units in Taylorsville that meet
               a certain income bracket for people, which is moderate income housing. Every year when applications
               are sent to Housing and Urban Development (HUD) in the Federal Government, they send funding for
               CDGB to fund projects. Every year they ask what the City is doing to remove barriers to affordable
               housing and what is being done to help people that are disadvantaged in the City. That affordable
               housing has a stigma attached that it is strictly for low-income clients. The truth is that it is more
               expensive to rent than to own right now.
