    2.2. Discussion of the creation of a Council subcommittee to recruit a City Manager - City
         Council Roundtable
Councilmember Steve Shields thought it should be the City Council’s responsibility to hire the new City
Manager, as he felt that city staff should not have to hire their new boss. He recommended a committee be
formed to find a new City Manager, which would include a Human Resources representative, at least two
Council Members, the City Attorney, and the Interim City Manager. Councilmember Jared Henderson agreed,
and suggested they should only need Human Resources and Legal to give guidance, since they had hired a firm
to conduct the actual job search. Councilmember Ohrn concurred and thought they should allow Council
Members to be involved if they wanted to be. Councilmember Smith felt as though the Council was not getting
the level or service paid for from the firm hired to conduct the job search. He commented m there had been
minimal changes in the weekly reports received, and he thought the majority of the work had been done by
the staff. He agreed staff should have a limited role in the hiring process, since it gave internal candidates an
unfair advantage, and he stated they needed to make sure that the process was protected. Councilmember
Shields said he was appreciative of the work the staff had done, and voiced concerns there was a difference of
expectations between the staff’s idea of what the hire process should look like and what the firm actually
did. He reiterated the City Council should be more involved.

Councilmember Henderson thought they needed to avoid micromanaging the firm since it was one of the best
firms in the country, and just because they did things in a different way than the City did not mean that the
Council should supersede their work. The Council Members discussed the need to clarify what was meant by
candidates that were listed as “in the pipeline” and other terms used on the weekly reports received from the
firm.

Councilmember Henderson expounded on his point that they only needed to consult with Legal and Human
Resources, and staff and Council should be limited to a supervisory role in the process. Councilmember Ohrn
contended it should be the Council’s responsibility to hire a new City Manager. Mayor Watts and
Councilmember Smith agreed the Human Resources Manager, City Attorney, and Interim City Manager should
be on the committee, along with the Council Members who wish to be involved. Mayor Watts opined any
Council Member would be capable of giving guidance but noted that he personally would not plan on attending
the majority of the meetings. He did request they have one-on-one interviews with the final candidates when
it reached that stage. City Attorney Chase Andrizzi clarified that from a legal standpoint, if they had more than
three Council Members in attendance, it would count as a Council meeting, rather than a committee. The
Council discussed some options as to the best way to designate the group, and Councilmember Ohrn voiced
concerns she wanted all of the Council Members to be able to attend if they wished. Mayor Watts summarized
the meetings would be noticed as a meeting rather than a special committee, but pointed out as it was a work

    7.1. Presentation of the Utah Transit Authority Micro Transit Pilot and Five-Year Service Plan
         - Carlton Christensen, UTA Board Chair
Utah Transit Authority Board Chair Carlton Christensen offered an update on the Utah Transit Authority
Micro Transit Pilot and the Five-Year Service Plan. Chair Christianson said their test pilot had started in
November of 2019, and they were on track with their projections. Last March, the service actually exceeded
their expectations. When the COVID-19 pandemic became an issue, they did not shut down in order to service
frontline workers, although they did suspend their marketing efforts. He outlined the additional health
measures they had taken in light of the pandemic. He showed the trajectory of ridership and showed that from
November 2019 to March 2020 ridership had been strong. Even during the pandemic, the number of
individuals that had a wheelchair or another dependency that requested rides had remained fairly high, although
shared rides had predicable fallen.

UTA Innovative Mobility Solutions Director Jaron Robertson also touched on the impacts of COVID-19. He
said of 168 total trips, 77 of them had origins in Herriman, and 60 rides both started and ended in Herriman,
which put them at an average of 47 daily riders. 37-percent of the trips both started and ended at the terminus
of the line. He explained between the impacts of COVID and micro transit, ridership had decreased, and over
the last year they had averaged only 29 trips per day. He said in March 2020, they had surpassed 500 trips per
day. He showed some projections and thought that if not for COVID-19 they would have maintained around
400-450 trips per day in 2020. He thought that the idea was a success, and that regardless of circumstance,
they had maintained strong ridership.

Director Robertson showed a map of the service area, and explained it was available to everyone, regardless of
if they lived within the service area or not. He showed there were three rail lines and said they were able to do

    10.2. Discussion and consideration of a change order request for the Rosecrest Pump
         Station project - Justun Edwards, Public Works Director
Public Works Director Justun Edwards brought forth the change order request for the Rose crest pump station
project. He said he would provide some clarification on the differences between the two pump projects
mentioned. He explained the Rose crest Pump Station had been approved in the current budget and was
currently under construction, and that the J Bryant Miller pump station project was one that they hoped to
renovate in the future but was not currently under construction.
January 27, 2021 – City Council Minutes                                               Page 24 of 26
Public Works Director Edwards gave some background information on the Rose crest Pump Station. He stated
it had been constructed in 2004, and they had increased maintenance costs due to reliability issues with the
pump. They designed and taken bids the project and had been able to factor it in to last year’s budget. He
explained they had made some improvements for electrical efficiencies and related issues. He informed the
Council the project was slated to be completed near the end of March 2021. He stated the current cost of the
project with some owner-initiated change orders was $633,000. The budgeted amount was just over $1.4
Million, which included some of the design, construction management, and inspection services. He did not
anticipate their costs would be anywhere near $1.4 Million but wanted to provide the numbers for context to
in order to provide a better scope of the project.

Councilmember Ohrn asked how the budget had been overestimated by such a large amount. Public Works
Director Edwards replied when it was put in the budget, they had an engineer’s estimate, and about 30-percent
of the estimate accounted for the design. He explained since then, they had decided to not go through the full
design, and only had a general idea of what it would cost with the current plans. He said the changes to the
major design, as well as good engineering had decreased the price, size, and scope of the project.

Public Works Director Edwards expressed as City Council had made decisions to move forward with the bond,
this project was one of the things he had jumped on quickly in order to try and meet upcoming demands. He
stated the main purpose was to increase the capacity of the pump station by roughly 1,000 gallons per minute.
He reiterated it was included in the bond.

Public Works Director Edwards then explained how and why the projects were connected. He explained both
pumps needed to be offline to do the work, and they were both zone four pump stations that pumped the
same pressure zones and fed the same areas. Due to those similarities, they could not take both pumps offline
at the same time. He stated both projects needed to be completed before their summer demands, and therefore
the timing and coordination of the projects was critical. He expressed their hope was that the contractor from
Rosecrest would also perform the work for the other pump. Public Works Director Edwards explained they
would typically do a full bid and have a separate contractor, but the use of the same contractor in this case
would allow a quicker transition from one project to the next. He stated that in discussions with City Attorney
Chase Andrizzi, the two of them had decided it was a viable reason to issue the change order. City Attorney
Chase Andrizzi confirmed this and said it was permitted in both State and City code.

Public Works Director Edwards reviewed the progress for the J Bryant Miller station. He communicated they
had designed the project, and they wanted to move the project along quickly. Rather than get a contractor on
board and then procure pumps and motors, they did that at upfront. He noted it also saved them money
because they did not see overhead costs from those purchases being made by a contractor. He informed them
the costs to procure the parts would be roughly $110,000. He explained the demolition and the rebuild were
included in the change order, and after the contractor would be able to install the procured items, and then
commission the pump station. He stated the number for the change order cost was actually a change order
they had received from the contractor on the Rose crest Pump Station. Public Works Director Justun Edwards
felt this was a fairly accurate number.

Public Works Director Edwards pointed out there could be change orders. He mentioned he included more
detailed information in their packets and informed the Council the rough total estimate was $310,000. He

efficiency. He explained they anticipated that the current contractor would get the Rose crest pump station
operational, and then move directly to the J Bryant pump station. There was a brief conversation to answer
