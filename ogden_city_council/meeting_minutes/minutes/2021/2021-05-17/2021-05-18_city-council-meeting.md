Proposed Resolution 2021-7 approving the honorary designation of 2nd
Street from Century Drive to Wall Avenue as Bingham Ft-Chief Little Soldier
Ln
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider a resolution
approving an honorary street name designation. The memo stated Anna Keogh submitted a petition to assign an honorary street
name for a portion of 2nd Street between the Union Pacific railroad tracks and Washington Boulevard. The initially proposed
honorary street name was "Bingham Fort Lane" in honor of Bingham Fort, which was located in that area. The proposal has since
been amended to include the name of Chief Little Soldier. The new proposal would assign the honorary name as "Bingham Ft-
ChiefLittle Soldier Ln." The proposal was reviewed by the Planning Commission and a recommendation of approval for a shorter
distance was forwarded to the Council. As recommended by the Planning Commission, the honorary street name would apply to
the portion of 2nd Street between Century Drive and Wall Avenue, which the Planning Commission found to align with the fort's
historic location more closely. Upon initial review by the City Council, the Council requested that the proposal be reviewed by the
City's Diversity Commission. This review was completed, and the Diversity Commission forwarded a recommendation of denial
to the Council. The Diversity Commission's recommendation was based on the belief that the proposal did not accurately
represent and honor the perspectives of all the peoples of the area at the time. At the Council's February 2, 2021 work session,
Council members discussed the proposal and the recommendation from the Diversity Commission. Council members expressed
opposition to the proposal as presented. The petitioner requested additional time to develop an alternative proposal that would help
address concerns expressed by the Diversity Commission. The alternative proposal was reviewed at the April27, 2021 work
session meeting.
          Council Deputy Director summarized the memo and stated in preparing for tonight's meeting, staff discovered an error in
the public noticing for this public hearing; as a result, the City Attorney and Council staff recommend tabling this public hearing
for one week to allow for the error to be corrected. He suggested the presentation and public input be delayed until May 25,2021.
         Chair Blair then introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2021-7, entitled:
                  "A resolution of the Ogden City Council approving the honorary designation of 2nd Street from Century Drive
                  to Wall Avenue as Bingham Fort-Chief Little Soldier Ln."
      COUNCIL MEMBER LOPEZ MOVED THE PUBLIC HEARING BE CONTINUED AND CONSIDERATION
OF THE RESOLUTION BE TABLED UNTIL MAY 25,2021 DURING THE REGULARLY SCHEDULED CITY

Fiscal Year 2022 Tentative Budget. Proposed Ordinance 2021-26 adopting
the tentative budget for Fiscal Year 2021-2022
         A memo from Council staff regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2021-2022 Tentative Budget came before the
Council for consideration. The memo stated each year during the first Council meeting in May, the Mayor is required to present a
proposed budget for the upcoming fiscal year. The Council accepts the proposed budget for review and, through a series of work
sessions, reviews the proposal over the next two months. A tentative budget is adopted after holding an initial public hearing. A
second public hearing is held at the time of adoption of the fmal budget. During the budget hearings, the Council outlines their
proposed changes to the budget and accepts public input. Once the fmal public hearing is closed, the Council makes its changes
and adopts the fmal City budget. By State law, a budget must be adopted by June 30. An exception to the timing is allowed if the
Council is considering a proposed property tax increase. This evening, the Council will consider setting a public hearing on the
FY2021-2022 Tentative budget for June 1, 2021.
         Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide
Council staffs review of the Mayor's FY22 Tentative Budget. The Council has adopted fmancial principles for FY2021-2022,
including:
              •    Conservative revenue estimates;
              •   Balanced budget;
              •   Employee salaries as a budget priority;
              •   Investments;

               •     Fund Balance;
               •     Operating Reserves.
          Additionally, FY 2021-2022 budget goals and guidelines include establishing goals of the upcoming budget, setting
policy regarding tax rates, and establishing priorities for spending. The Mayor's tentative budget does not include a proposal to
increase property tax rates for FY22. High priorities items included in the Mayor's budget include employee compensation plan
implementation, replacement of eliminated line items in the FY21 budget (travel and education for employees), fleet replacement,
transition to a self-insurance model for employee health insurance, and CIP process streamlining and funding. Relative to staffmg
changes, five new positions will be created, one will be reclassified, and two renamed.
          Ms. Eller-Smith then presented a chart comparing general fund revenues from the adopted FY21 budget with the FY22
tentative budget; revenues are projected to increase by $7 million, or 11.98 percent. Using the same comparison, the total City
budget will increase roughly $30 million, or 16.30% when compared to the adopted FY21 budget. One of the primary directives
from the Five-Year Strategic Plan is to "leverage our unique location, outdoor recreation, diverse population, and rich history to
offer residents, businesses, and guests a vibrant community that leads people to call Ogden home." Council budget goals aimed at
meeting this directive include economic development, community safety, recreation, City image and reputation, and fiscal
sustainability and transparency. She briefly expounded on each of these budget goals and identified various budget items that are
aimed at achieving the goals, after which she touched on the adjustment to the process for reviewing the City's Capital
Improvement Plan (CIP) in conjunction with the budget. She concluded that at present there are no recommended changes to the
Mayor's Tentative Budget since it was submitted on May 5; the Council may direct additional changes to the budget via schedule
A-1 before adoption of the fmal budget. Council staff recommends the Council set the public hearing for June 1, 2021 to consider
adoption of the tentative budget for FY2021-22
