Regular Session, 7:00 p.m.

1. Call to Order
Commission Chair Jackson called the meeting to order at approximately 7:07 p.m.

2. Regular Agenda

2.1) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for review of a
Development Code Amendment adding in the Attainable Homeownership Overlay Zone.

Brittney Harris presented on the item with assistance from Jason Harris. She explained that the
AHOZ is a highly collaborative project which has been in development for over a year. She cited
studies on housing unattainability that emphasized the inflation within the housing market. She
expressed worry that if the Commission and Lehi City don’t decide on a plan to make housing
more attainable, the state will step in with a program that is not tailored to the community’s
needs. Jason Harris brought up the plans that other cities are going through with, including
Provo’s city-owned developments and the DADU program in Farmington. Brittney Harris
emphasized that the city’s main goals for this project are to raise the amount of essential workers
that are homeowners, allow the project to be market driven, and honor the traditions of Lehi by
making housing more attainable for community members.

As it stands, AHOZ will allow developers to make deals with the city wherein they can develop
higher density housing if the homes go for at least 20% lower than similar homes on the market.
Each development agreement that is made with the city will outline the density, the size of
homes, and the sales price, which will hold regardless of changes in the market. Brittney Harris
clarified that the risk factor lies on the developer, and that the Commission can recommend to

the City Council that reconsideration of pricing be included in the code, but that it is not
currently included.

Commissioner Everett asked which comparable would affect the price point, as it is currently
limited to a one-mile radius. He posited that the price should be comparable to attainable housing
in other areas. Brittney Harris explained that the current plan is set up to provide incentives to
developers, and that it has been reworked so that homeowners can now attain full equity in five
years rather than ten. Commissioner Everett was still concerned about the AHOZ code being too
vague. Brittney Harris clarified that it is the City Council that will be the ultimate decision maker
on which development deals get approved, and that it is the looseness of the ordinance that
allows the city more freedom. Commissioner Everett further worried that the wording would lead
to legal repercussions, which Brittney Harris countered by stating that as the overlay zone is
completely discretionary, there would be no legal recourse for the City if it were to deny deals.

Commissioner Lockhart discussed density as a desire for developers and infrastructure as a
desire of the city. She asked why savings aren’t already being passed down to the customer when
increases in density are approved. Brittney Harris explained that the developers Lehi City works
with don’t always have the control to affect prices without government intervention. Jason Harris
added that the price of housing is also affected by the supply of properties that local governments
provide. Commissioner Lockhart asked him what was driving up the price. Jason Harris stated
that it is both the market and the cost stack. Brittney Harris explained that the AHOZ would help
prevent bidding wars between buyers that drive up housing prices.

Brittney Harris continued with her presentation on the topic, explaining that the AHOZ would
encourage income diversity within the city and reduce long-distance traffic. She presented three
studies that show that attainable housing is usually accompanied by a neutral to positive effect on
surrounding property values. Negative effects were the result of poor management. Profit
incentives are a part of the plan for better or for worse, as they are needed to get capitalist
investment. She emphasized that this is a baby step for the community and that change is
necessary if the housing situation in Lehi is going to get better. Jason Harris stated that not every
builder is going to jump at this opportunity, but even so it is the best plan that he’s been able to
come up with.

Commission Chair Jackson opened public comment.

Jason Turner commented online that he is a fan of the idea, and that he likes the idea of making
homes easier to build.

Leia Jay gave a presentation for the organization Strong Towns Lehi. She explained that housing
boils down to a density issue. She stated that allowing denser areas to be priced lower is
important for Lehi’s financial stability as a city. Her concern is making housing in general
attainable, rather than making individual houses attainable. If the supply increases, then houses
will naturally see a decrease in value. She stated that it was a matter of looking at housing as a
necessary shelter rather than as an economic investment. She also mentioned that treating
property as investment has caused major economic downturn in the last decade.

Marshall Gary stated that while the AHOZ is a big deal, it’s not going to have as radical an
impact as the Planning Commission is worried about. He shared a personal anecdote about his
great-great-great grandparents who were able to use their property to care for multiple
generations of family, including himself. He explained that it was important to him and his
family that others have that same opportunity. He asked the Commission to give the AHOZ a
positive recommendation.

Kenzie Stirling is a senior at Skyridge High School. She talked about wanting to own a house
one day, but not seeing a possibility for her to do that in Lehi. She explained that the AHOZ
would help young people enter the housing market. She stated that she understood that this might
lead to a curbing of amenities, but that she is able and willing to manage her expectations.

Evan Hilton is an American Fork resident. He discussed how the cities are interconnected, and
that the benefits of including the AHOZ will not only apply to Lehi citizens, but anyone who
needs to travel between cities. He also mentioned that he is a fan of the smaller lawns that will
result from the AHOZ.

Eva was also present with Strong Towns Lehi. She stated that she is a big proponent of
incremental change. She explained that there is not going to be a perfectly elegant solution to the
problem, and that the city needed to be okay with compromise for the sake of the greater good.

Commission Chair Jackson closed public comment.

Commissioner Lockhart asked how many cities accepted this same goal that was assigned by the
state. Brittney Harris explained that the various city plans were all available online. Jason Harris
mentioned that Weber County was using a similar system, but without the development
agreement.

Commissioner Kunze asked if a housing authority would be needed to monitor ownership of the
house. Brittney Harris explained that since everything would be outlined in the development
agreement, a deed restriction would come up on any title search until it no longer applied. The
authority that governed this would be decided on a case-by-case basis. Commissioner Eyre asked
about the potential for getting equity before the five-year stipulation was up. Brittney Harris
explained that owners can attain 40% of equity before the deed restriction was lifted.
Commissioners Eyre and Jackson agreed that it would require a housing authority to enforce.
Brittney Harris offered that an HOA could also be a possibility.

Commissioner Jackson stated that he doesn’t see the downsides being collectively absorbed by
the community. He sees the AHOZ as hiding the value of the land and artificially suppressing the
price. Jason Harris stated that the prerogative is to get families in homes more than it is about
building houses. Not every home builder is going to participate, just those that have the desire to
help build the community. He states that there are plenty of people developing land for rent. He
emphasizes that if no steps are taken, it is likely that many members of younger generations are
never going to own homes. He reiterates that equity gained from owning property is a result of
not having enough housing on the market.

Commissioner Lockhart once again mentioned that current density bumps haven’t affected
housing prices. She asked if the overlay zone is necessary for social good, and once again asks
why developers aren’t passing savings down to the consumer. Jason Harris explains that the
costs of building have gone up faster than home prices, and that the take home margin for many
developers is 6-10%. Demand for housing drives up costs in all areas, which also drives up
prices. He stated that the AHOZ would help to slow that process.

Commissioner Everett stated that there is a finite supply of land for homes within city limits, and
therefore the supply isn’t entirely in control of the city. He also worried about the ability of
current water and sewage systems to keep up with increased density. He stated that he would
prefer not to have a housing authority. He is worried that with the way the code is written that it
will cause the problem to get worse once the deed limit goes away. He is also concerned about
transparency, given that it is reliant on the current City Council, something that can and does
change frequently. He recommended crafting an ordinance that would require owner occupation.
He also stated that he liked the plan that Provo is going through with and suggests that the city
should purchase a small piece of land as a sample to see if something like that would work for
Lehi. He argued that working directly through the city would make the process more transparent.

Commissioner Everett also mentioned several minor clarifying changes to the ordinance itself.
Commissioner Lockhart asked that the name change be reflected throughout the document.
Commissioner Everett reiterated that he would like to take a different route in pursuit of housing
attainability.

Commissioner Lockhart stated that the AHOZ doesn’t address her concerns. She explains that
while it addresses the cost, it doesn’t address the market. She also stated that she thinks it is
unfair, as other people who have already gotten mortgages might be negatively affected if the
market were to be negatively impacted. She states that 75% of Lehi citizens are homeowners,
and that this ordinance wouldn’t help them in any way. She stated that she wants more clarity on
the outcomes and more work done on the ordinance. Commissioner Eyre clarified that the
purpose of the ordinance is not to help every resident in Lehi, but that it is supposed to support
critical workers and first-time homeowners.

Commissioner Jackson wanted to know that the deed wouldn’t count as a comp. Commissioner
Eyre stated that he doesn’t think the city would be able to guarantee that, but it wouldn’t be
insurmountable.

Commissioner Lockhart stated that she is against growing the government, as there are already a
lot of things the city needs to pay for without the addition of a housing authority.

Commissioner Jackson acknowledged that this will not lead to truly affordable housing. He
stated his concern that the 20% decrease in price might not be enough for the people who need it
the most. He asked that a very defined list of critical workers be developed, as well as a
definition of what counts as a “Lehi resident.” He suggested that the required period of time be
lengthened beyond a year. He mentioned that he would like to have the ordinance adjusted so
that the 30-day limit is reset if a deal falls through. Jason Harris reassured him that developers
would be eager to sell the house because that’s when it costs the most money with the least
amount of profit.

Jason Harris reiterated that this isn’t an all or nothing deal, but he would like a positive
recommendation from the Commission, even if it comes with caveats.


                Motion: Commissioner Everett moved to provide a negative recommendation to
                Lehi City Council, as the ordinance is not in conformance to the general building
                code without structural changes to the general plan. The AHOZ has too many
                parts that aren’t defined. The findings of fact are difficult as all outcomes at this
                point are supposition. The quality of life of Lehi citizens could also be negatively
                affected by increasing density, overcrowding schools, and public services like
                water and sewage systems. Commissioner Lockhart asked that the motion be
                amended to state that more stakeholders from the mortgage side of the issue
                should be contacted. Commissioner Everett agreed. Commissioner Lockhart
                seconded the motion.

                Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes. Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner
                Lockhart, yes. Commissioner Eyre, no. Commissioner Kunze, yes. Motion passed
                four for, one against.
