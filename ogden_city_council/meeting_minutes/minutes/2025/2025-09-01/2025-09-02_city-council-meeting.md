Proposed Ordinance 2025–28 recognizing revenues and appropriating
funds totaling $3,342,875
          A memo from the City Finance Director regarding a proposed Fiscal Year 2025-2026 budget amendment came before the
Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposed amendment recognizes revenues and appropriates funds totaling
$3,342,875. Proposed revenues come from fund balance ($750,750), a State grant for storm sewer improvements ($158,000), a
state homeless mitigation grant for equipment purchase ($305,000), and state homeless mitigation grant for public safety wages
and other direct costs ($1,597,125). Recommended appropriations will fund a property purchase ($350,000), police vehicle
purchases ($645,000), storm sewer improvements ($158,000), and public safety wages and operational costs ($1,439,125).
          Finance Director Sorensen summarized the memo and presented a spreadsheet highlighting the source of revenues
involved in the budget amendment, as well as the specific line items in the budget in which the appropriations will be accounted
for.
          Council member Choberka thanked Administration for its willingness to share homeless mitigation funds with The
Lantern House; the services they provide are impactful.
          Chair White introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2025-28, entitled:
                    “An ordinance of Ogden City amending the budget for the Fiscal Year July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026 by
                    increasing the anticipated revenues for a gross increase of $3,342,875 from sources as detailed in the body of
                    this ordinance; and increasing the appropriations for a gross increase of $3,342,875 as detailed in the body of
                    this ordinance; and by providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final
                    passage.”
          Chair White then opened the public hearing.
          Linda Sanchez expressed concerns about the criminalization of homelessness and the need for more funding for homeless
mitigation in the City.
          Steve Griffin thanked the City for its partnership with My Hometown Ogden, and he highlighted the organization's
contributions to the community. By the end of the current calendar year, the organization and other charitable entities will have
completed projects at over 1,600 properties in inner-city Ogden; this includes support from 2,500 volunteers in the community
who will have donated 66,000 hours of service. The Organization is also hosting three community resource centers that will have
taught over 100 classes to over 2,000 students over three semesters. He noted the purchase of property on Childs Avenue would
benefit all in the community; it is not only a historic site for the community, but one that can continue to be used as a center that
can meet the needs of many in the community.
          Teresa Bramwell shared her experiences with crime and homelessness in her neighborhood and called for more funding
to address these issues.
          There being no further persons appearing to be heard, VICE CHAIR HYER MOVED THE PUBLIC HEARING BE
CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER GRAF, ALL VOTING AYE.
          Chair White encouraged responses to the comments made during the public hearing. Council member Choberka stated it
is her understanding that there has been increased criminalization of people who are living in homeless shelters and that may be
the basis of Ms. Sanchez’s comments; however, she believes City Administration and the Police Department have worked
relentlessly to consider alternatives to criminalization and she has full faith that the City’s victim advocate program helps with
those efforts as well. Mayor Nadolski agreed it has not been the Police Department’s practice to criminalize homelessness; rather,
they consider behavior to determine if criminal acts have occurred and how they should be addressed. He discussed the success of
the homeless advocate programs and noted Ogden City is the first he is aware of to have implemented a homeless medical
advocate program, which is designed to provide medical services to people where they are at, including in and around homeless
shelters. This has proven to reduce the number of people who need to be transported to the emergency room. He stressed that
Ogden City is dealing with the bulk of the homelessness issue in northern Utah without much help from neighboring cities. He
understands Ms. Bramwell’s concerns and is working to address them for her and others throughout the City, while also being as
compassionate and caring as possible when dealing with issues related to homelessness.
          Council member Myers thanked Mr. Griffin for his leadership and the work being done by My Hometown Ogden; he
noted some of the classes that are offered by the organization and encouraged anyone who needs service to research the offerings
of the organization. He also supports the purchase of the Childs Avenue property to be used as an additional learning center.
          ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS AND SECONDED BY VICE CHAIR HYER, ORDINANCE
2025-28 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL MEMBERS

Public Comments
         Chair White stated she believes the majority of those present this evening are interested in the topic of the Taylor Canyon
Elementary School property; she invited Mayor Nadolski to provide information about City Administration’s position regarding
the property before opening the meeting to public comment.
          Mayor Nadolski stated that the Ogden School District has determined to initiate the process to sell the property
commonly known as Taylor Canyon; the City has some interest in the property and has reached out to the District to ask for more
than 90 days to make the determination regarding the acquisition of the property. He wants the City to first assess the current
amount and use of open spaces in the community before deciding whether to proceed with purchasing the property. He then noted
that it will take up to 60 days to appraise the property, and he also wants to reach out to the neighborhoods directly surrounding
the property to determine their feelings about the potential real estate transaction. There are many options that can be considered
that will serve to stabilize the neighborhood in question.
          The following individuals addressed the potential purchase of the Taylor Canyon Elementary School property and
encouraged the City to purchase the property and preserve it as an open, green space rather than to allow it to be developed for a
high-density housing project: Heath Satow, Stacia Ryder, Hannah Wagner, Arlo Kunzler, Elsa Bowman, Jordan Wilkinson, Laura
Lewis, Suzanne Vincent, James Humphreys, Martyn Long, Chelsea Alexander, Kirk Clausse, Phil Okarma, Kurt Kunzler, Jackie
Francisco Okarma, Linda Sanchez, Josie Wilkinson, Karen Barrett, and Craig Rollins.
          Teresa Bramwell stated she can understand the position of those who want the Taylor Canyon property to be purchased
by the City and preserved as open space; however, she has her own personal experience with Lester Park, which the City has
promised to develop and improve for several years, but that project has not been a priority and there has been a significant
increase in crime and homelessness in the park. The people who have spoken tonight have even talked about the disrepair of the
two existing parks in their neighborhood. She noted Ogden City is lacking single family homes and taxpayers and she feels the
Taylor Canyon property could be used for that purpose and perhaps it could include a pocket park. There is currently way too
much high-density housing in downtown Ogden, especially below Harrison Boulevard, and it is time for the people who live
above Harrison Boulevard to take their fair share.

Mayor Comments
          Mayor Nadolski commented on his feelings about the need for open space and programmed spaces in the community that
can be used for recreational activities and sports. Close access to park spaces and sports fields is important. However, the
comments made by Ms. Bramwell are a reality as well; there has been an increase in crime and homelessness in Lester Park and
some of the things that have occurred there are unacceptable. The City made an enormous promise to the neighborhoods around
Lester Park about a decade ago, but it was not possible to deliver on that promise, and he has since been engaging the community
on the matter to develop plans for the first phase of a multi-million improvement project at Lester Park. He noted he will not be
proposing apartments for the Taylor Canyon property, and he is committed to a well-thought-out approach to the future use of the
property. He then expressed his gratitude to the Council for voting to support the budget amendment that will give the City the
ability to purchase property on Childs Avenue and partner with My Hometown Ogden to provide service programs to the broader
community to help combat some of the issues that have been referenced by Ms. Bramwell. He reiterated he is committed to a
deliberative process regarding the Taylor Canyon property, as well as all other open spaces owned and maintained by the City,
and he will not be making any proposal to the Council that has not been well thought out and considered.

Council member Comments
          Council member Graf thanked the public for their valuable input tonight. Vice Chair Hyer agreed and added that any
resident can reach out to any member of the City Council if they want to discuss their concerns in greater detail; there is a lot of
exploration and work left to do on the Taylor Canyon property.
          Council member Blair stated that he is proud to be from Ogden and to have attended Taylor Canyon Elementary School;
he learned a lot of valuable lessons at the park, as well as practiced his skills in many sports throughout the year and he
understands the passion with which so many residents expressed their opinions this evening. He has fielded many phone calls over
the past several days and he is happy to hear the vision of those that live around the property. He does not disagree with anything
that has been said tonight about the value of open space; it makes for better and healthier communities, and he believes the City
has a wonderful opportunity to create something great on the property. He looks forward to a collaborative process with City
Administration to make Ogden better through this opportunity.
          Council member Choberka stated she also lived just a few houses away from Taylor Canyon Elementary for the first six
years she lived in Ogden; she understands the support for preserving the property. However, she now lives in downtown Ogden
near Lester Park and loves the neighborhood she lives in. She does not have the same experiences with Lester Park as have been
expressed by Ms. Bramwell, but she is excited about the recent developments to plan the first phase of the improvement of that
amenity.
          Council member Myers also thanked those who provided input this evening.
          Council member Richey also thanked those who participated tonight; he is also grateful to those who have reached out to
him personally. He is pleased by the Mayor’s comments about his decision-making process and is happy to know a final decision
has not been made. He also looks forward to a collaborative process with City Administration.
          Chair White echoed the gratitude to the public for providing input tonight. She reiterated no decision has been made
regarding the matter and she looks forward to working through the process to evaluate the property. She supports strategic
evaluation of this space and all open spaces and parks in the community to determine the benefit they offer to the community is
maximized.
