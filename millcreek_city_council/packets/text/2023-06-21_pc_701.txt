       1.1 Consideration              of ZT-23-008,           Proposed          Millcreek          Code        Text       Amendments             Altering
       the R-I      Zones         and Residential         Compatibility            Overlay           Zone         (RCOZ)          Planner:       Robert
       May
       Robert     May       said the commission            would       not be taking             action      on the two agenda items.                  Staff's
       intent   was to embed the standards                    of RCOZ       in the R-1 zoning                  code and try to make               smaller
       lots more attractive            to homeowners           by adding         development               alternatives          while    preserving
       single-family          neighborhoods.           The R-I      zone means,            residential         - single      family       - square feet
       of lot (R-1-3          for example       has 3,000       square feet). RCOZ                 was adopted             in 2009 as an overlay
       zone covering           the majority        of residential      zones in Millcreek.                   It was meant          to balance
       neighborhood            compatibility        for those who wished                 to expand,           develop,      improve,         or otherwise
       make exterior           modifications        to single     family    dwellings.             It further        restricts     residential
       development            in terms     of height,     setbacks,      lot covetage,            and massing.             The significant           changes
Millcreek      Planning         Commission           Meeting            Minutes                                21 June           2023        Page 2 of 6



    in the code he proposed               were two additional                  building         envelope        alternatives,             increasing         the
    building      height      from    28 feet to 30 feet in the R-1-3,                      R-1-4,        R-1-5,       R-1-6,      and R-1-8          zones,
    and removing           the RCOZ        option      C. The 45 degree angle building                              envelope         requirement             (after
    8 feet above original             ground      surface)         would       remain       and apply          specifically          to lots in the R-1-
    8, R-I-10,      R-1-15,       and R-1-21        zones. The first proposed                         alternative         would      be a 60 degree
    angle building          envelope      (after 12 feet above original                       ground         surface)        and it would           apply      to
    narrower      lots in the R-1-6            zone. This allowed                 for the addition            of a second           story.      The second
    proposed       alternative        was an envelope               at the building           setback        line instead          of the property             line
    for the R-1-3,         R-1-4,      and R-1-5      zones. This would                    allow      30 feet above the original                     ground
    surface     at a 90 degree angle. He noted the alternative                                  envelopes          did not apply             to much        of the
    city. May      said removal          of RCOZ            option      C presented           too many         legal concems                 for the city by
    having      the plai'ining       commission         members             determine           special      exceptions.           Variances         would          be
    the solution        and they are reviewed                by the land use hearing                    officer     who is appointed                 by the
    city and trained          to hear and decide             requests         for variances.


    May      said the staff goals for revising                  the code standards                  were to: identify              and continue             what
    worked,      modify       what did not work,               write     better        definitions,        comply         with     state statutes,           put all
    the requirements            into one zoning             regulation,         and follow          the General           Plan which           calls to find
    a balance      between       supporting        infill      development               and preserving            neighborhood               character.
    May      reported      that 94% of those building                   a new home              in Millcreek            were able to build               under
    the current      code without          need of a deviation                 or special          exception        and 92% of those seeking                         to
    do residential         additions     in Millcreek           were able to build                 under      the current          code without             need
    of a deviation         or special     exception.          A large majority               of the homes              built,    and residential
    additions      perinitted        without    seeking        relief       were likely          in the R-1-8           and R-1-10           zones. The
    vast majority        of the R-I       zones are R-1-8               and R-1-10,           which        account        for about 40% of the
    entire     city. These zones are typically                     larger     allowing        for more        flexibility,         making
    development          easier. The remaining                 permits        issued for new homes                     and residential            additions
    that sought      relief     were likely       due to smaller              lot sizes and/or            homes         recognized           as
    noncomplying            due to setbacks,         lot coverage,              etc. Side yards            are wider,           particularly        on wider
    lots. This helps keep the mass of a home more                                     consistent      with     surrounding            uses. He said for
    the most part, especially              on lots in an R-1-8                 zone and above,               it is easy to put a second                     story
    on a home.       Gable       and dormer         exceptions           ensure that these second                      stories      are useful,        while
    reducing      the mass of taller           homes.        RCOZ           considers       neighborhood               compatibility,             by allowing
    applicants      to modi'fy        setbacks,     heights,         and lot coverage               based on the characteristics                       of
    surrounding         homes.       This is a rarely          used tool but has proven                      useful.


    May      said an area for improvement                     with     RCOZ           included      making         the definitions             better.      The
    building      envelope       standards       prevent        a second          story on homes              on narrower            lots, particularly               in
    an R-1-6      or R-1-5       zone. This could              impact        infill     opportunities          and neighborhood                   stability.
    Maximum         heights      may be a little            low,     considering           market      preferences              for taller     ceiling
    heights.     Having       two different        zoning          standards          created      confusion.          He said the proposed
    changes      would      help to address the following                       General         Plan goals:         N-1, N-2,             and HE-7.         May
    noted the Millcreek              Community          Council's           recommendation                 letter was received                 that day.


    Jamie Walker,           Millcreek      Community               Council,           referenced       the council's             letter    and would
    speak during         the hearing       as a resident.            Learu'ie Hansen,              East Mill        Creek        Community             Council,
    said the council          supported        the proposed            code changes.             She said the council                 did not discuss
    removing       RCOZ         to the east of I-215.

    1.2 Consideration              of ZT-23-009,             Proposed           Millcreek            Code      Text      Amendment             Altering
    the R-2 and R-4 Zones                     Planners:       Brad      Sanderson             and Carlos               Estudfflo
    Carlos     Estudillo      presented         maps of the R-2 zones in the city to the commission.                                         The proposal
    was to combine            the R-2 (duplexes)              and R-4 (fourplexes)                    zone standards             into the new medium
    density    zone. The R-4-8.5                zone would           be designated            as a legacy           district     so new applications
    for legacy       zones would          no longer         be accepted.          The proposal              would        consolidate        uses and site
    standards,       and new graphics               and tables would             be added. The minimum                          lot area, minimum            lot
    width,     and setbacks           (except     rear setback)         would            remain      the same. Brad             Sanderson       said the
    changes      included         removing        all nonresidential             uses except           civic     and institutional           uses
    (conditionally),         not imposing            design     standards          on two-unit            dwellings,           increasing     the rear
    yard setback          from 15 to 20 feet, increasing                   the building              height     from      28 to 30 feet, having             a
    90 degree building             envelope,        allowing         limited      projections            into the setbacks           (2 feet into side
    yard and 4 feet into front                 and rear yards),         providing           new tables          for uses and spatial
    requirements,          and providing            new graphics          for zoning          concepts.          He said the intent            was to
Millcreek       Planning           Commission             Meeting       Minutes                             21 June        2023     Page 5 of 6



    allow      medium           density     residential,      not the extra uses. He presented                    the proposed         permitted
    and conditional              uses table.


    Estudillo         presented      the proposed            setback    and lot size requirements                 table. The current             lot
    coverage          standards      would         remain     the same in the R-2 and R-4 zones. The building                                envelope
    would       be modified          from      45 degrees to 90 degrees                 at the building          setback     line. Lots in the R-2
    and R-4 zones tended                   to be narrower          than R-1 lots. This would                provide        more flexible           options

    the style of units built               in the R-2 and R-4 zones.
