Engineering Projects Update

               Shane Ellis, Engineer, updated the Council on the following projects:

   -   The bid for the Sego Lily project came in high. Part of the project is being rebid and other
       possible funding options are being looked at. The tentative completion date has been
       extended to the end of 2024.
   -   A safe sidewalk grant was awarded for Onyx Lane. An environmental study will need to
       be completed and the grant does not cover 100% of the project. The Council will need to
       come up with $250,000 in matching funds. He will recommend funding for the project in
       the 2024 capital improvement project requests.
   -   The lights for the canal trail will start being installed this month. A grant was obtained for
       the rest of the canal trail with the metro township needing to come up with $50,000.

He would prioritize the canal trail and the Onyx Lane sidewalk project as high priorities. Rori
Andreason has updated the Smartsheet with all the proposed projects and the Council’s priorities.
A cost estimate for those projects has been created. He does not know where city hall will land
or how the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD) will handle a new building.

            Mayor Flint stated if the bid for Sego Lily comes in higher, the remainder of the
ARPA funds can be put together. The 2024 project wish list may have to be set aside to fund
Sego Lily.

               Mr. Ellis stated a list of proposed projects need to be proposed to the MSD, he will
include the canal trail and Onyx Lane sidewalk match.

Unified Police Department (UPD) and Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area
(SLVLESA)

               Paul Ashton stated attorneys for the metro townships have been meeting to
discuss the effects of H.B. 374. They are recommending the metro townships sign a joint letter,
to be submitted to the Political Subdivision Committee, proposing the metro townships be
converted to cities or towns. Representative Jordan Tuescher has agreed to sponsor a bill.
Depending on how the bill is written, there will be at least one election to determine the form of
government. The conversion would allow the metro township to collect property tax.

A meeting will be held tomorrow with the Salt Lake County Mayor, Sheriff Rivera, Mayors of the
metro townships, and attorneys. They will be talking about what the future of policing may look
like. Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA) has appointed a new attorney,
Rachel Anderson.

Amending Titles 18 and 19 of the White City Metro Township Zoning Code

              The Council reviewed the following Ordinance repealing and replacing Titles 18 &

ORDINANCE NO. 2023-O-01                                                        Date: June 1, 2023

       AN ORDINANCE OF WHITE CITY METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL TO REPEAL
       AND REPLACE TITLES 18 & 19 OF MUNICIPAL CODE, PROVISIONS ON
       ZONING AND SUBDIVISIONS

       WHEREAS, the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District provides services to the five
Metro Township in the Salt Lake Valley, unincorporated areas, and the Town of Brighton; and

        WHEREAS, White City Metro Township adopted each county ordinance in effect at the
time of its incorporation pursuant to Utah Code Ann. Subsection 10-2a-414(3) and has authority
to amend or repeal the county ordinance when it determines it is necessary; and

       WHEREAS, White City Metro Township is a municipality and has authority to regulate
zoning and subdivisions in general pursuant to Utah Code Ann. Subsection 10-3c-103 (2); and

        WHEREAS, White City Metro Township has authority to adopt zoning and subdivision

Development, and Management Act, (“MLUDMA”), Title 10, Section 9a, Utah Code, to protect
public health, safety, and welfare; and

in order to ensure compliance with State Statute, encourage conformity with the White City
General Plan, promote ease of use for residents and staff, and preserve the unique character of
the White City community; and for the protection and preservation of the public health, safety, and
general welfare.

notice as required by Subsection 10-9a-205(1)(a) and, if applicable, Subsection 10-9a-205(4) and

Justice Court Audit and Future Process

                                               6
DATE            THURSDAY                                JUNE                                 1, 2023


                Paul Ashton stated Salt Lake County has given notice that it will dissolve the
justice courts that the metro townships use to handle court matters. Salt Lake County is committed
to retaining the justice courts until the metro townships have found alternative options. If the metro
townships do not find a place, cases will go to the third district court. In 2022 Salt Lake County
asked for an audit and it brought up some concerns. The auditor followed up and the justice courts
carried out about half of the recommendations. The Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District
(MSD) has asked for more information regarding the funds. The Salt Lake County Auditor’s Office
has more issues beyond the published audit. The cost of a justice court includes a judge, staff,
and contracts. Citizens are entitled to legal representation and the metro townships will have to
contract for indigent legal. The Council needs to start looking into what options are available.

               Mayor Flint asked what the process is for retaining a justice court.

              Mr. Ashton stated the Council will enter into an agreement with a justice court,
then get approval from the Utah Judicial Council.

               Council Member Perry, seconded by Council Member Price, moved to direct Mayor
Flint and the Administrator to look for an alternate justice court. The motion passed unanimously.

      RESOLUTION NO. 2023-08-01                                DATE: August 3, 2023
              A RESOLUTION OF THE WHITE CITY METRO TOWNSHIP
                  ADOPTING THE AMENDED 2023 FEE SCHEDULE
                                            RECITALS
       WHEREAS, the White City Metro Township (“White City”) inherited the version of Salt
Lake County’s Code that was in existence on the date of White City’s incorporation in 2017
pursuant to Utah Code § 10-2a-414(3); and

        WHEREAS, as a result, Section 19.94.070 of the White City Metro Township Code (the
“Code”) set forth the civil penalties for zoning violations (the “Civil Penalties”) within the Code
rather than within the White City Fee Schedule; and

       WHEREAS, as part of White City’s recodification of its Code, the White City Council
(the “Council”) desires to move the Civil Penalties from Title 19 of the Code to the White City
Fee Schedule; and
        WHEREAS, when the Council adopted the 2023 White City Fee Schedule on December
21, 2022, the revisions to Title 19 were still in process and, as a result, the Council adopted the fee
schedule subject to the civil penalties remaining in Title 19 until such time as Title 19 could be
restated to remove the Civil Penalties; and
       WHEREAS, White City has adopted a final version of Title 19 that does not include the
Civil Penalties; and
       WHEREAS, because Title 19 no longer includes the Civil Penalties, the Council desires to
amend the 2023 Fee Schedule to include the Civil Penalties that were previously set forth in Section
19.94.070; and
       WHEREAS, the Council also desires to revise language in Section 5.02.060 of the Code
regarding the fees White City applies to business license withdrawal fees to coincide with the 2023
Fee Schedule.

    NOW, THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED BY THE WHITE CITY METRO TOWNSHIP
COUNCIL that effectively immediately:
       1.      The attached amended 2023 fee schedule is adopted: and

       2.      Section 5.02.060.B of the Code is amended to read as follows:

 Glossary
 CODE: Code enforcement

 Condominium Plat: The procedure to review and record a condominium plat is subject to the Condominium
 Ownership Act (57.8- Utah Code). Staff review includes addressing all units, a review to verify compliance with
 the zoning ordinance and conditions of approval previously imposed and an engineering review to verify
 compliance with platting requirements.

Planning and Development Services
COM: Commercial

DIRECTOR: The Director of Planning and Development or Designee.

ENG: Engineering

Equation: Used when the fee is based on an equation structure that cannot be easily defined in a table.
Please contact the appropriate department for more detail on what that fee includes.

General Plan Amendment: Planning Commissions make a recommendation to the Council who must
authorize Amendments to a General Plan. A study that includes public involvement is conducted after Council
gives the direction to proceed to the Development Services Director.

Home Daycare I Pre-school Application Fees: Although a home daycare I pre-school is operated out of a
private residence, it is not considered or reviewed in the same manner as a home business. Therefore, they
are listed separately in the fee schedule and in the ordinance.

Modification to a Recorded Subdivision Plat: Utah Code requires a specific process be followed to amend,
vacate or alter a recorded subdivision plat. This involves application, notice, a public hearing before the
planning commission and executive (commonly referred to as a 608 hearing/ Mayor's Meeting).
Additionally, an engineering review of the preliminary and final plat prior to approval and recording is required.
Fees may include: Planning Commission Review, Additional Public Body Review, Technical Review.

MSD: Means the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, which White City has contracted with to
provide planning and zoning, building permit, business license, and code enforcement services.

PUD (Planned Unit Development): In those zones which allow development of a PUD they are listed as a
Conditional Use, which requires review by the Planning Commission. For developers who intend to sell
individual lots within the PUD both the Planning Commission Review Conditional Use and a Subdivision
Preliminary Plat review would be required, and a Technical Review prior to final approval is also required. Per
the fee schedule each of these reviews requires separate fee.

Additionally, because more than one review process is required the application would also involve an Agency
Review Meeting. Fees may include: Agency Coordination Meeting, Planning Commission Review (Conditional
Use), Planning Commission Review (Preliminary Plat), Technical Review.

The conditional use approval (planning commission approval) is required prior to preparation of the subdivision
preliminary plat to ensure that the recommendations of the Planning Commission are properly incorporated
into the preliminary plat.

Re-Zone (Zoning Map Amendment): A request to change the existing zoning (re-zone) requires: review and
recommendation from the planning commission (Public Body Review) and final decision by the council
(Additional Public Body Review) and technical work (Technical Review) for map and index work).

Res: Residential

Signs: Signs vary in the type and complexity of review process required therefore they are listed under several
review types. It is intended that the fees are assessed per review process and not per sign. For example, a
business that had 2 signs which required Planning Commission review would be charged for 1 planning
commission review. However, a business which had 1 sign which required planning commission review and
another sign which did not, would be charged for 1 planning commission review and 1 staff review.

Subdivision: A request to subdivide property requires review and approval of a preliminary plat, and a
Technical Review of the Final Plat. Additionally, an Agency Review Meeting is required. Note that in the case
of a "one-lot" subdivision there might also be an Administrative Review for the proposed Single-Family
Dwelling. Fees may include: Agency Coordination Meeting, Planning Commission Meeting, Technical Review,
Staff Review of a Site Plan.

Valuation: The estimated construction cost for the project
