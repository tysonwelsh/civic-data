5. Informational Items
        a. Review of Balanced Housing Report for 2025

     Mark Forsythe presented background on the Balanced Housing Ordinance that was adopted
     in 2014. The ordinance was created to manage multi-family development and ensure
     density occurs in appropriate locations. Over time, the ordinance has been amended to
     include exceptions for areas deemed suitable for multi-family housing, such as transit station
     overlay districts, larger planned communities, and highway interchange zones.

     The city’s target housing ratio is 77% single-family and 23% multi-family. Trends indicate
     steady growth in single family housing since 2023, while multi-family development
     fluctuates. Achieving the 77/23 ratio would require 8,516 additional single-family units and,
     at the current pace, could take 31 years assuming no new multi-family units are built.

     Commissioner Roberts asked clarifying questions about how the ratio is adjusted if a multi-
     family unit is approved. Larry Gardner gave information about how the ordinance has
     successfully guided development, but how the definition of single-family housing excludes
     attached single-family homes. Mark Forsythe clarified that these numbers are acquired
     based on how many building permits were issued during the year.
