On the request of Drew Parcell, representing DC Joint Ventures for a Site Plan and Plat
Amendment in order to approve a parking lot addition in the Draper Cove development on 2.16
acres of property at 839 East Pioneer Road. Applications: SPR-1035-2020 and SUBD-1036-2020.
Staff Contact: Jennifer Jastremsky, 801-576-6328, jennifer.jastremsky@draper.ut.us

Jennifer Jastremsky, Senior Planner, indicated that phase one of the project had already begun and
that the project was in the Town Center land use area and zoning designation. She noted that the
applicant was looking to add a property to their site and explained the proposed changes with the
proposed site addition to their plan, which mainly included the removal of the two north west
building units and the expansion of the parking lot onto the new property. She noted they had
acquired shared access with the office building to the north.

Ms. Jastremsky remarked that the applicant’s plans met the parking standards and landscaping
standards. She pointed out there was a plat amendment with the application so the commercial
units could be sold individually and create a common ownership for the parking and other common
areas. She provided site photos of the new property they purchased as well as the commercial
building in phase one currently under construction.

Commissioner Squire inquired if they were also developing the corner lot.

Ms. Jastremsky replied that they recently saw a site plan for the property on the corner. She
clarified that it had a separate property owner but were building a similar thing. She shared that
the applicant intended to purchase the home South of the parking area and redevelop it.

Commissioner Ogden asked if the parking along Pioneer Road counted toward their parking count.

Ms. Jastremsky stated the parking spots along Pioneer Road and 8th East did not count toward their
minimum parking count.

Commissioner Van Hoff inquired how they would manage the percentage of use.

Ms. Jastremsky indicated they would be evaluated with the building permits as they came in.

Commissioner Van Hoff requested clarification on why there was parking in the front of the
buildings.

Ms. Jastremsky indicated the Town Center Zoning required parking to be either behind or to the
side of a building. She clarified that the project was going to be done in phases and that once all
the phases were complete the project would meet that required parking standard.

Drew Parcell, Applicant, explained they believed they needed more parking. He added that they
did not purchase the future building site since there was an elderly woman who was still living on
the property that did not want to sell the property at this time but they have a contract with the
owner to acquire the property in the future.

6.     Public Hearing: Beehive Academy Site Plan and Plat Amendment Requests
       (Administrative Action)

A request by Pace CM representing the Rasmussen Family Limited Partnership for approval of a
site plan for a new charter school and approval of a plat amendment to create two lots from one
existing lot. The site is located at approximately 12197 S. 300 East. Application SPR-1005-2020
and SUBD-1017-2020. Staff Contact: Maryann Pickering at (801) 576-6391 or
maryann.pickering@draper.ut.us

Maryann Pickering, City Planner, identified the location of the site and indicated it was currently
zoned A5 and had a land use of neighborhood commercial. She noted that at their last meeting
they forwarded a negative recommendation to Council for the zone change. She reported that City
Council voted unanimously at their meeting to deny the zone change and explained they would
only be reviewing the site plan application at this time as the plat amendment could not be
processed with the current A5 zoning.

Ms. Pickering continued her presentation and noted that charter schools were primarily governed
by State Law. She shared that Staff reviewed the site plan for compliance with several standards
but noted some standards could not be required per State Law. She reviewed the site plan request
which included a 44,032 square foot building, parking lot, and new road from Day Dairy to the

300 East roundabout. She explained that the area south of the proposed road is shown as a future
development.

Ms. Pickering remarked that the proposed parking required a deviation. She clarified that the
applicant planned for 311 spaces which meets Draper City Municipal Code, but 113 of the stalls
were to be set aside for drop off and pick up stacking and queuing making it no longer in
compliance. She shared that the applicant provided a parking study that indicated 198 parking
stalls were adequate for their use.

Ms. Pickering indicated that Staff had reviewed the Traffic Impact Study provided by the applicant
and indicated they had found some deficiencies and noted that the applicant, UDOT, and the City
would work together to address some of them. She noted that emergency access may be affected
by the proposed site layout and that the emergency fire lane and access around the site could
become blocked during drop off and pick up. She presented site photos as well as site elevation
renderings.

Commissioner Squire inquired if the 300 East exit would mainly be used during pickup and not
drop off.

Ms. Pickering replied that was correct.

Commissioner Squire asked if the driveway into the post office lined up with the 300 East exit.

Brien Maxfield, City Engineer, replied they did not align. He clarified that the post office access
was located north of the north parcel line.

Chairman Adams inquired were the stacking and queuing stalls were.

Ms. Pickering responded they were located along the last two rows of parking on the east side of
the parking lot

Commissioner Squire stated that it looked like drop off and pick up would be happening in the fire
lane.

Don Buckley, City Fire Marshal, replied that was his understanding as well. He added that with
the size of the building they were required to have a 26-foot-wide fire lane that could not be
obstructed in any manner at any time. He suggested that the traffic circulation be reconfigured to
avoid blocking the fire lane.

Tyler Bodrero, Pace CM Representative, explained they anticipated the 300 East egress to be used
mainly at pickup since the drop off times were more spread out. He reiterated their proposed car
queuing and circulation plan and noted the flow of traffic was meant to encourage the use of the
new roadway and lessen the impact to 300 East. He explained that there were two lanes nearest
the school one for pick up closest to the curb and another adjacent lane for bypassing. He indicated
that he believed it was determined that the bypass lane as well as the parking lot travel lane met
the standard for the fire lane.

Chairman Adams requested further details on the parking stalls being used for car queuing since
it will be taking away a portion of the usable parking stalls. He noted that parking is always an
issue.

Mr. Bodrero stated the two furthest east lanes of parking would be dual striped to allow them to
use those spaces to create better traffic flow on the site. He explained that the five-lane stacking
created with the two rows of parking would be used for parking for events such as plays, parent
teacher conferences, and more. He indicated that he believed they were meeting the State
standards as well as City requirements.

Chairman Adams inquired how many high schoolers they would allow to drive to school.

Mr. Bodrero responded that they currently had 64 driving age students. He noted that if parking
needed to be limited, they could investigate issuing parking passes.

Chairman Adams expressed that he liked the five lanes of inflow for traffic but was still concerned
that they were asking for such a large parking deviation.

Commissioner Van Hoff asked how many staff members there would be at the school.

Mr. Bodrero replied there were 65 staff members at max capacity at this location. He noted there
was typically more elementary students in charter schools than junior high or high school students.

Chairman Adams inquired why they could not use the soccer field as parking if there were no
sports at the school.

Mr. Bodrero explained it was a play field. He remarked that if they were to add 113 stalls in that
location, they would exceed the parking minimum that was required by the State.

Scott Sabey, Applicant’s Representative, stated that they believed the current design was
satisfactory to the Fire Department and requested time to be able to adjust the design to meet the
Fire Department’s needs.

Chairman Adams asked if they could continue the item without public comment at this time.

Mike Barker, City Attorney, requested time to look at the rules before he advised them on how to
move forward.
