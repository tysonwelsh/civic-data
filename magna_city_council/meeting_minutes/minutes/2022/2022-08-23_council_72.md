Water Conservation and Landscaping Restrictions

               Council Member Hull stated a few individuals that live in a Homeowners
Association (HOA) have reached out to her regarding letters they are receiving from their HOA
about the condition of their lawns. Residents are trying to follow Magna Water District’s watering
guidelines; however, the HOA has a problem with their lawns turning yellow. Is there anything the
Council can do to help with the situation?

                 Clint Dilley, General Manager, Magna Water District, stated Utah is in a drought
and Magna Water District has had to move the level of conservation from voluntary to mandatory.
Last year there was a 20 percent reduction in water usage and this year has been equally as
good. Magna Water District has issued a level 2 mandatory water conservation plan that includes
watering only 3 days a week between the hours of 6:00 pm – 10:00 am. HOA’s have their own
criteria and it does not always align with Magna Water Districts guidelines. Water districts cannot
supersede HOA guidelines. The issue was proposed during the legislative session but was
ultimately shut down. Magna Water District is willing to work with the homeowners and the HOA
to help create reasonable expectations.

Magna Dog Park

                 Council Member Hull stated a dog park has been in the works in Magna. During
a cleanup day in the spring, a Salt Lake County Parks and Recreation (Parks and Recreation)
employee stated that it may be interested in dog parks. As a service project Dominion Energy
cleaned up an area, supplied benches, dog bags, and other things. She obtained a grant for
$4,000 for fencing; however, it will not be enough to fence the entire area for a dog park. The park
in its current state is not suitable for small dogs and the ground needs to be remediated. If Parks
and Recreation is interested in developing dog parks, would it be able to help Magna?

Recreation, stated Parks and Recreation does not currently have dog parks but is interested in

Resolution No. 22-08-02 – Appointment of an Emergency Manager
Resolution No. 22-08-03 – Magna Metro Township Emergency Communications Plan

                Paul Ashton stated the Legislature adopted House Bill 96 in 2021 that requires
each municipality to designate an emergency manager. The designated individual will need to
provide its information to Unified Fire Authority (UFA). The emergency manager will be the point
of contact for Magna Metro Township. The resolution presented would designate Council Member
Hull as its emergency manager.

                Council Member Pierce stated she has issues with both the appointment of an
emergency manager and the Emergency Communications Plan. Magna Metro Township pays
Salt Lake County Emergency Management, specifically James Woodward, Emergency Municipal
Planner, UFA, to represent Magna as an emergency manager. If the Council is designating
someone, the letter should say Salt Lake County Emergency Management is the designee with
the Mayor or Mayor’s designee as the interim successor and would be part of the group directing
what the wants and needs are in Magna during an emergency. Once someone takes on the title
of emergency manager, more than what the letter states, that person is responsible for creating
the emergency plan for the area, coordinating with officials to ensure an effective response,
providing an interim successor, and working with those people as part of the interim succession
act. Mr. Woodward made the comment that he lives too far away to be able to respond to an
emergency in Magna and therefore, cannot be the emergency manager. Magna Metro Township
pays Salt Lake County Emergency Management for this service, someone from that office needs
to take that role. She does not like that designations are given without the details of what that
person is responsible for. An emergency manager is certified to a certain extent and has a lot of
training.

In addition, the Emergency Communications Plan does not designate what the Council will do in
the event of an emergency or what tools and resources are available for communication. There
have been conversations with Mr. Woodward about what the needs are in Magna and what type
of access it has to a radio during an event. Everyone has cell phones, but during an event, cell
service could be down or intermittent, should the Webster Center have satellite phones as a
backup. The plan just says a designee from the Council will call the other Council Members.

              Council Member Hull stated during the 2020 earthquake, the Council could not
contact each other because cell service was down.

                Council Member Pierce stated she would like to table the appointment of an
emergency manager and the approval of the Emergency Communications Plan until further
clarification can be made on what is needed and what Magna Metro Township is paying for.

             Council Member Prokopis stated he was under the impression that Council
Member Hull would be the emergency manager for the Emergency Operation Center (EOC) for
Magna. The EOC needs to know who to contact in Magna in the event of an emergency.

            Council Member Pierce stated House Bill 96 states specifically what the
emergency manager is responsible for.
