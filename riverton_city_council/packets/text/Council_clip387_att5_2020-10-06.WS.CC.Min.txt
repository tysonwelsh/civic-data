36      a. Proposed Ordinance regarding Freedom to Assemble
37
38   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, proposed a new ordinance regarding public gatherings related to
39   protesting. He said the ordinance includes the purposes, definitions, time, place and manner of
40   free speech activities, expected appeals, applications, First Amendment guidelines and targeted
41   residential demonstrations/ picketing.
42
43   Mr. Carter stated that the government allows cities to regulate the “Time, manner and place” of
44   these types of gatherings, allowing residents to maintain their first amendment rights within the
45   bounds of public safety concerns, and without property damage or other negative impacts.
46
47   Mayor Staggs clarified that the proposed ordinance would be presented at a future date to the
48   Council for approval, and that it was not a land use item and would not be presented to the
49   Planning Commission. Mayor also requested that the term “peaceable” be injected in the
50   ordinance.

22       b. Discussion of Maximum Allowed Square Footage for Accessory Structures
23
24   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director explained that Council had recently had
25   discussions regarding the method of measuring for allowing accessory structure permits. In this
26   proposal Mr. Lethbridge explained that the current method of measurement used to determine
27   the size of these structures was based on the percentage of the rear yard size (approximately
28   25%). The proposed measurement method changes would be to calculate a percentage of the
29   total lot size. He said that other cities use between 8 and 12% as their standard. He said that
30   Staff recommended adjusting the measurement allowed for accessory structures to be
31   calculated at 10% of the lot size.
32
33   A brief discussion took place and Councilmembers felt 10% was a good number to present to
34   the Planning Commission.

36       c. Ranked Choice Voting
37
38   Councilmembers Tawnee McCay and Tish Buroker introduced Kory Holdaway, Utah Rank
39   Choice (RCV) Voting. Mr. Holdaway explained RCV is a voting program cities can opt in if they
40   choose. Since 2018 two Utah cities have opted to use RCV, Vineyard and Payson.
41
42   Mr. Holdaway stated that he feels that RCV was gaining favor and was successful in the Utah
43   County cities that used it in the 2019 Election. He said the key benefit is cost savings because
44   the need for a primary election can be eliminated. He explained other cost savings such as the
45   elimination of two campaign seasons, which he said benefits the candidates and they
46   appreciate the savings as well as the citizens. Additionally he stated that the type of debates
47   that occur in a RCV election are more collegiate. There was an example used of two opposing
48   candidates for the same position in an RCV run election who stated, “Hey if I am not your
49   number 1 choice let me be your number 2 choice.” He said another benefit of RCV was the
50   ability to get to a majority.

37       a. Resolution No. 20-62 - Approving a Riverton Fire Service Area to Fund First
38          Responder Services Within Riverton City
39
40   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, and Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, explained that
41   Riverton City has completed a lengthy process to create the Riverton Fire Service Area. The
42   City Council adopted Resolution No. 20-31 that formally commenced the process to begin the
43   creation of the Riverton Fire Service Area, or “RFSA” on May 5, 2020.
44
45   Mr. Carter further explained that the City Council held a public hearing on June 16, 2020 which
46   allowed the public to ask questions and obtain further information regarding the issues
47   contained in resolution No.17-42, pursuant to See: Utah Code Ann. §17B-1-210. This hearing
48   also commenced the time for persons to file protests against creation of the district if they so
49   desired. The 60-day deadline to file protests (following the August 15 public hearing) lapsed on
50   August 15, 2020. Riverton City received no protests from the public by this date, nor has the
51   City received any protests afterward.
     Page | 6 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   Mr. Carter said that in order to perfect creation of the Riverton Fire Service Area, one of the
 2   remaining steps was to file an appropriate local entity plat with the State of Utah, Office of the
 3   Lieutenant Governor, which was ordinarily required to occur within ten days of filing a resolution
 4   approving creation of the district. However, said districts, once approved commonly run into
 5   problems with filing said plat within the ten day deadline. Mr. Carter said that Staff has done
 6   what it can to the date of this memorandum to assure a smooth filing process but we don’t yet
 7   have the verification we need from Salt Lake County to be fully assured. He said that, in a
 8   “worst case scenario,” an entity which cannot file a plat within the 10 day filing period simply
 9   needs to adopt another approval resolution to reset the filing period, and the appropriate plat
10   would be accepted by the Lt. Governor’s Office at a later date.
11
12   Councilmembers made a few technical and grammatical changes. Mr. Carter said he would
13   make the changes and prepare the final draft for the next City Council Meeting.
