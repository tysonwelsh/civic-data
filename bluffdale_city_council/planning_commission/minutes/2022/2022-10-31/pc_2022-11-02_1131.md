4.       Planning Commissioners HB-409 Training - Staff Presenter. Grant Crowelk Zoning
         Administrator.


City Planner/Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, provided the training. He first
addressed the parking, which he stated can be a very complex issue. The consensus ofthe Planning
Commission was for Mr. Crowell to address residential and suburban parking. Mr. Crowell explained
that any time he goes to a professional conference,the issue ofparking is invariably discussed because
it is a thoroughly researched topic.

Parking requirements begin with categorizing the proposed project since the category of the project
will dictate the parking requirements. For example,for a church, the requirement is one stall per 100

square feet of the building. If it is a hospital, it must be one space per 200 square feet. A medical
office would be different. Engineers are not parking analysts, so more detailed information needs to
be provided to Staff so that they can determine what the required parking will be. Sometimes, a
multiple-use development creates unknowns because only time will tell how the space will be used
and how that use will impact the parking requirements. The last building in a multiple-building
business park might pose challenges to parking when an owner applies for a Business License.

Commissioner Swanson brought up discussions that took place with The Centrum. It was noted that
the City's parking requirements were quite high but they were willing to meet that requirement. Mr.
Crowell explained that since there is still no public transit service in Bluffdale, there are impacts on
parking. In homes where there are garages, families are typically large, so the garage becomes a
storage area rather than a parking area. Thus, residential parking spills onto the street, thereby
creating the need to require more parking spaces.

In areas where there are apartment complexes,developers tend to have longitudinal data that indicates
that the parking requirement should be 1.7 to 1.8 parking stalls per apartment. The City's minimum
is two parking stalls per apartment despite what developers suggest. The neighboring residents
believe that the requirement should be even higher to mitigate parking issues in their neighborhood.
Mr. Crowell acknowledged that the best efforts by the City will still not eliminate the challenges that
will occur on Rising Star Way.

Mr. Crowell reviewed the history of Independence, which dates back to before 2010. A new version
was developed in 2012 and even more, changes took place in 2014 that led to the creation of The
Market Place and Beacon Hills. The requirement was two spaces per residence but there was not
much discussion about guest parking. The problems associated with guest parking resulted in
research in 2016 by Senior City Planner, Jennifer Robison that focused on parking at Independence
at the Point. The discussions included the needed size ofdriveways so that sidewalks are not blocked.
Also, the number of bedrooms in a residence was factored into the parking requirement. The City
Council also realized that alleys are not a particularly favored option as part of the housing product
mix.


Some parts of Bluffdale have a very complex mixture of residential types. Some of the initial
assumptions that were made proved to be inaccurate because people will do what they want to,
regardless of what the planners anticipated. Mr. Crowell explained that some of the later
developments, such as Cobalt Village, have generated fewer complaints about parking because ofthe
enhanced parking requirements that were adopted. Different cities take different approaches than
Bluffdale does.


Currently,for single-family dwellings, Bluffdale requires two parking spaces per dwelling unit. That
includes the requirement for a two-car garage with a 20-foot driveway. Shared driveways are not
common but the guest parking for such residences is one guest parking stall per three dwelling units.
The public street cannot be counted toward the parking requirement as was allowed previously. For
multi-family dwelling units, the parking requirement is two per dwelling unit. If it is a townhome
with a 20-foot driveway, the guest parking requirement is one space per four dwelling units. Multi-
family townhomes without a driveway require one guest parking space per three units and two spaces

in a garage. For apartment buildings, the parking requirement is two spaces for one or two-bedroom
units and 2.5 spaces for three or more bedrooms. He reported that some communities have eliminated
all parking requirements because the market should decide. The reason for that lack of requirement
was unknown. One complaint that is sometimes expressed is the fact that someone has parked on a
public street in front ofa person's house that is ostensibly "reserved" for the homeowner. The bottom
line is that there will always be parking complaints. The more parking that is provided, the more
parking demand there will be, and someone will have a parking problem and become disgruntled.

Commissioner Loumis could foresee something happening at Porter Rockwell Boulevard and 1-15
when the prison property develops. He urged the City to keep an eye on that area to see how it is
developed and what kinds of provisions are made for pedestrian crossings and parking. Mr. Crowell
stated that the Executive Director for The Point will be present at the City Council's Work Session
on November 9 to provide an update. The overarching concept is that it will be a "15-minute city,"
which means that there will be so many things available in that small area that a person could get to
anything within a 15-minute walk. Mr. Crowell liked the concept but was curious to see how
economically feasible the concept will be and how it will negatively impact Bluffdale. Bluffdale does
not have a duration ordinance for parking, except for winter parking when there is a snowstorm. In
instances where a person is using the street as a long-term parking site, evidence could be gathered to
determine ifthe parking is associated with running a business,ifthe individual has a Business License
to run that business, and if the parking falls within the terms of the license. Mr. Crowell explained
that unattached trailers and unlicensed vehicles pose a different set of challenges that could be
addressed.


If there are situations that present a safety hazard,the City Engineer should be consulted to determine
whether there is empirical data that would warrant corrective action, including red-curbing a portion
of a street. It is important to make decisions based on empirical data and not on ad hoc anecdotal
information.

Mr. Crowell briefly reviewed the types of roads the City has classified, such as:
