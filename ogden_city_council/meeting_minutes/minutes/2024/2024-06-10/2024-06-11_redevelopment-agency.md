Proposed Resolution 2024-2 adopting the budget for the Redevelopment
Agency for Fiscal Year 2024-2025
         A memo from the Management Services Department regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 budget came
before the Board for consideration. The memo stated on May 14, 2024, Administration presented the FY2024-2025 Agency
budget. The Board accepted the budget for review and set a public hearing for June 11, 2024, for final consideration.
         Board Administrator Eller-Smith summarized the memo and stated the proposed FY2024-2025 RDA budget totals
$20,280,075. The is an increase of $4,511,075 or 28.61 percent over the FY2023-2024 budget. The RDA is funded primarily
through tax increment received from the various redevelopment districts. Contributions from Business Depot Ogden (BDO) lease
revenue are also included in the Agency’s revenue. A contribution from the Agency to the General Fund covers services provided
to the RDA by city administrative staff. Ms. Eller-Smith used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to review the history of the
RDA, summarize the number of redevelopment areas included in the budget, discuss project area funding sources, and provide a
year-over-year budget comparison. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2024-2, entitled:
                   “A resolution of the Board of Directors of the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency adopting the annual budget
                   for Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 as required by Section 17C-1-601.5, Utah Code Annotated, 1953.”
         Chair Richey then opened the public hearing.
         Heath Satow offered some push back on the concept of tax increment financing (TIF); there has not been any private
sector competition for some of the projects that have been undertaken by the RDA so there has not been competition for TIF. The
WonderBlock project was given to a single developer with no competition, and that developer received all the TIF financing from
the Board for vital elements of their project. The problem is not with the fact that the RDA uses TIF, but what it is used for and
not allowing competition for the funding. The City does not really know what a developer would be willing to contribute to a
project when there is no competition for the RDA’s business and TIF. He suggested improved regulations for granting TIF for
participation in an RDA project.
          There being no further persons appearing to be heard, BOARD MEMBER MYERS MOVED THE PUBLIC
HEARING BE CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY BOARD MEMBER HYER, ALL VOTING AYE.
          Board member Choberka agreed with Mr. Satow’s comments about the benefit of inviting competition for participation
in an RDA project. Board member Hyer stated he also agrees there is a benefit to allowing competition for TIF financing;
however, some developers will not be willing to invest too much into a project without assurance that they will be the selected
developer and will receive TIF for their participation on a project.
          Executive Director Nadolski stated he believes there are opportunities to increase competition for TIF funds, as well as
utilizing a third-party to evaluate the appropriate amount of TIF for any given RDA project in the community and the viability of
developers and/or their proposals.
          ON A MOTION BY BOARD MEMBER HYER AND SECONDED BY BOARD MEMBER GRAF,
RESOLUTION 2024-2 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – BOARD
