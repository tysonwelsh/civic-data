Kevin Jones at 1:30 p.m. on May 9, 2023, in the Washington County Commission Chambers and via Zoom,

3. FINAL PLAT (Public Meeting - Administrative). The applicant is proposing a two-lot
subdivision. The location of the property is approximately 221 S 300 W Veyo, Utah 84782. The
applicant is Brett Emett.

Mr. Messel explained the location of the proposed Emett Subdivision located in Veyo. The County
Commission will review this application on the consent calendar as signatures are required from
the County Commission on the Final Plat.

Recommendation:

The Planning Commission reviewed the Final Plat request in the May 9, 2023, Planning
Commission Meeting. The Planning Commission approved the Final Plat subject to the pending
the decision on the road improvement waiver from the County Commission. The road
improvements waiver will either need to be granted or the applicant will be required to complete
road improvements along the subdivision frontage. The road improvement would require
paving with asphalt to a width of 28’ with ditches on both sides of the roadway. Total roadway
width is 50’ as described by Public Works standards. The final plat for the Emmet Subdivision
was approved based on the following findings and conditions:

                             Community Development


    1. The applicants have adhered to the application process requirements and paid applicable
       fees.
    2. The proposed Final Plat meets zoning requirements for RA-1 (Residential Agricultural
       1-acre minimum lot size).
    3. Each parcel has been verified to meet the zoning requirements for RA-1 (Residential
       Agricultural 1-acre minimum lot size).
    4. Each parcel has water, power, septic approval, and fire service available.
    5. The final plat was drawn and wet stamped by a Professional Land Surveyor.

    6. The applicant is seeking a waiver of road improvements by the County Commission. If
        the road improvements are not waived, the applicant will be responsible for complying
        with county road improvement standards.
    7. The applicant has provided written approval from the NWSSD to ensure fire apparatus
        have adequate space for access and turn around on 300 W, which is a private roadway.
    8. The final plat is consistent with the approved preliminary plat.
    9. The public utility and access road need to be labeled on the plat.
    10. A hammer head will be required for emergency vehicle turn-around in accordance with
        county and fire district standards.
    11. County Commission will need to approve any reduction in the road improvement

Recommendation:

Staff has reviewed the Amended Project Plan, Zone Change Amendment, Lot Line Adjustment,
and Hillside Development application and recommends that the Planning Commission send a
positive recommendation to the County Commission to approve the Amended Project Plan,
Zone Change Amendment, Lot Line Adjustment, and Hillside Grading Development Permit,

        required.
    8. A rockfall shadow angle report will be provided showing the data collected by AGEC.
    9. A Hillside Grading Development Permit with all conditions of development that attach
        to the Project Plan will be given by the county upon compliance with county
        requirements.
    10. A cash bond or irrevocable letter of credit as per Washington County Code 10-24-7 will
        be required to assure hillside restoration.
    11. Applicant will provide an indemnification agreement with language acceptable to the
        county and including indemnification for any hazards like rockfall or landslide.

       5. Landscape and Water Conservation Ordinance (Public Hearing – Legislative). Review
       proposed revisions to Title 10 zoning regulations for landscape and water conservation standards
       and ordinances.

       Mr. Messel spoke of the need to conserve water and do what we can to reduce water consumption
       for residents in the county.

                              Community Development



Ms. Hales stated the Washington County Water Conservancy District has set forth guidelines for
waterwise landscaping, which county residents are not able to participate in at this time. Adopting
the ordinance would allow residents in their service areas to participate in those programs.

Commissioner Owens voiced his concern about the County’s ability to enforce the Landscape and
Water Conservation Ordinance.

Chairman Jones shared his concern with the level of specificity for fixtures and specific language
which would be difficult to enforce.

Ms. Hales stated the ordinance will apply to new construction.

Mr. Messel stated inspections will be done on Commercial Developments.

Commissioner McMullin asked how we can enforce landscape requirements when the Certificate
of Occupancy is typically issued before the yard is completed.

Ms. Hales stated new developments and subdivisions will need to comply with requirements as it
relates to square footage of grass areas. This is easy to do for new development, but not existing
developments.

Chairman Jones opened the Public Hearing.

Mr. Gardner voiced his concern with many areas of the county not being part of the Washington
County Water Conservancy District. He wondered why we would require residents to comply
with restrictions the residents cannot benefit from in terms of the rebate.

Ms. Hales stated the regulation would only apply to one acre around the home even if the lot is a
large five acre lot, for instance.

Chairman Jones closed the Public Hearing.

Ms. Hales stated the guidelines apply to all areas of the county. However, the rebate will not be
available to all residents of the county unless an ordinance is in place.

Mr. Owens asked if approving the ordinance changes should be tabled until we can understand
what areas of the county will benefit and which will not.
