BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY

This application is a request for approval of Deviations from building and site design
standards for a future medical office building to be built on the approximately 35.87 acre
Lone Peak Hospital campus located at 11925 S. State Street (Exhibit B & C). The property is
currently zoned CR. The applicant is specifically requesting deviations from facade
articulation, parapet and roofline variations, and the minimum percentage of primary
materials, and placement of the main building entrance.

The Lone Peak Hospital campus currently contains several buildings built in separate phases
with open undeveloped areas surrounding the hospital buildings on the northwest, south,
and southeast. The future building to which this deviation request would apply is referred to
in the submittal documentation as Medical Office Building 4 and would be located on the



Lone Peak Hospital                                                App. No. 2024-0175-VAR

southwest corner of the overall site. The applicant has submitted a deviation request letter
(Exhibit F) and desires to obtain approval for deviations from building and site design
requirements prior to submitting a complete application for the Site Plan Amendment. A file
has been opened for receipt of this Site Plan Amendment (Application No. 2024-0225-SP)
however a complete application has not yet been submitted as of the writing of this report
and therefore this application is not able to be reviewed in light of a complete or reviewed
development proposal for the property.

Criteria for Approval.

DCMC Section 9-22-030 states that the Planning Commission may approve deviations from
the design standards of DCMC Sections 9-22-040 and 9-22-050, with the exception of the
height and roofline design standards of DCMC Section 9-22-040(D), based on meeting at least
three of the following criteria:

    1. The proposed development includes additional amenities or pedestrian and bike
       connectivity in excess of what is otherwise required.
    2. The overall landscape and open space area provided within the proposed development
       exceeds required amounts by more than ten percent (10%).
    3. The proposed development is a transit oriented development (TOD) and provides direct
       connectivity for active transportation methods to a transportation facility.
    4. The traffic impacts of the proposed development will be mitigated through creative design,
       traffic calming measures, companywide carpool or transit pass programs, or other similar
       measures.
    5. The proposed development is designed to meet LEED certification standards.
    6. The proposed development involves redeveloped buildings, additions to existing buildings,
       minor structures being added to a site, or is a new building within a previously approved
       phased development and the deviation is necessary to create a consistency with other
       existing buildings.
    7. Vegetative landscaping (excluding lawn, turf, and groundcovers) exceeds the amounts
       required in this Title by at least ten percent (10%).
    8. The building is designed by a licensed architect in a specific architectural style, that utilizes
       facade materials differently than prescribed in subsection 9-22-040(F) of this chapter.
    9. The amount of primary materials used for each building facade exceeds the required
       amount by more than ten percent (10%).

The applicant has indicated in their deviation request letter how they believe that they
meet at least four (4) of these criteria. The letter specifically identifies Criteria 1, 2, 6, and 8
from the list above.

Staff has reviewed the deviation analysis provided by the applicant and provides the
following analysis of the claims presented:

     •   Staff agrees that the amenities proposed are in excess of what is required for the
         scenario proposed at this time to meet Criterion 1, as shown in the Amenity Plan
         (Exhibit I). Because the conceptual plans provided with the deviation request do not
         constitute a complete or official site plan amendment proposal, staff recommends



Lone Peak Hospital                                                        App. No. 2024-0175-VAR
Deviation                                          5
         that the if the deviations are approved, the applicant be required as a condition of
         approval to provide the same percentage of additional amenities as indicated in the
         conceptual Amenity Plans (25%) for the amended site plan application. The only
         application submitted and under consideration at this time is this deviation request.

     •   Staff generally agrees with the applicant’s assertions that Criterion 2 is met. The
         existing site landscaping, and that shown in the conceptual plan, as shown in the
         Landscaping Calculations (Exhibit J) would continue to exceed the minimum required
         landscaped area by more than ten percent (10%). Although, a future amended site
         plan proposal could change in respect to the specific amount or location of
         landscaped area, staff recommends that if deviations are approved, that as a
         condition of approval, that the overall landscape and open space area provided
         within the development continue to exceed the minimum required amounts by more
         than ten percent (10%).

     •   Regarding Criterion 6, the Planning Commission will need to determine whether the
         submitted conceptual elevation drawings (Exhibit H) represent a design that creates
         consistency with the other existing buildings. Staff concurs with the assertion that
         the new building is part of a multi-phased site that includes other existing buildings.
         If deviations are approved, staff recommends that the applicant be required to
         submit elevations in compliance with all ordinance requirement, excepting only the
         approved deviations that are substantially consistent with the design theme
         presented in the conceptual plans in Exhibit H, as a condition of approval.

     •   Regarding Criterion 8, the new building will be required to be designed by licensed
         architects, and the architect that provided the analysis of the conceptual drawings in
         the deviation request letter has indicated that the building is intended to be designed
         in a modern architectural style. No specifics were provided, however, as to how the
         conceptual design utilizes facade materials differently than prescribed in the City’s
         requirements. The architect specifically noted the unique articulation of the glazing
         using rhythmically spaced aluminum mullions, as a defining characteristic of the
         style, however as doors, glass and roofing materials are not included in the facade
         material calculations per DCMC Section 9-22-040(F)(3), this response does not
         answer how the intended architectural style will use facade materials differently.
         Staff does not believe that this criterion has been fully met, however the Planning
         Commission will ultimately need to determine if this criterion has been met.

Dear Mr. Draper,

This letter and the attached exhibits constitute a request for deviation from the requirements prescribed in
the Draper City Design Standards and Guidelines for the proposed Lone Peak Hospital MOB #4. The 4-
story, 125,000 square foot medical office building will be built on the Lone Peak Hospital campus. The
building will house out-patient medical tenant spaces including an ambulatory surgery center, imaging,
and various clinics. The construction type of the structure is intended to be Type IIB.

The project is seeking the following deviations:


        1.      A deviation from strict compliance to section 9-22-040 F.2 “Primary Materials”, which
                states that, “At least seventy five (75%) of each building’s finished face shall be in
                primary materials.”
        2.      A deviation from strict compliance to section 9-22-040 D.1 “Parapet Variations”, which
                states that, “All buildings shall include varied rooflines in order to add architectural
                interest and avoid the appearance or sense of monotonous roofline expanses by 1.
                Providing roofline and parapet variations where there are long, continuous, and
                undisturbed rooflines fifty feet (50’) or longer.”
        3.      A deviation from strict compliance to section 9-22-040 C.2 “Façade Articulation”, which
                states that, “Buildings with facades one hundred (100’) or greater in total length shall
                have at least one significant façade variation from the primary wall plane whose depth is
                at least five feet (5’) and whose width is at least twenty feet (20’).”
        4.      A deviation from strict compliance to section 9-22-050 A.1 “Building Placement”, which
                states that the main building entrance shall face the principal street to which the building
                has frontage.

Per section 9-22-030, the project will meet the Deviation Criteria listed below. Additionally, the attached
exhibits provide detail description of the project design intent and adherence to the proposed criteria.

        1.      9-22-030 B.1; “The proposed development includes additional amenities or pedestrian
                and bike connectivity in excess of what is otherwise required.”
                The project will include additional outdoor amenity areas that will service the building as
                well as the hospital campus. The current requirement for the development is seven (7)
                amenity areas. The project is proposing nine (9) total areas. See Exhibit A.
        2.      9-22-030 B.2; “The overall landscape and open space area provided within the proposed
                development exceeds required amounts by more than ten percent (10%).”
                The project will include generous landscape areas adjacent to State Street and directly
                adjacent to the building with emphasis on the most prominent public facing portion of the
                site. See Exhibit B.
        3.      9-22-030 B.6; “The proposed development involves redeveloped buildings, additions to
                existing buildings, minor structures being added to a site, or is a new building within a
                previously approved phased development and the deviation is necessary to create
                consistency with other existing buildings.”
                The proposed design for the medical office building is intended to be a complimentary
                addition to the architectural vocabulary of the campus. While each building on the
                campus has a nuanced architectural character, the predominate style is defined by a
                simple material palette of EIFS and glass with select accents of masonry tile and metal
                panel. See Exhibit C.
        4.      9-22-030 B.8; “The building is designed by a licensed architect in a specific architectural
                style, that utilizes façade materials differently than prescribed in subsection 9-22-040(F).”
                This building will be designed by licensed architects from Devenney Group Architects.
                Similar to criteria 9-22-030 B.6, the intent of the proposed building design is to
                complement the existing architectural campus vocabulary. The existing buildings are
                designed in a modern style that deviates subsection 9-22-040(F). Simple massing and
                unique articulation of glazing, using rhythmic spacing of aluminum mullions are defining
                characteristics of the existing architectural style. See Exhibit C.



We believe that this project will be an incredible asset to the surrounding community. In addition to
providing additional access to critical life-saving healthcare services, the building will be a welcomed
addition to the Lone Peak Hospital Campus by enhancing the aesthetic character of the property and
improving the overall patient experience. Please do not hesitate to reach out with any questions or
concerns.
