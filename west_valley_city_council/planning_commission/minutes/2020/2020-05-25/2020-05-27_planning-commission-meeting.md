ZT-3-2020
West Valley City
A zone text change to amend the residential building design standards in the CC (City
Center) zone


On March 10, 2020 the City Council adopted Ordinance # 20-13 that included several revisions
to the City’s multi-unit residential design standards found in Chapter 7-11 of the Zoning
Ordinance. The City Center zone also includes design standards for residential buildings that are
very similar to the multi-unit residential design standards. The purpose of this proposed zone text
change is to align the standards for residential buildings in the City Center zone with the recently
updated design standards for multiple unit dwelling residential in Chapter 7-11.

GPZ-4-2020
Rockworth Companies, LLC
3036 and 3078 S Decker Lake Dr.
General plan change from entertainment to very high density residential and zone change
from M with the Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone to RM without the Decker Lake
Station Overlay Zone
2.79 acres

Adam Davis with Rockworth Companies, LLC has submitted a General Plan/zone change
application for two parcels at 3036 and 3078 S Decker Lake Dr. totaling 2.79 acres. The property
is currently zoned M (manufacturing) with the Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone. The proposed
zone is RM (multiple unit dwelling residential) without the Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone.
The current General Plan designation is entertainment and the proposed General Plan designation
is very high density residential (over 20 units/acre).

The current General Plan designation of entertainment is unique to this area. The Plan states the
following regarding the entertainment designation: “This section of the City already contains
several entertainment-focused venues including the Maverik Center and the Hale Center Theatre.
This district will encourage more entertainment and other complementary uses such as hotels and
restaurants within or nearby to build upon existing vitality. Broad goals in this area include the
encouragement of a more pedestrian-friendly environment and increasing the rich variety of
services and opportunities to attract more visitors to this vibrant and fun area.”

Surrounding zones include M to the north and west, RM to the east and C-2 (general
commercial) to the south. Surrounding land uses include Rocky Mountain Power transmission
lines to the north, My Place Hotel and vacant property to the west, the West Pointe Apartments
to the east and the Maverik Center to the south.

The applicant is proposing a total of 219 apartments within a 7-story building. The proposed
density of the project is 78.5 units/acre, which is similar to the two apartment developments at
Fairbourne Station. The attached concept plan, application materials and development agreement
proposal from the applicant provide details on and support for the proposed project.

In reviewing this application, staff suggests that there are two primary questions to answer:

   1. Is this the right location for an apartment development?

   2. Assuming the location is appropriate, does the quality of the project further City goals?

These two questions are explored below.


Is this the right location for an apartment development?

When considering this question, there are many aspects to consider including recent code
changes, transportation infrastructure, neighboring land uses and balancing City objectives
across the City. Each of these topics is addressed below.

Recent Code Changes

One consideration is the City Council recently amended the criteria to determine whether a
property would be eligible to apply for the RM zone. Prior to this change, the subject property
was not eligible to be rezoned to the RM zone. The recent code change made properties located
on within ½ mile of the Decker Lake light rail station eligible to be rezoned to RM. The subject
property is adjacent to the Decker Lake stations. While the property is eligible for RM zoning,
the City may still deny requests for RM zoning.

Transportation Infrastructure

One of the recent code changes made requires a traffic impact study prepared by a registered
traffic engineer for projects with 100 or more dwelling units that are requesting RM zoning. The
required traffic impact study is included with this report. In summary, all study intersections are
currently operating at an acceptable level of service (LOS) under existing conditions and are
anticipated to continue to operate at an acceptable LOS in 2025 with the proposed project.
During the Planning Commission study session, Commission Winters asked if the traffic impact
study for this project included the proposed units in the application from DAI (GPZ-3-2020) to
the north. Staff confirmed with the traffic engineer that the traffic impact study for this project
does include the units from both projects as this project came in after the DAI proposal.

Of all apartment developments within the City, this development would be the closest to a light
rail station with the station directly adjacent on Decker Lake Dr.

Neighboring Land Use

In staff’s view, the proposed apartments are compatible with the neighboring hotels to the west
as well as the light rail station and apartments to the east. The 75’ height of the building is
comparable to the Maverik Center to the south that ranges from 75’ to 85’ in height. The height
of the My Place Hotel is just over 51’ to the roof ridgeline. The power corridor property to the
north is underutilized and would benefit from further improvements like parking and
landscaping.

Another land use consideration for the area is the number of existing or proposed apartments.
The E Gate Apartments just north of Rocky Mountain Power’s property include 304 units and
the West Pointe Apartments to the east include 104 units. The City also received a General
Plan/zone change application (GPZ-3-2020) for the property at 2892 South Decker Lake Dr. on
the northwest corner of 3100 S Decker Lake Dr. where 320 apartment units are proposed. If the
two proposed apartment developments are constructed there will be 947 units within ¼ mile of
each other.

Balancing City Objectives Across the City

When reviewing a General Plan/zone change for apartments in a specific area of the City, the
Commission should consider not only site-specific issues but citywide goals as well. Given the
regular inquiries staff receives regarding apartment development, it seems clear that apartments,
if allowed, would be built just about anywhere in the City. However, doing so would preclude
other important objectives from being accomplished and would put our housing stock out of
balance. Therefore, deciding where to approve zoning for new apartment developments takes
careful consideration of both area specific issues and citywide goals.

Below are statements within the General Plan in quotations that highlight the need for a
comprehensive view when considering a zone change for apartments along with some
commentary from staff:

      “In areas where retail development is aging (at least 30 years old) and underperforming
       and where transit is readily accessible, consider the addition of some multi-family
       residential as part of a redevelopment strategy to add more retail demand.” This objective
       was part of the reason for approving apartments on the old Kmart site on Redwood Road
       as well as the apartments at Valley Fair Mall.
      “Maintain zoning in areas currently zoned for office or industrial development to allow
       opportunities for new commercial development.”

       “West Valley City should work to maintain at least one job for each person in the labor
       force within the City.”

      “When considering rezone requests for high density residential development, the
       proximity to transit, the size of the project and the proposed level of quality (i.e. exterior
       and interior finishes and amenities) should be primary factors for evaluation along with
       other site-specific considerations.” The proposed level of quality is addressed later in this
       report.

      “Redevelop blighted multi-family residential with higher density residential.” Examples
       of this would be the redevelopment that occurred on Harvey Street and in Fairbourne
       Station. There are other areas in the City where this approach will be needed.

In summary, when considering where to approve apartment developments, the City should
balance considering area specific issues which have already been discussed, providing retail
support within retail areas, preserving land for job creating uses, supporting transit and
redeveloping blighted areas. While the site has no neighboring retail, it is closer to the
restaurants to the south along Decker Lake Dr. and the commercial uses along 3500 South than
the apartment proposal to the north (GPZ-3-2020). With less than 3 acres, the proposed use
would consume much less land that could be used for job creating uses than the apartment
proposal to the north. Being directly adjacent to the light rail station, it seems this use would
support transit.

Does the quality of the project further City goals?

City Code requires requests for the RM zone to be accompanied with a development agreement
proposal that addresses dwelling unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, landscaping and
project amenities. Each of these items is addressed below. As a reminder, the recent code
changes mentioned earlier included several changes to the City’s design standards for multiple
unit dwelling residential.

Dwelling Unit Sizes

The proposed project includes a mix of studio, one-bedroom and two-bedroom units. As
described below, all of the proposed unit sizes meet or exceed the minimum required for units
that are stacked on top of each other.
    Unit Type         Number of Units        Minimum Size from            Proposed Size (Sq. Ft.)
                                             Ordinance (Sq. Ft.)
 Studio                               58                       400                              527
 One-bedroom                          64                       600                 682, 715 and 751
 Two-bedroom                          97                       850                 850, 935 and 974
Exterior Materials

For materials, the City’s multi-unit residential design standards state: “All multi-unit residential
Building exteriors shall be brick, stucco, stone, tile, metal panels or fiber cement siding. With the
exception of brick or stone which may be used on the entire Building exterior, no single material
shall comprise more than 60 percent of a Building’s exterior. Where stucco, tile, metal panels or
fiber cement siding are used, at least 20 percent of the Building’s exterior shall be brick or stone.
Wood and vinyl may be used as trim, soffits and/or accent materials only.” The applicant is
proposing a combination of metal panel, fiber cement siding, brick or stone and stucco, which
are all permitted materials.

Architecture

Based on a preliminary review of the architecture by staff, the proposed apartment building
appears to meet or exceed the City’s architectural standards. If this application is approved, the
construction plans will be reviewed to verify compliance with City standards.
Landscaping

The ordinance requires a minimum of 15% usable open space with no more than 60% building
coverage. The proposed useable open space is approximately 28% in the form of open
landscaped areas at grade, recreation space above the structured parking and shared outdoor
patios. The building coverage is approximately 55%.

Project Amenities

The proposed project exceeds the City’s requirements for amenities. For a project of this size, the
ordinance requires five amenities. The proposed project includes 8 amenities listed in the
ordinance: rooftop patios, outdoor grill, clubhouse, fitness center, a business center with Wi-Fi
and computers for residents, pool, hot tub and bicycle storage. In addition, the project also
includes a dog wash.

Interior finishes and amenities proposed by the applicant include 9’ ceilings, walk-in closets in
master bedrooms, 2-tone paint, stainless steel appliances, microwave ovens, garbage disposals,
granite or solid surface countertops in kitchens and baths and high-speed internet.

Other Issues

      Parking: The Zoning Ordinance allows the Planning Commission to utilize the following
       parking ratios for developments with multi-unit dwellings with at least 40 units: 1.3
       spaces per unit for studio units, 1.5 spaces per unit for 1-bedroom units, and 1.75 spaces
       per unit for two-bedroom units. Using these ratios, the minimum required parking is 332.
       The latest concept plan shows 323 stalls on-site with an additional 32 stalls on or
       accessed from the Rocky Mountain Power property to the north. Therefore, staff is
       recommending that the addition parking on the Rocky Mountain Power property be
       included in the development agreement. Also, the code was recently changed to state:
       “All parking shall be made available free of charge.” The proposed management
       standards include this statement: “Every unit will be required to rent at least one interior
       garage parking stall as part of the recurring monthly rental charges.” This statement
       implies that a charge could be levied for additional parking spaces.

      Exceptions:
          o One minor exception with the building is the height. Most of the building is 75’
              tall, which is the maximum allowed in the RM zone. There are a few areas on the
              building where features are 76’ tall. Staff recommends that an exception be made
              for these features in the development agreement. Otherwise, the plans can be
              modified so that no portion of the building is taller than 75’.
          o The RM zone requires a minimum 25’ front setback and a 20’ street side yard
              setback. Given the design of the building, staff recommends a different approach
              to the streetscape as described in part in the next bullet point.
          o The existing improvements along Decker Lake Dr. include a 6’ sidewalk and the
              existing improvements along 3100 South include a 5’ park strip and a 4’ sidewalk.
              The Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone requires a 6’ park strip and 8’ sidewalk
              along Decker Lake Dr. While the applicant is requesting to remove the overlay
              zone, staff recommends that the park strip and sidewalk widths in the overlay
              zone be used for this project to tie in with development to the south. These
              standards are included in the recommended development agreement revisions
              from staff.

      Recommended Development Agreement Revisions: In addition to addressing the parking
       and exceptions mentioned above, the recommended development agreement revisions
       from staff include a few other clarifying changes.

Staff Alternatives:

   3. Approval. This application should be approved subject to a development agreement that
      includes the proposal from the applicant and the recommended changes from staff.

   4. Continuance. This application should be continued to allow the applicant time to further
      refine the proposal.

   5. Denial. This application should be denied to preserve the land for job creating uses and to
      focus apartment development in areas that accomplish more citywide objectives.


Discussion: Steve Pastorik presented the application. Commissioner McEwen asked Steve if
Rocky Mountain Power will allow the applicant to park on their property. Steve explained there
is currently an easement for access to Egate Apartments that will also apply to the subject
property. Steve stated there will possibly be a need for an easement to allow the use of Rocky
Mountain Power’s land for parking.

Commissioner McEwen asked Steve if the concept plan includes parking on Rocky Mountain
Power’s land. Steve explained the concept plan breaks down the parking on the subject property
and the land owned by Rocky Mountain Power. Steve explained the applicant would need 10
stalls on Rocky Mountain Power’s land to meet the minimum parking requirement according to
the ordinance.

Adam Davis, representing the applicant, addressed the Planning Commission. Mr. Davis stated
the applicant is willing to meet and exceed the RM ordinance. Adam then addressed the
question of the subject property being built in the correct location. Mr. Davis stated there have
been different market studies in support of this location. He pointed out the proximity of the
TRAX station, the amenities offered, and the target market all point to this being a great location
for this proposal. Mr. Davis also expressed his feeling of how adding the subject property will
increase the population and therefore draw in business. Commissioner Winters asked Mr. Davis
why they proposed to have apartment buildings on the ground floor as opposed to retail shops.
Mr. Davis explained the apartments coincide with current market demands. Commissioner
Porter asked Mr. Davis what the intentions for parking are. Mr. Davis explained all parking will
be free of charge per the development agreement.

Craig Larson, an adjacent business owner, expressed his concerns with the proposal as it pertains
to parking. Mr. Larson explained how overflow parking from E-Gate Apartments is an issue for
his business and he does not feel there are enough stalls in this proposal to meet the parking
needs. He stated this will have a negative impact on his business. Steve explained that the City is
working with E-Gate to add more onsite parking. Steve also explained the City Council added
free parking to the ordinance to address issues with the parking at E-Gate. E-Gate Apartments
were charging for parking on-site and that caused tenants to look for free parking elsewhere.
Steve explained the parking on the subject property would be free for tenants. Mr. Davis stated
the number of cars located on the subject property will be minimized by the ability to use the
TRAX station. Mr. Larson and Mr. Davis continued their conversation addressing the parking
issues that will impact the subject property, and the adjacent properties. Steve explained any
comments made during the public hearing should be addressed to the Planning Commission and
continued conversation between property owners should take place elsewhere. Mr. Larson asked
the Planning Commission to table this proposal until there is agreement about the parking issues.

Commissioner Winters asked Steve if a plat agreement will be needed to address parking. Steve
explained if the applicant would like to modify the parking that would require a plat agreement.

Commissioner McEwen asked Steve if the surface parking for the subject property is gated.
Steve stated the surface parking is not gated in the proposed site plan. Chairperson Meaders
asked Steve if the staff feels there will be adequate parking for the subject property. Steve
explained with the use of Rocky Mountain Power’s land, staff feels they will meet the minimum
parking requirement.

Commissioner Winters asked if there is a way to move forward with this proposal allowing more
time to address the parking. Steve stated his feeling would be to wait and see if there will be
cooperation between the property owners so the applicant will not be stuck in limbo.

Commissioner Winters pointed out two issues with parking. One is if the applicant will be able
to use Rocky Mountain Power’s land and the second issue is between the property owners.
Steve explained any revision to the easement pertaining to the access to the subject property, and
the Egate Apartments might require several different parties’ involvement. Steve explained he
would need to look further into the easement to see if the issue can be resolved between the
property owners or if other parties would need to be involved.

Commissioner Fuller asked if the Planning Commission approves a change to the zone today
would the applicant need to come back to the Planning Commission for further plans? Steve
explained the applicant would need to come back to the Commission for a conditional use
agreement or a plat amendment.

Commissioner Winters explained he did not see this parking issue addressed in the development
agreement and asked Steve if the concern is to add the parking issue to the development
agreement. Steve explained the Planning Commission could table this issue with the concern
being the sharing of parking and flow of traffic. Steve stated with the current proposal the
applicant would meet the minimum parking requirement.

Chairperson Meaders stated she doesn’t feel the need to table this proposal where any further
conversation will not affect the development agreement.

Commissioner McEwen stated he isn’t sure how the applicant will meet the minimum parking
requirement without the use of Rocky Mountain Power’s land. Steve stated the development
agreement includes the provision that they provide the parking on Rocky Mountain Power’s
land. If Rocky Mountain Power does not agree with the use of their land the applicant can
modify the plan to have all parking onsite. Steve explained the staff feels using Rocky Mountain
Power’s land is something that can be arranged.
