1.1 Continuing Business: Consideration of ZM-20-003, Rezoning Approx. 1.2 acres
from R-1-10 to R-1-5 Location: 4524 S. 785 E. Applicant: Bryan Colemere Planner:
Robert May
Robert May said the applicant wanted to rezone property located at 4524 South 785 East
from an R-1-10 to R-1-5 to increase the maximum density to 7.0 units per acre from 4.0 units
per acre. He showed the Commission current pictures and the zoning map of the property.
The subject properties were currently zoned R-1-10 and consisted of approximately 1.18
acres and designated as a Neighborhood 1 on the Future Land Use Map in the General Plan

which has a typical range of density between 1-6 dwelling units per acre. The underlying
base zones along 785 East range from an average of 5-8 dwelling units per acre. The
immediate area surrounding 785 East does contain some higher density zoning capable of
multi-family housing. May said the original application was to rezone to R-1-3 which would
have increased the maximum density to 11 units per acre. The applicant held a neighborhood
meeting on February 18, 2020 and the six residents that attended expressed concern about
traffic at the 785 E. and 4500 S. intersection.
May said at their meeting on March 3, 2020, the Millcreek Community Council
recommended denial of the proposed rezone but stated in their motion that a density similar
to an R-1-6 Zone (6 units per acre) would be considered. The applicant chose to continue the
application ZM-20-003 to attempt to re-visit the Millcreek Community Council with more
information. The applicant amended the proposal to rezone to R-1-5, but under the recent
circumstances due to social distancing parameters and orders of priority, and in light of their
recommendation from March, the Millcreek Community Council declined to review the
applicant’s revised plans during their April meeting. May said this was partly due to the
Millcreek Community Council being comfortable with recommending a density similar to an
R-1-6 Zone. Staff found that maintaining an R-1-5 zone comfortably harnessed the R-1
single-family neighborhood and that the addition of 3 more units at slightly over 7,000
square feet per unit would not negatively impact 785 East. He then went over the
comparisons from the R-1-10 Zone and R-1-5 Zone.
May showed the Commission a map of the property and said there would need to be 4,000
square feet of right-of-way dedication. He said the applicant did not intend on developing the
property themselves but provided a plan that demonstrated what type of development could
be approved in anticipation of the rezone and agreed to a development agreement. Due to the
neighborhood response and possible traffic impacts, Millcreek commissioned a professional
traffic report which was paid for by the applicant. The traffic report was commissioned when
the applicant’s proposal was to rezone from an R-1-10 to an R-1-3 and potentially creating
21 separate dwelling units. The proposed units count had since been amended to 7 units. The
key summary and findings in the staff report did not show a negative impact or a major effect
on the existing and proposed (including Cypress Credit Union not yet constructed) level of
service. The applicant and homeowner provided a signed petition of residents who reside on
785 East that were in favor of an R-1-5 Zone proposal. Staff found that the R-1-5 zone was
compatible to the neighborhood and that it would not negatively impact the current traffic on
785 E. May recommended the Commission grant a favorable recommendation of the rezone
to the City Council with the development agreement conditions found in the staff report.
Commissioner Allen said since the traffic study was for 21 lots, there was no reason to redo it
for the 7 lots. May confirmed. Commissioner Stephens asked about the duplex being
different from two single family lots with the lot lines. May said a R-1-5 zone allowed the
zero-side yard setback, appearing like a duplex, but the lot line would still be there, and each
home would function as a single-family home. Commissioner Stephens said not more than
two homes could abut on the garage side. May confirmed and said that could be added to the
development agreement. Commissioner Reid asked if the development agreement conditions
would be legally binding for the rezone. May said the development agreement would be a
binding contract between the City and the developer, tied to the land.

1.4 Consideration of EX-20-002, Request to Have a Second Dwelling in a Single-Family
Home Declared Legal Through Special Exception Location: 918 E. 3385 S. Applicant:
Heather Wood Planner: Erin O’Kelley
Erin O’Kelley said the applicant had an additional dwelling on the property at 918 E. 3385 S.
located in the basement. Due to the current zoning of R-1-8, the use as a duplex or mother-in-
law was not a permitted use. The applicant was proposing, through application, as per
Chapter 19.88.140 of the Millcreek Code to have the use of a duplex/mother-in-law declared
legal through special exception. The property owner originally received a conditional use
permit for the property that allowed part of the first level of the home to be used as a salon
with a separate entrance on 900 E. Historically, the original house was built in 1959 and had
since been designated under multiple residential zones. O’Kelley said all special exceptions
are reviewed by the community councils. At their regularly scheduled meeting on April 7,
2020, the Millcreek Community Council offered a positive recommendation to approve the
special exception to declare the non-conforming use legal. Staff did not receive formal public
comment on the application, but two residents inquired about the project and one resident
seemed concerned but did not offer specifics. Millcreek Code 19.88 required that the
applicant provide evidence proving the following standards had been met: the use exists on
the property at the time of the application and has been in continuous violation of the zoning
ordinance for a period exceeding ten years; no complaint has been made to the development
services division concerning the violation for a period exceeding ten consecutive years
during which the violation existed; and continuation of the use will not have a detrimental
effect on the health, safety or welfare of persons or property in the vicinity. The applicant
met the standard of proof on those points. O’Kelley recommended the special exception to
have a second unit be declared legal.

Erin O’Kelley said the applicant had a lot with an existing home that was demolished and
would like to rebuild a new single-family home. She said code required that all new
development must have full public improvements which includes a sidewalk, though
property owners could request an exception. The exception application gets reviewed by the
Public Works Department, the community council, and Planning Commission, and then the
determination is made by the Mayor. The Mt. Olympus Community Council gave a positive
recommendation to approve the sidewalk exception. The Millcreek Public Works Director

addition of sidewalk was not necessary at this time of development, staff was recommending
that a deferral agreement between Millcreek and the property owner be recorded and placed
against the subject property that ran with the land in perpetuity. The recorded deferral
agreement places the responsibility on the property owner that when the installation of
sidewalk becomes required, the owner of the property would be responsible for the costs of
the sidewalk improvements and related costs.
