Proposed Ordinance 2024-21 adopting salary schedules for employees of
Ogden City
         A memo from Council staff regarding proposed salary schedules for Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 came before the
Council for consideration. The memo stated that in conjunction with consideration of the final FY 2024-2025 Budget, the Council
will need to adopt salary schedules to set compensation ranges for all employees including elected officials. City Administration
has contracted with McGrath Consulting, Inc. to perform a compensation study and changes to pay ranges and titles are being
proposed based on that study. The Council may set a public hearing for consideration of the salary schedules for August 6;
however, a final determination on ranges and titles will be made after the hearing is set on July 9. The FY 2024-2025 Tentative
Budget was adopted by the Council at the June 18, 2024, meeting. As part of the adoption of the tentative budget, there were two
changes made as part of Schedule A as well as the required changes to the Justice Court Judges’ salaries. No other changes were
adopted as part of the tentative budget.
         Council Deputy Director Symes summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the
history of discussions and actions taken regarding each of the three consent items, and to identify the next steps to set public
hearings and take action on the proposals in conjunction with action on the FY2024-2025 Final Budget. For a copy of the
presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Chair Richey then introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-21, entitled:
                   “An ordinance of Ogden City adopting salary schedules for employees of Ogden City.”
         COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS MOVED A PUBLIC HEARING ON THE PROPOSAL BE HELD IN THE CITY
COUNCIL CHAMBERS ON AUGUST 6, 2024, AT 6:00 P.M. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER
GRAF, ALL VOTING AYE. Council member Hyer and Vice Chair White were not present when this vote was taken.

Proposed Resolution 2024-6 awarding Arts Grants for Fiscal Year 2024-
2025
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025
arts grants awards came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated each year the Ogden City Council appropriates
grant funding to support various local arts organizations and programs. The Ogden Arts Advisory Committee and Administration
review each application and provide recommendations to the Council. The FY2025 budget for arts grants is expected to be
$150,000. This year Ogden City Arts received 31 applications requesting a total of $248,500 in funding. Of this, 11 applications
applied for a total of $130,000 in general operating support; 20 applicants applied for a total of $118,500 in project support.
         Chair Richey then introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2024-6, entitled:
                   “A resolution of the Ogden City Council awarding Arts Grants for FY2024-2025 and setting forth findings
                   pursuant to Utah Code Annotated § 10-8-2.”
         COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS MOVED A PUBLIC HEARING ON THE PROPOSAL BE HELD IN THE CITY
COUNCIL CHAMBERS ON AUGUST 6, 2024, AT 6:00 P.M. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER
GRAF, ALL VOTING AYE. Council member Hyer and Vice Chair White were not present when this vote was taken.

Proposed Ordinance 2024-22 adopting the budget for Ogden City for Fiscal
Year 2024-2025
         A memo from Council staff regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 Final Budget and Certified Tax Rate
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Council will consider setting a public hearing on the FY 2024-
2025 Final Budget for August 6, 2024; the Tentative Budget was adopted by the Council at the June 18, 2024, meeting. As part of
the adoption of the tentative budget, there were two changes made as part of Schedule A as well as the required changes to the
Justice Court Judges’ salaries. No other changes were adopted as part of the tentative budget. Further changes to the budget are
anticipated with the Final Budget including implementing the final compensation study recommendations, potential changes to the
Certified Tax Rate, changes to the BDO Lease Revenue transfer, and any other changes the Council feels are necessary for final
adoption.
         Chair Richey then introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-22, entitled:
                   “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, adopting the budget for Ogden City for the Fiscal Year from July 1, 2024,
                   to June 30, 2025; and providing that this ordinance shall take effect immediately upon its adoption and deposit
                   with the City Recorder.”
         COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS MOVED A PUBLIC HEARING ON THE PROPOSAL BE HELD IN THE CITY
COUNCIL CHAMBERS ON AUGUST 6, 2024, AT 6:00 P.M. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER
GRAF, ALL VOTING AYE. Council member Hyer and Vice Chair White were not present when this vote was taken.

Public Comments
          Heath Satow stated he has expressed concerns regarding the paid parking system, a matter that was discussed during
tonight’s work session. He noted that staff has compared the City’s proposal with programs that have been employed in other
cities, but he does not view those examples as ‘success stories’, nor convincing as justification for proceeding with the program in
Ogden. He cited differences between Ogden and the other cities that were relied upon, including population disparities, access to
free shuttles, and times of day that free parking is available in the downtown areas of those other cities.
          Linda Sanchez stated she recently had a great interaction with Anna Davidson; she is compassionate, non-judgmental,
and is likely not compensated enough for what she does for Ogden City, She then also referenced the Council’s discussion of the
paid parking program during the work session. She stated that taxpayers have paid for the government buildings located in
Ogden’s downtown and should not be required to pay to park near them. It is offensive to charge people to park and many people
on fixed incomes will not be able to afford this additional expense. She stated that the City needs to make access to federal
government buildings free. Taxes continue to increase in order to pay City employees more money, but the general public is not
receiving a pay increase. She stated she is frugal with her money, and she advised the City to do the same.
          Laura Lewis stated that there are often presentations given during the Council work session meetings and she asked that
those presentations be made publicly available prior to the meeting so that the public can review them and be prepared with
questions. She also cited the paid parking program and noted staff mentioned that businesses are supportive of the program and do
not want people loitering in their business. She stated that she owned a small business for years and she wanted people to stay in
her store because that meant they would spend more money. She also asked if each business as well as the general public were
polled to determine the level of support for a paid parking program; it is likely that many residents will simply drive to other
communities to shop in order to save the costs of paid parking. She echoed Ms. Sanchez’s comments about residents living on a
fixed income; these individuals will receive a 2.6 percent cost of living adjustment (COLA) in their social security benefit, which
is nearly nothing. That increase will be consumed by the City’s property tax increase and the paid parking program, and this will
reduce the public’s standard of living.
          Steve Jones also expressed concerns about the paid parking program; he believes the program will drive people and
businesses away from Ogden because there are other viable alternatives very close by that do not charge for parking. He
encouraged the Council to carefully consider the impacts of their decisions; Ogden is not like the many other cities that were cited
by staff, and he cautioned the Council against ruining what the City has.
          Chair Richey stated that no decisions have been made regarding the final details of the paid parking system, but actions
have been taken in the past that have solidified the decision to implement some type of paid parking program in downtown Ogden.
Many of the topics discussed during tonight’s work session are yet to be determined, such as a 15-minute free parking timeframe,
scaling the program based upon demand for parking in downtown Ogden. Chief Administrative Officer Brown added that there
will also be a free trolley service in downtown Ogden. The City is still receiving public feedback and will collaborate with Weber
County to determine that the program benefits the entire community.
          Chair Richey then referenced Ms. Lewis’s question about the presentations that are made during work sessions; the
meeting packet is posted the Thursday before the meeting, but the Council does not receive the presentation documents until the
day of the meeting. Council staff briefly illustrated the steps to access meeting packets for each Council meeting. Ms. Lewis
reiterated her suggestion that the presentations be made available before the meeting. Council staff emphasized that the Council
does not even receive the presentations until the night of the meeting. Council member Graf added that action is not taken during
work session meeting and there is time for the Council to continue to receive public comment on any matter that is discussed
during a work session prior to action being taken.

Mayor Comments
         Chief Administrative Officer Brown reported Administration is still working to respond to the request for closure of a
section of Taylor Avenue between Country Hills Drive and the Weber State University (WSU) campus. The Ogden City Police
Department has collaborated with WSU Police for increased enforcement of traffic violations in the area, additional signage will
be posted, and Administration will meet with WSU Administration to further explore options.

Council member Comments
        Council member Blair thanked Ms. Brown for addressing the neighborhood concerns regarding Taylor Avenue near
Weber State University.
