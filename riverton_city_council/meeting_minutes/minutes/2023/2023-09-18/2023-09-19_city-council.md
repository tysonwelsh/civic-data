         South Valley Sewer District Presentation

Joel Thompson, General Manager of the South Valley Sewer District, provided an overview of
the district's slip lining project, which involves rehabilitating sewer pipes using a trenchless
technology called CIPP (Cured-In-Place Pipe) lining. He explained that the district covers the
Page | 3 Minutes – City Council Meeting
September 19, 2023




south end of Salt Lake County, providing wastewater collection and treatment services to
several cities.

Mr. Thompson discussed the importance of addressing defects and damage in sewer pipes,
which can lead to infiltration and reduce system capacity. He highlighted the benefits of using
CIPP lining, such as cost-effectiveness and reduced disruption to property owners.

The presentation included images of damaged sewer pipes and the CIPP lining process, where
resin-soaked liners are inserted into pipes and cured in place. Mr. Thompson also shared a map
showing completed and planned lining projects within the district.

Councilmember Buroker asked why the work would continue until late into the evening. In
response, Mr. Thompson explained that late-night work during the curing process is primarily
due to the inability to stop once it has started. Once the curing process begins, it must continue
until the liner is fully cured and hardened. Additionally, the contractors aim to time the work to
complete it efficiently, but there may be challenges related to bypassing or diverting flows, which
can make it necessary to work late into the evening. The contractors understand the importance
of completing the job and restoring sewer services to residents, so they work diligently until the
project is finished and the quality meets the required standards.

Councilmember McDougal asked about Riverton City’s inspections and whether certain lines
are at the end of their life. Mr. Thompson responded by saying that the most critical and urgent
repairs to Riverton's sewer lines have been addressed as a top priority. These critical repairs
took place between 2021 and 2022, and the current phase of the project focuses on less severe
and less urgent repairs, as well as the slip lining process to extend the operational life of the
sewer lines. The sewer lines in Riverton have received necessary attention to ensure their
functionality and minimize potential issues.

         Riverton Police Department Presentation

Chief Barfuss shared several important updates and statistics regarding the recent transfer of
Animal Services and Code Enforcement to the police department's supervision. This transition
occurred on July 1, and the data presented covered the two months following this change.

Chief Barfuss noted that there has been a significant increase in call volume for Animal Services
since the transfer, with calls going from seven in July to thirty-three in August. This surge in calls
has put pressure Officer Watson, impacting her ability to proactively address issues. Regarding
licensing, it was mentioned that there has been a decline in pet licensing, raising concerns
about public safety and disease control.

Chief Barfuss said there was a notable increase in calls for service in Code Enforcement, from
61 in July to over 100 in August. Officer Juarez is struggling to keep up with the rising demand,
and cases typically take over 30 days to address.

Chief Barfuss explained that the records division experienced a complete turnover in personnel.
The division is responsible for daily transcriptions, and the statistics showed a significant
workload increase, with over 1,700 daily transcriptions from May to August. The division also
faces the challenge of fulfilling governmental records requests within the mandated 10-day
timeframe.

Lastly, Chief Barfuss discussed broader police department statistics. Traffic citations were noted
to have increased by 34% compared to the previous year, likely due to increased traffic in the
area. Arrests, while on the rise, have not increased as much as expected, which was attributed
to proactive efforts and community-oriented policing.

Councilmember McCay asked why there was such an increase in Animal Control calls. In
response, Chief Barfuss explained that both Animal Services and Code Enforcement have
always been active but may not have been as comprehensively documented in the past. He
noted that due to the high volume of incoming calls, there hasn't been much time for proactive
work, which is an issue that is being addressed.

Councilmember McCay questioned whether the City should continue requiring dog permits,
given the limited number of residents who obtain them. She suggested that those who do
obtain permits might be responsible pet owners who are already ensuring their dogs are
properly vaccinated and cared for.

Mayor Staggs acknowledged Councilmember McCay’s suggestion as a topic worth discussing
with the City Council. He noted that revenue from dog permits could help fund additional code
enforcement officers. He expressed surprise at the drop in the number of dog licenses issued
after the City transitioned to the Stone Ridge Animal Center partnership. He emphasized the
importance of this revenue for supporting code enforcement officers and acknowledged that the
way calls are tracked shows an increase in demand for code enforcement services.

Councilmember Wells expressed concern about reducing or eliminating dog permits, citing
several serious dog bite incidents in his district over the past four years. He emphasized the
importance of dog permits in ensuring that dogs are immunized, preventing the spread of
diseases, and addressing potentially dangerous situations. He mentioned that the goal of
bringing animal control and code enforcement under the police department was to have more
active enforcement rather than relying solely on complaints. However, he recognized that it's
only been a couple of months since the change, and there have been efforts to address record-
keeping and other issues.

Councilmember Wells suggested that in the future, they could discuss the level of active
enforcement in the City, not just related to animals but also concerning other issues such as
problem properties. He expressed interest in receiving a report on the City's progress in this
regard.

Councilmember Buroker expressed appreciation for the data presented during the meeting and
mentioned that it would aid in making informed decisions in the future and extended her
gratitude to these officers for their work.

Councilmember McDougal expressed gratitude towards Riverton City for addressing the issue
of Code Enforcement in an organized and data-driven manner. He highlighted residents'
appreciation for the City's proactive approach in addressing long-standing code issues that had

Councilmember Pierucci expressed appreciation and then raised two questions. He asked for
clarification on whether the City provides weekend service and, if so, what that service entails.

Chief Barfuss confirmed that both Officer Watson and Officer Juarez are available on call during
weekends to respond to animal control and code enforcement issues. However, he also noted

officers. He mentioned that they are working on a five-year strategic plan for growth that will

Chief Barfuss said they have taken a collaborative approach with Mountain View Village,
particularly in dealing with issues related to retail theft and organized criminal activity. They are
exploring technology-based solutions, including license plate readers, while also respecting
individuals' constitutional rights to travel. The police department is actively working with
Mountain View Village to address the challenges in the area and is open to using various
resources to improve public safety.
