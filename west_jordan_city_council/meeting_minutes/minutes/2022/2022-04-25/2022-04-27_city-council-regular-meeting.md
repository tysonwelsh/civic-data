CALL TO ORDER
Chair Whitelock called the work session to order at 5:30 pm

A.       2022 Fireworks Restrictions Discussion
Fire Chief Derek Maxfield spoke of severe drought conditions in the last year and wanted to get a
jump on fireworks restrictions for the 2022 season. He proposed a map for 2022 based on the
restrictions map approved in 2021, with City parks added as restricted areas. Chief Maxfield
recommended approval of the proposed map, with the caveat that if drought conditions continued
to deteriorate, the Council would reevaluate the map closer to July.

Council Member McConnehey asked if there were steps the Council could preemptively take to
provide the Chief with authority to take actions close to the July holidays without calling a last-
minute special Council meeting. Chief Maxfield responded that changes needed to be made by the
legislative body. Council Member McConnehey was interested in the possibility of approving a set
of circumstances under which further restrictions could be implemented without last-minute
Council interaction. Vice Chair Green was not interested in delegating the Council’s authority.

Council Member Bloom expressed concern regarding enforcement, and shared personal
experiences where citizens ignored the restrictions. Chief Maxfield said the Police Department had
enforcement responsibility and assigned officers to patrol restricted areas. He said he agreed that
enforcement was difficult. Council Member Worthen suggested Council members talk to their
neighbors to help spread awareness of the restrictions.

Responding to a question from Council Member Jacob, Chief Maxfield said he suspected the Federal
Aviation Administration (FAA) had fireworks restrictions associated with the South Valley Regional
Airport. Chair Whitelock thanked Chief Maxfield for the presentation.

B.       Firework Show Alternatives Discussion
Council Member Worthen asked if Council Members would be interested in looking into the
possibility of doing a drone show instead of a firework show in July. She noted that it might help
those with PTSD and pet sensitivity. Council Member Bloom had looked into the option while on an
events committee a few years ago. The prices were high, but she felt confident that prices would go
City Council Work Session Minutes – April 27, 2022                                              Page 2

down over time as more companies offered the option. Council Member Bloom suggested a drone
show may be a more viable option down the road.

Council Member Jacob pointed out that the City’s firework show was only a small portion of the
overall impact in terms of PTSD, pets, and smoke. He expressed the opinion that eliminating the City
firework show would not make a big difference and the drone option seemed cost prohibitive.

Council Member McConnehey said the option was discussed previously by the Airport Board and
asked the Mayor if he remembered the discussions. Mayor Burton got a cost estimate in 2020 that
was too expensive. He hoped the option became affordable eventually because he liked the idea.
Public Services Director Isaac Astill found recent pricing to be from $80,000 to $300,000 range.

Chair Whitelock asked if proximity to the Regional Airport would be an issue. Council Member
McConnehey responded he did not think the location of the City show was close enough to the
Airport to be a concern. However, he said there may be additional restrictions he was not aware of
for events involving more than one drone at a time. Council Member McConnehey would not be
opposed to informally inquiring into the possibility but would not want to spend significant staff
time. It was pointed out that wind and rain would potentially be more of a problem with a drone
show.

Chair Whitelock summarized that Council Members were not opposed to the idea, and although it
may not work for 2022 it may be worth looking into each year for possibility in the future.

C.      Cultural Arts Society of West Jordan Discussion
Chair Whitelock stated financial statements for the Cultural Arts Society of West Jordan were
included with the agenda packet. She expressed the opinion that the financial statements did not
provide a complete picture of revenue and expenditures. For example, she said the statements did
not include tuition participation fees collected from youth who participated in programs. Chair
Whitelock suggested the Council should give a clearer explanation of what was wanted in the
financial statements (e.g. tuition fees collected, fundraiser and concessions income).

Council Member Worthen agreed and noted that she had provided a list of items she expected to
see on the financial statements. She suggested revisiting the contract and perhaps reducing the
amount given by the City. She wanted to feel that the Cultural Arts Society had skin in the game.

Vice Chair Green did not believe a community arts program would make money, which was fine
with him. However, he agreed the contract needed to be revised. Vice Chair Green was interested in
knowing how many of the youth participating in shows lived in West Jordan and expressed concern
with the City funding programs if participants were not primarily from the West Jordan community.
He suggested City financial participation in the form of matching funds, with the amount given
without a match reduced over time. Vice Chair Green suggested taking portions of productions, such
as musical numbers, to the Senior Citizen Center and elementary schools to reach out in the
community. He expressed the opinion that if comp tickets were distributed, they should be given to
underserved portions of the community. He wanted to make sure the City was truly supporting
community arts rather than a regional program.

Council Member McConnehey thought it was important to remember that a few years ago, the group
was an arts committee within the City. He felt the individuals involved with the Cultural Arts Society
were skilled in the arts, but not necessarily familiar with financial statements and running a non-
profit. He said it had been suggested that separating and becoming a non-profit would make things
City Council Work Session Minutes – April 27, 2022                                              Page 3

easier for the group and the City, but the Cultural Arts Society had not received continued guidance.
Council Member McConnehey suggested providing a minimal number of hours of the
Administrative Services Director’s time to help the Cultural Arts Society get up to speed. He
emphasized the Society was still in a transitionary period and wanted to be as supportive as
possible.

Council Member Pack agreed with Council Member McConnehey and would not be in favor of
reducing the allocation from the City. Council Member Pack spoke of benefits to City businesses if
individuals were to come to performances in West Jordan. He commented that supporting the arts
was part of having a well-rounded city. Council Member Pack did not want to make City funding
contingent on having a certain number of participants or attendees.

Council Member Jacob left the dais at 6:12 pm, and returned at 6:15 pm

Vice Chair Green noted that some of the money from the County was matching funds. He expressed
the opinion that if the City was going to fund the Cultural Arts Society 100%, it should belong to the
City. If it was going to function as a non-profit, fundraising needed to happen. Regarding the
contract, he did not want the Council overseeing their operations, but believed the Council needed
to ensure money allocated was accounted for, and the City was getting what was expected. Vice
Chair Green wanted the contract to include a requirement that a certain number of performances
be taken to the Senior Citizen Center to expand those touched by the arts.

Chair Whitelock believed she heard the Council express a desire to see the Cultural Arts Society
flourish, but it was hard to tell from information provided where the money was coming from and
going, and the Council wanted to know how many West Jordan citizens were participating.

Council Member McConnehey wanted to see which funds were provided directly from the City and
which funds were County ZAP funds passed through the City. He wanted to know how many
participants ages 8-17, and 18+ were from West Jordan versus other cities, and how many people
attended each event. He was not concerned about giving comp tickets but wanted to know what the
comp ticket policy was. He requested a basic income statement/balance sheet, and a one-page
summary showing some of the same measurements provided by the Community Development
Block Grant (CDBG) and youth athletic groups.

Council Member Worthen wanted to be able to defend the financial statements to the citizens. For
her it was not about making a profit but about how money provided by the City was spent.

Staff clarified that West Jordan no longer received ZAP grant funding to pass on to the Cultural Arts
Society. Council Member McConnehey was interested in knowing the zip codes of the individuals
both participating in and attending performances.

Chair Whitelock summarized that the Council needed a clearer picture of what was done with the
money provided by the City. The Council also wanted to know in which city participants lived for
ages under 18, and over 18, and wanted attendance numbers with, if possible, the zip codes of those
attending.

Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck suggested including an exhibit with the contract that
provided a format for exactly what was wanted.

City Administrator Korban Lee asked what would be necessary for the Council to release the
$60,000 allocated for the Cultural Arts Society in the current fiscal year. Chair Whitelock responded

        d. Fencing and Screening Setback Requirements
City Planner/Zoning Administrator Larry Gardner referred to City Code language that stated “no
wall, fence or opaque hedge or screening material shall be installed or constructed within ten feet
of a right of way line”, and explained that a business owner in a M-1 Zone wished to place a security
fence around his property closer to the right of way line rather than ten feet to keep as much storage
space on the property as possible. Mr. Gardner expressed the opinion that current code language
prevented the property owner from being able to use his property in the way he wanted. He
proposed removing the 10-foot requirement from the back of the right of way line allowing the
property owner to install a fence on the right of way line. Mr. Gardner noted that City Code allowed
fencing on right of way lines in every residential zone of the City. He explained that master plan
mechanisms would come into play if the City needed to widen the road in the future.

Mr. Gardner stated the corner sight line would be protected. Council Member Jacob commented on
the City-wide applicability for one person’s situation and asked if a person would be able to request
an individual fence variance. Mr. Gardner was not aware of any fence variance having been granted
in West Jordan. He explained that a variance would run with the land and would possibly be an issue
for the City in the future.

Mr. Gardner noted that hundreds of properties would be affected by the proposed amendment. He
did not know the rationale behind initially putting the 10-foot restriction in place and suggested it
City Council Meeting Minutes – April 27, 2022                                                   Page 5

may have had something to do with convenience and cost-savings to the City if roads were
eventually widened.

Chair Whitelock opened a public hearing at 7:55 pm

Comments:
Steven Malone represented the property in question for two local West Jordan businesses. He
mentioned that the Planning Department had been wonderful to work with. He explained that the
intent was to improve the property. They were not asking for anything other businesses in the area
did not already have. Mr. Malone commented that a 20-foot total setback would result in continued
illegal parking on their property.

Chair Whitelock closed the public hearing at 7:59 pm

MOTION:       Vice Chair Green moved to approve Ordinance No. 22-16 amending the 2009
              West Jordan City Code (repealing fencing and screening setback requirements in
              the Professional Office, Commercial, and Manufacturing Zones, Section 13-14-4).
              Council Member McConnehey seconded the motion.

Council Member Jacob believe City-wide code should not be changed for the benefit of one property
owner. He would rather see a variance or some other application for the specific property. Council
Member Pack had the same concern. He enjoyed serving on the Board of Adjustments for 10 years
and suggested there may be other considerations that should be taken into account for something
that would apply beyond the subject property. Council Member Pack believed looking at a City-wide
change may make sense if a proliferation of requests were coming in. He had no problem with the
specific property obtaining what was asked for.

Vice Chair Green felt that if the request was already allowed for residential properties in the City,
he did not see a big deal allowing it for those in a manufacturing zone. He wondered if a property

extra 10 feet back. Council Member McConnehey said he believed the cost to property owners in the
form of lost usable space outweighed the potential cost to the City to relocate a fence if a road were
expanded in the future. He said he would rather err on the side of property owner rights. Council
Member Bloom said she agreed with Council Members Green and McConnehey.
