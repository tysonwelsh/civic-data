           A memo from the City Finance Director regarding a proposed amendment to the Fiscal Year (FY) 2025-2026 budget
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated City Administration is proposing to allocate $8 million from the
Capital Improvement Fund to three projects: $5 million for the acquisition and demolition of the county jail building, $2.5 million
for Union Station Museum and campus improvements, and $500,000 to General Park improvements for an all-abilities park. The
$8 million is from ARPA funds the City received and had to encumber by December 31, 2024.
           Assistant Financial Director Jensen summarized the memo and stated the City received a total of $29.6 million in federal
ARPA and state ARPA funds. The Council had previously approved the appropriation of approximately $20 million in previous
actions. The Council approved a budget amendment on December 17, 2024, to appropriate the remaining $9,042,825 of ARPA
funds. In that budget action, the Council approved an ordinance reducing public safety wages and replacing them with the ARPA
funds and in turn placed $8 million of the offset into a restricted CIP account. Any use of funds from this account would need
additional Council action. The additional $1,042,825 from the ARPA funds was allocated to Lester Park improvements. While the
$8 million was set aside with the budget action on December 17, 2024, the Fiscal Year 2026 budget included the appropriation of
the funds to the three projects discussed with the initial budget amendment proposal in December 2024. Though included in the
budget, the proposed allocation was not discussed in detail during the review of the budget. With the approval of the Fiscal Year
2026 budget on June 17, 2025, the Council amended the final Schedule A-1 document to remove the allocation of the $8 million
for the three projects and place the funds back into the restricted CIP fund. The work session on July 1 allowed the Council to
discuss the proposed use of funds as initially proposed in the Fiscal Year 2026 budget.
           Council member Blair asked if the funding for the Marshall White Center scholarship is the first allocation for that
program. Management Services Director Stout answered yes, and Mayor Nadolski stated Administration continues to work to
secure funding that will make it possible for more people to use and access the Center, while using a membership-based approach
to the use of the Center.

Proposed Ordinance 2025-22 amending the budget for Fiscal Year 2025-
2026 by adjusting the staffing document to add five unfunded firefighter
positions
          A memo from the City Finance Director regarding proposed amendments to the Fiscal Year (FY) 2025-2026 staffing
document came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated City Administration is proposing to add five unfunded
firefighter positions to the staffing document; these additional positions will provide the Fire Department with more flexibility in
the hiring process and enable proactive hiring in anticipation of known or expected vacancies. Adding the positions will have no
fiscal impact on the Fiscal Year 2025-2026 budget.
          Assistant Finance Director Jensen summarized the memo and Fire Chief Mathieu discussed operational impacts of the
proposed amendment; the ability to complete an advanced hiring process when a vacancy is anticipated or expected will provide
the Fire Department with the staffing flexibility that is currently supporting the Ogden Police Department. This flexibility will
support Ogden Fire Department’s efforts to maintain full operations in both the Fire Operations and Medical Divisions.
          Council member Richey asked if there is some reason that he is proposing five positions. Chief Mathieu stated that he
would typically propose three positions since the Fire Department has three shifts, but creating five new positions will make it
possible to spread the positions across the three shifts with a greater impact. He feels the number of positions is ample and he
would not recommend greater than five in order to avoid overextending the Department financially.
          Council members expressed their support for the proposal and thanked Chief Mathieu for taking a conservative approach
to this matter.
           Chair White introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2025-22, entitled:
                    “An ordinance of Ogden City amending the budget for the Fiscal Year July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026 by
                    adjusting the staffing document as detailed in the body of this ordinance. the ordinance neither increases or
                    decreases the anticipated revenues or appropriations of the budget; and by providing that this ordinance shall
                    become effective immediately upon posting after final passage.”
          Chair White then opened the public hearing.

amazing and she hopes that will always be the case. She encouraged the Council to support this proposal.

Public Comments
          Laura Lewis inquired as to the status of the renewable energy program the City was pursuing with other cities and Rocky
Mountain Power. She added she visited the City’s water treatment plant recently and was very impressed with the operations and
the competency of the staff. She also appreciates the Green Waste facility and the services offered there; the staff is very helpful,
and the facility is very clean. She noted she likes the idea of improving Lester Park as discussed during tonight’s work session
meeting. The City should focus more on public amenities that are free of charge rather than economic development that will
financially burden residents. Quality of life is very important for residents. She asked if the pickleball courts at Lester Park will be
lit. She addressed the idea of amending the Rules and Norms to address the amount of time a person can speak during a public
meeting; she suggested development of guidelines that will ensure impartiality. She concluded she recently saw a marketing
campaign where Ogden City was referred to as a ‘spillover option’ for the Park City market; real estate in Park City is very
expensive and Ogden was listed as a more affordable locale for those in the market to purchase a home. This is concerning to her
because she is worried about current residents being able to afford homes.
          Linda Sanchez stated she was out of town recently and heard of a fire on the City’s bench; she was reminded of Council
member Graf’s comments in a recent meeting about the risk of fire in that area and what the City should be doing to protect
residents. She extended her thoughts and prayers to the family that lost their home in the fire and encouraged the City to revisit the
issue of fire restrictions. She relayed her own personal experience of living in a town that shot fireworks from an area where the
embers would fall into a lake as an effort to mitigate risk of fire. She noted that she appreciates and agrees with Ms. Lewis’s
comments.
           Malik Dayo addressed looming United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) raids in the community. He
is present to advocate for those at risk of being impacted by the raids; residents fear family separation, arrest, or being forced to
leave their homes. This does not only address undocumented immigrants, but documented United States citizens as well given that
documented citizens have been arrested in mass raids. He is not asking the City to protect criminals or those in the country
illegally, but he is asking for leadership that provides clarity. City Administration should stand against federal overreach and the
use of United States military force in Ogden City. He encouraged vocal opposition from the elected leaders of the community to
communicate that Ogden prioritizes its residents over federal actions. He asked the City to take immediate action to implementing
a “know your rights” campaign; this would include distributing multi-lingual information material giving people information
about legal support. He stated ‘the City’s hands are not tied’ and last week he respectfully addressed a point made by the Mayor
suggesting that if any rights are violated during federal enforcement, legal action can be taken afterward. While he agrees that
people can seek justice after an incident, he believes it is more important to prevent rights violations from occurring in the first
place. He suggested proactive measures by the Mayor and City Council.
           Heath Satow echoed Mr. Dayo’s comments and asked for clarity regarding the role of the Ogden Police Department in
the ICE raids or response to those activities. He also addressed the consideration of amending the Council Rules and Norms
specific to public comment; this would only be imposed in events when many people are in attendance, but that is problematic
because most often when there is a large turnout at a meeting, most of the people in attendance have never attended a meeting and
the fact that they are present signals that an issue is very important to them. Limiting their ability to provide input is concerning
and will only discourage people from participating in the future. He concluded by addressing the proposed plans for the Lester
Park improvements and suggested a different color scheme that is not as bold/bright.
           Debra Darrington stated the process to access the Landmarks Commission is very difficult; she and her architect are not
sure how detailed project drawings must be in order to appear before the Commission. She has spent upwards of $8,000 on her
design but is still not sure if it is sufficient to receive approval from the Commission; however, she cannot afford to wait another
month in the event that the documents are rejected or tabled. She asked for a liaison that could help answer those questions. She
then stated she loves preserving historic buildings, but some can be very costly and time-consuming as has been proven with the
buildings at the old Ogden Stockyards. She relayed her own personal experiences in trying to install landscaping on a historic site
and suggested that it would be helpful for the City to waive some permits or costs associated with restoring historic properties.
           Travis Pate echoed Ms. Darrington’s comments about the importance of providing some flexibility relative to historic
preservation; additionally, the timeline for the Landmarks Commission to consider a historic project is too strict and includes
several conflicts for applicants and the City. He asked that the Council carefully consider this matter and help to streamline the
process and remove conflicts of interest.
           Teresa Bramwell stated that there was previously a police substation in the area of 25th Street and Monroe Boulevard and
there was much less crime in the area than there is today; it has been well proven that there is an enormous amount of crime in that
area and a restored police presence in that area would be helpful. She then noted she recently contacted a commercial real estate
agent that claims to be the best in the area, controlling 40 percent of the commercial market in the community. She presented to
him the options that she feels she has for her property, but he stated he feels those options are bad and the best option is to
demolish the building and sit on vacant land for many years before it is acquired by someone else. She does not agree and thinks
that is a terrible idea; she is looking for different uses or tenants for her property.
           Chair White stated the Council and Council staff will attempt to answer some of the questions asked during the public
comment period; she addressed the question about the renewable energy program with Rocky Mountain Power and noted it is her
understanding that project has stalled. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reported Rocky Mountain Power has issued a
request for proposals (RFP) seeking a developer to build a solar farm in the State to support the program. There are also some
proceedings before the Public Services Commission that should conclude before the end of the year; this includes negotiations of
cost to participate in the program.
           Chair White thanked residents for their input regarding fireworks restrictions and noted the City continues to work with
State lawmakers on the issue as local governments have restricted authority in decisions related to fireworks. She thanked Ms.
Darrington and Mr. Pate for their input regarding the processes that involve the Landmarks Commission and asked that City
Administration take those matters under advisement. She thanked Ms. Bramwell for her input and noted she has forwarded an
email from Ms. Bramwell regarding the use of her property on to the Community and Economic Development Department. She
commented on the input regarding ICE raids and referred the public to the input provided by Police Chief Sube during the July 1,
2025, work session meeting. She appreciated the suggestion from Mr. Dayo regarding a ‘know your rights’ campaign. She
concluded that the real estate campaign referring to Ogden as a ‘spillover option’ did not come from the City, but from a private
realtor.

Mayor Comments
         Mayor Nadolski stated that he has concerns about fire risk on the City’s benches; he commended the Police and Fire
Departments for their response to the recent fire on the east bench. He reiterated Chair White’s comments that the City continues
to work with the State Legislature to push for more control of fireworks at the local level. City Administration is working on a
plan to address fire risk over the July 24 holiday. He then thanked the Council for their support of the budget items on tonight’s
agenda; the allocation of funding for a scholarship program for Marshall White Center will increase access to the facility and
allow the City to keep rates lower for residents than non-residents. He also referenced the renewable energy project and stated
City Administration is waiting for a response from Rocky Mountain Power; there were several questions and concerns regarding
the program, and he will not recommend proceeding with the project until those questions are answered. He added he does not
know if the pickleball courts at Lester Park will be lit. Council member Graf stated Public Services Director Anderson actually
reported the courts will be lit during tonight’s work session meeting. Mayor Nadolski then acknowledged Ms. Lewis’s comments
about the importance of free public spaces and amenities; he agreed those things are important, but economic development is also
important, and it is necessary to pursue a balanced approach to development of the community. He also referenced Ms. Lewis’s
comments about marketing Ogden as a ‘spillover option’ for Park City; he noted he actually interpreted the campaign as
referencing Ogden Valley, not Ogden City. He does not view Ogden City as being similar to Park City. He thanked Mr. Dayo for
his comments; he did not like how things happened over the past weekend in terms of ICE raids; he is mindful of his words and
his ability to address fear in the community. He is not entirely sure of the definition of the term ‘federal overreach’, but if there are
instances of the federal government acting illegally, the City can respond. If overreach is simply something that some do not agree
with, the City cannot respond. He has not seen anything illegal occur in Ogden City and he will not take steps based upon
something different happening in other communities. He will make it clear that he expects laws to be followed in Ogden City and
until that happens, he will direct the Police Department to ‘stay in its lane’. He stated the Police Department is not participating in
enforcement of ICE policies. He acknowledged Ms. Darrington’s comments about appearing before the Landmarks Commission;
this is an issue that has been brought to his attention in recent days, and it has become clear to him that it needs his attention.
Therefore, he will be publishing an advertisement seeking new members of the Commission soon. He invited Ms. Darrington to
stay after tonight’s meeting to discuss the matter further with himself and Chief Administrative Officer Brown.
