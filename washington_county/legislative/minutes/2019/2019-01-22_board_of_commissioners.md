County Attorney Brock Belnap explained that the Drug Task Force has been in operation
for over twenty (20) years, and is a great tool to efficiently investigate and prosecute
controlled substance abuse offenses throughout Washington County.

Washington County Sheriff Sergeant Danny Kroff presented Drug Task Force cases from
January 1, 2018 through present day. The Washington County GIS Department has put
together several interactive web maps, highlighting where each case is; how drugs are
distributed throughout various jurisdictions; and where overdoses occurred within the

County.

The Task Force is primarily funded through grants and is a beneficial resource to the
County due to various resources available throughout multiple jurisdictions.

Mr. Belnap mentioned that the prescription database can only be accessed with a warrant.
He also explained that with the addition of Proposition 2, the law was revised by the
legislature in order to facilitate the proper management and regulation of the use of
marijuana within the community.

CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE _ NO._2019-1129-O, AN ORDINANCE
AMENDING AND SUPERSEDING WASHINGTON COUNTY CODE TITLE 1
CHAPTER 14, SECTIONS 2, 5, 6, AND _7 IN ORDER TO DEFINE AND
DESIGNATE __THE BOUNDARIES _OF__ ELECTION __ PRECINCTS _IN
WASHINGTON COUNTY

Mr. Hafen explained that election precincts cannot have more than 1,250 voters. As a
result, several election precincts needed to be modified, in order to facilitate smooth and

effective elections.

Proposed boundary adjustments included: splitting SG45 and adding SGS0; splitting SG39,
SG41, and SG46, and adding SG51; splitting HU60 and adding HU61; and modifying
WA64 and WA65 to prepare for additional growth.

Additionally, Mr. Hafen explained that several of the school board members’ district
boundaries were adjusted as well.

CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION _NO._R-2019-2423, A RESOLUTION
AMENDING GENERAL FEES FOR SERVICES FOR THE WASHINGTON

COUNTY PUBLIC WORKS DEPARTMENT

Public Works Director Ron Whitehead explained that the fees for services performed by
the Public Works Department was updated to reflect current prices for equipment and
material rates. These fees are charged to municipalities for services performed by the
County, which is done on a case-by-case basis.

Three (3) criteria must also be met in order for the towns to obtain County assistance which
are: the size and resources of the town; the road’s impact on area tourism and economic
development, and the road’s proximity to a county-maintained road. Town roads used
primarily for residential use are always the responsibility of the town.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Almquist to Adopt Resolution No. R-2019-
2423, A Resolution Amending General Fees for Services for the
Washington County Public Works Department. Motion seconded by
Commissioner Iverson and carried by unanimous vote, with
Commissioners Cox, Iverson and Almquist voting aye.

CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION _NO._R-2019-2427, A RESOLUTION
AMENDING THE BOUNDARY OF THE HURRICANE VALLEY FIRE SPECIAL
SERVICE DISTRICT TO INCLUDE THE AREA WITHIN THE ROCKVILLE-
SPRINGDALE FIRE SPECIAL SERVICE DISTRICT

Clerk/Auditor Hafen explained that sixty-one (61) signatures were received as a part of the
protests, and 57 were counted because they were from registered voters or property owners.
The protests represented ten point twenty-two percent (10.22%) of the number of votes
cast in Rockville and Springdale for the office of the President of the United States at the
most recent election and five point twenty-five percent (5.25%) of the total private land
and four point nine percent (4.9%) of the value of all private real property within Rockville
and Springdale. The protests received were insufficient to statutorily prevent the proposed
amendment.

Deputy County Attorney Eric Clarke further explained that if amended, a winding down
period will take place in order to ensure the proper transfer of assets from the Rockville
Springdale Fire Special Service District (RSFSSD) to Hurricane Valley Fire Special
