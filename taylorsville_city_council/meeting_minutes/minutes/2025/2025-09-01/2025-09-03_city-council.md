3.2 WASATCH FRONT WASTE & RECYCLING DISTRICT — Pam Roberts
General Manager/CEO Pam Roberts began her report by expressing pride in her leadership team’s
succession planning preparation. The board of trustees appointed Evan Tyrrell on August 25th
following the interview committee's recommendation, with Roberts praising Council Member
Barbieri and the rest of the board's "stellar" selection.

Evan Tyrrell said he felt privileged to continue Ms. Roberts! 18-year legacy. He detailed his dual
graduate degrees in public affairs and environmental science, previous service as Solid Waste and
Fleet Services Director for Flagstaff managing residential/commercial collection, regional landfill,
household hazardous waste facility, and recycling transfer operations. His experience as Executive
Director for Grand County's Solid Waste Special Service District included managing collection,

Ms. Roberts then highlighted the District's positive public presence goals, detailing participation
in Taylorsville's annual cleanup with the Green Committee. The District participated in the
Taylorsville parade and Taylorsville Dayzz, maintaining an active Facebook presence with
sustainability coordinator James Kelsey posting recycling education content.

Service statistics showed Taylorsville comprised 16% of the district's 86,000+ customer base.
January-July 2025 comparisons revealed landfill voucher usage had decreased slightly, potentially
due to QR code implementation preventing non-resident abuse by landscapers and construction
workers. Ms. Roberts praised the self-service portal for can replacements, with Chair Harker
confirmed next-day service for her cracked recycling can replacement. Ms. Roberts emphasized
that the 5-7 business day service standard was often exceeded, highlighting in-house customer
service with live phone and chat support.

Diversion statistics showed Taylorsville achieved 16% waste diversion through green waste,
curbside recycling, and glass collection at both the central liquor store on 2700 West and through
curbside service. The city had over 1,600 green waste subscribers and 121 curbside glass
subscribers. A District-wide 19% diversion rate exceeded the 18% goal. Disposal costs were $37
per ton for garbage and $40 per ton for recycling-- one dollar less than in 2024.

Ms. Roberts addressed bulk cleanup container challenges, reiterating that the change was
necessary due to CDL driver shortages. Despite budgeting for 10 seasonal drivers, they started
with ten but ended with only seven. For 2025, the board considered shifting resources from the
underutilized trailer rental program to add a full-time position guaranteeing 60+ containers daily
for Taylorsville. She noted that delivering 63 containers daily "is a lot."

Council Member Burgess praised the current system as being more controlled, safer, less messy,
and keeping materials in driveways rather than on streets. His neighborhood reported no
complaints, considering it "a better way to do it." Ms. Roberts explained the priority reservation
system. giving previous year's wait-listed residents first booking access, early access for non-
participants, and general reservations for previous recipients. Statistics showed 1,200 containers
delivered over 19 days from 1,935 requests, with $34,000 in disposal fees. Despite having fewer
drivers, 2024 numbers nearly matched 2023.

Before and after photos illustrated safety improvements eliminating street piles from illegal
dumping and preventing dangerous container climbing. Roberts stated container street placement
would never resume due to these issues. When managing 120 street locations required twenty CDL
drivers plus 8 ground crew, current driver shortages made that impossible.

Ms. Roberts provided historical context, crediting Taylorsville as the catalyst for creating the
administrative control board in 2010 when Mayor Wall pushed for the four major cities
(Taylorsville, Herriman, Holladay, Cottonwood Heights) to have "seats at the table." The board
formation helped her navigate district management after starting three years prior. Cities pushed
for separation leading to Wasatch Front Waste & Recycling District's January 1, 2013
establishment, with employees helping design the logo and select the name.

Ms. Roberts concluded her report by praising current Taylorsville leadership as being the "right
people in the right seats" and Mr. Tyrrell as the "right person" to assume leadership of WFWRD.
The council expressed extensive appreciation for Roberts’ years of service and education about
behind-the-scenes operations making "trash disappear" through significant effort. Council
Member Barbieri pointed out the high level of employee satisfaction she was responsible for and
acknowledged how much they would miss her guidance.
