Proposed Resolution 2025-12 amending the budget for the Fiscal Year July
1, 2024 to June 30, 2025 by increasing the anticipated revenues and
appropriations for a gross increase of $1,033,000
          A memo from the Finance Director regarding a proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 budget amendment came before
the Board for consideration. The memo stated the proposed budget amendment totals $1,033,000 and will increase the FY 2024-
2025 Redevelopment Agency budgets for the Airport ($480,000), Kiesel ($63,000), and Adams (140,000) project areas, and the
Redevelopment Agency Housing Fund ($350,000). Appropriations are for contractual services, corridor and environmental
studies, arts programming, and the Quality Neighborhood program, respectively.
          Finance Director Sorensen summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the
proposed budget amendments; he provided a spreadsheet identifying the total revenues and appropriations involved in the budget
amendment and identified the source of funds for each revenue. He also briefly summarized the intended use of the new funding
as follows:
               • Hinckley Airport Project Area – terminal expansion improvements project;
               • Kiesel Project Area – Wall Avenue Corridor Study from the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) and
                   Union Station Environmental Assessment from Utah Transit Authority;
               • Adams Project Area – Arts/Area programming;
               • Redevelopment Agency Housing Fund – Quality Neighborhood Project – asbestos and demolition of 2325
                   Madison Avenue.
          For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Chair White introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2025-12, entitled:
                   “A resolution of the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency amending the Budget for the Fiscal Year July 1, 2024
                   to June 30, 2025 by increasing the anticipated revenues for a gross increase of $1,033,000 from sources as
                    detailed in the body of this resolution; and increasing the appropriations for a gross increase of $1,033,000 as

          Chair White then opened the public hearing.
          Linda Sanchez stated it seems odd to her to allocate funds into certain areas and then to disburse them to other areas; as a
taxpayer with a private budget, she chooses to fund some things and to not fund others or to participate in certain activities. She is
not supportive of high-density housing and asked if any of the Redevelopment Agency budget adjustments are related to a high-
density project. She has observed the City’s budget process and believes the City is wasting a lot of money while herself and
others are ‘just barely scraping by’; her first thought when she saw the proposed budget amendments on this agenda was how this
would increase her own taxes. She asked if there are partnerships between the City and other entities that will benefit both partners
while financially burdening the citizens. She asked the Board to think of opportunities to save money; she questioned why the City
of Ogden needs seven elected officials when it may be possible to get by with three or four based upon the City’s size. She stated
sometimes the feels the City Council is a ‘rubber stamp’ body and she misses former members who would stand up for the
residents that elected them. The budget process is not supposed to be easy, and she encouraged the elected leaders of the City to
do more questioning when these types of issues are brought before them.
          Heath Satow stated that in last week’s Redevelopment Agency meeting, he raised a concern about an overlooked aspect
of tax increment financing (TIF), specifically how it impacts long term residents. When he mentioned that increasing property
values can result in a tax increase for fixed income residents, the Board discussed his concern a bit but concluded that the matter is
complicated. He noted, however, that there is a fairly easy way to understand it; even with the State’s property tax formula, which
is intended to adjust downward to offset inflation, property owners can and will pay more when their property value increases
faster than the City average. The explicit goal of Redevelopment Agency project areas and TIF is to increase property values in
targeted areas and people who own property in those areas will see their property values increase higher than the rest of the City.
Residents in and near a Redevelopment Agency area will experience a tax increase even without an official vote from the City
Council to increase the certified tax rate. This is especially beneficial to private investors who buy and sell properties, but for long
term residents on fixed incomes, the invisible tax increases can create a huge hardship and he believes the government has a duty
to present the benefits and drawbacks of Redevelopment Agency project areas; while a project area can eventually boost revenues
for the City and improve neighborhoods, they can also displace residents who have invested years into the community. He would
be willing to discuss these issues further with any Board member; there are other approaches that will allow the City to pursue
development while protecting those who helped build the community.
