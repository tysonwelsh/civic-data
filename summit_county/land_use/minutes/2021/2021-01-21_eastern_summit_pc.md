2.  Public hearing and possible action on potential amendments to Section 11-6 of the 
Eastern Summit County Development Code. The purpose of the amendments is to 
provide permitting regulation for both commercial and private campgrounds in 
Eastern Summit County - Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner shared the background for this application. The proposed language for 
commercial and private campgrounds was discussed. Commercial campgrounds are for 
profit and are prohibited within a platted subdivision. Private campgrounds could be three 
spots or more, have hookups and amenities on-site, are not for profit, and are also

prohibited within a platted subdivision. Exemptions include: an agricultural operation; 
storage of licensed, unoccupied RVs; and incidental use by the household on private 
property. The required criteria and items to submit a campground operation include: water 
and wastewater addressed; the campground must stay under one ownership; setbacks are 
met; recycling, garbage and trash collection are provided; fire protection addressed; limits 
of 14-day stay within a 30-day period. 
Commissioner Wheaton asked if a specific lot size needs to be included in the criteria. 
Does it matter how many separate times a month the campground is used? Planner 
Milliner stated it is popular to camp for a weekend and he will clarify the language. Chair 
Sargent stated that an RV can remain unoccupied and it would be difficult to enforce any 
stay limitations. 
Commissioner Rydalch asked if there should be a seasonal timeframe. Planner Milliner 
answered that campgrounds would be part of a Conditional Use Permit review. RV use can 
have a seasonal timeframe, but generally the snow dictates the time of use. Commissioner 
Clyde stated that hunting season needs to be allowed. Chair Sargent stated that the 
timeframe could regulate itself by default and would be tough to enforce. Situations such as 
a family reunion or gathering of more than three campers should have an exception. 
Commissioner Rydalch stated that incidental uses need to be better defined. 
 
The Public hearing was opened.  
Jayson Kesler stated that the Planning Commission should consider including a time 
period limitation for stored or occupied use on private property.

Janis Miller stated that there is a lot of creativity in the Samak Hills platted subdivision. 
Are the setbacks consistent for private storage? Some people do not adhere to property 
lines. There are some neighborhood concerns for exemptions for a small lot. Clarification 
needs to be made about private roads. A seasonal window does not negate many concerns. 
There needs to be consideration for the wear and tear that will occur on County roads. 
Brooke Simpson is newer to the area as a full-time resident and realtor. It is surprising to 
find that a single-family residence neighborhood is being considered for campground use. 
There are campers in the summer. There is contention for who gets the right-of-way on 
private roads in the neighborhood. There are more humans than cows and Samak Hills 
should be left as a neighborhood. 
Brandon Bowers stated that the language in the Code talks about an individual owner and 
an LLC should not be considered one owner. A dump ticket should be created for water and 
sewage. There should be a permit to apply for larger gatherings. 
Steve Parkinson owns a cabin and some family members bring up a trailer in the spring 
and take it back to their own property in the fall. The family should be able to leave a trailer 
on their property for the summer. 
Paul Battaini clarified that private campgrounds are prohibited within a subdivision but 
asked if seasonal use is still allowed within a subdivision. How does this affect permits for 
development for seasonal use? Enforcement needs clarification. Samak Hills is a platted 
subdivision, so neither personal nor commercial use is allowed in Samak Hills. Planner 
Milliner answered that campgrounds are not allowed, but personal RV use would be. 
People living in trailers without sewer and water is considered in the language.

Don Green stated that no trailer owners are attending this meeting. There should be a 
better way to notify those trailer owners of the meetings. 
Denise Kesler stated her main concern is how to handle those who are already living in 
trailers in a platted subdivision and how those trailers affect the asset value of the 
neighbors and how they are damaging the environment. 
Kayli Roller asked if there is a junk ordinance in the works and when a draft of that 
ordinance will be complete. It is important to include a trash collection fee for those who 
are not permanent residents. All full-time owners pay for trash collection. There needs to 
be clarification between annexing lots versus owning multiple lots. 
The Public hearing was closed. 
 
Planner Milliner stated the proposed amendments are written to allow someone to leave 
a trailer unoccupied on their lot for the summer. If setbacks cannot be met then the use is 
not allowed. The Code defines an LLC as a person/individual and would be allowed 
ownership. There are different ways of enforcement, including an Administrative Law 
Judge and the Health Department. Notice cannot be given to every person, so residents 
should watch the newspaper and the Summit County website. Contact Planner Milliner 
with any individual concerns. The Planning Department is working on a Junk Ordinance 
and the draft should be ready in April or May. The Junk Ordinance should address many of 
the concerns in the Samak area. Those currently living in RVs will be addressed on a case-
by-case basis, but the new Code language will apply moving forward once the changes go 
into effect. Any two lots side-by-side will have double capacity of one lot, annexed or not.

Chair Sargent stated the proposed amendments to the Code are an effort to balance the 
health, safety, and welfare of all residents. It is hoped that HOAs will step up and regulate 
their platted subdivisions to help with the concerns of the residents. 
Commissioner Sonntag stated that property rights include use and occupancy. There is a 
concern that historic uses will be taken away. For those with enforcement concerns, the 
County does not have someone driving around looking for violations. Enforcement is 
operated on a complaint basis. It would be a full-time job just for enforcement in the Samak 
area. Chair Sargent stated there are some grandfathered rights. 
Commissioner Clyde stated that people are not dealing with reality. The Samak area is 
zoned for grazing land. All the lots of concern are non-conforming. Trying to fix Samak 
seems futile. Commercial campgrounds fit in the RC (Recreation Commercial) zone, not 
grazing zones. Enforcement seems impossible. The proposed amendments do not address 
the complaints in the Samak area. There is not enough sense of what the neighborhood 
wants. This is a gentrification issue. Samak is becoming a suburban Deer Valley. People are 
looking for an affordable lot, but trailer use is not compatible. There is an incidental use 
problem, which addresses RVs and not tents. Areas similar to those around Moab and Zions 
need to be considered for learning how to fit something between a commercial 
campground and a personal yurt. There are pages of definition of manufactured housing to 
use. The 100-foot setback should be enough to prevent a campground in cabin 
neighborhoods but may drive an applicant to the Board of Adjustment. A smaller setback 
makes sense. It feels like expectations in a neighborhood like Samak are different from 
those like Jeremy Ranch.

Commissioner Rydalch stated the concerns are overwhelming, but the proposed 
amendments are a good starting point. The setback requirements and the water and 
wastewater regulations are good. The idea of conditioning a campground on a zoning 
change would involve the Planning Commission, which would be good. 
Chair Sargent stated that the grandfathering issue needs to be fleshed out. There should 
be an exception for family reunion gatherings once a year. RC zoning could be appropriate 
for campgrounds. At least one more meeting is needed to discuss the proposed 
amendments. 
 
 
Commissioner Rydalch made a motion to continue this item to a future meeting to 
allow Staff to take the comments from this meeting and put forth a revised ordinance. 
An additional Public Hearing should be held. Commissioner Wheaton seconded the 
motion. 
• MOTION CARRIED (5-0)

1. Discussion Regarding the Eastern Summit County General Plan Amendments – Ray 
Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Director Putt stated the Planning Commission is being asked for a general high-level 
direction on the Mission Statement and land use element to better reflect the conditions 
that Eastern Summit County is dealing with now.

Chair Sargent stated he would like to see more word description of the small-town rural 
character. Projects should help support the current small-town rural character.  
Commissioner Clyde agreed that small-town character needs to be defined. There is a lot 
of focus on agricultural preservation, but it is not economically successful anymore. The 
biggest issue is infrastructure. Affordable housing without sewer and utilities provides no 
way to increase the density and decrease prices. Improving infrastructure drives growth, 
which needs to be deliberate and up front. Funding is needed for infrastructure. Traffic and 
congestion issues are not adequately addressed. 
Commissioner Wheaton stated it is not the County’s responsibility to protect private 
property rights. Sustainability on all levels such as environmental and transportation 
should be included. 
Commissioner Sonntag stated there needs to be a specific mission for affordable housing 
and housing on all levels. Infrastructure is needed. 
Commissioner Rydalch stated that there needs to be a balance between economic 
diversity and protecting the rural feel of Eastern Summit County. All kinds of economic 
diversity should be included, not just second-home buyers and transplants. 
Director Putt stated that good language can be inserted to refer to subsequent elements. 
Infrastructure and sustainability will be addressed. The last change to the Mission 
Statement moved away from protecting agriculture. Language will be included to address 
growth and the need for more housing. Adjustments will probably have to be made. 
Chair Sargent stated that rural design principles should be addressed. Smaller paragraphs 
with bullet point supporting statements make the Mission Statement easier to read.
