2.              Truck Ranch; 9204 South 5600 West; Amended Site Plan; SC-3 Zone; Truck
                Ranch/ Jadon Wagner(             applicant) [#    28362; parcel 26- 02- 426- 003]


Larry Gardner said that the Truck Ranch preliminary site plan and subdivision plat were approved by
the Planning Commission last year, but due to economic constraints the site has been redesigned. The
lot size has been reduced to 6. 7 acres and lot 2 was enlarged. There is no change to road dedication on
the plat. The building looks very similar, but the second story was removed. Staff wanted to bring this
change to the Commission so they were aware and to have them reapprove the changes. Most of the
10, 000 square feet that were removed are with the second story, but the footprint was reduced by about
one bay due to the change in road location. The final site plan is being reviewed by staff.

3.
         Golden Healer Service Dogs; 1902 West 7800 South; Conditional Use Permit for Pet
         Boarding and Veterinary Service; SC- 2 ( ZC) Zone; Golden Healer Service Dogs,
         Inc./Mike Carlson ( applicant) [# 29830; parcel 21- 27- 377- 025]



Mike Carlson, owner and founder, said they are a non-profit and have outgrown their facility in Draper.
Their service dogs are for every type of disability except for seeing eye dogs. He explained the model
of the business. Their business started in 2019 and they now provide therapy dogs and emotional
support animals with training and education for the community. With the increased cost of travel and
the need for more volunteers, they have decided to open this therapy dog center where people come to
them. The proposed space has already been utilized for dog boarding, so it has some ready amenities.
They prepare dogs to become facility dogs to provide emotional support and comfort to those in crisis
situations. They are also partnering with facility dogs for alcohol and drug treatment centers. They hope
to bring groups into the center, such as first responders, veterans, and schools.

Mr. Carlson responded to questions from the Commissioners. Fees will be charged to visit the center
and to work with therapists. There are currently about ten dogs in training for service work, but they are
looking to have more. There are 15 on the therapy dog team that includes dogs at their own facility and
volunteer therapy dog handlers located throughout the state. Regarding any state or federal regulations,
he wasn' t aware        of
                               any. And there are no required                certifications or credentials.

Mr. Carlson explained that there won' t be staff overnight at the facility at this time. However, there will
be someone at the facility until 10 p.m. when the dogs are let outside for the last time. There will be
security cameras to be able to check on the dogs. After a busy day of sessions, the dogs are exhausted
and will sleep well all night. Even at a typical boarding facility with dogs unfamiliar with each other,
there is no overnight staff.


He said there will be minimal outside activities right now, but that could change as the business grows.
They try to have only one dog at a time go outside, but puppies will usually go out in groups. Currently
they have limited staff, so he would take out as many as possible at one time. There will always be one
or two handlers in the yard when the dogs are out. He has been able to manage 15- 20 dogs by himself
in a residential area without complaints from the neighbors. They also hope to help people train their
own therapy dogs.

Mike Carlson said they breed their dogs and get some from a kennel in Arizona. He explained the
difference between service dogs and therapy dogs. He pointed out the outdoor area on the north side of
the building that will be fenced. He said that there will not be excessive barking, and he did not
anticipate any complaints due to barking. Waste is cleaned up immediately, is put in a container, and
the container is put in the trash. The Animal Welfare Act requires 10 square feet of space per dog, so
the boarding area would allow for up to 120 dogs. Parking requirements were reviewed.

Tayler Jensen clarified that when the notices were mailed it included a request for indoor recreation, but
that is no longer being considered and is not needed. He reviewed the site plan and the fenced outdoor
area on the north. A neighboring property owner was concerned that the outdoor area might be on the
south end. The parking code requires one parking stall for 250 square feet of boarding area, which
would be five stalls. Seventy- four parking spaces are provided, which would allow the business to grow
quite a bit before meeting that number. If the veterinary service use grows, it could take a large amount
of the available parking.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission approve the Conditional Use Permit for Golden
Healer Service Dogs, located at 1902 West 7800 South in an SC- 2 zone, with the conditions and City
Code Requirements listed in this report.


Conditions and Requirements of Approval:

effects of this use. If the Planning Commission disagrees with Staff' s analysis and/ or identifies any

George Winn thought that noise would be a minimal issue, but he asked if that would be handled by
code enforcement if it did become a problem.


Tayler Jensen said yes.


Ammon Allen appreciated that the business is small at this time and is looking to grow over time in the
same building. He asked how that expansion is going to be supervised at a staff level and if inspections
are performed.




Tayler Jensen said the animal services division will do periodic checks to make sure the facility is
providing a healthy environment for the animals. Those inspections will help to identify any conditions
that need attention. He also explained that the veterinary services might not come on board at this time,
but the applicant wanted to be up front in saying they would like to have those services in the future.

Mike Carlson said a boarding facility typically does not have a veterinarian on site. They have one on
their board and she has agreed to check the dogs every month for good health, but she will not be on
staff full time. However, they have talked about offering vet clinics from time to time.

Ammon Allen opened the public hearing.

Paul Brockbank, West Jordan Fire Marshal, said this was a former education building that has a full fire
suppression and fire alarm system that will be inspected annually, which will provide protection for the
dogs when no staff is present.


Ammon Allen noted that the Commission had received an email from resident Jeff Judge concerning
noise abatement.




George Winn felt that the noise issue had been adequately addressed.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.


Jay Thomas   asked   if each dog needs   to   be registered   and/ or   licensed   with   the city.

was his understanding that it was not required.

Tayler Jensen said     City Requirements#   1 states they have to comply with all city department
requirements. So, if animal control determines licenses are needed then they would be required.
