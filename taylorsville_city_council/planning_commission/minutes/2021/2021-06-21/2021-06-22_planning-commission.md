                                          BRIEFING SESSION – 6:30 P.M.

1.         The briefing session to review the Agenda was conducted by Mr. Jim Spung. The Agenda consisted of
     a Zoning Map Amendment, File #11Z21, by Brad Reynolds Construction for properties located at 4701 S
     1065 W.; 1054 W. 4800 S.;1066 W. 4800 S.; 1078 W. 4800 S.; and 1088 W. Turnberry Way from R-1-10 to R-
     1-8 and R-1-6, which was previously heard by the Planning Commission on May 25th, 2021 and received a
     negative recommendation to the City Council. It was brought back by the developer with substantial
     changes for reconsideration. Mr. Spung gave a summary of the changes. It would be all single-family
     dwellings with some zoned R-1-6 and some R-1-8 size lots. The originally proposed number of homes has
     been reduced by 19, which is substantial and would not require a General Plan amendment because it would
     then be in compliance. Staff has reviewed the request and found it to be consistent with General Plan and
     long-term plans for the City. It also would provide much needed housing. The developer feels this is a good
     compromise to meet the most needs. Commissioner Russell asked what else was different besides the
     reduction in the number of homes. Mr. Spung advised one main difference was that the R-1-8 lots all abut
     the existing surrounding R-1-10 homes. Commissioner McElreath asked how many of the R-1-8 lots are
     actually closer to being R-1-10 and Commissioner Willardson answered that four of them are 10,000 sf or
     better and there are 14 that are 6,000 sf. Mr. Spung reminded the Commissioners that tonight’s review was
     not for the subdivision but rather the zoning only. Commissioner Burggraaf said that if the zoning is
     approved by the Planning Commission and City Council, then the subdivision would come before the
     Planning Commission for approval. Commissioner Burggraaf received a communication from Commissioner
     Quigley in his absence that said he was in favor of the revised proposal of the developer to convert that
     vacant land into single family homes which are designated R-1-8 and R-1-6 lots. That he trusts that the
     Commissioners will make a wise decision.

     Commissioner Burggraaf asked to hear the City Council meeting attendance discussion at this time.
     Commissioners who attended the most recent City Council meetings briefed on what transpired during their
     assigned meetings. Discussion most notable for the Planning Commission was the City Council upheld the
     Planning Commission negative recommendation regarding the proposal by Cowboy Partners for a General
     Plan amendment for the property located at 1758 West 5400 South from Regional Commercial to High-
     Density Mixed-Use as not being an appropriate use for this particular site. Also discussed was the transfer of
     funds under CDGB to begin upgrades of the Carriage Square commercial center and also a separate upgrade
     of the Center Pointe Master Plan with a long-term vision for that whole center.

                                          ZONING MAP AMENDMENT


 3.    11Z21 - Recommendation to the City Council for a Zoning Map Amendment for properties located at 4701
       S 1065 W.; 1054 W. 4800 S.;1066 W. 4800 S.; 1078 W. 4800 S.; and 1088 W. Turnberry Way from Single-
       Family, R-1-10 to Single-Family (R-1-8) and Residential Single-Family (R-1-6). (Jim Spung/AICP/Senior
       Planner)
3.1       Mr. Spung presented this item.           The original zoning map amendment application requesting to
          rezone the property from R-1-10 to RM-10 and R-1-6 was filed with the City on May 7, 2021 and was
          presented to the Planning Commission on May 25, 2021. The Planning Commission forwarded a
          negative recommendation to the City Council based on public comment and concerns related to
          amending the General Plan map. The applicant has now amended the application to address
          neighborhood concerns and Planning Commission comments and is requesting the revised application
          be re-considered by the Planning Commission, pursuant to the Taylorsville Planning Commission Policies
          and Procedures (Section 4, page 7): which says “Reconsideration of Applications: An application which
          has been denied by the Planning Commission may be reconsidered by the Commission if an applicant
          presents new evidence which the Chair and Community Development Director determine is sufficient to
          merit reconsideration of the application. A request for reconsideration shall be made in writing and
          filed within ten days from the date of the original decision. If a request for reconsideration is granted,
          the Commission shall reconsider the application in the same manner as an original application was
          considered.”

          3.1.1    Mr. Spung continued that the review tonight is related to the zoning designation for the
                   subject area and does not include anything to do with the subdivision. The images to be shown
                   this evening are for reference only. The subdivision will be reviewed at a later date via a public
                   hearing before the Planning Commission.

          3.1.2    The project involves 8.5 acres which are currently owned by one owner and is a farming site,
                   which includes an accessory building on the site used for that purpose. The site is bordered by
                   single family dwellings on and south and east sides and mobile homes to the north. It is
                   currently zoned low density residential. The requested change would include establishing R-1-
                   6 zoning to the north and R-1-8 zoning to the south.

          3.1.3    Staff recommends approval of the project submitted tonight for reconsideration.

3.2       FINDINGS:
          3.2.1  This application was initiated by Paul Randstrom, Brad Reynolds Construction.

         3.2.2     The subject property is made up of nine (9) individual parcels with a cumulative area of
                   approximately 8.54 acres.
         3.2.3     The subject property is zoned Single-Family, R-1-10.
         3.2.4     The subject property is in the Low-Density Residential designation in the City’s General Plan.
         3.2.5     On May 7, 2021, the applicant filed a request for a Zoning Map amendment for the subject
                   property from Single-Family Residential (R-1-10) to Single-Family Residential (R-1-6) and Multi-
                   Family Residential (RM-10).
         3.2.6     On May25, 2021, the Planning Commission forwarded a negative recommendation to the City
                   Council for the zoning map amendment request, based on public comment and concerns
                   related to the City’s General Plan policy.
         3.2.7     On June 1, 2021, the applicant submitted a request to amend the original filing.
         3.2.8     The applicant’s intended development plan includes 17 single-family lots with a minimum lot
                   area 6,000 square feet (R-1-6) and 14 single-family lots with a minimum lot area 8,000 square
                   feet (R-1-8).
         3.2.9     The applicant’s intended development plan requires the subject property to be rezoned to
                   Single-Family Residential (R-1-6) and Single-Family Residential (R-1-8).
         3.2.10    The applicant’s intended development plan and zoning map amendment request are
                   consistent with the City’s adopted General Plan.
         3.2.11    City Staff supports the applicant’s request.
         3.2.12    The City Council is the decision-making authority for a Zoning Map amendment request and
                   may adopt or reject the amendment as it deems appropriate pursuant to Section 13.05.030
                   and other applicable sections of the Taylorsville City Code.

3.3      STAFF RECOMMENDATION: Staff recommends the Planning Commission reconsiders the motion made
         on May 25, 2021 and send a positive recommendation to the City Council to amend the zoning Map
         from R-1-10 to R-1-8 and R-1-6, as identified in Exhibit F (which is a map of the proposed zone change of
         the area in question), based on the Findings as outlined in this Staff Report.

3.4      APPLICANT ADDRESS: Brad Reynolds                  expressed appreciation to the Planning Commission for
         taking the time to look at the revised application. He advised these are challenging times with building
         costs being at all time high levels, especially lumber which has risen 320%. That was the main reason
         the original application included townhomes. However, in order to accommodate concerns expressed
         by the Planning Commission and neighbors previously, the project concept has been changed
         eliminating the townhomes and reducing the number of homes being proposed from 49 to 31 units,
         with the square footage varying between 8,000 and 13,000. This proposal now complies with the
         existing General Plan of low density residential, so amending the General Plan is not necessary. He felt
         this will be a great product for the community and still provide affordable housing. Commissioner
         Wright asked him what the price range will be and Mr. Reynolds said they were anticipating starting at
         $675,000 to $700,000. Commissioner Russell asked when he anticipated construction would begin and
         was informed by Mr. Reynolds that it would probably take 1 ½ months to move the cattle off the site.
         Then they plan to start as soon as all approval steps have been completed.
