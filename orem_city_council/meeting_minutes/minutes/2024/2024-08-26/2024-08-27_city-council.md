400 E 1600 N Phase | Concept and Estimate Presentation Presenter: Taggar Bowen, City
Engineer and John Dorny, Traffic Engineer

Mr. Bowen presented about the 1600 North 400 East intersection. In 2018, the City
applied for funding from the Mountainland Association of Governments (MAG), This
intersection has the Murdock Canal Trail come through it and many users will cut diagonally
across the intersection. This project was awarded $1.5 million from MAG. JUB was hired to
give project alternatives. They originally provided 12 alternatives to the Transportation
Advisory Commission (TAC). TAC was able to narrow the alternatives down to five. JUB
prepared information for those 5 alternatives. The five alternatives were A) directional
crosswalks; eliminate all right turn lanes, place landscape features to separate directional

pedestrian ramps, realign trail prior to intersection and add bulb-outs; B) re-route trail to about
350 East and cross 16000 north; use HAWK signal with bulb-outs and include signal timing
adjustments; C) grade separated crossing at the intersection under 40 East and 1600 North; D)
single lane roundabout; and E) Pedestrian scramble with directional pedestrian ramps.

Communication Survey Results Presentation Presenter: Peter Wolfley, Communications Division

Manager and Ron Gailey Onpointe Insights

Mr. Wolfley introduced Ron Gailey from Onpointe Insights. Mr. Gailey will send a link
to the City Council with the full results of the survey. Onpointe conducted a communication
survey from August | to August 27, 2024. A total of 854 surveys were completed. Mr. Galey
showed the demographics of those who completed the survey.

The survey asked how informed are the residents and the majority were somewhat
informed and a large majority would like to be informed. The survey asked residents what
communication methods are you aware of. The digital newsletter, website and City Council
Meetings had a large percentage, but Facebook, the printed newsletter, Instagram and YouTube
had much smaller numbers. The next question asked how frequently the different types of
communications were used. Seventy percent of those surveyed, who were aware of the
communication, use the digital newsletter but all other types of communication were used much
less. The survey showed that if a resident is aware of different types of communication, most
marked they would use that type of communication.

Mr. Gailey discussed with the City Council some of the options that the City may want to
pursue from the results. For example, if the City were to text links to different types of
communication then more residents would probably look at that communication method. The
results gave information about the types of content the citizens would like to see. City events,
City Council updates and City services were the highest preferences. He reviewed comments that
were collected from the survey. Discussions were held about the possibilities that could come
from this survey.

Public Hearing — Ordinance - Enacting Section 22-11-67 PD54 (Canyon Park Tech Center
velopment) Z ndi he Orem City Code, and amendin ticle
22-5-3(A) and the zoning map of the City of Orem by changing the zone on approximately
24.3 acres from the PD6 zone to the PD54 zone located generally at 1203 North 630 East.
Presenter: Gary McGinn, Acting Development Services Director/Assistant City Attorney

Mr. McGinn explained that this project is a rezone of the Canyon Park Tech Center area.
The request is to change part of the tech center to residential housing. He showed maps of the
area and the residential zones in that area. This area is a little over 23 acres of land. The proposal
has 150 lots and was presented to the planning commission. Some of the issues discussed in the
planning commission were density, parking, and traffic. Mr. McGinn showed images of the floor
plans and elevations that are being proposed.

Steve Waldrip, the governor's housing policy adviser for Utah, discussed the critical issue
of affordable housing in the state. He emphasized that the lack of affordable housing could
hinder Utah's growth and future development, citing projections that by 2030, Utah will need
200,000 more homes. Despite Utah’s attractiveness and growing population, housing production
has decreased, exacerbating the housing deficit. This shortage has driven up prices, making Utah
the third least affordable state in the U.S.

Mr. Waldrip highlighted the broader societal impacts of this issue, emphasizing that
homeownership is key to building generational wealth. He pointed out that the wealth gap
between homeowners and renters is staggering, with homeowners having a median net worth 40
times greater than renters. This gap is a major concern for the future of the state and the
well-being of its residents. Waldrip framed affordable housing as not just a policy or economic
issue but a moral one, stressing the need for bold, intelligent decisions to ensure that future
generations can achieve the American dream. His closing remarks called for change in how
housing is approached, recognizing the challenges but also the necessity of taking action to
address them.

Mayor Young raised questions about affordable housing and the community surrounding
this area. He was grateful for Mr. Waldrip and would like to continue conversations on

revitalizing areas of Orem with the governor’s office.

Mr. Zack Hartman with Land Advisors, he is representing the owners. He explained that
the demand for office space has declined and the plan is to convert part of the office space into
residential housing. Originally the proposal was more high density housing and that was not well
received by the public. The second proposal was 170 units which was also not well received.
This current proposal comes from TriPoint homes which is represented by Brian Prince.

Mr. Prince began the process by holding a neighborhood meeting and the proposal
included 230 lots with a mix of townhomes and single family homes. The feedback was for more
single family homes. The second neighborhood meeting presented the current proposal of 150
lots and only single family homes. These would be homes in the range of $700,000. Mr. Prince
had a traffic impact study completed and has worked with City staff to work to accommodate
traffic and street connections. The current proposal is a 24 acre boundary.

City of Orem by rezoning the property located generally at 1230 West 2000 South from the
OS5 zone to the PD55 zone (approximately 9.43 acres). Presenter: Gary McGinn, Acting

Development Services Director/Assistant City Attorney

Mr. McGinn explained that this is a rezone of the area generally known as 1230 West
2000 South. Currently this area is an OS5, which is a holding zone with limited development
options. This property is in the extreme south west of Orem near the planned Lakeview Parkway.
The proposal is to change this zone to a PD55, which is community commercial. Mr. McGinn
showed the proposed plan of development for this area that was shown to the planning
commission. He went over the details of the differences between a C2, C3 and PDS55 zone. He
answered questions from the City Council about the different zones.

Mr. Paul Washburn is the developer on this project. He gave a history of the property. He
spoke about the C2/C3 zone and the reason they decided on a PD55 zone. He explained that they
are bringing this to the council now to be inline with the construction of the Lakeview Parkway.
The City Council asked questions about the intersection and future road development. Mr.
Washburn explained that the drawings are conceptual but a gas station is planned in the
development. He gave a description of the types of businesses that would be attracted to this
area.
