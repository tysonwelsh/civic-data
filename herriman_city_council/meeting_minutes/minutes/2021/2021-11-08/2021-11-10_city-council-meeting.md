    2.1 Consideration for a Modified Roadway Cross Section for Springbok Drive - Jonathan
        Bowers, City Engineer
City Engineer Jonathan Bowers oriented the Council of the proposed Herriman Dental Office who submitted
a land use application and noted staff had been reviewing plans to prepare for Planning Commission review.
He offered a background of the proposal and observed the two access locations: off a shared access from
Anthem Boulevard and 2) off Springbok Drive, which served as the Timbergate Community private road.

Engineer Bowers highlighted the 50-foot public access and noted the standard would require all streets
conform to the City typical roadway cross section; otherwise, a deviation to the standard would need to be
approved by City Council after receiving a recommendation from the City Engineer and Planning Commission.
He suggested an alternative to the 53-foot standard cross section, the applicant proposed a cross section which
would fit within the exiting access easement. A second option could be to consider the removal of the proposed
second access off Springbok Drive. Engineer Bowers indicated Unified Fire Authority would approve the
modified cross section with the additional requirement to have no parking signs be installed on one side of the
road. Engineer Bowers acknowledged the potential implications on the application for the adjacent property
to develop as it showed access through Springbok Drive.

The Planning Commission recommended approval of the proposed modified cross section with the
requirement to install a six-foot sidewalk on the south side of the road. Council consensus determined the
second access with the modified roadway cross section was the best alternative to the situation.

    2.2 Traffic Committee Discussion and Update - Bryce Terry, Staff Engineer
Staff Engineer Bryce Terry offered a background of the Herriman Traffic Committee monthly meetings to
discuss issues and concerns regarding traffic and transportation. In 2021, approximately 60 complaints and
concerns were discussed for speeding, crosswalks, visibility, traffic signal and stop sign requests, and traffic
flow pattern concerns.

Engineer Terry offered an overview of the speed complaint process to determine if improvement was
warranted which could include awareness campaigns, driver speed feedback signs, increased enforcement, bult-
outs choke points, improve signage and striping and mini-roundabouts.

Engineer Terry reviewed crosswalk concerns and noted the problematic situations which arise due to crosswalk
requests that ultimately do not meet the standards for stop signs or other traffic control devices. He explained
all the solutions needed a standard for consistency. Adding new crosswalks needs to show demand for the
improvement as well as its proximity to existing crossing. Possible solutions could include increased signage
and striping, rectangular rapid flashing beacons, and pedestrian hybrid beacon or HAWK signal. Alternative
solutions currently being explored include painted crosswalks, distinct surface crosswalk, raised crosswalks, in-
road bollard signs, and pedestrian islands.

Engineer Terry observed issues with driver visibility as they pullout from intersections and explained sight
distance triangles have been set by the American Association of State Highway and Transportation Officials
November 10, 2021 – Herriman City Council Minutes                                         Page 3 of 11
and are based on the speed limit of the roadway. Generally, traffic signals would be installed based on four
factors (1) delay of vehicles on minor road (2) vehicle safety issues (3) pedestrian safety concerns (4) traffic
optimization within the network. Storm signs are installed to assign the right-of-way based on traffic volumes
or road classifications, vehicle safety issues, or pedestrian safety issues.

Engineer Terry explained many of the warrants are national recommendations based on studies of how to
optimize safety and efficiency. Installing improvements without proper warrants could create more safety
hazards than it would solve. He added this would help implement the correct solution for the situation. When
installing improvements outside of the nationally recognized recommendations would allow for higher risk of
litigation including rear-end accidents at an unwarranted traffic signal or a pedestrian being hit at an
unwarranted stop sign. Engineer Terry noted the warrants indicate when improvements should be considered
as not every intersection should have a traffic signal but provide guidelines as to when they would be deemed
appropriate. It was the responsibility of the City to provide equitable treatment for the entire community.

City Manager Nathan Cherpeski cautioned the Council to consider expanding beyond warrants.
Councilmember Henderson indicated there should be a process to determine if it would be warranted.
Engineer Terry agreed and suggested publishing information to help the public understand the process to
determine if an improvement was warranted.

Councilmember Shields noted the data provided in the analysis would not identify the number of near misses
and could make it difficulty to quantify if an improvement should be incorporated. He wanted to look at
observational studies in areas as the lack of actual accidents did not mean it was not a dangerous area.

    8.5. Discussion and consideration of the Hamilton Well Repair and Redevelopment Proposal
        - Justun Edwards, Public Works Director
Public Works Director Justun Edwards explained water department staff observed the presence of sand being
pumped from the Hamilton well. This is typically caused by damage to or wearing of the screened sections of
the well casing. After consultation with city staff and well experts, it was determined the best course of action
was to continue to operate the well through the summer and make the repair through the winter. Staff
implemented mitigation efforts to minimize the risk of increased damage to the well casing and pump. Through
the remainder of the summer and into fall, the City procured an engineering firm (AE2S) to assist the contractor
selection process and consultation of the appropriate repairs for the well and procured a contractor to perform
the repair and redevelopment of the well. The contractor was selected through a request for qualification
November 10, 2021 – Herriman City Council Minutes                                    Page 8 of 11
process. Due to the unknowns and complexity of the project, it was determined the Construction
Manager/General Contractor (CM/GC) contract would be the best process for this project. This process
allows the CM/GC to be involved in the design and risk mitigation process of the project to determine the
best course of action.

The project was divided into three separate phases. (1) Review information to identify most likely causes of
failure. Determine best course of action to mitigate risk and successfully repair and redevelop the well. The
CM/GC would provide a guaranteed maximum price to complete phases two and three. The City executed a
contract with the CM/GC for this phase of the project. The contracted amount for this phase was $15,400
and has been completed.

Phase 2 would obtain required permits and approvals through Division of Drinking Water, contractor mobilize
to site, pull well motor and pump, camera well casing to identify any visible damage or cause, perform Dual
Swab inspection of well to identify exact location(s) of damaged or worn well screen. Lastly, Phase 3 would
allow for the performance of repair of the pump and motor, repair the well screen, camera repaired areas,
chemical treatment, and the redevelopment of the well, reinstallation of the pump and motor, start-up and
recommissioning of the well.

The project completion date was scheduled for April 15, 2022, so the well would be operational prior to the
high demand season. Director Edwards anticipated the final project cost would be less than $607,883 but there
was a high level of risk with the unknown associated with performing work several hundred feet below ground.
He included a 30-percent project contingency to account for those.

Councilmember Smith asked about the age and lifespan of the well. Director Edwards responded the well was
commissioned in 2004 and explained this was a premature failure and recommended the Council approve.

Councilmember Smith moved to approve the proposal received from VanCon in the amount of $607,883 to repair and redevelop
the Hamilton Well. Councilmember Henderson seconded the motion.

The vote was recorded as follows:
Councilmember Jared Henderson           Aye
Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn              Aye
Councilmember Steven Shields            Aye
