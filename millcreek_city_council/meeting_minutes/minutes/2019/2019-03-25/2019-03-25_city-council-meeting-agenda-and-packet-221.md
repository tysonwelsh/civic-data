from the County. The new General Plan outlines a lot of goals and strategies to accomplish

ordinance. She showed a map to the Council of where signs are allowed in Millcreek. The

responses and the second got 220 responses. The second survey was sent to 300 business

square feet, and window signs in the M zone be allowed at a 50% coverage. Mayor

some businesses use it as a design element and not as an advertising sign. Council Member
Catten asked if the proposal included paint on windows. O’Kelley said paint on the

size limit on the window. O’Kelley said they could finesse language that incorporates that.
Council Member Marchant said it seemed like staff was creating a solution where there was
not a problem. Lilly said the Millcreek Community Council thought the proposed 50%
coverage was too onerous. Lilly said South Salt Lake has a restrictive ordinance on window

3. Discussion on Traffic Calming Policy; John Miller, Public Works Director
John Miller said Millcreek has been following best practice management on traffic calming.
He said he had made updates to the policy since the version he submitted in the meeting
packet. Miller went over the draft policy. He said there are different physical devices and non-
physical methods to reduce speed. The Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices says that
stop signs will not be used as a means to control speed. The policy addresses an “affected
neighborhood” which shall be defined as all properties located within one-thousand feet of
any traffic calming device as well as other streets that may be impacted. There are
qualifications for a traffic calming plan development in the policy and Miller went over two.
He said a traffic speed study must show that speeding is a problem based on the standard of an
85" percentile speed of at least 6 mph over the posted speed for minor local roads and streets
and 11 mph over the posted speed for major local roads and streets. A study may be
conducted if 90% of residents in the “affected neighborhood” file a petition. Miller said he
copied policy suggestions from Summit County. The application process provides three
different ways when Millcreek Public Works will collect traffic data, 1) when requested in
writing by the community council representing the area, 2) when requested in writing via
petition by at least 60% of an “affected neighborhood” of any city road or street segment in
the City Road Inventory, and 3) when requested by the Millcreek City Council, the Public
Works Director, or the Unified Police Department. After the application is submitted, a traffic
study will be done, a non-physical element will be implemented, and a 6-month period of
evaluation of the non-physical measure will take place. Mayor Silvestrini clarified that staff
may try to do something non-physical first before installing a physical method. Council
Member Uipi asked about resources for evaluations. Miller said enforcement was one way to
evaluate, so are flashing signs, and a pace car program.

Council Member Uipi asked how requests are prioritized. Miller said if a request comes from
a resident, staff would encourage them to go to their community council, but there is an
application to do it themselves to gather petition signatures. He said the criteria for traffic
calming plan ranking on a point scale are speed, volume, sidewalks, and crash history. A
traffic calming project selection which receives a 67% or greater approval from the “affected
neighborhood” are presented to the City Council as a consideration. He said installation of a
temporary test measure of the proposed feature for a period of one month would require an
investment on the City’s part in those resources. The Mt. Olympus Community Council
brought up that they want residents to be able contribute money for traffic calming projects.
Miller said for project evaluation, traffic devices can be recommended for removal if the City
Engineer determines it is unsafe or if 2/3’s of the neighbors no longer want it. Mayor
Silvestrini asked about a time limit for the residents to change their mind. Miller said it is six
months depending on weather conditions. Miller went over the associated forms in the policy.

1.2 Unified Police Department Officer of the Month for February 2019

Chief Steve DeBry said he wanted to let the Council know about the Mental Health Unit
and about the great work that they do. He introduced Sergeant Jody Sampson to the
Council. Sergeant Sampson said the Mental Health Unit was started 18 months ago. The
model came from the Los Angeles Police Department. She said they respond as a
secondary unit and have had 800 outreaches since November 2017, and 3,540 mental
health related calls which does not include welfare checks or domestic calls. She said the
foundation of the unit is what they do with the case after the call, they do follow-ups. The
unit consists of 1 fulltime detective, 8 secondary officers, and 1 Licensed Clinical Social
Worker (LCSW). The unit responds during and after crisis and are proactive so call
volumes drop. Council Member Uipi asked how one LCSW can handle all the calls.
Sergeant Sampson said she prioritizes calls and does research on cases she sends the
LCSW to. The LCSW is employed by the University of Utah and works with Unified
Police on a memorandum of understanding (MOU) which will end this summer. Mayor
Silvestrini said as a member of the Unified Police Department (UPD) Board, he will
support refunding the MOU. He then relayed an example in which the mental health
unit’s services came into play in his neighborhood. Sergeant Sampson said the last
resource for their clients is the judicial system. Mayor Silvestrini thanked Sergeant
Sampson for her work. Council Member Uipi said there will be a Suicide Prevention
Night on April 11" with Granite School District. She requested feedback on how to get
parents to attend. Sergeant Sampson said she and Council Member Uipi could get
together.

Chief DeBry presented Detective Doug Lambert as Officer of the Month for February
2019. Detective Lambert worked on and cleared multilevel and multi-state fraud cases.
Mayor Silvestrini said Detective Lambert has worked tirelessly on identity theft crimes

3.1 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 19-06, Amending the Employee

Policy and Procedure Manual
Laurie Johnson said the proposal was for minor changes to the employee handbook as
discussed at the work meeting one week ago. She said it changes the standard work
week from midnight on Saturday to noon on Friday which allows for flexible
schedules for 9-hour days and every other Friday off. The other change is to increase
the maximum accrual of hours of paid time off (PTO) to 320 hours to allow for short-
term disability or other uses. She said not every employee will retain that much, but it
gives them the option. Mayor Silvestrini said this does not affect the hours Millcreek
City Hall will be open, which are Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. — 5 p.m. He said it
will help with recruitment and retention of employees and will be on an honor
system. Johnson said the Human Resources Department will meet with every
department to discuss the details but hope to kick it off by May 1. She said employees
do not have to choose the flexible schedule. Mayor Silvestrini said it does not put the
City in jeopardy with liability on PTO. Council Member Uipi asked about a leave
bank policy. Johnson said in the first year of employment if an employee’s leave bank
goes into a deficit, they can have leave approved by the City Manager.

Council Member Uipi moved to approve item 3.1. Council Member Catten seconded. The
City Recorder called for the vote. All Council Members voted yes. The motion passed
unanimously.

more training for code enforcement officers. He read it into the record:
“Be it resolved by the Legislature of the state of Utah:
WHEREAS, code enforcement officers provide an essential service by helping to maintain the
standards and dignity of our communities;
WHEREAS, recent tragic events have highlighted the fact that many code enforcement
officers enter dangerous situations in the performance of their duties;

WHEREAS, due to the function they serve and the risks they encounter, code enforcement
officers deserve the respect and dignity afforded to all public servants who

regularly risk their own safety for the betterment of our communities, along with the resources
they need to assure their own safety; and

WHEREAS, in response to the need for continuing education of code enforcement

officers, certain nonprofit organizations such as Utah Ordinance Compliance Association and
American Association of Code Enforcement provide access to education, training, and
resources that could increase the safety and awareness of all code enforcement officers:
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that the Legislature of the state of Utah

strongly encourages code enforcement officers to become familiar with organizations and
resources that provide training to increase situational awareness, recognize and understand
personal property rights, develop professional conduct, and aid them in all other duties
associated with their position.

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the Legislature of the state of Utah encourages

local municipalities to adopt ordinances and policies to greater facilitate the training and
protection of code enforcement officers, including but not limited to the resources cited in this
resolution.

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that a copy of this resolution be sent to the Utah League

of Cities and Towns and the Utah Association of Counties for distribution to all their
constituents. ”’

4.5 Unified Fire Authority Report

Chief Woolsey said the one paramedic to one EMT transition has been going for a month
and it has gone well. Unified Fire Authority (UFA) is looking at data to see if the change
has captured the advanced life support calls. The recruitment camp is halfway through.
He said they typically lose more recruits in the first couple of weeks due to fitness
conditions, so this year they set up a physical training program in advance to assist in
recruitment and as a result have not lost any recruits due to lack of fitness. He said there
will be 26 firefighters who are going to complete camp in May. The three fire stations in
Millcreek ran 335 calls for the month of February; 63 fire and 272 medical. Millcreek
Station 101 was the highest responding station. He said Unified Fire Authority is doing
Fire School 101 and offered enrollment to the Council. He said UFA has been doing a
media push to get qualified candidates for recruitment. The recruitment testing process
will start in August and another camp will be next spring. Chief Woolsey said there are
over 400 people on the list right now. He then presented a poster of 2018
accomplishments to the Council. He also mentioned there was a recent fire in Alta and all
Millcreek units responded.

Mayor Silvestrini said the Finance Committee with Unified Fire Authority decided to add
an increase to salaries last year and he hoped that helped with recruitment this year. UFA
is working on the rebuild of Station 112 on Jupiter Drive. They are looking to acquire
more property for parking on site within their regular budget. Station 112 is prioritized
first for replacement. The new model of UFA stations has a more modest footprint which
shows they are responsible for taxpayer dollars. Mayor Silvestrini has authorized
Millcreek’s planning department to initiate a city sponsored rezone for the property
where the station sits to assist in eliminating a setback next to the shopping center.

4.4 Unified Police Department Report

Detective Jared Nichols presented February 2019 crime statistics to the Council. His
presentation had graphs that showed calls for service trends and response times by
priorities from the last five years during the month of February. Another graph showed
the UCR crime view overall trends in February which showed an increase in family
offense, homicide, and robbery and a decrease in assault, burglary, kidnapping, larceny,
sexual offense, and stolen vehicles this year. Mayor Silvestrini requested annual numbers
be presented in the future that are not month specific. A graph showed a large trend
increase in citations this year which Detective Nichols attributed to the Community
Crime Suppression Unit that are proactive in enforcement. Millcreek Street Crimes
served two knock and announce search warrants at two locations and on several vehicles,
which involved a drug distribution case. The result of the warrants produced evidence of
distribution of narcotics, a recovered firearm, and four arrests. A search warrant was
served at a house on Quail Park Drive which resulted in recovered stolen property and
drugs. Several people were arrested from that and the residence was closed to occupancy
by the Health Department. UPD cleaned up transient camps under 3900 S. 300 W. and
250 West Central Avenue. They are launching a school bus safety initiative on March
28", Millcreek was approved for a grant from DPS GEARS which will focus on
distracted drivers and pedestrian safety. This will include 30 (4 hour) shifts for the month
of April. Detective Nichols said the UPD has a new app that can be obtained through the
app store or UPD’s website. The Council thanked Detective Nichols for his work.
