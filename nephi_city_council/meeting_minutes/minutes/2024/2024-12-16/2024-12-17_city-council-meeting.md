ORDINANCE 12-17-2024 SUBDIVISION CODE ADOPTED:

     City Administrator Seth Atkinson reviewed with the council Ordinance 12-03-2024, an ordinance that revised Title 10 and Title 11 land use, subdivision, site plan, and development code.  Revisions to the code were needed to meet the Utah State code requirements. 
     Mr. Atkinson stated that the law clearly delineated between legislative and administrative actions.  The law stated that plan review for a subdivision was an administrative action which should be handled by a city’s staff.  The city code that created the requirements for the subdivision and the city’s General Plan would fall under a legislative responsibility.  The state law also revised the review process for applications and set time frames for those reviews.  It streamlines the process for developers but removes the responsibilities for approval that the Planning Commission and City Council currently possesses.  It also removes the need for public hearings on applications that meet the city code.  These public hearings have often been misunderstood by the public and became forums to simply disagree with a development occurring nearby rather than to point out where a development may or may not have met the city code.  This change would encourage public input at the legislative level when a code is being proposed or altered, at the time of a zoning change, or when the city’s General Plan is being updated or amended.  
     Mr. Atkinson added that once the review process is complete, a developer’s plans may be approved as final by the Development Review Committee (DRC).  This may expedite a developer’s plan review if it closely adheres to the city’s code upon its first submission.  The review process may repeat four times but could be completed prior to that if it complies with the city’s code. The DRC will have primary responsibility for plan review and approval.  The proposed code changes currently have the appeal authority as a third-party independent entity.  This could be a land use attorney or an attorney from another city. In addition to the changes made in the subdivision code, the revisions consolidate the areas of the code regarding subdivisions and plan reviews into Chapter 11 of the city code.  Chapter 10 will now focus mainly on land use.  Mr. Atkinson said that staff will keep working on refining the code as time goes on. 
     Councilor Skip Worwood moved to adopt Ordinance 12-17-2024-Subdivision Code.  Councilor Jeramie Callaway seconded the motion.  The motion passed on the following roll call vote.

ANNEXATION AREA POLICY PLAN AND MAP UPDATES APPROVED:

    Mr. Atkinson updated the council that the Planning Commission had approved and had held a public hearing about revising the Annexation Area Plan and map.  One of the items that had been requested from property owners for the purposes of annexation was a concept plan.  A concept plan has been a useful tool for the analysis required by the Annexation Area Policy Plan but has not officially been included in the Annexation Area Policy Plan.  Staff had updated the Annexation Area Policy Plan to include the following paragraph:
“To aid in an informed decision regarding expansion of the city’s municipal boundaries, concept plans to show intended uses and a potential street layout must be presented to the City Council.”
    Mr. Atkinson also stated that staff thought it was time to also update the Annexation Area Map. Several new areas on each side of the city area were recommended to be added to the Annexation Area Map as a Level 3.  The map showed the proposed new areas in the lighter color of orange.  There were also a few parcels on the north and south ends of the city that are being recommended to be made a Level 2 and subject to the analysis found in the Annexation Area Policy Plan.
     Councilor Skip Worwood moved to approve the recommended changes to the Annexation Area Plan and map.  Councilor Shari Cowan seconded the motion.  The motion passed unanimously.

CRIMSON FLATS AGREEMENT APPROVED:
   Mr. Atkinson explained to the council that the development agreement presented is a new agreement with Wright Direction LLC to replace an earlier agreement the city entered with them in July of 2022.                 The Crimson Flats development would be the construction of a baseball/softball field complex and a commercial subdivision.  The city went out to bid, per the original agreement, to construct the infrastructure for the subdivision earlier this year.  Based on the bids received, both parties felt that it was in their best interests to restructure the agreement in order to achieve a more efficient cost on the subdivision construction.  The agreement outlines this new arrangement with The Wright Direction, LLC.  The new agreement will supersede the previous agreement.  
     The new agreement specifies that The Wright Direction will now primarily be responsible for the costs of infrastructure development and the City will contribute funds to this aim.  In exchange for these funds, the City receives property for a large detention pond, improvements to a lot still owned by the city, improvements to a lot reserved for the construction of a police facility, and an off-site water line installation.  
   The agreement was presented to the City Council for review at the public work session on 12/3/24.   It was previously recommended that the agreement be presented in two public regular meetings prior to adoption to ensure that the agreement was transparent to the general public.  Due to favorable weather conditions and an available work crew, it is now being recommended for action by the City Council at the 12/17/24 meeting.  Mr. Atkinson stated that the available work crew can take advantage of the favorable work conditions and move forward.  The agreement is still public record and can be requested by review of the public at any time.  
     Councilor Callaway said that last year's more severe winters slowed progress on the project, and he thought the city should take advantage of the milder weather conditions so that construction could begin. 
Councilor Slip Worwood moved to authorize the mayor to sign the agreement with The Wright Direction, LLC for the Crimson Flats Subdivision.    Councilor Jeramie Callaway seconded the motion.  The motion passed unanimously. 

AWOS MAINTENANCE CONTRACT WITH DBT APPROVED:
      Mr. Atkinson updated the council that the project to install the new Automated Weather Observing System (AWOS) was almost completed.  DBT was the winning bidder on the contract, and they have also been the company who has maintained our AWOS system for many prior years.  They have proposed a new 3-year maintenance contract for the AWOS system.  
    The city has received quality service from DBT in the past and they have been very responsive in the AWOS installation project.  Their familiarity with the AWOS system and our airport makes them a great fit for ongoing maintenance.  They will also warranty their work on the new AWOS system for two years.  The contract will now include maintenance for a new service that will post the AWOS data on the internet for pilots to view.  This is a service that was not available in the past. 
     Councilor Shari Cowan moved to authorize the mayor to sign the three-year AWOS Maintenance Contract with DBT.  Councilor Callaway seconded the motion.  The motion passed unanimously. 
AGREEMENT WITH CHAD LEDY - ROLLIN’ HOME RV PARK OWNER- APPROVED:
    Mr. Atkinson explained to the council that Chad Ledy is the individual who had recently purchased the Rollin’ Home RV Park.  As part of that purchase, the property was subdivided.  With the subdivision came requirements for road improvements along the frontage of the RV Park lot.  The improvements, along Four-Mile Road, are not ready to be implemented at this time due to uncertainty over whether or not to improve the condition of the road and where the gutter system would be installed along the east side of the road.  Due to this uncertainty, the city has allowed Mr. Ledy to provide a cash payment for these improvements.  With the payment, Mr. Ledy would like to have some assurances that his frontage obligations are met with his cash payment.  
     Mr. Ledy proposed an agreement to achieve these aims.  City Attorney Wright made some changes and those are reflected in agreement.  Mr. Atkinson advised the council that they were under no obligation to sign the agreement.  The agreement will allow for a time extension for Mr. Ledy to fulfill his obligations from development and specifies what the fee-in-lieu payment will cover.  Mr. Ledy would be required to pay 125% of the cost of the improvements which would be around $208,000.
     Councilor Skip Worwood moved to authorize the mayor to sign the agreement with Chad Ledy for the Rollin’ Home Subdivision.  Councilor Jeramie Callaway seconded the motion.  The motion passed unanimously.
