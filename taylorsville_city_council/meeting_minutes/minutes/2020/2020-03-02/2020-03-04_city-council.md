2.      APPOINTMENTS

           2.1 Advice and Consent of Taylorsville Committee Chair Appointments
               – Tracy Cowdell

Tracy Cowdell explained that there were many Taylorsville residents who volunteered their time
by sitting on any number of committees created by city ordinance. The Staff wished to improve
the way in which committees were organized to be more along the lines of what was spelled out
in City Code or conversely, amend the code to be more aligned with current practice. He
recommended that the Code remain the same and if that was acceptable to the Council, names of
potential chairs would be brought to the Council at a future meeting, generally in the form of a
single resolution.

Mr. Cowdell pointed out that this did not apply to the Planning Commission, Taylorsville Dayzz
or the Arts Council which were not committees.

Staff would attempt to do a better job of working with the committees and following what was
outlined in City code. Mayor Overson agreed that the desire was simply to formalize the process
of naming committee chairs and to do so on an annual basis.

Councilmember Burgess inquired if he, as the advisor of the Green Committee, should explain to
the committee that they were welcome to choose a chair but to understand that the Mayor would
formally recommend a chair to the City Council. Mayor Overson agreed that was the intended
process.

            3.2 Unified Fire Authority Report – Captain Richard Rich

Captain Rich presented statistical information regarding the final quarter of 2019. He asked that
the City Council let him know what type of information they wished to receive in future reports.
He was also interested in any feedback they wished to provide regarding report format.

Call volume had remained consistent during the quarter, as had the general type of call taking
place in the City. Taylorsville was UFA’s second busiest community, just behind Millcreek
City.

He and his staff were conducting statistical analysis in order to boost call response time.
Employment offers were extended to 33 new recruits who were currently in training. The Public
Information Officers were following the recruits and documenting their progress.

UFA crews would soon begin their wildland refresher courses as Spring approached.

Captain Rich described a call received from a Taylorsville resident on Christmas Eve wherein
someone needed assistance moving and assembling a hospital bed in their home. The fire crew
responded, moved the old bed out and helped to install the new bed. He explained that was just
one example of some of the many times that his crews went above and beyond.

He expressed appreciation that some of his crews had the opportunity to participate in Read
Across America Day at Arcadia Elementary

Councilmember Harker expressed appreciation to Captain Rich and his crews for the good work
they did on a regular basis.

5.      PLANNING MATTERS

            5.1 City Center Open Space Update - Mark McGrath

Mark McGrath updated the City Council regarding the status of the City Center open space,
explaining that the development design of the project had been completed.

Councilmember Cochran returned at 7:36 pm

The primary objective was to create a community gathering place that was cohesive with the
existing City Hall.

Mr. McGrath presented various slides showing different aspects of the project. The lobby of the
performing arts center would include materials like those used in the main lobby of the Eccles
Theater in downtown Salt Lake City. A great deal of emphasis had been placed on the primary
entrance which would be off 5400 South and include water features, landscaping and signage.
There would also be a boardwalk, skating rink and shaded patio area among many other outdoor
amenities.

Included was a playground that might have to be value engineered out, although he hoped the
funds would be available the following year.

Chair Harker inquired about how much water would be used on the water features. Mr. McGrath
responded that what water was used would not be potable and would be constantly recirculated,
using the same water repeatedly. That was a requirement of the Health Department.

Vice Chair Christopherson asked how much funding was provided by Salt Lake County
Tourism, Recreation, Culture and Convention (TRCC). Mayor Overson responded that the
County had provided a $1.5 million grant which the City matched. She also explained that when
Mr. McGrath referred to some amenities being “value engineered out,” he was referencing the
fact that some features might have to be cut in order to keep the project within budget.
Moreover, engineering estimates were not yet complete.

Chair Harker asked what the next step in the process was. Mr. McGrath responded that the City
would next seek a contractor and pointed out that the bidding world was currently quite
unpredictable. He was hopeful that the exterior plaza area would be complete by the first week
of November.

7.       OTHER MATTERS

         7.1 Taylorsville’s Proposed 2020-2024 Consolidated Plan and Annual Action
Plan – Ken Donarski

Mr. Donarski explained that every five years, the City was required to submit a proposed plan in
order to receive CDBG funding. He turned the time over to Muriel Xochimitl of X-Factor
Strategic Communications which was a firm that specialized in grass roots public outreach. Her
firm had worked closely with Zions Bank Public Finance on this project

Ms. Xochimitl identified the parameters the City would use in determining the allocation of
federal funds. There were four different types of federal funding programs from which the City
could receive funds: They were:

     •   Community Development Block Grant (CDBG)
     •   Emergency Solutions Grants
     •   Home Investment Partnership program
     •   Housing Opportunities for Persons with Aids (HOPWA)

In order to determine the type of services that community members were most interested in, a
survey was created in both English and Spanish. The survey included flyers which were
distributed and posted online to social media, seeking community input.

Over 200 comments were received which was a good sample for a city of Taylorsville’s size.
Housing services, transportation and mental health services were the top three priorities of the
survey respondents.

Benj Becker presented an overview of the demographical data pertaining to the City which was
contained within the proposed 2020-2024 Consolidated Plan. He also explained that in addition
to the five-year plan, the City was required to have a one-year Action Plan for how it intended to
expend the provided funds. That plan was included in the agenda packet.

            7.1.1    Taylorsville’s Community Development Block Grant Application and
                     Funding Recommendations for FY2020-2021 – Ken Donarski
Ken Donarski explained that the City received seventeen applications totaling over $800,000.
However, the City was receiving $382,000 in federal funding, only 15% of which could be used
for public services.

Mayor Overson explained that she had submitted her recommendations for the Council’s review
and was open to any input they wished to provide. She stated that she had based her
recommendations on what programs would help the largest number of people.

                    7.1.2 Public Hearing – Public Hearing regarding the City of Taylorsville’s
                          Proposed 2020-2024 Consolidated Plan, Annual Action Plan and FY
                          2020-2021 CDBG Funding Recommendations – Ken Donarski

Chair Harker opened the public hearing at 8:31 pm

back to 1999 and learned that his agency had invested $875,000 worth of funding in helping to
