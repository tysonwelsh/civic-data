Discuss the Future Empire Creek Water Flow:
Clint McAffee, Public Utilities Director, presented this item. He reviewed the history of
the Judge and Spiro Mine Tunnels and indicated they were the major water sources for
the City. This water had high concentrations of metals. In 2009, the Environmental
Protection Agency (EPA) directed the City to apply for Utah Pollutant Discharge
Elimination System (UPDES) permits for both tunnels. Water quality standards were set
and the water exceeded acceptable metal levels. The City stopped using water from the
Judge Tunnel for drinking water in 2013 and the water was diverted to Empire Creek. In
2014, the UPDES permits were finalized and the City entered into a compliance order.
Currently, work was set to pipe Judge water to 3Kings Water Treatment Plant (3KWTP).
He noted other projects being executed by his team as well.

McAffee stated some in the community expressed concern that when Judge was piped,
there would be less water flowing in Empire Creek. He displayed a cost spreadsheet
with alternatives. Alternative One was the current plan to take the water from Empire
Creek to 3Kings Water Treatment Plant and Empire Creek would return to being a
seasonal creek. Alternative 2.1 would use the City drinking water to pump water into the
creek. This alternative would require a new treatment plant since creek water standards
were more stringent than drinking water standards. The water would be treated and
pumped uphill to be discharged, thus incurring a significant cost. Alternative 2.2 would
use the existing Rockport water system and it would be pumped uphill which would also
incur the additional cost of a pump station and a pipeline.

Council Member Worel asked if Judge had run clear at one time. McAffee stated the
water came out of the mine and went through a turbidity meter. If the water was clear it
was disinfected with chlorine gas and moved to the Empire Water Tank. When the
water was not clear, the tank valve would shut and the water would discharge into a
gooseneck to the stream until it cleared up. Council Member Worel asked if the Judge
water would be different if it was tested today than it was when the City entered the

Council Member Doilney asked how much water went into the creek from Judge
currently, to which McAffee indicated 900 gallons per minute or one million plus gallons

year. McAffee stated there were no other water sources that could be diverted. Snow
could be stored, but it would not equal one million gallons per day. Pumping part-time
was possible, but there would still be a cost to construct the infrastructure.

Mayor Beerman stated the City used a bioswale for the Prospector drain which used
natural processes to reduce the metals in the water. McAffee stated there was a
bioswale that treated some of the Prospector drain, but it didn’t treat it down to stream
water standards. It was more effective in the summer and only treated a small stream.
Mayor Beerman asked if a portion of the water could go back into the stream and if a
bioswale could be looked into. McAffee stated one alternative was onsite treatment, but
that was eliminated and a bioswale would take up more room than a treatment plant.
This site was regulated because of the contamination, so it was challenging. Another
challenge was that the City did not own this land, but only had an easement across the
land.

ways to keep that flow. He asked if all options had been explored, and stated keeping
the flow wasn't worth $25 million. He realized the current flow from Judge did not meet
creek water quality standards. Council Member Joyce stated one thing missing from the
alternatives was it didn't allow for one million gallons per day. He stated in a drought

people liked to hear it. Council Member Gerber thought a compromise needed to be
reached, but the harder part was finding a lower budget. Another compromise would be
an understanding that during drought situations, the creek would not flow.

Parley's Canyon Fire Incident Summary - Deputy Fire Chief Pete Emery:
Council Member Henney reviewed the report on the Parley's Canyon fire. Pete Emery,
Deputy Fire (PCFD) Chief, thanked Council for their support. He gave a breakdown of
the Park City Fire Fighter efforts and the efforts of other agencies. He stated 10 minutes
after the fire was reported, there was a helicopter, two single engine air tankers, and
three forest service and other vehicles at the site. The fire rapidly grew and Summit
Park and Pinebrook neighborhoods were evacuated. He thought it was fortunate that
most aircraft were not committed to other fires and were able to assist in the effort to put
out the flames. Neighboring fire districts assisted as well. Most of the firefighting efforts
were by air. It was determined 541 acres were burned.

Council Member Worel was impressed with the response by PCFD, other agencies and
the neighbors. She asked if the air support was related to the high values of the homes
near the fire. Emery stated that structures near the fire gave weight to the urgency of
support needed. Council Member Worel thought much of the success could be
attributed to good planning. Council Member Henney asked if the shut off power line

1. COVID-19 Update from the Summit County Health Director, Dr. Phil Bondurant:
Phil Bondurant, Summit County Health Director, presented this item. He stated with
COVID there were many unknowns and uncertainties. The County had high vaccination
rates, but the pandemic was taking the lives of unvaccinated individuals. He wanted to
create a path forward to find ways to protect the residents as ski season began. He
stated Utah had a reduction in cases for the first time since May. He expressed concern
for the five to 11-year-olds who were not eligible for vaccination. Monday, the Summit
County Council extended the health order that extended the 2% threshold for
elementary and middle school mask mandates.

manageable, a mask order could be challenged and then the County wouldn't have that
option when the case rates got worse. The Health Department's focus for now was
messaging. Council Member Joyce asked if there was a number that, if reached, would

   After giving legal notice to the Summit County Manager (the “County Manager”), the
Summit County Health Officer (the “Health Officer”) declared a Local Public Health
Emergency related to the COVID-19 Pandemic on August 21, 2021, for a period of thirty (30)

   On September 20, 2021, the Summit County Council (the “Council”) extended the Local
Public Health Emergency until December 31, 2021 (Utah Code §26A-1-114(8)(b)).

    In Utah, local health departments are vested under Utah Code § 26A-1-114(1)(d) with
authority to establish measures as may be necessary to promote and protect public health and
control the spread of disease.

   COVID-19 is a contagion that spreads from person to person. The Health Officer and County
Manager recognize the need for Summit County and the public to continue to work
cooperatively and proactively to slow the spread of COVID-19 and to address the myriad
challenges COVID-19 has created.

    The Health Officer and County Manager find that COVID-19 poses a continuing, increasing,
and immediate threat to the public health of Summit County residents and visitors. The Delta
Variant of the COVID-19 virus is the dominant strain actively circulating in Summit County and
is more contagious than the original virus and other variants.

to remain in school with minimal interruption to the learning environment. As such, individual
campuses may be able to avoid activating the Test-To-Stay program given the effort to curb an
outbreak at early onset.

    Accordingly, the Threshold Trigger is met where 2% of the school population (inclusive of
students, faculty, and administrative staff), in each Middle School or Junior High School, as
determined by the school district superintendent, test positive for COVID-19 over a 14-day
period. The methodology for determining a school associated case is set by the Utah Department
of Health and applies to all staff, faculty, and students who meet the established criteria.

    Based on the foregoing, the Health Officer and County Manager conclude that requiring
students, faculty, and administrative staff to wear Face-Coverings when the Threshold Trigger is
met for an in-person school setting under certain circumstances will help mitigate further
widespread transmission of COVID-19 in the early stages of a potential outbreak while keeping
students and their families safe and allowing in-person learning to continue.

THEREFORE, PURSUANT TO UTAH CODE §26A-1-114, BE IT HEREBY ORDERED
BY PHILIP BONDURANT, Dr. PH, MPH, SUMMIT COUNTY HEALTH OFFICER,
AND THOMAS C. FISHER, SUMMIT COUNTY MANAGER, AS FOLLOWS:

Section 1.      Purpose. The intent of this Order of Constraint (the “Order”) is to establish a
standing Threshold Trigger, which when met, requires all students, faculty, and administrative
staff within a public or private Middle School (including K-8 schools) or Junior High School in
Summit County to wear a Face-Covering or Face-Shield while on the school’s campus. All
provisions of this Order shall be interpreted to effectuate this intent.

Section 2.    Definitions.

       2.1    “Face-Covering” is a physical barrier that:

                      i. covers the nose and mouth without openings that can be seen through;

                      ii. is made of synthetic or natural fabrics;

                      iii. secures under the chin;
                                           Page 7 of 10
                       iv. fits snugly against the nose and sides of the face; and

                       v. does not have an exhalation valve or vent.

       2.2     “Face-Shield” means a face covering that:

                       i. covers the entire face;

                       ii. protects the eyes of the wearer;

                       iii. is made of clear plastic or similar nonpermeable transparent material;

                       iv. secures around the top of the head;

                       v. does not secure under the chin;

                       vi. does not fit snugly against the nose or sides of the face; and

                       vii. can be used in conjunction with a mask for enhanced protection.

Section 3.     Face-Coverings and Face-Shields Mandatory. In the event that 2% of the
school population (inclusive of students, faculty, and administrative staff) on any public or
private Middle School (including K-8 schools) or Junior High School campus have tested
positive for COVID-19 during any 14-day period (the “Threshold Trigger”), Face-Coverings or
Face-Shields will be required for all students, faculty, and administrative staff during the school
day, including after school extra-curricular activities, at that school’s campus for fourteen (14)
consecutive days following the date upon which the Threshold Trigger was met (the “Trigger
Date”). The 14-day positive case total will be re-evaluated on the 14th day following the Trigger
Date. In the event that the re-evaluated 14-day positive case total falls below the Threshold
Trigger, Face-Coverings or Face-Shields will no longer be required for indoor settings on that
school campus. However, in the event that the 14-day positive case total continues to equal or
exceed the Threshold Trigger, Face-Coverings or Face-Shields will continue to be required until
the 14-day positive case total is below the Threshold Trigger for that school campus.

Section 4.     Exemptions.

       4.1     Notwithstanding any other provision of this Order, an individual required to wear
               a Face-Covering or Face-Shield may remove such in the following situations:

               4.1.1   while outdoors;

               4.1.2   while actively eating or drinking, provided the individual remains in place
                       while eating or drinking;



                                             Page 8 of 10
      4.1.3   while alone or only with other members of the same household in a room,
              cubicle, school-provided transportation, or similar enclosure;

      4.1.4   individuals who are hearing impaired, or communicating with an
              individual who is hearing impaired, where the ability to see the mouth is
              essential for communication;

      4.1.5   while obtaining or providing a service that requires the temporary removal
              of the Face-Covering, such as speech therapy services;

      4.1.6   while actively performing as an athlete at a school-organized or -
              sponsored athletic event;

      4.1.7   while giving an educational, artistic, cultural, musical, or theatrical
              presentation or performance at a school for an audience;

      4.1.8   while exercising or engaging in athletic training while indoors and
              maintaining at least six feet of physical distance from any other individual;

      4.1.9   while performing a task which would create a risk to the individual related
              to their work, as determined by local, state or federal regulators or
              workplace safety guidelines;

      4.1.10 while a student is napping; and

      4.1.11 while swimming or on duty as a lifeguard.

4.2   Notwithstanding any other provision of this Order, the following individuals are
      exempt from the Face-Covering and Face-Shield requirements:

      4.2.1   an individual who is unconscious, incapacitated, or otherwise unable to
              remove the Face-Covering or Face-Shield without assistance;

      4.2.2   an individual with a medical condition, mental health condition, or
              intellectual or developmental disability, that prevents the individual from
              wearing a Face- Covering or Face Shield; and

      4.2.3   an individual who has an Individualized Education Program (“IEP”)
              under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act, 20 U.S.C. § 1414,
              or an accommodation under Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973,
              29 U.S.C. § 794, that would necessitate exempting the individual from
              wearing a Face-Covering or Face-Shield.
