Governor’s Economic Development Strategic Plan — Executive Buttars is working
with the Cache Chamber of Commerce and plans to have a rough draft of the plan by

the end of November to be presented to the Governor on January 17, 2018. They are in
the process of prioritizing the three most important needs and assets in Cache County.
Assets include a well-educated and young workforce, great educational opportunities
and good high tech jobs, but our low unemployment rate is a concern for new
businesses seeking employees. Transportation is an important need, especially SR30.

County Road Report - Director Josh Runhaar reported that 80% of the asphalt work to
be done on the FEMA side of issues is completed. There will still be ditching, culvert
work and shoulder stabilization. He will be working through as much as possible during
the winter months. Runhaar projects mid-to late summer of 2018 for completion of the
flood repairs. The federal money will come back in, but it will take time.

Fairgrounds Construction Update — Work is moving forward and half of the footings
have been poured. Conduit line for electrical is being run. The first delivery of the metal
building is scheduled for this Friday. Masonry work will start when the building is on the
footings. The landscaping of the area where buildings were removed will not be
completed this fall. The construction will probably not be completed in time for the
Cruise-In as originally thought, but will be completed in time for the Fair.

$SR30 — There is a public hearing to provide input on the EIS on November 15, 2017.

Sale of North Logan Land — The 6f transfer land west of the new high school has been
appraised and will be put up for sale. Buttars will present the initial resolution on the
item at the November 28, 2017 Council meeting. A major portion of the revenue can be
used to pay for the property at the American West Heritage Center.

Cache County Human Resources Director resigned last month and applications are
being received and the position will be filled by the first part of 2018.

UAC Convention Agenda — Executive Buttars has received the agenda and the Council
members should have received theirs. The agenda can be accessed at uacnet.org.

national trainings and will apply for a grant to bring another national training to Cache

grant funding for the victim attorney instead of a victim advocate? Attorney Baird said
the position just needs to be authorized by the Council as a victim attorney instead of a
victim advocate because the funding is already received.

PUBLIC HEARING: NOVEMBER 14, 2017 — 5:45 P.M. - Rosehill Farms Il Rezone-Request

for approval of a rezone of two contiguous parcels totaling 40.0 acres from Agricultural (A10)
Zone to Rural 2 (RU2) Zone located approximately 6600 South Hwy 165, near Hyrum

Since the two requests were by the same applicant and related to one another, the Public
Hearings were combined with the approval of the applicant, Tim Wilkinson.

Chris Harrild gave an overview of the requests and stated the Planning Commission
recommended denial due to the inappropriateness of the location near a mink farm and density
allowed in the RU2 Zone. The properties are currently in AG Protection Areas.

Chairman Merrill opened the Public Hearing and invited public comment.

Wayne Jewkes, property owner near proposed rezones, is concerned about the traffic problem. His
father was hit on a tractor in the area and Jewkes had a car almost hit him two weeks ago. The flies in
the area are bad and there is noise from farm equipment at night. Water settles on the parcels in the
rezone request area.

Colette Mathews, mink farm owner, said lighting is a problem as it affects the mink. She is also
concerned that people who might build in the rezoned area would not fully understand mink farm
operations. Traffic is also a concern as her husband was almost hit recently.

Alton Hebdon, friend of mink farm owners, noted a mink farm does not smell good and people will not
want to live near it.

Tim Wilkinson, the applicant, said he is moving forward with Hyrum annexation procedures and has the
signatures of eight property owners in the area.

Tyce Mathews, mink farm operator, is a 4" generation mink farmer and acknowledged the fly problem.
His family has bought up some of the land around them over the years, but it is now too costly to do so.

ACTION: Motion by Vice Chairman Erickson to close the Public Hearing-November 14,
2017-5:30 p.m.-Rosehill Farms | Rezone AND 5:45 p.m.-Rosehill Farms Il Rezone. White
seconded the motion. The vote was unanimous, 7-0

Rosehill Farms Il Rezone requests. Worthen seconded the motion. The vote was
unanimous, 7-0.

Executive Buttars agreed the location is not a good place for a lot of homes and predicted

be appropriated to fund additional training costs. Total department expenditures are offset to the Assessing and
Collecting fund at the rate of 9.0%. The expenditure allocation associated with this proposal is $1,100, which excludes
the transactions for Hyrum City prosecutions.

services to be provided by a third party agency. Existing medical staff positions have been terminated and funding

remaining in the associated payroll accounts is proposed to be allocated for payment to the third part agency. The total
amount of the Transfer is $353,700.

The full amount of the additional sales tax revenue is not needed to fund current expenditures and $212,500 can be
contributed to the fund balance.

CACHE COUNTY RESOLUTION 2017 - 28

A RESOLUTION SUPPORTING ONE-TIME FUNDING FOR THE UTAH FESTIVAL
OPERA AND MUSIC THEATRE

BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CACHE COUNTY COUNCIL:

WHEREAS, the Utah Festival Opera and Music Theatre (UFOMT) has, for 25 years,
provided a high quality summer cultural series; and

WHEREAS, UFOMT has a significant impact on summer hotel occupancy, restaurant
utilization, and the economic well-being of other businesses in Cache County; and

WHEREAS, UFOMT is an important factor for many Summer Citizens in deciding to
live in Cache County during the summer; and

WHEREAS, continuance of UFOMT is important to the public image of Cache County;
and

WHEREAS, UFOMT is facing an immediate cash flow problem; and

WHEREAS, the Council believes that the future economic viability of UFOMT is

important to Cache County and will require changes in certain operating procedures within
UFOMT; and

WHEREAS, Utah State Code allows Cache County to appropriate money to or provide
nonmonetary assistance to a nonprofit entity.

NOW THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that based on the identified benefits of the
UFOMT and in order to facilitate its continued contribution, the Cache County Council supports
providing UFOMT one-time support from the Recreation, Arts, Parks, and Zoos (RAPZ) tax and
the transient room tax for a total amount of $80,000, if the following conditions are met prior to
funding being received:

1. UFOMT publically announces a 2018 performance season together with a FY 2018
budget that has been approved by the UFOMT Board of Directors.

2. The UFOMT Board of Directors is expanded to include a representative with financial
experience jointly designated by the Logan City Mayor and the Cache County Executive.
UFMOT will provide quarterly financial statements to Logan City and Cache County through
2018.

3. The UFOMT Board of Directors enacts a “Continuity and Succession” plan that will
ensure that UFOMT continues to have strong artistic and executive leadership.

4. The UFOMT Board of Directors approves changes in UFOMT bylaws that provide
assurance of improved financial and operational policies.
