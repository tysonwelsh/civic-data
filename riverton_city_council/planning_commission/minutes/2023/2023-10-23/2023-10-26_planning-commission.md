2.     PUBLIC HEARINGS

       A.     LOFTS AT ROSE CREEK CROSSING PHASE 2 AMENDED, PLZ-23-1005,
              A BOUNDARY LINE ADJUSTMENT AT 13641 SOUTH STONE ROSE LANE
              (3620 WEST). APPLICANT - TOM SPENCER.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich, presented the Staff Report and stated that the application is for
a Boundary Line Adjustment to the Lofts at Rose Creek Crossing. The subject property is
located at 13641 South Stone Rose Lane (3620 West). Mr. Prestwich reported that the
project has been approved, recorded, and is under construction. A property rendering was
displayed. He reported that the amendment pertains to the southwest corner of the
development which contains five units. The Plat was recorded before construction and
discrepancies in the parcel boundaries were identified while going through the process
relative to the building itself. There is no new developable space, and the request is to shift
the property lines to reflect the reality of what was built. Mr. Prestwich reported that to
comply with the law, this item must return before the Planning Commission for approval as
an amendment and be recorded with the Salt Lake County Recorder’s Office. He confirmed
that any other agencies that must sign off will be the responsibility of the developer. Staff
recommended approval with conditions listed in the Staff Report.

Chair Gilchrist asked if the open space to the north has been reduced. Mr. Prestwich stated
that there are no issues with compliance and the open space requirement since they are
only shifting boundaries. The request was found to comply with the Ordinance and the
original approval.

Rose Creek Lofts, LLC. The proposed property shift measures 1.25 feet and was from when

       B.     RIVERTON COVE AMENDED, PLZ-23-8019, AN APPLICATION FOR AN
              AMENDMENT TO A PREVIOUSLY APPROVED SITE PLAN LOCATED AT
              12900 SOUTH 1830 WEST. APPLICANT – RYAN KENT.

Mr. Prestwich presented the Staff Report and stated that the above application is for an
Amendment to a previously approved Site Plan for Riverton Cove. The subject property is
located at 12900 South 1830 West. An aerial view of the property was shared. The project
has been approved and recorded, and homes are under construction with some occupancy.
The project was originally approved with gates at both access points as proposed by the
developer with the condition that the south access point onto 12960 South be an exit only.
The applicant was identified as Ryan Kent. It was brought to the attention of Mr. Kent that
the gates had yet to be constructed and Staff requested input on timing. Mr. Kent returned
to Staff with the request that he not be required to build the gates. Staff informed him that
that could not be approved at the Staff level and requires Planning Commission approval
since it is an amendment to the Site Plan. Despite the gates not being a required element,
it was an element of the approval.

Mr. Prestwich reviewed the application and stated that the access point onto 1830 West was
identified in the plan and reflects a center median, the call access pad, and a Knox box that
contains the emergency access key. Posts were put into the ground for the gate structure
which was later removed and the asphalt patched. A second gate on 12960 South was

described, which also included a Knox box. One of the concerns expressed was that the
dimensions of the roadway were perhaps prohibitive. Mr. Prestwich presented images of a
28-foot gate at the Vista Development as well as a gate with a 25-foot right-of-way located
on a private lane off 1300 West. From a technical standpoint, gates and various installation

requirement for gates at the two access points.

Commissioner Cannon recalled when this was first reviewed, the project was a higher
density than is shown on the Master Plan. He believed he may have been more acceptable
to a higher density zoning because of the gates. Mr. Prestwich confirmed that the original
Master Plan reflected .25-acre lots and felt his view was relevant and best determined by
the Commission. The project is required to return before the Commission as he believed
Staff should not make judgment calls as to what elements were important. Whether it would
have been approved without gates was part of what should be considered.

Commissioner Matheson asked if the request to eliminate the gates was based on anything
other than a financial burden. Staff suggested that the question be directed to the applicant.

Commissioner Park asked if a redesign of the gate from what is shown on the plan requires
additional approval from the Planning Commission and the Planning Department.
Mr. Prestwich confirmed that the plan will require review from both bodies if a different type
of gate or configuration is to be considered. The primary review would need to be conducted
by the Fire Department.

Chair Gilchrist asked that if the Planning Commission maintains the requirement for the gate
will a redesign of that specific aspect of the plan require additional approval by both Planning
bodies or if a redesign was possible with approval from the Fire Department. Mr. Prestwich
encouraged the Planning Commission to include its desire in the motion, which would
specify approval of a redesign of the gate to be reviewed at Staff level subject to the Fire
Code and other ordinances.

Commissioner Rushton asked Staff if they can locate the original application and conditions.
He questioned if the request is determined to be part of the application and approval process
and is a Homeowners Association (“HOA”) issue if it is something the City would get involved
in. City Attorney, Ryan Carter, responded that Staff does not know what kind of impact the
request will have on an HOA if established. They were also unaware whether there was an
HOA functioning at the time the application was submitted and whether any property had
been placed under contract by any prospective buyers. He reported that where this has
already been presented, they could be looking at affecting investment-backed expectations
that property owners might have on the property. He believed they should exercise caution
and decide where the City stands in relation to those with an interest and who are willing to
come forward.

Commissioner Park reported that during a public hearing, it was stated that fees have been
paid and properties purchased based on the gated community idea. He asked what sort of

action the property owners are entitled to if this body takes action to remove the gates.
Mr. Carter stated that he cannot put himself in a position where he represents the legal
interest of any prospective buyer and was not in possession of the contracts. He was unsure
if the lots had been placed in a contract or closed. It was believed that the proposed action
may potentially put the City at risk and impact the investment-backed expectations of the

The Staff Report conditions were referenced. A question was raised regarding if the gate
was removed adjacent to 12960 South, they would not be able to control that as an exit only
and would violate the condition of approval. Mr. Prestwich stated it would make compliance
much more difficult and was uncertain of another method that would satisfy that condition.
Mr. Carter added that the exit-only provisions are material as they went to traffic flow as it
interfaced with the public streets. The challenge the Planning Commission faces is in the
event the gates are removed how would the City go about ensuring an exit-only system is
in place that is just as reliable as gates. If they were to entertain the removal of the gates,
they would need to exercise caution when looking at how they can maintain an exit-only
provision as effectively as the gates would deliver. It was confirmed the original approval
took place on October 28, 2021.

Commissioner Cannon found it interesting that Staff provided an example of a 28-foot gate
located in the Vistas development that includes 54 homes. He believed it had been
adequately maintained for over 20 years and questioned what would happen if the developer
came back with another amendment to remove something else that had been promised to
the home buyers. Staff explained in cases where a developer fails to perform or meet
requirements and standards, it becomes an enforcement issue.

Commissioner Cluff asked if there was a certain point when the developer would have been
or will be required to have the gates in place. Mr. Prestwich stated that the response from
the applicant was that during the major construction of the development and came to a head
when they notified the developer, they could proceed no further without the gates in place
to meet that requirement.

The applicant, Ryan Kent, was present representing Riverton Cove Partners. He
appreciated the input and wished to have a healthy discussion regarding how they should
install the gates. He reported that his children live in the subdivision and are not opposed
to the gates. Consultants and contractors have been dealing with the design and installation
of the gates and unexpected issues came up. The designs depicted in the plan were double
gates opening inward with the flow of traffic. There is a home located on the furthest south
lot and when opened, the gate would almost touch the driveway. After the gates were
designed, the fencing company was on the property with one-half of the gates and when
installing the posts, they struck the storm drain. He confirmed that a larger gate was
considered with the removal of the median and shifting the post to the south but discovered
the entire park strip is full of fiber, power, and utility lines. There is a sidewalk to the north
of the entrance where a single gate may be a solution. Snow removal was of concern. He
believed there may be room for a 36-foot wide, single-hinged gate on the north side of the
road with access off of 1830 West and a single pinned hinge gate could also be considered

Commissioner Rushton asked if there was any evidence of any type of determination from
an engineering firm or gate company stating the impossibility of gate installation at these
locations or demonstrating the shared issues. Mr. Kent replied that he did not state it was

Commissioner Park commented that the applicant started his statement by saying the gate

can be done but not the optimal design or experience that was desired when plans were

of the original plan, a gate can be built but would need a redesign.

Commissioner Cannon found fault with the engineering design of the east entrance and
questioned increasing the width of the east road to provide access past the gate. It was
Mr. Kent’s opinion that although there may be issues with the engineers' and consultants'
design, he believed it was too late to mitigate the issue with the storm drain placement and
utilities located in the park strip. There is a sidewalk to the north, and he believed it not
necessary to expand that side of the road and indicated there is adequate space for a
redesigned single-arm gate hinged on the north side.

Mr. Prestwich acknowledged that the Planning Commission may provide direction but is not
required to specify or comment on design. He suggested that a motion could include a
redesign requirement and authorize Staff to work with the developer to redesign the gate. It
is not necessary to include any kind of requirement or direction as to the type of gate as it
will be subject to other requirements including the Fire Code.

A Commission Member struggled with the idea that because the utilities’ location conflicts
with the design, the gate construction cannot be completed. He has worked on hundreds of
projects where utilities have been relocated and is not uncommon. He pointed out the
utilities are located within the project because the developer put them there. Mr. Kent found
validity in the point. The storm drain was tied into an existing storm drain on 1830 West and
was not involved in the design of the power and fiber. The south access appeared more
appropriate as an exit and entrance and noted their traffic study did not support the exit-only
design. He requested clarification and guidance on what would be appropriate.

Commissioner Matheson stated that he had visited the subject property and found the roads
to be small. He understood the feeling of the residents benefiting from the gate by
eliminating random vehicles driving through the neighborhood. The idea of having the exit-
only access having a metal traffic spike system was discussed. He felt that the main access
should be located along the east road and asked about the developer's preference. Mr.

Chair Gilchrist opened the public hearing.

Megan Chan reported she lives in Riverton Cove and purchased her home knowing that the
development would be gated. She has to cross the street to access the sidewalk and a gate
is desired to eliminate unnecessary cars to increase walkability. Although a gate helps
improve property values, it also provides safety. She was disappointed to learn of the
conflict from Riverton City rather than the developer.

Mr. Prestwich reported that all electronic comments were forwarded electronically and
printed for the Commission’s review.

Kevin Rasmussen identified himself as the owner of Lot 12. He purchased the Lot for the
added security of the gate, which was a selling point. A contractor is actively advertising the
homes with rental basements in the development, and he expressed concern about the
potential increase in traffic. It was his opinion that the gates need to be constructed.

John Hang commented that the southwest side of the street does not include a sidewalk.
He believed what is proposed would be an appropriate location for the hinge of the gate and
could be designed to swing away from Lot 1. He felt that the main entrance design could
be reconfigured and believed there is space to work around.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Commissioner Rushton reported that the subject property was originally an infill
development with higher zoning than the surrounding area. Impact and traffic concerns
were considered through input from the community. With these kinds of projects, the
development presents either a gated community with a higher density or senior
developments. The subject property has always been presented as a gated community and
a condition on the Site Plan. He felt it should be left as it was with conditions of approval
and with some kind of a timeframe, the expectation should be to have the gates installed.

Commissioner Park agreed with Commissioner Rushton’s comments and suggested a
number of different designs be put in place. He was opposed to the request to eliminate the
gates and asked for direction to provide the opportunity for a redesign to meet the original
intent of the Commission when approved. Mr. Prestwich reviewed the motion should they
wish to deny the application and direct Staff to work with the applicant on any necessary
redesign of the gates.

Commissioner Rushton asked Staff if it is within the Commission’s purview to set a
reasonable time for installation. Mr. Carter explained there are Certificate of Occupancy
requirements and Staff does not intend to retreat from those deadlines as the project moves
forward. The infrastructure would need to be installed before Certificates of Occupancy are
issued or a fixed deadline may be added.

Commissioner Cannon was impressed by the developer’s finished product and the work
provided to get to that point. He believed there was no more blighted area in Riverton City
than the subject property. If the original density would have held with the increased road
width and fewer lots, it would have created a four or five-lot difference. The density granted
was a huge benefit to the developer and approval was based on all of the items discussed.

Commissioner Park referred to the deadlines and Certificates of Occupancy and asked what
would be reasonably required. Mr. Prestwich stated there have been instances where they
have suspended permits and inspections based on requirements not having been met.

Commissioner Matheson moved the Planning Commission DENY PLZ-23-8019
Riverton Code Amendment to a previously approved Site Plan located at 12900 South
1830 West and direct Staff to approve administratively any necessary redesign of the
gate subject to applicable Riverton City and International Fire Codes. Commissioner
Rushton seconded the motion. The motion passed with the unanimous consent of
the Commission.
