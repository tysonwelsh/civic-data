   2. HILLSIDE PERMIT (HS) Administrative

      A. Consider a request for a hillside development permit at the South Rim at Foremaster Ridge Phase 4
         development. The applicant is proposing to allow structures in the 30’ ridgeline setback in
         anticipation of the construction of a residential home on lot 85. The property is generally located at
         1709 Ridgeline Circle and is currently zoned Single Family Residential, minimum lot size 10,000 sf
         (R-1-10). The applicant is Robert and Rhonda Marshall. Case No. 2022-HS-007. (Staff – Carol
         Davidson)

         Wes Jenkins presented the following:

         Wes Jenkins – Lots 85 and 86 are basically the same thing, I will present them together, but you will
         need to make 2 motions because they are separate applications. If you recall we brought lots 82, 83
         and 84 before you at the last meeting for the same request. This is to remove the 30-foot ridgeline
         setback. The argument is that there is no ridgeline there and there never was a ridgeline there. Staff
         agreed and we brought that to you. These lots are saying the same thing and would like to eliminate
         the 30-foot ridgeline setback on the plat. They are saying there is no ridgeline, it’s just a slope.
         Their argument is that there is just a rock feature there not a ridgeline. Wes showed a picture of the
         lots before they were disturbed and after they were disturbed. They had already excavated for their
         walls, and that is how this came forward, they were excavating and starting the construction of a
         rock wall and one of the inspectors saw it, stopped them and said hey this is a ridgeline setback and
         that is what initiated all of this. We surveyed the natural ground right along the base of the rock wall
         that they are starting. I wanted to see the elevation of it in relation to what it would look like. And
         really the elevation is about the same all the way across here on the natural ground and then on top
         of that rock feature. We surveyed the top and the bottom of the rock feature. On lot 85 there is
         about a 9-foot difference between the lot and that rock feature or ridgeline whatever you decide
         tonight. On lot 86 there is about a 13-foot rise. Their request tonight is that this is not a ridgeline
         and should have no set back associated with it, that it is more of a rock feature.

         Ray Draper – What constitutes a ridgeline?

         Wes Jenkins – Basically a ridgeline has a point on it then it goes flat on the other side. Their
         argument is that it’s not a ridgeline it is a rock feature that has been revealed over time. The intent
         of the 30-foot ridgeline setback is to set the homes back further so that the homes don’t look so
         ominous to those below, it’s more of a visual thing than anything. We have tried to protect
         ridgelines across the city, some like the old airport are at a 100-foot ridgeline setback.

         Commissioner Fisher – Read the definition of a ridgeline. It seems the definition is does it have a
         top and an edge.

         Wes Jenkins – Hillside recommended approval; they did recommend that any materials used be
         stained to match what is there so basically black.

         Commissioner Fisher – So how do you determine that this is not a ridgeline.

         Wes Jenkins – When I went out and looked at this I was torn, that’s why we took it to hillside to get
         feedback from them, if I thought it was a ridgeline we wouldn’t have allowed them to go to hillside.
