                                Section 8015 – Donated Leave
                                     (Adopted 06/18/2024)

General Policy Statement:

    Full time employees who have a serious illness or serious injury may be granted donated
leave as detailed in this policy. Donated Leave is voluntary, and no employee is required to
donate leave.

Policy:

In the case of an employee’s serious illness or serious injury, city employees with an accrued
vacation leave balance in excess of 80 hours or accrued sick leave balance in excess of 480
hours (600 hours for sworn fire personnel assigned to a 2912 schedule) may donate leave hours
to a seriously ill or seriously injured employee. After the donation of leave hours, the donating
employee must have a minimum balance of 80 hours of accrued vacation or 480 hours (600
hours for sworn fire personnel assigned to a 2912 schedule) of sick leave. Leave hours will be
deducted from the donor employee’s leave balance account and added to the seriously ill or
seriously injured employee’s sick leave balance account. The City Manager and the department
directors of the employees involved in the transfer of leave hours must approve the transfer. In
order for an employee to receive donated leave hours, the employee receiving the leave
donation must meet the eligibility requirements of the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA)
and be on approved FMLA (refer to section 8010 Family and Medical Leave), have exhausted
all vacation leave, sick leave, compensation time leave, and holiday leave time available for use
in their combined leave balances, and not receive any paid disability leave or Workers’
Compensation. Employees who have been disciplined for abuse or misuse of their sick or
vacation leave within the 12 months of requesting the donated leave are ineligible to receive
donated leave. Donation of leave to an employee may not exceed 160 hours (240 for sworn fire
personnel assigned to a 2912 schedule) and will be forfeited after approved leave of absence
ends, and the donated hours will be credited back to the donors on a pro rata basis.

No leave will accrue while the serious ill or serious injured employee is receiving donated
leave. A city holiday that occurs during donated leave will be counted as holiday.

If an employee was granted donated leave and it was found the employee received such leave
on the basis of mis-stated, erroneous, or false statements, the employee will be required to
reimburse the donated leave and may be subject to disciplinary action, up to, and including
termination.
                                Section 8090 – Parental Leave
                                     (Adopted 06/18/2024)


General Policy Statement:

   Full time employees who meet the eligibility requirements under the Family and Medical
Leave Act (FMLA) will be granted paid parental leave as detailed in this policy. Refer to
Section 8010 Family and Medical Leave.

1. Eligibility. Eligible employees may take up to 80 hours (120 hours for sworn fire personnel
   assigned to a 2912 schedule) of paid parental leave for the following:

   1.1. the birth of a child and to care for the newborn child within six months after birth;
   1.2. the placement with the employee of a child for adoption or foster care and to care for
        the newly placed child within six months of placement; or
   1.3. the appointment of legal guardianship of a minor child or incapacitated adult within
        six months after appointment.

2. Parental Leave Rules. In no case will an employee receive more than the specified hours
   stated above in a rolling 12-month period, regardless of whether more than one birth,
   adoption or foster care placement, or the appointment of legal guardian of a minor child or
   incapacitated adult event occurs within that 12-month time frame. Employees may take paid
   parental leave in a continuous period or intermittently, but must use all paid parental leave
   during the time frame indicated in #1. Any unused paid parental leave will be forfeited at
   the end of the six months of the occurring event. The qualified employee shall continue to
   receive all employment related benefits and payments at the same level that the qualified
   employee received immediately before beginning the parental leave. Parental leave:

   2.1. must run concurrently with FMLA;
   2.2. will not carryover from one rolling calendar year to the next; and
   2.3. will not be paid out at time of separation of employment.

3. Parental Leave Requests. Employees must provide their supervisor and the human resources
   department with notice of the request for leave at least 30 days prior to the proposed date of
   the leave, or if the leave was not foreseeable, as soon as practicable. The employee must
   complete the necessary forms and provide all documentation as required by the human
   resources department to substantiate the request.
