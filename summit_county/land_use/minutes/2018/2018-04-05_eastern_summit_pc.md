4. Public hearing and possible action regarding a Conditional Use Permit for a 
commercial kennel for the boarding of up to 10 dogs; 460 N. Cedar Hill Lane; ACB-
2; Denise Blonquist, applicant – Sean Lewis, County Planner 
 
Planner Lewis said that Ms. Blonquist is seeking to have a permit for a dog kennel to 
board between two and ten dogs.  The property is located by the intersection of Chalk 
Creek and Border Station Roads.  The dogs will be boarded inside her house and in an 
outbuilding.  He said that Ms. Blonquist used to work for animal control.  She 
understands the rules associated with this enterprise.   
 
Planner Lewis said that Staff has no issues with this application due to the location of 
the home.  The closest house is approximately 400 feet away.  Staff recommends 
approval as outlined in the Staff Report.  Ms. Blonquist said this is something she has 
always wanted to do.    
 
COMMISSION COMMENTS AND QUESTIONS          
Commissioner Hanson asked how the dog waste will be taken care of.  Ms. Blonquist 
said she intends to compost it.  Commissioner Sargent asked what the rationale is for 
no more than 10 dogs.  Planner Lewis said that number was at the applicant’s request.

Ms. Blonquist added she wants to limit the dogs to 10 because she is the only one that 
will be operating this business.   
 
A discussion ensued on the number of dogs.  The applicant was told that an amendment 
would be required if she wanted to have more than ten dogs in the future.  Chair Clyde 
said given the size of the parcel, 10 dogs seems like the right limit.  Ms. Blonquist said 
her father recently passed away.  She will be inheriting more land adjacent to hers.   
 
Commissioner Wheaton asked why the owner’s personal dogs are included in the 
limit of 10.  Planner Lewis responded that Staff included the owner’s dogs so there 
would be a realistic expectation of how many dogs would be on the site.  Ms. Blonquist 
said she currently has one dog.   
 
Commissioner Wheaton noted there is a condition for a one-year review.  Will this be 
reviewed annually?  Planner Lewis answered it is a onetime review.  A year will be 
enough time to allow the neighbors to experience the impacts of the kennel, if any.  At 
that time, it can be decided if the impacts warrant a full review with a public hearing.   
 
Ms. Blonquist asked if the review will include the number of dogs allowed.  Planner 
Lewis said at the review, the number of dogs allowed would most likely not be adjusted.  
If it was decided to bring this back for a full hearing, the number could be discussed. 
 
Commissioner Hanson said a one-year review has not always been included with a 
doggie day care business.  If the Commission doesn’t want to have that condition it 
could be removed as part of the motion.  This doesn’t seem fair to this applicant.   
 
The public hearing was opened.

6.   Public Hearing and possible action regarding a Plat Amendment to remove the 
building pads that are recorded in the River Run subdivision Plat, for lot 4 & 5; 
533 & 561 River Run Lane; RIVRN-4-AM, RIVRN-5-AM; Don Sargent , applicant – 
Jennifer Strader, Senior Planner  
 
Commissioner Sargent said he will recuse himself from the agenda item because he 
assisted the applicant in preparing materials for submittal.  Planner Strader said this 
property is located off West Hoytsville Road.  It is located in the agricultural protection 
(AP) zone.  The original subdivision was approved in 2008.  The subdivision contained 
five lots.  If approved, the amendment will impact Lots 4 and 5.  Lot 4 contains 18.71 
acres.  Lot 5 contains 10.68 acres.  There are existing residents on Lots 1 and 3.  Lots 2, 
4, and 5 are vacant.   
 
Planner Strader said when the subdivision was approved, the applicant chose to place 
building pads on each lot, even though that wasn’t a Code requirement at the time.  The 
meeting minutes indicate the intent of the pads was to cluster the placement of homes 
near existing outbuildings.  The Staff Report indicated that clustering would ensure that 
the majority of the property would remain available for agriculture.  An aerial photo 
was displayed demonstrating where the lots are located. 
 
Planner Strader said the applicant wishes to amend the plat because he is having a 
difficult time selling the two lots.  A copy of the plat was displayed and the building pads 
were pointed out.  The FEMA 100 year flood plain was indicated.  A map was shown of 
where an independent engineer (Gus Sherry of Canyon Engineering) determined where 
the flood plain lay.  Lastly, the irrigation ditch was pointed out.     
 
Planner Strader said the building pads are partly within the flood plain.  Development 
within that area would have to comply with flood plain standards.  She said a section of 
the Development Code highly discourages building within a 100 year flood plain.  The

Engineering Department used the FEMA results, not the results of Mr. Sherry.  She said 
there is a process to modify the flood plain with FEMA.   
 
Planner Strader said that removing the pads would allow construction to take place

Mr. Sherry addressed the Commission.  He is very experienced with flood plain 
mapping.  He said that FEMA does not make flood plain maps.  They issue contracts to 
civil engineers.  This property is located in Zone A, which is an approximate study area.   
 
Mr. Sherry said he has completed a detailed study with a topo map.  One of FEMA tests 
is if the property can be made reasonably safe from flooding.  In his experience, the 
proposed pads west of the ditch are buildable and can be made reasonably safe from 
flooding.        
  
Mr. Sherry said it is known that the property is wet.  Even though Planner Strader said 
there are no wetlands, they know there is a high water table.   That means they have to 
put their footings down to solid material.  Grading away from the structure will be 
required.  This can easily be achieved within the proposed building pads.  The 
ordinance allows for information to be obtained from a consultant, such as himself.     
 
Chair Clyde asked if his topo information indicates the elevation of the FEMA

water flow.  Chair Clyde said he would like to see the information and know what the
