         E. Discussion Item: Home Occupation Prohibited Activities – Fitness facility.
            Municipal Code 5.38.040 (By Director of City Commerce/RDA Manager, Brian A.
            Preece, and City Attorney Ryan Loose)

City Commerce Director Preece said about 3 years ago, they had someone ask to do Olympic
level weight training instruction out of her home. Staff denied the application, based on its use.
That decision was appealed and they were granted the license because it was considered private
instruction, rather than a fitness facility. Now, they have another business license application for
baseball batting instruction. It will be conducted in an out building, which was allowed a few
years ago. Staff does not see a legal reason to deny the home occupation. The neighbors are
concerned about traffic. The application meets city requirements. Because this is personal
instruction, and staff has been overruled in the past on a similar application, staff does not see
how they can deny the license. He asked if the City Council would like to leave the code as is or
further define what a fitness or health spa use is? Is the City Council okay to continue to use out
buildings?

Council Member Zander asked what are the most common home occupation complaints? Mr.
Preece said traffic. He noted that Home Occupations are allowed 12 vehicle trips (6 round trip)
per day.

City Attorney Loose told of a business that was located next to a commercial property, and they
worked out a parking agreement with the commercial business. He said the number of people
allowed at that business is more than a home in the middle of a residential area.

City Attorney Loose said previously, they took out the neighbors right to protest a Home
Occupation because it is an administrative item. The neighbor does have the right to ask for the
code to be enforced.

City Attorney Loose reiterated that the current business license application for batting instruction
complies with city code. They feel it is the same as the power lifting instruction license from 3
years ago.

Mr. Preece said there is a part of the code that allows outdoor instruction such as swimming,
tennis, etc. In the case 3 years ago, the hearing officer said if they are teaching a skill, it is not
considered fitness.

Council Member Zander said she is okay with the batting instruction use. She is not okay if
people are showing up random hours, or if the homeowner is not there teaching instruction. She
knows of a case where that happened.

Mr. Preece noted that if the homeowner does not charge for the service, they can do it without a
license. Additionally, if they are not charging for the service, that does not count towards the
trips per day.

City Attorney Loose said there is a philosophical trend to allow people to use their homes to
work. Currently, with more people working from home, the impact level changes.

CM Whatcott said in the city, they previously made a shift in the code to allow the whole
property to be used to make a living at their home.

City Attorney Loose asked if the City Council sees a reason to change the Ordinance? He noted
that there is a neighbor present at this meeting that is concerned about the impacts to the
neighborhood. It was further noted that this application is located in the northeast part of the city.

Council Member Harris asked if there are unintended consequences with approving this license?
City Attorney Loose said if they can identify them, they will mitigate that upfront. He said staff
is also concerned about making sure it is enforceable.

Mr. Preece said if the City Council does not see a reason to change the Ordinance, they won’t.

Council Member Marlor joined the meeting at this time.

Council Member McGuire asked why fitness facilities were singled out before? Mr. Preece said
to prevent Gold’s Gym type facilities from happening in people’s neighborhoods.

Council Member McGuire said if someone did personal fitness training, would they be allowed
to do that as a Home Occupation? Mr. Preece said under today’s ruling, they would. He noted
that dance studios in homes are not considered fitness facilities. Mr. Loose said they also have
yoga and palates studios. They may need to consider the requirement that the person running the
business needs to be present.

Council Member Zander concurred that the use crosses over when the homeowners are not
present. It is different if people are providing a facility versus doing a service. Mr. Preece said
that is covered in the current Ordinance. Employees are not allowed.

Council Member Harris said the concern he has heard expressed was with parking. Do they have
to contain parking on site? Mr. Preece said they can park in the street, if it is on a public street.
CM Whatcott said the number of trips per day regulates the parking and traffic concerns.

Mr. Loose said they could change the code to require parking on site, if that is what the City
Council wants. He said currently, the building is not complete for this application. Mr. Preece
said in some areas, the HOA prohibits Home Occupations, but they have to regulate themselves.

Mayor Ramsey said she is not in favor of changing the parking requirement at this point. That is
why we have public streets and a limited number of trips per day. She asked do they want private
instruction allowed in the code? Council Member Marlor said it is not the use, but the number of
trips per day he is concerned about.

Council Member Zander said if the batting facility hires batting coaches, would that count as an
employee? Mr. Loose said yes and it would not be allowed. He noted that an employee is not
someone living at the residence.

Council Member Marlor said there is a business in the city where several employees congregate
at the house in the morning and then work off site the rest of the day. Mr. Loose said that is a
violation of the Home Occupation Ordinance.

Mr. Preece asked if the City Council is still okay allowing businesses out of the garage? Mayor
Ramsey said yes, no one is advocating to change that.

         F. Presentation Item: Open meetings training, land use and ethics. (By City Attorney,
            Ryan Loose)

City Attorney Loose reviewed a prepared presentation (Attachment A).

Council Member Harris said it bothers people when there is communication not over the
microphone. He asked if people started whispering, can they be called out, and ask what was
said? City Attorney Loose said they could call on them, but they can’t force the individuals to
disclose what was said.

Council Member Harris asked if it is appropriate to have side conversations away from the
microphone? Mr. Loose said they have a code of ethics, and they regulate their own body. They
decide what is appropriate. The code of ethics says that the City Council does their business
materially in public. He said if they are discussing the issue at hand, it is afoul to their code of
ethics. They should avoid even the appearance of impropriety.

ADJOURNMENT

Council Member Marlor made a motion to adjourn. Council Member Zander seconded the
motion. The vote was 4-0 in favor.

The September 1, 2020 City Council Electronic Study meeting adjourned at 5:55 p.m.
