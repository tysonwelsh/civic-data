 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
       a. Discussion of proposed Prose River Park Development
          Ryan Barker with Alliance Realty Properties said it was found during the
          preapplication process with staff that developing the proposed multi-family
          Prose River Park project with the Redwood Road Corridor Zone would not be
          feasible, because minimum floor area ratio requirements would ultimately force
          structured parking. He said structured parking was not financially feasible for the
          project. Mr. Barker requested Council feedback regarding whether a win-win
          solution could be found.

          Chair Lamb said the Council had looked at multiple development concepts for the
          subject location, and parking had always been an issue. Steve Jones with Dwell
          Design Studio said the proposed concept design (Option 1) would provide almost
          two parking stalls per unit. Council Member Bloom said the Council had seen a
          disturbing trend of residents being charged for parking in residential complexes.
          She said she would be in favor of preventing that from happening at Prose River
          Park. Mr. Barker said the intent was to not charge for parking with the proposed
          surface parking. He said development of structured parking involved such an
          expense that developers often needed to charge for use of the parking for the
          project to be feasible. He suggested that with the proposed parking ratio in Option
          1, residents would not need to park in the street.

          five-story building and a structured parking slip deck that would not work
          financially. The Conceptual Site Plan – Option 1 included only surface parking,
          and did not meet floor area ratio requirements.

          Council Member Green asked about plans for the west end of 6670 South. Public
          Works Director Brian Clegg said the City was currently conducting a study of
          6670 South (Canal Road) related to erosion and possible mitigation. Mr. Clegg
          said the road in its current condition would not handle increased traffic from the
          proposed development. He said the long-term plan was to extend and continue
          6670 South through. Council Member Green believed the plan to continue the
          road in the future needed to be reconsidered.

          Chair Lamb felt the subject property was a difficult piece of land to develop and
          did not think it would ever be developed at a resident-ownership level.

       b. Discussion of the Proposed Parker Place II Development
          Gary Canon, Riverton City resident and owner of a business in West Jordan, said
          he appreciated the opportunity to make a proposal. He believed Brinkerhoff
          Bluffs, a previous project of his in West Jordan, had been very successful. Mr.
          Canon spoke of residential projects in the area surrounding the subject property
          that had requested the Council remove the Senior Housing Overlay. He learned
          that individuals buying into an empty-nester community preferred to live in a
          community of more than 14 or 15 units and preferred an access gate and a sense
          of pride in the neighborhood. Mr. Canon reported that the subject property had
          been on the market for a commercial project for more than two years without
          success.

          Mr. Canon said the current proposal was for four single-family lots, with the lots
          averaging roughly 10% more square footage than lots in Parker Place or Sunset
          Villas. He said some of the lots would accommodate a third-car garage. He
          reported that the subdivision was already improved and would accommodate
          future improvement of Drake Lane, should that ever happen. Mr. Canon
          commented that a new storm drain was not required, but had been added
          providing direct benefit to West Jordan City.

          Council Member Green agreed that Brinkerhoff Bluffs was a successful
          development. Mr. Canon responding to Council Member Green’s question, said all
          four proposed lots would be over 5,000 square feet.

          Council Member Jacob believed there had been issues with parking on Drake Lane
          in the past, and parking was currently not allowed on a portion of Drake Lane. He
          asked if street parking would be allowed in front of the proposed lots. Vice Chair
          Whitelock pointed out the addition of four homes and driveways on the south
          side of Drake Lane. She noted that the homes would eliminate some of the space
          for on-street parking that had been considered in approval of existing
          development on the north side of Drake Lane. Vice Chair Whitelock expressed
          deep concerns about adding more houses at the subject location because of
          parking issues. She was unsure how long Majestic Elementary Arts Academy
          would be able to stay on the neighboring property. Vice Chair Whitelock also
          commented that her nephew lived at Brinkerhoff Bluffs and loved it.

          Council Office Director Alan Anderson referred to the summary of a discussion of
          parking and development on Drake Lane in the December 20, 2023 Council
