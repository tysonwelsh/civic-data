2.  Public hearing and possible action for the Fox Run South Lot 1 Amended, FRS-1 
located at 355 Fox Run South, Wanship, Mark Pearl, Applicant. – Tiffanie Northrup-
Robinson, Senior Planner. 
 
Planner Northrup-Robinson shared the background for this application. This application 
is for a plat amendment for a 9.7-acre parcel. The subdivision was originally approved in 
1999. The applicant owns the parcel. A home was constructed in 2006. The applicant is 
proposing to add a new building pad for an accessory building south of the home across

Fox Run South road because there is not a lot of buildable area in the current building pad. 
There is a steep slope north of the current building pad limiting where an accessory 
building could be built within the existing building pad. The application was sent to all 
service providers for review, with only technical corrections received. Two public inquiries

out and run-offs were created on both sides of the road. Commissioner Sargent stated he 
would be more comfortable if Engineering verified there are no drainage problems. 
Chair Sonntag asked if there has been any communication with the owner of Lot 2. Mark 
Pearl answered that the owners of Lot 2 did not build where they were supposed to, but 
there will not be any impact to their view. They were contacted by the applicant and have

Commissioner Wilde asked if the well and the septic system are within the home’s 
building pad. Mark Pearl verified that they are.

Findings of Fact:  
1. According to the Summit County tax assessment rolls, Mark and Diana Pearl are the listed fee 
title owner of Parcel FRS-1.  
2. FRS-1 is approximately 9.20 acres in size.  
3. FRS-1 is located at 355 Fox Run South, Wanship, Utah.  
4. Parcel FRS-1 is in the Agriculture-10 (AG-10) Zoning District.  
5. The Fox Run South Subdivision was originally platted in 1999 and consisted of 5 lots, ranging 
from 6.22 to 14.86 acres.  
6. A single-family home was constructed on parcel FRS-1 in 2006, within the platted building 
pad.  
7. On July 14, 2021, an application was submitted to amend the existing Fox Run South 
Subdivision Lot 1 to include a new 0.09 acre building pad across the private road that would be 
used for construction of a 1,200 square foot accessory structure.  
8. Section 11-4-5. E (3)a “The Planning Commission shall be the Land Use Authority for all 
subdivision plat amendments that result in building pad adjustments, subdivision title changes, 
plat note revisions, altering of utility easements, vacations and all other amendments that do not 
affect a public or private road.

9. The proposed new building pad meets all the current setback requirements from the property 
lines and private road.  
10. The amendment will not result in an increase in density.  
11. Service providers have reviewed the plat for compliance with applicable standards. Minor 
technical corrections were requested and have been completed.  
12. Staff has reviewed the plat for compliance with applicable Development Code standards.  
 
Conclusions of Law:  
1. There is good cause for the amendment as the proposed amendment will locate the accessory 
structure in the most suitable location on the lot while meeting all current setback requirements.  
2. No increase in density would occur because of this amendment.  
 
Conditions of Approval:  
1. All service provider conditions shall be met. 
 
Chair Sonntag made a motion to approve the proposed Plat Amendment according to 
the Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law, and Conditions of Approval found in the Staff 
Report. Commissioner Benson seconded the motion.

4.  Public hearing and possible action for an amendment to Chapter 11-3-16, Chart of 
allowed and permitted uses, Open Space, Recreational (Motorized) and Open Space, 
Recreational (Nonmotorized) of Eastern Summit County Development Code. – Ray 
Milliner, Principal Planner. 
 
Planner Milliner shared the background for this application. A few years ago, when the 
Code was amended, the definition of Recreation Commercial motorized and nonmotorized 
was changed, but the Use Chart was not changed to reflect the change in the definition. The 
proposal is to eliminate the Open Space Recreational motorized and Open Space 
Recreational nonmotorized and replace them with Recreational Commercial motorized and 
nonmotorized on the Use Chart. No other changes to the Code are proposed. The

Conditional, Low Impact in the zones are to remain the same. Staff recommends the 
Planning Commission conduct a public hearing and forward a positive recommendation to 
the County Council. 
Chair Sonntag asked if there is a list of specific uses and asked if a motorcycle track, ATV 
rentals, and boat rentals will be treated the same. There needs to be the ability to have 
mitigations for these uses. There should be subsidiary uses that are not allowed, like a 
motorcycle track. Planner Milliner stated there has been a lot of discussion and review on 
the definitions, but the Use Chart was not updated to match the definitions. The intent is 
not to redo the definitions. 
Chair Sonntag stated that nonmotorized uses are benign, but motorized uses could have 
some pretty obnoxious uses. There may need to be a zone that is to be approved on a case-
by-case basis. Planner Milliner stated that there could be criteria to go along with the RC 
zone that will have its own list of uses. Chair Sonntag stated he would rather have a 
requirement to rezone to the RC zone than have a Conditional Use Permit in AG20, AG40, or 
AG80 for motorized recreation. Commissioner Peterson agreed that these uses should not 
be allowed in the AG20, AG40, and AG80 zones. Commissioner Sargent stated that change 
could be made when the RC Zone is added to the Code. Planner Milliner stated that the 
Code has been existing this way for a long time. Staff will discuss criteria for the RC Zone or 
revamp the definition.  
 
The Public Hearing was opened.

Steve Luczak stated that these changes are for public, commercial use. Private motorized 
use on your own farm or ranch is not regulated in Summit County. Mr. Luczak had a private 
track for private use and had a grading permit to support his track. When rezoning was 
done, everyone objected to limiting private recreational use on private property. Chair

use in an agricultural zone, but should have to rezone to Recreation Commercial (RC). That

then later discuss the RC zone uses.
