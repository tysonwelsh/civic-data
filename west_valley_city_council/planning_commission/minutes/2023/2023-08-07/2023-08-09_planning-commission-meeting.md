Chris Clifford
5143 W 3500 S
General Plan change from Mixed Use and Large Lot Residential to Small Lot Residential
and a zone change from A to RM
4.34 acres

Chris Clifford has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for three parcels totaling
4.34 acres located at 5143 W 3500 S. The property is currently zoned A (Agriculture, minimum
lot size ½ acre) with a General Plan designation of Mixed Use for approximately the north 230’
and Large Lot Residential (2 to 3 units/acre) for the rest of the property. At this location, the
Mixed Use designation includes Office and High Density Residential (12 to 20 units/acre). The
proposed zone is RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential) and the proposed General Plan
designation is Small Lot Residential (4 to 7 units/acre).

Surrounding zones include A to the south, west, and a portion of the east and R-1-8 (Single Unit
Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square feet) to the north and the remaining east
portion. Surrounding land uses include single family homes to the north, south, and east and
single family homes and a six-plex to the west. The north parcel includes a home that, according
to the Salt Lake County Assessor, was built in 1900. The two parcels to the south are vacant.

At the time this report was prepared, staff had received one email from an adjoining neighbor
who is opposed to this application. The email is included with this report.

Development Proposal

As shown in the attached concept plan and rendering, the applicant is proposing to demolish the
existing home and develop a 21-unit planned unit development (PUD) of twin homes and one
single unit dwelling. The proposed density is 4.8 units/acre. The streets would be public and
utilize the 44’ right-of-way allowed in Section 7-13-705 for planned unit developments (PUDs)
with attached units.

The Fire and Public Works Departments will require a cul-de-sac at the south end of the
property. In addition, the City’s ordinance requires 4.75 parking spaces for single unit dwellings
and twin homes dwellings. The applicant is aware of both of these requirements.

Last year, Mr. Clifford submitted a General Plan/zone change application on the same three
parcels along with an additional parcel to the south. The General Plan request was the same
(Small Lot Residential); however, the zoning requested was RS (Residential Sustainability). A
table comparing this previous proposal with the current proposal is below.

In reviewing this application, staff suggests that there are two primary questions to answer:

   1. Is this the right location for a twin home development?

   2. Does the quality of the project further City goals?

These two questions are explored below.

Question 1: Is this the right location for a twin home development?

RM Zone Conditions
According to the City’s zoning ordinance, zone changes to the RM zone can only be considered
by the City if the following four conditions are met:

   1. For properties without existing Structures which are proposed to be developed, the
      property must be a minimum of one acre and the Density must not exceed 12 units per
      acre. For properties with existing Structures proposed to be redeveloped, the property
      must be a minimum of 20,000 square feet and the Density must not exceed 20 units per
      acre. For properties that are west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West
      Valley Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station or the Redwood
      Junction light rail station, the Density may exceed 20 units per acre;

   2. The property shall meet any one of the following four requirements: (i) have access and
      Frontage on a Street with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet (for properties
      under two acres) or 100 feet (for properties two acres or larger) as indicated on the Major
      Street Plan; (ii) be adjacent to existing multifamily residential development on two sides;
      or (iii) be located west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West Valley
      Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station, or the Redwood Junction light
      rail station; or (iv) if the property is vacant, was vacant as of January 19, 2021, will be an
      exempt housing development under 24 CFR 100.304 or successor regulation with a
      density no greater than seven units per acre, and have access and Frontage on a Street
      with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet;

   3. A Development Agreement must be proposed with the zone change application that
      addresses Dwelling Unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, Landscaping, and project
        Amenities; and

   4. A traffic impact study prepared by a registered traffic engineer is required for projects
      with 100 or more Dwelling Units.

On the first and second conditions, the property has access and frontage onto 3500 South, which
has a planned right-of-way width of 115’. On the third condition, the applicant’s development
agreement standards are addressed later in this report. The fourth condition does not apply
because the project is under 100 units.

In the General Plan, the north 1.5 acres of the subject property includes the option of High
Density Residential, which has a density range of 12 to 20 units/acre. The remaining 2.8 acres of
the subject property is designated as Large Lot Residential, which has a density range of 2 to 3
units/acre. The table below summarizes the number of units anticipated in the General Plan at the
low and high ends of the density ranges for the subject property.

In summary, for a project to comply with the existing General Plan designation, the number of

As pointed out earlier, zoning in the surrounding neighborhoods is R-1-8. Typical density within
the R-1-8 zone is around 4.2 units/acre. The proposed density of the project of 4.8 units/acre is
closer to the typical density within the R-1-7 zone.




Question 2: Does the quality of the project further City goals?

As mentioned earlier, the proposed density of this project is somewhat higher than the density in
the surrounding neighborhoods. For PUDs, the City’s ordinance allows for a density increase;
however, any increase in density must be compensated by increased amenities and improved
design which are proportional to the density increase. The sections that follow address dwelling
unit size, exterior building features, and amenities.

Dwelling Unit Sizes
The City’s ordinance sets the minimum size for single unit dwellings, twin homes, and two unit
dwellings at 1,000 square feet. This ordinance amount is limited by State law unless a
development agreement requires a larger amount. The applicant is proposing a 1,650 square foot
minimum area for rambler units and a 2,400 square foot minimum for two-story unit. All units
would include basements. For comparison, the City’s ordinance sets the minimum size for a
three-bedroom townhome at 1,400 square feet.

Exterior Building Features
The applicant is proposing the following exterior building design features:
    primarily fiber cement siding exteriors with 10 to 20% of the exteriors as brick or stone,
    a minimum roof pitch of 6:12, and
    variations in designs and setbacks.

Amenities
The applicant is proposing the following project amenities:
    a pavilion placed in the common area,
    a playground, and
    a walkway around the perimeter of the property.

Staff Suggestions

In addition to the standards proposed by the applicant, staff recommends these additional
standards along with a revision to the applicant’s standards.

      Number of units: The number of units shall be no more than 21.
      Fencing: Perimeter fencing along the west, south, and east sides shall be 6’ tall vinyl. The
       perimeter fence along the north side shall be 6’ tall, pre-cast concrete in the same color
       and pattern used by the City along 4100 South. An entry feature shall also be included
       along 3500 South. A 5’ wide area of landscaping shall be included between the sidewalk
       and precast concrete wall along 3500 South.
      Architecture: At least one alternative front elevation shall be developed and built for at
       least 6 of the proposed units. The alternative front elevation shall be reviewed and
       approved by the Planning Commission during the subdivision plat review. Brick or stone
       shall be used on at least 20% of the exterior of all homes. There shall be at least a one-
       foot setback difference between twin home units within the same building.
      Right-of-way dedication: 17.5’ shall be dedicated along the entire frontage of 3500
       South.
      The right-of-way width for the extension of Kiowa Way shall be 54’ to accommodate
       parkstrip and sidewalk on both sides of the street.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. This application should be approved subject to a development agreement that

      recommended by staff in this report.
   2. Continuance.

   3. Denial. Given the Large Lot Residential designation in the General Plan, the RE zone is

BACKGROUND

Dave Lloret is requesting preliminary plat approval for the Advanced RV Subdivision. The subject
property is located at 4581 West 3500 South and is bordered on the east by a small parcel zoned
C-2, and the Lofts on 3500, a new planned residential community. Property to the south is zoned
residential and property to the west is zoned C-2.

Mr. Lloret would like to divide the existing parcel to create an additional lot in the southwest
corner of the property. He will retain ownership of the smaller .75 acre piece and has an interested
buyer looking to purchase the balance of the property, approximately 4 acres, for a future
commercial use.

Access to the new lot would be gained by an access easement recorded as part of the plat. The
other access points from 3500 South will remain in effect as they are currently being used. The
applicant has coordinated the subdivision with UDOT and as no new or additional improvements
are needed, has received approval from UDOT.

As these properties further develop, conditional and/or permitted use applications will likely be
required in the future. Owners of these properties will work with staff for those applications. Staff
did inquire as to whether the existing buildings would remain on the .75 acre piece and that answer
was yes.

Due to this application being a commercial subdivision, staff and agency comments will be better
addressed during the conditional and/or permitted use applications. The subdivision plat will
contain easements and other information applicable to the division of property, but does not
address site design issues typically found in commercial developments.

BACKGROUND

Mr. Russ Platt, representing the property owner, is requesting preliminary approval for a
residential development to be known as The Towns at 2351. The subject property was rezoned in
2021 from the C-2 zone to the RM Zone. The subject property is bordered on the north by existing
residential housing, the east by property owned by Granger Hunter Improvement District and Red
Oaks PUD and the south by the Red Oaks PUD.


STAFF/AGENCY COMMENTS:
Public Works Department:

      Authorization required of ditch/water users for any abandonment, relocation, piping or any
       other modifications to existing ditches or irrigation structures.

      Contact Salt Lake County Auditor’s Office for approval of street names and subdivision
       names.

      UDOT approval required for access on Redwood Road.

      Redwood Road Streetscape required along the frontage of this project.

      Revisions to plat required.

      Standard Utility Easements required.

Granger Hunter Improvement District:

      Project is subject to all GHID requirements and design standards.

      Proposed fire hydrant locations need to be shown on subdivision plat.

      Project shall meet all provisions of Fire Code relating to this type of development.

Building Division:

ISSUES:

Project Overview:

       The Towns at 2351 will be a residential community consisting of 24 units on 1.84 acres.
       This equates to an overall density of 13 units per acre. A development agreement was
       reviewed and approved during the rezoning of this property.

       Proposed townhomes will be 3 story units. Per the development agreement, there are to be
       3 different unit types with the smallest being 2059 square feet and the largest being 2,534
       square feet. Each unit will have a two-car garage accessed from a private road system.
       The architecture for this project is using modern elements. The materials will be stone,
       cement board and stucco. Units will include a roof top patio and two of the three unit types
       will be provided with a deck off the living area. Each unit will also have a fenced entry
       courtyard.

       The developer is suggesting that two color schemes be used for this project. The colored
       variations will need to be substantial enough so that one color does not dominate the
       project. Final colors for this project will be reviewed at final plat review.

       All townhome units will have a two-car garage and driveway for parking. The
       development agreement does require an additional 10 guest spaces for a parking ratio of
       4.4 spaces per unit. Setbacks for this project are per the site plan.

Other matters related to this project:

Access:

       Access to the subdivision will be gained from Redwood Road and then an internal private
       street and drive court network. The preliminary plat indicates that the street width for
       private rights-of-ways will be 26 feet of pavement with a modified curb and gutter system
       for drainage. Sidewalk is planned on one side of the street, but the project has an internal
       sidewalk network as well.

       The internal design of the subdivision provides ample access and turnaround capabilities
       for fire needs. While the main road is 26 feet of asphalt, the drive courts will be 24 feet in
       width.

Building Setbacks and Height Requirements:

       As mentioned previously, setbacks for this project are illustrated on the site plan. Staff is
       comfortable with the setbacks which are: For buildings facing Redwood Road, the side
       setback is to be 10 feet. For other buildings along the north property line the setback shall
       be 15 feet and the south setback line adjacent to Red Oaks will be 25 feet.

Landscaping:

       While landscaping is generally addressed at the final plat stage, staff would like to mention
       a few things at this point in the review process. The overall open space comprises an area
       equal to 50% of the project site. Roof top patios are included in this calculation. Staff will
       review a formal landscape plan at final plat. The developer did extend the sidewalk system
       so the ability to walk around the community is available.

       A question was raised during the study session as to whether the sidewalk needed to be
       extended to the south property line. Staff believes this would be redundant as the sidewalk
       is located within 20 feet of that area along the rear of those units backing on to the open
       space. The ability to walk around the community exists with the proposed walkways.
      The developer will be required to install the Redwood Road Streetscape across the entire
      frontage of the project. The site plan accounts for the correct spacing for the parkstrip,
      sidewalk and lighting. Mr. Platt is familiar with this standard as he installed these
      improvements at Cobalt Landing on Redwood Road.

Amenities:

      Per the development agreement, a playground, picnic area and dog run will be provided.
      While the walking path mentioned previously is not required, it does add an additional
      amenity for this development.

Entry Feature:

      The project will require an entry feature. There is ample room at the entrance to this project
      to locate something to identify the community. Staff will work with the applicant as the
      project moves forward regarding plans for the sign.

Lighting:

      The project site will require internal street lights. In addition to the street lights, there will
      need to be lighting within and around the open space areas. Staff will recommend that a
      lighting plan be submitted at final plat.

Soils Report:

      As with all residential development, a soils report will be required. Although basements
      are not planned, the report will be used by the Building Inspections Division for
      construction recommendations and the report will also help with construction of the private
      street. Basements are not planned for this project.

Storm Drainage:

      Due to the limited area of this project, storm water will be collected in an underground
      stormtech system. This system will collect storm water generated from the site and is
      proposed to be located under the guest parking areas. Management and maintenance of
      this system will be the responsibility of the HOA. The City Engineering Division is
      reviewing the system to ensure the capacity being proposed will work for the site.

Garbage Collection:

      The development has been designed with a private street and drive court pattern. This will
      allow all residents to have their own garbage can. Dumpsters will not be needed in this
      project. The developer will need to coordinate garbage collection with a private agency
      for collection.
Fencing:

      The development agreement calls for a vinyl fence to be located along the north and south
      property lines. The east line is already fenced with vinyl fencing. During the study session,
      the Planning Commission inquired as to options for different materials, i.e., composite
      fencing. Staff discussed this with Mr. Platt and he was going to research some options to
      see if the property owner is willing to modify the fencing requirement as approved.
