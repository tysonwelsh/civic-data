D. THOUGHT OF THE DAY: Commissioner Froerer: A week ago today we had a little thing called an election. We
all have received a number of emails questioning the validity and integrity of that election. A poll said 85% of people
in Utah felt the results were (reliable). We had a chance yesterday with our Elections team to witness and participate
in an audit of 900 ballots selected by the state. I can say without a doubt at least from my experience when I viewed
what they went through with checking signatures and checking the ballot against what was put into the process was
100% accurate based on the audit yesterday. To the 15% that question the validity of the election, you have the right
to go witness the work done by Ricky Hatch’s team. This election was valid, every vote was counted, and we can be
secure that Ricky Hatch’s office did a great job.
Chair Jenkins: I have to agree with you. It was a lot of fun to watch the detail they go through. I was impressed by
the effort to make sure the votes were cured.

G. ACTION ITEMS:
     1. APPROVE DISCUSSION AND/OR ACTION ON THE 2022 TRANSPORTATION PRIORITY
        RECOMMENDATIONS.
            Brooke Stewart (Community Development): The County Sales and Use Tax statute, for transportation,
            requires that the Council of Governments create a transportation priority list and submit it to the Legislative
            Body for approval each year.

            Last week WACOG was presented with a detailed review of the 2022 transportation project applications. 11
            applications were received for consideration, totaling just over $37 million dollars in funding requests.

            WACOG unanimously approved a recommendation for funding of $2,750,000 in Corridor Preservation
            Funds and $18,833,000 in Sales Tax Funds, to be programmed as outlined on the 2022 Transportation
            Priority List.

            The recommendation includes funding for projects located in Pleasant View & Farr West Cities, Weber
            County, Ogden City, West Haven City and a transit project from UTA.
            The Corridor Preservation funds are programmed for the year 2024 and the Sales Tax funds are programmed
            for 2025
         Commissioner Froerer moved to approve the 2022 Transportation Priority Recommendations; Commissioner
         Froerer seconded.

    6. APPROVAL OF A FIRST READING OF AN ORDINANCE CHANGING SEWER SERVICE FEES.
         Scott Parke (Comptroller): Sewer fees have not been raised in several years. The proposed minor adjustment
         in the fees reflect the effects of inflation and past fee increases from Central Weber Sewer that have often been
         absorbed by the County. This Ordinance will separate the County’s and Central Weber’s charges allowing for
         pass through of any increases from Central Weber; customers will see one bill with two line items for the
         separate charges.
         Commissioner Froerer moved for approval of first reading of an ordinance changing sewer service fees;
         Commissioner Harvey seconded.
