    3. REAL ESTATE CONTRACT FOR THE SALE OF PROPERTY LOCATED AT 701 W. 12TH STREET TO THE
       STATE OF UTAH, DIVISION OF FACILITIES & CONSTRUCTION MANAGEMENT, FOR A YOUTH
       CENTER – CONTRACT C2015-55
         Kevin McLeod, of County Operations, stated that it has been a long process to sell the property to the
         State for the Juvenile Detention Center. Major contract changes include increasing the selling price
         from the original proposed amount by the State of $1.09 million to $1.225 million due to a re-
         evaluation of the property, and increasing the $150,000 that the State originally proposed as its
         participation amount for the construction of the new roadway off 12th Street (the new east entrance
         road) to $250,000.
1
       Commissioner Bell noted that the county purchased the property over a year ago as part of the plan
       for the State to build the youth detention center, and that it was also to help Marriott Slaterville City
       (for a business that needed to come in). This resulted in the need for a land swap.

       Mr. McLeod noted that there has been some question as to why the county would sell property
       purchased for over $2 million for $1.225 million and explained that this transaction is for only about
       7.5 acres of the 11 acres. Commissioner Bell concurred, and he stated that the 12th Street frontage is
       still in the county’s name. Chris Allred, County Attorney, had no questions or comments.
       Commissioner Ebert noted that the Youth Center’s concept is for consolidation of services, which is
       not the current practice. He supports the planned consolidation.
       Commissioner Ebert moved to approve Contract C2015-55 for sale of property located at 701 W. 12th
       Street to the State of Utah, Division of Facilities and Construction Management, for a Youth Center;
       Commissioner Bell seconded.
       Commissioner Ebert – aye; Commissioner Bell – aye

       and Parks Board for approval of the 2015 grant recommendations; Commissioner Bell seconded.

G. PUBLIC COMMENTS:
       Doug Hansen, of West Weber, spoke about the Little Weber cut-off project for flood mitigation. He
       said that he found out new information this past week that changed the calculations that he had come
       up with previously. He has been speaking to this item for weeks but had not been aware that at the
       end of this canal there is a water dam, which means water going into it leaves slower and can be held
       up, which also means the project requires a larger facility/cement canal. He said that originally the
       design of this canal was to be 10 feet deep and had a dam at its end because the bottom of the canal
       would be lower than the Little Weber channel into which it was to flow, and that he now understands
       it had to be redesigned to 8 feet. He said that the amount of changes depends on how this is
       calculated and that he submitted one alternative to the County Engineer this morning to see if it is of
       value and if a design change is possible. He wants to meet/work with county staff on details of this
       situation. Commissioner Ebert noted that as Mr. Hansen has come to the commissioners with
       concerns they have contacted the engineers and that it appears they have provided him with some
       answers as to how to address those concerns. They continue to listen and have open dialogue.
       Larry Hansen, of Warren, said that there was a presentation (on 2/17/2015) on the Ogden Bay
       Waterfowl Management Area Habitat Management Plan and the county was praised for its
       contributions to rehabilitation, however, the county came onto his property and destroyed more than
       half of his duck nests in 2013. During the study his property had shown to have 30 times more wood
       ducks hatch than Ogden Bay. He said that the county has not fulfilled its promise to put back what it
       had destroyed and to make him whole—this was 15 months ago. He said that Lance Peterson (of
       County Emergency Services) and the County Engineer both had stated that the county would rebuild
       and put things back, but this has not occurred. He has made several attempts to meet with the
       Commission, the Engineer, and Mr. Peterson but has been unsuccessful, therefore, he asked for
       financial compensation to be used to hire someone to put the habitat back as soon as possible.
