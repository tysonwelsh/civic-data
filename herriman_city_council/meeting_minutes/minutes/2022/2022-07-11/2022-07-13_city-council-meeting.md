and noted the school district would not participate again if the Council did not consider this.
Mayor Palmer asked if there was a financial drawback to the city if the extensions were

    2.2. Community Development Block Grant Discussion – Wendy Thomas, Assistant
         City Manager
Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas said in 2020 Herriman had become an entitlement
city, which entitled them to CDBG grant money because the population was over 50,000
people. She explained in the past, the city had contracted with Salt Lake County as a
member of the Urban County Community Development Grant to apply for funding relating
to the installation of ADA ramps. She elaborated it was not a large amount of money, and in
2020, it was estimated at approximately $145,000. She stated there had been an automatic
renewal fee, which was received prior to being aware of the status change. She said going
into 2023, the city wanted to be more proactive with the program. She requested direction
from the Council as to how they would like staff to proceed and explained there were three
avenues which could be explored. First option would be to remain with Salt Lake County
and file for grants as done previously. Secondly, the city could become an entitlement
July 13, 2022 – City Council Minutes                                         Page 3 of 18


grantee under HUD, which would require a lot of staff time to submit plans and federal
grants. Lastly, the city could enter into an Interlocal Agreement with the Urban Community
Development program similarly to what Millcreek had done.                 She offered staff
recommendation to go with the third option presented.

City Planner Michael Malloy said he reached out and asking for recommendations on
consultants and services, but he had not found any. He opined there wasn’t much of a
market for this service in the area as several jurisdictions contracted with the County or
State. Assistant City Manager Thomas observed the financial burden of bringing the work
in-house. She reported it cost Millcreek 35-percent of the entitlement funds to conduct the
reporting. The Council agreed to move forward with pursuing the Interlocal Agreement
option.

    2.4. Results of the Internet Speed Service Survey – Tami Moody, Assistant City
         Manager
Assistant City Manager Tami Moody reported on the internet speed survey launched in
May. She observed the valuable information gained and noted there were surprising
results. She said there had been a great response rate with 2,211 completed responses and
218 partial responses with a 2-percent margin of error. She said the survey had been
designed to answer three questions, one being the need and availability of internet service
in the City. Two, the interest of business and residents in a city-owned open access
network, as well as the interest of residents and businesses in a utility model network. She
showed a geographical map which indicated results had been captured from all areas of the
community.

Communications Manager Jonathan LaFollette said 98-percent of the respondents had said
internet was important in their home and 86-percent of those respondents indicated they
used internet to work full-time or part-time from home. 74-percent usage was reported for
schooling purposes. Manager Lafollette continued with the question pertaining to current
internet providers where 70-percent reported contracting services with Xfinity while 26-
percent used CenturyLink. It was noted the preferred choices were based on speed and
reliability. He said the responses indicated most people were concerned about speed,
July 13, 2022 – City Council Minutes                                          Page 4 of 18


performance, and affordability and 82-percent of respondents paid at least $60 per month.
This statistic showed residents were paying as much or more than what was typically
available on an open access network; however, were receiving slower speeds that what was
available on fiber networks. He discussed customer satisfaction and reported the greatest
satisfaction for customers was reliability, followed by speed, and a majority were
dissatisfied with current costs.

Assistant City Manager Moody said residents were asked about their thoughts on both city-
owned open access fiber networks and a utility model and 90-percent of respondents were
interested or very interested in city-owned open access and 77-percent were interested or
somewhat interested in the utility model. She said the survey received over 2,500
comments regarding the likes and dislikes of the two models. It was noted people wanted a
clear understanding of how the project would be funded and how it would promote
competition. Respondents also desired faster internet speeds, more reliability, and more
affordable options. There was concern from respondents about the failed utopia model in
other cities and didn’t want Herriman to make the same mistakes.

Assistant City Manager Moody asked Council for direction.           Councilmember Shields
thought the City should move forward aggressively with an RFP.

Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn arrived at 6:10 p.m.

Councilmember Shields wanted to start building the network. City Manager Cherpeski said
staff could get information from DA Davidson and the RFP would give an option to see how
other jurisdictions were addressing this issue. He relayed his desire to conduct the process
in an open setting. Since the survey had been launched, two other firms reached out to the
City to be considered for the project. Mayor Palmer thought an RFP should be conducted
followed by appropriate due diligence, as this was a big decision. He said he wasn’t sold on
the utility model yet and asked if an RFP could be broad enough to allow for either model.
City Manager Cherpeski replied the RFP would be conducted to find the partner, after
which the model discussion would commence.

Councilmember Shields wanted to make sure this decision was based on the goal to provide

a network the city could leverage and own for future development. He opined to achieve

going to be conducted, it should be clear as to what the City was requesting. Mayor Palmer
asked for goals from the Council. Councilmember Ohrn said the goal needed to satisfy a
purpose which served the whole community by providing broadband service to enable
residents and the City to operate more efficiently. She said if this could be achieved, it
would justify the request for everyone in the community to contribute to the cost in an opt-
out utility model as this would allow individuals who oppose the decision, would have the
ability to not participate.

   2.5. Discussion of a proposed amendment to the Creek Ridge Master Development
        Agreement allowing rear decks to encroach into the setback on certain lots
        adjacent to a future park and church – Blake Thomas, Community Development
        Director
Community Development Director Thomas said an email had been received from a
representative of SLR outlining a proposal in the Creek Ridge development. He oriented the
Council to the location of the project, and said the developer wanted some of the lots
abutting the park and future church property to have decks constructed with a walk-out
basement which would encroach into the setbacks. Director Thomas indicated the first
request was within the confines of the Master Development Agreement (MDA), which only
adjusted for patios. He offered an excerpt of City Code indicating if decks were less than
30-inches from ground, they could encroach up to two-feet into the setback area. The
request was to have the decks be allowed five-feet from the property line which was more
than the two-foot allotment. Director Thomas interpreted City Code to protect neighboring
properties from encroachment as he highlighted the lots the amendment would affect. He
recalled a conversation with Building Official Cathryn Nelson regarding fire resistant
construction being warranted with structures installed within five feet of the property line,
which brought challenges to the request.

Director Thomas asked for comments and concerns about the request. It was asked if the
houses currently conformed to building codes, and Director Thomas replied smaller patios
could be installed which could be in compliance; however, suggested homeowners would
then ask the Council to allow larger decks to be constructed. Councilmember Hodges asked
if there were any conflicting codes. Director Thomas responded there was some conflicting
language between the proposal and City Code and explained the language outlined in the
MDA would override City Code to allow for the installation of the decks. He also noted
walk-out basements were not directly addressed by City Code.

Councilmember Henderson opined City Code was written for specific reasons and felt
everyone wanted exceptions. Councilmember Hodges asked for an example of an
extenuating circumstance for the City to allow something of this nature and voiced
concerns of decks hanging over open space.

Director Thomas recalled his concern with the MDA was the impact fees would be locked in
at an aggregate amount and it would not generate enough income to reimburse
infrastructure obligations as part of the original agreement. He continued with the options
being offered on homes which were visible from the park. Councilmember Hodges thought
