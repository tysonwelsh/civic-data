2.  Public Hearing to discuss the update to the Summit County Moderate Income 
Housing Plan/Report as required by Utah Code 17-27a-408. – Jeffery B. Jones, AICP, 
Economic Development Director 
 
Director Jones shared the background for this discussion. State Code requires at least four 
of twenty strategies be implemented and a report submitted by December 1, 2021. This 
year is just updating current strategies.

Components of growth consider net migration and net natural increase that equals a an 
estimated population increase of about 418 in 2020. Long-range population growth to the 
year 2065 is estimated to be 70,750. Historic population growth went from 29,736 in 2000 
to 43,076 in 2021 for an average annual growth rate of two percent. The forecasted 
average annual growth rate is a little less than one percent. Eastern Summit County is 
forecast to have a faster growth rate than Western Summit County. The average household 
size in Summit County is 2.77. 
The impact from COVID has resulted in an employment loss for Summit County in 2020 
that is rebounding. Employment growth continues to outpace population growth. 
Unemployment in September 2021 was 2.2 percent. There is not a lot of labor to draw 
from. The labor participation rate is high due to labor imports.  Annual seasonal 
employment demand is normally 4800 to 6200 jobs, with usually 2500 filled via the visa 
work program. 
The average age in the county is changing dramatically to an older population. There have 
not been any dramatic changes in race/ethnicity, with white non-Hispanic still being the 
majority of the population. 
Zillow median home values in Summit County (July 2019 to July 2020) are indexed 
between the 35th to 65th percentile, with the typical home price of $1,116,000, including a 
one-year increase in value of $184,000. All areas in the County are having an increase in 
home values. The area median income (AMI) for a family of four is $120,800. There are 
very few houses in the market that are priced as affordable to Moderate Income 
households, with only two available at 80 percent AMI (Average Median Income) or below.

Renting rates also had a rapid jump similar to sales jumps. An October survey found only 
25 rental units available in Summit County as advertised on KSL Rentler and The Park 
Record. Income qualification for deed restricted housing and median household income 
rental rates by area were shown and compared to the average earnings per job by zip code. 
The cost-of-living index includes groceries, housing, utilities, health, and miscellaneous, 
with Summit County being 134.9 percent of the national average. The average earnings per 
job are lower than the state and national averages. The high cost-of-living lowers the 
earnings. A lot of workers are imported into Summit County. Jobs have grown dramatically, 
but there is an increase in those workers living outside of the county. In 2020 non-primary 
households outpaced primary households. 49.9 percent of homes are vacant in 2021, with 
the majority of homes in Western Summit County being non-primary and the majority in 
Eastern Summit County being primary homes. 
The housing needs for Eastern Summit County renters is 44 units, and sales needs are 33 
units. The cost-burdened households at 30 to 50 percent AMI are almost 2000, and those at 
more than 50 percent AMI are 1,650. A housing policy is needed to address the cost burden 
and other concerns. The combined annual unit demand, not including Park City is 297 
units. 261 units were constructed in 2021. Eastern Summit County has an annual demand 
of 77 units. Only four were under construction in 2021, for a deficit of 73 units. If the 
County does not start getting affordable, deed-restricted housing, there will not be any 
benefit to the local population. If housing is available there is an advantage to work closer. 
There are a number of policies in place the County is working on to fulfill the State 
requirement.

Commissioner Clyde stated there is a lot of growth in the pipeline, including the Cedar 
Crest development, and an affordable housing component needs to be built in. Director 
Jones agreed that it would be really smart to create a requirement for affordable housing. 
Most housing will be beyond the reach of those who live and work in the County. There will

Commissioner Benson asked if there are any tools to work with the schools to build up

ways to build their own affordable housing units. There are currently no requirements for

1.  Work session discussion regarding a Conditional Use Permit for a 20,000 sf 
Indoor Riding arena for private use by property owners and owners of boarded 
horses, a 3,000 square foot horse barn, limited horse boarding, and other 
recreational activities limited in scope by specific proposed conditions, located at 
1961 W State Road 32, Peoa, Parcel CAMP-2 (10 acres); Scott and Beth Cummings, 
Applicants. – Kirsten Whetstone, AICP, County Planner 
 
Planner Whetstone shared the background for this discussion. The applicants asked for a 
Work Session to discuss the proposal. The application has been noticed for another Public 
Hearing at the next meeting because there have been changes made to the application. The 
changes include a revised site plan with access from the north and the officially added barn 
with more details included. The applicants have included suggested possibilities for 
addressing any concerns and emphasize there will be very limited boarding. The applicants 
are working with the property owner to the north to get an access easement. 
Commissioner Peterson stated that the Planning Commissioners received emailed Public 
Comment that states that a six-house development was just approved to the north of the 
applicant’s property. Is the developer aware of this current application? Planner

Whetstone stated that the developer is getting infrastructure in place and the plat has

Scott Cummings, the applicant stated they have a signed agreement to allow the north 
access. The barn and the arena will be on about 5.6 percent of the entire ten-acre parcel. 
The arena is meant to be a private arena with standard horse property needs. There will be 
two outdoor riding arenas, two large pastures, and plans for a future tiny home.  
The primary changes to the application were discussed. The negotiated northern entrance 
from SR32 is a paved road, and will mitigate the concerns with the south and west gravel 
roads that will still be used as secondary and emergency access. There is a realigned site 
plan that will have a better view with a second outdoor arena and reduce the size of the 
southwest parking area. The arena is now oriented east to west, there will be a fourteen-
stall barn, and limited trailer traffic. There is a proposed landscaping plan with proposed 
landscaping around the buildings. There are five water shares for irrigation and one for 
culinary. The applicant plans to buy another one and a half shares of culinary water. The 
orientation is better with the new site plan and should minimize the view from the south 
and from the main road. There will be a shorter view of the arenas from the east and west.

buildings will also be included. The applicant would prefer the barn to be wood, but they
