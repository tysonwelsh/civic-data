c. Consideration of Approval, for Village at Plum Creek Phase 5 at 620 Plum Creek Lane. (Attachment
 2c)
 Mr. Josh Best stated he has purchased the last phases of Plum Creek, indicating Phase 5 had
 previously been approved for 20 units, and no changes have been made since that time. Because
 the approval has now expired, he is again requesting it be approved. He stated Phases 1-3 is
 complete and Phase 4 is currently under construction.
 Ms. Hale stated this consideration is based on a conditional use permit which was issued in 2017 for
 20 units consisting of four 4-plexes and two duplexes along Plum Creek Lane with five units to be
 serviced by a driveway as well as common area. She noted existing open space and fencing has not
 been well maintained in previous phases and the existing broken fence on 7th Street needs to be
 replaced before this phase can be recorded. The Commission is to consider whether the proposal is
 in keeping with the Group Dwelling Ordinance and the existing conditional use permit conditions as
 well as whether they fit into the overall group dwelling project. She also indicated Building Services
 is requiring a geotechnical report and assurance the existing retaining system is stable.
 Maintenance of amenities is required, as required by the CC&R’s, The proposal, as submitted is
 consistent with the subdivision and zoning regulations and is being reviewed by City departmental
 staff and their corrections and conditions of approval are applicable. Staff recommendation is for
 approval subject to satisfying all departmental comments, including the fence being repaired prior to
 the plat being recorded.
 Commissioner Blaisdell asked if the geotechnical report should be completed prior to its being
 approved. Ms. Hale stated the Staff review comment indicated it is required prior to any building
 permit being obtained. Vice-Chair Sandau noted the elevation for Phase 4 had been raised and
 asked if there had been any approvals of dirt brought in and stability of units in that area, stating
 there may be geologic issues. Ms. Hale commented there is no mention of fill being brought in in any
 of the previous Staff reports. Commissioner Schade stated the site plan and landscaping plan have
 some conflicts as common area is identified in the same place as a residential driveway, indicating
 there may be some errors in the plat. Commissioner Southwick expressed concern about requiring
    a new owner to be responsible for common open space of previous phases which have not been
    maintained. Ms. Hale clarified her recommendation is that these items be required prior to the plat
    being recorded.

    reviewed by City Staff, and once the geotechnical report is obtained determination will be made as to
    whether the site plan will need to be modified. She stated the overall project was initially approved in
    1999 and since that time the total number of units was identified. At that time, the Fire Marshal
    determined a single access is adequate for fire safety due to the installation of hydrants. Since that
    time, various developers have completed phases of the project. She stated the Group Dwelling
    Ordinance requires the perimeter of the project to be fenced, and the conditional use permit identifies

    Vice-Chair Sandau expressed concern about the amount of fill dirt that has been brought to the site

3. Preliminary Subdivision Approval, Liberty Subdivision 1000 Liberty Avenue. (Attachment 3)
  Mr. Cody Rhees stated this subdivision had previously been approved but expired prior to the plat
  being recorded. There have been no changes since the previous approval, and the submitted road
  design had been approved by Engineering Staff at that time, stating while an earlier concept connected
  to Gramercy, it is not proposed. He stated all remnant parcels will be deeded to Ogden City and
  become part of the street and storm water system.
  Mr. Rodriguez stated this subdivision had been reviewed by the Planning Commission in January 2019
  and its approval has now expired. The proposal is for 13 lots served by a public right-of-way. The
  property is zoned R-1-6 and is in the Hillcrest-Bonneville community, consisting of seven parcels which
  have never been developed, two of which have no frontage. Two lots currently have homes and
  access is provided between these to service 11 new residential lots. As the new road creates corner
  lots, two of these do not meet the lot area requirements and a variance from the Board of Adjustment is
  required prior to the plat being recorded. While a variance had been granted in 2018 for a reduced lot
  area for these, that approval also has expired. He stated while the existing homes meet setback
  requirements but because they are now corner lots, the required lot width is increased.
  He reviewed the previously approved plat in relation to the proposed plat, stating the right-of-way had
  been modified to satisfy Engineering standards. The project does create two remnant parcels which
  are not developable, and the applicant is proposing to dedicate these to Ogden City. Staff is
  concerned as to how these will appear, whether they will be landscaped, and how they would be
  maintained. He stated utilities can be provided and an additional eleven lots will have little impact on
  traffic in the community. While an existing irrigation ditch traverses the property, all water rights are
  required to remain as they currently exist. Staff recommendation is for approval subject to obtaining
  variances for the reduced lot widths for Lots 101 and 113, obtaining and satisfying all departmental
  requirements, and working with the ditch company to assure irrigation rights remain for existing water
  users.
  Commissioner Schade noted there is an engineering standard which requires 300’ between
  intersections, which is not in place with this new road to be dedicated and asked if Engineering has any
  concerns with its location.
  Mr. Terry Kerr, 1020 Liberty, stated his property backs onto this project. He expressed concern about
  care of the proposed remnant parcels as one is directly behind his home. He stated there are many
  junk trees in this area which should be removed, stating the area has long been neglected. He
  expressed concern that water use may be required to be reduced and wanted to be assured irrigation
  rights are maintained, particularly during construction. He further described water service in the area
  and how water can continue to be used by residents for irrigation.
  Vice-Chair Sandau read an email which had been received which expressed concern about water
  rights, trees and traffic in the community.
  Mr. Mitch Garrison, 785 Cross, stated the existing width of Liberty Avenue is inadequate in this area
  and its pavement width needs to be widened. He felt a single access to service 11 lots is inadequate
  and asked if a fence will be installed to surround the property.
  Mr. Rhees stated much of the secondary water line is currently covered with trees and foliage which
  will be removed and will actually improve the amount of water available. He described how the water
  will be r outed and piped under the roadway with areas where it can be maintained. He stated he is
  willing to work with existing residents to assure their needs are satisfied. Junk trees on the site will be
  removed and while no fence is proposed existing fencing on the west boundary will be improved. He
  stated in addition to the lot width variances, he had previously received a variance for a reduced road
  width. He stated there is no fire code requirements for a second access and the proposed cul-de-sac
  will provide adequate turning for emergency vehicles. In the chat service on Zoom, Kay Webb asked
  about the height of the homes. Mr. Rhees responded they would be 1-2 story with no basement,
  approximately 20’-23’ in height.
  Commission asked about the remnant parcels being created. Mr. Rodriguez responded that so far as
  he has been informed, there is no plan for these parcels as to their ongoing maintenance, and Staff
  recommendation includes that there will be plans on how these are to be developed and maintained in
  the future. Commissioner Safsten asked about the variance for the road width which the applicant
  noted, but was not identified by Staff. Mr. Rodriguez he understands this is for an Engineering
  standard which requires intersections to be separated by 300’, indicating the proposed access into the
  subdivision is less than 300’ from Cross Street. Commissioner Schade expressed concern about the
  proposed remnant parcels and their upkeep if deeded to Ogden City, noting there is no advantage to
  Ogden City to accept these parcels because of ongoing maintenance requirements. He also noted the
  Ordinance requires that these be resolved, and felt Staff can work out the details.
  MOTION:       A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to recommend approval of the proposed
                subdivision based on the findings the proposed lot dimensions can conform with zoning
                and subdivision standards, there is sufficient water supply and coverage disposal to meet
                demands of the proposed subdivision, all areas of potential hazard due to soil or
                topographical conditions have been identified and can be utilized in efficient ways, the
                proposed subdivision will not result in undeveloped parcels lacking municipal services or
                the prevention of the City’s ability to serve other properties and impacts on public health
                safety and welfare are mitigated. Approval is subject to the following conditions:
                1. Variances being obtained from the Board of Zoning Adjustment to reduce the
                    minimum width for corner lots 101 and 113;
                2. Obtaining and satisfying all departmental Staff and private utility requirements; and
                3. Developing and maintaining access to the irrigation ditch that intersects the
                    subdivision.
                Motion was seconded by Commissioner Southwick and passed unanimously with
                Commissioners Blaisdell, Safsten, Schade, Southwick and Sandau voting aye.

5. Consideration of Boundary Adjustment, between Ogden City and North Ogden City at 1512-1514
   Washington Boulevard. (Attachment 5)
  Mr. Brandon Bell stated this is a request from North Ogden City to address a development project
  where property owned by the same person extends from North Ogden into Ogden City. He stated the
  project has been reviewed by North Ogden and a rezoning has been approved subject to conditions of
  a development agreement. One of these conditions is that the property within Ogden City be
  incorporated into North Ogden so it can be developed as one project. He stated North Ogden has
  committed to accepting the property.
  Mr. Danny Wall and Mr. Rick Scadden identified themselves as the developers of the property. Mr. Hill
  stated their project has received approval from North Ogden City subject to the boundaries between
  the two cities being realigned so the project would be within North Ogden City limits. He stated the
  recommendations of Staff include a 10’ wide landscaped setback between the property and adjacent
  mobile home park residents to the south as well as a 7’ privacy fence being installed. He indicated the
  existing site plan allows only for a 5’ buffer between the two uses. He stated the proposed portion of
  the project in Ogden City consists of two buildings and 157 total residential units with a road to be
  constructed at the existing boundary line between the two cities. Mr. Scadden stated the main purpose
  of the petition is so the entire project would be serviced by the same utility and emergency service.
  Mr. Rodriguez stated North Ogden City has requested a boundary adjustment between the two cities in
  an effort to accommodate a proposed development. There are currently two parcels, with 1.7 acres
  within Ogden City and the bulk of the 5-acre project in North Ogden. The Mt. Lewis Community Plan
  does not specifically address this property, but it is somewhat isolated from other uses in the R-2 zone
  as a mobile home park exists to the south. He reviewed the development proposal stating it would
  have a commercial component as well as multi-family housing with a public road to be dedicated which
  would line up with the existing street to the west. He stated the existing uses are vacant single-family
  homes on the 5-acre property in North Ogden and vacant land zoned for R-2 residential in Ogden City.
  He stated the redevelopment of these parcels as well as permitting and providing municipal services
  would be simplified if the boundary adjustment were approved, while there would be a negative tax loss
  to Ogden City. Staff recommendation is for approval, subject to North Ogden City requiring a minimum
  of a 10’ landscaped buffer between the boundary of the existing mobile home park and the project and
  that North Ogden City complete the subdivision plat for the boundary adjustment.
  Commissioner Schade suggested a 7’ sound wall may provide a better buffer between the two uses
  than 10’ of landscaping. Mr. Rodriguez responded while a wall was a recommendation of a previous
  staff report, that recommendation also included a minimum of 8’ landscape buffer area containing
  upright evergreen trees such as emerald arborvitae. Discussion continued relating to a previous
  recommendation for a 30’ buffer. Mr. Rodriguez responded as the adjacent use is a building rather
  than a parking lot, this was reduced to 10’. Commissioner Safsten expressed concern about the ability
  of conditions of approval to be enforced if the municipal boundaries were to change as Ogden would
  no longer have jurisdiction. Mr. Montgomery explained the adjustment would include execution of a
  mutual agreement between the two cities, and the conditions of the Development Agreement
  established and approved by North Ogden are applicable. Commission further discussed the proposed
  landscape buffer and other options of screening the use to the north. It was felt the installation of trees
  and shrubs would be as effective as a sound wall.
  MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to recommend approval of the proposed
          boundary adjustment based on the findings the request will not adversely affect the City
          and is in keeping with the General Plan. Approval is subject to a 10’ landscaped buffer be
          installed between the project and the newly created City boundary to consist of planting of
          emerald arborvitae and shrubs to create a noise barrier, allowing both City Councils to work
          out details. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Schade and passed 4-1 with
          Commissioners Blaisdell, Safsten, Schade and Sandau voting aye and Commissioner
          Southwick voting no. Commissioner Southwick felt the action sets a bad precedent and is
          poor public policy

6. Consideration to Amend PRUD, Pointe at Stone Mountain Phase 2, Lot 15, 4757 S 1550 E.
  (Attachment 6)

  Ms. Vickie Turco, representing the homeowners association of Pointe at Stone Mountain, stated this
  request is change an existing building lot to common open space. She explained the lot is steep and
  had been offered for sale previously but due to its slope, was never purchased. She stated if a home
  were to be built here, it would be too close to other homes in the area. The HOA has now purchased it
  as a tax sale and desire it to become common open space.
  Ms. Patrick stated the property is zoned R-1-10 and while it meets the lot area requirements for a
  building lot, its slope affects its ability to be developed. She stated the Mayor has the authority to grant
  an executive order to allow its use as common open space and amend the PRUD upon Planning
  Commission’s recommendation. She stated the HOA considers Lot 15 as unbuildable as any home
  would be too close to other existing homes and feel due to its slope it would better serve the residents
  if converted to common open space. She stated its conversion would be consistent with the General
  Plan and would be in the best interest of the community. Staff recommends approval of amending the
  PRUD by vacating Lot #15 to allow it to be common open space.
  Commissioner Schade asked how the lot would be maintained. Ms. Patrick answered as it would be
  owned by the HOA, it would be their responsibility to maintain its appearance, and they are aware and
  willing to do so.
  Mr. David Dunn, 4771 S 1500 E. stated the creation of Lot 15 as a building lot was a mistake, and was
  a cash grab by the developer. He stated if a home were to be constructed on Lot 15 it would be only 2-
  3’ from the next home. He stated converting it to open space would save it from being purchased and
  built upon in the future. He expressed support of the lot being converted to common open space,
  indicating all residents as well as the HOA are supportive of the proposed action.

  MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to recommend approval of the
          PRUD amendment to convert Lot 15 to common open space and recommend the Mayor
          execute an executive order to amend the plat as proposed based on the findings vacating
          Unit 15 encourages better utilization of land, develops a better sense of community and
          ensures the continued compatibility with the surrounding neighborhood. Motion was
          seconded by Commissioner Schade and passed unanimously with Commissioners
          Blaisdell, Safsten, Schade, Southwick and Sandau voting aye.

  Mr. Vaughn Jacobsen indicated he has developed the first two phases of Sunset Ridge and has sold
  the remaining phases. Mr. Daniel Stephenson is present, representing phases 3 and 4. The request is
  to amend the layout of future lots in an effort to preserve vegetation and other unforeseen items which
  were not known at its initial approval. It also addresses ongoing maintenance of landscaping in both
  common areas and individual lots. He has received the recommendations of Staff and feels the
  requirement for a 6’ fence along Harrison serves no purpose due to the hillside.
  Mr. Daniel Stephenson asked about the recommendation to remove a fence on Sunset Valley Drive,
  indicating the homeowner had installed their own 6’ solid fence without permits and is within the require
  setback area, not complying with City regulations. He felt its removal and/or compliance should not be

8. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendments, to Titles 14, 15 and 17 of the Municipal Code to
   unify time of expiration of approvals for development.(Attachment 8)
  Mr. Montgomery explained the ordinances identify specific expiration times, and there has been some
  confusion as to whether approvals are valid. Some are valid for six months after approval while others
  are one year after corrections reports are sent. This proposal is to identify the beginning of the
  approval process as the application date, and for all approvals to be valid for 18 months past that date.
  He stated problems often occur when property changes hands, but also occur as approval is needed
  prior to financing being applied for, which often takes time. The proposed ordinance would allow a six-
  month extension to be approved by the Planning Commission. He stated both the Landmarks
  Commission and Board of Zoning Adjustment have expressed support of the proposed amendment.
  Commissioner Blaisdell felt the amendment makes sense. Commissioner Schade asked about the
  timing of the application, stating there may be times the planners are working on a project for several
  months. Mr. Montgomery responded the date of complete application, including the payment of fees,
  becomes the application date, and the 18 months would start at that time. While some may submit an
  application and pay the fees, but are missing important documents which need to be part of the review
  process, the application is considered incomplete. Commissioner Schade expressed frustration with
  projects which have been ongoing for a long time, and change hands several times, and the approval
  of 20 years ago remains valid, even though the ordinance requirements have changed. Mr.
  Montgomery explained once a subdivision is recorded, the Ordinance allows seven years for
  development for each phase, but once those seven years expire, the current ordinance would be
  applicable. Commission felt seven years is too long for such an approval. The exiting ordinance
  amendment would require building permits to be obtained within 18 months of the subdivision
  approval, and once permits are obtained, the time period for the second phase begins, again allowing
  seven years to obtain permits for each phase. The proposed Ordinance would still allow seven years
  for the initial permits, and all phases must be completed within seven years. If there has been no
  progress on a project within the initial 18 months, all approval becomes valid. Discussion continued
  relating to amenity requirements, indicating some of these are lost over time. It was indicated whether
  amenities are required within the first phase of whether each phase contains amenities based on the
  number of units have often been discussed, the current ordinance identifies a certain percentage of
  amenities is required before the next phase can be developed.
As there were no public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Schade to close the public
hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously.
