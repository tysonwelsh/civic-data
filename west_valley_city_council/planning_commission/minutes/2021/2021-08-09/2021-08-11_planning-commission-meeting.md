Z-5-2021
Brent Beutler
3532 South 5450 West
Zone change from R-2-8 to R-1-8
0.43 acres


Brent Beutler has requested a zone change on a 0.43-acre parcel at 3532 South 5450 West from
R-2-8 (Two Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square feet) to R-1-8 (Single
Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square feet). Surrounding zones include R-1-
8 to the east and south, R-2-8 to the north, and RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential) to the
west. Surrounding uses include homes to the north, east, and south and a future car wash to the
west. The subject property is designated as mixed use in the General Plan, which anticipates
office and residential uses.

Development Proposal

The subject property includes a home that was built in 1941. According to the applicant, he
would like to subdivide the property into two lots, keep the existing home on the south lot, and
build a new home on the north lot. While the existing lot is large enough in area to be subdivided
at 18,513 square feet, there is not sufficient lot width to allow for two lots without a development
agreement exception. The applicant is requesting that the lot width be 58’ for the south lot and
74’ for the north lot. For reference, the minimum lot width in the R-1-8 zone is 80’.

The existing zoning would allow for a twin home or duplex on the subject property if the
existing home was demolished. The owner would prefer to build a second home and is willing to
downzone the property if the lot width exception is granted.

Development Agreement

A development agreement is required for this project. Section 7-11-213 of the West Valley City
Zoning Ordinance states: “All new Subdivisions involving a rezone of property, or a PUD, shall
participate in a Development Agreement that addresses housing size, quality, exterior finish
materials, Streetscapes, Landscaping, etc.” For the development agreement, the applicant is
proposing to use the same standards that were used for a recent development agreement for
property at 6865 West 4100 South. These standards are attached for reference.
Staff Alternatives:

      Approval. The application should be approved. The recommendation for approval is
       made subject to a development agreement including the following standards:
          o The lot width of the two resulting lots may be reduced to 58’ and 74’.
          o The home built on the new lot shall follow the standards proposed by the
             applicant.

      Continuance.

      Denial.

BACKGROUND:

Mr. Shaun Johnson is requesting preliminary plat approval for the 2nd phase of the Weston Fields
Subdivision. The east portion of the subdivision is bordered by various phases of the Brookfield
Subdivision and phase 1 of Weston Fields, while the west portion of the subdivision is bordered
by property owned by Northrop Grumman, the Union Pacific Railroad and phase 1 of Weston
Fields. The subdivision is located at approximately 6840 West 4100 South and is zoned R-1-8
and RE.

ISSUES:

Overall Subdivision design:

The subdivision consists of 38 lots on 13 acres. This equates to an overall density of 2.9 units
per acre. This phase contains two separate areas each having different zoning. The eastern
portion of the subdivision is zoned R-1-8 and consists of 22 lots. The average lot size in the R-1-
8 zone is 9,380 square feet. For the portion that is zoned RE, there are 16 lots with an average
lot size of 15,022 square feet. All lots meet the frontage and area requirements of the R-1-8 and
RE zones.
Access:

The subdivision will gain access from 4100 South and from existing stub streets in phase 1 of the
Weston Fields Subdivision to the east. Although future residential development to the west is
not anticipated, the developer is providing a stub street between lots 204 and 205. The street
cross section used throughout the subdivision will be a 54-foot right-of-way which includes a 5-
foot sidewalk and 5-foot park strip. In addition to the internal streets, the developer will be
dedicating and improving 33-feet along 4100 South.


Grading/Drainage:

The proposed subdivision slopes downward approximately 30 feet from the railroad to 4100
South. The drop however, is over a large distance of approximately 900 feet. To address the
grade difference between lots, individual lot grading plans may be required prior to the issuance
of a building permit. Individual lot retention will be required to help alleviate potential runoff
problems from one lot to the next.

The City Engineering Division will thoroughly review the grading and drainage plans as the
subdivision moves through the review process. Should there be problems or concerns with
regards to existing or proposed grades, staff will address these with the developer and builder
during final plat review. During the course of final plat review, the engineering office will work
with the developers engineer to understand how the site will be contoured and what retaining
methods if any, will be used to ensure slope stability between residences.

There is a proposed storm drain detention basin in phase 1 to the east. Said basin will
accommodate storm water generated from both phases of the subdivision. During the review
process in phase 1, it was determined that the basin would become part of a platted lot with an
easement for City access. This will allow for better maintenance and upkeep of the basin by the
property owner of that lot.

Groundwater:

The developer has submitted a soils report for both phases of the Weston Fields Subdivision.
The report indicated that there was no ground water encountered to a depth of 14 feet.
Therefore, it is anticipated that all homes will have basements.

Fencing:

The west boundary of the subdivision is adjacent to agriculturally zoned property. City
ordinance will require a 6-foot chain link fence to be installed along the west boundary of those
lots. Similar fencing will be installed along the railroad.

There will also be a 6’ tall masonry wall along 4100 South. Since the City recently installed a
consistent style masonry wall along 4100 South between Bangerter Highway and 5460 West,
staff recommends the same style of masonry wall be installed for this subdivision.
Railroad:

An existing rail line is located at the south boundary of the subdivision. While developing the
Park Vista Subdivision to the north of 4100 South, a letter was submitted to the City when that
property was proposed for rezoning. The letter expressed concerns by Union Pacific about new
residential development near the rail line. Primarily, the concerns are noise, vibration,
trespassing along the railroad etc.

Staff has conveyed this information to the developer. During the rezone application, the City
Council required as part of the development agreement, that a note be placed on the plat that that
identifies the subdivision as being next to a rail line and will be subject to sounds associated with
an active rail line.

Overpressure Zone:

The proposed subdivision is located in the outer ring of the overpressure zone. City ordinance
will require that certain construction standards, specifically special windows be installed on all
new dwellings. This condition will be addressed with the building permit for all single-family
dwellings.

BACKGROUND

Mr. Ken Milne is requesting preliminary approval for a residential community to be known as
Heather Villas PUD. The subject property was rezoned in April 2021 from the A zone to the
RM zone. The subject property is bordered on the north by Parkway Blvd, the east by vacant
land owned by UDOT and the south and west by existing single family residential housing.


STAFF/AGENCY COMMENTS:

Public Works Department:

      Authorization required of ditch/water users for any abandonment, relocation, piping or
       any other modifications to existing ditches or irrigation structures.

      Contact Salt Lake County Auditor’s Office for approval of street names and subdivision
       names.

      Dedication and improvements required along Parkway Boulevard.

      Ensure that grade difference at property line is minimalized.

      Revisions to plat required.
      Flood control and drainage issues to be coordinated with Public Works.

      Research and determine status of existing 6100 West access road.

Utility Companies:

      Standard Utility Easements required.

Magna Water District:

      Project is subject to all requirements and design standards of the water district.

Fire Department:

      Proposed fire hydrant locations need to be shown on subdivision plat.

      Project shall meet all provisions of Fire Code relating to this type of development.

      Will require the developer to paint and/or post no parking in those areas where the street
       dead ends.

Building Division:

      Soils report will be required.


ISSUES:

Project Overview:

The Heather Villas PUD is a residential community for senior living which will consist of 84
units on 13.8 acres. This equates to an overall density of 6.1 units per acre. A club house is also
proposed for the site and will be located near the entrance to the project.

Residential units will be one story in height. All units will have a loft option that would add
approximately 900 square feet to each dwelling if selected by future buyers. The developer is
proposing units that are 1587, 1717, 1918 and 2024 square feet in size. All units will have a
large two-car garage of approximately 680 square feet.

Building architecture is very similar to that shown to the Planning Commission during the rezone
application but has been refined. Materials will be stone, fiber cement siding and stucco. All
units will have a private patio that is accessible from the dining and master bedroom areas. The
developer is proposing a minimum of 2 and potentially 3 different color schemes to be used
throughout the project to add variety.
As mentioned previously, all townhome units will have a large two-car garage for parking. All
units will have a driveway that will be sufficient in depth to allow parking. In addition to the
garage and driveway spaces, the developer is providing guest parking in and through the project
as well. At the present time, the site plan is showing a total of 386 parking spaces which equates
to 4.5 spaces per unit. Staff believes that some of the guest spaces at the end of the drive alleys
may need to be eliminated to provide more open space to meet the 50% requirement. Staff will
address this concern later in the report. Even with the elimination of those spaces, the project
will have more than enough resident and guest parking spaces.
Other topics related to this project:

Access:

Access to the subdivision will be gained from Parkway Boulevard. An existing but unimproved
right-of-way extending south from Parkway Boulevard and located along the west boundary of
the subdivision has been in existence for many years. This right-of-way has provided access to
two existing single-family dwellings for this same time. All indications are that this right-of-way
is private. The developer is having his title company research the chain of ownership which will
need to be resolved prior to final plat submittal.

The developer has discussed the proposed method of access with the residents who live down the
lane. It is anticipated that property wherein the right-of-way is located will need to become part
of the subdivision. Access to the existing residents would continue to be gained from a fully
improved road which would have a perpetual access easement for the existing residents. In
addition, Mr. Milne has expressed to them that they would not be responsible for maintenance or
repair of the road. That would be handled by the HOA.

The project has a looped street system which will satisfy the fire department for access and
maneuverability. The right-of-way width for the main road will be 50 feet with sidewalk on one
side. All interior roads will be private as well and will need to have a minimum asphalt width of
26 feet. Per City policy, gutters are not allowed to be counted towards the asphalt width. Staff
will coordinate this with the City Engineering Division and developer. A sidewalk is proposed
around the interior of the project that will extend out to Parkway Boulevard.

Building Setbacks:

Setbacks for this project are illustrated on the site plan. According to the RM zoning standards,
the perimeter setbacks are required to be 20 feet. According to the site plan, setbacks along the
perimeter of the development range in size from 20-25 feet. As the site continues to be reviewed
and refined, setbacks may change, but a minimum of 20 feet is required. For those units that
may have a loft and are taller than 25 feet, which is unlikely, but possible, an additional setback
would be required for those buildings if located along the perimeter boundary of the subdivision.

Site Amenities:

The developer is proposing 3 amenities for this project. At the present time, the applicant is
proposing a club house of approximately 3,400 square feet to include a gathering room with
kitchen, theater room, fitness room and mail room. A walking path around the perimeter of the
development is also included. A pickle ball court is planned on the east side of the clubhouse.
Although not an amenity listed in the development agreement, each unit will have a private
courtyard or patio space with access from the kitchen and master bedroom.

Grading:

The site is relatively flat. However, staff has some concern with the grades along the south and
southwest corner of the property. The engineering division will review the existing grade and
proposed grading plan to ensure that water runoff will not be a problem for adjacent properties.
Staff understands that some elevation change is to be expected for drainage and sewer
requirements, but staff’s expectation is that the site will have a similar grade as it does today
when finished. The grading plan is currently being reviewed, and findings and recommendations
to address the concerns noted above will be made available to the developer.

Landscaping:

While landscaping is generally addressed at the final plat stage, staff would like to mention a few
things at this point in the review process. The overall open space requirement is 50%. Staff will
work with the developer to define what is needed for the landscaping percentage and what areas
can be counted toward that number. As mentioned earlier, staff believes that some of the guest
spaces at the end of the drive courts may need to be eliminated to meet the 50% requirement.
Staff will work with the developer to ensure that the 50% number is achieved. In some
instances, the engineer preparing these numbers may not fully understand what is and is not
considered open space. A landscaping and irrigation plan will need to be submitted prior to final
plat review.

As part of this project, a portion of Parkway Boulevard will be dedicated and improved. Staff
will work with the applicant to provide a landscape plan for the area along Parkway Boulevard
and the entrance to the subdivision. An entry feature will be required and reviewed during the
final plat application.

Fencing:

During the rezoning process, the developer agreed to install a 6-foot wood grained vinyl fence
around the project. The design of this fence has yet to be selected. This condition will be
evaluated during the final plat application.

Entry Feature:

The development agreement also specifies that an entry feature will be installed. The developer
is working on this item but has yet provided an illustration of what is being planned. The entry
feature will be installed near Parkway Boulevard. Staff will work with the developer as this
project moves forward. The entry feature can be shown to the Planning Commission during a
future study session.
Lighting:
The project site will require both street lights and internal pedestrian lighting. It is anticipated
that the developer is proposing to use is a 14-foot street light very similar to the City’s acorn
light along the street portions. The developer is still deciding how to light the sidewalk areas and
open space areas within the project. An overall lighting plan will be provided during the final
plat application.

Garbage Collection:

The development has been designed with a good street pattern. The hammerhead areas do create
some safety concerns especially backing out. The developer will need to coordinate with a waste
management company for the collection of garbage. It may be that those units in the drive alleys
that the owners need to wheel their cans to the main road. This will be a topic of discussion
between the developer and waste management company.

as light manufacturing. The surrounding zone is M, manufacturing on all sides. The surrounding
uses include light industrial/outside storage to the north and west (Rasmussen Equipment), office
uses to the south and east (Zions Bank).

This building was originally built in 1975 and was most recently used as a Zions Bank office.
They have since left the site and GMC would like to locate their offices here and utilize the west
side of the lot for outside storage. They are a dumpster rental and demolition company. The yard
would be used for dumpster storage and associated equipment as outlined in their use
description. The only time a full dumpster will be on site is for staging only, meaning the
landfills are closed for the day and the truck comes back to park. This does happen on occasion,
but the dumpster is tarped and left on the truck and never set on the ground. It is dumped the
following business day. There would be no outside storage of construction debris or any
hazardous materials brought to this site.

The outside storage area shall be limited to the west side of the site and screened from public
view. The applicant proposes a chain link fence with slats. The site is surfaced with asphalt to
accommodate parking as well as the outside storage.

There is existing landscaping on the south and east sides of the building. The were previously
very large overgrown evergreen tress that have ben removed. Two of those trees were replaced
however the other trees no longer exist. Therefore, staff recommends that a total of 7 trees be
planted in these setbacks. The area is narrow and there are overhead power lines so smaller a
smaller tree type should be chosen.

C-17-21
R5 Development - Eagle Point (Light Industrial Complex)
3076 S. Decker Lake Dr.
M zone, 1.0 acres

The applicant, R5 Development, is requesting conditional use approval for a multi-tenant Light
Industrial complex at 3076 S. Decker Lake Dr. The zoning for this property is M, Manufacturing
and the General Plan designation is Entertainment. This property is also located in the Decker
Lake Station Overlay Zone. The surrounding zone is RM, multi-unit residential to the east
(future apartments), C-2 to the south (Maverik Center) and M to the north and west (Hotels).

The proposed use for this property is a light industrial building. The building will be
approximately 14,000 square feet and will contain 4 tenant spaces. The building will be
constructed of stucco with a brick wainscot and steel canopy accent features. There will be large
glass overhead doors on both sides of the building (north and south) to accommodate a variety of
light industrial uses. The overhead doors on the south side of the building are currently designed
to be walk up only but the site and landscape plan could be modified in the future if a tenant
wants drive-up access there as well. The building design must comply with the Commercial
Design Standards.

Adequate parking has been proposed to accommodate a mix of light industrial and office type
uses. Staff has recommended possibly reworking the layout of the site to avoid parallel parking
stalls that abut standard stalls as well as parallel stalls along the main access entrance for this
entire development. Staff will continue to work with the applicant to ensure the parking provided
can accommodate the proposed uses and that the layout is appropriate.

The landscaping along 3100 South, which is a High Image Arterial, will be provided as required
on site. The area adjacent to 3100 South is UDOT right-of-way and is not part of this project.
Staff recommends that there be some site coordination with the residential development to the
east so the entrance and parkstrip is developed in a similar manor. The existing landscape strip
on the north side is being modified to accommodate parking and the applicant is requesting to
eliminate the trees along the east side of the site. The residential development is proposing trees
on 30’ centers and a vinyl fence so that would satisfy the requirements for both sites.

There will be no exterior dumpsters provided for this building. The dumpsters will all be located
