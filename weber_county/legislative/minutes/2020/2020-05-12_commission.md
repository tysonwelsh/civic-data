    1. RATIFY CONTRACT WITH UTAH QUARTER HORSE ASSOCIATION FOR GOLDEN SPIKE FUTURITY
       TRIALS/CHALLENGE RACES
        Duncan Olsen, GSEC General Manager, presented the contract stating that this race is normally held in
        Washington but could not be held there. The Utah Association and State Racing Commission approached
        Weber County, which has the right infrastructure and has been fastidiously taking the necessary COVID-
        19 precautions, thus had the ability to do something so unusual. The commissioners gave staff great
        accolades. Commissioner Harvey thanked the County Surveyor’s Office for helping to make this possible
        by certifying the distances. The AQHA had told him we had the most accurate distances of any U.S. track.
        Commissioner Jenkins moved to ratify a contract with Utah Quarter Horse Association for Golden Spike
        Futurity Trials/Challenge Races; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
        Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye
    2. CONTRACT WITH ROCKY MOUNTAIN POWER TO INSTALL A NEW INCREASED SIZE TRANSFORMER TO
       UPPER VALLEY RADIO TOWER
        Bryce Taylor, with County Operations, stated that Rocky Mountain Power is upgrading the transformer to
        accommodate the multiple services that the county is adding to that Ogden Valley radio tower. The
        commissioners noted that without this critical infrastructure for the Valley a number of services could not
        be provided, including law enforcement’s needs.
        Commissioner Jenkins moved to approve the contract with Rocky Mountain Power to install a new
        increased size transformer to the Upper Valley radio tower; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
        Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye
    3. CONTRACT WITH E.K. BAILEY FOR CONSTRUCTION OF A STORAGE/TRAINING WAREHOUSE FOR SHERIFF’S OFFICE
        County Sheriff Ryan Arbon stated that there has been a plan for sometime to have a needed warehouse for
        the Sheriff’s Office. There have been multiple facilities overtime that the county has paid for storage.
        Currently the county pays about $6,000/month to house emergency equipment and there is an old facility
        where hundreds of thousands of dollars in equipment is stored outside. A training facility that had been
        dedicated for some storage and training for law enforcement was subsequently turned into the CSI facility.
        This project was bid out for the 33,000 square feet warehouse and includes storage space for the Jail,
        Emergency Management, and law enforcement. It also has a training facility, which will save quite a bit
        of money. The total cost is $1.8 million and this is broken into two phases. The cost of the first phase at
        completion is $1.157 million and the county is not contracted for the second phase—at completion of phase
        1, and if the opportunity is there, they would like to take advantage of it.
          The amount of funds from the Capital Improvements Fund is about $900,000 and the balance of $250,000+
          will come from the Sheriff’s Office through cost savings. Commissioner Jenkins asked why the second
          phase was in the contract if only phase 1 is being built. Sheriff Arbon said that the contract language states
          that they are only building phase 1 and notes that the county can go into phase 2. This can occur if the
          funds are available, but it is divided into two phases because this is the amount of money that was available
          at this time. Commissioner Harvey said that overtime this will save the county about 72,000 annually,
          plus give some longevity to the equipment sitting out in the weather. Chair Froerer agreed, stating that the
          present value of the lease over the next several years equals well over $500,000-$600,000 in savings.
          Commissioner Harvey moved to approve the contract with E.K. Bailey for construction of a
          storage/training warehouse for the Sheriff’s Office; Commissioner Jenkins seconded.
          Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye

    2. COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT BLOCK GRANT SECOND PUBLIC HEARING FOR CARES ACT FUNDING.
          Christy Dahlberg, Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC) Community Development Administrator,
          stated that this second public hearing was to allow public comment and on the scope of the projects noted
          below that were determined to be funded for the CDBG CV funding cycle. The supplemental CDBG
          funding is authorized by the Coronavirus Aid Relief and Economic Security Act, and these special funds
          will be used to prevent, prepare for, and respond to the Coronavirus pandemic. The WFRC will administer
          this, sponsored by Weber County for the following projects: rental assistance through the Housing
          Authorities, and small business emergency grants for businesses located in Morgan, Toole, and Weber
          Counties, excluding Ogden City, which has its own entitlement funds. Ms. Dahlberg addressed Chair
          Froerer’s questions including that the Wasatch Front region will receive just under half a million dollars,
          with $100,000 going to each housing authority, leaving $300,000 for small business grants. She said that
          eligible uses include rent/utility, payroll, outstanding debt, and cleaning/disinfecting costs. Ineligible uses
          include personal mortgage payments and tax debt. She outlined the estimated grant availability plan.
