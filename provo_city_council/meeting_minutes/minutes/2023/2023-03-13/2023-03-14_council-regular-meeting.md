2.         A public hearing regarding the HOME ARP Allocation Plan draft (23-027) (17:17)

finalized plan to discuss in the work session on March 28th.

used.

funding is to support. She added that the funds are to be used for all of Utah County, not just Provo City.

Dave Decker, Director of Public Works, presented. He discussed the Aquifer Storage and Recovery
Projects. He shared the cost estimates including the funding sources available. He also shared that this
would allow for long term storage.

Dan Follett, Director of Finance, provided details on how the loan would be repaid and what the interest
rate would be. He emphasized that this bond would not increase property taxes.

Chair MacKay opened the item for public hearing.

Robert Todd, of Provo, asked how the bond would be paid from revenues without the rates being
increased.

Mr. Decker answered that the current rates have anticipated this project. He added that there is other
water revenue that could be used. He shared that the financial planning that their department does is
always forward looking.

John Christopherson, of Provo, asked if this project will enhance or detract from a personal well that he
depends on watering his livestock.

Mr. Decker responded that the aquifer does not distinguish between public or private wells. He added
that this means it should be an overall enhancement for all wells.

John Salzman, of Provo, asked if the aquifer is shared with other municipalities. After sharing that he is
in the bond market, he asked where the city is going to get $36 million dollars at a one-percent interest
rate.

Ken Forrester, of Provo, shared his frustration at the transparency in this process. He said it appears like
rates were raised years ago to fund this project without anyone knowing.

John Yardley, of Provo, asked why the amount of the loan was not lower after the city received grant
money.

David Kang, of Provo, asked what percentage of water usage will be benefited by this project.

Robert Daniels asked if commercial businesses pay more for water than residents. He also asked who is
insuring the loan.

Mr. Decker responded to each of the questions asked.

Councilor Handley asked for Mr. Decker to remind the council about the fees that were increased for the
Water Treatment Plant.

Councilor Shipley asked about the water rights that this project would provide.

Councilor Whipple asked about the different levels of aquifers.

Mr. Decker provided an explanation for each of the questions asked by council.

4.         An ordinance amending Provo City code regarding standards for conditional uses- dance
           halls. Citywide Application. (PLOTA20220404) (1:06:30)

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2023-5, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Aaron Ardmore, City Planner, presented. He responded to concerns brought up at the last council
meeting.

Councilor Handley asked why there is a need for a zone change if the two intended uses for the space
are already allowed. He also asked if the staff recommendations had changed.

Mr. Ardmore answered that the staff had not changed their recommendation with the current proposal.

Chair MacKay asked when it was determined that the building did not have an adequate sprinkling
system and if the applicant was made aware of that.

Mr. Ardmore said he learned about the sprinklers after the last hearing when he met with the Fire
Marshal, who was doing inspections in the building for a remodel. He said he assumed the applicant was
made aware.

Councilor Shipley questioned the reasoning behind allowing a reception, which is likely to have a dance
with it, but not allowing a dance hall.

Mr. Ardmore responded by saying the standard land use code makes it difficult in these types of
situations because it is so specific. He mentioned that staff often times have a difficult time establishing
permitted uses and it is dependent on how things are worded on applications. He said if things are
worded one way, it might be denied, but if the wording is changed, it could be permitted.

David Kang, the applicant, stated the meeting hall for rent has been their intended use from the
beginning. He said he was not aware of the building not having a sprinkling system and said they would
be getting one installed to allow for more than 49 people.

Councilor Shipley asked if the application still needed to be addressed after determining the intended
uses were already allowed.

5.         An ordinance amending the Zone Map of approximately 11.71 acres generally located at
           5515 N Edgewood Dr from MDR and A1 to PO and authorizing a Development Agreement -
           Riverbottoms Neighborhood. (PLRZ20220005) (1:25:14)

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2023-6, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Aaron Ardmore, City Planner, presented. He reminded council that the start of this project was
approved back in October and mentioned the project plans submitted were approved. He said this
rezone is related to the plan to build office buildings. He stated the proposal went through the planning
commission and was approved. He also said the last item will be the street vacation of a portion of
Edgewood Drive, which will be presented at the March 28 meeting.

Councilor Whipple asked if the number of parking spaces required is calculated by the square footage of
the building spaces.

Councilor Ellsworth asked if access to the river trails will be included in the development agreement.

Mr. Ardmore shared that there would be two separate development agreements, one for the residential
side and one for the commercial side. He said that none of this property is along the river frontage, so it
was not addressed in the development agreement on the commercial side.

Councilor Ellsworth also asked about the location of the parking garage. She said she is looking forward
to the city having better ordinances to guide development near and adjacent to the river, so things are
not built adjacent to where people gather to enjoy nature.

Councilor Fillmore asked if the property owner of the adjacent parcel had any plans to develop the area
along the river.

Mr. Ardmore said the applicants tried to acquire that part of the adjacent parcel but were unsuccessful.
He said he was not aware of any plans on that property.
