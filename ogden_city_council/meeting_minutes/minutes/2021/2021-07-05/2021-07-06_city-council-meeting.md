Ogden-Weber Technical College- 50th Anniversary
           A memo from Council staff regarding the 50th Anniversary of Ogden-Weber Technical College (OWATC) came before
the Council for consideration. The College is celebrating fifty years of service to the academic community.
           OWATC President Taggart approached the Council and thanked them and the Mayor for the opportunity to address
them; today he wants to express his gratitude to Ogden officials for the privilege the College has to be located in and be a vital
part of Ogden City. He provided historical information regarding the evolution of the College, emphasizing that throughout the
many changes, the College's commitment to the community has remained the same. He discussed student enrollment in courses
offered at the College, noting one unique thing about OWATC is that it is the largest provider of apprenticeship training in Utah.
One in five of the school's students is an apprentice, means they are working in their desired field while they are still learning. An
additional benefit to students is the ability to graduate debt-free as tuition is subsidized by the State ofUtah at a very low rate; this
means the earnings graduates receive are directly benefiting the economy rather than being used to repay student debt. High
school students attend tuition free, helping students and parents save over $750,000 in tuition costs. OWATC is working to ensure
that its student population reflects the diversity of the Ogden community; 35 percent of the College's students are minorities, 28
percent of which are Hispanic/Latino. OWATC is leading the way in closing the gap in graduation rates for minority students.
Graduates are working in high-tech, high demand jobs in Weber County and northern Utah; $80 million in salaries are generated
in the first year after graduation. Additionally, OWATC has partnered with other colleges to increase offerings and opportunities
for students in this community. OWATC fmnly b.elieves that one cannot get anywhere without going through Ogden first, and that
includes taking classes at the College; the best way for a student to earn an associates or bachelors degree at a local university is to
first receive a tech certificate from the College. He then noted advanced education is for everyone; students can earn tech college
certificates and adult high school diplomas at the same time. This provides a seamless transition to complete associates and
bachelors degrees and OWATC will work with Weber State University to that end. Mr. Taggart then reported the Student of the
Year, Josh Gardner, is a student in the culinary arts program. He is a graduate of Ogden High School and he plans to complete a
business degree at Utah State University. He plans to come back to Ogden and open a Cajun style restaurant.

Proposed Ordinance 2021-36 amending Title 15 to allow reuse of
nonconforming single-family dwelling buildings in nonresidential zones
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the concept of residential reuse in
nonresidential zones came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated in Ogden there are many homes that have been
converted for commercial use. In some cases, these homes were originally in residential zones, but the zoning designation has
changed, and residential uses are no longer allowed. This issue has left some former homes in disrepair because of changes in
market trends. The proposed amendment would allow such structures to return to residential use despite being located in
nonresidential zones.
          Deputy Plaiming Manager Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the
proposed zoning text amendment, which would allow a home in a nonresidential zone that has been converted to a permitted use
to revert to a single-family dwelling. This would allow for a home to be purchased and renovated to avoid continued deterioration;
it also provides additional housing options in a tight housing market. The present situation was created as zoning and desired uses
evolved from single-family, multi-family, commercial, and industrial uses throughout the City; as zoning evolved, ordinances
were written in a fashion that segregated uses from one another. He provided photographs of properties to serve as examples of
accommodating nonconforming uses; requirements that would be placed on a property with a reuse petition include returning the

               A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed MRD zone text amendments
    came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated a petition has been filed by Boyer Company to amend the
    warehousing size limit and food manufacturing restrictions in the Manufacturing, Research, and Development (MRD) zone. Due
    to shifts in industry requirements, the petitioner feels it is necessary to increase the maximum warehouse size above the current
    limit, which is 50,000 square feet. Warehousing is not as heavy a use as other uses that are allowed in the MRD zone, so the
    Planning Commission found the increased limit to be acceptable under the guidelines of the General Plan. The Planning
    Commission also recommended setting a 50-foot height limit for buildings along 1200 West, which is the separating line between
    homes and BDO. Additionally, the petitioner feels that food manufacturing would be an acceptable use in the MRD zone, and the
    Planning Commission concurred, with the condition that noise, smell, and fumes need to be self-contained.
               Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the
    petition; BDO Management desires the amendment to allow warehousing larger than 50,000 square feet in other MRD areas not
    adjacent to the Ogden Nature Center and to allow food production in the MRD zone. He presented an aerial image to identify the
    current uses in BDO, including the MRD zone and DDR zone. The MRD zone was transitional in nature and located on vacant
    lands. The Planning Commission reviewed the proposal at its May 5, 2021 meeting and forwarded a recommendation of approval
    to the Council with a vote of7-0. The Planning Commission recommended including standards in Section15-23 for a 50-foot
    height limit along 1200 West. They also recommended that noise, odor, and fumes must be contained within structures where food
    manufacturing takes place along 1200 West. The Commission's recommendations are based on the fmding that the proposed
    amendment is consistent with the MRD zone's purposes, as outlined in the General Plan.

Proposed Ordinance 2021-32 amending Municipal Code Sections 10-5-6,
10-6-1, and 10-6-4 to amend fees related to parking violations
           A memo from the Management Services Department regarding proposed parking violations fee amendments came before
the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Fiscal Operations Office is proposing to adjust the tiered fee system so that the
minimum fee is slightly higher. The proposal also changes the time limits to three categories: within 15 days, 16-3 0 days, and over
30 days. Additionally, there will only be three citation categories-as opposed to five-to reduce the amount of space taken up on
the actual citation.
           Senior Accountant Johns summarized the memo and reported Administration is proposing a change in parking citation
fees. The proposed amendment is designed to increase fees to cover projected costs of running the parking citation program. When
compared to similar governing entities, Ogden's parking fees are quite low, but this proposal would increase minimum fees to
help sustain the citation program. This amendment would also simplify the fee schedule, clarify where parking enforcement may
occur in the city, and streamline the process for citation appeals. The impetus for these changes is the implementation of new
software, the need to consolidate and simplify fee schedules, and the need to cover costs and increase revenues. The scope of the
proposed amendments includes addressing infractions of existing parking ordinances, and not addressing City parking needs or
general parking policies. He then presented the current parking citation fee structure and offered a comparison with the proposed
adjustments. The current ordinance provides for infractions on certain types of public property and the proposed change would
clarify that all public property is subject to enforcement. The current ordinance allows for a reduction of penalties based on the
appeal process and the proposed change would remove the penalty reduction in favor of citation dismissal if an appeal is
successful. In addition to these changes, the administration is proposing the following:
                •    Clarifying that all city owned parking lots-including RDA parking lots-are governed by the same level of
                     parking enforcement.
                •    Changes to hearing officer provisions. It has become apparent that the way the hearing officer reviews parking
                     ticket appeals could be arbitrary and may not provide a good customer experience. Instead of decreasing fees
                     for successful appeals, the hearing officer would either uphold or dismiss the citation completely.
           Council member Hyer inquired as to the party responsible to pay any parking citation. Mr. Johns stated that the vehicle
owner is the responsible party. Councilmember Hyer asked how the vehicle owner will be notified of the citation in the event that
they were not the person driving the vehicle when the citation was issued. Mr. Johns stated that if a citation is not paid within the
first ten days, the process for notifying the owner of the citation will commence. The owner will actually receive two notifications:
one in the 15-30 day period after the citation is issued and another 30 days after the citation is issued. Council member Hyerasked
if any late fees would be waived, or even the citation fee waived, if a vehicle owner is able to prove they were not operating the
vehicle at the time the citation was issued and they were unaware of the citation. Chief Deputy Attorney Stratford stated that it
would not be appropriate for the City to waive or reduce fees in that instance; the law indicates that the vehicle owner is
responsible for their vehicle, regardless of who is operating it. This may not seem friendly, but it is important for the City to be
consistent and fair. Mr. Johns noted that the City regularly allows people to remit payment of parking fees via a payment plan.
           Acting Chair White inquired as to the location(s) where most parking citations are issued. Captain Scott of the Police
Department indicated the majority are issued in downtown Ogden and in the areas surrounding the Weber State University (WSU)

Lotus Partners. LLC Participation and Incentive Agreement
           The administration is proposing the RDA enter into an agreement with Lotus Partners, LLC to provide tax increment
funding to assist in the development of two phases of commercial and multi-family housing within the Ogden River
Redevelopment Project Area. Phase 1 consists of two commercial buildings and townhomes south of the Ogden River between
Washington Boulevard and Grant Avenue with Phase 2 consisting of multi-family housing on the former site of the River Inn
north of the Ogden River. The agreement would provide tax increment funding of up to $1.4 million from the Ogden River
Redevelopment Project Area to assist in the development of the properties.
           Community and Economic Development Deputy Director Cooper used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide
renderings and maps illustrating the layout of the Lotus Development; design conforms with the guidelines in the Ogden River
Master Plan style and design guide. The project includes 70 to 72 townhomes, two commercial buildings, infrastructure, and
public access. The proposed incentive amount is a maximum of$1.4 million, to be determined by taxes generated by the project.
The incentive term is until 2027 and project area collection is estimated at $1.7 million. The total project area budget is $15
million. The agreement requires substantial completion of Phase 1 by December 31, 2021.
           Board member Choberka asked if the townhome units will be for rent or for sale, to which Mr. Cooper answered for rent.
           Board member Nadolski asked if this development was a result of a public process or unsolicited proposal. Mr. Cooper
stated it is the result of an unsolicited proposal and he briefly reviewed the history of the project dating back to 2002.
           Board members suggested requiring the developer to include electric vehicle charging stations in the project in order to
receive the incentive from the RDA. This led to discussion of efforts to incentivize charging stations and low water landscaping in
projects throughout the City. Mr. Cooper noted that landscaping requirements are governed by the City's zoning ordinances. He
added that he is unsure that a rental townhome project with two-car garages is the right place to require electric vehicle charging
stations. Board member Lopez agreed but noted he would like Administration to explore opportunities for requiring charging
stations at other projects throughout the City that have area available for public or visitor parking. Vice Chair White agreed but
suggested that the infrastructure to accommodate charging stations in the future should at least be stubbed into new or
redevelopment projects. Board member Hyer noted that he does not feel it is appropriate to use tax increment funding to
incentivize something like an electric vehicle charging station because that is something thatthe developer, rather than the general
public, will benefit from. Vice Chair White disagreed; she noted that a developer will make money from a project regardless of
whether it includes electric vehicle charging stations and she supports requiring this element in future projects. Mr. Cooper
suggested that this is an issue that should be addressed in broader policy discussions, perhaps in the development of the
Downtown Parking Plan; government operation improves if good policy is implemented.
           Board Administrator Eller-Smith stated that an action item on the proposed participation and incentive agreement can be
included on a future Board agenda if there is no opposition from the Board. No opposition was voiced.
