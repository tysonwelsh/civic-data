1. One-year review of the Colony Hilltrac Lift – Amir Caus, County Planner 
 
County Planner Amir Caus recalled that last year the Planning Commission forwarded a positive 
recommendation to the Community Development Director for a private lift within The Colony to 
serve three property owners.  Because of concerns about the viewshed, the LIP was brought to 
the Planning Commission for Commission input and public input.  The Planning Commission 
requested that the LIP be reviewed within one year, and Staff has received information and 
photographs from Commissioner Klingenstein during the year regarding glare from the lift. 
 
Commissioner Klingenstein recalled that a grading permit was inadvertently issued for the 
homes and ski run on Pinecone Ridge, and a lot of vegetation was removed.  He stated that, 
along Highway 248 from just before the school campus and School District Office to Comstock,

concern for him.

2. Discussion regarding the Verizon UT2-Kayjay Low Impact Permit application – 
Sean Lewis, County Planner 
 
Principal Planner Ray Milliner presented the staff report and explained that the applicant is 
requesting a Low Impact Permit (LIP) for a stealth cellular antenna that would be approximately 
90 feet above grade.  It is proposed that the antenna look like a large pine tree.  He provided 
visual images of the proposed stealth antenna and a regular pole antenna.  Staff is requesting the 
Planning Commission’s opinion as to whether they would prefer the stealth antenna or a regular 
antenna.  He explained that the stealth antenna would be approved as a LIP, and the regular 
antenna would require a Conditional Use Permit (CUP).  He noted that the County does not have 
specific criteria regarding antennae, so this antenna would be subject to the normal Code criteria.  
He provided a visual analysis of the stealth antenna and pole antenna as seen from the 
roundabout by the Boyer property and from the outlet mall. 
 
Commissioner Peck asked why the antenna is 80 feet tall.  Nefi Garcia with Technology 
Associates explained that Verizon needs an 80-foot antenna to cover its objectives for both 
capacity and coverage.  The existing cell tower is getting overloaded, and the purpose is to 
offload that and provide coverage to the Kimball Junction area.  Commissioner Peck asked why 
this site was chosen.  Mr. Garcia replied that they looked at a search ring to accomplish their 
objectives and determined this was the best area to locate the tower. 
 
Commissioner DeFord asked if Verizon has considered micro-sites.  Mr. Garcia replied that 
they have, and that would not work for them in this area.  Commissioner DeFord stated that the 
pole and tree are equally obtrusive to him.  He asked if Verizon could share a tower with another 
carrier.  Mr. Nefi confirmed that Verizon would own this tower, and there would be space for 
another carrier.  The other towers in the area would not meet Verizon’s objectives.

2. Discussion regarding proposed amendment to Chapter 10-4-2 of the Snyderville 
Basin Development Code regulating the installation of wood burning stoves and 
appliances – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner  
 
Planner Milliner explained that these Code amendments were requested by the County Council.  
On February 11, 2015, the Council initiated a moratorium on the installation of wood burning 
devices in new construction, remodels, and repairs in the Snyderville Basin Planning District.  
The moratorium will last for six months, and the Council directed Staff during that time to create 
some ordinance language to codify what is in the moratorium. 
 
Phil Bondurant, Environmental Health Director for the Summit County Health Department, 
explained that one of the County Council’s strategic objectives is a high quality of environmental 
standards to protect the environment and the health of the citizens.  Air quality is a hot topic, and 
with air monitoring in Summit County, they are starting to see some inversions similar to those 
along the Wasatch Front.  With the growth Summit County is experiencing, now is the time to 
get ahead of air quality issues.  He provided a visual image of the types of particulate matter put 
into the air by different types of wood burning appliances and noted that a typical wood burning 
fireplace puts 28 pounds of fine particulate matter into the air for every 1 million BTUs spent.  
Research done by the Utah Department of Environmental Quality shows that one wood burning 
fireplace pollutes as much as five dirty diesel buses and can emit as much as 90 SUVs.  He noted 
that wood stoves are better than a fireplace, but they still have air quality problems.  66% of the 
plumes from wood stoves are converted to VOCs, which combine with other pollutants and other 
things in the environment to increase the overall PM2.5 in the area.  In addition to environmental 
hazards, there are health hazards associated with particulate matter getting into the lungs.  He 
explained that wood smoke is probably the most toxic particulate pollution and is greater than

greater than an equal volume of tobacco smoke, and an inversion affects the entire community.  
He emphasized that the County is not banning wood burning as a whole but is looking at limiting

Planner Milliner reviewed the ordinance and explained that it will be inserted in Chapter 4,
