1.    HOME OCCUPATION, PAULINA ESTRADA IS REQUESTING A CONDITIONAL
      USE PERMIT FOR A HOME OCCUPATION, “THE GOODS COOKIES” TO BE
      LOCATED AT 1766 WEST DALMENY WAY.

Development Services Director, Jason Lethbridge, presented the staff report and stated
that the above item is for a home occupation in a multi-family development. Customers
would place orders and arrange a time for pickup from the residence. There would be no
additional employees and no remodeling of the home, with parking available in the guest
parking lot within the complex. The applicant will operate under a Cottage Food Permit.
The zoning ordinance allows up to eight customers per day without seeking an exception
from the Planning Commission, however, the applicant is asking for up to 10 customers
per day. The proposal for “The Goods Cookies” meets all fixed standards and
qualifications, with the exception of the requested 10 customers per day.

The proposal is for food preparation to take place in the home. Mr. Lethbridge explained
that this was addressed previously but the proposed zone text change had not yet been
approved by the Council. Notices regarding the proposal were sent to neighbors within
300 feet of the property but no feedback had been received.

Staff recommended approval of the above item, with up to 10 visitors per day, and any
other conditions listed in the staff report.

Chair Cannon asked about restrictions in the homeowner’s association (“HOA”) that
would set limitations on a home-based business. He also wondered about the entrance
to the business. City Attorney, Craig Bott reported that the City did not enforce HOA rules

the front door.

The applicant, Paulina Estrada, identified herself as the owner of the property. She
reported that a commercial oven will not be required to handle the anticipated volume of
baking. The proposed hours will be from 8:00 a.m. and 6:00 p.m. Ms. Estrada noted that
she would tell customers where to park prior to their arrival.

2.    REZONE, NEWMAN CONSTRUCTION IS REQUESTING A REZONE OF ABOUT
      9.92 ACRES LOCATED AT 1300 WEST WITHERS LANE. THE CURRENT
      ZONING IS RR-22, THE REQUESTED ZONING IS RM-8.

Zoning Administrator, Tim Prestwich, presented the staff report and stated that the above
item is a rezone application for the Withers property. The applicant is Newman
Construction. The 9.92-acre site is currently zoned RR-22 (Rural Residential) and the
requested rezone is for an RM-8 (Multi-Family Residential). The current zoning in the
surrounding area is a mixture of open space, parks, commercial, RR-22, and RM-8.

The General Plan for the area corresponds with medium-high density residential.
Mr. Prestwich noted that similar zoning was planned but the zoning had not yet changed.
The application was seeking to alter that.

A conceptual site plan was shared with the Planning Commission. Mr. Prestwich stated
that staff had not evaluated the proposed site plan, but the current concept includes
smaller and larger attached units and single-family detached units. A number of public
written comments were received. Residents had concerns with the attached housing,
density, and the height of the buildings. The written public comments were shared with
Commission Members.

Commissioner Russell asked about the number of units on the Vistas to the east of the
area. Mr. Prestwich reported that there are 65 units on 9.7 acres. He noted that most
RM-8 (Multi-Family Residential) zones do not reach eight units per acre due to additional
site requirements. The density is typically lowered to approximately six to seven units per
acre. Commissioner Russell felt it was important to look at existing RM-8 (Multi-Family
Residential) zone developments for comparison.

Commissioner Lefgren asked about square footage minimums on the main floor. He
wondered if there may be limitations on two-story attached units under the RM-8 (Multi-
Family Residential) zone due to those requirements. Mr. Prestwich reported that the
minimums were 1,100 square feet for a single-story home or 900 square feet on the main
floor for a two-story home. Both units also require garage space.

Chair Cannon asked if zoning could be granted with a single-level restriction; if zoning
could be granted with an age restriction; and if there was a way to create a walkable trail
system within the community. Mr. Bott reported that zoning is standard and established
and cannot limit height to single-level, cannot be made age-specific through zoning, and
walkways and paths can be addressed during the site plan portion rather during zoning
discussions.

Commissioner Russell raised concerns about parking. He noted that when there are four
units together, residents often park on the public street and leave their driveway open.
This had been an issue in previous developments and Commissioner Russell wondered
if there was a way to mitigate this.

3.     CONDITIONAL USE AND MASTER SITE PLAN, COSTCO WHOLESALE IS
       REQUESTING APPROVAL OF A CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT AND SLR
       MASTER SITE PLAN AT THE NORTHEAST CORNER OF 13200 SOUTH AND
       THE NORTHBOUND LANES OF THE MOUNTAIN VIEW CORRIDOR.

Mr. Lethbridge presented the staff report and stated that the above item is the second to
fall under the SLR Master Site Plan, a Master Development Agreement for zoning in the
Western Commercial District. He noted that a specific zoning ordinance for the project
area was created. As such, the process and regulation would be different than what is
normally seen with a commercial project. The area is not subject to the same zoning
ordinances.

Approval of the Master Development Agreement meant that many of the development
rights were vested. As a result, there would not be the same level of review by the City
as long as terms of the Master Development Agreement and SLR Master Site Plan are
met. Mr. Lethbridge reported that the SLR Master Site Plan was designed to be as flexible
as possible to allow the site to grow and develop organically.

Costco was looking to develop an area referred to as CPA-1. The SLR Master Site Plan
contains a list of uses that are allowed as well as restrictions. In CPA-1, the only limitation
is that the first 250 feet along the north end of the property is limited to single-family
detached residential. Mr. Lethbridge reported that the Costco site complied with this
limitation as the proposed site plan is more than 250 feet from the area on the north side.

A question was raised as to whether the single-family detached residential area could be
an RM-8 (Multi-Family Residential) zone. Mr. Lethbridge stated that there is no limitation
on density and only on unit type. There would be an opportunity to review that further if
and when a developer comes forward with a plan for the area.

Mr. Lethbridge displayed and reviewed slides of the site plan. He noted that the building
itself and the gas pump area would be against Mountain View Corridor on the west side
of the property. There were a couple of pads outside of the Costco development that
would remain for future commercial developments. Mr. Lethbridge briefly discussed the
landscaping plan. He explained that the Master Development Agreement has different
standards and allowances for landscaping than are normally seen in a commercial
ordinance. However, he felt the site did a good job of providing landscaped areas with
trees, green space, and shade.

There would be several access points on the project area, including new access off of the
frontage road on Mountain View Corridor. The UDOT right-of-way had already been

by City’s engineering staff. Mr. Lethbridge informed the Planning Commission that

answer questions.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that part of the approval is a Conditional Use Permit under the
Master Development Agreement documentation. He noted that the Costco gas pumps
are a Conditional Use. The pumps have been reviewed by the Water and Fire
Departments for potential issues with wellhead protection areas. No objections were
raised by either department. The location of the gas pumps would allow access from the
new public road to the north and off of the Mountain View Corridor. The gas pumps were
reviewed as part of the Conditional Use Permit and no additional conditions needed to be

elsewhere in the City. Mr. Lethbridge noted that with the elevation of the Mountain View

not be possible. There would be some visibility from those areas. Mr. Lethbridge stated
that the loading dock will be located at the south end of the building. This will keep the
loading area far away from the proposed housing to the north. The site plan shows the
truck route for Costco deliveries. Trucks will enter from Mountain View Corridor, circulate
in, and then head back onto Mountain View Corridor.

The site plan complies with the SLR Master Site Plan and the Master Development
Agreement. It was approved by the Design Review Committee of the Master Developer
Suburban Land Reserve. The initial stages of construction would be related to
infrastructure. The new public right-of-way along the north part of the project and the
internal roadway will be built in the fall. Construction of the site itself will begin later and
does not require additional approvals from the Planning Commission.

the City is designated in the General Plan as a business park, which corresponds with
the C-G (Gateway Commercial) zone or a C-PO (Professional Office Commercial) zone.

Council has been working on a site plan to keep the existing home and convert it to a
commercial site with parking in the rear.

The home is located on the west side. The parking lot would come in from the east side.
As this is one of the first parcels to convert to commercial in the area, the developers
have had certain challenges. Interconnectivity had been difficult, as there was nothing to
connect to. UDOT required the developers to position the driveway on the east side so it
could be modified and used as shared access in the future. They have also provided a

cross access easement in the back, going to the west, so there will be interconnectivity

5.      ZONE TEXT CHANGE, RIVERTON CITY IS PROPOSING CHANGES TO TITLE
        18 REGARDING MAXIMUM BUILDING HEIGHTS IN COMMERCIAL AND
        INDUSTRIAL ZONES.

Mr. Prestwich stated that the above item has been brought to the Planning Commission
by the City. It is a Zone Text Change for commercial building heights. After a commercial
building off of Redwood Road was allowed to go up to 80 feet, the City Council wanted to
ensure this did not occur in other parts of the City. The commercial zones include:

      C-D (Downtown Commercial);

      C-G (Gateway Commercial);

      C-N (Neighborhood Commercial);

      C-PO (Professional Office Commercial);

      C-R (Regional Commercial);

      PCC (Planned Commercial Center); and

      M-1 (Manufacturing Light Industrial).

The M-1 (Manufacturing Light Industrial) has no maximum height. The buildings may be
limited by setbacks but not by height. Staff was proposing to add a maximum building
height of 50 feet. Mr. Prestwich noted that all of the ordinances have either a maximum
building height or maximum number of stories. Staff was also proposing to have height
rather than story restrictions.

Mr. Prestwich clarified that the height was measured from the grade of the home to the
highest point on the roof. Commissioner Rushton suggested that a 35-foot limit for
buildings adjacent to residential areas within a certain distance would improve sightlines
and ensure continuity. The Commissioners discussed building setbacks. Commissioner
Lefgren liked the idea of capping commercial building heights at 50 feet. He did not
believe that many buildings would want to go above that.

Mr. Prestwich clarified that the commercial building height limitation would not limit the
height of cell towers or silos as they have separate ordinances.

Commissioner Rushton moved that the Planning Commission approve the Zone
Text Change for Commercial Building Heights, as shown on the attached
documents with the following condition:

      1.     Buildings adjacent to Residential areas must have a 35-foot building
             height limit.

The motion was seconded by Commissioner James. The motion passed with the

7.    ZONE TEXT CHANGE, RIVERTON CITY IS PROPOSING CHANGES TO TITLE
      18.145 AUTOMOBILE PARKING REGARDING PARKING IN FRONT OF
      COMMERCIAL BUILDINGS.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the above item is related to

flow. However, when there is a small building, this can create issues. Staff recommended
