CALL TO ORDER
Council Chair Jacob called the work session to order at 5:30 p.m., and noted the meeting was open
to the public in Council Chambers, available remotely via Zoom, and streamed live on YouTube.

 A.    Landscape Ordinance
City Planner/Zoning Administrator Larry Gardner presented a proposed landscape ordinance,
modified from the ordinance approved in 2011. He said the ordinance approved in 2011 focused on
plant type, with an emphasis on water-wise plants, and said the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy
District’s current focus on water efficiency and conservation was reflected in the new document.
Mr. Gardner explained proposed changes, including a title change to “Water Efficient Landscaping
Requirements.”

Mr. Gardner said Mayor Burton submitted a separate, less restrictive proposed landscape ordinance
for Council consideration that would utilize water efficiency standards but allow property owners
to have more leeway. He showed photographs of water-wise park strip landscaping in West Jordan.

Mayor Burton explained that approval of the City’s landscape ordinance by Jordan Valley Water
Conservancy District (JVWCD) was necessary to qualify for lower water rates. He said JVWCD would
expect the ordinance to prohibit sod in park strips, and to limit the percentage of landscaping that
could be sod. The Mayor said he had tried to honor those requirements and still provide some
flexibility for property owners with his proposed ordinance.

Vice Chair Green pointed out the ordinance would only apply to new construction. He asked by what
standard future changes to existing landscaping would be measured. He suggested commercial and
residential requirements should be separated in the ordinance. Vice Chair Green asked if the cash
bond referenced in Section 13-13-4e should be included in the City Fee Schedule. Referring to
Section 13-13-6g, he suggested grass should not be required for churches. Vice Chair Green said he
City Council Work Session Minutes – April 14, 2021                                            Page 2

believed utility equipment could be screened without plants and suggested combining some
sections to be concise. Vice Chair Green said he believed the ordinance should emphasize that West
Jordan did not want landscaping to be 100% grass and suggested including a list of basic
expectations for residential sprinkler systems. Mayor Burton suggested an appendix could include
sprinkler system recommendations and an approved list of trees for residential use.

Council Member Lamb said the street on which he lived had park strips with grass, rocks, and one
with cement. He said he suspected the park strip with cement was probably against City Code. He
said he was not sure he liked the water efficient landscaping requirements and said he would prefer
to educate residents instead of requiring particular landscaping, especially since the requirements
were not enforced.

Council Member Whitelock said it was too hard for property owners to find information. She said
she agreed with the suggestion to separate residential and commercial requirements in the
ordinance and suggested further separating residential into two categories: landscaping that
existed prior to 2021, and landscaping installed in 2021 and after. Council Member Whitelock
emphasized that the City needed to think about and address water conservation. She commented
that creating heat islands with too much concrete and rocks would also be a problem. Council
Member Whitelock referred to Section 13-13-1b of the Mayor’s proposed ordinance regarding the
City being exempt, and said she was not in favor of the City being exempt from what citizens were
expected to do. She suggested requiring rain gauges for commercial sprinkler systems to prevent
watering during the rain, and stated the residential section needed to address artificial turf.

Council Member Pack said the issue seemed to him to be a matter of conservation versus aesthetics,
involving far more than just park strips. He stated it was hard to maintain a water-wise park strip.
Council Member Pack said he was in favor of incentivizing rather than punishing and said he would
not want a situation where neighbors were turning in neighbors for washing off concrete. He said
he believed conservation and sustainability should take priority over aesthetics.

Council Member Worthen said she agreed with Council Member Whitelock that the City should be
held to the same standards as businesses in the City. She spoke of the Flip Your Strip program.
Council Member Worthen commented that watering lawns was not going to be possible at some
point in the future because people would need water to drink. She said she did not know how much
longer watering lawns would be a choice. Council Member Worthen suggested identifying over-
grassed areas in City parks that could be transitioned to conserve water.

Council Member McConnehey said it would be nice to receive information prior to the day of Council
meetings. He suggested the Council should review the proposed documents paragraph by
paragraph. Council Member McConnehey said he liked the suggestion to require a rain gauge.

Vice Chair Green said he agreed the Council needed to have one version to review paragraph by
paragraph and suggested the landscape ordinance may need to go back to the Planning Commission
considering the comments made during the meeting. Vice Chair Green commented that park strips
were the biggest water-waster in the City and questioned why the City even had park strips. Mayor
Burton responded that park strips provided security for people walking on sidewalks. The Mayor
said he had considered the possibility of eliminating park strips. Council Office Director Alan
Anderson added that park strips provided a place for snowplows to put snow.

Mr. Anderson asked the Council to identify key points they wanted addressed with the documents
condensed into one. He commented that Utah was the second driest state in the United States (next
to Nevada), and the largest consumer of water per capita in the United States when he was on the
City Council.

Council Chair Jacob reminded the Council that use of water beyond a certain threshold meant a huge
cost increase from JVWCD. He said it was his understanding the City had gone over the threshold
for the last few years and owed JVWCD $2.3 million. If JVWCD standards were included in the City
ordinance, the City would be allowed to negotiate the amount owed, hence the sense of urgency to
adopt the landscape ordinance. Council Chair Jacob said he would prefer to work with the document
already reviewed and recommended by the Planning Commission. CAO Korban Lee said he was not
sure the amount was an outstanding debt but agreed that not including JVWCD standards in the
ordinance would have major financial consequences for the City.

Mr. Anderson explained that the two-tiered structure began on January 1, 2020. Council Member
Whitelock said it was her understanding that the City owed Tier Two rates because water use
exceeded the contract amount. Council Chair Jacob said it was his understanding the City had not
paid the extra amount yet and may have an opportunity to negotiate the amount down.
Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck said she believed the $2.3 million represented the
amount the City would have had to pay with the level of use over the last three years if Tier Two
premium rates had been in effect. She said it was her understanding that JVWCD had been honoring
the existing contract rate and had not asked for additional payment.

Mr. Lee said it was estimated that West Jordan would exceed the contract water amount by late
summer, so the City needed to renegotiate with JVWCD by early summer. The water efficiency
standards would need to be adopted by the end of May to renegotiate in June. Ms. Steck said she
concurred.

Council Member Whitelock said she understood the importance of being water-wise, but said she
believed aesthetics were also important and should be considered. She suggested an additional
work session for discussion. Vice Chair Green said he did not believe water-wise meant ugly. He said
one of the nicest yards in his neighborhood was water-wise. Council Member Whitelock said she
agreed water-wise could be beautiful, and said her concern related to preventing the use of too
many rocks and too much concrete.

Vice Chair Green said he did not want to dictate everything a yard should have. He expressed
frustration that the Council had multiple documents to consider and needed to adopt something by
May 28th. He suggested there was not time to send the matter back to the Planning Commission.

Mr. Lee asked for two or three Council Members to meet with Staff to go through the document line
by line to speed up the process. Council Chair Jacob said he agreed with the idea. Council Member
Whitelock volunteered. Council Member Pack said he wanted to respect the work already done by
the Planning Commission. Council Member McConnehey and Vice Chair Green volunteered to work
closely with Staff, with Council Member Worthen as an alternate.

Council Member Whitelock asked if the City contracted for enough water in the last year. Ms. Steck
responded the City purchased in excess of the contract amount for the last five years. Mr. Lee
explained how the contract was structured and said the best data available would be used to try to
contract for enough water, without contracting for water the City would not use. Ms. Steck said

   all cities adopt a landscaping ordinance prior to renegotiation.

       b. Dahle Rezone
Senior Planner Brian Berndt oriented the Council with a petition from Paxton Guymon on behalf of
Lloyd and Bonnie Dahle to amend the General Plan Land Use Map for 1.6 acres located at 7106 South
2200 West from Low Density Residential to Medium Density Residential, and rezone the same
property from A-1 (Agriculture 1-acre minimum lots) Zone to R-1-8C (Single-family Residential,
8,000 square foot minimum lots in Subzone C) Zone. Mr. Berndt showed a map of the area and
pointed out the surrounding residential zoning, including the nonconforming Jordan Valley
Apartments area.

Paxton Guymon, applicant, said the current property owner, Bonnie Dahle, was excited to see
development go forward. He commented that demand for housing far outweighed existing
inventory, and said more infill projects were needed. He said he was seeking to preserve the single-
family use in the area with a six-lot subdivision, with one of the lots already set aside for the current
property owners. Mr. Guymon said the Planning Commission recommended approval. He showed a
subdivision concept plan and concept designs for the homes. Responding to a question from Council
Member Whitelock, Mr. Guymon said he would not be able to develop more than six lots with R-1-
8 Zoning.

Council Chair Jacob opened a public hearing at 7:56 p.m.

Citizen Comments:
None

Council Chair Jacob closed the public hearing at 7:56 p.m.

Vice Chair Green said the issue had not been unanimous in the Land Use Subcommittee. He said he
had a problem with the nonconforming R1-10 area, and questioned how many other areas in the
City were nonconforming. Vice Chair Green asked the Mayor to look at nonconforming properties
across the City, and suggested the map needed to be updated. He said the proposed rezone would
be the start of a slippery slope in giving up the rural nature of that area of the City and moving
toward medium density, and asked if the Council was comfortable with that decision.

Council Member Lamb said he did not feel it would be a slippery slope, since R1-8 had been in the
area for at least 30 years. He said the Council was making a decision based on a resident who wanted
to build a smaller home and build other homes as well. He said he believed the request made sense
for the area.

Council Member McConnehey said he would prefer to see the property zoned R1-10. Mr. Berndt
explained R1-10 would allow a total of five units on the property instead of six. Mr. Guymon said his
deal with the property owners included six lots. Without the ability to develop six lots, the contract
would be cancelled. Council Member McConnehey responded his decision would not be based on
the feasibility of the applicant’s contract.

Council Member Worthen said she agreed with Council Member Lamb. She said she thought the
layout was fine, and had no problem with the proposal. Council Member Whitelock commented the
apartment complex down the street had already taken away the rural nature of the area. She said
she thought the lots would be a decent size, with homes she hoped would be a little more affordable.

Council Chair Jacob said he could see both sides of the argument, but said he did not see why the
proposed development would trigger a slippery slope any more than the other developments in the
area. Vice Chair Green pointed out the General Plan included a goal to maintain the rural nature of
West Jordan, and said he wanted the Council to be aware of the possible long-term implications of
approving the application.
