              b. Columbus Center naming update. Mayor Wood said the idea of
                 changing the name of the Columbus Center has been circulating for a
                 long time and a variety of reasons. The most recent push from the
                 City’s youth is something she wants to listen to and take seriously.
                 This added to her desire to help the community write its own history
                 and make more people aware of South Salt Lake. There is still
                 confusion over the two Columbus Centers in the City. With the

                  Columbus Library closing and the new one opening being called the
                  Granite Library, one more step was taken away from the former name.
                  She sees an open door for writing a new chapter for the building. New
                  programs have been added, with more to come, to fill the space of the
                  library closure. There is a new park adjacent to the Center named after
                  hometown heroes, Ida and Laurie Bickley. She is pleased to share the
                  City’s own unique history and the individuals who make us great.
                  They also named the promise park after Ser Nay Moo. It is her intent
                  to leave the words “Columbus School” above the entrance and add a
                  plaque about the history and its former name. The motivation is not to
                  erase history, but to tell a more complete and current story about the
                  community today. The City is fortunate to have a diverse, growing
                  community and such a beautiful, unique building where everyone is
                  welcome. She is grateful to the residents who have voiced their
                  feelings, and to several City Council and Youth City Council
                  members who have made a strong case that it is time to start fresh
                  with a new name that can unite us.

                  Youth City Council Advisor, Edward Lopez, gave an update on the

UNFINISHED BUSINESS

       1. Citizen Review Board Ordinance Discussion. Mayor Wood explained that
          this board would function much like the Planning Commission. They will be
          their own board, they won’t answer to anybody, and the members get
          appointed with advice and consent from the City Council.

          Council members made suggestions regarding the draft of the ordinance.

          The Council discussed the following:

          Allow the Board to review all use of force investigations but not require them
          to review all of them.

          Training of the Board need not be specifically prescribed by ordinance but
          should be given as advised by the attorney who is working with them. The
          Council would like to have a report of the training the Board receives.

          The Council would like flexibility in the creation of a mediation program.

          The Council would like monthly reporting from the Board for the first six
          months, then move to quarterly reports.

          They requested the name of the board be changed to the Civilian Review
          Board.

          To help the Board select which cases they will review, the Police Department
          may categorize the various uses of force and then the Board may select which
          cases to review.

          The Board may also have the ability to review cases that have received public
          complaints.

          The Council then moved to the budgeting aspect of the Civilian Review
          Board.

          Council Chair Bynum reminded the Council that three positions were needed
          for the Civilian Review Board; an attorney, a data analyst, and a police
          lieutenant to help with the review process.

          The Council was concerned about the cost of three new full-time positions and
          questioned where the funding would come from. They discussed adding just
          two of the positions or not hiring any of the positions.

          After discussion, the majority of the Council was okay with hiring an attorney,
          a data analyst, and possibly the lieutenant, if funding is there.

          Finance Director, Kyle Kershaw, advised that a lot of the numbers presented
          to the Council a few weeks ago were estimates from a high level look. He
          suggested that staff do a precise budget on what the requests are and then look
          at what the funding could be in year one and subsequent years after.

          Council Member deWolfe made a motion to move this item to a future
          meeting to be determined.
