Eyo shared details of the various public parking spots available in parking garages in the

6.      A discussion on alternative forms of voting. (23-013) (01:23:02)

Michael Sanders, Policy Analyst, presented. Ranked-choice voting was a policy topic for the
Council requested by Councilors Handley and Whipple. Mr. Sanders outlined some of the
previous discussions and presentations the Council has had on this topic in recent years. In 2018,
the State legislature approved HB35 Municipal Alternative Voting Methods Pilot Project, which
allowed municipalities to opt-in to a pilot program utilizing ranked-choice voting. The Council
did not opt into the program in any previous years when it was available. In 2021, 23 cities in
Utah participated in the pilot program, as compared to only two cities who utilized the program
in 2018. In 2021, the Council put out an Open City Hall (OCH) topic about ranked-choice
voting. There were 154 registered responses. Most respondents had heard of ranked-choice
voting but had never used it before. Mr. Sanders reviewed additional responses from OCH.

Mr. Sanders reached out to city recorders and clerks in many of the 23 cities that used ranked-
choice voting in 2021. Most of the cities were small towns or cities and therefore not necessarily
comparable to Provo. Many chose to participate due to the cost savings from not holding a
primary. Many counties have indicated that they may charge municipalities more to conduct
ranked-choice vote elections due to the increased education effort required. In response to a
question from Councilor Rachel Whipple, Mr. Sanders indicated this may cost more but the main

Mr. Sanders highlighted the pros of ranked-choice voting:
    Eliminates the spoiled or split-vote effect
    Increase voter turnout
    Encourages candidates to run a positive campaign
    Reduces polarization
    Reduced costs due to no primary

He also reviewed some cons of ranked-choice voting:
    Voter confusion
    Strategic voting
    Ballot exhaustion
    Compromise candidates
    Increased costs of voter education

Mr. Sanders clarified that this is a pilot program only and would be used in the 2023 municipal

Sanders highlighted several next steps for Council action. Council Chair Katrice MacKay opened
the item for Council discussion. She also thanked Mr. Sanders for his thorough materials.

7.      A discussion regarding micro mobility devices. (23-022) (02:05:53)

Mary Ditto, Assistant City Attorney, presented. Ms. Ditto highlighted the language in the current
City Code regarding skateboards, roller skates, and bicycles, as well as some of the issues with
the current code language. She reviewed the suggested changes to the code, which would expand
the definitions and safety regulations to include additional micro-mobility devices such as
scooters. They recommend removing the Provo Library from the provisions but allowing the
library to post their own regulations. The new language would also specifically reference the
City’s officially-contracted e-rider program. Property owners would also have permission to post
regulations, and it would be unlawful to violate those regulations.

Councilor George Handley asked about the exclusive City e-rider program; Ms. Ditto explained
they did not recommend limiting a specific speed of the device in code, but that this provision
could be cited in the City’s contract. Mr. Handley also asked about sidewalks and whether
devices were allowed on sidewalks. Brian Jones, City Attorney, clarified that in certain areas
riding is prohibited on sidewalks. Councilor Bill Fillmore commented on the complexity of this
statute and whether the legal staff have tried to simplify these provisions. Councilor Shannon
Ellsworth asked if there were any provisions about the use of micro-mobility devices for schools;
