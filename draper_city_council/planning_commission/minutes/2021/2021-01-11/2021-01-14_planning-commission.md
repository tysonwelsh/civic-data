4.     Public Hearing: Lot 27 Oak Vista No. 1 at Suncrest Plat Amendment Request
       (Administrative Action)
On the request of Adam Breen, representing Breen Homes, for a Plat Amendment and deviation
from 1999 Draper City Municipal Code Section 9-15-040(a) to modify the Limits of Disturbance
shown on the plat and authorize development in an area with slopes of 30% or greater. The property
contains 0.47 acres and is located at 14812 South Oak Bluff Place. Application SUBD-980-2020.
Staff Contact: Jennifer Jastremsky, 801-576-6328, Jennifer.jastremsky@draper.ut.us.

Jennifer Jastremsky, Senior Planner, introduced the Plat Amendment Request. She noted that it
was under a residential medium density land use designation and located in a multifamily
residential zone. She pointed out errors in the staff report including the wrong section number on
the first page which should be corrected to 4.2.3.5. She shared that the development requirements
on page 3 were not pertinent since the Suncrest Development Guidelines override those standards.
She pointed out that a similar mistake was made on page 5.

Ms. Jastremsky reviewed the Suncrest Development Guidelines which included buildings may not
be constructed in areas exceeding 30% slope, limits to disturbance, and special exceptions. She
noted that the Suncrest Master Plan which focuses on clustering developments to allow for more
open space preservation and pointed out where Oak Vista was located within the development on
a map. She shared some of the Suncrest Planning Objectives and Development Concept.

Ms. Jastremsky explained that the applicant was proposing to amend the subdivision plat in order
to reduce the size of the limits to disturbance and asking for a special exception to build within the
30% slope area. She pointed out the applicant wanted to change the current limited disturbance
area from 12,675 square feet to 5,178 square feet of the lot. She reiterated that they were proposing
to extend their structure into more of the hillside that has a slope greater than 30%. She remarked
that a geotechnical report was submitted and looked at slope stability.

Dave Simon, Geologist Consultant, indicated that the geotechnical company trenched the lot and
found the remnants of a landslide on the property. He noted that was not something that should be
built on.

Alan Taylor, Geotechnical Consultant, explained that the home was proposed in the portion of the
lot where the landslide was and so the engineers have proposed to dig footing down past the
landslide. He pointed out that a slope stability analysis was also done, and the site received a safety
factor of 3. He pointed out that the footings for the home would go down below the landslide slip
plane. He explained some of the notations on the map he presented including that the hatched green
area was the area identified as safe to build.

Ms. Jastremsky noted that the concept grading and drainage plan put the home further back on the
property, so it was on the steep slope. She then presented current photos of the site.

Adam Breen, Applicant, clarified that they planned to build a narrow home and place it as close to
the front of the lot as they could but remain within the safe buildable area. He explained that they
did this in order to show compassion to the neighborhood and the natural vegetation. He noted
they have proposed that the garage be on the basement level to keep the driveway at a lower slope.
He shared that they would put the home within all the standard Suncrest Development setbacks.

Chairman Adams opened the public hearing for the Agenda Item.

James Powell commented that in his prepared remarks he laid out the dangers the proposed plat
creates for the neighbors above the site and the deficiencies in the proposal. He announced that if
the amendment was approved, he would request the criteria that was used to assess harm and how
those lined up with current city policy. He pointed out that by law they could not approve the plat
if the harm score was not the same or lower than the existing score.

Loraine Stepp agreed with Mr. Powell’s comments. She remarked that when they purchased their
home, they researched the requirements for the available properties below them. She added that it
was unfortunate about the landslide area on the property but did not believe that harm should be
pushed onto other people in the development. She expressed that she was concerned that allowing
this could set a precedence and could cause additional issues. She asked they had considered the
possible effects on water runoff.

Chairman Adams closed the public hearing. He asked if Mike Barker, City Attorney, could discuss
harm scores and precedence.

Mr. Barker replied that there is no objective checklist in the code or Suncrest Development
Agreement that assigns points on harm. He noted that significant harm was up to the Planning
Commission to determine. He shared that concerning precedence that each application was
evaluated individually so what may apply to one lot will not apply to another.

Commissioner Mary Squire inquired if the proposed location for the home was within the zone
that has been determined to be safe to build in by the latest study and if the geotechnical experts

5.      Public Hearing: Siempre Event Center Development Agreement (Legislative Action)
On the request of Paxton Guymon, representing Siempre LLC, for a Development Agreement in
the C2 and RM zone regarding a future event center located at South Mountain Golf course. The
agreement would modify the number of parking spaces require for an event center development
and allow for a portion of the parking spaces to be constructed of geo-grass pavers. Application
DA-1106-2020.        Staff     contact:     Travis     Van       Ekelenburg,     901-576-6522,
travis.vanekelenburg@draper.ut.us.

Travis Van Ekelenburg, City Planner, introduced the item and noted the subject property was split
between the C2 and RM zones, but the potion they were looking at today was in the C2 area. He
pointed out the land use for the property was neighborhood commercial and that a site plan was
being reviewed separately since parking was a concern. He clarified that the applicant was
requesting an agreement to allow for the reduction in the number of parking spaces required in the
1990 Draper City Municipal Code and for 20 of the proposed parking spaces to be constructed of
geo-grass pavers. He noted that the term of the agreement was the same as the ground lease, which
he believed was 45 years.

Mr. Van Ekelenburg reviewed the parking analysis of the 1990 Draper City Municipal Code and
South Mountain Design standards which indicated 281 parking spaces would be needed for the
reception center, golf course, and restaurant combined with the use of shared parking stalls. He
noted that the current code would require 153 parking spaces. He shared that after reviewing the
parking agreement they believed there would be sufficient parking for the site.

Commissioner Craig Hawker asked if the parking was planned for everyday or for worst case
scenario and how they determined the amount of parking needed.

Mr. Van Ekelenburg replied that they look at the activities going on at the site, what time those
activities took place and more to determine the number of spaces needed.

Commissioner Van Hoff asked for additional information on the grass pavers.

Paxton Guymon, Applicant, explained they are an improved parking surface with road base with
a grass like appearance with grass going through it so it will not look like asphalt but allow for
overflow parking that will only be used occasionally during peak demand. He explained that 193
parking stalls were only needed when they were meeting the peak demand of both the golf course
and event center at the same time. He noted that those activities would have very little overlap. He
shared that with the geo-grass parking stalls they will have 224 parking stalls at the site.

Mr. Guymon indicated the project has been years in the making with Salt Lake County, who owns
the golf course. He explained that this was a public private partnership between the County and
them to help keep the golf course viable. He shared that they planned to make improvements to
the golf course including new exteriors, new landscaping, and the addition of a new event center.
He noted that this partnership would provide a better venue for people using the golf course for
corporate events while Siempre would use the event center for weddings in the evening. He noted
that it is based off the Sleepy Ridge Golf Course in Orem.

Commissioner Player stated he believed this was a good plan.

Chairman Adams opened the public hearing period.

Shannon Sandberg commented that when she moved in 15 years ago, they were told they could
pay extra for a premium lot next to county land that would never be built on. She expressed that
they were frustrated that they were told by the County they could not beautify the area, but the
County could do whatever they wanted. She reiterated that this effected the neighbors in a negative
way.

Cory Solum stated he was very excited for what has been presented. He pointed out that they
should address the intersection at Mike Wier Drive and Traverse Ridge Road since it was a
downhill blind corner with high speeds and could be dangerous with them adding a lot of nighttime
traffic on it.

Mark Armstrong remarked that his biggest concern is having the clubhouse and gazebo being
proposed on the east side. He suggested that the placement of the reception center should be on
the west side where the parking is being proposed. He noted there was a lot of wildlife that ran
through that area and that there was always enough parking at the golf course.

Chairman Adams closed the public hearing.

7.     Public Hearing: City Initiated Parking Ordinance Changes Text Amendment
       (Legislative Action)
On the request of Draper City for a Text Amendment to amend Sections 9-25-030, 9-25-060, and
9-25-080 of Draper City Municipal Code. This application is known as the City Initiated Parking
Ordinance Changes Text Amendment. Application TEXTMAP-1122-2020. Staff Contact: Todd
A. Draper, 801-576-6335, todd.draper@draper.ut.us.

Todd Draper, City Planner, indicated the intent of the application was to address the accessibility
and availability of parking spaces. He noted it was specifically intended to clarify access lanes,
stacking lanes, queue spaces, and other similar incumbrances. He pointed out the proposed text
had an additional section D to section 9-25-030 which clarifies that parking spaces need to be
continually available and dual uses of parking spaces is not permitted.

Mr. Draper showed additional changes to section 9-25-060 which mainly dealt with clarification
with terminology to use the word queuing instead of stacking. He shared that they also added
number six and seven to the section addressing that queuing spaces are used for the drop off and
pickup of passengers and cannot block street access, onsite parking spaces, or public safety access
or aisles. He pointed out additional updates made in section 9-25-080.

Commissioner Van Hoff recommended that under section D of 9-25-030 they change internal
vehicular aisles to internal vehicular circulation aisles. He pointed out under number 5 in section
9-25-060 they neglected to remove the term stacking. He remarked that he was not sure where the
queuing was on the image on the left under 9-25-1.

Mr. Draper stated the queue space is next to the fuel island.

Commissioner Van Hoff suggested clarifying the internal circulation aisle verses a queuing area
in their drawings. He noted that he liked images since they provided more clarity.

Chairman Adams opened the meeting up for public comments on the Item. No comments were
made, and the public hearing was closed.

Motion: Commissioner Ogden motioned to approve forward a positive recommendation to
the City Council for the Text Amendment as requested by Draper City for the City initiated
Parking Ordinance Changes with the two changes proposed by Commissioner Van Hoff,
Application TEXTMAP-1122-2020, based on the findings and subject to the conditions listed
in the staff report dated January 4, 2021.

Second: Commissioner Player

Vote: A roll call was taken with the Commissioners voting 5-0 in favor of the motion.

Mr. Draper introduced the text amendment regarding the land use buffers for industrial,
commercial, and multi-family properties to ensure the fencing and landscaping is both sight
obscuring as well as higher quality and design. He presented the proposed text which clarified the
location of the land use buffers in addition to some grammatical changes. He noted that they made

five, which clarified that the fence needed to be six feet tall, solid, and sight obscuring. He shared
that they removed the option for a lightweight steel reinforce polyethylene plastic and clarified
how the height of the fence was measured and that only a four-inch gap was allowed under the
fence. He shared that they tried to remove ambiguous terms about what is appropriate or natural
concerning existing vegetation. He shared that they were proposing to eliminate the section on
berms as an alternate to landscaping and walls.
