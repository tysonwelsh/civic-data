                                       Business Meeting

               The Council reviewed the following agenda items:

    Code Enforcement

               Alicia Gonzales, Code Enforcement Supervisor, Greater Salt Lake Municipal
Services District (MSD), reviewed current code enforcement cases.

    Engineering Projects

               Shane Ellis, Engineer, Salt Lake County Engineering and Flood Control, stated
construction on the 730 east sidewalk project has begun. He has a drawing set and bid package
for Ski Connect that will be sent to the Council. He reviewed the Sego Lily cross section that was
previously proposed and an updated cross section for the Council’s consideration.

    White City Metro Township Planning Commission Applicants

               Rori Andreason reviewed applications submitted for the White City Metro
Township Planning Commission stating that Weston Millen, Stephanie Stolte, Tyler Huish, and
Howard Van Horn have applied. Gene Wilson and Chris Huntzinger expressed interest but did
not get an application turned in.

    Firework Restrictions

                 Council Member Perry stated every year the Council has the opportunity to
update the firework restrictions map. There has been some confusion on how restrictions would
affect South East Township Days, which is not the case. He suggested a buffer around Dimple
Dell, restriction of personnel fireworks along the canal trail and Big Bear Park. The resolution has
been amended to specify that restrictions are for personnel fireworks so there is not confusion

Unified Fire Authority (UFA)

              Chief Bruce Cline reviewed calls for service for the month of March and the
quarterly report. He thanked Council Member Perry for attending the Sandy City Fire
Department’s townhall on April 25, 2022. Sandy City Fire Department is planning on being a part
of South East Township Days. There have been a couple of fires below 700 east in Dimple Dell
Park. He is working with UFA fire investigators to determine the cause.

               Captain Ken Aldridge stated Dominic Burchett was hired as the new Fire Chief
for UFA. The swearing in ceremony was held on April 2, 2022, in Riverton. Recruit camp is in its
nineth week. The wildland fire crews will be ready for wildfire season. UFA is working on arson
cases, including the fires that were off of 700 east.

Resolution 2022-04-02 – Adopting the 2022 White City Metro Township General Plan

              Erin O’Kelley reviewed changes that were made to the White City Metro Township
General Plan that include formatting and adding the definition for ESRI. The changes do not have
anything to do with the substance of the plan. She received an email with some changes, and
one was substantive.

               Mayor Flint stated the census has a different population for White City. She would
like to know what the 2020 census says.

                 Council Member Little stated on page 46, it states that Salt Lake County can
apply for a rezone for Big Bear Park; however, White City owns the park, and the language should
be clarified to indicate White City can apply for a rezone.

              Council Member Price stated on page 51, language needs to be changed to say,
“comply with development standards”.

              Council Member Little reviewed the following concerns

   •   Page 54, language regarding the bike and pedestrian infrastructure
   •   Page 55, language under the Parking and Unloading paragraph
   •   Page 71, language regarding zoning ordinances needing to be updated
   •   Page 98, under paragraph LC6, change language to “reasonable efforts” when discussing
       the improvement of the quality of the natural environment and mitigate negative
       environmental impacts.

              Council Member Little made a motion to amend the White City Metro Township
General Plan to include the 10600 south corridor as part of the residential neighborhood
character. The motion dies due to a lack of a second.

              Council Member Price, seconded by Council Member Cardenaz, moved to amend
the White City Metro Township General Plan as presented and discussed. The motion passed
unanimously.

              Council Member Price, seconded by Council Member Perry, moved to approve
Resolution 2022-04-02, adopting the White City Metro Township General Plan as amended. The
motion passed 4 to 1, showing that Council Member Little voted in opposition.

                                         RECITALS

   A.     The White City Metro Township ("White City") is a Municipality pursuant to Utah
          Code §§ 10-2a-401 et seq.

   B.     Several residents of White City worked with the Greater Salt Lake Municipal
          Services District ("MSD") Planning Department to review and make
          recommendations to the White City Metro Township Planning Commission
          ("Planning Commission") regarding the White City Metro Township    General
          Plan (“General Plan”);

   C.     The Planning Commission reviewed the proposed General Plan and held a public
          hearing on September 23, 2021 to receive public comments on the plan. The
          Planning Commission then made recommendations to the White City Metro
          Township Council to approve the proposed General Plan;

   D.     On March 31, 2022, a public hearing was held at Eastmont Middle School to receive
          public comment on the draft White City Metro Township 2022 General Plan;

   E.     The White City Metro Township Council feel it is in the best interest of the residents
          of White City to adopt a General Plan for the community.

                                       RESOLUTION

       THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the White City Metro Township Council, the Council
adopts the 2022 White City Metro Township General Plan as attached.

Council Reports

Unified Fire Authority (UFA) & Unified Fire Service Area (UFSA)

              Council Member Perry stated UFA hosted the Women on Fire Leadership
Symposium for women across the country. UFA was able to achieve state funding for a UFA
bomb squad. The legislature did not realize that bomb squads were locally funded and are hoping
to get more funding for them. The budget process has started. Dominic Burchett was appointed
as the new Fire Chief and was sworn in on April 2, 2022.

                               −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Unified Police Department (UPD) & Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA)

               Mayor Flint stated UPD is holding a UPD 101 training. Frank Pignanelli, Foxley &
Pignanelli, presented a report on financing options for SLVLESA. There is a need for a new
command post for UPD.


                                               6
DATE            THURSDAY                               APRIL                                7, 2022

               Chief Randy Thomas stated the mobile communications command post is a
vehicle that is deployed to various emergency and community events. The mobile
communications command post is not on the normal vehicle replacement rotation. It was last
replaced in 2007 and has since had the chassis replaced, unfortunately the vehicle cannot be
repaired anymore. UPD is also looking for funds to provide bonuses for crossing guards that have
remained employed. The mental health unit has a sergeant and a clinical social worker with each
precinct having a part-time or secondary employee. The mental health unit’s average call takes
about 90 minutes and two officers. Mental health officers are referred to an individual and start
making visits to them daily, weekly, and monthly, just to talk to them. There are no funded
positions, one of the requests is for all of UPD to have one officer.

               Mayor Flint stated if UPD needs personnel, then the funding should come from
within the budget. The metro township can help fund the replacement of a mobile communications
command post. If the Council agrees to contribute funds, its portion would be $1,200.

                                 −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
South Salt Lake Valley Mosquito Abatement District

                  Council Member Price stated two district employees went to the AMCA meeting.
Technology is becoming a bigger part in controlling mosquitos rather than spraying poisons.
There is a study in Florida where mosquitos are being genetically modified so they cannot
reproduce. Areas around the country are working on various issues involving mosquitos. The
district is hiring for the summer season, and some will start this month. The position is full-time,
starting at $14.00 an hour.

                                 −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD)

               Mayor Flint stated the board has discussed the transition study to move the
engineering department from Salt Lake County to the MSD. A new pool of contractors has been
selected to handle engineering projects. New 2023 capital project requests have been placed on
hold; however, ongoing projects can still be funded. There is not enough staff to work on projects.
The board is still working on getting a director of finance hired.

                                 −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District (WFWRD)

               Council Member Cardenaz stated the board reviewed a survey conducted by Y2
Analytics, to determine where improvements could be made. The survey had 3,220 responses,
with 95 percent of people being happy with their garbage pick-up and 90 percent of people being
happy with the drop-off. In White City, 85 percent were satisfied with WFWRD.

Salt Lake County Animal Services

                 Council Member Little stated Salt Lake County Animal Services is full with dogs.
Some of the dogs were transferred to the Humane Society and it will be having an adoption sale.
A lot of people got dogs while living in apartments and were unable to keep them. Animal Services
is writing letters to landlords to allow apartment residents to keep the animal for a few weeks until
