PUBLIC HEARING:

PC 25-053 The Foundry [Design Review & Subdivision Permit] Danny Macfarlane/Wasatch Land
Holdings LLC, authorized agent/owner, is requesting a design review permit to build six, three-story
townhome units with garage parking on the ground floor, located at 454 North 100 West in the Town
Center One (TC-1) Zone; TIN 05-081-0032, in the Ellis Neighborhood.

Staff: Aimee Egbert, Logan City Planner, reviewed the request to subdivide a 0.21-acre property into
six building lots and construct six three-story townhome units with garage parking on the ground floor.
The TC-1 Zone allows for 100% lot coverage and requires a minimum of 50% building frontage along
the front setback. There are no minimum requirements for open space or usable outdoor areas. The
proposed development includes 49% building frontage and 32% lot coverage. The Planning
Commission has the authority to approve a frontage adjustment of up to 10%.

The TC-1 Zone also requires 20% transparency (fenestration) on all fagades of residential buildings.
As proposed, the building provides 15% fenestration on the north and east fagades, 13% on the west
fagade, and 28% on the south fagade. The applicant has indicated an intent to offer the townhomes
at an estimated price point of $275,000, noting the challenge of meeting this target within Logan
City’s current development standards and the rising cost of construction materials.

The Planning Commission has the authority to approve adjustments to design standards, including
fenestration, when specific findings can be made. These findings must demonstrate that the deviation
is not driven by financial considerations on the part of the applicant, is compatible with surrounding
land use patterns and community design, will not adversely affect future development or design
expectations, and reflects the City’s intent to support high-quality, enduring development.

The Land Development Code requires a two-foot setback from property lines for driveways to
facilitate stormwater infiltration and reduce the potential for vehicle encroachment. For the proposed
project, a two-foot setback is not feasible without reducing the building width, which would
compromise garage functionality. The plans instead propose curb and gutter along the southern


property line to collect and channel stormwater runoff away from the adjacent property and into the
storm drain system to effectively mitigate stormwater impacts. To ensure vehicle clearance and
prevent encroachment across the property line, staff recommends installing “No Parking” signage
along the south side of the driveway.

Commissioner Peterson asked if the curb and gutter requirement was included among the conditions.
A. Egbert confirmed that it is listed in condition number five.

Proponent: Danny Macfarlane stated that their intent is to sell the townhomes at a lower price point
of approximately $275,000. He requested that the Commission allow a variance to the fenestration
requirement to help keep construction costs down and make the homes more affordable.

Public: David Perry, who owns the home directly north of the proposed development, asked about
the Fire Code requirement for a 26-foot-wide driveway access. Mr. Perry also expressed concerns
about the loss of privacy resulting from a three-story building next door and noted that the proposed
structure would block sunlight to his property.

Commission: Commissioner Peterson asked Mohammed Abdulahi, Logan City Attorney, to address
legal concerns raised in a public comment. M. Abdulahi explained that while the comment stating
that “illegally subdivided properties should not be recognized by the City” would typically be correct,
this case is an exception because the property was previously sold to the State, which is not subject
to local land use regulations. When the State owned the property, Logan City recognized it as legally
subdivided and continues to do so—two owners later. Had the property been sold to anyone other
than the State, the City would not have recognized it as legally subdivided.

Commissioner Lewis said it is good to see this type of housing being developed in Logan. D. Lewis
stated that he is not concerned with the setbacks but would like to see the project meet the required
20% fenestration.

Commissioner Heare said he wanted to clarify concerns raised in the public comment and asked if
there are any transition requirements for the project. A. Egbert responded that there are no transition
requirements because the project is not located across the street from single-family zoning.

Commissioner Heare also asked about the applicant not owning the land. A. Egbert explained that
the property owner will sign at the final plat stage.

Commissioner Peterson asked whether the 20% fenestration requirement applies to all sides of the
building or only the street-facing fagade. A. Egbert confirmed that the 20% fenestration is required
on all sides of the building.

Commissioner Duncan said that aesthetically, the north fagade should meet the 20% fenestration
requirement. Commissioner Heare added that the west fagade should also meet the 20%
requirement.

Commissioner Heare asked for clarification on the width of the driveway and whether that
measurement includes the curb and gutter. A. Egbert responded that the driveway is 20 feet wide,
including the curb and gutter.

Commissioner Heare proposed that the project be required to meet the 20% fenestration standard
on the north and west fagades.

Commissioner Lewis said he does not agree with Commissioner Heare’s proposal. D. Lewis stated
that he supports the project as conditioned, with 20% fenestration on all sides. He added that the


price point should not be a consideration for the Commission; rather, the focus should be on design
quality and compatibility with surrounding development.

Commissioner Duncan said she agrees with Commissioner Lewis and questioned whether requiring
one or two additional windows would truly increase the cost by $25,000.

Commissioner Lewis noted that several design options could be incorporated to meet the fenestration
requirement. He emphasized that achieving 20% fenestration on the north and west facades is
essential.

Commissioner McNamara said she agrees with Commissioner Lewis regarding both compatibility
and the need for 20% fenestration on all sides.

Commissioner Heare concluded that the Commission has the latitude to reduce the fenestration
requirement in order to support more affordable housing options.

MOTION: Commissioner Heare made a motion to conditionally approve a Design Review Permit
for PC 25-053, with amended conditions of approval, requiring 20% fenestration on the north, west,
and south facades, and allowing a zero-foot driveway setback on the south property line with the
addition of curb and gutter and “No Parking” signage. The motion includes findings for approval as
listed below. Commissioner Duncan seconded the motion. The motion was approved unanimously.

PC 25-054 LDC Amendment — Infill & Flag Lot Development Standards [Code Amendment] Russ

Holley/Logan City is requesting an amendment to the Land Development Code, Chapter 17.30.180
to clarify road standards and access specifications for single-family and multi-family infill and flag lot
development.

Staff/Proponent: Russ Holley, Logan City Planner, reviewed the proposed amendments to Land
Development Code Chapter 17.30.180 Flag Lot and Middle of the Block Infill Development
Standards:

A.

B.

Standards in this section will only apply to new single and multi-family residential developments
within the interior of existing Logan blocks, where development exists around at least 50% of the
block perimeter, and the proposed project site is below the lot width or frontage requirements.
The project must include at least one new residential building that is not adjacent to a public
street. A proposed project large enough to create standard city blocks and new public street
connections would not be considered infill.

Single Family Detached Flag Lots.

Single-family detached residential flag lot subdivisions of up to two lots total shall meet the

following standards:

1. The minimum size of the proposed new flag lot and existing base lot shall both meet the
minimum standard lot size be-+60% of the zone.

2. Shared Driveways providing access to the new flag lot shall be a minimum of 20 feet wide.
The driveway shall maintain a two-foot setback from adjoining property lines and an eight-foot
setback from existing residential structures.

3. Building setbacks for new homes shall include a 20-foot front setback from the shared
driveway, with side and rear setbacks equal to 459% 125% of the zone’s standard.

C. Single Family Detached Middle of the Block Infill.

Single-family detached subdivisions of three to six lots shall meet the following standards:

1. The minimum size of the each new lot shall be 125% of the minimum lot size in the zone.

2. The private road accessing the new single-family homes shall be provided with a separate,
commonly owned property and may terminate with a cul-de-sac, loop, or emergency vehicle
turnaround. The private access road shall contain two travel lanes, each 10 feet wide, and
one on-street parking lane, 8 feet wide. The parking lane may be removed from up to 30%
of the road length to address narrow sections (“pinch-points”) of the project site. Curb and
gutter shall be provided along both sides of the access road with a 4-foot-wide sidewalk along
one side. The private road and sidewalk shall maintain a minimum 2-foot setback from
property lines and an 8-foot setback from existing residential structures.

3. New construction shall comply with the minimum setbacks required in the zone.

Commissioner Peterson asked whether the shared driveway setbacks would make flag lots
accessible and whether any of the proposed changes would affect ADUs. R. Holley responded that
the proposed changes would not impact ADUs and explained that 20 feet is the minimum driveway
width required for fire access, the 8-foot setback maintains the privacy of existing homes, and the 2-
foot property line setback is the standard driveway setback.

Public: Steve Coppieters, developer, recommended maintaining 5-foot and 2-foot setbacks and
cautioned against increasing infill lot sizes, noting that larger setbacks and lot size requirements can
make infill projects infeasible.

Joshua Gardynik stated that he supports the proposed infill code and the 8-foot setback from existing
structures for safety purposes. He suggested reducing driveway setbacks to 5 and 2 feet for single-
family developments only, not for multi-family projects.

Adam Machelprang, project engineer, requested that the sidewalk be allowed within the 8-foot
setback to assist their project.

Commission: Commissioner Heare stated that single-family flag lots should not be required to be
150% of the minimum lot size for the zone, suggesting that flag lots should be the same size as the
standard minimum lot. He also recommended that side and rear setbacks be 125% of the zone
standard.

Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director, explained that the increased lot size requirement
is intended to provide a buffer for existing homes.

Commissioner Lewis stated that as a resident in an area where infill is possible, he appreciates the
150% minimum lot size as a means to provide additional buffering for neighboring properties.
