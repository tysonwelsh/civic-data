2.1 North Point Solid Waste District Update- Neil Schwendiman
Neil Schwendeman from North Point Solid Waste District presented an update on the district. He
reported the recent data which included an increase in construction and demotion waste. He
informed the Council that there will be an increase in fees and requested that the city consider
this as they prepare the budget. He also noted that North Point is currently in the design process
for facility improvements and will present those plans at a future meeting.

2.2 Bike and Pedestrian Master Plan Update- Planning Department
Mike West provided an update on the Bike and Pedestrian Master Plan, highlighting the city's
progress in active transportation and focus on safety. West presented data showing Lehi had over
75 miles of paved trails, 17 miles of bike lanes, and 1.8 miles of separated bikeways. Most of
these were built through developments, density in lieu fees, and area plans. The goals included
addressing missing sidewalks, enhancing trail safety, improving access to key destinations, and
creating more pedestrian-friendly spaces. West shared trail usage data, noting significant
increases in trail usage after new connections like the rail trail bridge, with some trails seeing
average daily usage of 300-700 people. The plan aimed to prioritize routes, analyze gaps in the
network, and improve overall connectivity and safety for cyclists and pedestrians.

2.3. Discussion of Public Improvement Districts and Sidewalks
Dean Lundell discussed the potential implementation of special assessment areas (SAAs) for
sidewalk construction in older parts of the city. He explained that these districts would allow
residents to finance infrastructure improvements, with the city potentially covering some costs.
He explained that the model can be set up to the Council’s preference. Council members debated
the merits and challenges of such a program, including participation thresholds, funding
mechanisms, and the responsibility for sidewalk installation. The discussion highlighted
concerns about resident affordability, the impact on property values, and the need for
comprehensive safety data to justify sidewalk investments. While some council members
supported exploring alternative funding sources like grants and city funds, others emphasized the
importance of resident contribution and the potential benefits of a special improvement district
approach.

    would remain limited in density and structure type by a development agreement, which

14. Consideration of Ordinance #28-2025 approving a Development Code amendment to
    Chapter 20, Home Occupations, to address landscape and construction companies.
    The petitioners were BreAnn and Mitchell Beckstrom.

    The Beckstroms had previously operated their business from their residential property and
    stated that they had done so based on an issued business license and an understanding that the
    use was consistent with how the property had been utilized under its previous owner.

    During the presentation, the Beckstroms detailed their experience, including a forced
    relocation of business operations due to code enforcement action, the financial and emotional
    impact of this disruption, and their desire to return to operating from home. Brianne

    occupations, permitted landscape and construction-type businesses by conditional use permit,
    allowed limited deliveries and retail sales, and included restrictions to prohibit certain
    vehicles and activities. It also proposed the introduction of traffic management plans and
    limits on the number of employees as additional safeguards.

    adopting a citywide ordinance to address a single-use case. Councilmember Stallings raised
    concerns regarding the legal implications and enforceability of conditional use permits, the

    not be appropriate to enact a code amendment that could have broad and unintended impacts
    on neighborhoods throughout the city.

16. Consideration of Ordinance #31-2025 approving a Development Code Amendment to
    Table 37.080, Off Street Parking.
    Mr. Struthers noted that currently, the parking table defines parking stall requirements for
    various uses, but for parks, playgrounds, open space, and trails, it simply states "as approved
    by planning commission." The proposed amendment would shift the parking determination
    authority from the planning commission to the DRC or zoning administrator. Struthers noted
    that this change would typically involve staff recommending parking requirements, especially
    since many of these facilities are city-owned. When Michelle Stallings asked if there was
    typically public comment on such issues, Kim confirmed there was not, as these are generally
    permitted uses where staff would make recommendations.

    Motion:     Councilor Newall moved to approve Ordinance #31-2025 approving a
                Development Code Amendment to Table 37.080, Off Street Parking; included all
                Development Review Committee Comments. Councilor Stallings seconded the
                motion.

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Excused; Councilor Hancock,
    Yes; Councilor Newall, Yes; and Councilor Stallings, Yes. The motion passed with 4 in favor

  Jason Walker discussed the historical sign marker for the Cutler property. He explained that
  while this was not necessarily a city action, the city's involvement was primarily financial.
  Walker had sent an email earlier that morning outlining various options for addressing the

    involved, and the city might consider providing financial assistance to resolve the situation.
    Walker emphasized that he was seeking guidance from the council on how to proceed and was

    Councilor Stallings opposed using city funds to modify the marker, emphasizing that the

    other families with more significant historical contributions (like the Harris family with the

    believed that changing historical documentation to appease one family would be inappropriate
    and could create a larger historical preservation issue. Newall argued that the Historical
    Society, not individual families, should determine historical significance.

    Councilor Hancock agreed that city funds should not be spent but was open to exploring
    alternative solutions. Mayor Johnson suggested leaving the matter open to see if a palatable
