2. Discussion Items
    2.a. Public Infrastructure District Policy Discussion
    Marlin Eldred, Economic Development Director, informed that Council of a Public
    Improvement District (PID). It’s an opportunity for a municipality to create a PID area under
    governance for a private entity, and they can then bond for public infrastructure. The caveat is
    that they become a taxing entity, and they will have a mil levy. The PID tax would show on
    the property tax notice for that area. He said that the State is requiring a notice for home buyers
    within the PID area so that potential buyers are aware of the tax. Every landowner within the
    PID has to agree to the tax. Typically, you’ll see it used where 1 or 2 landowners want to
    develop the property. Mr. Eldred distributed a document that shows a potential policy for the
    City to adopt before a landowner asks the City for one. A Developer would want to use this
    because they could bond for infrastructure improvements for up to 40 years. The city is not
    required to grant a PID. If the city finds that a proposal will not benefit the city, then they do
    not have to grant the request. Mr. Eldred explained how an application process could work in
    the City.
