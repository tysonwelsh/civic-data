Open & Public Meetings Act Training – Craig Carlston, City Attorney (41:33)

City Attorney Craig Carlston addressed the Council and gave an update on the Annual
Open and Public Meetings Act training which is required by law to be completed
annually. He stated the Open and Public Meetings Act was enacted upon the premise that
the State, its agencies, and its political subdivisions exist to aid in the conduct of the
people’s business. As such, a public body should deliberate and act openly. The Open and
Public Meetings Act requires that members of a public body be provided with annual
training on the requirements of the Open and Public Meetings Act.

Mr. Carlston, for the benefit of the public clarified that the municipal employee ethics
act permits employees and elected officials to contract with the City, so long as there is
prior disclosure.

Mr. Carlston also said the public notice requirement of 24-hour notice is still the same,
but there is a requirement of a Class A notice. The requirements are satisfied by posting
the notice on the Utah Public Notice Website, the city’s website, and it is no longer
required to be posted in various public locations beyond City Hall.

There was a change in a definition that is not on the outline. House Bill 36 passed
changed the definition of a meeting. "Meeting" means a gathering: (a) of a public body or
specified body; (b) with a quorum present; and that is convened: (i) by an individual:
(A) with authority to convene the public body or specified body; and (B) following the
process provided by law for convening the public body or specified body; and (ii) for the
express purpose of acting as a public body or specified body to: (A) receive public
comment about a relevant matter; (B) deliberate about a relevant matter; or (C) take
action upon a relevant matter. (Utah Code Anno. §52-4-103)

A reminder that a closed meeting may be held if: (a) (i)a quorum is present; (ii)the
meeting is an open meeting for which notice has been given under Section 52-4-202 ; and
(iii) (A) two-thirds of the members of the public body present at the open meeting vote to
approve closing the meeting; (B)for a meeting that is required to be closed under
Section 52-4-205, if a majority of the members of the public body present at an open
meeting vote to approve closing the meeting. (Utah Code Anno. §52-04-204)

1.   Intent of the Act:
     a.      Public bodies “exist to aid in the conduct of the people’s business.”
     b.      Public bodies should “take their actions openly; and conduct their deliberations
             openly.”       (Utah Code Ann. §52-4-102)

2.   Definition of a Meeting:
     a.      The convening of a public body, with a quorum present (whether in person or by
             electronic means), “for the purpose of discussing, receiving comments from the
             public about, or acting upon a matter over which the public body…has
             jurisdiction or advisory power.”
     b.      “Meeting” does not mean a chance or social gathering. Cannot use a chance
             meeting or social gathering to circumvent the purpose of the Open and Public
             Meetings Act. (Utah Code Ann. §§52-4-103(6), 52-4-208)

3.   Definition of a Quorum: “Quorum” means a simple majority of the membership of a
     public body, unless otherwise defined by law. (Utah Code Ann. §52-4-103 (11)

4.   Public Notice for a Meeting:
     a.     At least 24-hour notice of the meeting, including the agenda, date, time, and
            location of the meeting.
     b.     Notice must be posted at the principal office of the public body and on the Utah
            Public Notice website and newspaper or local media correspondent.
     c.     The schedule of regularly scheduled meetings must also be noticed once a year.
     d.     Emergency meetings once approved by a majority of the public body require the
            best notice practicable. (Utah Code Ann. §52-4-202)

5.   Agenda for Meeting:
     a.    Agenda items should have reasonable specificity to notify the public.
     b.    Items raised by the public, but not on the agenda, may be discussed but no action
           may be taken. (Utah Code Ann. §52-4-202)

6.   Written Minutes/ Meeting Recording:
     a.     Written minutes of an open meeting include the date, time, and place of the
            meeting; the names of the members present and absent; and the substance of the
            matters discussed; a record of each vote taken; a summary of public comments.
     b.     A recording of an open meeting shall be a complete and unedited record of the
            open meeting. (Utah Code Ann. §52-4-203)

7.   Closed Meeting:
     a.     A closed meeting may be held if a quorum is present; the meeting starts out as an
            open meeting for which proper notice has been given; two-thirds of the members'
            present vote to approve closing the meeting; the reasons for closing the meeting
            are entered into the record of the public portion of the meeting; a recording of the
            closed meeting is kept (exception for discussion on individual or for security
            issues).

RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommended that the Planning Commission recommend denial to the Municipal
Council for a rezone of approximately 1.80 acres of property located at approximately
1360 North 200 West (TIN# 05-041-0056) from Mixed Residential Low (MR-12) to
Mixed Residential Medium (MR-20).

Land use adjoining the subject property

North: CS: Commercial Uses East: COM: Commercial Uses
South: NR-6: Residential Uses West: MR-12: Residential Uses

Background
In 1992, the Logan City Planning Commission approved the six (6) lot Royal Point
Subdivision located south of 1400 North between 200 and 400 West. Each lot was
approximately 1.30 to 1.80 acres in size. Lot #6 is 1.80 acres and the location of this
request. In 1992, the zoning was R4, which allowed multi-family at a rate of one unit per
every 6,000 square feet (SF) and an additional 1,000 SF for each subsequent unit (4-plexs
would require 9,000 SF). Over the course of two (2) years, seven 4-plexs were built on
Lot #6. In 1995, after the seventh fourplex was built, the owners illegally subdivided lot
#6 into eight tax identification numbers (TIN) at the county and never received city
permits or approvals for this action. The remainder eighth area (0.24-ac.) was
subsequently sold off and is now the subject property for this request. In 2000 zoning
changed to MFH-14 and the project became "legally existing nonconforming" because
lot#6 (the city recognized 1.80-acre building lot) sits at 15.5 units-per-acre with the
existing seven permitted four-plex's. In 2013 zoning changed to MR-12, and the project
remains nonconforming. Since 2000, zoning has been based on a units-per-acre density
rather than the 6,000 SF plus 1,000 SF formula.

The current applicant (Jed Willets) purchased this remainder property (illegal building
lot) thinking he could build a duplex because it was zoned MR-12, and it was a 0.24-acre
property size. Logan City decided to rezone the 1.80 acre building lot to MR-20 in 2016
                                                                                 10 | Page
           Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ November 19, 2024
to allow for a duplex to be built. This same applicant received a Design Review Permit
for a duplex but never pulled a building permit and allowed this entitlement to expire.
Again in 2023, the City upzoned the 1.80 acres to MR-20 and issued a new Design
Review Permit for the duplex. Again, this same applicant let this entitlement expire in
March of 2024 without obtaining a building permit.

This request is from the same applicant; however, this request is an upzone to build a
four-plex rather than the duplex. If up-zoned to MR-20, the 1.80 acre building lot could
support 36 dwelling units max. With seven existing four-plex's (28 units existing), that
could allow for a new 12-plex.

GENERAL PLAN
The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP) identifies this area as Mixed Residential. It does not
distinguish between medium and high. As part of the revised Land Development Code
{LDC) and Zoning Map update in February of 2011, the area was zoned Mixed
Residential Medium (MRM) rather than Mixed Residential High (MRH) because this
area was over-saturated with multi-family structures exceeding the balance of housing
options outlined in the General Plan.

Numerous Bridger Neighborhood meetings and public comments from the Logan City
School District has indicated a desire to down-zone developable areas to get more low-
density detached single-family homes for better neighborhood balance and longer-term
occupancy in a neighborhood that struggles with transiency. There have been several
down zones in the area and new housing developments in the neighborhood over the last
several years have been predominately single-family homes (Quayle Meadows, Lloyd
Estates, Cottonwood Estates).

LAND DEVELOPMENT CODE
The Land Development Code (LDC) allows for up to 20 residential units per acre of land
in the MR-20 zone. The MR-20 zoning district permits a range of multi-family residential
structures. The current zoning designation of MR-12 allows for similar structures but at a
lower density.

SUMMARY
Jed Willets originally purchased the property thinking he could build a duplex. After
discovering the illegal subdivision, the City of Logan rezoned the property twice. Once in
2016 and again in 2023, for the applicant to build a duplex. Both times, the applicant
failed to build a duplex. The most recent entitlement was issued in March of 2023. The
applicant submitted a duplex building permit application just days prior to the one-year
(March 2024) deadline of that entitlement.

Over the last six months, since March of 2024, the applicant has not completed the
building permit plans as instructed and paid the fee for final building permit issuance.
The Logan City Building Department cancelled the building permit application after six
months and the failure of the applicant's part to finalize and pay the fee, in September of
2024. A typical duplex building permit application is finalized and issued with fees paid
in 2-4 weeks.

This application is requesting an upzone for a different reason now, the applicant is

efforts to downzone remaining developable areas into single-family zones, to better
balance out the housing stock that currently has a much higher concentration of multi-

RECOMMENDED FINDINGS FOR REZONE DENIAL
The Planning Commission bases its decision on the following findings supported in the
administrative record for this project:
1. The Logan City FLUP identifies the area as MR. The surrounding areas are zoned MR-
12 and this property should also be zoned MR-12 for consistency.
2. The Bridger Neighborhood was specifically downzoned to reduce the number new
multi- family developments in order to better balance housing stock in this neighborhood
as compared to other Logan City neighborhoods.
3. The MR-12 zone would prevent future multi-family development as this 1.80 acre lot
is already above the maximum density of 12 units per acre.

On October 24, 2024, the Planning Commission recommended denial to the Municipal
Council for the Willets Rezone from MR-12 to MR-20. Planning Commissioners vote
(4-0).

RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommended that the Planning Commission recommend approval to the Municipal

REQUEST
This is a proposal to update the definition of a Behavioral and Mental Health Receiving
Center in 17.62 to address the placement of supportive housing with a Behavioral and
Mental Health Receiving Center in a Public Zone.

BACKGROUND
The Planning Commission approved a Conditional Use Permit for the Bear River Mental
Health Receiving Center on September 12, 2024. The site is zoned Public and the
proposed project included a supportive housing component. The Public Zone does not
permit most types of housing. The approval was conditioned upon an amendment to the
definition of a receiving center in the Land Development Code to clarify that supportive
housing is a permitted element of a receiving center.




                                                                                   14 | Page
           Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ November 19, 2024
SUMMARY OF CHANGES
The proposed definition in the Code Amendment Application is as follows:

"Behavioral and Mental Health Receiving Center" means a 23 hour nonsecure program or
facility that is responsible for, and provides mental health crisis services to, an individual
experiencing a behavioral or mental health crisis. A Behavioral and Mental Health
Receiving Center may also provide on-site, supportive long-term, short-term term or
transitional housing for individuals needing a supervised living environment due to
emotional, psychological developmental or behavioral dysfunctions or impairments.

After additional internal discussions, the proposed definition has been modified as
follows:

"Behavioral and Mental Health Receiving Center" means a 23 hour nonsecure program or
facility that is responsible for, and provides mental health crisis services to, an individual
experiencing a behavioral or mental health crisis. A Behavioral and Mental Health
Receiving Center may also provide on-site, supportive long term, short term or
transitional housing for individuals needing a supervised living environment due to
emotional, psychological developmental or behavioral dysfunctions or impairments.
Supportive housing shall be designed and integrated into the overall site design as an
accessory use. Stand-alone housing without a receiving center is not permitted.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION AND SUMMARY
With the exception of some very specific types of living arrangements , housing is not a
permitted use in the Public Zone. Supportive housing, as used in the proposed definition,
fits into a narrow type of living arrangement that is consistent with the purpose of the
Bear River Mental Health Department and aligns with other types of unique living
arrangements outside of regular housing. Staff's approach in this proposed language is to
not permit general or market rate housing in the Public Zone, but rather to allow for
supportive long term, short term, or transitional housing for individuals that need some
form of supervised living arrangement due to some underlying emotional, psychological
developmental, or behavioral dysfunction or impairment. The services provided to
individuals requiring some level of supportive housing are typically aligned with either a
governmental or non-profit entity providing supportive types of services. The definition
is narrow enough with the additional proposed language that ties housing into the overall
receiving center's campus design, is considered "accessory" to the receiving center, and is
not permitted without the receiving center.

GENERAL PLAN
The Land Development Code was prepared and adopted to implement the vision
expressed in the General Plan. The purpose of these code changes is to update and clarify
provisions in the Land Development Code relative to the provision of housing in
conjunction with a Behavioral and Mental Health Receiving Center. The proposed
updates are consistent with the General Plan.
