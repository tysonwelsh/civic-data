    2.1. Discussion regarding nonprofit organizations - Wendy Thomas, Assistant City Manager
Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas indicated she had four goals for this discussion which included the
Council directing staff to accept applications for a startup advisory board of directors to make recommendations
to City Council on mission, vision, to draft articles of incorporation and bylaws, to propose a location for
operational purposes, to propose a name for the organization, and to set a startup operating budget of $5,000.

The Council discussed naming options and requested the use of Friends of Herriman Parks, Trails and Culture. The
Council suggested utilizing the same advisory board incorporation process as used in all other board and
commissions. Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn recommended the board of directors be set to three members.
The Council agreed. Assistant City Manager Thomas asked if the Council was supportive of the non-profit
organization using the Community Center as the place of business as the articles of incorporation required a
physical address. The Council concurred. Assistant City Manager Thomas notated the $5,000 start-up budget
would be utilized out of existing park and recreation funds which would help get through the organization’s
initial fees and needed software.

     2.2. Discussion regarding a Sewer Reimbursement Agreement with South Valley Sewer District -
           Blake Thomas, Community Development Director
Community Development Director Blake Thomas reported Jordan School District and Herriman City have
coordinated and worked to plan for the installation of infrastructure improvements, including roadways and
utilities, to service the new elementary school in the Hidden Oaks development. The City made an agreement
with the school district notating the improvements would be complete prior to the school opening in the fall of
2022. One of the improvements required for the new school would be sanitary sewer. The sewer line would
connect from the new school to the sewer main running parallel to Butterfield Creek, under the proposed
roadways of Silver Sky Drive and Twisted Oak Drive. The sewer line would also be able to service a portion of
the Olympia development, thus making it eligible for reimbursement by South Valley Sewer District as a system
improvement. Councilmember Clint Smith reported the district approved the reimbursement agreement at their
previous meeting.

Director Thomas recommended moving forward with the reimbursement agreement with Ivory Homes and
finalize an agreement with South Valley Sewer to replace those funds. He noted the agreement would come back
to the Council at a future meeting. The Council agreed.

    2.6. Transportation Master Plan Progress Update - Jonathan Bowers, City Engineer
Staff Engineer Bryce Terry updated Council on the Transportation Master Plan since its adoption in 2020. He
observed the impending boundary adjustment to incorporate land into Herriman City’s boundaries which caused
staff to ensue discussions with the developer to address the necessary road infrastructure as the current planned
land use of Olympia was not included. This update would correct come socioeconomic data and update
population projections based on the 2020 census. With these updates in mind, a new traffic model was created
for the master plan and would be presented to the Council once finalized, along with an updated Impact Fee
Analysis and Impact Fee Facilities Plan.

Wall Consultant Group Engineer Jeremy Searle presented the 2050 demographic projections. The initial
alignment assumptions would likely change, and an additional study would evaluate other alternate alignments.
Utah Department of Transportation had performed an environmental impact study for future U-111 alignment
between 11800 South and Herriman Boulevard. The initial 2050 volume projections could be handled by a three
or five lane cross section. The pinch points at Mountain View Corridor at 11800 South, 12600 South, and 13400
South. He offered comparisons of the feeling of those locations in the future. The traffic projections offered
some insight of which projects would need to be completed to accommodate future growth. The preliminary
additional projects were:
     • Widen Herriman Blvd/12600 South to seven Lanes (Mountain View Corridor to 6000 West)
     • Widen 13400 South (from Mirabella to 6400 West) to five lanes
     • Potentially widen 11800 South to seven lanes

Mr. Searle offered a closeup of the daily traffic for northern Herriman. The similar volumes would be comparable
to University Parkway in Orem, Utah. The base model roadway cross section within the annexation area would
require either a three-lane or five-lane roadway.

Mr. Searle continued with the Transportation Master Plan assumptions where there were no Bluffdale City
connections along Real Vista Drive or 5600 West which, if installed, would present altering assumptions. He
offered a comparative analysis of the current and updated transportation plan outlining substantial increases of
traffic on Herriman roadways. The traffic increases could bring forth the necessity of potential projects such as
widening main roadways to seven lanes.

    8.1. Discussion and consideration of a draft ordinance to amend Herriman City Code Title 10 Chapter
         29 to comply with Utah State Code for internal accessory dwelling units (File No. Z2021-078) -
         Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy presented the draft ordinance to amend Title 10 of the Herriman City Code to
comply with recent changes in the Utah State Code for internal accessory dwelling units in single-family
dwellings. Staff chose to limit the ordinance amendment to the state standards. He presented the timeline for
the proposal commencing in June 2021. The Planning Commission offered a recommendation to approve the
text change.

City Planner Maloy offered a summary of HB 0082 indicating the City must permit internal accessory dwelling
units in any area zoned primarily for residential use. It could exclude up to 25-percent of eligible residential
October 27, 2021 – City Council Minutes                                                                Page 8 of 10
zoning districts. the bill prohibited cities from using the term “unrelated” when defining family and could not
adopt a single-family limit of less than four individuals. The bill indicated cities could prohibit short-term rentals
of less than 30 days.

City Planner Maloy recommended the adopting the proposed amendments:
    • Updating terminology to accessory dwelling unit from accessory apartment
    • Allow accessory dwelling units in agricultural and R-1 zoning designations
    • Limit one accessory dwelling unit per residential parcel larger than 6,000 square feet
    • Require the accessory dwelling unit and home to be directly adjacent
    • Owner occupancy required
    • Limit occupancy to a definition of “family”
    • Restrict the accessory dwelling unit to be used as a short-term rental
    • If an additional access has been provided for the accessory dwelling unit, the entrance must be on the
        side or rear of the home
    • Require one additional parking stall for the accessory dwelling unit
    • Maintain compliance with the building code

Councilmember Ohrn moved to approve Ordinance No 2021-29 amending Title 10 of the Herriman City Code to comply with
state code for internal accessory dwelling units. Councilmember Smith seconded the motion.

The vote was recorded as follows:
