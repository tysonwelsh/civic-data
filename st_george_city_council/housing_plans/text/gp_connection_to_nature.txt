Connection to Nature
The natural beauty of St. George is a vital asset to the city and its residents. With a fantastic climate and extensive trail network, it is easy to enjoy St. George’s magnificent scenery.
These natural treasures constitute an essential component of the quality of life in St. George and impart a generational responsibility for their stewardship.
They should be protected and preserved with the following strategies:
PROTECT SCENIC VISTAS
a. Define priority views within the city and create policies to support and preserve associated view corridors.
i. Identify priority views in undeveloped areas of the city and promote roadway design to preserve these views.
ii. iii. Identify strategies and tools to assess the impact of development on the view shed. Implement policies to support view preservation, such as roadway standards that support connected roadway networks that allow for views to the surrounding landscape.
PROTECT AND ENHANCE THE NIGHT SKY BY MINIMIZING LIGHT POLLUTION
a. Promote community education to raise awareness about the importance of dark skies.
b. Identify strategies to reduce unnecessary light at night.
c. Consider updating ordinance to enhance dark sky requirements for new developments.
ENHANCE ACCESS TO TRAILS, PARKS, RECREATION, AND OPEN SPACE
a. Enhance, expand, and integrate the trail network into the urban fabric. Align with goals identified in Transportation.
b. Encourage new developments to identify and designate sensitive or unique land features as park and trail areas in the future.
i. ii. iii. iv. v. Balance designation of special landscape features as open space with standards for usable outdoor space. Coordinate park and open space with Neighborhood Areas. Align with goals identified in Lifestyle. Standardize designation criteria to ensure parks have a variety of landscapes and features, limiting grass to specific sports fields and active use areas. Assess all parks, trails, and open space areas owned and managed by the City. Update policy to enable smaller parks in revitalization or redevelopment areas.
c. Connect parks and trail systems and provide support to complete the trail network through new developments as they occur. Align with goals identified in Accessible City.
PRESERVE SENSITIVE LANDS, INCLUDING STEEP SLOPES, RIDGE LINES OR SENSITIVE BLUFF PLATEAUS, HABITAT, WETLANDS, AND FLOODPLAINS WITHIN ST. GEORGE
a. Expand the scope of the Hillside Ordinance and the Hillside Review Board to include additional sensitive lands such as landslide areas and sensitive bluff plateaus.
b. Maintain and enhance the Flood Plain Ordinance to support preservation of flood prone areas within the City.
c. Identify areas that pose substantial risks or hazards and should be considered to be placed in a public land trust to support the health and safety of the community and provide tax incentives for the property owners.
i. ii. Prioritize flood, landslide, and rockslide prone areas for open space or public land trust integration. Consider opportunities for development right transfer from sensitive lands to other priority development areas.
d. Expand policies for new development to mitigate impacts on sensitive habitats and land.
i. ii. iii. Integrate landscape and water use policies into the City Ordinance for landslide-prone areas. Integrate riparian area policies for riverbeds, washes, and other flood-prone areas. Consider creating a habitat overlay and cultural resource overlay to support sensitive land preservation.
e. Create a cultural resource policy to support preservation of archaeological sites and resources. See Arts and Culture Integration for more detail.
f. Ensure the City’s expectations of preservation are clear and predictable for developers and land-owners by providing clear policies.
PROTECT CLEAN AIR AND WATER
a. Support water conservation efforts by implementing strategies and promoting goals identified in Responsible Growth.
b. Promote clean energy opportunities by and support energy conservation in keeping with goals identified in Responsible Growth.
c. Integrate appropriate stormwater strategies into City Ordinances to support watershed health, recognizing the alkaline nature of the soil in and around St. George.
