    4. DISCUSSION AND/OR ACTION ON THE TAX REVIEW COMMITTEE RECOMMENDATION
       REGARDING THE REQUEST FOR PRIOR YEAR TAX RELIEF FROM BRANDON C. BOPKO,
       TAX PARCEL 21-026-0061.
         Steffani Ebert (Clerk/Auditor Office): Received a request for prior year disabled veteran tax relief from
         Brandon Bopko on February 26th, 2025 for abatement back to 2020. He was informed the Clerk/Auditor’s
         office could only abate taxes with a late appeal for up to one year. He filled out and is approved for tax relief
         for 2024 and 2025. He claims he is only required to submit an application one time for tax relief as a disable
         veteran. While the Tax Commission standard does in fact state disabled veterans are required to submit one
         application for their ongoing exemption, the code also states annual contact with taxpayers receiving any
         exemptions is necessary to ensure the exemptions are applied consistently and equitably. We need to be able
         to verify that ownership or occupancy has not changed, the claimant is still living, or the disabled rating is still
         the same. Mr. Bopko did not turn in the tax relief forms for 2020 through 2023. His case was heard on March
         12th, 2025 by the Tax Review Committee where they recommended to not grant tax relief for those years.

         Commissioner Froerer moved to approve the Tax Review Committee recommendation to deny the tax relief
         request from Brandon C. Bopko for years 2020 to 2023, Tax Parcel 21-026-0061; Commissioner Harvey
         seconded.
         Chair Bolos – aye; Commissioner Froerer – aye; Commissioner Harvey – aye

    2. Public Hearing regarding parking violation fine increases in areas near Pineview and Causey Reservoirs.

         Sean Wilkinson (Community Development Director): This is a First Reading for an ordinance to increase
         parking violation fines near Pineview and Causey Reservoirs. Weber County has seen an increase in parking
         violations at both reservoirs, particularly during the summer season. These parking violations create a public
         safety hazard by increasing pedestrian traffic near moving vehicles and reducing travel space available for
         emergency vehicles. Currently parking violations are $50 if paid within 14 days and $100 after 14 days. The
         new proposed fines will be effective between May 15th and September 15th each year on any highway or road
         where Weber County has parking enforcement authority in the following two locations: all locations within
         one quarter mile of Pineview Reservoir high watermark and the road to Causey Reservoir and all roads
         branching off of it east of the Weber Memorial Park entrance. If the new fine is paid within 14 days, $150
         with an increase to $300 after 14 days. If someone had fines in the prior year, the fine within 14 days is
         $300, after 14 days it is $600. The increased fine for repeat offenders is new and limited to the proposed
         areas.

         Commissioner Froerer: Pineview will be under the new city’s jurisdiction as of January 1, 2026. Will this
         ordinance still be in effect?

         Mr. Wilkinson: The ordinance will still be in effect until the new city makes a change.

    3. Public Hearing to rezone, by ordinance, approximately 25.69 acres of land located at approximately 600
       South 4700 West, from the A-1 zone to the RE-15 zone- File #ZMA2024-09.

         Tammy Aydelotte (Planning): This was at work session in August 2023. The applicant would be providing
         significant connectivity, a parks donation of $7500 per unit with 75 units expected. We have gone back and
         forth about the Hooper Canal pathway. The canal is located immediately south of the project boundaries.
         The developer proposed providing some pathway without being disproportionate. This went before the
         Planning Commission in August of last year and minor changes have been made to the pathway layout. The
         Planning Commission Chair and Vice Chair have agreed the changes did not have to come back to the
         Planning Commission. Five foot wide sidewalks will be installed where there is no pathway. Staff feels this
         meets the connectivity requirements and the intent of the General Plan. This is reflected in the Development
         Agreement and will help ensure necessary infrastructure, landscaping, and travel pathways are all installed.

         Commissioner Froerer: I am concerned about the amount of traffic being dumped on that road.

         Ms. Aydelotte: The development agreement covers this. This is slated to be 132 foot right-of-way. 28 park
         strips are included to allow for future widening of the road.
