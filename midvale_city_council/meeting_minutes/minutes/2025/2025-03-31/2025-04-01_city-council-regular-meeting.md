6:00 p.m. – WORKSHOP
    • Legislative Update
Dave Spatafore and Ashley Mirabelli provided the Council with an update on recent
legislative developments and bills that may impact the City. A total of 582 bills were
passed this year, with a significant focus on land use and housing. Notable legislative
changes include provisions allowing closed sessions for discussions on city-owned
property, a prohibition on adding fluoride to the water supply, and new election laws
restricting mail-in voting. Additionally, after 2029, voter verification will require the last four
digits of a social security numbers instead of a signature. Several bills were reviewed and
discussed in detail.

Mayor Dustin Gettel said on March 22nd a 16-yr old Hillcrest High School student was
killed in a car accident. The student, Elijah Bustamante-Martinez, was very active in the
Midvale community. Mayor Gettel said he is planning to honor Elijah at the next council
meeting. The council held a 15 second moment of silence in honor of Elijah.

Chief Morse reported that UPD officers are investigating the fatal car crash that Elijah
was involved in. She said the Hillcrest High School Resource Officer was quickly on
scene and was able to make the initial identification of the student involved. There are
still shock waves rippling through the community and UPD is doing all they can to
support the students.

The Chief shared that UPD hosts a Kids and Cops Art Competition each year. They
have teamed up with Sorenson Legacy Foundation and apply for a grant each year. All
of the schools that UPD serves can participate. The students can submit art in any
medium, the topic this year is anti- violence, anti-drug, anti-bullying, and officers in my
community. The prizes are an iPad for the 6 first place winners, an iphone for the 6
second place winners, and earbuds for the 6 third place winners. The student that wins
also is treated to a pizza party for their entire class. The contest started in March and
submissions are due April,11.

        E.      Salt Lake County Animal Services Report
Carrie Seibert reported on the Salt Lake County Animal Services 2024 Annual Report.
The highlights of 2024 include Animal Control officers responding to over 17 thousand
calls for service, volunteers provided over 7500 hours both in the shelters and in the
community, over 2800 pets were adopted or sent to rescue organizations, and 4200
pets were sterilized by their in-house veterinary clinic. The shelter took in over 6800
animals: mostly dogs and cats with over 2400 adoptions. 95% of the animals at the
shelter left in a positive manner. They are one of the few shelters able to take in a wide
variety of animals. The shelter issued 718 licenses with the majority being an annual
license renewal.

Carrie Seibert reported that the Midvale Urban Wildlife Program trapped 5 racoons and
answered questions and provided general wildlife education to19 residents.

She recapped the Petapalooza, their largest annual adoption event held at Wheeler
Farm. It is a very popular event, and they are considering extending it from one day to
two days this year. She also introduced the Paws Clinic that was launched in October of
2024. This mobile clinic visits every city in Salt Lake County providing free pet
sterilizations, vaccinations, and microchips. Between their launch in October and the
end of 2024 they performed 184 spay and neuter surgeries. Residents can email
paws@saltlakecounty.gov for more information about how to sign up for these services.

Andrew Stoddard, proud Midvale resident of 15 years. Andrew represents Midvale City
at the State Legislature since 2019. Every year it gets a little harder because of the bills

rights for the LGBTQ+ community. He thanked the city council for their service and

Levi Ernest, property manager and owner of a family LLC, said he has developed
property on the corner of Catalpa and Millennial streets, near the McDonalds and the

V.    CITY MANAGER REPORT
      A.    Matt Dahl asked Adam Olsen to discuss the changes to the development
agreement process.

Adam Olsen said his office is going back to the original process of how to present and
process the Development Agreements with Zoning changes. They will now present the
zoning changes to the Council and if the Council deems a development agreement is
appropriate, they will then work with the applicant to put a development agreement
together.

Matt Dahl said they experimented with changing the process, and it was a good effort
but with the feedback from the Council, they will move back to the former process. He
asked Erinn Summers to discuss the grant process.

Mayor Dustin Gettel said that the City’s participation in the Utah Rivers Council Rain
Barrel program has resulted in 26 of the 100 barrels allotted being sold. Information on
how to purchase a barrel is on the City website and social media channels, they are $61
+ tax.

Erinn Summers said in December the City created a reimbursement grant for
businesses using some of the mitigation funding from the State. This was created to
offset safety costs incurred by businesses in a half mile radius of the homeless shelter
due to undesirable activity from the homeless community. There was enough money for
10 grants of $20,000 each. Last month the City had three grants in process. As of today
all three are signed, and one is beginning to submit reimbursements. One more
application is fully executed and three more are in process now. Another went to legal
review and has been approved. They City currently has nine grants. The remaining
grant will go to Landmark Grill, which they have applied for and are moving to the
location Sweet Hazel occupied. Most of the grants are being used for security cameras,
lighting, fencing, and signage.

Council Member Paul Glover said he has spoken with the business owners, and they
expressed gratitude for the assistance.

Matt Dahl said tomorrow night at 6 p.m. there is a meeting being held at city hall for the
Community Council that they are looking to get going again. The purpose of the meeting
is intended to convey information about what a Community Council can be and to get
feedback from people who are interested in being on the Community Council and what
they would like to see the Community Council do.

      B. CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 2025-R-15 AUTHORIZING THE MAYOR TO
          SIGN A TELECOMMUNICATIONS FRANCHISE AGREEMENT WITH ZAYO
          GROUP, LLC.
Aubrey Ruiz said Midvale Municipal Code Chapter 5.52 requires companies providing
telecommunications within Midvale to acquire a franchise agreement with the City prior
to providing services. Franchise agreements require compliance with the City’s
ordinances and further specify a number of requirements including those for insurance
and bonds.

Zayo Group, LLC has submitted complete applications for a telecommunications
franchise. Zayo Group, LLC is an infrastructure provider. It has indicated that it wishes to
install and provide telecommunications services within the City primarily through fiber-
optic lines.

The negotiated franchise agreements with Zayo Group, LLC, are materially the same as
the franchises executed with SQF, Crown Castle and ExteNet. They were reviewed and
approved by the City Council in 2020 and 2018 respectively. Zayo Group, LLC did not
request any changes to the City’s current agreement language. The applicant has been
waiting for an agreement before beginning any work in the City. Staff recommends
approval of the agreement.

FISCAL IMPACT: The City will receive revenue under its franchise agreement for access
to the City’s right-of-way, as well as access to City owned poles within the right-of-way.
