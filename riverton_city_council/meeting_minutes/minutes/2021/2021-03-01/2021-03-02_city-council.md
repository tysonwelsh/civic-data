Presentations

       Suburban Land Reserve Presentation – Benson Whitney, Vice President

Mayor Staggs introduced Benson Whitney who is the Vice President of Suburban Land Reserve
and turned the time over to him for his presentation.

Benson Whitney explained that the Planning Commission approved their site with the exception
of what to do with Sunday Drive. Mr. Whitney explained that the Planning Commission
recommended that Sunday Drive should not go through. He talked about options for their site
plan.

Councilmember Stewart said that he thought the entire development would have all private
roads and expressed concern about having public roads in this development. He said that he
would fully support Sunday Drive not going through if this was a private road.

Mr. Whitney said that the Planning Commission had debated whether the roads should be
public or private. He said that because each lot would have its own tax identification number
similar to other neighborhoods then these roads should remain public.

Councilmember McDougal asked about connectivity and wanted to know if the pool and park
were open to the public.

Mr. Whitney said that the pool and park were only for the residents of this development.

Councilmember McDougal said that he supported Councilmember Stewart because the facilities
in the development were only for the residents and that road should be a private road.

Councilmember Buroker said that she also thought the road should be private.

Mr. Whitney said that they wanted the road to remain a public road but would support the
council’s decision so that the project could move forward.

Councilmember Wells said that he would like Sunday Drive to go through for public safety
reasons.

Mayor Staggs asked Mr. Whitney to clarify the entry points to the community.

Councilmember Stewart said he was concerned that this would set a precedence for future
developments if the development had public roads. He also said he would like the current MDA
issues regarding park space to be addressed.

Mr. Whitney said that he was waiting for the City to come back with a plan for public park space
but this would not be in this development.

Councilmember Stewart expressed frustration that SLR continued to promise public park space
but had not come to the City with a plan.

David Brickey said it is not the responsibility of the City to tell SLR where to put the park space.

Mr. Whitney said that they want to work with the City to create a park space plan.

Mayor Staggs explained that the requirement in the MDA is for continuous park space of four
acres. He said the need for a regional size park is critical so that residents can avail themselves
of this park space. Mayor Staggs said that the MDA is very clear that these plans have to be
initiated by SLR. He appreciates the open and collaborative way that the City and SLR have
been able to work together through this entire process.

Ryan Carter explained that the City Council had provided informal input on what this space
should look like by using broad strokes. He said the City has never been in a position to plan
space that is owned by SLR. The City would like to see something concrete brought back on a
paper.

Mayor asked what the timeline was for this development.

Mr. Benson explained that they were trying to break ground in July and get asphalt by
October/November. He believes that once the issue with Sunday Drive is resolved then staff
will accept the block plan.

Mayor Staggs expressed appreciation for the Suburban Land Reserve presentation and said

Discussion Items

         Community Events 2021 - Sheril Garn, Parks and Public Services Director

Sheril Garn said that her staff had participated in a survey that asked cities about whether they
were having their summer events. The feedback was 58% are moving forward no matter what,
31% are waiting to see what happens with COVID and the remainder have cancelled events.
Ms. Garn presented a slide with the list of community events for Riverton City and explained
each event and what they are doing to follow health guidelines.

Councilmember Wells suggested that staff communicate with the residents about the spring
cleanup and why the City is only providing this service in two different locations instead of four.

Councilmember Stewart is fully supportive and looking forward to these activities.

Councilmember McCay asked for clarification about the location for Friday fun night and the
concerts.

Ms. Garn said they would be held in the center of the park to allow for social distancing.

Councilmember Buroker said she was thrilled to see the activities come back and doesn’t think
the activities will put anyone at risk. She asked where the parade route would be this year.

Ms. Garn said the parade would run on 2700 West this year. She talked about the logistics of
the parade and the rules for throwing candy.

         Improvements In and Around Riverton City Hall and Tied to Redwood Road -
         Mayor Staggs

Mayor Staggs said that he wanted to discuss the development of the parcel of land that is north
of Jim’s Restaurant and currently owned by the homeowners association. He said the
homeowners association is willing to convey this land to the City. He said property is narrow
and could be landscaped with park benches and basic irrigation or there was a suggestion to
make it into a sculpture park.

Councilmember McCay suggested that the City use local scapes which was recently approved
by Herriman for all of their landscaping needs because it uses less water. She thinks that a
sculpture park would be a good idea and is supportive of that idea.

Councilmember Wells said that he thinks local scapes would be a good idea and that sculptures
would be nice if the City has the budget for them.

Councilmember Stewart asked if the agreement for this land space has any restrictions.

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, does not believe it has restrictions but said the storm water areas
would need to be managed.

Councilmember Stewart said he agreed with the other councilmembers but was concerned with
future restrictions getting in the way.

Discussion Item

         Recommendation for Ordinance Regarding Farm Animals - Ryan Carter, City
         Attorney

Councilmember Stewart said that the ordinance was re-written to add language for setbacks,
number of animals, and size of the animals in regards to pygmy goats.

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, said that Riverton City is the pioneer in creating an ordinance with
pygmy goats. This type of ordinance is generally found in rural areas. Mr. Carter said that the
basic concept for this ordinance would be a minimum lot size of 20,000 square feet and then
twice the distance of the minimum setback from the neighboring structure. This
recommendation will go to the Planning Commission and then it will come back to the City
Council.

Councilmember McCay says that residents have actually gotten rid of the pygmy goats and
supports a change to the ordinance.

Councilmember Stewart MOVED that the Riverton City Council proceed forward with the
recommended changes on farm animal ordinance. Councilmember McDougal SECONDED
the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion; there being none, he called for a
roll call vote and the vote was as follows: Councilmembers Buroker-Yes, McCay-Yes,
McDougal-Yes, Stewart-Yes, and Wells-Yes. The motion passed unanimously.

         Riverton Personnel Policies and Procedures Manual - David Brickey, City Manager
         and Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director

David Brickey said that he asked Kevin Hicks to prepare slides addressing this issue.

Mayor Staggs said that he was surprised that we were the only city who offered the 10% salary
benefit and was worried that there was no year requirement to receive this benefit.

Mr. Hicks clarified that the current policy is only available to employees who retire from URS
and retire from Riverton. Employees cannot receive the benefit if they have previously retired
from URS and are now retiring from Riverton.

Councilmember Stewart said that the salary benefit should start at 5 years and the benefit could
change at 7 years and 10 years.

Councilmember Buroker said that there should be a length of years of service requirement but
was neutral as to how many years it should be.

Councilmember McDougal said he thought there should be a tier vesting system.

Councilmember McCay said that the benefit should be 5% of pay at 5 years, 6% of pay at 6
years, 7% of pay at 7 years, and so on until the benefit capped at 10% of pay at 10 years.

All of the councilmembers agreed with Councilmember McCay’s suggestion.

Mr. Hicks asked the council to clarify if the employee had to hit the year mark to get the
percentage.

The council all agreed that the employee must hit the mark to get the percentage.

Mr. Hicks said that he would prepare the paperwork for this change to the manual.
