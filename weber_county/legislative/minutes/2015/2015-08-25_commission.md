    2. PUBLIC HEARING TO CONSIDER/TAKE ACTION ON A PROPOSAL TO AMEND THE FOLLOWING
       SECTIONS OF THE WEBER COUNTY LAND USE CODE: DEFINITIONS (101—1-7), LAND USE PERMIT,
       BUILDING PERMIT, AND CERTIFICATE OF OCCUPANCY (102-4), CONDITIONAL USES (108-4), AND
       SUPPLEMENTARY AND QUALIFYING REGULATIONS (108-7) TO UPDATE AND CLARIFY PROVISIONS
         RELATED TO CONDITIONAL USE PERMITTING AND PROCEDURES

         Charles Ewert, of the County Planning Division, stated that this item is a result of coordinated efforts
         with the Ogden Valley Planning Commission and the Western Weber Planning Commission from
         separate work sessions regarding conditional uses. He noted that the county’s current conditional use
         chapter does not comply with State code and currently supposes that all conditional use permits shall
         be denied unless they meet certain standards. The State’s language state that they shall be approved
         provided that certain standards are met. The amended county ordinance takes language from State
         code, lists new standards and clarifies the county’s process to provide better customer service and to
         clarify due process for conditional use permits.
    3. PUBLIC HEARING TO CONSIDER/TAKE ACTION ON A PROPOSAL TO AMEND THE FOLLOWING SECTION
       OF THE WEBER COUNTY LAND USE CODE: HOME OCCUPATION; SHORT TERM VENDORS;
       TEMPORARY OUTDOOR SALES; FARMERS MARKETS (108-13), TO PROVIDE FOR INSTRUCTIONAL
         ACTIVITIES IN YARD AREA AND ACCESSORY BUILDINGS AND TO UPDATE AND CLARIFY PROVISIONS
         RELATED TO HOME OCCUPATION PERMITTING AND PROCEDURES

         Charles Ewert, of the County Planning Division, stated that this item is to address home occupation
         procedures. An application was submitted by a resident of western Weber County who has a large
         accessory building on the property and would like to run a business there teaching basketball.

         The county’s current code permits home occupations if they are 100% enclosed within the residential
         building on the property. The code amendment will allow certain instructional activities outdoors
         and in accessory buildings. Standards have been added for truck traffic, lighting, noise, etc. Chair
         Gibson expressed support for this amendment. Commissioner Ebert is also supportive and wants to
         ensure there is enforcement of the standards and the county is not overwhelmed in its ability to do so
         and that there is a process to be responsive to complaints.

         Chair Gibson invited public comments on both public hearings and the following is a summary:

         Jan Fullmer, of Eden, asked if a home in a home owners association (HOA) that has covenants and
         which restrict any type of business in the home or outer building take precedence. Mr. Ewert
         responded that the HOA covenants still govern the property and the county is not involved in
         enforcement of HOA regulations. Homeowners are accountable to both the HOA and the county’s
         regulations.
