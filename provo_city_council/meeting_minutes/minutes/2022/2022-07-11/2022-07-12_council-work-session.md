5.      A presentation by the Neighborhood Program Review Committee regarding the
        committee's recommendations for the Neighborhood Program. (22-055) (2:57:12)

Karen Tapahe, Community Relations Coordinator, presented. Ms. Tapahe shared history of the
neighborhood program, which was created over 50 years ago and has experienced many changes
over the years. She also reviewed the challenges of the current iteration of the program,
including low turnout, lack of a code of conduct, meeting locations and meeting access, outsized
turnout or demand of certain neighborhoods, and the size of the program with 34 neighborhoods.
Ms. Tapahe reviewed the process the Neighborhood Program Review Committee has gone
through and she reviewed the proposed changes to the Neighborhood District Program. The
program is founded on community building, consistent engagement, and government
transparency. The new program would have five districts of 6-8 neighborhoods each. Each
neighborhood district would have an executive board. Residents of the district can have a voice
in selecting the board leadership. She highlighted some of the feedback received from
neighborhood chairs, including concerns about favoritism in the appointment process, how to
represent less-engaged neighborhoods, a suggestion to eliminate term limits, the fact that
monthly meetings are too often for most neighborhoods, and that district size was too large.

John Magness, Council Interim Executive Director, introduced the changes to the ordinance.
This proposal originated with a request to implement a code of conduct for neighborhood chairs
that had a lot of negative feedback. As a result, staff have prepared these proposed updates to the
program which would build on social capital in neighborhoods. The proposal would rescind the
existing chapter of the code and replace it with the proposed ordinance. Mr. Magness outlined
the impetus for five districts, which would allow for a more manageable workload for staff who

6.      A presentation from the Administration regarding the possible allocation of
        restricted use funds from the American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA). (22-017) (3:50:57)

Wayne Parker, CAO, presented. Mr. Parker reviewed background information on the American
Rescue Plan Act and how the restricted funds can be used. The Administration has recommended
two main water and wastewater projects to the Council ARPA Committee. They have also
suggested setting aside $10 million for revenue replacement. One of the recommended projects is
to supplement the construction costs for the wastewater treatment plant, which has been
impacted by construction inflation over the last year. To keep the project on-budget, they
eliminated the use of bioreactors in the treatment plant, as this seemed to be the only way to keep
the project on-scope and on-budget. The State Department of Environmental Quality has
encouraged the City to retain the use of bioreactors and last year granted the City a low-interest
loan and a $5 million grant to try and restore the bioreactors. There is a gap of about $3 million
to completely fund the bioreactors portion to restore the project to its original scope.

The second project relates to a culinary water treatment plant in Provo and Provo’s aquifer
storage and recovery (ASR) project. This would fund a 10-million-gallon-per-day water
treatment plant with some shelled in improvements that would allow for future expansion to a
30-million-gallon-per-day plant. There are some related improvements that are part of this
project to facilitate the ASR component, including additional water lines and pump stations. For
those projects the total budget is $72 million and $69 million. They are requesting $19.7 million
toward this project. Public Works recently secured an allocation of Utah County ARPA funds of
$14 million, and they anticipate applying for a State revolving loan of $38.5 million. They also
applied for a federal BRIC grant, which they will hear about in the early fall. This is the initial
proposal they shared with the Council’s ARPA Committee. Mr. Parker anticipated talking about
ARPA funds at upcoming meetings as well, before doing an appropriation in the future.

Councilor David Shipley asked if they had projections for revenues or cost-savings to the City
that would result from having in-house water treatment capabilities that would make up for the
servicing on that debt payment or whether this project would result in new debt.
