Proposal 
 
The applicant is requesting a Low Impact permit for a 131-ft. x 70-ft. (~9,200 sq. ft.) “Recreation 
and athletic facilities, private use.”  The property owners have stated; “We are building 
pickleball courts for our personal use. With it being such a social activity, we wanted the ability

Analysis and Findings 
 
Per the Snyderville Basin Development Code, “Recreation and athletic facilities, private use” are 
reviewed under a Low Impact Permit within the Rural Residential (RR) Zone.  
 
The Snyderville Basin Development Code defines “Recreation and athletic facilities, private use” 
as; A recreation facility operated on private property and not open to the public, including 
recreation facilities owned by a homeowners' or property owners' association for private use by 
members. 
 
Section 10-3-4(E) of the Snyderville Basin Development Code requires that any use qualifying 
for a Low Impact Permit must meet the following criteria: 
 
Criterion 1: The use does not significantly increase vehicular traffic, unless the increases are 
consistent with previously approved plans for which appropriate mitigation has been 
contemplated and which has been implemented in an appropriate manner to accommodate 
the proposed amendment.  
 
Analysis: The owners have stated that the facility would be for their personal use with 
the ability to have guests join them to play at the same time and then switch groups. 
The proposed facility would be the primary use on the property. The owners and their 
guests would be travelling to and from the property.  
 
According to the Institute of Transportation Engineers (ITE) Trip Generation Manual the 
P.M. peak for tennis courts is 4.21 trips per court and the P.M. peak for a single-family 
home is 0.99 trips per single-family detached house. The average rate for daily trips is 
30.32 for tennis courts and 9.57 for single-family homes. The Trip Generation Manual 
does not differentiate between private and public courts.  
 
Parcel SL-D-241 is located in the Mixed Use area of the Snyderville Basin General Plan 
Silver Creek Neighborhood Future Land Use Map. There are other non-residential uses 
on Beehive Court and higher levels of traffic are anticipated with Mixed Uses. Staff 
requests a discussion with the Planning Commission on whether it is reasonable to 
recommend a condition of approval regarding the maximum number of vehicles. 
REQUEST DISCUSSION

contemplated and which has been implemented in an appropriate manner to accommodate

Findings of Fact: 
 
1. Beehive Court LLC (Seth and Jennifer Lansky) are the owners of SL-D-241 (2.43 acres), 
located at 1201 Beehive Dr., Silver Creek, Summit County, UT 84098. 
2. Parcel SL-D-241 is located in the Rural Residential (RR) Zone.  
3. Parcel SL-D-241 is currently undeveloped. 
4. Parcel SL-D-241 is of rectangular shape and slopes down South to North at ~10% slope. 
Parcel SL-D-241 is bordered by single-family residential uses with one vacant lot to the 
east.  
5. The applicant is requesting a Low Impact Permit for a ~9,200 sq. ft. “Recreation and 
athletic facilities, private use” facility on Parcel SL-D-241. 
6. The proposal includes but is not limited to; three pickle ball courts (~7,200 sq. ft.), a golf 
simulator area (~280 sq. ft.), lobby (~850 sq. ft.), one bathroom with a shower, one half-
bath, with utility and storage areas.  
7. The property owners have stated; “We are building pickleball courts for our personal 
use. With it being such a social activity, we wanted the ability to have couples join us and 
all be able to play at the same time and then switch groups. There is extremely limited 
indoor court time during the winter which is the primary reason for building the courts 
versus driving down to SLC multiple times per week. Our son is competitive and plays 
singles some of the time, which was another use for the extra courts. We will be the only 
ones using the courts and by NO means will be public in any way. We have planned for 2 
bathrooms; as sometimes between games everyone is in need... One bathroom will have 
a shower should that convenience be needed. The social area is just that, a place off the 
courts to enjoy time together. Our son is also a competitive golfer and we thought to 
build space for a future golf simulator which the adults may enjoy also.”  
8. The applicant is proposing 12 parking stalls for the project.  
9. Snyderville Basin Development Code Section 10-4-9(A) states; “Every effort shall be 
made to minimize the amount of impervious surface that is created for parking 
purposes.”  
10. The proposed amount of parking accommodates four players per court with each player 
driving their private automobile to the site.  
11. The proposed Building is 131 feet by 70 feet, and approximately 25 feet in height.  
12. The exterior materials proposed are metal wall panels and exposed concrete.  
13. The façade is linear and as a result, the applicant is implementing a prominent shift in 
mass via a series of shifts which result in a cumulative offset of at least 15 feet as 
required by the Snyderville Basin Development Code Section 10-4-19(K.2).  
14. Snyderville Basin Development Code Section 10-4-19 (Architectural Regulations for All 
Structures) states; These regulations are intended to guide the architectural character of 
all buildings. They are intended to ensure unity in "function", the relationship of the 
spaces that meet specific needs, "structure", including the method used to enclose the 
space, and "beauty" or the artistic character. They are intended to allow and promote 
creativity in design within the context of a mountain character that is desired in the 
Snyderville Basin. Variety, interest and elegance in architectural designs shall be 
incorporated into all designs. All designs shall also be compatible with the desired 
mountain design character as indicated in these standards and described in the general 
plan.
