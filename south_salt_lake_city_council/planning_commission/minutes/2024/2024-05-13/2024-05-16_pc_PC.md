1.      PUBLIC MEETING – An Application to Approve a Preliminary Subdivision Plat to
        Consolidate Seven Parcels into One Lot Located at 2264 South 900 West. The
        Applicant is J-U-B Engineers.

ACTION ITEM

Applicant:     J-U-B Engineers, Represented by Jerron Atkin
Address:       2264 South 900 West

Ms. Ungricht presented the Staff Report and explained that the application is for the UTA Jordan
River Rail Service Center. It is a plat application and the property is located at 2264 South 900
West. She shared an overview of the parcels and reported that the applicant wants to consolidate
seven parcels into one lot. The property is located in the Flex land use district. After consolidation,
the lot would total 32.36 acres. The building to the north is an existing UTA facility and the
building to the south is the old Gumball Depot building. UTA has purchased that building and the
property to the south and wants to consolidate these parcels to expand its rail service operations.

Images of the existing conditions were shared. Ms. Ungricht reported that this illustrates the
existing rail service. There is a warehouse where repairs are done. An image of the old Gumball
Depot building was reviewed. She explained that there will be some remodeling and it will be
expanded for office use as well as parking and an expansion of the rail facility. 2300 South will
remain and it will not be vacated as part of this application. That street will still be city-maintained.

Ms. Ungricht clarified that there is a street in the middle of the subject property. The request
currently before the Planning Commission will make some of the parcels conforming, because
right now, those are landlocked and there is no access to a public road. Consolidation will provide
access to a public road. The proposed Site Plan was reviewed. Ms. Ungricht stated that there is
adequate parking because the plan is to put in some parking and pave around the old Gumball
Depot building. All of the plat standards are met with the application. She noted that there are
some transmission easements and high-voltage lines. Those will not be buried for safety reasons.

The Proposed Plat was shared. Ms. Ungricht reported that there are existing easements that need
to remain in place for different utilities and access. The application meets several General Plan
considerations: Land Use and Neighborhoods Goal #1 (continue to welcome new residents and
businesses into South Salt Lake) and Community Values Goal #3 (take advantage of opportunities
relating to the City’s location at the center of the regional transportation, transit, trails, open space,
library, recreation, and school system to serve residents, businesses, and visitors).

This consolidation will make it possible for UTA to expand its service. As a result, Staff
recommends that the Planning Commission approve the Preliminary Subdivision Plat to
consolidate seven parcels into one lot, located at 2264 South 900 West, based upon the information
submitted by the applicant, the General Plan considerations, and ordinance analysis. Staff proposes
several Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law, and Conditions of Approval for the subdivision in
the Staff Report and enumerated on the record. The Commission can approve, deny, or continue.

Commissioner Bellina asked to further review the Preliminary Plat. If the street will remain open,
she assumed the access to the Jordan River Trail will also remain open. She wondered whether the
applicant could improve the trail access there as part of this approval. Mr. Weidenhamer noted that
there have been discussions about this at a Staff level. It was confirmed that the bridge at the top
left corner of the document is adequate to continue the current level of service. Staff is comfortable
with the application that has been presented to the Commission.

Commissioner Pechmann explained that there are access issues with the bridge. Mr. Weidenhamer
clarified that the City cannot force a connection. The current level of service that exists will be

maintained with this application. Commissioner Pechmann wanted to know how it would be
possible to connect to that trail. Mr. Weidenhamer explained that there would need to be a Streets
Master Plan, Trails Master Plan, or something similar. Lacking those technical documents, the
City does not have the tools through the subdivision process to request that of the applicant.

Commissioner Pechmann understands that there are limitations to what can be done at this point.
However, if the Master Plan includes the connection of the Jordan River to Parleys Trail, that
connection will be lost through this process. Commissioner Bellina asked if language can be
clearly stated in the Parks Master Plan and the application table. Mr. Weidenhamer reported that
the pending application would trump that. City Attorney, Brianne Brass, explained that the
application submitted has vested rights. Currently, all of the zoning requirements for the
subdivision are met. Commissioner Pechmann had a question for the applicant. He wanted to know
if the plan is to fence the entire property and remove the existing access point.

Chair Carter referenced the comment about the current level of service. He wondered whether that
included the path at the end of the roundabout to access the trail. Mr. Weidenhamer reported that
through this subdivision, the City cannot force a connection across the river. Discussions were had
about the location of the access point. Staff pointed out the location for the benefit of those present.
Commissioner Pechmann noted that if it was fenced off, bicyclists would need to exit the trail
system further down and bicycle along the street for a certain distance. He pointed out that the
street does not have the best infrastructure, so connections are essential.

Chair Carter reiterated his question about the level of service. He wanted to know if that included
the dirt path. Mr. Weidenhamer denied this and explained that it is everything else, because the
dirt path would not be on public property, but private. There were no further questions for Staff.

The applicant representative, Jerron Atkin, explained that he is with J-U-B Engineers. Nate Henrie
was also present and stated that he is with FFKR Architects. Mr. Atkin believed the issue here
relates to property rights. Currently, UTA owns the property. Historically, he believed it was a
park at one time, which is the reason for the configuration. The trail does not continue across the
property. Commissioner Pechmann noted that if there is a fenced area, then there is a gap, and then
there is the other fenced area. He wondered whether UTA would consider maintaining access to
that public street from the Jordan River Parkway. Mr. Atkins explained that regardless of whether
it is possible to get through now, UTA has represented that there is an intention to block that with
a gate. Commissioner Pechmann stated that there is a gate at the end of a street and then pedestrian
access. Mr. Atkins explained that this does not necessarily mean there is legal access.

Chair Carter opened the public hearing. There were no comments. The public hearing was closed.

Discussions were had about the application. Chair Carter pointed out that if the access is not legal
to use currently, then nothing will be changed with the application. Commissioner Pechmann
explained that currently there is a dirt path and then a pedestrian gate in that area. Chair Carter
appreciated that but clarified that it is private property. That will not change with the application.

Ms. Ungricht reported that a new pedestrian bridge opened last year that is just north of this project.
It connects over the Jordan River. There is an easement and access to that connection. She pointed
