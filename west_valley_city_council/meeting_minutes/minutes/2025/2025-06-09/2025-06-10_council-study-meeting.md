REVIEW AGENDAS FOR REGULAR CITY COUNCIL, REDEVELOPMENT
AGENCY, HOUSING AUTHORITY, AND MUNICIPAL BUILDING
AUTHORITY MEETINGs OF JUNE 10, 2025
Alex Kidd, Community and Culture Department, addressed the Mayor and City Council
regarding a grant opportunity that the Healthy West Valley resident committee wished to
accept. The grant, titled “Overdose Data to Action” (OD2A), originated from federal
funding that was passed through the state to counties, with $20,000 allocated to West
Valley City through Salt Lake County. Alex explained that last year, the Salt Lake County
Health Department, in partnership with the Healthy West Valley Committee, conducted a
community resource assessment focused on opioid awareness. The results placed the
community at stage 3 out of 9, indicating a general but vague awareness of opioid misuse
issues, with limited understanding of how to respond or access support. In response, the
committee proposed using the grant funds to hire a short-term summer intern to help carry
out the work. They also planned to develop educational materials, including a bilingual
toolkit in English and Spanish containing information on opioid misuse and local resources.
In addition, they aimed to produce short-form videos for platforms like Instagram to
reinforce the same messages. The proposal also included organizing in-person educational
classes during the summer and potentially into the fall, with curriculum designed for
ongoing use. Further, the funds would be used to provide training for city staff and first
responders on harm reduction and community support strategies. Lastly, a portion of the
grant would support the expansion of the “Partners in the Park” program by adding a sixth
event in collaboration with South Salt Lake. This event would focus on connecting
residents with harm reduction resources and education.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang, members of the Council had no further questions or concerns
regarding items listed on the Agenda for the Regular City Council, Redevelopment
Agency, Housing Authority, and Municipal Building Authority Meetings scheduled later
this night.

     Steve Pastorik, CED Director, discussed proposed Ordinance 25-15 that would
     amend the West Valley City General Plan to Update the City's Major Street Plan.

     Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
     information as follows:

            The Major Street Plan defines street alignments and right-of-way widths for
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JUNE 10, 2025

             existing and proposed major streets within the City and is referenced several
             times in the City’s Zoning Ordinance. The City first adopted a Major Street
             Plan in August of 1980 as Ordinance 80-05. Since that time there have been
             numerous revisions made to this Plan as the City has grown and changed.

             Staff is proposing a comprehensive update to the City’s Major Street Plan
             based on travel demand modeling for the year 2050 performed by the
             transportation consulting firm Parametrix. This effort was undertaken for
             the reasons listed below:
                 • Staff wanted to verify that the street right-of-way widths
                     recommended in the Major Street Plan were still supported by the
                     latest travel demand modeling.
                 • Staff is working on several updates to the General Plan to comply
                     with State code requirements that must be met by the end of 2025.
                     While making these State mandated changes like adopting station
                     area plans and a water element, staff wanted the rest of the General
                     Plan, which was last updated in 2015, to be refreshed.
                 • Staff is also preparing an update to the City’s transportation impact
                     fees. This effort also requires travel demand modeling, but for a
                     shorter planning horizon of 10 years to comply with State code. Staff
                     retained Parametrix under one contract to run the travel demand
                     model for the year 2034 (the project started last year) for the impact
                     fees and for the year 2050 for the Major Street Plan.

     Mayor Lang inquired about the availability of the County’s Corridor Preservation

ACTION: ORDINANCE 25-16, AMEND SUBSECTION 7-2-112(3) OF THE
WEST VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO PERMIT A MOBILE
FINANCIAL INSTITUTION AS A TEMPORARY USE
Jody Knapp, CD, discussed proposed Ordinance 25-16 that would amend
Subsection 7-2-112(3) of the West Valley City Municipal Code to Permit a Mobile
Financial Institution as a Temporary Use

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
information as follows:

   The applicant submits this request based on the following considerations:

       1. Continuity of Financial Services: When a financial institution
          undergoes remodeling or reconstruction, community members who rely
          on in-person banking services may experience significant disruption.
          Allowing a financial mobile branch ensures uninterrupted access to
          essential banking services, such as deposits, withdrawals, loans, and
          financial counseling.
       2. Alignment with Existing Temporary Land Use Permits: The
          ordinance already permits mobile medical units and temporary
          structures for construction and sales offices. Financial mobile branches
          serve a similar public benefit by maintaining community access to
          critical financial resources during construction.
       3. Economic and Community Stability: Access to financial services
          plays a vital role in maintaining economic stability for individuals and
          small businesses. Allowing temporary mobile branches helps customers
          avoid financial hardship by ensuring they can continue managing their
          banking needs locally.
       4. Regulated and Temporary Use: The proposed amendment maintains
          reasonable land-use regulations by requiring financial mobile branch
          permits to be renewed every six months while construction is ongoing,
      MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JUNE 10, 2025
                           -6-


                  ensuring they remain temporary and are not used indefinitely.

       Mayor Lang asked whether mobile banking services would be subject to the same
       proximity restrictions as food trucks, specifically if they would need to stay a
       certain distance away from brick-and-mortar banks. Jody clarified that no such
       restriction was being proposed. Mayor Lang asked if there were any limitations for
       location. Jody clarified that if a financial institution was constructing a permanent
       branch with an active building permit, a mobile unit could remain on-site for up to
       six months. Otherwise, mobile banking services would need to operate in
       partnership with another business, rather than parking randomly. Under these
       conditions, they would be allowed at a given location no more than twice per week,
       with a maximum of eight hours per day.

       The City Council will consider Ordinances 25-16 at the Regular Council Meeting
       scheduled June 24, 2025 at 6:30 P.M.

RESOLUTION 25-86: APPROVE A CERTIFICATION OF ELIGIBLE
EMPLOYEES TO THE DEPARTMENT OF PUBLIC SAFETY
Paula Melgar, HR Director, presented proposed resolution 25-86 that would approve a
Certification of Eligible Employees to the Department of Public Safety.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The Local Public Safety and Firefighter Surviving Spouse Trust Fund provides
       financial support to agencies by reimbursing the cost of continued health coverage
       for the surviving spouses and children of Public Safety Officers and Firefighters
       who die in the line of duty.

       For the upcoming fiscal year, the contribution rate is $100 per eligible employee.

       Participation in the Trust Fund is mandatory for all agencies employing one or
       more Public Safety Officers or Firefighters eligible for Utah Retirement System
       service credit under Title 49 of the Utah State Retirement and Insurance Benefits
       Act, unless exempt under Utah Code Section 53-17-201(3).

       Effective May 7, 2025, two statutory changes were enacted by the Utah
       Legislature.

          •   Vision and dental premiums are now eligible for reimbursement by the
              fund.
      MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JUNE 10, 2025

           •   Reimbursement for health, dental, and vision insurance now begins on the
               date of the line-of-duty death, eliminating the previous requirement that
               the employing agency cover the first 12 months of premiums.

       These changes enhance the scope of support provided to the families of fallen
       public safety employees and reinforce the state’s commitment to those who serve
       and sacrifice for their communities.
