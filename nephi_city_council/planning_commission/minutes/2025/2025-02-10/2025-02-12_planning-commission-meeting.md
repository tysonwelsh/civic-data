                  TRAINING:
                  Introduction to Utah Code Section 52-4-101
                     • Public bodies must conduct the public’s business openly with full
                        transparency. OPMA is the abbreviation.
                     Who is Subject to the Act
                     •   Administrative, advisory, or legislative body that is created by the
                         Utah Constitution, statute, rule, ordinance, or resolution.
                     •   It consists of two or more people
                     •   Expends, disburses, or is supported by the tax revenue
                     •   Is vested with the authority to make decision for the public’s business.

                     What is a public Meeting?
                         •   Convening of a public body when a quorum is present for the
                             purpose of discussing, receiving public comment, or acting on a
                             matter over the jurisdiction or advisory power.
                         •   Conducting business via email or other electronic means risking
                             violating the act.
                     What is not a public meeting?
                         •   A chance or social gathering.
                         •   Convening when no public funds are appropriated, and no formal
                             action taken.
                         •   ALWAYS err on the safe side.
                     Emergency Meetings
                         •   Notice requirements may be discarded only if the public body must
                             hold a meeting to consider something urgent.
                         •   Public Body must give the best notice practicable of the time and
                             place of the meeting
                         •   Emergency meetings may not be held unless an attempt has been
                             made to notify all members of the body.
                     What does the act require?
                         •   Notice to the public at least 24 hours, agenda must include date,
                             time and place of the meeting.
                         •   Annual notice of regularly scheduled meeting posted with date,
                             time, and place of meetings for the year.
                     Meeting Minutes
                         •   Date, time, and place.
                         •   Names of members present and absent.
                         •   Substance of all matters discussed.
                         •   Record of each vote taken.
                         •   Name of person who testifies/ commented.
                         •   Anything else that a member requests be entered into the minutes.
                     Recordings
                         •   Be complete and unedited
                         •   Be properly labeled with date, time, and place of the meeting.
                         •   Retained for 3 years under GRAMA.

                     Why can a meeting be closed?
                         •   All reasons for a closed meeting:
                                 o Discussion of character, competence, physical or mental
                                     health of an individual
                                 o Collective bargaining
                                 o Pending or imminent litigation
                                 o Purchase of real property
                                 o Security systems
                                 o Criminal misconduct investigations
                                 o Certain procurement discussions.

   4. Ordinance 02-18-2025-A - Public Improvements
            a. Staff Presentation
                Mr. Seth Atkinson presented that the city was recently involved with a
               decision with the ombudsman and a developer. This came about because of
               what Nephi City requires for utilities, and specifically the electric department.
               Certain requirements are required for developments, and one of those is that a
               loop or create redundance within the subdivision. The point of that was to
               prevent power disruption for long periods of time and considered it a level of
               service for residents. Capacity was also a requirement, because it was
               unknown the size of the homes that would be entering that property area and
               to prepare for that. However, a developer pushed back on that.
                When the decision came back it was you do have the right do set your own
               service level for the city, however your code is ambiguous and errs on the side
               of the developer. The ombudsman focused mainly on the looping issue, so the
               purpose of this ordinance is to clarify the code for electric.
                One thing we do want to cover is that the ombudsman cited the case that
               went to the U.S. Supreme Court known as Nolan and Dolan cases. These
               cases are known and discuss on how the government can require a “taking”.
               Essentially it clarifies what can be done when there is a legitimate reason to
               the government interest, and it has be roughly proportionate to the
               development. For example, you can’t require a sidewalk beyond 600 yards
               beyond the development, but you can require it in front of a development. So,
               tonight we want to clarify that level of service, making clear that the power
               superintendent or city engineer determines that level of service. We shall also
               provide sufficient source, transmission and distribution capacity and sizing for
               the subdivision and redundance looping.
