    2.2. Discussion relating to high-speed internet service in Herriman - Chase Andrizzi, City
         Attorney
City Attorney Andrizzi addressed the importance of providing high-speed internet to businesses and residents.
He said the high-speed option needed to be one-gig speed through fiber utility and not cable. He acknowledged
the issue was how to get fiber into Herriman, and noted an immediate benefit of providing high-speed internet
service was increased speeds for residents and business. He said it was especially important now because of
the effects of COVID on adjusted schedules. He voiced the opinion that people were increasingly home-
bound because of COVID, and this change would be permanent for many residents.

He referred to previous discussions on small cell wireless facilities being added in the future, and explained
high-speed internet was important because technology would be connected to having a fiber utility in the City.
He said obtaining the infrastructure now was crucial to be able to adapt to changes, which included the addition
of small cell wireless facilities in the future.

Attorney Andrizzi presented a map of existing fiber and potential conduits in Herriman City. He explained
the blue lines were third party fiber lines that were usually connected to newer developments. The purple lines
represented Advanced Traffic Management System (ATMS) lines, which were installed conduits that
potentially gave the City the option to run fiber through them. He mentioned the ATMS and fiber excluded
the bulk of the City’s residents and businesses as they did not have enough reach to impact residents in the
central and south-western areas of Herriman.

Attorney Andrizzi explained the City only had two cable-based internet services which did not run at the
previously discussed high speeds. He voiced the opinion of only having two services was not good for
consumers, and it was beneficial to bring in more provider options. He explained the current services did not
September 9, 2020 – Special City Council Minutes                               Page 6 of 18
do joint ventures with the City because they did not have incentive to expand out of the newer developments
and into the unreached areas. Attorney Andrizzi reviewed a map that depicted the current locations with higher
speed internet. He stated the locations were spotty and did not offer a secure foothold to branch out given
the current infrastructure.

Attorney Andrizzi provided the option of partnering with Utopia. He credited staff for meeting with Utopia
to look at options for bringing fiber utility to the City. He explained it would take Utopia two to three years
to build the necessary infrastructure to provide high-speed internet services to every home in Herriman. He
stated Utopia would front the costs with a rough estimate of 30 Million, and the City would act as a backstop
on the loan. Attorney Andrizzi informed the Council that Herriman would not have to pay any costs if 35-
percent or more of the residents signed up for the service. However, if less than 35-percent of residents
participated, the City was responsible for the bill.

Councilmember Shields asked what the average monthly cost would be for a resident to sign up for Utopia.
Attorney Andrizzi responded the base cost for the use of Utopia would be $40 per month. There would then
be an additional fee for Utopia to partner with 10 to 15 different internet service providers. This would be
beneficial to residents because it would provide them more choices. Attorney Andrizzi noted that the prices
were comparable to Xfinity and Comcast. He asserted this option was beneficial because Utopia would cover
the costs to build out the infrastructure for fiber. He communicated the City would not pay any costs up front,
and would only be backing the loans. He specified these payments would come from sales tax, franchise fees,
and other funds.

City Attorney Andrizzi outlined the requirements for partnering with Utopia. The City would only have to pay
if less than 35-percent of residents signed up for the program. He stated this cost roughly 165K per month
assuming the building costs were 30 Million. Mayor Watts requested clarification on the 35-percent
participation minimum and asked whether the City would be on the hook for proportional amounts under 35-
percent participation, or if the City would be responsible for all 30 Million if the 35-percent participation was
not reached. Attorney Andrizzi clarified if resident participation remained under 35-percent, the City would
be responsible for the dollar amount associated with the remaining percentage before hitting the threshold.
There was a brief discussion as the Councilmembers broke the concept down by using hypothetical
percentages.

Attorney Andrizzi reminded them they would not collect any fees from Utopia, and any revenue generated
would be maintained by Utopia. He mentioned this had the possibility to change during the negotiation phase.
He then addressed the question posed by Councilmember Shields, and said the benefit was that Utopia only
installed the groundwork fiber that any internet service could use. He stated it gave Herriman residents many
options for a provider, and it would cost approximately $80 per month for one-gig internet speeds. He restated
this was comparable to services like Comcast.

City Attorney Andrizzi presented the second option for obtaining high-speed internet in Herriman was to have
it be government owned. He explained this option would entail Herriman City owning and providing the
utility and groundwork fiber. He stated they would come in as the utility provider and contract to install, own,
September 9, 2020 – Special City Council Minutes                                     Page 7 of 18
and manage the fiber. He further stated by owning the utility they may face legal hurdles. He said they may
have to create an enterprise fund, manage the utility, and bring on more employees, and other steps to properly
manage the fiber utility. City Attorney Andrizzi noted this was beneficial in a way similar to the private
partnership model as they would be able to provide many internet service provider options off the groundwork
fiber. He said it would also allow an easy transition to install small cell towers in the future.

City Attorney Andrizzi acknowledged the concern the City would have to pay. He said there were options as
to how to fund this model, such as entering an interlocal agreement or creating a district or special assessment
area. He stated these things required further examination. He further stated the City would maintain revenue.
He briefly discussed this in terms of the participation rate and the 30 Million construction cost. City Attorney
Andrizzi said because the City would be earning revenue, they would be able to make any payment on the
bond. He also said they would be able to improve and maintain the necessary infrastructure and technology
in that scenario.

Councilmember Ohrn expressed significant concern with the City paying for and managing the groundwork
fiber. She voiced the opinion she fundamentally disagreed with the government using taxes to compete against
the private market. She disagreed with the idea the City would set up an enterprise to do so using taxpayer
funds. City Attorney Andrizzi acknowledged her concern, and offered the counterweight that there was a
demand for this type of utility. He stated there was a need in Herriman for the utility, and in theory the
government would provide a service that the private market was not willing to do at a reasonable price. He
also stated if a reasonably priced option could be found, it would be a great option. He noted the conversation
was to determine what the available options were, and which would be best for Herriman in financing the
infrastructure. Councilmember Shields voiced the opinion the private enterprises had not provided the service

were not. He further stated the private enterprises had only offered to tackle the fiber issue if the City were to
backstop it. He expressed the sentiment that taxpayer money was already being used by private enterprises to
guarantee profit from the project. He said he did not feel the City would be in competition with private
enterprises because there was no one with whom to compete. Councilmember Ohrn responded it was unclear
to her who provided what service. She noted the Council was shown pictures that high-speed internet was
being provided to some places and not others. She inquired about why the infrastructure could not be
expanded. She also asked why the private businesses did not want to expand. She speculated it was not cost
effective for a private business and asked what would make it so for the City.

important for them to find out what they did and did not know. He reviewed the positives and negatives of
entering the partnership with Utopia. He said it would be two to three years before every home and business
in Herriman received fiber. He also said the city would backstop and be responsible for the loan and reiterated
