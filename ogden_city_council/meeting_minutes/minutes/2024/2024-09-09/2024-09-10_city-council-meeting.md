Proposed Ordinance 2024-27 amending the Ogden Municipal Code to
revise advisory board regulations and requirements; and renaming the
commission to the Ogden Community Engagement and Opportunity
Commission; AND
Proposed Joint Resolution 2024-3 adopting the Ogden Community
Engagement Charter
         A memo from the Mayor’s Office regarding proposed amendments to the Diversity Commission came before the Council
for consideration. The memo stated the proposed ordinance amends advisory board regulations and requirements to 1) remove the
requirement for diversity relating to personal identity characteristics; and 2) to limit the time a board member may serve beyond
the date of the expired term. The proposed ordinance also amends the diversity commission ordinance. The changes will bring
these ordinances into alignment with the requirements of House Bill (H.B.) 261, Equal Opportunities Initiative, which was passed
in the 2024 general legislative session. The Council will also consider adopting a proposed joint resolution to rename the Diversity
Charter the Community Engagement Charter and to reaffirm the values, mission, and vision of the original charter.
          Community Engagement Administrator Lartigue summarized the memo and expounded on the requirements of H.B. 261.
To comply with the requirements of H.B. 261, the City Attorney and the Administration are recommending changes to Ogden
Municipal Code § 3-2-1, et. seq. applying to all board and commissions, and §3-18. et. seq. relating to the Diversity Commission
as follows:
               • OCM §3-2-1, et. seq. (Applies to All Boards and Commissions)
                         o Removes the requirement for diversity relating to personal identity characteristics.
                         o Limits service of a board member serving in a term that has expired to not more than six months
                              beyond the date of the expired term.
                         o Makes various grammatical, structural, and other technical corrections.
               • OCM §3-18-1, et seq. (Ogden Diversity Commission):
                         o Renames the Diversity Commission the Ogden Community Engagement and Opportunity
                              Commission.
                         o Acknowledges a change in name of the Office of Diversity Affairs to the office of Community
                              Engagement.
                         o Restates the purpose of the ordinance as promoting opportunity in the community.
                         o Modifies the membership qualification requirements to remove expertise, advocacy experience,
                              community involvement, student status, profession, education, and references to personal identity
                              characteristics.
                         o Aligns the appointment process with other city committees. (Appointment is done by the Mayor with
                              advice and consent of the City Council).
                         o Adds that the Commission shall not engage in or promote prohibited discriminatory practices based on
                              personal identity characteristics.
                         o Makes various grammatical, structural, and other technical corrections.
          Ms. Lartigue concluded the proposed joint resolution renames the Diversity Charter the Ogden Community Engagement
Charter and reaffirms the values, mission and vision of the original charter.
          Council member Choberka thanked Ms. Lartigue for working through this process and keeping the Council informed of
the purpose of the proposed amendments. Chair Richey echoed Council member Choberka’s gratitude and clarified that asked
from renaming the Charter, the language in the Charter is not changing and the purpose of the Charter is to celebrate and embrace
the uniqueness of Ogden City.
          Council member Choberka then asked what will happen to the Commission members whose terms of service no longer
align with the membership limits in the new ordinance. Ms. Lartigue stated that two members have served the maximum number
of terms allowed by the ordinance, but they agreed to remain on the Board until their replacement could be appointed. One of
those appointments was made earlier in tonight’s meeting, and the other has resigned their position.
          Vice Chair White stated that she was initially very bothered by H.B. 261, but as she has been able to discuss the matter in
greater detail with her fellow Council members and City Administration, she recognizes that the Commission is still very
important and that their role may even be expanded or broadened as a result of the changes that are being considered tonight. She
appreciates the opportunity to be more diverse and inclusive.
          Council member Hyer stated that he was one of the two Council members that worked with a citizens committee to create
the initial Diversity Charter; it was a very enlightening process, the intent of which was to highlight each individual’s value. He
stated he believes those values are still intact. He stated that he is of the opinion that there are some negative aspects of
implementing diversity, equity, and inclusion initiatives, which is why he assumes the State Legislature took the matter under
advisement and ultimately adopted H.B. 261. He believes the changes that are proposed tonight are positive and will allow for
everyone interested in diversity to move forward. Ms. Lartigue agreed; the Diversity Commission truly worked for the greater
good of the community and did not get involved in divisive matters.
          Chair Richey thanked Ms. Lartigue for her leadership throughout this process; he read the following statement from the
Charter document, noting he is heartened by the commitment to continue to address this issue, even when setbacks occur: “In
signing this document, community elders, current leaders and hopeful young people will commit to come together as many times
as it takes, especially if setbacks occur. In this endeavor, we intend a living framework to help us build an ever more inclusive,
engaged community where each new generation builds on lessons from our past.” He then invited Mayor Nadolski to make
comments.
          Mayor Nadolski also thanked Ms. Lartigue for her leadership. He then offered his comments on the state of the nation in
terms of the things that are creating a divide. There are many ill intentions at the present time, especially in partisan politics, and
what gets lost in that is people. There is a reason local political are non-partisan: local leaders are tasked with taking care of local
residents regardless of their personal characteristics. There is a lot of talk about unity, but unity does not necessarily mean that
everyone must agree. It is acceptable to disagree, but unity means seeking to understand the other side of an issue. He believes that
most people are good and rooted in love and service. There are families and children ‘falling through the cracks’ while leaders are
fighting about politics. He wants to be different and to act based upon human values rather than political values. Anyone that does
not agree with the Ogden Community Engagement Charter should not be ‘at the table’ for the discussion because they are making
the cracks wider. He encouraged everyone to find a way to serve in a meaningful way and with love.

Public Comments
         Tea Nutonga expressed her gratitude to the Council members and the Mayor for their efforts to bring a new lens and
perspective to the Lester Park amenity in the City; she worked at the branch of the library near Lester Park for nearly four years
and the park was a great concern to the function of the library for many reasons. She stated that she appreciated Council member
Graf’s renewed focus on the park and looks forward to further discussion of improving the park. She also noted her goal is to
strengthen civic engagement in Ogden, but she is very new to this area. She believes the majority of Ogden residents are
uniformed and do not know how to get involved and participate in the City’s business and she asked for guidance and ideas on
how an ordinary citizen can be better engaged. She thanked the Council’s staff, especially the individual who provides
information on social media, for the videos they make to inform residents. She thanked Mayor Nadolski for his comments about
the need for everyone to come together for the betterment of the community.
         Emily Beeli stated tonight’s work session was very informative. Additionally, she discovered a link on the City Council
page of the City’s website entitled ‘citizen information packet’. She clicked on the link and found a great deal of information. She
believes the link should be on the homepage of ogdencity.com because it is fantastic. She then discussed upcoming community
events, including a townhall event for Senator Johnson, who represents much of Ogden City, at the Huntsville Library on
September 12, and a meeting with local and State school board members at the Roy Library on September 18. These are
opportunities for residents to interact with their elected officials on issues that are important to them. She would like something
similar in Ogden soon so that residents can meet with City leaders about issues, such as the City’s budget and the benefits
residents can expect following the recent property tax increase.
         Marcus Carr agreed with Mayor Nadolski about the divisive issues currently plaguing the State and the nation; however,
based upon statistics and real-life events, it is necessary to combat political warfare and be better with one another. It is necessary
to improve communication and agree to do what is right for all residents. Political divides are hurting children and all citizens, and
he wants to work to unite everyone, even though it will never be possible to get everyone to agree about everything.

Council member Comments
          Chair Richey thanked Ms. Nutonga for her comments and for being engaged. Council member Choberka also thanks Ms.
Nutonga and extended an invitation to meet with her to discuss her ideas for improving community engagement. Chair Richey
stated that Council meetings are very formal and do not present the best opportunity for open dialogue and engagement; any
resident is welcome to reach out to any elected official or other community representatives at any time to discuss any issue they
are concerned about. Council member Hyer also expressed a willingness to meet with constituents but added that the best place for
a resident to start to promote community engagement is within their direct neighborhood with the residents they are closest to.
          Vice Chair White reported on her attendance at the recent Utah League of Cities and Towns (ULCT) conference and
noted that Mayor Nadolski was elected to League’s Board of Directors, which is great and will provide great representation for
Ogden City. She then discussed the relationship between her day job and her role as a Council member; she cited the discussion
during tonight's work session regarding the City’s Five-Year Consolidated Plan, during which there was some mention of
residents not having good transportation choices for commuting to their job. She is excited to work on policies to help improve
transportation choices as part of her day job.
          Council member Choberka stated she was also able to attend the ULCT Conference and she attended many sessions
regarding housing challenges in the State. She then commented on the actions taken tonight regarding the Diversity Commission
ordinance; it is a real shame that the State Legislature had to take action to ban certain words that can no longer be used in certain
institutions. She believes there were some misunderstandings about the real disparities that those that belong to particular
populations face and she will continue to work on those matters.
          Council member Hyer stated that his neighborhood applied for and received a ‘Neighbor Up’ grant; the event that was
funded by the grant was held over the weekend and he was pleased with the turnout of those of all ages and races who attended
with in the spirit of love and support for all.
