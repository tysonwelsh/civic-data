Congressman Burgess Owens arrived and provided an update on his legislative work and
emphasized opportunities for the community to benefit from federal funding. He began by
praising Utah’s culture of innovation, collaboration, and community service, and stated his
intent to help preserve these values in Washington, D.C. He highlighted his personal
background, including his decade-long residency in Utah and experience working with at-risk
youth, which led to his eventual decision to run for office. Congressman Owens explained that
he currently serves on two key House committees: the Education and Workforce Committee,
which he has served on since arriving in Congress, and the Transportation and Infrastructure
Committee, which he joined to better address Utah’s rapid growth. He noted that both
committees have relevance for Kearns and expressed his hope that initiatives from them would
benefit the area directly. In discussing education, Owens argued for reform and innovation in
the system, likening the need for change to advancements seen in the tech industry. He voiced
concern over the outdated structure of public education and stressed the importance of
adapting to students' individual talents and career paths from an early age. He also expressed
a strong desire to restore respect and financial sustainability to the teaching profession, and
announced his candidacy for chair of the Education and Workforce Committee to further these
goals. On infrastructure, he discussed Utah’s rapid expansion and highlighted the importance
of strategic transportation development to support the local economy and maintain quality of
life.

Casey Saxton, Congressman Owens’ District Director, explained the federal Community
Project Funding process. Each congressional district may submit up to 15 funding requests per
year, generally capped at $5 million per project and focused on infrastructure. He mentioned
past projects in and around Kearns that had received funding, including for stormwater
systems and police equipment. Though Congress had delayed the final funding decision until
December, the appropriations bill still included several local projects totaling approximately $22
million for Utah’s Fourth District.

Congressman Owens concluded by reiterating that the Community Project Funding program
was fiscally responsible, constituting only a small portion of discretionary spending and not
contributing to the national debt. He emphasized the importance of Utah communities
becoming more proactive in voicing their needs to secure funding. He expressed optimism
about working with Senator-elect John Curtis, who he believed would help expand

          c. Consider Ordinance 2024-10-02 Amending Title 18, Subdivision Provisions
             and Procedures

Jay Springer, with Smith Hartvigsen, presented an update on legislative changes to
subdivision ordinances for 2023 and 2024. He began by referencing the earlier 2023 updates,
which introduced review cycles and the establishment of an administrative land use authority.
The current ordinance before the council had already undergone a public hearing process with
the Planning Commission and was intended to bring the municipality into compliance with
2024 state legislative changes. The primary focus of the 2024 updates was to clarify
procedures around subdivision improvement plans, specifically the civil drawings required
during development. The amendments stipulated that substantive reviews of these plans must
occur during designated review cycles, not outside of them. Additionally, the updates included
revisions to development agreement provisions and other general cleanup measures. This
included removing outdated references to “township” or “metro” in relation to Kearns, aiming to
streamline and modernize the ordinance language. He noted that the changes aligned with
House Bill 476, which was set to take effect on November 1. Because these adjustments
primarily impacted internal administrative processes, and the Municipal Services District (MSD)
was already preparing for them, there was minimal anticipated impact on external
stakeholders. An adoption ordinance was included in the meeting packet, and Springer
indicated that no further changes were expected. However, he acknowledged that some
communities prefer to allow for additional edits or feedback before final adoption.

Council Member Snow moved to approve Ordinance 2024-10-02, Amending Title 18,
Subdivision Provisions and Procedures. Council Member Schaeffer seconded the
motion; vote was 4-0, unanimous in favor. Council Member Peterson was present in the
online meeting but did not voice his vote.

6. Stakeholder Updates/Information

           a. Kearns Improvement District (KID)

Greg Anderson provided several updates on behalf of the Kearns Improvement District,
beginning with a preview of a video highlighting recent accomplishments. He announced that
the district’s budget hearing would be held on November 12 at 6:00 p.m., during which the
board would discuss potential increases in water service costs. He invited council members
and the public to attend. He also shared progress on the 5400 South water line project. The
project had gone out to bid, with five bids received. It is part of a federally funded initiative
supported by Congressman Burgess Owens, who helped secure $2 million in Environmental
Protection Agency (EPA) funding. The EPA is currently reviewing the bids, and the project is
expected to begin in spring. The construction will extend from 5600 West to the Mountain View
Corridor, running along the center lane of 5400 South, while keeping travel lanes open.
A significant portion of his remarks focused on a recent award received by the Kearns
Improvement District. The district was honored with the 2024 EPA WaterSense Award for
Excellence in Education and Outreach. The award recognizes the district’s efforts in promoting
water conservation and public awareness. He noted that only eight water providers in the
nation received the award, out of 2,200 participating organizations. He emphasized the scale
of the WaterSense program, which includes 46,000 certified water-efficient products and
significant nationwide impact in water and energy savings. In 2023 alone, WaterSense efforts
saved 1.2 trillion gallons of water and nearly 1 trillion kilowatt-hours of energy, equivalent to the
planting 997 billion trees and the cooling effect they would have. He credited the award to
strong community engagement and support, particularly through the district’s participation in
local events, outreach efforts, grant-funded landscape renovations, and conservation-themed
art contests. He described interactions with schoolchildren, including one student who
expressed interest in working for the district in the future. He also highlighted the support of
first responders in emergency preparedness exercises and acknowledged Congressman
Owens for providing certificates of special congressional recognition to winners of the district’s
conservation art contest. He closed by expressing pride in the community’s contributions to
water conservation and appreciation for the recognition received at the national level.

Lorrin P. Colby Jr. addressed the council to express long-standing concerns about the traffic
configuration and safety hazards at the intersection of Cougar Lane and 5400 South. He
described the current layout as chaotic and dangerous, particularly when drivers
inappropriately use the far-right lane to make a right turn and then aggressively merge across
multiple lanes to turn left under the railroad tracks—causing conflicts with drivers who are
lawfully turning right from the correct lane.

Mayor Kelly Bush responded by confirming that the city had secured a grant to address this
intersection and that improvements, including dedicated right and left turn signals, were in the
works. She explained that these modifications would help organize traffic flow and reduce
conflicts.

Mr. Colby proposed an interim solution: the installation of additional signage clarifying lane
usage. He suggested placing lane direction signs above each lane on the signal arm,
indicating that the middle lane is for both right turns and through traffic, the far-right lane is for
right turns only, and the left lane is for left turns only. This, he argued, would help prevent
confusion and reduce aggressive or unsafe lane changes.

Council Member Tina Snow added that 5400 South is a UDOT-managed road, limiting what
the city can independently modify, but affirmed that collaboration with UDOT is ongoing.

Mayor Bush reiterated the severity of the issue and asked Chad Anderson, one of the city’s
engineers, to follow up.

Chad Anderson confirmed he would coordinate with Doug (presumably a UDOT contact) to
assess the signage situation and consider installing clearer directional signage on the signal’s
overhead mast arm. The discussion concluded with appreciation for Colby’s input and a
commitment from city staff to act on the interim signage request while longer-term
improvements move forward.

asserted that the role of city manager is distinct and necessary, and without one, either the
mayor or an administrator would still need to fulfill these responsibilities.

Council Members Tina Snow and Al Peterson, discussed compensation amounts in detail.
Council Member Snow highlighted the challenges of attending daytime meetings with a part-
time wage and supported a livable, full-time salary for the mayor. Council Member Peterson
referenced historical compensation figures, pointing out that compensation for elected officials
had been based on earlier assumptions about workload. He presented COLA data from 2022
to 2025, showing a cumulative increase of over 20%, and proposed a possible 25% increase in
the mayor's salary to about $45,000 annually. He also noted that if a city manager were hired,
the mayor’s role might revert to part-time and suggested adjusting the salary accordingly.

Mayor Bush strongly objected to any reduction in compensation, citing the responsibilities,
liability, and personal toll of the role. She emphasized the mayor's exposure to lawsuits and
accountability, arguing that the salary should reflect those burdens. Maro Bush reiterated her
dissatisfaction with reconsidering decisions made after extensive public input, stressing that
doing so disrespected the democratic process and community involvement.

Council Member Chrystal Butterfield expressed that council member roles were part-time and
generally should not include benefits, although this was challenged by others who believed the
matter had already been resolved in a public hearing.

Mayor Bush clarified that the issue of adjusting or removing council member benefits had not
been formally addressed in a public hearing and stated that it would need to be discussed and
scheduled for a public hearing if changes were desired.

The meeting concluded with the consensus that no changes would be made to the mayor’s
compensation previously approved in Ordinance 2025-O-04 at the February 2025 City Council
Meeting, nor to the current city council member compensation or benefits.

    D. Discussion Regarding Adjustment of Procurement Amount, currently set at $100,000

Mayor Kelly Bush initiated a discussion regarding a proposed adjustment to the city’s
procurement policy, specifically to increase the threshold for construction-related projects. She
explained that the current limit of $100,000 was proving to be restrictive, particularly because
the Metro Services District (MSD) lacked a pre-qualified vendor pool for services like asphalt
and concrete work. The result was a burdensome bidding process for even minor construction
tasks, such as small segments of cement work, due to rising costs. She proposed raising the
procurement threshold for construction projects to $250,000 to streamline project execution
and avoid delays.

Nathan Bracken clarified that any change would require a formal resolution and amendment to
the purchasing policy, and that the discussion item was not up for a vote during the meeting.
He inquired whether the proposed increase would apply to all purchases or be limited to
construction. The mayor confirmed it would be exclusive to construction-related work to
prevent unnecessary public bidding for relatively small but high-cost projects.

Chad Anderson, in agreement with the mayor, stated that the proposed threshold would be
sufficient for the projects under consideration.

Mr. Bracken outlined the current procurement structure, which includes categories for small
purchases, intermediate purchases requiring two to three informal bids, and sealed bids for
higher amounts. He suggested the city could adopt new thresholds: $50,000 for small
purchases, $100,000 for intermediate bids, and $250,000 for sealed bids specific to
construction.

Mr. Anderson supported continuing to gather multiple bids for smaller construction projects but
without the full public bidding process, noting that this would allow the city to remain cost-
effective without the delays of formal procurement procedures.

Mayor Bush confirmed that the city typically does solicit multiple bids even without a formal
process, but the comprehensive bidding process is too time-consuming for frequent, smaller-
scale projects.

Mr. Bracken also introduced the concept of establishing an “approved vendor list,” or
contractor pool, created through a request for qualifications (RFQ) process. Qualified firms
would be selected by an evaluation committee, potentially including council members, and
could be used for streamlined bidding on future projects. Mayor Bush asked whether
contractors listed on the state’s procurement site could be automatically included, but Mr.
Bracken explained that while the city’s code references the state list, it would still be beneficial
to develop a Kearns-specific pool.

AN ORDINANCE OF THE KEARNS COUNCIL UPDATING SUBSECTION
19.46.120 REGARDING THE INSTALLATION OF INFRASTRUCTURE AND

                                        RECITALS
       WHEREAS, the City of Kearns is a municipality and has authority to adopt land
use regulations, pursuant to Utah Code § 10-9a-501 in accordance with the Municipal Land
Use, Development, and Management Act, Title 10, Section 9a, Utah Code; and
        WHEREAS, the Council deems it necessary to update Subsection 19.46.120 re-
garding the City’s requirements for building or land use permit applicants to provide curb,
gutter, sidewalk, street, and urban hydrology improvements along the entire property line
which abuts any public road or street in cases where it does not exist at municipal standards;
and
       WHEREAS, these improvement installation requirements are subject to the limita-
tions on exactions in Utah Code; and
      WHEREAS, the Kearns Planning Commission held a public hearing on May 5th,
2025 to consider amending Title 19 as set forth in Exhibit A (the “Proposed Action”) in
accordance with Utah Code §§ 10-9a-205and 10-9a-502; and
        WHEREAS, the Planning Commission has recommended that the Council amend
its land use ordinances set forth in Exhibit A for the protection and preservation of the
public health, safety and general welfare.
BE IT ORDAINED BY THE KEARNS CITY COUNCIL as follows:
1.     Adoption of Revisions. Section 19.46.120 Infrastructure and Public Improve-
       ments, is repealed in its entirety and amended to read as set forth in Exhibit A.
2.     Severability. If a court of competent jurisdiction determines that any part of this
       Ordinance is unconstitutional or invalid, then such portion of this Ordinance, or
       specific application of this Ordinance, shall be severed from the remainder, which
       shall continue in full force and effect.
3.     Direction to Staff. Staff are authorized and directed to take such steps as may be
       needed: (a) for this ordinance to become effective under Utah law, including but not
       limited to compliance with the requirements of Utah Code § 10-3-711; and (b) to
        finalize and post the ordinance to Municode, including but not limited to making
        non-substantive edits to correct any scrivener’s, formatting, and numbering errors.
4.      Effective Date. This Ordinance will take effect immediately upon posting pursuant
        to Utah Code § 10-3-712.
PASSED AND ADOPTED this 12th day of May 2025.

19.46.120 Infrastructure and Public Improvements
The minimum requirements for public improvements shall be a combination of standards set
forth in Title 14 Highways, Sidewalks, and Public Places and applicable standards set forth in
this Title or adopted in Kearns’ Master Transportation Plan.
A. Off-Site Improvements Required.
   1. Off-Site Improvements. Subject to the essential link and rough proportionality tests for
      exactions in Utah Code Section 10-9a-508, an applicant for a building or land use permit
      for a dwelling, or a commercial or industrial use shall provide curb, gutter, sidewalk,
      street, and urban hydrology improvements along the entire property line which abuts any
      public road or street in cases where it does not exist at municipal standards.
   2. Access to public right-of-ways. New and reconstructed vehicular entrances to the
      property shall be provided as required in Section 14.12.110. Height, location, structural
      specifications, maximum and minimum cut radii and minimum roadway approach angles
      to the centerline of the street are subject to the approval of the Municipal Engineering
      Division.
   3. Exceptions.
       a. The planning commission may grant exception to the installation of the sidewalk in
          industrial areas where the planning commission determines that the sidewalk is not
          necessary to serve the public need, and the elimination of the sidewalk does not
          jeopardize the public health, safety or welfare.
       b. The planning commission may grant exception to the installation of curb, gutter and
          sidewalk in rural or estate areas where topographic or other exceptional conditions
          exist, provided that the public health, safety and welfare is preserved.
       c. The planning commission may require the applicant to sign a delay agreement
          binding the current and future property owner to pay for their share of any required
          improvements that are installed by Kearns within 10 years of the waiver.
B. Acceptance of Public Streets.
   1. Street lighting shall either be chosen from the municipality’s approved streetlight list or
      installed to match a theme set by developments within the zone or neighborhood.
   2. Street lighting shall be installed in conformance with Title 18.
   3. Street Signs and Markers. Standard street name signs shall be installed at one corner of
      all street intersections. The size, design, materials, location, fabrication, installation, and
      maintenance of the signs and poles within the public right of way and elsewhere shall be
      in accordance with the Utah Department of Transportation, the Manual of Uniform
      Traffic Control Devices for Streets and Highways (MUTCD), or the municipality’s
      adopted policies, as applicable.
C. Private Streets.
   1. Private streets or roads shall be designed and constructed to meet or exceed the public
      street standards set forth in Title 14 Highways, Sidewalks, and Public Places, as
      applicable.
       a. Private streets or roads are allowed in the following circumstances:
           (1) If shown on an approved development plan as private streets;
           (2) For multi-family developments; or
           (3) For residential subdivisions where the street is equally shared between properties.
               Such streets shall have a maintenance and operations plan included in the
               declaration of covenants, conditions, and restrictions for the subdivision.
D. Ingress and Egress Requirements. No building with human occupiable space may be erected
   or enlarged on a parcel in any zone unless such parcel abuts upon or has access to a publicly
   accepted and maintained street, a private driveway leading to an approved public street, a
   private road, or a public or private alley.
E. Intersecting Streets and Clear Visibility. In all zones which require a front yard, no
   obstruction to view in excess of three feet (3’) in height may be placed on any corner lot
   within a triangular area formed by the street property lines or right-of-way lines and a line
   connecting them at points forty feet (40’) from the intersection of the street lines, except
   mature trees which are located in the clear sight triangle shall be pruned to a height of at least
   seven feet (7') above the established sidewalk or street elevation.
                   Figure 19.46.120 A: Intersecting Streets and Clear Visibility.
F. Intersecting Streets and Driveways. In all zones, no view obstruction, including a sight-
   obscuring fence, wall, sign, other similar structures, and landscaping which exceeds three
   feet (3’) in height shall be placed within a triangular area formed by a diagonal line
   connecting lines located at the curb line or sidewalk line and driveway line ten feet (10’)
   from the projected intersection of such lines.
                   Figure 19.46.120 B: Intersecting Streets and Driveways.




G. Acceptance of Private Streets. Prior to acceptance by the municipality, any private street, or
   any driveway allowed for access with a development that is not constructed and maintained
   to the municipality’s adopted street standards shall be improved to the municipality’s adopted
   street standards.
H. Driveways. A driveway shall be allowed for vehicular access according to the standards set
   forth in Title 14 Highways, Sidewalks, and Public Places, and Chapter 19.48, Parking and
   Mobility.
I. Pedestrian and Bicycle Mobility.
   1. Each lot shall have pedestrian walkways and sidewalks that provide connections between
      the building entrances, neighboring building entrances, parking areas, open space, and
      public trail. Such systems shall be designed to connect with all elements within the
      development, adjacent areas, and transit stops and can include sidewalks along public or
      private streets, wide outside travel lanes, bike lanes on roadways, and walkways and trails
      in alternative locations as appropriate. Design, location, dimensions, dedications,
      easements, and reservations shall conform to applicable municipal policies and standards
      for sidewalks, bicycle routes, and trails.
   2. Walkways and trails shall be designed to maximize the safety of users and the security of
      adjoining properties with respect to location, visibility, and landscaping.
   3. Bicycle Facilities. Bicycle facilities shall be provided in accordance with the standards
      set forth in Chapter 19.48, Parking and Mobility.
