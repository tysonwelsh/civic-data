1. Discussion of Zone Ogden Code Update

Mr. Rypien stated he has been working with the consultants and the steering committee
and certain recommendations are now being proposed for consideration. The consultants
have completed their assessment of the existing code and prepared a report which they
are using as a basis for recommendations. The second stage is drafting sections of the
Code for review, presenting these at various groups, including the community advisory
committee, Planning Commission and City Council. A public open house will also be held
to allow input from various stakeholder groups. He outlined the community sentiment
ideas, stating the new code should improve predictability, reliability and transparency.

The consultants first reviewed the current approval process and made some
recommendations relating to how this could be expedited. The intent is to provide better
consistency as the current code identifies several approval entities including Planning
Manager, Director, Mayor, Attorney, Planning Staff and Planning Commission. They have
organized procedures into category types, with simple Type | and II being a staff approval
while items requiring both Planning Commission and City Council approval would be a
Type V. He reviewed particular decision types which would fit into each type and what
findings would be needed for approval as well as the appeal process for each land use.
He asked the Commission to think about whether there are other application types which
need to be discussed, whether any of them have been involved with the process and
whether the application process went well or whether some improvements should be

identified. He also asked about the comfort level of allowing Staff to make decisions
based on the existing ordinance standards.

He then addressed required lot sizes in residential zones, stating there are some lots which
are dysfunctional due to excessive property which is uncared for. He suggested lot widths
or setbacks might be reduced or flag lots might be allowed, which would create more
opportunity for single-family housing. He also suggested parking requirements either be
reduced, eliminated or allowance made for tandem parking to obtain two legal parking
spaces. Commission discussed alley access which is an option for much of the City, but it
was noted most of these are not well maintained due to cost to residents. Commission
also discussed setback requirements, noting rear yard setbacks are designed to provide
legal parking as well as play area for families. Staff suggested side yard setbacks be made
uniform in all residential zones, feeling a minimum of 5’ setback would be adequate. Staff
also is suggesting side yards facing a street be reduced stating current standards require
more lot area than interior lots and felt lot widths could be reduced to match that of interior
lots. It was felt corner lot owners are unable to use much of their rear yard area due to the
required side yard requirements as they are unable to fence this area. Commissioner
Southwick suggested street widths in many areas might be reduced, which could provide
more space for residential development. It was indicated street width would be addressed
in the subdivision portion of the new unified development code. Discussion continued
relating to accessory parking for recreational vehicles, stating if parking is reduced,
accommodation might need to be made for accessory parking. The reduced side yard
might affect the ability for off-street parking in some areas but might allow for infill or
accessory dwelling units to be established. They also discussed whether the regulations
for accessory buildings might be changed. The current standards indicate maximum of
25% of the rear yard is allowed to be hard surfaced or building coverage, while items such
as swimming pools or hard-surfaced landscaping features do not count in that regulation
and whether items such as an outdoor patio counts Is unclear. The intent for an open rear
yard is to provide play or recreation areas, allow storm water to be contained as well as
limiting heat from being produced by hard-surfaces.

Building height also is regulated in all residential zones, with most identifying the option of
2% stories or 35’. Discussion continued relating to ceiling heights, which may increase the
height beyond 35’ but still be less than 2% stories. Commission felt a step-back concept
could allow taller buildings when adjacent to commercial buildings, or taller buildings being
allowed in the center of a block with a step-down in height would gradually bring buildings
toward the edges to be single story or two story. Commission felt there should be specific
measurements in feet rather than identifying the number of stories. Staff also is
suggesting the minimum building height of one story might be eliminated. Commission
felt while there was historically a need for basement homes, these are not desirable in
neighborhoods and felt there should be a one-story minimum requirement.

Mr. Rypien indicated the State legislature passed legislation which prohibits cities from
regulating design standards unless an area was established prior to 1950. He presented
photos of types of homes which might be allowed if no standards are adopted. The

proposed ordinance would first identify established neighborhoods and then establish
standards which would protect the character of these neighborhoods. He presented maps
of areas which could be identified as established neighborhoods and stated these areas
would likely be regulated by a separate chapter of the code. Regulations might include
standards if the footprint of a building is to be altered, height increased or for new
construction on vacant or infill lots on a case-by case basis. Commission felt standards
should include the front of buildings facing the street, covered entryways, eaves, front
windows and roof pitch to fit the surrounding area. It was felt a defined entryway and
gabled rooflines are important to the character of the area, and that new construction
should be in scale with the character of the surrounding neighborhood. As much of these
established neighborhoods have detached garages, it also is suggested garages be
required to be recessed from the front wall of the home. Discussion continued relating to
reduced lot widths and the ability of buildings to have adequate width without making the
garage a prominent feature of the home and whether some exemptions might be allowed.
It was felt the charm of these older neighborhoods should not be compromised by new
development. It was indicated if there is an entry feature leading to the front door such as
a porch, the door need not necessarily face the street so long as the entrance is defined.
Discussion also addressed converting existing parking into living space, indicating there
are areas where homes are small with attached garages or carports. While some have
been done in the past, care needs to be taken that legal parking not be eliminated, and that
driveways in the front yard which no longer lead to legal parking are removed. It was felt
design review should be based on the surrounding existing neighborhood.

Mr. Rypien indicated the next section for review will include infill, cluster cottages and
group dwelling standards. An open house will be scheduled for April or May with an
attempt to involve many stakeholders and citizens in the review process. He reminded the
Commission if they had any other additional ideas they could reach out to him by email
and these could be further discussed with both the consultants and planning staff. He
encouraged the Commission to become familiar with the Zone Ogden web page and to
share it with others in the community.

Mr. Brierley then reviewed items for the upcoming March meeting of the Planning Commission.

There being no additional business before the Commission, the meeting was adjourned at

7:30pm.
