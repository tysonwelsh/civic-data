    3. MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING WITH STATE OF UTAH, DEPT. OF TECHNOLOGY SERVICES, TO ALLOW FOR
       INSTALLATION & USE OF AN ALBERT SENSOR TO PROVIDE ADDITIONAL CYBERSECURITY MONITORING TO THE COUNTY
       Ricky Hatch, County Clerk/Auditor, became involved with elections cyber security on a federal level in 2017
       and one of the first things the group did that he helped establish was to encourage states to install an Albert
       Sensor. Utah already had one and this is partly why it had no breaches in 2016. These sensors monitor and
       collect governmental network data and packet information and send it for analysis to ISAC (the multi-state
       Information Sharing and Analysis Center), an extremely high tech center, that helps identify malicious malware.
       The State of Utah obtained funding to provide these sensors to every county in Utah. Commissioner Jenkins
       stated that Mr. Hatch is considered one of the foremost experts in the nation on voting by mail and asked him to
       address it in Weber County.
       Commissioner Jenkins moved to approve the Memorandum of Understanding with the State of Utah, Department
       of Technology Services, to allow for installation and use of an Albert Sensor to provide additional cybersecurity
       monitoring to the county; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
       Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye

    3. PUBLIC HEARING TO DISCUSS & TAKE ACTION ON A PROPOSAL TO AMEND THE ZONING CODE FOR CV-1 &
       CV-2 ZONES OF THE OGDEN VALLEY TO REDUCE THE FRONT YARD SETBACK, INCLUDE COMPLETE STREET
       DESIGN, AND ADD SECTIONS FOR BUILDING MAINTENANCE CONTRACT AND CROSS ACCESS EASEMENT
       Charles Ewert, of the County Planning Division, stated that currently both zones have a front yard setback that
       is away from the public right-of-way and also a side yard setback that is away from the property line. This is an
       initial step toward implementing a full ordinance that will create villages as prescribed by the Ogden Valley
       General Plan to facilitate pedestrian-friendly storefronts, provide sufficient shared parking in the buildings’ rear,
       etc. The shared parking would need perpetual maintenance agreements among property owners in order to have
       cross access easements recorded with the properties for operation/maintenance of these internal block parking
       facilities. If the neighboring property owner won’t participate in the agreement, then the setback will be 10 ft.
       from the side and rear.
   4. APPLICANT-INITIATED PROPOSAL TO AMEND COUNTY CODES §104-7 & 108-7 TO ALLOW CULTIVATION &
      PROCESSING OF CANNABIS CULTIVATION IN A-2 ZONES PROVIDING COMPLIANCE WITH SPECIFIC STANDARDS
      Charles Ewert, of the County Planning Division, stated that the request is to allow for cannabis cultivation. The
      proposal prohibits any outdoor cultivation, the operation has to be secured, the setback is 100 ft. from the
      property line, the parcel must be at least 20 acres, etc. To get a license from the State costs tens of thousands of
      dollars, and those are few in number, thus operation is somewhat limited and controlled. Commissioner Jenkins
      strongly wished to restrict the number of growers in the county. Mr. Ewert responded that the Planning
      Commission discussion about minimizing that concern was to require a large amount of acreage and there were
      drafts for 75 and 50 acres. The planning commissioners made a positive recommendation with 20 acres, as listed
      in the A-3 Zone. He addressed Commissioner Harvey’s question stating that he had discussed different
      possibilities with the applicant, including creating an overlay zone for cannabis and rezoning the subject property
      to A-3 as it is two parcels away from the A-3. The applicant had concerns with security and the property being
      identifiable. Commissioner Jenkins liked the idea of a zone-appropriate conditional use permit that gave the
      Commission more control and Mr. Ewert noted that those permits are very difficult to deny and are approved by
      the Planning Commissions, and he suggested amending this ordinance limiting to one permit in the A-2.
   5. PUBLIC COMMENTS: Attorney Seth Gomm said that he is well versed in this industry and a stakeholder in Zion
      Pharmaceuticals, the applicant, that they have one of the 8 awarded licenses, and each can only cultivate up to
      100,000 sq. ft. The 83 acre site has 1 million sq. ft. of greenhouse, and if they maximize that single license, they
      would only be using 1/10 of that greenhouse space. He said that medical marijuana is virtually identical to
      industrial hemp and that Weber County is in the top 3 counties of patients with cards for cannabis.
