“Miracle Heart” Life Saving Event. Recognizing Hunter Clarke, Andy
Blodgett, Rob Matasic, GB Burningham, Chris Reid, Kent Taylor, and
Natalie Day for their preparation, quick thinking, and extraordinary efforts
that saved the life of their friend, Jeff Wilson
          A memo from Council staff regarding recognition of Hunter Clarke, Andy Blodgett, Rob Matasic, GB Burningham,
Chris Reid, Kent Taylor, and Natalie Day for providing life-saving efforts in an emergency situation came before the Council for
consideration. Fire Chief Mathieu reported on the incident that occurred during a basketball game on Thanksgiving morning
when Ogden resident Jeff Wilson collapsed from a heart attack. The quick-thinking efforts of those seven individuals saved
Wilson’s life. The Ogden Fire Department strongly emphasizes the critical importance of CPR training and the availability of
AEDs in responding to cardiac emergencies. They also encourage the community to engage with the PulsePoint app, which
notifies trained citizens of nearby cardiac emergencies. Studies show that immediate CPR significantly improves survival rates,
and the hope is that this program empowers even more members of the community to act in future emergencies.
          The Council then heard from representatives of Ogden Regional Medical Center regarding their advocacy for CPR
training and the use of AEDs in the community; they thanked Chief Mathieu for promoting training opportunities and expressed
their appreciation for their partnership with Ogden City. They also issued Life Saved Awards to Mr. Clarke, Mr. Blodgett, Mr.
Matasic, Mr. Burningham, Mr. Reid, Mr. Taylor, and Ms. Day and first responders from the Ogden Fire Department.
          Council member Blair read the certificate of recognition issued by the Ogden City Council and Mayor Nadolski and
presented Mr. Clarke, Mr. Blodgett, Mr. Matasic, Mr. Burningham, Mr. Reid, Mr. Taylor, and Ms. Day with certificates of
recognition.
          Mr. Wilson expressed his gratitude to his friends and the medical professionals that saved his life; he considers the event
a big miracle made of several small miracles, including the CPR training his friends had received as well as their intimate
knowledge of how to use an AED. He thanked the Mayor and Council for recognizing his friends and the first responders and
expressed his willingness to help to further training efforts in the community.

Proposed Ordinance 2025-3 amending Fiscal Year 2024-2025 budget to
fund two new positions in the Mayor’s Office for a net change of $0 in the
total budget and to update the adopted staffing document to reflect the new
position.
         A memo from the Mayor’s Office regarding proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 budget and staffing documents came
before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposed amendments add two executive level positions to the
Mayor’s staff. The initial proposal was to add a Deputy Chief Administrative Officer and a Chief of Staff with both positions
funded at the Deputy Executive Director level AED190. The Mayor’s Office has since modified the request to add a Deputy
Chief Administrative Officer funded at the Deputy Executive Director level AED190, and a Chief Executive Assistant funded at
the Division Director level DD180. Funds to cover the cost of the additional positions are being transferred from other
departmental and non-departmental budgets and therefore, the impact to the FY2025 budget is net $0. The FY2026 budget will
require additional funding of between $344,000 and $413,000 in the Mayor’s budget.
         Mayor Nadolski summarized the staff memo and provided his opinions regarding why he feels the new positions are
needed. He emphasized the proposal is budget neutral but will allow him to drive executive level leadership from the top and set
organizational directions and priorities that can be achieved across departments of the City.
         Finance Director Sorensen used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide an overview of the proposed
interdepartmental budget transfers intended to fund the proposed staffing adjustments.
         Vice Chair White stated that she appreciates the explanation and justification for the new positions, but in the past, there
was an unwritten agreement between the City Council and administration to use vacant positions and attrition to help fund and
support ongoing wage increases for existing staff.
         Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2025-3, entitled:
                  “An ordinance of Ogden City amending the budget for the Fiscal Year July 1, 2024 to June 30, 2025 by neither
                  increasing or decreasing the anticipated revenues for a net change of $0 from sources as detailed in the body of
                  this ordinance; and neither increasing or decreasing the appropriations for a net change of $0 as detailed in the
                  body of this ordinance; and by providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting
                  after final passage.”
         Chair Richey then opened the public hearing.

Proposed Ordinance 2025-4 classifying property located at 397 and 399
North Harrisville Road from Two-Family Residential (R-2) to Two-family and
Small Lot Residential (R-2S)
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed rezone at 397-399 North
Harrisville Road came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated MKIB Real Estate, LLC has submitted a petition to
rezone 4.9 acres from R-2 to R-2S. The petitioner hopes to establish zoning on the property to allow for a townhome project
(between 60 and 85 units) that will be platted for individual ownership.
          Planning Director Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
proposed rezone; the subject properties are three parcels located near 400 North and Harrisville Road. There are older single-
family dwellings on two of the parcels, including a home built in 1948 at 399 Harrisville Road, and a home built in 1930 at 397
Harrisville Road. The third parcel is a vacant remnant of the Buddhist church property to the west. The current zoning of the
subject properties is R-2. The R-2 zone is considered a moderate density zone, with allowances for single-family (6,000 square
foot lot size) and two-family dwellings (12,000 square foot lot size). The R-2 properties adjacent to the proposed rezone are the
Ogden Buddhist Church and some single-family properties to the southeast. Other zones abutting the subject properties are R-3
(Multiple-family residential) and C-2/CP-2 (Community Commercial). The property to the south is zoned R-3 and consists of two
multifamily projects known as Bramblewood Apartments and Parkwood Estates. The property across Harrisville Road to the east
is C-2/CP-2 which includes America First Credit Union and Pinebrook Apartments. The intent of the proposed R-2S zone is to
provide moderate density residential development with an emphasis on homeownership of individual dwelling units. The R-2S
zone allows single-family rowhouses with a minimum of 2,500 square feet per dwelling unit. The developer anticipates that the
project will include between 60 and 80 townhome units. The subject property is part of the Lynn Community Plan and single-
family residential is the main land use in the Lynn Community. The Planning Division transmittal explains that the proposed
rezone meets the General Plan zone change policies and the Lynn Community Plan policies. The Plan suggests that the Planning
Commission and City Council should consider measures to ensure that future development is in harmony with historic sites and
features of the area. The petitioner’s narrative explains that they plan to glean from existing buildings and architecture in the area
to design an attractive community. The Administration has not recommended any historic preservation actions related to this zone
change. On November 6, 2024, the Planning Commission considered the proposal, heard public comment, and recommended that
the Council approve the proposal with a condition that a parcel boundary adjustment be completed with the parcel to the west, so
the property lines match the zoning boundary. For a copy of Mr. Brierley’s presentation in its entirety, see the information packet
for the meeting.
          Chair Richey invited input from the applicant.
          Brian Smith of MKIB, LLC approached and indicated Mr. Brierley’s presentation was very thorough; he reported on his
discussions with the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) regarding access points to the project and efforts to align one of
the accesses with the ingress/egress to American First Credit Union across the street. He stated that he is not seeking more density
than would be allowed on the property under the current zoning; the zone change will allow him to build townhomes, and he is
committed to develop units that are available for individual ownership. He then briefly discussed the architectural plans for the
project, amenities that will be included, parking accommodations and availability of electric vehicle charging stations. He
concluded he is open to suggestions from the City Council.

                    passage.”

Proposed Ordinance 2025-5 classifying property located at 2869 Grant
Avenue from Regional Commercial (CP-3) to Multiple-Family Residential
with Conditional Overlay (R-4/CO)
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed rezone at 2869 Grant
Avenue came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Race Ostler has submitted a petition to rezone 0.24 acres
from CP-3 (Regional Commercial) to R-4 (Multiple-family Residential). The petitioner requests to establish zoning on the
property to allow a single-family home or multiple-family units on the site.
          Planning Director Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
proposed rezone. The subject property is a vacant parcel located at 2869 Grant Avenue and is zoned CP-3. There are two
commercial developments to the north that are zoned CP-3. There are several single-family properties to the south and west, with
the majority of the block being zoned R-4. The CP-3 zone is a regional commercial zone typically established on larger parcels of
property with access to major roads. The CP-3 zoning in this area has been established primarily along Washington Boulevard
from 28th Street to 36th Street. In November of 2024 the Council approved a text amendment that prohibited new residential
dwelling units in the C-2/CP-2 and C-3/CP-3 zones. The R-4 zone is a multiple-family residential zone that allows for single-
family, two-family, and multiple-family dwellings. The R-4 zoning designation on the subject property would allow for four
condominium units or single-family row houses, or one-single family dwelling. The subject property is part of the Jefferson
Community plan. The land-use map from the Jefferson Community plan indicates the R-4 zoning is a desirable option for the
subject property. The Planning Division transmittal explains that rezoning the parcel to R-4/CO is consistent with several of the
General Plan vision strategies for this area, including the strategy to integrate new multiple-family and single-family development
into the area. On November 6, 2024, the Planning Commission considered the request and recommended that the council approve
the proposal to rezone to R-4/CO. There were no public comments about the proposal during the Planning Commission meeting.
For a copy of Mr. Brierley’s presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
