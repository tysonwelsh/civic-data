3. Discussion and Action Items
   3.1. Discussion and consideration of a Resolution adopting a proposed rate of tax and
        levying taxes upon all real and personal property within the Herriman City
        Safety Enforcement Area – Kyle Maurer, Finance Director
Finance Director Kyle presented the Certified Tax Rate for 2025 had decreased to
0.001368, which would generate approximately $9.7 million, equal to last year's revenue
plus around $396,000 in new growth. The average home value in Herriman had increased
from $613,600 to $640,000, and under the tax rate, the average tax bill for such a home
would be about $482.

Director Maurer recommended a property tax levy adjustment of $513,535, representing a
5.3% increase. This adjustment was intended to cover $236,510 in inflation-related costs for
FY 2025 and apply a 2.89% inflation factor based on the December CPI to begin addressing
inflationary impacts anticipated for FY 2026. The proposed increase would raise the tax
rate to 0.00144 and generate about $10.2 million in revenue. For the average homeowner,
this would result in an annual tax increase of approximately $25.

Trustee Henderson discussed the long-term sustainability of funding public safety through
property taxes alone. City Manager Nathan Cherpeski emphasized that while commercial
development contributes higher property taxes and does not receive the 45% residential
exemption, certain types of commercial uses such as hotels, bars, and big-box stores could
drive significant police service demand. The Board also noted that property tax revenues
from new developments often are delayed behind actual service demands by 12 to 18
months, creating a structural funding gap. Trustee Ohrn acknowledged the importance of
continuing to explore supplemental revenue sources, such as sales tax allocations, to
maintain service levels amid continued population and commercial growth.

The board concluded the item with a motion from Wendy Thomas to approve Resolution
R25-01, adopting the proposed property tax rate and levying taxes in the amount of
$10,211,240. The motion passed following the discussion.
