       4.2     Point In Time. Homeless Committee Presentation. (Presenter, Rebekkah Allen).


Rebekkah Allen spoke on behalf of the Salt Lake Valley Coalition to End Homelessness Point in
Time("PIT") Count Planning Committee. Ms. Allen is also a Community Engagement Coordinator
for the Salt Lake City Homeless Team.


The PIT count is an annual count ofhomeless persons to:

       1.      Find homeless persons living on the streets and gather information in order to connect
               them with available services.


       2.      Help policymakers and program administrators set benchmarks to measure progress
               toward the goal ofending homelessness.


       3.      Help plan services and programs to appropriately address local needs,and help identify
               strengths and gaps in our community's current homelessness assistance system.


       4.      Inform public opinion, increase public awareness, and attract resources.

       5.      Create the most reliable estimate of people experiencing homelessness throughout
               Utah.

       4.7     Presentation of the 2023-2024 Fiscal Year Audit Report Hansen Bradshaw
               Malmrose & Erickson. (Presenter, JeffMiles), [Joint Presentation of Audit: Citv
               Council. Local Building Authority, and Redevelopment Agency Board.]

Jeff Miles was present from Hansen, Bradshaw, Malmrose & Erickson("HBME")and presented the
2023-2024 Fiscal Year Audit Report. He noted that his firm was awarded a five-year contract to
provide services to Bluffdale City. He met with the City's newly formed Audit Committee in
September to kick offthe audit and then again earlier in the week to discuss the Audit results in detail.
He was available to answer any questions that arise as the Council members review the full report.


The report found that, "In our opinion, the City's financial statements present fairly, in all material
respects, the respective financial position ofthe governmental activities, the business-type activities,
each major fund, the aggregate remaining fund information of the City as of June 30, 2024, and the
respective changes in financial position and, where applicable, cash flows thereof and the respective
budgetary comparisons for the General Fund and the major special revenue funds for the year then
ended in conformity with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America."


The City's total net position increased by $17.3 million, primarily due to continued investment in
infrastructure and the new Public Works building. The small decrease from the previous year is
indicative of statewide trends, and his firm believes the City maintains a strong economic position.
The unassigned General Fund balance is capped at 35% ofbudgeted revenues,and Bluffdale's balance
is approximately 34%. Mayor Hall clarified that the net increase is in both funds and assets.
Mr. Miles stated that the increase is primarily infrastructure and services, not cash.


Mr. Miles reported that every governmental auditor is required to gain an understanding ofthe internal
controls of each city, and through that process they did not identify any significant deficiencies or
material weaknesses. The Audit Committee identified some joumal entries that were not finalized,

potentially exceed acceptable limits out to a distance of240.8 meters from the main antenna beam at
a 25-degree angle, but as the site currently exists, no nearby buildings or terrain would be impacted.

also provided example photographs of similar installations and eight-foot, sight-obscuring metal
fencing as originally proposed for this site. The site itself would have a gravel road base with no
landscaping.
