Z. Bylaws - Final Consideration and Possible Action regarding Amendments to the Planning

Commission Bylaws and Rules of Procedure

Duncan Murray explained that the process to update the bylaws began at the last meeting. Changes,

which were mostly made for consistency, were submitted as Option A. Included in the changes it

allows for three members to make up a quorum for purposes of the pre-meeting. Option B includes the

reference to Section D in Section A2. The motion was seconded by Jay Thomas and
passed 6-0 in favor. Ammon Allen was absent.

3. Pierson Farms Rezone; 1996 & 1986 West Gardner Lane; Rezone 16.36 acres from A-1

(Agriculture 1-acre lots) Zone to R-1-8C (Single-family Residential 8,000 square foot
minimum lots) Zone; McArthur Homes/John Gassman (applicant) [#31954; parcel 27-03-
126-007; 21-34-352-005]

John Gassman, McArthur Homes, said that they build about 150 homes per year and have built about

700 homes in West Jordan over 30 years. He referred to McArthur subdivisions Bateman Farms and

Boulder Ranch that this proposal will be similar to. The property is 16 acres in size and owned by a

family trust, and they propose about 55 lots. He reviewed the five criteria that are considered for a

rezoning. The future land use map calls for low density, single-family uses and the surrounding

property includes R-1-8, R-1-10, and R-1-12 zones. In order to make the homes as affordable as
possible they need as many lots as possible without changing the future land use to a higher density.
The low-density designation has a range of 3.1 to 5.0 units per acre. Their proposal will be 3.5 units
per acre, which is similar to the surrounding neighborhoods. The preliminary concept was shown. The
road at 1950 West was always intended to connect to the property and will be connected to Gardner
Lane. This connection will improve traffic flow and potentially reduce emergency response times.
Although it was not required at this time, a traffic impact study was provided. It shows that the
intersections at 1980 West and 2200 West on Gardner Lane have an acceptable level of service and
that will continue with the projected traffic flow. However, the one at Redwood Road is currently at a
failing level of service and it is in the planning and financing stages with UDOT for a traffic signal.
Once that signal is provided, it will meet the current and projected levels for service. Utilities are
available in Gardner Lane or in 1950 West. He is working with the Kirkman family who own 11 acres
to the west, and they made a gentleman’s agreement that if the engineering works and if Mr. Kirkman
can get the permits, they will grant an easement to stub sewer into the property for the future. Public
services in the area will be improved with the proposed development. There are no overlay districts in
the area. They are excited for the opportunity to develop this great piece of property.

Mark Forsythe reviewed the mix of residential uses in the surrounding area that includes single-family
on 10,000-13,000 square foot lots to the north and south. Rural residential zoning to the east is
developed with a mix of duplexes, apartments, and single-family homes on lots ranging from 12,000
square feet to 2 acres in size. To the west is the South Jordan canal, farmland, single-family homes,
and a church. Staff felt that an 8,000 square-foot-lot subdivision would be consistent with the
neighborhoods to the south and north. The future land use map shows low density residential, which
includes the R-1-8 zone. He reviewed the density table that is found in the general plan. The low-
density designation has a range of 3.1 to 5 units per acre, and the proposal is 3.5 units per acre. He
reviewed the density study and utility connections in the area. Regarding water availability, the
Utilities Engineer reviewed the equivalent residential connections, and they are entitled to 88 lots with
only 55 lots being proposed.

Based on the analysis and findings contained in the staff report, staff recommended that the Planning
Commission forward a positive recommendation to the City Council to rezone the parcels comprising
16.36 acres and located at 1996 and 1986 West Gardner Lane from an A-1 zone (Agriculture — 1 acre
lots) to an R-1-8C zone (Single-Family Residential - 8,000 square foot lots).

Jay Thomas asked for an explanation on the ERC numbers and the discrepancy that was discussed in
the pre-meeting.

Mark Forsythe said in June 2023 an overall update to the general plan was made, which included
adopting a new future land use map. In the plan and on the map the very high density designation was
removed, but the density range was left intact and the names of the designations were shifted.
However, the water master plan had not been updated, so the ERC table will have to be reviewed and
shifted up. The property was always designated to have 88 ERCs but the name change confused the
calculation.

John Roberts asked what the ERC calculation would be without the name shift.

Mark Forsythe said without taking the discrepancy into account the ERC would have been 49. This
property along with many other areas in the city were medium density and shifted to low density.
George Winn opened the public hearing.

George Winn referred to three emails received. Trisha Kingston strongly opposed the application for
tiny, cookie cutter houses and yards, and she wanted to keep open spaces. January Kingston opposed
the application with primary concerns of traffic, school overcrowding, water resources, and cookie
cutter houses on tiny lots. Emily Sosa supported McArthur Homes to develop the property.

Steve Losee, West Jordan resident, provided an information paper to the commission and stated that he
has two adjacent lots at the east. He would like to see a barrier or good fence between the one-acre lots
and the new homes to separate animals from people who might potentially get hurt.

Jeff Bigalow, West Jordan resident of the Park Pointe subdivision for 29 years, stated that he lives on
an 8,000 square foot lot, the homes are nice and not cookie cutter. He felt that the development by
McArthur Homes would enhance the neighborhood and increase his home’s value. It will be a nice
addition to their neighborhood, and many of his neighbors are in favor of it. The road connection will
also provide a quicker access to his church. Although expansion and growth are hard, West Jordan has
an opportunity to have a good development of quality homes from quality developer like McArthur.
He could see no detriment to the proposal.

Vernel Kirkman stated that his family owns the property to the west of the canal. As a contractor, he
has worked with McArthur Homes and has nothing but accolades for their work. As the adjacent
property owner, they want something that will enhance the value of the neighborhood, which they felt
this will do.

Jennifer Sorensen, West Jordan resident, said she lives on 1950 West. She bought there 3 4 years ago
knowing that the property would probably be developed and she supports it. However, her biggest
concern, which was escalated by a recent car chase in the neighborhood, was if there is a straight road
from Sugar Factory Road to Gardner Lane it could be dangerous without some kind of traffic calming
measures or a road design that is not a straight shot.

Heather Pierson Moore said it was a hard decision to make for their property to know what would be
best for the family and the community, and they felt like this was the right thing to do. She stated that
they interviewed many developers and she and her husband as well as her sister and her sister’s
husband really felt confident with John Gassman. It is a quality product, and he is good to work with
and gets things done in a timely manner and addresses concerns quickly.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

Emily Gonzalez said a concern regarding the elementary school was raised in an email and she asked
staff to point out the location.

George Winn asked staff to comment on the fencing and traffic calming suggestions that were raised.

Mark Forsythe said the application tonight is for a rezoning of the property, which gives certain

entitlements, such as density. If this is approved by the City Council then the applicant will return to

the Commission for preliminary subdivision review. That is when they get into the subdivision design
and compliance with ordinance, which includes any fencing that could be required. The matter of

traffic calming would be addressed with the subdivision review by the Traffic Engineer. There are a

variety of traffic calming methods that could be considered.

Jay Thomas indicated that Steve Pierson had been a good friend of his. He suggested that the person

with fencing concerns be proactive in discussions with the developer.

MOTION: Jay Thomas moved, based on the information and findings set forth in the staff
report and upon the evidence and explanations received today, to forward a
positive recommendation to the City Council to rezone the parcels comprising
16.36 acres and located at 1996 and 1986 West Gardner Lane from an A-1 zone
(Agriculture — 1 acre lots) to an R-1-8C zone (Single-Family Residential - 8,000
square foot lots). The motion was seconded by John Roberts and passed 6-0 in
favor. Ammon Allen was absent.

MOTION: Jay Thomas moved to adjourn.

The meeting was adjourned at 6:46 p.m.
