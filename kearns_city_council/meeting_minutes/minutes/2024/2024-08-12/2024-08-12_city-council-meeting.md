B. Authorization to Issue a Request for Proposals for a Certified Decontamination
Specialist for Abatements of Hazardous Materials
Mayor Bush explained the reasons for needing this request for proposals, to handle
abatements of hazardous materials in abandoned or contaminated homes that have not been
taken care of in a timely manner.
Nathan Bracken provided background on legislation that mandates cities act on meth-
contaminated properties if health departments do not act within six months.
Mayor Bush emphasized the importance of addressing these homes promptly to avoid
community blight and acknowledged recent success in reducing the number of contaminated
properties. The RFP will designate Alicia Gonzalez as the contact.
Council Member Snow moved to authorize the issuance of a request for proposals for a
certified decontamination specialist for abatement of hazardous materials. Council
Member Butterfield seconded the motion; vote was 4-0, unanimous in favor. Council
Member Schaeffer was excused from the vote.

Nathan Bracken explained that impact fees are a limited tool for cities to cover cost coming
from growth. There were no impact fees with the county, which means Kearns still does not
have them after incorporation as a city. When a new development comes in, cities charge an
impact fee to cover the costs directly related to infrastructure impacts from the new
development. Without these fees, current residents bear those costs associated with
infrastructure strain. Impact fees are limited and regulated based on the development being
done, and this is not a fee charged to existing residents or businesses. He also discussed
using impact fee waivers to incentivize affordable housing. The first step would be creating an
impact fees facility plan, hiring a consultant to assess the current level of service in the city
along with infrastructure, coming up with a facilities plan. The council would then review that
plan and decide if pursuing impact fees are warranted. If the council decides to proceed with
implementing impact fees, they would then need an impact fee study, which looks at the
potential fees and how Kearns would assess those according to current regulations. If the
council reviews that report and decides to assess those fees, the council would then vote to
approve an ordinance adding those fees for the city. The resolution tonight would be
authorizing the city to begin the process of developing an Impact Fee Facilities Plan by issuing
a request for proposals (RFP).

Mayor Bush moved to approve Resolution 2024-08-01, amending the title of the
resolution to authorize the issuance of an RFP to prepare an impact fees facility plan
that seconded by Council Member Snow seconded the motion; vote was 4-0,
unanimous in favor. Council Member Schaeffer was excused from the vote.

5. Stakeholder Updates/Information

A. Kearns Improvement District (KID)
Greg Anderson provided updates on two major initiatives. First, he reported that KID
completed its inspection for lead in water pipes as mandated by the EPA. The inspection
revealed no lead in the district’s infrastructure, and the findings were approved by the Utah
Division of Drinking Water. Second, he discussed the installation of automated water meters,
which will allow residents to monitor their water usage via an app. Anderson presented a
promotional video on the new metering technology, which will be available to customers in the
coming weeks. He also noted extended hours at the KID office, now open from 7:30 AM to
5:30 PM. He talked about participating in the parade, thanking the city for their support, and
sharing Congressman Owens’ support for the winners of their recent contest.

B. Kearns Library - None

C. MyKearns Community Coalition
Kristen Dietz shared updates on several grant applications. The Coalition applied for a
parenting program grant to support community-based family engagement initiatives. They also
sought funding for youth activity programs and are awaiting a response on a Peer Court grant
application. Kristen highlighted recent community involvement at events, including Hometown
Night and the Pacific Islander Month kickoff, and ongoing partnerships to advance a
community garden project.
Mayor Bush spoke with Josh on a legislative situation, noting that they are going to ask
MyKearns Community Coalition to lead that project.

Renee Plant announced the schedule for the SCRAP Program, noting that dumpsters would

Wasatch Front Waste Recycling District’s additional rental services, such as green waste
trailers and bulk waste trailers, and emphasized keeping construction materials out of recycling
bins. She also discussed the QR vouchers accessible online and reminded everyone to keep
construction items out of recycling bins.
