       6.2     Healthy Bluffdale Coalition Strategic Plan. (Presenter^ Brishton Wilson),

Healthy Bluffdale Coalition Chair, Brighton Wilson introduced prospective Board Member, Camille
Hawkins, and stated that they will be presenting the Strategic Plan they developed through the
Coalitions Lite grant. The mission ofthe Healthy Bluffdale Coalition is to create a resilient, healthy
community by fostering health and overall wellness, connecting youth, parents, families, and
community members, and by promoting risk prevention through evidence-based initiatives.

Ms. Hawkins reported that over the past year, they have been working through the Coalitions Lite
grant to identify health concerns in the community and create strategic initiatives to address those
issues. The main priority identified for 2024 is mental health, with an emphasis on connections. This
topic was chosen due to the very specific data collected from community members.

Ms. Wilson stated that in partnership with the Health Department,they found that Bluffdale's Adverse
Childhood Experiences("ACE")scores are higher than Utah averages. Contributing factors include
a lack of connections to family or community and lack of commitment to schools. Through an
extensive evaluation process and with the support of the Salt Lake County Health Department, they
were able to determine what initiatives would be most impactful for the community and have chosen
to focus on improving mental health through creating and strengthening connections to family,
community, and schools, and using those strategic, evidence-based initiatives as they focus on
prevention moving forward. She indicated that the focus is in line with Bluffdale's motto of "Life,
Connected."


Ms. Hawkins stated that existing resources were identified and added to the Healthy Bluffdale
Coalition website.   The first strategy they were focusing on was providing information through
education, which could be through presentations, workshops,or seminars to help increase knowledge

Senior Planner, Caitlin Tubbs presented the Staff Report and displayed a photograph of the State of
Utah's first penitentiary, which was built in 1855, and Sugarhouse Park. As the suburbs began to

fastest-growing counties in the state, and as such Bluffdale and neighboring communities have been
feeling those development pressures.


Camp Williams was established at the tum of the 20th century. Bluffdale was settled in the 1880s
and Bluffdale and Camp Williams have coexisted for over a century. Bluffdale is proud to support
veterans and active service members at Camp Williams and Bluffdale City. It recognizes that Camp
Williams has a vital mission to continue training active service members to protect the people ofboth
the State ofUtah and the United States. As Bluffdale works to accommodate the current development

demand,it is also seeking to collaborate with Camp Williams to ensure that they are able to continue

In the most recent legislative session, the State passed House Bill 256 regarding military-compatible
Land Use amendments. The bill requires municipalities to collaborate with military installations
within their jurisdictions and adopt a Compatible Use Plan for all areas located within 5,000 feet of
military installations. The plan must be adopted by July 1, 2025. In addition to developing and
adopting the plan, Bluffdale is also required to provide notice to the Utah Department of Veterans
and Military Affairs ("UDVMA") of any Land Use applications, with the exception of Building
Permits, received for properties within that 5,000-foot buffer. That includes zoning amendments.
General Plan amendments, and site plans. The UDVMA then has a 90-day response period in which
they will provide a written response stating whether they find the proposal to be compatible or
incompatible with Camp Williams' mission. Once the response is received, the City is able to move
forward and take final action on the application. The statute also requires the City to consider its
Compatible Land Use Plan when making those decisions.

Ms.Tubbs presented a map ofthe affected areas ofBluffdale, which covers the southern halfofPerry
Farms and south of 15200 South, as well as a portion ofthe gravel pits and Camp Williams itself.

The West Traverse Mountain Compatibility Area Study was completed in 2022 and 2023. The effort
was spearheaded by the Moimtainland Association of Governments("MAG"). It was created from
existing data from the 2012 Joint Land Use Study that Bluffdale participated in with Camp Williams.
The process also included representatives from Camp Williams and the Utah National Guard, as well
as elected officials, staff, and members ofthe public of partner communities. The study went above
and beyond what is currently required by statutes and considered all land uses within five miles of
Camp Williams. It is the tool that the UDVMA uses to determine if something is compatible or
incompatible with their mission.


Several factors were considered and then narrowed down to the ones that would have a direct impact
on surrounding communities, including trespassing concerns, housing availability, public utility
infrastructure, etc. Larger impacts addressed by the study include:

it into the General Plan, they can go through that process at that time. She noted that Herriman City
has adopted it as an element oftheir General Plan.
