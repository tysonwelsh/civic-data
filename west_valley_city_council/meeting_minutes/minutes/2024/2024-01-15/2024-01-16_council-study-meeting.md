A.   ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING THE WEST VALLEY CITY
     POLICE DEPARTMENT'S ACCEPTANCE OF A JUSTICE ASSISTANCE
     GRANT (JAG) THROUGH THE U.S. DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE
     PROGRAMS
     Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council
     Meeting scheduled February 6, 2024, in order for the City Council to hear and
     consider public comments regarding the West Valley City Police Department's
     Acceptance of a Justice Assistance Grant (JAG) through the U.S. Department of Justice
     Programs.

       Proposed Resolution 24-16 related to the proposal to be considered by the City
       Council subsequent to the public hearing, was discussed as follows:

       ACTION: RESOLUTION NO. 24-16, AUTHORIZE WEST VALLEY CITY
       TO ACCEPT THE EDWARD BYRNE MEMORIAL JUSTICE ASSISTANCE
       GRANT
       Colleen Jacobs, Police Chief, discussed proposed Resolution 24-16 that would
       authorize West Valley City to Accept the Edward Byrne Memorial Justice Assistance
       Grant.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

              The U.S. Department of Justice Program is awarding the West Valley City
              Police Department the 2023 Edward Byrne Memorial Justice Assistance
              Grant (J.A.G.). The allowable expenses of the grant include the purchase of
              equipment and the funding of training and programs they approve through
              the application process. The funds are allocated to West Valley City based
    MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JANUARY 16, 2024
                           -3-


              on a formula that considers the population of West Valley City and our part
              one crime statistics. There is separate funding allocated to each state to
              ensure fairness. The J.A.G. Program allows states and local governments to
              implement otherwise unfunded programs designed to improve the criminal
              justice system.

              The West Valley City Police Department Investigations Units, Patrol
              Division, Special Operations Units, SWAT Team, Intelligence Unit, and
              Evidence Unit are in need of updated software and equipment. Department
              funds are not sufficient to cover the additional expense of equipping these
              units with the new or updated equipment or software and funds under this
              grant award will assist with this immensely. This equipment and software
              will increase the department’s ability to respond to, prepare for, and/or
              prevent crime or disorder in our community.

       The City Council will consider Resolution 24-16 at the Regular Council Meeting
       scheduled February 6, 2024 at 6:30 P.M.

ORDINANCE 24-04: AMEND SECTION 2-1-106 OF THE WEST VALLEY CITY
MUNICIPAL CODE TO AMEND CERTAIN REQUIREMENTS CONCERNING
THE ELECTION OF A MAYOR PRO TEMPORE
Nichole Camac, City Recorder, presented proposed Ordinance 24-04 that would amend
Section 2-1-106 of the West Valley City Municipal Code to Amend Certain Requirements
Concerning the Election of a Mayor Pro Tempore.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Section 2-1-106 of the West Valley City Municipal Code establishes requirements
       for the Council to elect a Mayor Pro Tempore. The current requirement is that a
       Mayor Pro Tempore shall be elected at the City Council meeting following the
       expiration of the Mayor’s term of office. This ordinance would amend Section 2-
       1-106 to require the election of a Mayor Pro Tempore every two years.

The City Council will consider Ordinance 24-04 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
January 23, 2024 at 6:30 P.M

Coby Wilson, Public Works, presented proposed Resolution 24-17 that would approve
Modification No. 2 to a Federal Aid Agreement Between the City and the Utah Department of

      Transportation for the Cross Towne Trail.

      Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
      follows:

             The West Valley City Engineering Division has finalized the design for a segment
             of the Cross Towne Trail along Parkway Boulevard. This project will close gaps in
             the trail between Bangerter Highway and 2700 West. Improvements include the
             addition of sidewalk where needed along with minor roadway widening to
             accommodate new bike lanes in Parkway Blvd. In addition, a pavement
             preservation treatment will be applied to the entire roadway within the project
             limits.

             The final project estimate ($2,764,200) exceeds the authorized amount of the
             previously executed federal aid agreement ($2,384,200) resulting in the need to
             modify the agreement.
             The City previously applied for and received $806,130 of federal Transportation
             Alternative Program (TAP) funds and $1,350,000 of County Transportation funds
             for use on this project.
             TAP funds require a local government match of 6.77% ($54,575) and the City is
             responsible for the difference between the available funding and the project total
             cost. The additional $380,000 required with this modification will be paid with
             local transportation funds.

             The right of way for this project has been cleared. It is anticipated that this project
             will be advertised for bids in February and that construction will begin in the spring
             of 2024.

      Councilmember Harmon verified that the plan is finalized. Coby replied yes.

A.  WESTFEST DISCUSSION
    Jamie Young, Parks and Recreation Director, indicated that the rules for insurance
    were changed in 2023 as they applied to vendors at the WestFest annual event. She
    indicated that these changes would require that individual vendors provide their
    own insurance policy. Jamie noted that this is difficult for many vendors because
    most of them are small businesses who can’t afford insurance policies. She
    indicated that after discussion with the WestFest Committee, it was determined that
    the best course of action would be to move the festival under the City’s umbrella
    so that it could provide insurance for the event. She noted that while WestFest is
    now under the City, nothing has changed internally. Jamie indicated that the only
    change has been that contracts now go through the City instead of the Committee.
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JANUARY 16, 2024
                            -7-


       Councilmember Nordfelt asked if there is anything the City can do to further
       support WestFest. Steve Vincent, WestFest Committee Member, replied no. He
       indicated that the Committee has always had a great partnership with the City.
       Councilmember Huynh asked if more committee members are needed. Steve
       replied that the Committee is always looking for more members and volunteers.
       Mayor Lang asked how much the City contributes for fireworks. Mr. Vincent
       replied approximately $17,000. He noted that sound and tents tend to be the most
       expensive part of the event. Councilmember Harmon asked if there is more impact
       on staff now that the event falls under the City’s umbrella. Jamie replied that the
       only increase that occurred was the routing and review of contracts between her
       and Legal but she doesn’t foresee that as an ongoing increase. She noted that the
       change wasn’t due to cost but was just the impact of requiring every vendor to have
       individual insurance policies. Mr. Vincent noted that Police and Fire have been a
       huge help at the WestFest event each year. Councilmember Huynh asked if the City
       would like to see more vendors at the event. Jamie replied that there isn’t much
       space for more vendors and added that it’s more beneficial to the success of the
       vendors if there aren’t excess booths.

       The Mayor and Council had no further questions or concerns.

F.     COUNCIL MEETING SCHEDULE
       John Flores, Interim Assistant City Manager, stated that the Council had expressed
       interest in potentially adjusting the Council meeting schedule and reducing the
       number of meetings to twice per month. He indicated that after discussing this with
       staff, there was no concern with this if the Council would like to proceed. He noted
       that staff would just need to adjust deadlines. Nichole Camac, City Recorder,
       indicated that this action would not take an ordinance change but would be a policy
       direction from City Council. She indicated that the only thing that would need to
       be adjusted is the Annual Meeting Schedule. She provided a brief run down of other
       City Council schedules after inquiry by Mayor Lang.

       Councilmember Christensen stated that he is fine with whatever the Council would
       prefer but would like there to be consistency for public comment for residents.
       Councilmember Nordfelt stated that he would prefer to keep the schedule as is so
       that the Council is available for public comment each week. Councilmember
       Huynh, Councilman Harmon, and Councilman Fitisemanu agreed. After further
       discussion, the Council decided to hold a Regular Meeting each week, regardless
       of whether regular meeting agenda items are scheduled.
