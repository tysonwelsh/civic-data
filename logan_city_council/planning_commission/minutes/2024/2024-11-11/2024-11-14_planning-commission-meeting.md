PC 24-046 Logan Central Cottage [Conditional Use Permit] Don A. White/Intrail Holdings LLC,
authorized agent/owner is requesting a Conditional Use Permit to operate a three-bedroom Short
Term Rental within the entire home located at 171 East 200 South in the Traditional Neighborhood
Residential (NR-6) zone; TIN 02-007-0008 (Wilson Neighborhood).

CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL

1. All standard conditions of approval will be recorded with the Conditional Use Permit and are
available in the Community Development Department.

2. This Conditional Use Permit authorizes a two to three (2-3) bedroom short-term rental
occupying the full single-family home.

3. This Conditional Use Permit authorizes two (2) people per bedroom with two (2) additional
individuals for a maximum of six to eight (6-8) occupants in the STR.

4. A short-term rental may not be the subject of multiple rental contracts by separate individuals,
groups, or parties for the same night or nights.

5. Occupancy for the single-family home portion of the structure shall comply with LDC
regulations.

6. A minimum of three (3) parking stalls are required for the STR. All parking areas shall be located
off-street, outside the front and corner yard setback, and paved with an approved hard surface.
The three (3) total parking stalls shall meet minimum LDC dimensions (9x18) per stall.

7. Parking of recreational vehicles, trailers, boats, ATVs, or similar vehicles associated with the
STR shall be legally parked on-site and are not permitted to be parked on-street.

8. Rental contracts for the STR shall be for 30 days or less.

9. Rental contracts shall not be offered to the same individual, group, or party for more than two
consecutive rental contracts in a six (6) month period. The owner shall maintain rental records
available for review and inspection by Logan City.

10. Quiet times shall comply with the City noise ordinance of 10 pm to 8 am.

11. A Logan City building/fire inspection is required which may result in building remodeling and
building permits prior to the issuance of a business license.


12. A Logan City business license is required before a rental contract can be issued.

13. The STR shall comply with local and state Building, Health, Fire, Safety, Maintenance, and
Nuisance Codes prior to operating.

14. Exterior lighting shall be concealed source, down-cast and reviewed and approved prior to the
issuance of a building permit and shall comply with current LDC regulations.

15. No signs are approved with this Conditional Use Permit. All signage shall be approved and
permitted by staff in accordance with the Land Development Code.

16. No fences are approved with this Conditional Use Permit. All fences shall be approved and
permitted by staff in accordance with the Land Development Code.

EINDINGS FOR APPROVAL

1. The proposed project conforms to the requirements of the Land Development Code and is listed
as a conditional use in the Use Table.

2. This STR meets the City’s STR density and location parameters as specified in Chapter
17.37.130.

3. As conditioned, the use is compatible with the surrounding land uses and will not interfere with
the use and enjoyment of adjoining or area properties.

4. The streets providing access and other infrastructure to the subject property have adequate
capacities and level of service for the conditional use.

5. The Planning Commission has substantiated the findings for a Conditional Use Permit in Chapter
17.42.050.

Motion: Commissioner Heare made a motion to approve all items under the consent agenda.
Commissioner Lucero seconded the motion.

PC 24-045 Latitude 41 [Design Review & Subdivision Permit] Nathan Leishman/Latitude 41 Land
LLC, authorized agent/owner is requesting a Design Review & Subdivision Permit to subdivide two
properties into three lots and build one 6,000-square-foot retail building and two four-story, multi-
family residential buildings with 109 total residential units and one 750 square-foot commercial unit
located at 774 & 776 South Main Street in the Mixed Use (MU) zone; TIN 02-065-0043; -0015
(Woodruff Neighborhood).

Staff: Aimee Egbert, City Planner reviewed the request for a subdivision and design review permit
at approximately 790 South Main Street to subdivide two properties totaling 4.64 acres into three lots
and construct one 6,000 square foot commercial/retail building on lot one, one 54,800 square foot
mixed-use building that will include 750 square feet of commercial space on the ground floor and 54
residential units on lot two, and one 54,800 square foot multi-family apartment building which will
include 55 residential units on lot three. The project includes a total of 109 residential units with a
variety of residential amenities including a clubhouse, pickleball court, hot tub, dog park, and a
pavilion. The proposed project also includes an approximate 20,000 square-foot road dedication for
the 80 East roadway that will run between lots two and three and eventually connect to 700 South to
the north and Golf Course Road to the south.


A. Egbert stated that the Land Development Code (LDC) requires 60% building frontage along the
street. The commercial building on lot one adjacent to Main Street is proposed at 52%. The
proponent requested that the detached, freestanding 17.75 square foot pergola & patio south of the
proposed commercial building be included in the overall frontage calculations. If the proposed 17.75-
foot pergola is included, the project would meet the 60% frontage requirement.

The base residential density in the MU zone is 20 dwelling units per acre. Density bonuses are
permitted in the MU zone through a number of density bonus paths, and depending on the type of
project enhancement, an additional 30 units per acre may be permitted. The proposal contains a
total of 109 units on the 4.64 acre site which equates to an overall density of 23.5 units per acre.
This number exceeds the base MU density of 20 units by approximately 16 units, or 3.4 units per
acre. The proponent is proposing to increase the amount of useable outdoor space in order to utilize
the density bonus.

The LDC allows for a density bonus of up to 30 additional units per acre if the required amount of
useable outdoor space is increased by at least 150%. The applicant is asking for an additional 3.4
units per acre, which is 17% of the potential maximum density bonus. The required useable outdoor
space for this project is 10% or 20,212 square feet. As proposed the project includes a total of 27,138
square feet of useable outdoor space, representing a 134% increase in the required base amount of
useable outdoor space. A. Egbert stated that the Planning Commission will need to determine if this
specific type of density bonus can be scaled.

The total parking required for the proposed project is 230 stalls. The proposed project has provided
a total of 215 parking stalls which is a deficit of 15 stalls. The proposed parking is broken down as
follows: 184 parking stalls for the residential component (deficit of 8 parking stalls) and 31 parking
stalls for the commercial & restaurant space (deficit of 7 parking stalls).

The LDC allows for a parking reduction of up to 25% for vertical mixed-use projects with the approval
of an alternative parking plan. The applicant has submitted an alternative parking plan that is
requesting a 20% parking reduction for the commercial and a 5% reduction for residential.

In the applicant's parking study, site visits were made in March and early April to 3 apartment
complexes in the early morning, afternoon, and late evening. The study concluded that, on average,
fewer parking stalls were being used than was required by Logan City’s standards and that this
project proposal could safely reduce the parking requirement by 5-10%. The applicant is requesting
to reduce the parking requirements for studio units from 1.5 to 1.25 stalls/unit and is proposing to
allow for the sharing of parking between the residential and commercial uses. The applicant is
suggesting that the parking demand for either residential uses or commercial uses are offset by the
typical hours of need.

Commissioner Lucero asked if the complexes visited during the parking study were at full occupancy.
A. Egbert said the parking study references three apartment complexes that were studied, one was
at 100% occupancy, one was at 90% occupancy, and they were not able to get occupancy
information on the third.

Commissioner McNamara asked what days of the week the parking studies were conducted. A.
Egbert said they were completed on a Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday.

Commissioner Lucero asked if the commercial businesses to the north are parked to regulation
because that area seems to be under-parked.


Commissioner Lucero asked if the Commission is able to approve a proportional increase in
additional outdoor space for the density bonus. There was a discussion between the Commission
and staff about whether the LDC permitted or prohibited proportional density bonuses.

Mohammed Abdullahi. Logan City Attorney read LDC Chapter 17.34.050.G: Partial density bonuses
can be granted based on the type and amount of the proposed bonus elements being undertaken.
For example, a project that includes a parking terrace sized to accommodate 25% of the required
parking stalls, could qualify for an additional 10 residential units per acre.

Mike DeSimone said this code allows the Commission the discretion to determine whether a
proportionate density bonus is allowed.

Proponent: Nate Leishman spoke about their project planning process over the last year, trying to
meet City Code requirements, and stated that they are asking for a proportional increase in density
with their request for an additional 3.4 units per acre representing an approximate 17% increase in
density. They are providing an additional 37% of outdoor space in return.

N. Leishman spoke about their request for a reduction in parking and said, they are requesting a 17%
parking reduction based on the public transit that is nearby, the parking study showing similar
apartment buildings with 10 to 12% excess parking, and the ability for commercial and residential
uses to share parking because their typical hours of parking need are offset.

Commissioner Guth asked if they are going to lease the rental units according to available parking.
N. Leishman said that each rental lease will address if there is parking provided for that unit or not.

Justin Campbell, architect on the project asked if the apartment facades facing 80 East are in
compliance as proposed with the fenestration requirement. Commissioner Doutre said the
commission will discuss the fenestration after public comment.

Public: Denzil Anderson said he is concerned with the extra traffic this project will bring to the existing
portion of 80 East which is a narrow street.

Jeannie Simmonds, City Council asked if a density bonus in the Mixed Use zone is scalable, if a
building can be counted toward useable open space, if each lot is required to meet the residential
and commercial building percentage requirements of the MU zone, why the proposed 80 East does
not connect to the existing 80 East, why this project only has one access, through the commercial
businesses to the north, and if there is a crosswalk connecting the project to the bus stop across
Main Street.

Josh Molitor said that Logan City does not need 109 more rentals.

Paul Cressell said that 80 East needs to be developed to the south to connect with Golf Course Road.
Steven Taylor said there is a crosswalk on Main Street that connects this project to the bus stop, and
that more housing is what Logan City needs. Aimee presented the most recent aerial photo of this
area showing the signalized crosswalk at the “Y” intersection immediately adjacent to this site.

Bruce Leishman said that it is likely the apartments will be at 85 to 95% occupancy.

Commission: Commissioner Doutre said they need to see plans for the proposed gazebo on lot one
before the Commission can determine if the gazebo can count towards the frontage requirement.


Commissioner Doutre asked if the applicant counted the clubhouse towards the usable outdoor
space. A. Egbert said they are not counting the clubhouse towards the usable outdoor space.

M. Abdullahi said that LDC Chapter 17.34.050.F states that, “density bonuses are cumulative in that
a proponent could propose to incorporate multiple bonus elements into the project design in order to
qualify for a density bonus provided the total density bonus does not exceed 30 additional units per
acre”.

M. Abdullahi said that the proposed additional 34% of useable outdoor space should qualify the
project for some additional units per acre. Commissioner Guth said that 34% (the additional outdoor
space proposed) of the 150% (additional outdoor space required to receive the bonus of 20 units per
acre, as stated in the LDC) is 23% which would allow the 4.5 additional units per acre.

Commissioner Doutre said she is not comfortable reducing the parking as proposed because the
majority of the parking shortage is within lot one and two where the commercial uses are, and
because the commercial businesses to the north are underparked. Commissioner Guth said she
would be ok with a reduction to the residential parking because there are 14 studio units.
Commissioner Lucero mentioned that parking can be controlled at the leasing stage with the
apartment units. M. DeSimone said the commission may want to ask for a parking management plan
included in the alternative parking plan.

Commissioner Lucero asked if 80 East will eventually connect to 465 North in Providence. Darren
Farar, Logan City Engineer said that there are no current plans for a connection to the Providence
street, however it can be looked into. D. Farar spoke about 700 South and 80 East both having the
capacity for the level of traffic the proposed project will add to the area, and the multiple access points
to this project with the required cross access easements with the businesses to the north.

The Commission discussed pedestrian connectivity on the site and stated that the drawings were a
little unclear as to how residents moved around the site.

M. DeSimone addressed the public comment regarding Logan not needing more rentals and said
that land decisions cannot be made based on ownership versus rental.

Commissioner Heare asked if the development will be phased or built all at once to address the
public comment regarding the project meeting the residential and commercial percentage
requirements of the MU zone.

A. Egbert said they are not planning on phasing the project. N. Leishman confirmed that they are
planning to build everything at the same time.

Commissioner Doutre said that the fenestration is fine as proposed because they meet the required
50% including the building’s projections.

The Commission discussed continuing the project to the next meeting to provide additional time for
the applicant to submit the following items:

« Parking management plan for the Alternate Parking Plan.

« Gazebo Plans.

« Pedestrian Connectivity Plan.

Motion: Commissioner Peterson made a motion to continue PC 24-045 Latitude 41 to the December
12" meeting. Commissioner McNamara seconded the motion.


Moved: E. Peterson Seconded: S. McNamara Approved: 7-0

PC _ 24-047 Willow Lakes Phase 5 — PD Workshop Brett Jensen/Willow Lake Holdings LLC,
authorized agent/owner is requesting a Planned Development Overlay for Phase 5 of the Willow Lake
Community. The proposed concept layout for Phase 5 includes a 40,000 SF fitness/Boat Club, five
Beachfront Homes intended for short-term rental, 160 Luxury Condominiums within four, three-story
buildings, and a 60-bed Assisted Living Center located at approximately 1100 South 1400 West in
the Traditional Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) zone; TIN 02-080-0007; -0008; -0010, 02-081-0004,
02-338-8001 (Woodruff Neighborhood).

Russ Holley, City Planner reviewed the Willow Lakes concept layout for the 12-acre Phase 5 of the
Willow Lakes project. Phases 1-4 have already been approved and platted. This proposal is for a
Planned Development Overlay (PDO), which will eventually result in both a Land Development Code
(LDC) Amendment and a rezone application for the overlay zone.

The PDO process requires an initial concept plan review by the Planning Commission, City Council,
and Neighborhood Councils for informal review and feedback

R. Holley reviewed the four areas of the Phase 5 of the concept plan:

Boat Club & Fitness Commercial Building

The concept plan shows a three-story, 40,000-square-foot, multi-use commercial building with upper-
floor observation decks located along the north shore of the Big Wake and Tube Lake. The
commercial building is planned for a Boat Club area, Clubhouse/Events area, Fitness/Gym area, and
office space. There are also outdoor swimming pools, hot tubs, and an artificial wave simulator just
offshore.

Commissioner Doutre asked if the commercial area would be open to the public or a private
neighborhood amenity. R. Holley said the commercial area will be open to the public with a
membership.

Beach Homes

Five beachfront single-family homes are proposed just east of the fitness center along the Big Wake
and Tube Lake north beach. These five homes are being proposed as short-term rentals. The homes
have vehicular access from the north through the fitness center parking lot and the south side of the
homes has direct access to the beach.

Luxury Condominiums

Four, three-story condominium buildings are proposed further east of the beach homes along the
north shore of Slalom Ski Lake. Each building contains 40 dwelling units ranging from 1 to 4
bedrooms for a total of 160 units. There is structured parking on the ground floor of the east and
west wings of each building and upper-floor balconies on both the north and south facades.

Assisted Living


Along 1000 West at the east side of phase 5, the concept plan proposes a two-story, 60-bed assisted
living building. The assisted living building is shown at lower heights and is somewhat separate from
the other areas of phase 5 being located across the street and having no associated lake frontage.

R. Holley reviewed the following points of discussion:

« Are the proposed Multi-Family structures and uses appropriate in the Woodruff
Neighborhood?

¢ Is 40,000 SF of commercial uses compatible? The Public Fitness/Boat Club would create
more traffic, noise, and larger parking lot footprints.

e Are Short Term Rentals concentrated in one located appropriate?

e The proposal appears to lack connections from residential to commercial uses, or to the larger
trail network.

* Condos are setback 10 feet from the street. If single-family residential went in across the
street the setback would need to be 25 feet.

Commissioner Guth asked where the access points are to the development. R. Holley said there are
three points of access to Willow Lakes: 1000 West, 1400 West, and a stub to the north from 1100
South at approximately 1300 West for future access and connections to the north.

The Commission discussed the development of 1400 West. R. Holley said that when Willow Lakes
Phase 1-4 was approved, one of the conditions was to complete 1400 West from 1100 South to 900
South.

Commissioner Doutre asked if the traffic impact from Phase-5 would be significant enough to require
1400 West to be developed further north to connect to 600 South. R. Holley said that will be looked
into and the developer will be required to build the amount of roadway that is proportionate to the
impact of the development.

There was a discussion on how the public was notified about this workshop and making sure a
neighborhood meeting is held to get feedback on the project from the local neighbors and Logan City
residents.

Bret Nelson with Willow Lakes spoke about the Phase-5 plan and the need for commercial uses in
order to allow public access to the amenities and to help spread out the costs associated with
providing the amenities. B. Nelson spoke about the clubhouse offering event space for up to 200
guests, and short-term rentals only being allowed in the beach homes, not within phases 1-4.

B. Nelson spoke about the parking and said they have enough parking stalls to meet the LDC
requirements for each use and are not asking for any parking reductions.

B. Nelson spoke about the condo setback and said that 1100 South was a 66-foot right of way,
however, with the development the paved section was moved 15-20 feet onto the Willow Lakes
property creating an 85-foot right of way to allow for a much wider park strip for the property to the
north, creating a larger buffer area for future residential across the street.

B. Nelson spoke about the height of the condo buildings being approximately 45 feet tall, mid-gable,
and being able to change the roof style and lower the parking floor height if needed to better match
the future residential zone across 1100 South.

B. Nelson said there are multiple access points around both lakes to get from the road to the beach
areas as well as paths in front of the condos next to the street and behind the condos next to the


lake. Commissioner Peterson said it would be helpful to see on the plans where the pedestrian
accesses and paths are.

Commissioner McNamara asked if there will be a pedestrian connection to the Assisted Living area.
B. Nelson said they are planning a crosswalk to connect the areas and a shuttle to transport residents
to the clubhouse.

B. Nelson spoke about the water amenities and said there is one large swimming pool and two hot
tubs on the beach area near the clubhouse and one hot tub and a viewing deck on the island in the
Slalom Ski Lake. The surf feature is a deep-water standing wave that is 65 feet wide and can
accommodate full-size surfboards.

B. Nelson spoke about the beach homes being built as rentals with more bedrooms and large
gathering areas. Each beach home will be 5,000 to 6,000 square feet and designed according to the
CC&Rs with four-sided architecture.

B. Nelson spoke about building the condos to reach a broader demographic, offering 4-bedroom
units, which are needed in the area. The intent for the condos is to help eliminate some of the
transiency that comes with multi-family, by offering units that people want to stay in for longer periods.

B. Nelson spoke about the density and said with the future residential to the north and industrial and
mixed residential to the south, they do not feel that the density they are proposing is out of character
for the area. The other consideration is that of the total Willow Lakes 186 acre project area, there
are approximately 115 acres of open space. Commissioner Doutre asked about a trail through the
open space. B. Nelson said there have been discussions between the City and the development
regarding installing a bridge on the north end of Trapper Park and continuing Logan City’s Blue Trail
through the west side of the development to eventually connect to 600 South. Willow Lakes will
provide two access points to get to the new trail from the street. Commissioner Doutre said the trail
development could be required as part of the density considerations.

Commissioner Peterson asked about 1400 West. B. Nelson said they are not against developing
1400 West from 1100 South to 600 South.

Commissioner Heare asked about the traffic entering 1000 West from the development. B. Nelson
said that UDOT is allowing a right-hand turn lane for southbound traffic to enter the development and
left turns exiting the development. The future traffic light planned for 1000 South will help open gaps
in the traffic to exit the development.

Commissioner Doutre asked what benefit this PDO would offer to the broader City. B. Nelson said
that the lake, Health & Fitness Center, and boating will be accessible to the public and spoke about
the range of price points they offer for housing.

Commissioner Doutre asked about the condos meeting the 25-foot front setback on 1200 South. B.
Nelson said that would limit the parking and possibly not allow the 4-bedroom units. Nelson spoke
about the dramatic articulation in the condo buildings with portions of the building set back 50 to 60
feet creating large recessed courtyards to offset the smaller setback. Mike DeSimone mentioned
that there may be a way to design the condos with less building mass next to the street. B. Nelson
said they could move portions of the condo buildings back and only have 30% within the front setback.

Commissioner Guth spoke about the importance of a neighborhood meeting to gather feedback from
the neighbors before the formal project returns to the Planning Commission. Commissioner Guth
also stated that she would like to see a pedestrian connection plan.


Commissioner Heare said there is a need for this type of condo that does not look like an apartment.

The Commission discussed adding height transitions to the condo buildings and additional
landscaping for a better transition to the future residential use across the street.

The Commission reviewed the following suggestions brought up during the discussion:
e Neighborhood Meeting with the developer.
e Pedestrian Connection/trail plan.
e Extension of 1400 West Street
e Condo Setback

The Commission also discussed the next steps in the review of this proposal.

Meeting adjourned at 8:23 p.m.
