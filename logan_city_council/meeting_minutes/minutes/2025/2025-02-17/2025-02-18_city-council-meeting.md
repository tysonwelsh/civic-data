MAYOR/STAFF REPORTS:
Update on First Dam Tank Transmission Line Alignment – Mayor Daines (6:00)

Mayor Daines read the following letter regarding the First Dam Tank Transmission Line
Alignment.

 Update on First Dam Tank Transmission Line Alignment, February 18, 2025

Since we discussed the project last, we’ve contracted with Whitaker Construction
as the general contractor for the transmission line and Canyon Road
improvements. Whitaker is a highly regarded heavy construction contractor in
our region with many successful projects of this type.

As you know, JUB Engineers evaluated seven possible alignments for the
transmission line based on specific metrics. We had a well-attended public
information meeting on December 5th where we collected lots of comments from
the public and answered many questions. In the weeks since, we’ve received and
considered additional comments both in support of the project and expressing
concern about specific aspects of the project and have considered any reasonable
suggestions for viability. We’ve also been waiting for the final geotechnical
report, which is now complete.

We sincerely appreciate the public engagement and involvement. We’ve used the
process described to ensure we make the best possible decision for the overall
community. That takes into account both design, cost and constructability. Each
alternate alignment or modification considered comes with significant cost and
additional disruption to more homes and properties.

The least expensive alternative would impact 90 additional properties and cost an
extra $2.4M just for the pipe and installation. With the added right of way
purchases required and additional utility relocations for the longer route, it could
easily double that cost.

As most citizens are aware, water rates have already increased as we work to
upgrade aging water infrastructure. To put it in context, an additional $4.8M
would require at least a 2.5 percent water rate increase for every household in the

city over the 20 year life of the bond, all to save seventeen trees that are not in the
best of health and are likely nearing the end of their life.

With this project we also have a significant opportunity to improve the overall
safety, function and beauty of the entire Canyon Road corridor for a wide cross
section of users. This corridor is listed in the Transportation Master Plan as a
Collector Road with an 80 foot wide right of way. This project would narrow that
planned road width which will slow traffic, could implement long-planned bike
and pedestrian connectivity, and potentially plant over 200 new trees, investing in
the future and long term beauty of this area.

As with all large projects, these decisions come with trade-offs and are not always
popular or preferred by everyone. As Mayor, my job is to make the decision that
is best for the entire community.

With this additional information, we’ve confirmed the recommended alignment for
the water transmission line along the north side of Canyon Road is the most
economical and beneficial for the entire community. Even though this pipeline
alignment requires the removal of the widely discussed Ash trees along Canyon
Road, I believe this is the best decision going forward.

As we continue the design of the pipeline, it is our intent to work through the
Wilson Neighborhood Council on how the roadway will be put back together
(which is scheduled for 2026), including potential curb and gutter, sidewalk or
trail options, and park strips with trees.

You as council members have already appropriated $20M towards this significant
and necessary infrastructure project. Sometime in the coming weeks, as we
finalize more design details, we’ll be coming to you with a budget to complete the
project based on the decision I’ve outlined tonight. At that time, following our
typical budgetary process, we will hold a public hearing on the budget to allow for
input from citizens.

Thank you to our residents for their engagement and to you, the City Council
members, for your substantial investment of time and energy as you’ve done your
own homework and talked with citizens about the project. I am asking tonight for
your head nod as we continue with the selected alignment for the transmission line
and design of the roadway improvements so that Public Works can begin
preparing the budget request for a future City Council agenda.

Chair Simmonds inquired if the Council desired to see the project come forward and a
budget be provided.

Councilmember M. Anderson refrained from making a comment at this time as he will be
impacted by the project because he lives on Canyon Road. He abstained from making any
comment.

Councilmember A. Anderson wished that the Canyon Road trees would not need to be
removed but also understood that they need to be removed in order to complete the
project. She stated that comments have been made both in favor and against especially
the fact that residents do not want to pay an increase in fees.

Vice Chair Johnson shared similar sentiments with Councilmember A. Anderson. He said
it is a difficult decision, but ultimately it is just as important to keep costs low for
citizens. The road also needed to be improved to better bike and pedestrian connectivity.

Chair Simmonds reiterated the fact this has not been any easy decision, but it is a
necessity. The road will remain the same size as it is today rather than being expanded
into a collector road. The pipe is critical, especially in the event of a fire to prevent what
occurred in Los Angeles. She recommended the proposed project move to the budget
stage so the Council may have an understanding of what the incurred costs will be.

Mayor Daines emphasized the fact that every alternative had been considered. However,
the dramatic increase in costs for the same benefit to citizens simply did not align. The
budget will come forward to the Council and there will be a public hearing where citizens
can comment. As the project design commences, the Wilson Neighborhood will also be
involved.

Landfill/Transfer Station Closure on Saturday, February 22 – Mayor Daines (17:20)

Mayor Daines announced that on Saturday, February 22 the Environmental Department
plans on closing the landfill and transfer station due to the SR-30 road construction and
closure. Waterline and sewer relocation at the intersection of 1400 W and SR-30 will
close access to the landfill and the culinary water will be shut off starting on Saturday,
February 22 morning through Sunday, February 23. The Environmental Department will
send out an announcement of the closure on social media and will post signs at the
landfill.

Planning Commission Update – Councilmember Simmonds

Chair Simmonds reported that the Planning Commission reviewed the proposed water
tank design and location. It was approved with a conditional use permit. USU commented
that solar panels could possibly be installed on the roof of the water tank and used to
create solar energy.

Board and Committee Reports – Vice Chair Johnson, Councilmember A. Anderson
and Councilmember López (19:10)

Vice Chair Johnson reported the Audit Committee met to select a new auditing company.
BPAC (Bicycle Pedestrian Advisory Committee) discussed Bike Week and are making
plans for their event starting on May 11, 2025.

RESAB (Renewable Energy and Sustainability Advisory Board) met and discussed their
bylaws which have not been approved by the City Council since the committee was
established in 2009. There will be changes to the bylaws and will be presented to the
Council at a future meeting.

The Ellis & Wilson Neighborhoods met; the Wilson Neighborhood discussed their
Neighborhood Master Plan. The Woodruff Neighborhood discussed the Willow Lakes
Planned Unit Development (PUD). The Adams Neighborhood discussed the addition of a
bike lane. The Legislative Policy Committee (LPC) met to discuss ongoing legislation
and the possible impact on cities and towns. Lastly, he attended a meeting with the Light
& Power Department to discuss local renewable energy options.

Councilmember A. Anderson reported that the Parks & Recreation Board, Public Arts
Committee, and Library Board did not meet this month. The Library Board at a prior
meeting discussed the ongoing library construction-related issues that continue to linger,
and that are still being addressed. All patrons using Hoopla will be limited to two items
per month in order for the library to remain on budget. The subscription is pay per use
regardless of whether the item is actually used, the library must pay for the item
borrowed.

The Adams Neighborhood Council met and discussed the new bike plan for 500 North
which will be shared with the neighborhood at their next meeting on April 16, 2025, at 7
p.m. at Adams Elementary. Lastly, she invited the public to attend a Utah Department of
Transportation (UDOT) meeting on February 25, from 4:30-6:30 p.m. at Mount Logan
Middle School. UDOT is evaluating improvements to enhance mobility on US-89/91
(Main Street).

Councilmember López was excused from tonight’s meeting and did not provide a report.

No further Council Business items were presented.

PUBLIC HEARING - Consideration of a proposed resolution indicating the Intent
to Adjust the Municipal Boundary between the City of Logan and Millville City –
Resolution 25-04 – Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director (32:41)

At the February 4, 2025 Council meeting, Community Development Director Mike
DeSimone addressed the Council. He stated that Logan City has received a request from
Global Properties (Zollinger) to adjust the Municipal Boundary between Logan and
Millville City. There are five separate tax parcels, 02-089-0014, 0015, 0025, 0033, and
0041 along with a building on building on parcel# 02-089- 0033 that cross the existing
recorded municipal boundary. This proposed action will adjust the municipal boundary to
match existing parcel boundaries.

The proposed resolution of intent signifies the City's willingness to formally pursue this
municipal boundary adjustment in this specific area and as directed by State Law. The
proposed municipal boundary adjustment affects the following properties:

Utah Municipal Code 10-2-419 governs the adjustment of common municipal
boundaries. Procedurally, (1) each municipal shall adopt a resolution indicating their
intent to adjust the common boundaries; (2) hold a hearing no less than 60 days after the
adoption of the resolution; (3) publish a public notice for three weeks in the newspaper
and on the Utah Public Notice Website; (4) hold a public hearing; (5) adopt an ordinance;
and (6) file with the County and the State. While each jurisdiction will hold their own
hearings, we are coordinating with Millville City on hearing dates and fulfilling
minimum noticing requirements. The proposed workshop & hearing dates on an adoption
Ordinance are April 15, 2025 and May 6, 2025.
