33   2.0    Review Business Session Agenda.
34
35   The Agenda was reviewed and discussed. There was one Business Item. Project CUP-23-012 was
36   a Conditional Use Permit request to operate a dental clinic at 1845 East Fort Union Boulevard.
37   The request was from Van Leeuwen Dental. Senior City Planner, Samantha DeSeelhorst, shared
38   an image of the subject property as well as a vicinity map. The property is close to the interstate
39   and is tucked behind Whole Foods Market. The property is located within the Neighborhood
40   Commercial (“NC”) Zone. All uses are considered conditional in the NC Zone, which was the
41   reason a Conditional Use Permit was required. She noted that the property is also within the
42   Gateway Overlay Zone. As a result, approval from the Architectural Review Commission
43   (“ARC”) would be required for any exterior work or site changes. There had been coordination
44   with the applicant on that separate process.
45

 1   Ms. DeSeelhorst reported that the proposal is to operate a dental clinic within an existing
 2   commercial building. The applicant, Van Leeuwen Dental, has practiced as a licensed business in
 3   the City since 1978. The practice outgrew the current space, which is further east on Fort Union
 4   Boulevard. There was a desire to relocate somewhere it could grow. The business use will consist
 5   of general dentistry, dental sleep treatment, laser dentistry, and surgery. Business hours are
 6   Monday through Friday from 7:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. There could also be special appointments on
 7   Saturdays if needed. The dental office will have 10 employees with approximately 30 patients per
 8   day who will be staggered.
 9
10   Information about the site was shared. Ms. DeSeelhorst reported that most of the modifications to
11   the site will be interior to transition the building from a bank to a dental clinic. The applicant
12   proposed the addition of new parking spaces and the demolition of the former bank drive-thru to
13   accommodate a rear entrance. The proposed site changes comply with the City Code in terms of
14   building setbacks, heights, parking requirements, and landscaping. Ms. DeSeelhorst reported that
15   the site already complies with the parking requirements but new parking spaces were proposed to
16   ensure that there is enough room for patients to come. City Staff discussed adding some circulation
17   signage to improve traffic flow on the site. There was also a request to provide measurements for
18   the Americans with Disabilities Act (“ADA”) stall and accessible route. It was anticipated that
19   those requests would be fully resolved before the plans were approved.
20
21   There were no public comments received on the application in advance of the Planning
22   Commission Meeting. The matter was scheduled for a public hearing during the Business Session.
23   Staff found the proposed use to meet all of the standards for issuing a Conditional Use Permit.
24   There were findings outlined in the Staff Report. Staff recommended approval with the following
25   conditions:
26
27          1.      The applicant shall obtain a Certificate of Design Compliance from the
28                  Architectural Review Commission (“ARC”) for site and building exterior changes.
29
30          2.      The Applicant shall comply with all Staff corrections, including those for ADA
31                  accessibility and site circulation.
32
33          3.      The Applicant shall obtain a Building Permit for any work requiring a permit.
34
35   Commissioner Anderson noted that overnight services are not permitted but that dental sleep
36   treatment is listed. She asked for clarification about that service. Commissioner Poulson stated
37   that dental sleep treatment can cover conscious sedation. Usually, a patient would be observed for
38   one or two hours. Those types of appointments are not normally scheduled at the end of the day,
39   which means the hours of operation are unlikely to be impacted.
40
41   Commissioner Anderson stressed the importance of appropriate signage coming in and out of the
42   site. She asked who reviewed the final submittal. Ms. DeSeelhorst clarified that Staff reviewed
43   the item when it came back. Commissioner Anderson wanted to better understand the parking
44   requirements and what is proposed. Ms. DeSeelhorst reported that the requirement is 10 parking
45   spaces on-site, however, the site currently has 16 parking spaces. In addition, the applicant wants
46   to add four more parking spaces, which would ultimately result in 20 parking spaces. There was

 1   a requirement for one ADA stall, which would be relocated, but preserved on site. The applicant
 2   seemed to have a good understanding of the amount of parking needed for the use.
 3
 4   Commissioner Smith noted that surgeries are referenced in the materials. He asked if it was
 5   necessary to further define that. Ms. DeSeelhorst stated that further clarification could be
 6   requested. In terms of use, there would need to be assurance that there is no after-hours or
 7   overnight care taking place. If dental surgery is performed within the standard operating hours,
 8   there would not be an issue with that from a use perspective. Commissioner Smith suggested that
 9   the language specify “oral surgery”. Ms. DeSeelhorst believed that was in the applicant's narrative.
10
11   Commissioner Steinman questioned whether there were any circulation concerns inside the
12   parking area. Ms. DeSeelhorst reported that the City Engineer asked that signage be added to
13   clarify the circulation. The City Engineer did not have any concerns or objections to the existing
14   widths but there was a desire to make the traffic pattern obvious to all. Commissioner Steinman
15   asked if it made sense to allow for a right-hand turn only at the exit. Ms. DeSeelhorst explained
16   that the Planning Commission could include a Condition of Approval asking the applicant to look
17   into that possibility with the City Engineer. However, if the City Engineer felt that was necessary
18   to include it, it likely would have been suggested already. Given that there is relatively slow traffic
19   volume, she did not believe a right-in-right-out was needed on the applicant site. There was
20   discussion regarding traffic volume and some of the surrounding uses. Ms. DeSeelhorst noted that
21   the site has been vacant for a few years. As a result, Staff believed the proposed use would be a
22   positive step forward and the practice has the potential to revitalize the existing site.
23
24   Commissioner Poulson asked about the hours of operation. Ms. DeSeelhorst explained that the
25   applicant proposed operating hours of 7:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. The City Code did not specifically
26   state that those needed to be the hours of operation but that there could not be after-hours use. That
27   is traditionally defined as after 7:00 p.m. Commissioner Poulson explained that occasionally with
28   a dental practice, someone needs to be seen immediately. Those emergency type of situations
29   might not fall within the hours of operation. He wondered if there would be an issue if this
30   occurred a few times a year. Ms. DeSeelhorst believed it would be an issue, technically speaking,
31   since there cannot be after-hours treatment. Commissioner Poulson suggested a Condition of
32   Approval to state that this type of situation can occur on occasion. Ms. DeSeelhorst clarified that
33   there could not be a Condition of Approval to allow that because the Code specifically states that
34   after-hours use is not permitted.
35
36   Chair Mills asked what could reasonably be requested in terms of improvements in that particular
37   section of Fort Union Boulevard. Ms. DeSeelhorst explained that it is not within the purview of
38   the City to require frontage improvements unless the site is fully redeveloped. Given that the
39   primary structure will remain and no significant modifications were to be done, it is not appropriate
40   to require frontage improvements. Staff reviewed the proposal against the Fort Union Area Master
41   Plan, which was the reason the front entrance would remain. Initially, there was a desire to remove
42   the front entrance and only utilize the rear entrance; however, in the Fort Union Area Master Plan,
43   there was a requirement for a street-facing entrance. She reiterated that frontage improvements,
44   such as wider sidewalks or a bicycle lane, would not be triggered in this case.

 6   if there was an opportunity to partner with the applicant so additional improvements could be made

 8   project or grant application for a certain area. One example was on the opposite side of Fort Union
 9   Boulevard between the two townhome projects. If something like that was possible in the future,
10   it was anticipated that there would be coordination with the property owner. At the current time,
11   there was no funding available to address that area.

13   Community and Economic Development Director, Michael Johnson, believed Chair Mills was
14   suggesting that funding be made available for future frontage improvements. He noted that this
15   would need to be a City Council initiative that is prioritized during the budgeting process.
16
17   Chair Mills referenced the second Condition of Approval. He wanted to see some specificity in
18   terms of what level of ADA compliance there needs to be. For instance, ramps, widths, doorway
19   sizes, restroom accessibility, and so on. There were a lot of layers to ADA accessibility.
20   Ms. DeSeelhorst recommended leaving the wording as presented because there would be an
21   opportunity to reference corrections from the Engineering and Building Departments. From an
22   Engineering Department standpoint, they were looking at how someone from the ADA stall would
23   access the building. They requested that widths and specifications be provided. With the tenant
24   improvements, the Building Official will determine what is required for restroom upgrades. She
25   recommended the condition language remain fairly broad.
26
27   Chair Mills referenced ingress/egress and fire suppression. He wanted to make sure there was
28   approval from the Unified Fire Authority (“UFA”) so it was clear that an engine could enter and
29   exit the site. Ms. DeSeelhorst reported that the Fire Department needs to sign off on the Business
30   License. It was at that point that the UFA would look at the application and determine whether
31   modifications to the fire suppression system needed to be made. Access would also be considered.
32   Mr. Johnson pointed out that the UFA has certain procedures that they follow in those instances.
33
34   Chair Mills discussed lighting and noted that one pole would be removed. He wondered if there
35   was more that could be asked of the applicant in terms of lighting. There was a desire to maintain
36   a sense of safety in the parking lot but it was also important to be sensitive to adjacent neighbors.
37   Ms. DeSeelhorst believed that the removal of the pole would reasonably mitigate any negative
38   impacts. Leaving the pole could cause glare or the site to be too bright. The removal made sense
39   in this case. If a Condition of Approval is desired for additional lighting, it would need to be
40   directly tied to a fact about the lack of lighting on site. Mr. Johnson stated that one lighting pole
41   would be removed. If any new lighting is proposed on the site, it would need to comply with the
42   current outdoor lighting standards. He reminded the Commissioners that what is proposed is more
43   of a tenant improvement than a new project.
44
45   There was additional discussion about lighting. Ms. DeSeelhorst reported that the ARC will look
46   at outdoor lighting, specifically if any new lighting is proposed for the site. Chair Mills thought it

20   Commissioner Steinman asked what opportunity the City has to implement a Master Plan outside
21   of new development. For instance, if it was possible to encourage the implementation of a Master
22   Plan without full redevelopment. Ms. DeSeelhorst explained that implementation does not always
23   occur until redevelopment takes place. She pointed out that the entrance will remain on the subject
24   property because of the references in the Master Plan. Any time funding is available, it would be
25   possible to speak to different property owners about certain improvements. It was confirmed that
26   the property will be reviewed by the ARC. Ms. DeSeelhorst reported that anything the applicant
27   chooses to change will need to meet City standards. It was not, however, possible for the City to
28   require additional improvements to be made outside of what is proposed to be changed on the site.
29
30   Ms. DeSeelhorst explained that Conditions of Approval need to be tied to a negative impact.
31   Mr. Johnson added that any requirement the City makes needs to be roughly proportional to the
32   impact. In the case of a bank moving out and a dental office moving into the space, the impact is
33   fairly minimal. It is important to be mindful of what is considered proportional. Commissioner
34   Steinman asked if there were impact fees associated with this type of application. Mr. Johnson
35   explained that there will only be impact fees if the impact is different than it was before. For
36   example, if a property shifts from one home to an apartment complex, there would be a different
37   impact. Shifting from a bank to a dental office would likely not create a different impact. It is
38   commercial to commercial, so the overall impact would be similar.
39
40   Chair Mills asked if plumbing and electrical would appreciably change on the site. This was
41   confirmed. Mr. Johnson stated that all of the work done on the inside of the building will need to
42   meet the Building Code. Commissioner Chappell pointed out that there are specific triggers within
43   the existing Building Code, such as the percentage of the property being substantially remodeled.
44   Those were issues the Building Official needs to pay attention to. In response to a question raised,
45   Ms. DeSeelhorst explained that the Health Department will need to sign off on the Business
46   License as part of that process.

 2   Mr. Johnson explained that Staff wants to implement the Fort Union Area Master Plan. That being
 3   said, this particular application is simply a change of use in an existing building. Commissioner
 4   Steinman thought there should be some kind of coordination with the City Council to determine
 5   what Redevelopment Agency ("RDA”) can be put in place to incentivize Master Plan work moving
 6   forward. He wondered if there was an opportunity to create an RDA to incentivize local business
 7   owners and developers to meet the needs of the Fort Union Area Master Plan. Discussions were
 8   had about the Hillside Plaza area. Mr. Johnson explained that when a reinvestment area is created,
 9   there are normally other taxing entities included. As the property grows, changes, and redevelops,
10   the property value will theoretically increase. Participation from the taxing entities will ensure
11   that a portion of the increase in revenue is earmarked and can be used to reinvest in the site. The
12   process of negotiating and getting all of the taxing entities on board was difficult. The long-range
13   plan for the Hillside Plaza was to get taxing entities on board. Typically, the County and the
14   School District wanted to see a plan first to determine what is needed.

28   to impose a moratorium, which meant that no sports courts over 500 square feet could be approved

 2   Ms. DeSeelhorst reported that the request was for a Conditional Use Permit to operate a dental

 4   the NC Zone, which was the reason the item had been brought to the Planning Commission. Staff

 6   relevant standards. As a result, Staff recommended that the Conditional Use Permit be granted

 9   The applicant, Adam Van Leeuwen, introduced himself and stated that Van Leeuwen Dental has
10   been in Cottonwood Heights for more than 40 years. He joined his father in the business
11   approximately 16 years ago. The business has outgrown the current space and there was a desire
12   to move to a more appropriate location. He was excited to aesthetically improve the building.

14   Commissioner Smith asked if there would be additional dentists working at Van Leeuwen Dental.
15   Mr. Van Leeuwen explained that his father is on the cusp of retirement, but he has an associate

17   growth, another dentist could be brought on in the future.

22   treatment or dental sleep therapy is for people who suffer from sleep apnea. Often, people with
23   sleep apnea use a CPAP machine. Another approved treatment for that is an oral appliance. He
