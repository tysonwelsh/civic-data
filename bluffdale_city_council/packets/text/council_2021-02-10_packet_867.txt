 9   Mayor Timothy reported that the required annual training will also apply to the LBA and RDA
10   portions of the meeting. Mr. Sheeran conducted the training that takes place during the first City
11   Council Meeting of each year. He reported that the Open and Public Meeting Act specifies that
12   State agencies and political subdivisions exist to aid in the conduct of the people's business. The
13   intent is to keep discussions deliberately open and to ensure that deliberations occur in the open.
14   All open meetings are to open to the public unless a meeting is specifically closed.
15
16   A meeting is defined as when a quorum of the public body is present. A meeting is not a chance
17   or social gathering and a quorum represents a majority of the body. Mr. Sheeran stressed the
18   importance of following each aspect of the rule. Any time there is a quorum and City policy is
19   discussed, it must have been noticed or the group should separate. Mr. Sheeran commented on
20   discussions that take place after a meeting has adjourned.
21
22   With regard to group emails and online collaboration, Mr. Sheeran consulted with other attorneys.
23   One opinion was if it pertains to City policy, it needs to be in the public. If it pertains to more
24   administrative issues, it does not pertain to the legislative aspect. Issues regarding the use of
25   personal emails were discussed. Any email having to do with City business will be subject to the
26   Government Records Access and Management Act (“GRAMA”). He recommended that any City
27   business take place through City-issued email addresses.
28

30   Closed Meeting and activities that are not appropriate in a Closed Meeting. A violation is a Class B

COVID-19 Report
Police Department staff started to receive the COVID-19 vaccinations, with about
45% participating thus far. The Police Department has had no new cases of COVID
within its staff. The Police Department continues to take all reasonable precautions to
protect staff and citizens from COVID.

Legislature
Several bills dealing with law enforcement issues have been filed. Many of these bills
suggest or require polices that we as a Police Department already have in place.

Calls For Service
During 2020, calls for service went up in most categories except traffic related calls.
We estimate that calls for service will increase by at least 8% in 2021 over 2019. This
estimate is based on the average of the increase of calls for service at 7.9% from
2012 through 2019, and an analysis of the last six months of 2020, during which time
calls for service increased by 8.4% when compared to the same time period in 2019.
With the continuing trend of the last few years, there have been a higher number of
higher risk calls that require more than one officer to respond. Traffic cases are
getting back up to normal levels.

TO:          City Council
FROM:        Grant Crowell
DATE:        February 5, 2021
SUBJECT:     Updates to City Alcohol Licensing Code

Pursuant to discussions with the City Council at a recent study meeting, Staff has prepared
updates to the City’s alcohol licensing code. The impetus of these changes was to allow
additional retail license options for local consent, which could facilitate a bar license for
‘Bout Time Pub and Grub and a hotel license to be issued in a zoning district previously
approved by the City Council. Additionally, Staff has updated the code to reflect changes in
the Utah State Code as relative to updated definitions, classifications of retail and off
premise beer retailers, and some other ministerial changes. As the Council reviews these,
the biggest policy decision is whether to allow certain types of retail licenses and whether to
place limits on those in the community.

                               RESOLUTION NO. 2021-XX

A RESOLUTION AUTHORIZING AN ABATEMENT OF PAYING UTILITY BILLS FOR
ACTIVATED NATIONAL GUARD, RESERVE, ACTIVE MILITARY PERSONNEL AND
DISABLED MILITARY VETERANS

       WHEREAS, Bluffdale City has a number of residents who serve in the National
Guard, Reserves and Active Military who have been activated with the recent conflicts;
and
       WHEREAS, these men and women who serve in the National Guard, Reserves or
Active Military are often sacrificing income to serve the people of the United States
and then citizens of Bluffdale City; and
       WHEREAS, the activation of these individuals has often created a financial
hardship on their families; and
       WHEREAS, military veterans who have become disabled through their military
service has often created a financial hardship on their families; and
       WHEREAS, in recognition of the service and sacrifice of the men and women
serving their country, and protecting those of us in the City of Bluffdale and
throughout the country, the City desires to show its support for the activated or
disabled personnel and help mitigate financial hardships on their families because of
their service; and
       WHEREAS, the City Council has determined that there is a need to extend this
benefit because of continued armed service demands;

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE BLUFFDALE CITY COUNCIL:

       Section 1. The City of Bluffdale will abate the basic residential utility services
charged by the City for residents of Bluffdale that are members of the military who
have been activated to full-time military duty or disabled military veterans. Basic
residential service is defined as the fees associated with the water fee plus up to
10,000 gallons of water, 1 garbage can service, 1 recycling can service, the street lights
fee, and the storm water fee.
       Section 2. In order to qualify for this abatement, the military personnel must
be a resident of the City of Bluffdale.
        Section 3. For activated military personnel, eligibility shall be established by
the person, or family member, bringing a copy of the United States military activation
papers to the City’s utility billing office. For disabled military veterans, eligibility shall
be established by the person, or family member, bringing a copy of the Salt Lake
County verification of disability to the City’s utility billing office. City personnel will
verify the disability status and percentage with the County at that time of application
and annually thereafter.
        Section 4. The monthly abatement shall be effective on the month of activation
or disability and beginning no sooner than February 1, 2021. The abatement for
activated military personnel shall be extended to remain in effect until the date when
the qualified person is released from active duty. The abatement for disabled military
veterans shall extend as long as the disability remains.
        Section 5. This Resolution shall become effective immediately upon its passage
and authorizes the Mayor to execute and cause to be delivered the same.
