PC 22-056 Mountainside Estates Amendment -Continued from the November 17th meeting.
[Subdivision Permit] Brent Lawyer/Capstone Consulting LLC & Shree Giriraj Ji Inc, authorized
agent/owner are requesting a Subdivision Permit to modify their existing subdivision plat by adding
4 building lots. The new proposed lots meet the density and minimum lot size requirements for the
Suburban Neighborhood Residential (NR-4) zone.

STAFF: Russ Holley, Senior Planner, reviewed the request to amend the original Mountainside
Estates Subdivision located at approximately 1200 East 1400 North. The original subdivision was
approved for a 64-lot single-family subdivision. The proposal is to amend the original eight lots along
1300 North and divide them into twelve lots to gain four additional lots. The main concern with the
new proposed lots is that they are located in a critical lands overlay, where the slopes are greater
than 30%. Staff requested additional engineering to look at geotechnical soil stability, civil grading,
retaining walls, and stormwater retention. The applicant did come back with a cross-section that
addresses building in this sloped area using a special type of backfill, unique home types, retaining
walls, and sump pumps. Each home will have to have a geotechnical engineering report completed
prior to obtaining a building permit. Staff is recommending conditional approval if the conditions can
be met. The conditions include extensive engineering requirements that have to be met and a
required note to be placed on the plat so potential buyers are aware of the specific home type
required for these lots.

Commissioner Guth asked if there has been any documentation of movement with this property. R
said he is unaware of any documentation.

PROPONENT: Brent Lawyer spoke about their extensive work with a Geotechnical Engineer on this
project to ensure that all concerns are being met.

PUBLIC: Jack Peterson, who lives near the project said it has been nice to see the gravel pit
developed. Mr. Peterson questioned the portion of the staff report that states, “the City Engineer
does not recommend approval of this project”. Mr. Peterson read sections of the Geotechnical
Report submitted by the applicant. Page two reads, “lowest factor of safety of those elevated”, page
three reads, “slope configuration meets the minimum factors of safety”, “with the addition of geogrid
reinforcement we achieve the minimum factors of safety for global stability”, “we can solve this by
strongly recommending re vegetation as soon as possible to reduce surface erosion”. Mr. Peterson
asked if this project is an engineering marvel or a catastrophe.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Lucero asked to hear from the City Engineer. Darren Farar, City
Engineer, spoke about the steep slope, the proposed sump pumps for water retention, and the
retaining walls that would have to run across property lines causing the development of these lots to
be well choreographed. The property owners will have to work together on the development as well
as the ongoing maintenance of the retaining walls and sump pumps.

Commissioner Guth asked how the homes will be built at different times without impacting existing
homes. D. Farar said that goes back to the development being well choreographed to make sure
one property development does not impact another property.

Commissioner Lucero asked the city attorney to speak about the liability aspect. Mohamed
Abdullahi, Assistant City Attorney said that as long as the code is followed, the city would not be
liable. M. Abdullahi referenced a specific finding the Commission has to make that is listed in Section
17.40.060.A.6 of the Land Development Code: The Subdivision meets the approval of the City
Engineer for technical specifications, standards, and conforms to the conditions imposed on the
subdivision by the Commission.


Commissioner Doutre asked if the original lots had geotechnical requirements. R. Holley said that
the original subdivision required a geotechnical report as well for the south slope that included the
original eight lots.

R. Holley addressed Commissioner Guth’s comment regarding the timing for the homes to be built
and said that it is hard to condition any type of sequencings for the development of these lots because
they are being sold and developed by the individual owners. B. Lawyer said that there is an HOA in
place that will be required to sign off on all house plans, grading, and retention plans.

Commissioner Croshaw asked if the setbacks are the same for all of the lots. R. Holley said that all
setbacks are the same throughout the subdivision. Commissioner Heare asked if the geotechnical
report speaks to the timeline for the development of each lot. B. Lawyer said that they are planning
on building one spec home to show how the homes need to be built and they will not be marketing
the lower lots until they get the upper lots planned out.

Commissioner Heare stated that this type of development on slopes is done all the time. They have
done their homework with the geotechnical report, and it shows that it can be done.

Commissioner Doutre stated her concern with the planning commission’s role in determining if the
minimum safety requirements have been.

Commissioner Lewis doesn’t think this is a good idea, but how does the commission deny if all
conditions can be met.

Commissioner Croshaw said this is an engineering nightmare and spoke about a similar sloped
project he worked on. He also spoke about the upper homes having a view of their neighbor’s roof.

Commissioner Guth said she has a high level of concern with the project, it places a lot of faith in
engineering. She spoke about projects she has worked on where the retaining walls failed during
construction due to rain and landslides that caused lawsuits. She stated that the role of the
government is the health and safety of the citizens, and this project is not a good idea.

Commissioner Peterson said that sump pumps are a good idea if they work. He said he doesn’t love
the idea, but he is not sure if there is a basis for denial.

M. Abdullahi said that the Governmental Immunity Act applies here, a government entity is immune
from liability from acts constituting a government function. Also, the Land Development Code
requires the approval of the city engineer for this project.

D. Farar said that the geotechnical work has been done and a report submitted to support the
subdivision. There has not been enough time for the report to be reviewed in detail. Where there is
such a significant burden on the homeowner to maintain the retaining walls and sump pumps, he
would like more time to take a closer look at the report before approving. His professional opinion is
to not move forward on this project at this time.

Commissioner Lewis asked if there would be an engineering approval if all of the engineering
conditions could be met. D. Farar said that the engineering conditions pertain only to the
development, not the ongoing maintenance that would need to be done for the next 20-30 years.

Commissioner Lewis asked if an HOA could be responsible for maintaining the retaining walls and
sump pumps. R. Holley said the homeowner is responsible for what is on their property.


D. Farar said that sump pumps should work well in the gravel soil that is in that area but for how
long. Is there a mechanism in place that lets the homeowner know when a professional needs to
come in and service their sump.

Commissioner Doutre asked if the Commission needs the City Engineer’s approval on this project to
conditionally approve.

Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director said that if the Commission cannot make the
finding listed in the Land Development Code 17.40.060, then the Commission should not approve
the subdivision.

M. Abdullahi said that the Commission has the authority to prevent the project from creating or
exacerbating hazardous conditions that are adverse to public health, welfare, and safety.

Commissioner Lewis asked if a sequenced development of the lots could be one of the conditions.
M. Abdullahi said that cannot be conditioned.

Commissioner Doutre asked if there is any portion of the grading/retaining that could be completed
by the developer before the lots are sold. Commissioner Croshaw said that the retaining walls will
have to be completed at the same time as the basements.

MOTION: Commissioner Heare moved to Continue PC 22-056 to the January 26th meeting to allow
for more time to review the geotechnical engineering details. Commissioner Peterson seconded the
motion.

PC 22-066 Rivergate [Subdivision Permit] Ben Steele/Rivergate LLC, authorized agent/owner are
requesting a Subdivision Permit to subdivide approximately 30.54 acres into 109 single-family
residential building lots located at 1900 West 600 South in the Traditional Neighborhood Residential
(NR-6) Zone; TIN 02-076-0002; -0008 (Woodruff Neighborhood).

STAFF: Russ Holley, Senior Planner reviewed the request for a Subdivision Permit to create a 109
single-family Subdivision on two parcels of land that were recently annexed into Logan City, totaling
30 acres located at approximately 600 South 1900 West. There are existing wetlands on this
property in the northeast corner for which the applicant is in the process of obtaining approval on a
wetland delineation. The wetlands may impact phases two, three, and four of the proposed
subdivision. If the approved delineation comes back different from what has been proposed with this


application, the applicant will need to return to the Planning Commission for an amendment. This
proposal includes 4 points of connection, two southern access that align across 600 South, one west
access that connects to 1900 West, and a north street stub for future development areas. No
connections or future stubs are shown connecting to the east. The City has requested that the
applicant dedicate enough land to plan for the future development of 600 South into an arterial street
which will require a 102-foot right of way. For now, with this development, the road will be developed
as a 70-foot modified collector street, there will only be parking available on the north side of the
street. The Planning Commission will need to determine if the proposed connectivity follows the
city’s grid pattern sufficiently, and the appropriate width for the landscape buffer next to the street.
The minimum landscape buffers listed in the Land development Code are 15 feet for a local street,
20 feet for a Collector street, and 30 feet for an Arterial street.

Commissioner Peterson asked for clarification on the location of the wetlands. R. Holley said that
phase one is the east side of the development, completely outside of the wetland area.

Commissioner Lucero asked about the approved delineation being different from the proposed one.
M. DeSimone said that the approved delineation should come back consistent with what was
proposed.

Commissioner Doutre asked if the commission looks at the connectivity per phase or for the entire
subdivision. M. DeSimone said to take the broader approach and look at the entire subdivision
layout.

Commissioner Doutre asked if the landscape buffer is developed with each phase or all at once. R.
Holley said that the frontage is built per phase.

Commissioner Lucero asked R. Holley to clarify if they are approving the entire subdivision or phase
one. R. Holley said that the commission is approving the overall 109-lot subdivision with the
proposed phasing plan. The subdivision will be completed and recorded in phases.

Commissioner Guth asked how water retention will work with the phases. R. Holley said they may
have to have temporary retention ponds for each phase until the water can flow to the final destination
point. The City Engineer will look at that for each phase for approval before it is recorded.

PROPONENT: Ben Steele, Visionary Homes asked the commission if they had any questions.

Commissioner Peterson asked what size buffer they are recommending. R. Holley said that the
applicant’s proposed buffer is at 15 feet, which was proposed assuming 600 South was going to
remain a 66-foot right-of-way. B. Steele said since the engineering conditions were updated, they
are now proposing a 4-foot buffer outside of the right of way that is HOA-owned then a shared 24-
foot buffer that extends to the curb.

PUBLIC: No Public comments were made.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Doutre asked R. Holley to explain the right of way and buffer. R.
Holley explained that the right of way measures from the center of the street and includes the curb
gutter park strip and the buffer starts at the sidewalk and extends onto the privately owned property.

Commissioner Lucero said there is potential for another connection to the north near the proposed
1650 West depending on the wetlands in that area.


M. DeSimone said the Commission can add a condition for another stub to be added to the north in
phase four depending on the wetland delineation.

Commissioner Doutre spoke about not being comfortable approving a 15-foot buffer when the plan
is to have an arterial road that would take up all of that buffer for the right of way. M. Abdullahi said
that anything built along 600 south will be affected and their property will be eaten into whether the
street is a 102-foot right of way or an 80-foot right away. The road may not be widened for a long
time, there is a lot of land along 600 South the city would have to acquire.

R. Holley read the updated engineering conditions, numbers 22, 23, and 24 which are different from
what the commission received in their packets.

Condition 22 states: 600 South is identified as a minor arterial on the City’s transportation master plan
and as such requires a 102’ right of way. Dedicate sufficient property to achieve a total (existing plus
new) 84’ of right of way as measured from the existing south right of way line. Prepare construction
plans to build improvements associated with a modified collector street cross-section. The north back of
curb shall be located 24’ south of the new north right-of-way line and the improvements shall include
asphalt that ties into existing asphalt, curb & gutter, sidewalk, and park strip.

Condition 23 states: 1900 W is designated as a gridded street and as such shall be designed and
constructed to Logan City’s gridded street standards.

Condition 24 states: 575 S shall remain a minimum 125’ from the 600 S / 1900 W intersection as
measured from nearest face of curb edges.

D. Farar said that the arterial road is not likely to be built for 30 years or so, but we still want to plan
for it. The developer has offered to dedicate 84 feet to go toward the future 102-foot right of way. If
there isn’t an agreement made between the city and the developer to accept the 84 feet then there
would have to be a proportionality exercise done that looks at the impact of the development on the
city’s roadway system to make sure the city is proportionality obtaining roadway. If the study
determines there isn’t enough impact to obtain the 84 feet then the city has to either buy the land
from the developer or allow them to build in the right of way and possibly end up condemning those
lots in the future for the right of way. The city doesn’t budget to purchase right of way, and the cost
to condemn lots in the future will be 6 times what it would be to figure it out now. The best solution
the city and the developer came up with was to dedicate the 84-foot right of way and reduce the
buffer not knowing when it will be needed, ultimately if the 102-foot right of way gets built it will put
those lots closer to the right of way than what the current standard is.

The Commission discussed updating conditions 22, 23, and, 24 to reflect the engineer’s amended
