        West Point Mixed-Use Concept Plan – Introduction and Discussion Regarding a Conceptual Site Plan for
        the Properties Located at 3815 W. 5400 S.; 3871 W. 5400 S.; 3855-3879 W. 5400 S.; and 3835-3849 W.
        5400 S. (Mark McGrath/AICP/Community Development)

 1.      Mr. McGrath gave a presentation showing the history of this site and surrounding area over the years.
         The former occupants of the site are a K-Mart store and an Albertson’s store, along with various smaller
         stores and uses. Presently the site is almost vacant, partly due to UDOT’s change in Bangerter Highway
         from a continuous flow intersection to an interchange, which included removal of the off ramp onto this
         site. K-Mart closed entirely, and Albertson’s was purchased by Fresh Market, however, did not open a
         store at this location.

         1.1       This site has been vacant for a decade now. Subsequently the City constructed a new road
                   access into the shopping center from 5400 South and there have been developers expressing
                   interest in doing something there, but nothing came to fruition. For over five years now there
                   have been no serious proposals brought forth. He showed images of the developments which
                   have occurred on the north side of 5400 South which have started to change the tone of the
                   area.

         1.2       Right now, the land use market is not good and experiencing housing shortages, with affordable
                   housing especially being in crisis. Therefore, no one wants to develop any type of retail at this
                   time. The market for office space is also being impacted with so many people working from
                   their homes due to the pandemic which in turn accelerates the need for more housing. The

                   area is demographically good but has very little available space to expand. The Salt Lake City
                   area is growing very rapidly right now, with population projections expected to increase 55% or
                   65,000 people. Utah has the highest birth rate and lowest death rate in the nation and along
                   with that there is a need to accommodate all these people expected to migrate here in the near
                   future. Thus far, housing production has not kept up with the growth, especially in urban areas
                   of Utah, and is only building a portion of what is needed.

         1.3       There is a need for 103,000 more units to have a healthy market. To achieve that some cities
                   are building just to house people but including no amenities or aesthetics. He showed some
                   examples of how to achieve higher density but at the same time achieve a desirable look to
                   them. He also showed one example which depicts how not to do a desirable project that was
                   built in Herriman, Utah. There they have achieved the density but at a cost of having no quality
                   elevations, amenities, safe roads or adequate access.

         1.4       The site being discussed tonight has been a commercial shopping center to date. He felt it is
                   time to start considering evolving that property into a different land use, a well-designed high-
                   density project. Commissioner Quigley asked how this compares to the Muirhouse project in
                   the same general area and Mr. McGrath said Muirhouse is medium density which is less units
                   per acre. Mr. McGrath went over the guiding principles of a high-quality project the City is
                   looking for as follows: (1) Design: Architecture; Landscape; Urban Design; (2) Mixed Use; (3)
                   Walkable; (4) Sense of Place; (5) Open Space and Amenity. He explained the reasons for
                   wanting each element and showed examples of each one. He suggested the Planning
                   Commissioners go to the proposed developer’s web site to see what they have done in other
                   areas.

         1.5       Mr. Harper thanked Mr. McGrath for his excellent presentation and asked the Commissioners to
                   interact with the applicants with questions during their presentation.

2.       APPLICANT’S ADDRESS:
         2.1       Mr. John Thackeray (applicant)          said he was excited about this site. He explained his
                   team’s resume’ and felt the architecture being proposed is especially exciting. He explained he
                   wants to present something unique for this site and introduced his team and what their
                   functions are. He turned the time over to Mr. Laterveer for presentation. Commissioner
                   Burggraaf thanked the applicant for looking at this site, saying it is a win-win proposal for
                   Taylorsville.

         2.2       B.J. Laterveer (CEO) said they have locations throughout the U.S.A. in over 30 states. That they
                   have done a tremendous volume of apartment complexes and multi-family projects.                 That
                   every application has to fit in with the City and to do that it is critical to very carefully analyze
                   each situation. There must be a strong partnership with the City to meet all expectations. They
                   didn’t look at this as a 16-acre site but more importantly that high density must align with the
                   experience of the City. His team wants to look at the site and figure out the best way to show
                   off 5400 South and to create a scenario with five large (3-story) buildings. They plan to create
                   common area pockets and parks while creating micro-communities. He discussed at length the
                   proposed landscaping and pocket parks, along with the entry drive from 5400 South. The idea is
                   to create a mixed-use environment while understanding how this will impact the neighbors to
                   the south.           He wanted to make sure the Commission understands the whole package,
                   including all the elements, amenities, and streetscapes with sidewalks big enough for people to
                   pass each other comfortably.

                   2.2.1      There will be landscape out in front of the retail units. He showed a map of the site
                                   and explained that as entrance is made off the street there will be commercial
                              architecture, a brownstone feel element to make it feel more like an urban
                              environment. There will be elevated amenity decks throughout which will enhance the
                              residential experience. This will create a lifestyle/a place where people will want to be.

                   2.2.2      Mr. Laterveer explained the level of design he wants to bring to this project and
                              suggested that the Planning Commissioners do look at this company’s web site, which
                              will show that they take a lot of care with design to be appropriate for each
                              destination. They use Community Design Guidebooks               for each project, which
                              also shows projects done in other cities for mixed use developments. He showed key
                              element slides depicting what they are looking at doing specifically here in Taylorsville.
                              He went over potential uses and facilities needed to accommodate that. Mr. Laterveer
                              allowed that site furnishings have a huge impact on the feel of the project especially in
                              areas dealing with screening and enclosures such as metering, dealing with trash,
                              commercial delivery, utilities, etc. He felt that designing is critical with regard to
                              gathering, open spaces. He hoped to have shown the level of thought they put into
                              their projects. That once the guidebook for this project is approved, then they will
                              come back and address building by building to make designs.            He also showed
                              examples of commercial tenant signs being proposed.

         2.3       Commissioner Quigley asked if it was correct that these will be basically apartments and not
                   “for sale” units.      Mr. Laterveer said that was correct. Commissioner Quigley then asked
                   if that would involve approximately 650 units and Mr. Laterveer said that is what has been
                   shown in the current site plan. Commissioner Quigley assumed that there would be a variety of
                   sizes included from studio to three- or four-bedroom units, and said he was just trying to find
                   out what market was going to be the target for this because he had heard that the target was
                   for people who want to change to this type of lifestyle. With that in mind, he asked what the
                   applicants see as the demographics of the typical unit, i.e., family orientation which involves
                   children running around in this complex, or mostly single or empty nesters. He was just trying to
                   get some idea of what to expect as being the target market. Mr. Thackeray said would be a
                   mixed income project. There will be some income restrictions for people . All units will be
                   finished the same. There will be so many units allocated to all different types of income. They
                   want to attract all variables of income to this project to live together as a community.
                   Commissioner Quigley asked what the impact would be on local schools because in the
                   neighborhood where he lives after a large apartment complex went in it severely overpopulated
                   the schools in the area. Mr. Thackeray said that 45% of the units are one bedroom, 40% two
                   bedrooms, etc. This will not be targeted for larger type families and most of the units will be for
                   one resident. Commissioner Quigley commented that even though this is just in the preliminary
                   stage, as soon as any type of notices are sent out there will be a great deal of input from the
                   neighbors. He continued on to say, from what has been presented he noted there would be five
                   buildings and wondered if there would be retail in all five buildings. Mr. Laterveer said currently
                   there is commercial usage shown in two of the front buildings that lead into the development.
                   The rest of the buildings will have various amenities that will be outward facing, have that same
                   commercial feel and appearance. They plan on keeping the commercial restricted to the front
                   of the site so that customers thereto will come from not only the residents but the outside
                   community as well because they are aware that no retail would survive if it depended solely on
                   the residents. Commissioner Quigley had concern that if the retail is all up front that people
                   would be mulling about in the open spaces, parks and beautiful walkways being shown as
                   resident amenities.

         2.4       Commissioner Burggraaf asked for their definition of “Co-working”. Mr. Laterveer explained
                   that would be designated small office space for tenants to use to work in when they needed to
                   work at home but not necessarily in their apartment.

         2.5       Commissioner Quigley suggested another of his concerns is a situation like transpired in the
                   Summit Vista project wherein they promised amenities but have only produced the buildings so
                   far. He wanted to make sure that the amenities would be installed at the same time as the
                   buildings are in this proposal.        Mr. Laterveer advised that while this project will be done
                   in phases, each phase will have self-contained amenities done at the same time.

           2.6     Commissioner Willardson        said he liked what he was seeing in their presentation but did
                   not like having the buildings so tall and felt that would not fit into the surrounding
                   neighborhood scheme. Mr. Laterveer advised that they would be building in different shapes
                   and designs to break down the masses better. Mr. Harper sited Sorenson Research Park on
                   4500 South as an example of blending in different types of buildings to soften the height issue.

         2.7       Commissioner Wendel felt this was a great project but asked how they meant to insure there is
                   upwards mobility options and allowance for people of differing incomes.             Mr. Isaac said
                   there is uniqueness in what the Thackeray Company is trying to provide as far as diverse
                   housing stock and accommodation for socio-economic strata’s is shown in every project, they do
                   along the Wasatch Front. They have a project going in Sugarhouse now that has an element of
                   affordable units in it and they are blended units with the same access to the pools, security, bike
                   lockers, covered/secure parking as does the market rate tenants in the building. They are trying
                   to diversify the tenants and quality of housing stock on the Wasatch Front by putting their
                   money and bet on the fact that upward mobility as Commissioner Wendel mentioned is so
                   critical in the communities, whether it is
                   racial, socio-economic, single moms, single fathers, etc. Whatever the case may be, they are
                   trying to do a social cause component to every project they build on the Wasatch Front.
                   Presently they are working on a project on the east bench in that realm and would love to do
                   the same thing in Taylorsville. He discussed the idea of five or six different building types and
                   wanted to explain further the five-story height issue. That if they can efficiently stack and
                   program units and build more efficiently, they can provide for wider sidewalks, more common
                   areas, more open space and more amenities in the project. He said they are trying to do things
                   right      and with 16 available acres on this site it gives them the opportunity to develop
                   something nice on the west side. A big part of the project is to provide mixed income levels in
                   each of the buildings.

         2.8       Commissioner Wright        added that this is not that big of site generally but is a big site for
                   Taylorsville. That it seemed like the biggest need would be for two-bedroom units. Mr.
                   Laterveer         said in a typical project like this there would be studio units, ones, twos and/or
                   town homes and with a project of this many units it would probably include three bedroom
                   units as well but there would be a stratification to make sure that everybody is covered, that
                   there is diversity in product across that many buildings. The studio units could run from as low
                   as 500 square feet per unit to units of three bedrooms at 1,600 square feet. There would be a
                   wide variety and range.           One of their main goals is to drive better quality so they can build
                   better amenities and draw people to live in Taylorsville. Commissioner Wright asked what was
                   unique about Taylorsville to attract them here. Mr. Isaac said first of all it is in the very center of
                   the valley, ten minutes from a million-dollar international airport, has good infrastructure, good
                   freeway access, located right next to Bangerter Highway, and there are a lot of jobs in the area.
                   One thing there is not, is a lot of available places to live in right now.

         2.9       Mr. Laterveer added that their company doesn’t have all the answers tonight.          They don’t
                   try to bring the whole solution forward in the first meeting, but when they start a project there
                   is a lot of fact finding and investigation done first to make sure the situation works well.

         2.10      Mr. Isaac said they would like to create the nods and sense of community, with amenities etc.,
                   to get people to spend time together in Taylorsville.       That when the sun sets on those
                   mountains it is very beautiful and would create a sense in people to want to live here. They
                   want to create a uniqueness of features and architecture on this project - an awareness that a
                   person knows they are in Taylorsville now. He said they are excited about doing something on
                   the west bench that has not been done before.

         2.11      Commissioner Quigley agreed that Taylorsville is 15 minutes to just about anywhere in the
                   valley. A fact that has been one of the best kept secrets ever. He felt when the Church of Jesus
                   Christ of Latter-Day Saints temple is completed it will make Taylorsville a landmark. He
                   addressed the applicants saying the fact that they are here tonight tells the Commission they
                   only have one shot to get this right. The City is short on green space and this needs to be done
                   right and become the focal point of the valley.

         2.12      Mr. Thackeray         said this is not just a project for them but a legacy type of development
                   which will be good for everyone. He wanted to assure the Commission that they intend to come
                   up with a great project that will be good for everyone.

         2.13      Commissioners asked general questions which were answered by the applicants regarding:
                   Access (entry/exit points), possibility for a stub street to the south (Staff informed them that
                   was not planned to be done), possibility of adding another stop light (will be answered when
                   applicants receive their traffic study), sewer and water requirements (applicant is working with
                   Taylorsville-Bennion Improvement District presently), What is being done for lighting, also
                   taking into account to maintain the neighbors to the south’s privacy by the height of the
                   structures (Lighting plans will be later, slope of the site will soften the height of the buildings),

         2.14      Mr. Harper         said he appreciates the developer and partners who wants to invest in this
                   property coming here. This is a big change, and everyone needs to wrap their heads around
                   changing from commercial into something completely different. A meeting was held with
                   people from both teams, clarifying expectations as to creating a sense of place and a livable
                   community. What the City doesn’t want to see is having someone just say, “okay this is a place
                   where I drive in, go up to my room, sleep and in the morning drive off again”. This project needs
                   to have services there so somebody can go to a bakery, have coffee, entertainment or a place
                   for an office. Those things will come in, but this has to be a place to be able to relax with your
                   dog or throw a blanket out on the lawn and take it easy. He believed after watching Mayor
                   Overson and John Thackeray in a meeting today, that they are both of the same accord that that
                   is what is going to be built here.

                   2.14.1     This is going to be a little bit different process. Because the current owner or the
                              property and their demands on having entitlements put in place before these
                              applicants can close and the short time frame that they have, there is not the luxury of
                              time that was there with Summit Vista or Muirhouse during the SSD zoning procedure.

                   2.14.2     First set of approvals will be an ordinance that will come in with guiding verbiage and
                              criteria. Later, other elements and approvals will come back for secondary approvals
                              by the Planning Commission and City Council.

                   2.14.3     The Community Design Guidebook that has been talked about is going to become very
                              critical for the Planning Commission to make a recommendation on this. He then laid
                              out the time frame of what is going to happen. Mr. Harper explained why it is really
                              important to make sure the Planning Commission has seen everything they need
                              before there is a formal application. The applicant is going to submit a formal
                              application to the City to go through and rezone this piece of property.

                              2.14.3.1 The notice of public hearing will be published in the paper and on the State’s
                                       public notice web site on the appropriate date. This is a notice for a public
                                       hearing before the Planning Commission on April 13th.

                              2.14.3.2 Between now and the public hearing, City Staff will have given the information
                                       to the applicant for them to hold a neighborhood meeting, so the neighbors
                                       are aware of what is going on prior to the Planning Commission meeting.

                              2.14.3.3 City Staff will also inform The Utah Department of Transportation per State
                                       Code that there is a major land use change proposed from commercial to
                                       residential so that they are aware of that. If they have any concerns, they
                                       have a 30-day comment period during which to notify the City. He doubted
                                       they would have any because the traffic study is going to show this will
                                       generate less traffic than having a major commercial center take up the entire
                                       property like WinCo or Costco type businesses.

                              2.14.3.4 Then the public hearing will be held on April 13th. The intent is to take this to
                                       the City Council on May 5th, with all recommendations and all documents so
                                       that they can study it and have the opportunity to take action on this prior to
                                       when the due diligence period ends for this group and they have to close. This
                                       is a tight process. City Staff doesn’t like to do this with an SSD but felt that is
                                       the way to do it in this instance. So, there is an ordinance and a development
                                       agreement that will come to the Commission but all the details that the
                                       Commission normally sees; the architectural elevations, the materials,
                                       landscaping, amenities, etc., will be in separate approvals. You will see the
                                       density range, parking spaces, performance criteria later on in the process.
                                       Mr. Harper said he just wanted to make sure the Commissioners understood
                                       the process for this application in particular and see if there were questions or
                                       concerns about that.

         2.15      Commissioner Wright asked what would be the exposure to the City in this instance going that
                   route? Mr. Harper said that is why they are talking in the terms of performance criteria. This
                   will be a development agreement to the sole benefit of Thackeray. There will be no permits or
                   other approvals done. As to what happens if they sell it without having all the approvals in
                   place. he said he was aware that they are not intending to sell it but wanted to make sure there
                   are checks and balances within that document.         He advised that the day after tonight’s
                   meeting, there will be meeting between City Staff and Thackeray Staff to talk about what is in
                   the Community Design Guidebook. That covers some of the criteria and things that have to be
                   submitted and, in the meantime, the City’s Attorney and Thackeray’s attorney are going to be
                   working on the Development Agreement and all the legal nuances.

         2.16      Commissioner Willardson said the City knows that the owner of the property is in a difficult
                   situation, so he hoped that everyone understands that they have got to move now and do it
                   within his timeframe, so it doesn’t go away. Mr. Harper said that was an excellent point.
