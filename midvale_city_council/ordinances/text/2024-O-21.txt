MIDVALE CITY, UTAH
ORDINANCE NO. 2024-0-21
AN ORDINANCE ADOPTING THE MIDVALE CITY JOB CLASSIFICATION
PLAN, SALARY PLAN, ELECTED OFFICIAL’S SALARIES, AND
BENEFIT PACKAGE FOR FISCAL YEAR 2025

WHEREAS, Midvale City has heretofore established a Job Classification Plan, Salary Plan and
Benefits Package for FY2025; and

WHEREAS, City jobs are classified with pay grades established according to job requirements,
qualifications, and supervisory responsibilities; and

WHEREAS, the Midvale City Council may authorize a cost-of-living increase in accordance with
Chapter 2.12.040(B) of the Midvale City Municipal Code; and

WHEREAS, the Midvale City Council held a duly noticed public hearing on June 18, 2024,
regarding the proposed changes to the compensation of Midvale’s elected and appointive officers in
accordance with Utah Code Ann. 10-3-818; and

WHEREAS, it is in the best interests of Midvale City and the citizens thereof that a Job
Classification Plan, Salary Plan, Elected Official’s Salaries, and Benefits Plan for FY2025 be formally
adopted.

NOW THEREFORE BE IT ORDAINED by the City Council of Midvale City, Utah as follows:

SECTION 1: The City Council desires to approve adjustments to the Fiscal Year 2025 Salary Plan,
including for all appointive officers, that has been determined and made according to data derived from the
compensation survey conducted by the Midvale City Human Resources Department.

SECTION 2: The City Council desires to adopt the Fiscal Year 2025 Job Classification Plan, Salary
Plan, and Benefits Package attached hereto as Exhibits, are hereby adopted.

SECTION 3: A 3.3% cost-of-living adjustment to the Mayor and City Council salaries be
implemented in accordance with Chapter 2.12.040(B) of the Midvale Municipal Code.

SECTION 4: This ordinance is effective upon publication in accordance with Section 10-3-711 of
the Utah Code. All salary adjustments shall be effective as of July 1, 2024.

PASSED AND APPROVED this 13" day of August, 2024.

RETIREMENT
UTAH RETIREMENT SYSTEMS: Retirement Plan for public service employees subject to the
terms, conditions, and limitations as defined and regulated by the Utah State Retirement Board,
the City provides coverage for employees in eligible employment classifications in the Utah State
Retirement System.
MISSION SQUARE PERFORMANCE PLAN 401(a) RETIREMENT PLAN: Retirement Plan for
general employees. The city will provide a 1% match to the 401(a) when the employee contributes
1% to acity sponsored ICMA 457, URS 401(k) account or one of the URS IRA accounts.
MISSION SQUARE PERFORMANCE PLAN 457 RETIREMENT Plan: Tax deferred retirement
plan. Employees may defer up to $22,500. Catch up 55 yrs. or older $30,000.
RETIREMENT HEALTH SAVINGS PLAN (RHS):
Contribution Sources and amounts: Mandatory Employee Leave Contributions: See Accrued
Sick Leave per City Policy. Only participants with accrued sick leave in excess of 480 hours at the
end of the first full pay period in January are eligible for contribution to the RHSP. Contribution is
equal to sick leave earned during calendar year less sick leave used during calendar year
multiplied by 50%. Hours necessary to reach 480 hours are considered “used.” AFTER the
contribution to the RHS plan, participants may elect to convert 25% of remaining sick leave in
excess of 240 hours to vacation leave.
City contributes 10% of employee’s accrued sick leave to RHS at time of separation from service.

PARENTAL LEAVE
Employees may receive six (6) weeks parental leave for the birth or adoption of a child. Parental
leave runs concurrently with FMLA.
CAREGIVER LEAVE
Employees may receive six (6) weeks caregiver leave to care for an immediate family member with
a serious health condition according to FMLA criteria. Caregiver leave runs concurrently with
FMLA.
LOANS AT WORK (Optional)
Employees may be eligible to obtain a personal loan from BMG Money between $500 and $5000
to be repaid through payroll deduction. Amount is income based for employees who have been
employed with the City for one year. One loan per household.
FLEXIBLE SPENDING PLAN (Optional)
Employees may elect up to $2,700 for medical expenses and $5,000 for childcare pre-tax.
HEALTH SAVINGS ACCOUNT
Employees selecting the High Deductible Health Plan may elect up to $4150 single and $8300 two
party and family for medical expenses. At age 55, an additional $1,000 contribution is allowed
annually. City pays 90% of high deductible $1440 for a single and $2880 for two-party and family.
