2.2) Public Hearing and discussion of a proposed amendment to Chapter 26, Accessory
Uses. The proposed amendment is to increase the required side and rear yard setbacks for
accessory buildings over 16 feet in height.
Katie Bussell presented information on the agenda item.

Commissioner Newall opened the public hearing.

Bart Peacock stated his property borders the development where the large garage is being built.
He is concerned about the amount of time it is taking to build the building. The building is
encroaching on his property. The builder is asking Bart to let him on his property to work on the
building. Bart said he wants to help his neighbor but does not want to be held liable. He called
his insurance company to see what would happen by letting him on his property and the
insurance company stated there are concerns. He is trying to keep his water off the other
adjoining properties. His property is being affected drastically. The builder is meeting the code
but it is causing issues. He doesn't want to be the bad guy, but the overhang requirements need to
change because it is hanging on his property.

Scott Carlson stated you can't build over the property line, even with the overhang. Mr. Carlson
stated some structures can’t be over a certain amount of feet, regardless of where the property
line is.

Commissioner Newall closed the public hearing.

Kim Struthers clarified code from agricultural zoning (garages, shops, barns, etc). The setback is
from the wall or the foundation. They are measured from any overhang or eve.

Commissioner Jackson prefers the OSHA language and that permits should be required,
especially with buildings being that tall. Commissioner Bunker feels the OSHA standard will be
too restrictive. Commissioner Eyre stated he is concerned that the footings were dug right at the
property line. The Commissioners discussed what would be considered a protrusion.

Commissioner Newall re-opened the public hearing.

Bart Peacock asked what regulation would be if he were to build a building right up to the
property line. He stated this would put homes extremely close. Bart stated what it sounds like
this is a first come first serve. Bart stated if one of the buildings catches fire it proposes a mess.
Agriculturally, this is affecting his property and is having an impact.

Commissioner Newall stated there may be a code that prohibits that. Kim Struthers stated that
may be a question for the building and inspection. Craig Chambers stated it depends on the type
of building that would restrict building.

Commissioner Newall closed the public hearing.

Commissioner Newall stated she understands the situation and is also concerned for the property
owners, both existing and new. Commissioner Britton is curious to know if the property that
borders an agricultural zone would make a difference as opposed to a residential zone, based on
certain circumstances. Commissioner Britton was under the impression the zoning could be
changed as a matrix of sorts, including water, plumbing, power, etc. Commissioner Eyre brought
up the concern of the zone changing in the future.

2.3) Public hearing and recommendation of Brent Ventura’s request for review of the
Skyridge Property General Plan Amendment from Public Facility to VLDR (Very Low
Density Residential) on 4.07 acres located at approximately 260 East 2700 North.
Katie Bussell presented information on the agenda item.

The applicant, Jason Mandal Harris, was present to represent the item. Four acres are going to be
developed into a very low residential area.

Keith Greenwood’s yard backs the property. He wanted to clarify if the land was bought or under
contract. It hasn't closed because the school wanted to use it through the summer.

Katie read an online comment that was opposed to the item from Bracken Moehler.

Scott Carlson clarified he is here as a citizen. He stated this is a sad circumstance made by voters
as a failure to pass the bond. He stated the school district made its decision based on the voters,
and it has had a sad outcome. The request to make it a low-density residential is a perfect request
for this area. He feels this is the right thing to do. The General Plan should say what it should be,
not what it couldn't be. He encourages support.

Reno Mahe stated he just remodeled his house. They are trying to get the zoning down to four
houses. Regarding the comments about the growth of Skyridge, the school’s number of students
is going down. Regarding growth, it's six families, maybe four, moving to those four acres. As
citizens, it was voted to not have open space.

Commissioner Bunker doesn't have any concerns with the development. He understands losing
green space but is in support. Commissioner Jackson also agrees with the development. He stated
not voting on the bonds, or increasing taxes, developments are going to be built to fund the district.
Commissioner Eyre asked about the first right of refusal with the City.

Motion: Commissioner Bunker moved to send a positive recommendation. In all DRC comments
and findings stated in the report. Commissioner Jackson seconded.

        Motion: Commissioner Bunker moved to send a positive recommendation of Brent
        Ventura’s request for review of the Skyridge Property General Plan Amendment
        from Public Facility to VLDR (Very Low Density Residential) on 4.07-acres located
        at approximately 260 East 2700 North; including all DRC comments and findings
        stated in the report. Commissioner Jackson seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Bunker, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes; Commissioner Newall, yes;
        Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Britton, yes. The motion passed unanimously.
