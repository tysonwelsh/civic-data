     prosecuted 2,625 felony cases, 1,312 misdemeanor cases, 1,698 juvenile cases and 482 justice court cases, assisted

     in restitution, theft, kidnapping, organized a county-wide meeting for county advocates to collaborate ideas/resources,
     have lead the way with a facility dog to help child victims, and offer a high school/collage students internship program,

 G. ACTION ITEMS:
     1. WEBER COUNTY BOARD OF CANVASS-CERTIFICATION OF RESULTS OF PRESIDENTIAL PRIMARY ELECTION.
          Ryan Cowley, County Elections Director, explained the statutory order for three Board of Canvass members,
          which today consisted of the two commissioners and John Bond, County Treasurer. One of the unique things
          happening for the first time this year included consolidating polling places into one centralized vote center at the
          Union Station, an iconic, centrally located building next to all major county transportation routes. This worked
          quite well, and there was a lot of positive feedback and the negative was the travel distance for some people and
          that some did not know about it. However, it was noticed on the county’s website, was mailed in each ballot, was
          posted all over social media as well as in many Standard Examiner newspaper articles, etc. This location worked
          great for the disability community with the created shortcuts. This was done with 1/3 of the resources, it alleviated
          poll workers’ long hours, etc. County Elections is very small, having three fulltime staff, compared to other
          similar-sized counties around the country. They processed 14,138 voter registrations since Christmas, in addition
          to running the election, handling the State tax referendum, etc. The election figures included that there were
          24,763 Republican ballots cast, 13,767 Democrat ballots cast, that of the ballots received, 91% were cast either
          by-mail or cast early. The total registered voter turnout was an amazing 59.7%. The commissioners, Mr. Bond,
          and Ricky Hatch, County Clerk/Auditor, commended the innovative and professional elections team, one of the
          best in the nation, who care about the integrity of elections.
      Mr. Hatch said that County Elections continues to improve on the process. Mr. Cowley thanked his small,
      hardworking staff who do amazing work.
      Commissioner Harvey moved to approve the canvass results certification of the Presidential Primary Election;
      John Bond seconded.
      Commissioner Harvey – aye; John Bond, County Treasurer – aye; Commissioner Froerer – aye
   2. CONTRACT WITH VITALCORE HEALTH STRATEGIES FOR INMATE HEALTHCARE SERVICES.
      County Sheriff Chief Aaron Perry presented this contract, which was done through procurement, for provision of
      medical/psychiatric services (Constitutional requirements for inmates) in both the Kiesel and the 12th Street
      facilities. The county was performing below national standards. He has seen firsthand the nurses objectivity,
      compassion and tireless hours to serve the inmates, and this contract affords them additional needed resources to
      do their job more effectively. He outlined how the county currently provides medical services, including
      contracting with various companies for various services. The county seriously looked to improve this process to
      meet the care, statutory requirements, reduce liability, and be fiscally responsible. They looked at national
      standards and trends and visited a Dallas facility. This company will provide all the contracts/services into one
      and with some indemnification. Currently, nursing, medical and clerks are county employees but will become
      contract employees, and VitalCore has guaranteed them employment with no pay reduction. This is a 1-year
      contract with four optional annual renewals (with a 90-day exit clause) and the annual cost is just under $3.3
      million, which is in the Sheriff’s budget for two years. They believe they will see significant savings in decreased
      transports to hospitals, etc. To build the county’s own equal services it would take a lot of time and be potentially
      much more expensive to achieve all the benefits. He addressed Commissioner Harvey’s questions including that
      there will be a 4% annual increase, which should be covered by the savings. Chair Froerer concluded that the
      county does not have the resources without contracting this out.
      Commissioner Harvey moved to approve the contract with VitalCore Health Strategies for inmate healthcare
      services; Chair Froerer seconded.
      Commissioner Harvey – aye; Chair Froerer – aye
