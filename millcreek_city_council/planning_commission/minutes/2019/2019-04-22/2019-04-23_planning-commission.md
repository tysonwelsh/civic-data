1. Discussion
1.1 City Center Master Plan
Francis Lilly reminded the Commissioners to submit the volunteer forms handed out last
week.
Lilly highlighted the city center master plan map between Richmond Street (1300 E.) and
Highland Drive around 3300 S. He emphasized that the plan is not only a planning effort,
but an economic development effort too. The strategy moving forward is offsetting other
costs such as parking and utilities. The city center master plan lays out five goals: a
walkable urban place that is iconic, which encompasses “Mill Park” located along the
fault line and showing outdoor movies on the back wall of the Villa Theatre; an amenity
and open space for the whole city; civic and public gathering places; an engine of
population and tax base to help keep taxes low for Millcreek; and the design should
support transportation alternatives.
Commissioner Stephens asked about the City’s strategy to make sure the envisioned park
comes to fruition. Lilly said staff is looking to adopt a form-based code that overlays the
area. He said the master plan imposes street standards and by adopting the master plan,

for donations or purchases for the signature feature, like “Mill Park.” Lilly said the park
provides more visibility for businesses. Lilly said City Hall may be relocated to the city
center. Commissioner Mumford asked if Venture Out activities would take place in the
City Center. Lilly said yes, they will start to this year even if it is in a parking lot to teach
people that it will be a gathering space. He then highlighted the mixed-use buildings that

Lilly showed a table of the market absorption study by Zions Public Finance which

asked about the demand for office space. Winder said there is less demand for office
space, but there is still some. Lilly said office spaces want to be next to transportation and

want food trucks or vendors who are not located in Millcreek doing business in
Millcreek. He would like there to be a way for Millcreek residents to be able to provide

then have a retail area underground as other cities around the world have done.

Lilly said the city center master plan guiding principles are: the city center should
emphasize walkability and minimize internal traffic and parking demand; the city center
should include municipal property owned by the city and designated for public use; the
design of Millcreek’s city center should represent the city in ways that are distinctive
from other cities and unique to Millcreek; and the city center should emphasize culture,
art, ideas, and interaction between citizens. Lilly brought up strategies for the city center
master plan on traffic and parking: make a public parking facilities area part of the
community reinvestment area implementation strategy; streets will have angled or
parallel on-street parking which may eventually need to be managed; residential parking
districts for the areas east and west of the city center if the residents who live there want
them; reduce Highland Drive to a 3-lane cross section which has marginal impact on
travel times and they work just as well as four lanes; signal optimization at Richmond
Street and Highland Drive; pedestrian actuated signals on Richmond Street and Highland
Drive in the project area; and pedestrian refuges and landscaped medians where
appropriate on Richmond Street and Highland Drive. Commissioner Mumford asked if
the traffic study was based on current traffic or projections. Lilly said it was based on

Lilly went over the 2019-2020 implementation goals of the plan: 1) adopt a code that
imposes design standards, a lite form-based code approach; 2) create a temporary

Center CRA; May/June — adopt city center master plan; May — corridor study for

the street. The buildings will have an active ground floor use, parking beneath or within,
step backs, roof gardens, and a 30-foot maximum storefront width on the ground floor
with varying levels of transparency on the various stories. Commissioner Mumford asked
about seasonal solar penetration. Lilly said a step back would allow more sun in the park.
Lilly said there will be more high quality, energy efficient designs in the center. Lilly
cautioned having rooftop gardens near residential zones for privacy sake. A concern for
fire access was raised with buildings that have step backs. Lilly said there can be
solutions to allow for better fire truck access. He said he would talk with the Fire
Marshal.

1.2 Guest Parking Requirements for Multi Family Development

Francis Lilly said a guest parking study was conducted by Hales Engineers on Super
Bowl Sunday. The study vindicated the City’s code that 1/3 of a stall per unit is an
appropriate guest parking ratio. The study also recommended a different approach for
tandem parking and guest parking in a driveway. Lilly showed a video by Strong Towns
to the Commission on parking requirements. The Hales Engineer’s parking study findings
were: the guest parking requirement is sufficient; the City should impose restrictions on
tandem parking so that it does not fulfill guest parking requirements; guest parking
requirements apply to multifamily projects, PUDs, and projects on private lanes; and
single-family and duplex developments on public streets should have driveways and on-
street parking. He then shared best practices from a planning standpoint towards a
market-based approach: regulate how on-street parking is used, charge for it, and limit it;
Millcreek should lead on shared parking facilities; recognize that Millcreek can build a
place for people or a place for their cars, rarely both can be accomplished at the same
time; parking takes a lot of space that can be used for open space or buildings; if ratios of
2 per unit or 1 per 3,000 square feet of commercial space are exceeded, the result will be
a suburban development and not a town center; and relaxing parking ratios in centers and
transit-oriented development areas should be a goal, but Millcreek needs to be proactive
about alternatives and policy implications.

Commissioner Mumford asked if Lilly recommended less than 2 parking stalls per unit.
Lilly said in the town and city centers where the City wants to focus on transit
development, yes, but in a typical suburban project, no. Janson said a parking district for
the city center made sense. Chair Healy brought up an example from San Jose where the
city owns the center of the block and puts parking there and businesses rent space in it.
Commissioner LaMar asked about a requirement that businesses work together on shared
parking. Lilly said a parking agreement needs to be recorded to the property and be for at
least 5 years in duration. Robert May brought up a shared parking maintenance
agreement solution for an existing commercial property in the City that is recorded on the
plat. Commissioner LaMar asked how effective parking agreements are with
enforcement. Chair Healey said that the City could regulate the on-street parking at the
right price and then it becomes self-policing. Lilly brought up unbundling the parking as
justification to reduce the parking ratio, which is to charge separately for the unit and
separately for the parking. He would not recommend it without a time or visitor parking
