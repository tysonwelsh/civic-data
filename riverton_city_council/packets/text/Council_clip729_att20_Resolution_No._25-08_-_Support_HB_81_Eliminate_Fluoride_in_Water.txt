                                   RIVERTON CITY, UTAH
                                   RESOLUTION NO. 25-08

  A RESOLUTION OF SUPPORT FOR THE PASSAGE OF HOUSE BILL 81, IN THE
               2025 SESSION OF THE UTAH LEGISLATURE

       WHEREAS, during the 2025 session of the Utah Legislature, state representative Gricius
sponsored House Bill 81, which, if adopted, would prohibit the addition of fluoride into any
public water system; and

        WHEREAS, the term fluoride is defined by Utah Code § 19-4-111 to mean “a chemical
compound that contains the fluoride ion and is used to fluoridate drinking water, including: (i)
fluorosilicic acid; (ii) sodium fluorosilicate; or (iii) sodium fluoride”; and

       WHEREAS, injecting fluorosilicic acid into a culinary water system is the prevailing
method for fluoridating water because its liquid state enables it to be injected into culinary water
systems; and

         WHEREAS, fluorosilicic acid in pure form is extremely acidic, with a PH rating of 1-2,
meaning it is capable in small quantities of corroding glass, metal, and dissolving biological
tissue, including bone; and

       WHEREAS, according to the Fluoride Action Network over 54 publicly reported
accidents have occurred nationwide which resulted in spilling fluorosilicic acid or overfeeding
the same into culinary water systems, and these incidents can injure consumers and even kill
workers whose duty is to fluoridate drinking water, and

        WHEREAS, Riverton City Council finds and determines that the use of fluorosilicic
acid, currently required by Salt Lake County law, will potentially endanger the health of
employees called upon to administer its distribution, and members of the public who will
consume City water, if an accident occurs; and

       WHEREAS, data which tracks oral health internationally shows little difference between
countries that fluoridate culinary water and those that don't, likely because countries in either
category have instilled better knowledge about oral hygiene, improved access to dental care, and
permitted alternate means of access to fluoride in retail products such as toothpaste and
mouthwash. For example, the United Kingdom, a country which generally does not fluoridate
drinking water, experiences a slightly lower rate of tooth decay in children 12 years of age than
the United States, a country which fluoridates approximately 70% of its water supply; and

        WHEREAS, where the question as to how beneficial the fluoridation of drinking water is
unresolved, while the possibility of health hazards caused by overexposure is undeniable, the
issue of whether to fluoridate water can be a divisive issue in communities; and

       WHEREAS, members of the public who wish to consume fluoride may do so by
obtaining a variety of fluoridated products available in the market, but members of the public
who wish to avoid consuming fluoridated water face a more challenging experience in obtaining
drinking water; and

        WHEREAS, over 90% of water distributed through culinary water systems is not
actually consumed, but used for activities such as bathing, washing and providing water to
plants, which necessarily means that over 90% of fluorosilicic added to the City’s culinary water
is wasted; and

        WHEREAS, the Riverton City Council therefore finds and determines that the
fluoridation of drinking water for the purpose of enabling fluoride consumption, is likely the
most inefficient use of taxpayer dollars, with questionable if any benefit, year over year, in Salt
Lake County; and

        WHEREAS, the City Council of Riverton City, Utah, does hereby determine that it is in
the best interests of the health, safety, and welfare of the citizens of Riverton City to support the
passage of House Bill 81 now pending before the Utah Legislature, empowering residents to
pursue more efficient ways of improving dental health;

        NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED by the City Council of Riverton City, Utah,
as follows:

       1. The Riverton City Council hereby voices its support for the passage of House Bill 81,
          to eliminate the addition of fluoride into culinary water systems in the state of Utah.

       2. This Resolution shall become effective immediately upon passage.

       PASSED AND ADOPTED by the City Council of Riverton, Utah, this 4th day of
February 2025 by the following vote:
