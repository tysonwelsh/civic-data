PC 22-039 Northwest Fields Business Park -Continued from the August 11th meeting [Design
Review Permit] Jason Larsen/Quayle Family Properties, authorized agent/owner are requesting a
Design Review Permit to construct five new warehouse buildings on 50.69 acres located at
approximately 1000 West 1800 North in the Industrial Park (IP) zone; TIN 04-078-0012, 04-081-
0002 (Bridger Neighborhood).


STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a Design Review Permit to build five new warehouse
buildings that are each approximately 150,000 square feet, surface parking, loading docks, open
space, and landscaping on a 50-acre, two-parcel project site in the Industrial Park (IP) zone located
at approximately 1000 West 1800 North. The flex-style buildings can accommodate warehousing,
manufacturing, administration offices, and similar types of uses. This project also includes the
dedication and creation of portions of 800 West and 1600 North streets.

As proposed, the project meets the minimum setback, lot coverage, design and fenestration, building
height, and parking requirements in the Land Development Code (LDC) for the IP zone.
The parking location in the IP zone indicates placement at the side or rear. In cases where
functionality and primary building use present conflicts, the Planning Commission may make
exceptions to the parking design per the LDC. This proposal includes semi-truck loading docks and
large truck circulation in the rear areas of the buildings, creating conflicts between automobile parking
and semi-truck loading/unloading. Given the layout of the building’s floorplans and the context of
the surrounding large-scale industrial businesses, separating the parking lot and semi-truck loading
dock should be a consideration in this situation. The neighborhood character in this part of town
shows numerous parking lots positioned between the street and the building. As conditioned with
the Planning Commission determining the location of parking lots, the project meets the standards
in the LDC.


The submitted “conceptual” site plan does not show sidewalk circulation. As conditioned with full
pedestrian circulation to adjacent street sidewalks, the project complies with the LDC.

There was one comment received from a neighboring business to the north, they have concerns with
their use of sensitive laser equipment being disturbed by the construction of this development. This
comment will be passed to the developer to work with the neighboring business on these concerns.

Commissioner Peterson asked who is responsible for developing the other half of 1600 North. R.
Holley explained that the street development is required to be done with the development of the
adjacent property. If a vacant property is not developed in the future, the city may step in and develop
the other half of the street.

Commissioner Guth asked about the undeveloped properties to the north and south of this
development along 800 West and if the city will be developing those portions of the street. R. Holley
said that the city does own a parcel to the north and will be developing the west half of 800 West
along their property. The city may step in and develop the small portions of 800 West along the
vacant properties to the north and south of the development.

Commissioner Dourte asked at what phase of the development the streets will be developed. R.
Holley said that the streets are developed proportionately to the phases of the development.

PROPONENT: Jason Larsen said he did not have any comments at this time and asked the
commission if they had any questions.

PUBLIC: No public comments were made.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Guth asked about the frontage parking exception. R. Holley said that
condition number three allows for parking in front of the buildings. This is a common exception in
the IP zone for safety due to the loading docks.

MOTION: Commissioner Heare moved to conditionally approve a Design Review Permit for PC
22-039 with the conditions of approval and findings for approval as listed below. Commissioner Guth
seconded the motion.

FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL
1. The proposed project is compatible with surrounding land uses and will not interfere with the
   use and enjoyment of adjacent properties because of the building design, site layout, materials,
   landscaping, and setbacks.
2. LDC 17.43.080 authorizes the Planning Commission to make design adjustments not
   associated with numerical standards and requirements if they are consistent with surrounding
   areas and do not compromise future approvals.
3. The large-scale semi-truck loading docks and circulation conflict with automobile parking and
   warrant a design adjustment.
4. The allowance of the parking lot locations will not compromise the neighborhood character in

5. The proposed project provides required off-street parking.


6.   The proposed project complies with maximum height, density and building design, open space

7.   The project met the minimum public noticing requirements of the Land Development Code and

use this as a long-term rental as well. T. Rice said that the 3-bedroom portion would be licensed as

 PC 22-049 100 West Executive Suites [Design Review Permit] Justin Campbell/Jabaa LLC,
 authorized agent/owner are requesting a Design Review Permit to construct a new 80,920 square
 foot, five-story hotel building with parking on the 1st floor and 50 executive suites on upper floors.
 Located at 162 South 100 West in the Town Center-One (TC-1) zone; TIN 02-046-0019 (Ellis
 Neighborhood).


STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a Design Review Permit for a new 5-story extended stay
hotel with ground floor parking and 4 floors of hotel rooms above located in the Town Center One
(TC-1) zone at 162 South 100 West. The proposed project meets the building material, access and
circulation, setbacks, lot coverage, frontage, and height requirements listed in the Land Development
Code for the TC-1 zone.

A hotel is required to have 1 parking stall per room plus four additional stalls for employees. The
proposal shows 50 parking stalls within the parking structure and one (1) additional stall near the
dumpster area for a total of 51 parking stalls. The applicant has submitted an alternative parking
plan to the Director of Community Development for a 5.6% reduction in the overall requirement. The
alternative plan included vacancy data, anticipated use of ride share, micro-mobility devices, and off-
hour demand of cleaning employees to guests for reasons to reduce. This hotel is intended to have
a maximum of two employees on-site. The Director has the discretion of allowing up to a 15% parking
reduction. Reductions of more than 15% must be approved by the Planning Commission.

The transparency requirement for the TC-1 zone is 60% on the ground floor and 20% on the upper
floors. As proposed the building is at 40% on the ground floor and 40% on the upper floors. The
transparency on the ground floor is proposed as wall openings in the brick material to the main level
parking and the two pedestrian entrances. In addition to the wall openings, there are approximately
8 feet of planter strip along the front of the building that could be landscaped with shrubs and narrow
trees to help break up and screen the areas of blank brick wall. As conditioned with additional
landscaping and/or metal trellising for a total of 60% fenestration on the ground floor, the project
meets the requirements of the LDC.

Commissioner Croshaw asked how long an extended stay is. R. Holley said that an extended stay
for a hotel is 30 days or less.

Commissioner Doutre asked what the difference would be if these units were to be used as long-
term rentals. R. Holley said the required parking would change from 50 stalls to 75 stalls.

Commissioner Lewis asked if commercial space on the ground floor is required in this zone. R.
Holley said that in the TC-1 zone, ground floor commercial is only required along Main Street and
400 North.

Commissioner Lucero mentioned that floor plans were not included in the proposal. R. Holley said
a copy of the floor plans was requested but the architect is still working on them.

Commissioner Doutre asked how the short-term stay would be enforced. R. Holley explained that
enforcement is based on complaints received. In a hotel, a guest could stay for 30 days and then
simply sign a new agreement to stay for additional days. 30 days is the maximum agreement term.

Commissioner Dourte asked If the current owner was to sell the property would it have to stay as a
short-term rental. R. Holley said that the permit runs with the property and will be recorded on the


deed. The building permit obtained will be for a hotel, and once complete the certificate of occupancy
will be for a hotel and that is recorded on the property as well.

Commissioner Croshaw asked about the number of bedrooms. R. Holley said that hotels do not
have occupancy restrictions. The fire code would set a maximum occupancy for the building but
hotel management would determine the number of guests allowed in each room.

Commissioner Peterson asked about the transparency on the main floor and adding trellises to the
wall to get to the 60%. R. Holley said they could add more openings in the brick wall or add trellises
to the brick wall or additional landscaping in front of the brick wall to count towards the fenestration
to get to the 60%. This is an aesthetic requirement and the Planning Commission has the authority
to accept the proposed 40% transparency.


PROPONENT: Justin Campbell, Architect on this project asked the commission if condition number
two (no long-term residential uses (more than 30 days) are allowed) can be removed because this
is a hotel and will operate as a hotel, they will not have any lease agreements that are longer than
30 days. Hotels are permitted in this zone and they will be operating as one. For condition number
4, they want to keep the open airflow to the parking area and would not want to plant large evergreens
in front of the openings. He said they are willing to work with the city to meet the transparency
requirement.

Commissioner Croshaw asked if the units are all one-bedroom. Justin Campbell said that all of the
units will be approximately 700 square foot studios with a kitchenette, and they will also provide
laundry facilities in either the units or a single facility for the building.

Commissioner Lucero asked about the reasoning for removing condition number 2. Justin Campbell
stated that they have an issue with the 30-day stay limitation, this would not be a condition for any
other hotel. If someone wanted to stay for 45 days they would sign an agreement for 30 days and
then extend their stay another 15 days. R. Holley said that this would be a similar condition put on
any other hotel as they are not an apartment complex. The condition is saying there is no long-term
residential use, it needs to be used as a hotel, not an apartment.

Mohammed Abdullahi, Assistant City attorney said that all this is saying is that someone cannot sign
a lease for longer than 30 days.

Andy Bently, the project developer said that someone can stay at a hotel for 8 months if they like.
No one tells someone how long they can stay at a hotel. He said he doesn’t care how long someone
stays, he is not going to monitor their stay and kick someone out at 30 days. They can check out and
check back in at 30 days because they will not be able to book a room for longer than 30 days. He
stated that a hotel is allowed in this zone, they are building a hotel, and therefore a condition should
not be added regarding the hotel use.

PUBLIC: Jeannie Simmonds asked if there is a front entrance, office, or sign that lets people know
it is a hotel. As proposed it looks like an apartment complex.

Gary Saxton, Downtown Alliance said that this is a good project for downtown. The design is
attractive and fits in with the surroundings.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Peterson said that there needs to be something added to the ground
floor to break up the brick wall.


Commissioner Guth agreed with commissioner Peterson and said that the ground floor should have
a more welcoming presence.

Commissioner Croshaw said he is concerned with the reduced number of parking stalls proposed.

Commissioner Dourte said she is also concerned with the parking. This area gets very busy when
there are events happening across the street at the Logan High School and Rec Center and there is
not a lot of street parking available.

Commissioner Heare made a comment about there not being many people who arrive for an
extended stay at a hotel without a car.

R. Holley said that a 15% or less reduction can be approved by the Community Development
Director. A 15% – 25% reduction can be approved by the Planning Commission. Mike DeSimone,
Community Development Director has looked at the alternate parking plan and approved a 5.6%
parking reduction.

Commissioner Heare and Doutre discussed removing the portion of condition number two that states
no long-term residential uses. R. Holley said removing that would not change the land use
classification. The reason this was added to the condition is because the floor plans have not been
submitted and 700 square-foot rooms seem somewhat ambiguous. Often times these properties get
sold and it is important that it is clear on the recorded permit that this is not an apartment complex,
it is a hotel. Condition number two simply states the definition in the LDC - Hotel is transient
occupancy and the residential use is limited to no more than 30 days of continuous occupancy.

There was a discussion among the commission on requiring 60% fenestration on the ground floor,
submitting floor plans for the committee to review, and continuing the project to review these items.

MOTION: Commissioner Lewis moved to continue PC 22-049 to the September 22, 2022 meeting.
Commissioner Croshaw seconded the motion.

Moved: D. Lewis                  Seconded: R. Croshaw                       Approved: 7-0

Yea: Lucero, Heare, Croshaw, Doutre, Guth, Peterson, Lewis              Nay:           Abstain:


PC 22-038 Land Development Code Amendment-Mixed Use, Chapters 17.10 & 17.34 -
Continued from the August 11th meeting. [Code Amendment] Mike DeSimone/Logan City are
requesting a Code Amendment to the Land Development Code to Chapter 17.10.100 Mixed-Use
Development Standards and Chapter 17.34 Residential Density and Height Bonuses. The changes
modify specific density, design & development standards for projects in the mixed-use zones as
well as establish a density bonus program for mixed-use projects.


STAFF/PROPONENT: R. Holley reviewed the updates to the proposed Mixed Use code amendment
from the August 11th meeting and asked the commission to determine if the 1,000-foot measurement
for commercial proximity in lou of commercial space should be measured as a pedestrian walkable
distance on public sidewalks, or as the crow flies. The Commissioners agreed that the 1,000 feet
needs to be measured as a public walkable path. R. Holley asked the commission for clarification
on the 15-foot sidewalk width. Does the 15-foot sidewalk include an 8-10 foot walking space and a
5-7 furnishing area for planters, benches, tables, etc. Or was the entire 15-foot sidewalk intended


as a clear walkable sidewalk. Commissioner Croshaw said the 15-foot width was intended to include
the 5-7 foot furnishing area. R. Holley reviewed the change to the increased usable outdoor space
option for a density bonus. The provision of additional useable outdoor space in order to obtain a
residential density bonus was increased from 100% to 150%.

PUBLIC: No public comments were made.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Lucero said she is happy with where the mixed-use code is.
Commissioner Doutre said this is a good start toward the future plan. Commissioner Guth asked if
there has been any feedback from developers. R. Holley said that Michael Jewell and Bracken
Atkinson have both reviewed the updates and have not voiced any concerns. Commissioner
Peterson said he is concerned with parking reductions but overall is in support of the amendment.
Commissioner Doutre asked if the parking reductions will be looked at for approval on a case-by-
case basis. R. Holley said that the planning commission will have the authority to reduce parking
requirements subject to the alternative parking plan standards.

MOTION: Commissioner Doutre moved to forward a recommendation of approval to the Municipal
Council with the amendments discussed above on PC 22-038 Land Development Code Amendment-
Mixed Use, Chapters 17.100 & 17.34. Commissioner Croshaw seconded the motion.

RECOMMENDED FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL
1. Utah State Law authorizes local Planning Commission to recommend ordinance changes to the
   legislative body (Municipal Council).
2. The Code Amendments are done in conformance with the requirements of Title 17.51 of the
   Logan Municipal Code.
3. The proposed Code Amendments are consistent with the Logan City General Plan.
4. The proposed Code Amendments to the Mixed Use Development Standards and the Residential
   Density Bonus Standards will further refine the City’s approach to mixed use projects and ensure
   integration into existing neighborhoods.
5. The proposed Code Amendment defines the base residential density and includes provisions for
   a performance based residential density program.
6. The proposed language in the Code Amendment provides sufficient guidance and flexibility for
   both project proponents and the Planning Commission in designing and evaluating projects
   within the Mixed Use Zoning District to ensure that the stated intent of the Mixed Use Zone is
   being met and implemented.
7. Public comments and testimony regarding this proposed Code Amendment have been received
   and were considered by the Planning Commission in their deliberations on this matter.

Moved: S. Doutre                 Seconded: R. Croshaw                     Approved: 7-0

Yea: Lucero, Ken, Croshaw, Doutre, Guth, Peterson, Lewis           Nay:           Abstain:
