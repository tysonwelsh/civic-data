         2.1    Presentation and Discussion of Tentative Budget for the 2024-2025 Fiscal Year.
                (StaffPresenters,Stephanie Thaver and Bruce Kartchner),

Administrative Services Director, Bruce Kartchner, provided an overview ofthe Budget process. He
explained that the City is required by State law to pass a balanced Tentative Budget by the first
Tuesday in May. Bluffdale usually passes its Tentative Budget between late March and late April.
In 2024,they added a Budget Review Committee("BRC"), which delayed the process slightly. Prior
to tentative approval, it is viewed as the City Manager's Budget. Once it passes, it is then the City
Council's Budget. After approval,the Council will continue with an in-depth review and discussions
and provide additional direction on the final numbers.

There will be a Public Hearing at the City Council Meeting later that evening before the Tentative
Budget is passed. The Final Budget must be passed at a Public Hearing in June. The Budget is what
Staff anticipates will happen,but adjustments need to be made at various times, and amended Budgets
will be presented throughout the year. That is a normal part of the process. As part of this process,
the City Council will have the option to do a property tax increase, which will trigger a Truth in
Taxation Hearing, a standalone hearing about proposed changes to property taxes in August.

The City Council had one meeting regarding the general Budget requirements where they heard from
all Departments about their needs, expectations, and desires. As part ofthat, Mr. Kartchner presented
an overview of budgeting concepts. He gave that overview again to help residents understand the
process.


When creating the Budget, Staff and the City Council identify what is important to residents and
business owners in the community. The government is set up to service residents and business
owners, so it needs to know what they want. As an example, a study was done with respect to what
is important to citizens. Parks and trails were identified as the highest and most important concern.
That is an important budgetary consideration. Bluffdale is a very diverse city, and not just
geographically. It has wealthy, middle-class,and lower-income residents. It has empty nesters, young
families, and families with teens, as well as some families with all three. Some citizens are very
social, and others are more private. There is a variety of people in Bluffdale, and they all have
different priorities. It is up to the Staff and City Council to try to balance the different needs and
wants ofcitizens. That can be challenging due to money constraints.

The Budget is the City's revenue and expenses. As the Staff and Council go through the budgeting
process, they prioritize, but there is always a balance. For example, if 100% ofrevenues go toward
police service but nothing goes to roads, the police would not be able to get where they need to go.
There is always a balancing act between the different needs and wants ofthe City and its residents.

Staff is tasked with budgeting the City's restrained resources to its many needs. To do that, they
approach things differently than in a business. Income and expenses are broken into different Funds.
The General Fund covers most ofthe City's services and operating expenses. The Water Fund looks

and operates like a business, so its income statement looks like a business income statement. For
example,the Water Fund Budget includes depreciation. There is no depreciation expense in the City's
other Funds. The other various Funds are restricted in that they are set up to collect and expend money
for very specific things. The money in those Funds cannot be spent on einything else.

With regard to priorities, one thing that has been highlighted in previous years is Fire Department
salaries. Since the 2018-2019 Budget,the City Council has been working to improve Fire Department
salaries in order to man the stations at an appropriate and safe level for both the firefighters and
residents. Mr. Kartchner presented a chart depicting City growth. Fire fees have increased
dramatically each year, proportionate to growth. Administrative Department costs have risen much
more slowly than the Fire Department because it is possible to leverage administration dollars over
more people as the City grows.

Both in the Budget Retreat and over the coming weeks as the Council identifies and priorities items
to focus on, the City Council will be considering requests and ranking them based on their level of
importance to the Council. The Tentative Budget is based on the direction of City Manager,
Mark Reid. His goal is to ensure that the Budget reflects the Council's requests, but it is fi"om this
point forward that the Council will make its own determinations for the Final Budget.

As Staff goes through the budgeting process, they identify costs and revenues. The City provides
specific primary services like street cleaning and maintaining the parks. Some primary services are
recurring; for example,park restrooms must be cleaned, and garbage emptied weekly. Some primary
services occur less frequently; for example,recent flooding at one ofthe parks in Perry Farms. Some
services are discretionary, meaning they may or may not happen as funds permit.

Not all revenue sources are equal. Some,like property taxes, are recurring. Some are non-recurring;
for example, new developments pay one-time development and permitting fees. Property taxes are
the City's most stable revenue stream. Franchise fees related to energy costs are another somewhat
stable source ofrevenue but are slightly more volatile than property taxes. Other service fees include
street lighting, stormwater, and garbage collection. These items combined cover the City's basic
expenses.

Slightly more volatile income sources include sales taxes, business licenses, court fines, and things of
that nature. They go up and down quite a bit. For the last few years, sales taxes have been a stable
form of review. However, in 2008-2010 when the economy crashed, cities that relied on sales tax
revenue suffered because it dropped off significantly as people's buying habits changed.

The final component is development-related fees, which can be very volatile. When Mr. Reid first
came to the City,they only had three building permits in a full year. Over the last few years, hundreds
of building permits have been pulled every year. Additional costs are associated with those specific
things that need to be covered.


Mr. Kartchner discussed budgeting basics. City budgets serve a different purpose than business
budgets, and as such the basic tenets of municipal budgeting differ from business budgeting. In
business, the budget is a goal, something they are trying to achieve. It may or may not be aggressive.
In government,the expenditures budget is called Appropriations. Rather than being a goal or forecast
of what they are expecting to spend, it is the maximum amount they are allowed to spend.

At the end ofthe year, if the City spends less than budgeted, it doesn't mean they were sandbagging
or expecting to make more or spend less. It's just that that was the maximum they could spend and
what they were expecting to spend, but for whatever reason they did not. An example is a previous
year when the Fire Department could not fill open positions; $500,000 ofits salaries budget was left
unspent at the end ofthe year due to the vacancies.

By State law, the City Budget is balanced. That balancing must include an unappropriated Fund
balance of 5% to 35%. At the end of the year, part of a balanced Budget includes having those
reserves available. In preparing this year's Budget, Mr. Kartchner reviewed another city's budget
documents. That city has a goal to maintain its unappropriated Fund balance at between 30% and
35%. If it drops below that level, they are required to specifically address the shortfall and discuss
their plans to bring it back to the requirement.

Based on available year-to-date information, sales tax revenue is approximate as budgeted for Fiscal
