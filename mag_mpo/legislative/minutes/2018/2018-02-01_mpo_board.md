Mountainland Association of Governments (MAG) Metropolitan Planning Organization (MPO) Orientation – Shawn Seager, MAG Staff The MAG MPO is the Transportation Planning authority for Utah County. The MAG Regional Planning Committee is the Board of Directors for the MPO and is made up of the 25 Utah County Mayors, 3 Utah County Commissioners, a UDOT Commissioner and a UTA Trustee. All members have an equal vote. This committee meets the first Thursday of each month. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee February 1, 2018 Page 4 The MAG MPO is one of four MPO’s in Utah. Other include the Wasatch Front Region Council (Salt Lake, Davis and Weber), Cache MPO (Logan) and Dixie MPO (St. George). MPO’s are required by the US Department of Transportation in areas over 50,000 population. MPO’s empower Mayors to make important transportation decisions. The MAG RPC does three main things 1. Convene decision makers 2. Develop a Region Transportation Plan 3. Select and fund Transportation projects There is also a MPO Finance Advisory Committee composed of the top sales tax generating cities, Chamber of Commerce and UDOT, and a Technical Committee Advisory (TAC) composed of city staff. Regional Transportation Plan Update and “A New Day is Dawning” Video Presentation – Shawn Eliot, MAG Staff Mountainland’s current Regional Transportation Plan, TransPlan40, was completed in 2015 and includes $10 billion of regional transportation projects and programs. MPO staff have tools to create the plan and predict where future improvements are needed with the use of local and regional land use plans to predict future traffic conditions. Work on the next plan, TransPlan50, has begun and will be completed in 2019. New state population projections for Utah County estimate a growth from 620k people today to 1.3 million people by 2050 and 1.6 million by 2065. This growth presents major challenges for the transportation system as the current system is evolving from rural to urban. There are also geographic challenges of a large lake and mountains, inadequate connections between communities, and development pressures. Mountainland will be conducting a series of Transportation Summits in March and May. Three meetings will be held each month, one in Lehi, Orem, and Spanish Fork. Invitations have been sent to a large group of decision makers and technical staff for their respective areas. Invitees include mayors, county commissioners, city managers, city councils, planning commissions, city staff, and representatives from UDOT and UTA. The goal of the meetings is to educate what the demands are on our transportation system, get feedback and ideas of how they will address the future transportation needs, and inform them on the findings of the state’s long-term demographic and economic projections. Invitations will be mailed out this month. MAG staff encourages city councils, staff, and planning commissions to attend. MAG Transportation Improvement Program Project Selection Idea Scores and Studies Approval - Bob Allen, MAG Staff The Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) is a compilation of regional transportation projects sponsored by municipalities, Utah County, UDOT, UTA, and others utilizing various federal, state and local funding sources for the next 4 years. MAG has begun the 2018 TIP project selection process and completed the project idea portion of that process. The scoring results are available online and were provided in the agenda packet. These results are not final for road, transit, active transportation or ITS projects. However, they do provide feedback from the TAC to the applicant. According to MAG policy, project idea scoring is a final recommendation for studies and the following have been recommended by TAC to RPC to be funded: UVU Area Transportation Study - $310k ($200-MAG, $50-UDOT, $10-UTA) North Lakeshore Major Corridors Study - $300k State Street Transit Study - $300k ($250-MAG, $50-UDOT) SR-92 North Regional Traffic Study - $75k Provo UPRR Crossings and 500W Corridor Study - $200k Salem City Active Transportation Study - $45k Total Study Costs = $1,230,000 Total MAG Funds = $1,120,000 Mayor Brunst moved that the identified studies be funded as outlined and be added to the TIP. Mayor Frost seconded the motion.
