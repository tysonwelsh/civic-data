Proposed Rezone at 550 East 900 North from R-1-6 to R-3
          A developer has petitioned to rezone a vacant church property from R-1-6 (Single-family Residential) to R-3 (Multi-
family Residential). The purpose of the rezone is to demolish the vacant church and build townhomes. The Planning Commission
determined that this rezone request does comply with the General Plan, and that townhomes would create an effective transition
from higher density housing at Washington Boulevard to single-family housing to the east ofthe property in question. It was also
noted that a new development would be preferable to a vacant church lot, which could fall into disrepair and negatively impact the
neighborhood. The Planning Commission unanimously recommended rezoning the property from R-1-6 to R-3.
          Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the intent of the zone change
application. He presented an aerial image to orient the Council to the location ofthe subject property and identified adjacent land
uses. There is presently a church located on the property and the applicant is seeking the zone change in order to develop multi-
family housing, which is not permitted in the R-1 zone. Under the present zoning, there are two development options: five one-
half acre lots on the existing road, or .35 acre lots on a cul-desac. The R-3 zoning would allow one 58-unit apartment building or
ten four-plex units per building group dwelling.
          Council member Hyer inquired as to the type of development that would be allowed if the property were zoned R-2. Mr.
Montgomery stated the R-2 zone would allow individual lots with single family or duplex units on them; five duplexes could be
built on the property, for a total of I 0 units.
          Mr. Montgomery then explained the Planning Commission considered General Plan policies, including providing a
defmite edge and buffering, ensuring zoning reflects the prevailing character of the area unless the area is in transition, ensuring
the same or similar zone on both sides of a local street, providing a transition from higher to lower density land uses, and ensuring
concept for the development will reflect neighborhoods and how residents within those neighborhoods will interact. Th~ staff
analysis of the application yielded the following fmdings:
               •    The property to the south and west are zoned multi-family, R-3 having an adjacent rehab facility and condo
                    townhomes.
               •    Rezoning this property to R-3 will allow the petitioner to demo the vacant church building and build
                    townhomes.
               •    The prevailing character of this area is predominantly single-family to the north and east neighborhoods.
               •    Directly to the north is zoned RMH-1 and include the Viking Villa mobile home development.
               •    Single-family lots facing Jefferson Avenue back up to townhomes, in the R-3 Zone.
               •    The rezone would create a consistent pattern along these properties and buffer these abutting single-family
                    homes.
               •    The new multi-family project will be required to add screening and buffers from single-family zones per group
                    dwelling/multi-family development code regulations.
               •    Zoning on the same side of the street vs across the street.
               •    Transition from R-4 to R-1
               •    Continual edge of homes that face Jefferson
          The Planning Commission ultimately voted to recommend approval of the rezone from R-1-6 to R-3.
          Council member Hyer stated he has heard from residents in the neighborhood who are very concerned about the zone
change; they do not feel that it reflects the character of the neighborhood and he suggested that a conditional overlay zone be
applied to the property to ensure that it is developed in a fashion that existing residents are comfortable with. Mr. Montgomery
stated that staff and the Planning Commission has heard from residents who were mainly concerned about the height of the
building to be constructed on the property. He noted this is a legislative issue and the Council has the ability to reject the zoning
petition. This led to discussion among the Council regarding the implications of the zone change; there was a focus on whether the
requested zoning will strengthen the neighborhood and if the proposed use will meet the demand for different housing types in the
community.

Proposed Zone Text Amendments- Business Exchange Industrial (BEl)
Zone
         The Business Exchange Industrial (BEl) Zone currently has a limit of 50,000 sq. ft. for warehousing. A petitioner is
asking to increase the maximum limit so they can pursue distribution services on their property. Market trends seem to be
increasing the demand for distribution services, which require a large amount of warehousing space. The Planning Commission

found the request to be in compliance with the purposes of the BEl Zone, the West Ogden Community Plan, and the General Plan.
The Planning Commission recommended increasing the maximum warehouse size from 50,000 to 70,000 sq. ft. in the BEl Zone.
          Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the implications of the
proposed zone text amendments, which would allow warehouse and distributions buildings to be larger than 50,000 square feet in
the BEl zone and provide for greater capacity to have warehouse and distribution uses as a main use. There are three vacant lots in
the BEl zone: Lot 3, which is three acres; Lot 11, which is 4.38 acres; and Lot 8, which is 5.1 acres. Each lot has open space
connections. The purpose of the BEl zone is to create a light industrial research, development and manufacturing of outdoor
recreation and associated business uses. Based upon existing regulations, there is a warehouse use in small scale as part of
industrial use but is not the main use in this zone. When considering changing conditions, it is appropriate to consider the overall
goal of the area and whether that goal has changed. Additionally, it is important to consider whether the proposed use is desirable
for this area. The property abutting the subject property to the west is occupied by Bloom's Recycling and the Planning
Cmrunission considered whether the proposed use would be appropriate adjacent to the existing use. It may be that the proposed
use is a good transitional use for the area. Planning staff recommended allowing Lot 8 to have up to 110,000 square feet as a
condition use transition use, but not apply the changes to the other lots in the BEl zone. However, after the Planning Commission
reviewed the application and staffs recommendations, they voted to recommend increasing the warehousing size on the remaining
three lots from 50,000 to 70,000 square feet. The two Commissioners who voted in opposition to the recommendation have the
following concerns:
               •    One Commissioner felt that the provisions should have additional buffer requirements.
               •    The other Commissioner felt that staffs recommendation was preferable.
          Council member Choberka asked if the Council could accept Planning staffs recommendation rather than the Planning
Commission's recommendation. Mr. Montgomery answered yes. Council member Choberka stated that she feels that there was a
great deal of importance placed on preservation of the outdoor recreation space adjacent to the BEl area and she will need to
consider whether the increase in warehousing space would negatively impact that space. Council member Hyer agreed and
suggested it may be helpful for the Council to physically visit the area to gain an understanding of the present conditions in the
BEl area before voting on the application. There was a brief discussion about the justification and reasoning for the increased
warehousing square footage, after which the Council invited input from Community and Economic Development Deputy Director
Cooper. Mr. Cooper noted that he feels the recommendation made by Planning staff is appropriate; the vision for the BEl
development was for headquarter companies to manufacture items and provide jobs for the community. However, distribution has
now become a major component and the applicant is a buyer who is seeking to meet that demand. He believes staffs
recommendation is acceptable to the applicant and they are willing to make some revisions to the site plan to orient the truck
parking away from the Trackline bike park. Truck circulation around the building cannot be eliminated, but parking can be
relocated. He stated there is a strict timeframe for the acquisition of the property and he encouraged the Council to make a timely
decision on the application without sending it back to the Planning Cmrunission. He stated he would make himself available to
participate in a site visit with individual Council members or smaller groups of Council members.

Proposed Zone Text Amendments - Conditional Use Permits
          The Planning Department is proposing to change the approval process for certain Conditional Use Pennit (CUP)
amendments. Staff currently refers all CUP amendments to the Planning Commission, but this process is time consuming and
inefficient. The proposed zone text amendment would allow certain minor changes to CUPs to be approved administratively, by
the Planning Manager. Doing so would allow minor amendments to be approved more quickly, improve customer service, and
make better use of staff resources.
          Deputy Planning Manager Brierly used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the proposed text
amendments. He noted one of the strategies identified by the City Council is to create the most inviting, responsive local
govermnent in the state by continuing to promote a business-friendly enviromnent and by reducing bureaucracy and revising
unnecessary regulations for development. The City's current code regarding CUPs dictates that once issued, a CUP shall not be
enlarged, changed, extended, increased in intensity, or relocated except by approval of the Planning Commission. The proposed
text amendment would allow minor amendments of approved CUPs to be reviewed at the staff level. He stated minor amendments
could be defmed as one of the following:
               • A modification to a telecommunication facility that replaces, modifies, or adds equipment on an existing tower
                   or structure or modifies ground equipment and that does not increase the height or outer dimensions of the
                   facility.
              • An exterior change that would not create new or increase reasonably anticipated detrimental effects.
              • A change to a different conditional use within the same use category that would not create new or increase
                   reasonably anticipated detrimental effects on surrounding properties or the enviromnent.
              •    An addition that would increase the floor area or outdoor area occupied by the conditional use by no more than
                   10 percent ofthe originally approved floor area or outdoor area, up to a maximum of2,000 square feet.
              • A modification to a parking, loading or maneuvering area that adds no more than 2,000 square feet of
                   impervious surface.
          The Plam1ing Cmmnission recommended approval of the proposed text amendments.

Flagship Community Reinvestment Project Area Plan and Budget
          The Administration is recommending the Board approve the plan and budget for the Flagship Community Reinvestment
Area (CRA). The proposal includes a total tax increment budget of$2,904,933 over a maximum often (1 0) years. The proposed
incentive is post-performance and may be used to reimburse the developer, in this case Amer Sports, for improvements made to
the property. The project area, as established in the Flagship Survey Area, is Lot 58 Business Depot Ogden (BDO) Plan 18. The
necessary interlocal agreement with the Board is proposed to include Ogden City, Weber County and Ogden School District. No
other taxing entities are participating in the Area.
          Community and Economic Development Deputy Director Cooper used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to review the
draft project area plan for the Flagship CRA. The objectives of the CRA are to retain and grow Amer headquarters in Ogden's
AmeriCan Center and facilitate the location and expansion of the Amer Distribution center in BDO. He briefly discussed the
economic impact that Amer has had on the Ogden community, noting the distribution center will have a direct and indirect impact
on jobs and earnings in Weber County; jobs in Weber County will increase by a total of292 and earnings will increase by $16
million. Proposed incentives for the project include support from the State of Utah and locally; the State will provide a EDTIF
post-performancetax credit up to 30 percent of new state tax revenues over 10 years for a total of$1,314,054 and an IAF grant of
$2,000 for each job that is retaining in Utah for a total of $300,000. The local incentive includes tax increment for the real and
personal property at the site; the real property incentive will be $360,000 and the personal property incentive will be $2.1 million.
He presented a graphic to illustrate the manner in which tax increment is created and calculated. The base year valuation for the
subject property is $20,758,000 and with improvements, the added taxable valuation is expected to increase to $57,508,000. The
term of the CRA would be 10 years with an entity participation rate of 100 percent. The maximum City tax increment contribution
will be $645,133. The maximum collectable by the agency is $2.9 million, broken down as follows:
               • $2.4 available for general development investment;
              •    $242,493 available for housing investment; and
              •    $202,740 available for agency administration costs.

Junction Recreation Center Naming Rights Agreement
          The Administration is proposing the Board approve a naming rights agreement with Amer Sports Winter & Outdoor
Company (AS WO) for the Recreation Center at 2261 Kiesel A venue and 3 34 23rd Street within the Junction. The naming rights
for the recreation center, currently named "Salomon Center," would be continued to December 31, 2034 with additional areas
inside the recreation center named "Atomic Climbing Wall" and "Suunto Fitness Studio." All of the names are brands that fall
under the ownership of ASWO. There is no monetary compensation for the naming rights; Administrative staff indicate that the
naming agreement provides mutual benefit to the Redevelopment Agency and ASWO.
          Community and Economic Development Deputy Director Cooper noted this item is related to the previous agenda item.
He used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide background information on the naming rights for the Junction Recreation
Center. At this time it is an extension of the agreement; the RDA, ASWO, and the building tenant desire to maintain the Solomon
Center name. The benefits include maintaining a widely recognized building name that is already in common use and maintaining
awareness that Salomon, a nationally recognized outdoor brand, operates in Ogden. The terms of the agreement are as follows:
              • Extends term through 2034 with option to extend further by mutual agreement.
              •    Terminates if ASWO ceases to operate in Ogden.
              •    Ogden RDA can terminate if ASWO engages in conduct deemed contrary to community standards of justice,
                   honesty, or good morals.
              • ASWO is responsible to install and maintain associated signage.
              •    RDA and tenants are not prohibited from displaying additional signage.
          Board member Stephens inquired as to the price being paid for the naming rights. Mr. Cooper stated that when the
original agreement was negotiated, the naming rights were granted in consideration of Amer relocating to Ogden. Maintaining that
agreement through the expansion seems appropriate.
          Council Deputy Director Symes stated there will be an item on the next meeting agenda to schedule a public hearing for
both this item and the previous agenda item.
