New Carwash at 4681 South Redwood Road, Taylorsville, Utah. (Jim Spung, AICP/Senior Planner)

3.1 Mr. Spung presented this item. He stated that Elliott Smith, on behalf of Mister Carwash, is requesting
Planning Commission review and approval of a final non-administrative conditional use permit and site plan to allow
the construction and use of a carwash to be located at 4681 South Redwood Road Taylorsville, Utah. The applicant’s
proposal includes the removal of existing structures (the existing Village Inn Restaurant) to make way for the
construction of a new tunnel carwash facility to be owned and operated by Mister Carwash. Staff conducted a site
visit accompanied by Police Department representatives from Taylorsville to determine any safety issues they

The applicant receives canal company approval for stormwater discharge related to this development prior
to issuance of a building permit.
3.4 APPLICANT ADDRESS: Elliott Smith. Mr. Smith wanted to address the use of columnar type trees,
indicating he did not feel that would alleviate the problem of noise and further that they grow much slower.
He felt the bigger head type trees they are proposing would better buffer the neighbors both at planting
and maturity He explained that the owners have added more berming along 4700 South and would
be installing boulders, as well as additional landscaping. Regarding the wall height, they preferred the 6’
height for safety reasons in that the 8’ high wall would be easier for someone to hide behind fully concealed.
Commissioner Wilkey asked the applicant about his comment estimating in a three-year period that
there would probably be 500 cars a day go through that and wondered if that would create a backlog onto
the street. If it was all even there would be 41 cars in an hour go through that in what looks to be a double
lane which could probably accommodate a dozen. Mr. Smith said it is a triple lane, called the POS (Point of
Sale) System there. There are three lanes which he figured provides a 25 to 30 car stack before going into
the tunnel, with five cars in the tunnel at a time. Close to 35 to 40 car stack at peak times. Also designed
into this is putting the que lane as far east as possible to maximize the space to back up. UDOT also had a
concern about this as well and asked about it when the applicant went through the UDOT Variance process
with them, and which has subsequently been approved. They had to go through the Variance process
because whenever the land use changes and any changes are made to their access points, whether asking
for a new one or in this case shutting one down they have to go through the Variance process with UDOT.
If you take the 35 or 40 car stack to get to the back of the POS right before you get to the cross access to
the north, there are probably 40 cars at that point and then there are another ten cars that stack before it
goes out onto Redwood Road. Number two they feel it is safer for everybody to plan it this way.

concerns mentioned by the Commissioners. Mr. Spung added that the Commissioners
must still clarify the proposed wall height in the motion.

noise. That pear trees do grow fast but also die fast. He was not sure there was the need to plant

5s 9§21 - Public Hearing and Consideration of a Preliminary Subdivision Plat to Create Nine Residential Lots
at Approximately 4924 South 3200 West, Taylorsville, Utah. (Jim Spung, AICP/Senior Planner)
5.1 Mr. Spung presented this item.

5.1.1 Mr. Taylor Smith, Clue Management, is requesting Planning Commission review and approval of a
9-lot residential preliminary subdivision plat “GT Estates” for approximately 85,900 square feet
(1.972 acres) of real property located at 4924 South 3200 West, Taylorsville, Utah. Approval of
the preliminary subdivision plat would allow for final subdivision review and approval of nine (9)
new single-family residential lots as well as a new public street (Snarr Haven Court). Two of the
proposed lots (Lot 7 and 8) are “flag lots” with a shared private access drive from Snarr Haven
Court. The proposed street configuration has been reviewed and approved by the City Engineering
Department. The proposed subdivision complies with the minimum lot area and dimensional
requirements. For lots that do not have 55 feet of frontage along a public street, the front building
setback line is measured from where the lot is 55 feet wide rather than from the front property
line.

5.1.2 The applicant is requesting approval from the Planning Commission to allow more than 20 percent
of the lots in the subdivision to be flag lots, per Section 13.21220 (Flag Lots). The current
subdivision proposal includes two flag lots which calculates to 22 percent of the total lots. This
exceeds the maximum allowed; however, the Planning Commission may approve a subdivision
layout where more than 20 percent of the lots are flag lots.

5.1.3. Due to the lot size and configuration, the buildable area of Lot 8 is located on the western portion
of the property If this configuration is approved, Planning Staff recommends that a future dwelling
be oriented facing eastward, with the rear property line being the western property line. This
would result in a minimum 15-foot setback from the western property line rather than a 5-foot
minimum setback if it were treated as a side yard.

5.1.4. There is an existing irrigation canal that runs along the northern boundary of the proposed plat,
adjacent to proposed Lots 8 and 9. According to Section 13.28.110, development adjacent to a
canal is subject to the following:
5.1.4.1 Any parcel being subdivided or developed that is adjacent to or has within its boundaries

ZONING TEXT AMENDMENT
6. File 2222 - Public Hearing and Recommendation to the City Council for a Zoning Text Amendment to
Section 13.04.090, Related to the Minimum Land Area Required to Establish a Zoning District. (Jim
Spung, AICP/Senior Planner)

6.1 Mr. Spung presented this item.

6.1.1 The City of Taylorsville is proposing to amend the Land Development Code to eliminate the
minimum land area requirements to establish a nonresidential or mixed-use zoning district. The
current Land Development Code requires a lot or parcel to be a certain size to qualify to be rezoned
to a particular zoning district.

6.1.2 This approach is common with older zoning codes and is used as a regulatory mechanism to ensure
that a property is a particular size so it can accommodate certain types of development. As
Taylorsville has developed and evolved, there are very few lots and parcels that meet these
minimum area thresholds that are being considered for development or redevelopment In some
cases, a zone change is appropriate to align the property’s zoning designation with the City’s
General Plan; however, these minimum area thresholds act as a barrier to infill and redevelopment
potential in the City.

6.1.3. Staff recommends that the minimum area thresholds be eliminated to allow the City Council to
determine on a case-by-case basis whether or not a property should be rezoned to a particular
district, without being constrained to only considering properties with a particular land area.

6.1.4 Allother development standards still apply to development projects in these zoning districts. Staff
does not anticipate any detrimental impact or effect associated with this amendment.

6.2 STAFF RECOMMENDATION: Staff recommends the Planning Commission forward a_ positive
recommendation to the City Council to amend the Land Development Code related to the minimum land
area required to establish a zoning district, as specified in Exhibit A of the Staff Report
