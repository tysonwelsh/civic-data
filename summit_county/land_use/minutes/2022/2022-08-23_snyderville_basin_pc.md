Jenn ifer Strader , Senior Planner, presented for continued public hearing and possible action on 
a propose amendment to the Snyde rville Basin General Plan , to add a Moderate-Income 
Housing Element per Utah Code . A total of 12 strategies were being proposed, 7 of which 
curren tly exist in the General Plan. Planner Strader asked for specific changes from the 
Com mission with a reminder of the October 1st deadline . 
Comm issioners provided commen ts and asked clarifying questions of Planner Strader . Jennifer 
Strade r, Patrick Putt and Helen Strachan responded to the questions and provided additional 
information to the discussion. 
Commissioner Kucera opened the public hearing (6:48 P.M) 
Bonnie Park (6:49 P.M.) addressed the Commissioners . She had compared the presentation 
from 8/9 to the current packet. She noticed in Strategy A that several implementation measures 
were eliminated and most all of those that were deleted contemplated if a major trans it 
investment corridor was created. It appeared from the outside that with the recent fund ing for 
224 BRT improvements that might be predetermined to be a major transit investment corridor. 
Ms. Park added that with respect to Strategy C, she was the one person that opposed the 
eliminate of impact fees because that is the way developmen t pays its own way for recreation, 
water , sewer and transportation . She suggested that if they keep that strategy they reverse the 
orde r of implementation. She was highly supportive of Strategy H and has always been a 
champion of interlocal agreements. She thought Strategy J "hold community public hearings " 
possibly should be an implementation measure rather than a strategy. She also suggested in 
the Implementation Measures that follow 6.1.35 and 6.1.36 adding the language "are closed " 
following "public hearings " as she could see those public hearings going on for a little while. 
Commissioner Kucera closed public hearing. (6:54 P.M.) 
Commissioner Kucera stated the Commissioners had a lot to decompress, and asked the 
Commiss ioners if they were accepting a Top 5 approach or if they wanted to suggest an 
alternative view. 
Commissioner Conabee stated that he would like to hear from staff about what their top 5 might 
be. 
Commiss ioners further discussed strategies and options and asked questions . Planner Strader 
and Director Putt responded to quest ions provided additional perspective to the discuss ion. 
Commissioner Conabee moved to forward a positive recommendation to the Summit 
County Council for the adoption of the Moderate-Income Housing plan based on the 
Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law in Section Ill of this report with emphasis that 
Strategy A, B, E, F, G, HI, J be approved, that Strategy C and D be eliminated and that 
Strategy J be examined for legality and assessment of current and future impacts as the 
Planning Commission has concerns about the creation of Housing and Transit 
Reinvestment Zones within the County that have not been previously planned for and 
discussed. Commissioner Cooke seconded the motion; all voted in favor (6-0) (7:27 P.M.)

Mike Tanner, COO Park City School District, (7:37 P.M.) addressed the Commission regarding 
concerns of closures on Blue Bird Lane. He stated that parking in front of school, drop-off and 
pick-up for parents was the same, but due to delays there was a short-term fix that put buses 
loading and unloading on Blue Bird lane. The potential closures would be for approximately 20 
minutes in the morning and 20 minutes in the afternoon and would be designed to ensure the 
safety of the kids. 
Commissioners asked follow up questions of Mike Tanner and Planner Caus.

Commission Comments (7:47 P.M.) 
Commissioner Cooke (7:47 P.M.) stated that at one time there was nothing in code to allow for 
trailheads with parking. He added that people just parked at trailheads and it became a non­
complying or non-conforming thing in the code and in a lot of cases were situated in residential 
neighborhoods. It seemed like a good thing to have more access points to trails and more trail 
systems. He added that there was a proliferation of over-parking at the Round Valley North 
trailhead, spilling out onto the street, parking on both sides of the street and parking on and 
blocking the bike path. He wondered if adding trailhead parking to the code was an example of 
creating unforeseen negative impacts to rural residential neighborhoods and what would 
happen if trailhead parking was taken out of the code in rural residential neighborhoods. There's 
no enforcement on street parking happening, but there's a significant challenge happening to 
the neighborhood . He wondered if this was something the Commission should proactively 
consider and talk about.
