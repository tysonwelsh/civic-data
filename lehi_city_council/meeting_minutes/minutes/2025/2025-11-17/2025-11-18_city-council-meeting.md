11) Consideration of Resolution #2025-84 authorizing the issuance and sale of Sales Tax
     Revenue Bonds to finance all or a portion of the construction of a science and
     technology building on the campus of Thanksgiving Point.

McKay Christensen and Jason Burningham explained the project’s mission, economic impact
and financial benefits. They said that the project expands STEM education access, especially for
low-income kids who already receive free admission to Thanksgiving Point’s programs. They
Lehi City Council Meeting                        4                              November 18, 2025
said that an independent study projected more than $400 million in total economic and fiscal
benefit over 20 years, including about $54 million directly to Lehi (roughly $2.2 million per year
in new general-fund revenues). By using a sales tax revenue bond backed by the city’s strong
credit, the interest rate is significantly lower than Thanksgiving Point could get on its own,
generating an estimated $6.9 million in present-value savings. Because Thanksgiving Point is a
501(c)(3), those savings must be reinvested in facilities and programs rather than paid to
shareholders.
Council and staff also emphasized that Lehi is leveraging its credit. Multiple protections were
built in: a ground lease and building as collateral, debt service and capital reserve funds funded
by Thanksgiving Point, and a pledge of net revenues from all Thanksgiving Point facilities (not
just the new center), supported by an endowment. Utah County is also anticipated to cover
roughly the first three years of bond payments.

Michelle Stalling’s concerns centered on the use of public credit and overall taxpayer risk. She
was uneasy pledging Lehi’s sales tax revenues for a facility run by a private nonprofit, citing the
Utah Constitution’s limits on lending public credit and noting Lehi already has substantial debt
backed by pledged revenues. She preferred exploring a conduit bond (or similar structure) that
could still provide tax-exempt financing without tying up city sales tax and wanted a side-by-side
comparison before committing. Michelle was also concerned that Utah County or the state may
ultimately contribute less than expected and was uncomfortable with the city owning a building
operated by Thanksgiving Point under long, complex lease/ground-lease arrangements.

Other Councilmembers concluded that the community educational benefits, projected revenue to
Lehi, and layered risk protections justified moving forward with the sales tax revenue bond
structure.

Motion:               Heather Newall moved to approve Resolution #2025-84 authorizing the
                      issuance and sale of Sales Tax Revenue Bonds to finance all or a portion of
                      the construction of a science and technology building on the campus of
                      Thanksgiving Point and that in the lease agreement section 3.5 which refers
                      to $1.50 per each ticket, be changed to the net revenue of all facilities of
                      Thanksgiving Point, and also include in either the ground or lease agreement
                      that the science and technology center facility will be operated the same as
                      the current Thanksgiving Point facilities.

                      Randy Farnsworth, legal counsel, clarified that agreement be modified from
                      ticket sales to net revenues from all of Thanksgiving Point facilities, and
                      secondly, that the new science technology facility be operated substantially
                      similar to public programming as Thanksgiving Point currently operates.

                      Heather Newall seconded the motion.

                      Michelle Stallings suggested making the language broader rather than
                      applying it to a specific section. She also asked to clarify the facility
                      operations.
