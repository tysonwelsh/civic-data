    of the University of Utah Health regarding policy that effects legislation and
    community engagement. He indicated that the University of Utah sees over 700,000

    traveled by West Valley City residents to get their healthcare from the University

       Councilmember Christensen asked how many EV charging stations are needed.
       Jonathan replied that this is a moving target and there is no way to determine a
       specific number as it will increase based on how many electric vehicles are
       produced and purchased by residents or people who work in West Valley City.
       Mayor Lang stated that the State and Salt Lake County have put in money for
       various water grants and asked if this information will be on the website for
       residents to learn how to obtain some of those funds. Jonathan replied yes. Mayor
       Lang asked how the RDA incentives would work for businesses. Jonathan replied
       that no new incentives are being proposed but every time the RDA looks at doing
       a tax increment incentive, staff uses a ROI matrix that looks at everything from tax
       benefits to the City versus developer, jobs, salary of those jobs, indirect financial
       benefits, etc. He indicated that sustainability efforts have now been added to that
       review and evaluation process. Mayor Lang asked if electric farms are available to
       Utah. Jonathan replied yes and noted that as we get closer to 2030, this may be a
       consideration that the Council will need to look at. Councilmember Whetstone
       asked how the data was collected for some of the stats provided. Jonathan replied
       those numbers were obtained from Rocky Mountain Power.

       The Mayor and Council had no further questions or concerns.

  Mayor Lang stated there has been interest from members of the City Council to
  rejoin the Utah League of Cities and Towns. Nicole indicated that a memo was sent
  to the Council from Wayne Pyle with a history and financial cost impact. She
  indicated that staff is open for Council direction. Councilmember Harmon stated
  that he has participated in trainings and sees value in being able to attend those.
  Councilmember Nordfelt agreed but added that the City is not prevented from
  attending those trainings, there is simply a higher fee for non-members.

  Councilmember Huynh asked if Cameron Diehl, Director of ULCT, could provide
  a snapshot of what the League does. Cameron indicated that ULCT represents
  municipal government interests with a strong, unified voice at the State and Federal
  levels. He stated that the Legislative Session is truly a year-round effort and noted
  that in the coming weeks, the League will be working on hundreds of bills and
  appropriations. Cameron stated that the League works on virtually every policy
  issue with the exception of healthcare and education. He indicated that the value of
  membership is where the League gets its strength and detailed how communities
  work together to get positive outcomes for their municipalities. Cameron explained
  how bills are approached and noted that the top three focuses for the League are
  generally land use, public safety, and growth.

  Nicole stated that the City is extremely appreciative to Cameron and his
  responsiveness and efforts to help accommodate some of West Valley City’s unique
  challenges. She indicated that one of those challenges is that West Valley City is
  nearly built out and something that may work for hundreds of other City’s, may not
  work for West Valley City. She noted that this can make it difficult when it comes
  to supporting or not supporting certain bills but added that Cameron has been great
  at trying to find a way to facilitate a path forward. Lincoln Shurtz, Government
  Relations Director, agreed with comments expressed by Nicole. He detailed some
  specific issues that the City has had different perspectives on in the past, including
  homelessness, and indicated that these issues can be addressed using a tandem
  approach with City staff and the League. Lincoln indicated that it’s important that
  West Valley City has its representatives who are looking at benefits and cons
  specific to the City but noted that it can also be important for staff and the Council
  to work with the League and collaborate on specific issues as well.

  Councilmember Whetstone stated that he sees value in rejoining the League.
  Councilmember Huynh agreed. Councilmember Christensen stated he was
  probably the most involved with the League and provided a brief history on his
  interactions in the past. He indicated that he does not have a strong preference either
  way on re-joining. Mayor Lang asked if the City would be able to participate in the
  National League as well. Cameron replied yes. Councilmember Fitisemanu stated
