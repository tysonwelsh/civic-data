        3. TXT-20-01; MEDICAL CANNABIS & SPECIALTY TOBACCO USES &
           REGULATIONS; ZONING ORDINANCE TEXT AMENDMENT;
           COMMERCIAL & INDUSTRIAL RELATED ZONES; CITY STAFF
           (APPLICANT)



PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION JANUARY 8TH, 2020
Mr. Alex Murphy presented that UT HB 3001, adopted in December 2018, and UT SB 1002,
adopted in the 2019 first special session, provides for the licensing and regulation of Cannabis
Production Establishments (including cannabis cultivation facilities, cannabis processing
facilities, and independent cannabis testing facilities) and Medical Cannabis Pharmacies
throughout the state of Utah. Included in these legislative acts are requirements and limitations
imposed on municipalities governing the establishment of cannabis-related uses.

Staff approached the City Council in July 2019 seeking direction on how to incorporate
cannabis- related and, because of their similarities in State regulations, specialty tobacco uses
into the City’s Zoning Ordinance. In response to that discussion, Staff prepared a summary of the
proposed land use regulations and sought input from the Planning Commission in October 2019
before moving forward with the formal ordinance amendment process.

The draft summary includes new use categories, appropriate zones for the new uses, proximity
requirements, sign standards, and definitions. These requirements are generally copied from HB
3001 and State Retail Specialty Tobacco Business regulations.

A few items to note regarding the proposed language:

   •   Proximity requirements for cannabis-related uses are slightly differently than
       requirements for retail tobacco specialty businesses regarding residential uses and zones.
       Cannabis-related business proximity requirements are based on areas zoned primarily for
       residential uses while retail tobacco specialty proximity requirements are based on areas
       that are used or zoned for residential use. “Zoned primarily for” limits the areas that can
       be considered in the proximity calculations to strictly residential zoning designations,
       such as the SF and RM zones, while “used or zoned for” means any nonconforming or
       mixed-use areas and zones must be accounted for when considering a location for a retail
       specialty tobacco business.

   •   Proximity requirements apply across City boundaries. For example, a community
       location in Murray or Sandy will impact where cannabis and retail specialty tobacco
       businesses may locate in Midvale. Staff will need to verify proximity requirements are
       satisfied for locations near City limits, which can be accomplished by obtaining approval
       from the adjacent jurisdiction or reviewing their zoning maps.

   •   State law requires cannabis production establishments be permitted in at least one
       industrial zone. The draft summary proposes allowing production establishments in all
       zones where manufacturing uses are permitted (Clean Industrial, Bingham Junction, and
       Jordan Bluffs Subareas 1-3.) Even where permitted by zone, these uses are still subject to
       applicable proximity requirements.

   •   State law requires medical cannabis pharmacies be permitted in all zones, except where
       prohibited by proximity requirements. The proposed zone list below accounts for areas
       primarily zoned for residential use but does not otherwise limit location of medical
       cannabis pharmacies.

   •   State law prohibits the City from regulating cannabis-related uses in a manner that
       conflicts with the State’s requirements. This includes establishing additional proximity

       requirements between cannabis establishments, medical cannabis pharmacies, retail
       tobacco specialty businesses, or alcohol outlets. While not explicitly indicated, this
       restriction also appears to include homeless shelters.

   •   The definitions listed have been updated following changes made in SB 1002 and match
       the definitions in State law as of December 23, 2019.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION:

Staff has received several questions from the public about the scope and scale of the proposed
amendment and recommends the Planning Commission hold the public hearing before
considering a recommendation.

Ms. Lesley Burns presented that on October 9, 2019, the Planning Commission amended the
Small-Scale Master Plan for the Meadows at Riverwalk Apartment for a garage area expansion
at 1046 West Jordan River Boulevard and approved the attached preliminary site plan with the
following conditions:

   1. The applicant shall prepare a final site plan to be reviewed and approved by the City
      Engineer, Fire Marshal, and City Planner.
   2. The 0.3-acre parcel shall be incorporated into the Meadows at Riverwalk Apartment lot
      to create one property prior to final site plan approval.
   3. A maintenance agreement between the property owner and the City shall be in place for
      the landscape area between the public right-of-way and the perimeter fence of the project
      prior to Certificates of Occupancy being issued for the new structures.

The applicant has been working with staff on these conditions and is nearing completion of
conditions 1 and 3. Condition 2 has become challenging due to HUD financing used to construct
the original apartment development in 2010 that still encumbers the original property. Any
changes made to the original property, such as incorporating the additional 0.3 acres for the
garages, will require full HUD review and approval, essentially starting at step 1 of the original
loan process with new appraisals, inspections, etc. This process is both costly and time
consuming, typically taking over a year to complete.

The original intent of condition 2 was to ensure the garage expansion is used exclusively for the
benefit of the residents of the Meadows at Riverwalk Apartments and does not become a
separate business venture for the owner. In working with the applicant, the applicant’s attorney,
and the city attorney, we have established another possible mechanism, through a Restrictive
Covenant on the garage expansion parcel, that fulfills the original intent of the condition. We are
requesting the Planning Commission considers amending the original condition 2 to allow this
project to move forward without the need to immediately combine the two parcels into one by
amending condition 2 as follows:

   2. The 0.3-acre parcel shall be incorporated into the Meadows at Riverwalk Apartment lot
      to create one property or a restriction shall be placed on the garage expansion parcel
      that precludes the owner from separating the garage use from the adjacent apartment
      complex use prior to final site plan approval.

It is the intent of this amended condition to utilize the attached Restrictive Covenants which has
been reviewed and approved by the City Attorney.

These changes to the configuration have not affected the overall open space calculation, which
remains at 39.5% of lot area. The base requirement for open space is 40% of lot area; however,
as discussed in the Dec. 11 Staff Report, the zoning ordinance provides several methods to
reduce the required open space, such using a licensed landscape architect, providing a landscape
design of special merit, or preserving existing mature trees. Based on the challenging
configuration of the lot, the applicant’s use of a licensed landscape architect in designing the
plan, and the applicant’s efforts to screen impacts from the adjacent properties, Staff
recommends the Planning Commission allow a minor reduction in required open space, limited
to 1-2%, which would allow the design to meet ordinance requirements as-is.

Ms. Lisa Garner, Midvale City Attorney, presented her legal analysis of what subjective
standards the Commission can look at when reviewing conditional uses. This review was
formally requested by Commission during the December 11th, 2019 Planning Commission
meeting. Under the statute, as a land use authority, the Commission cannot use general or broad
conditions and must use specific standards as set forth by the ordinance. Under the statute it was
further explained that conditional uses are presumed to be allowed but the Commission may
place conditions to lessen the detrimental impacts of the proposed use. The conditions must be
reasonable, must address the identified impacts, and cannot be more restrictive than the specific
standards in the existing ordinance. However, if it does not meet the standards of the ordinance,
then the permit can be denied.

DISCUSSION

The Commission felt that not removing snow around occupied parking will create a shortage of
guest parking spaces because snowplows will not be able to remove snow from them. The
developers plan includes manual removal of snow and pushing snow into the fence. The
Commission cited skepticism that the manual removal will occur. The Commission further noted
that the force and pressure from the snow could be damaging to the fence.

Chair Judkins stated that because the plans do not meet current landscaping requirements it
should not be approved.

Commissioner Wallin stated that the site plan should show enough space for landscaping and
snow removal as to not interfere with parking. He recommended tabling the request providing
the applicant an opportunity to make changes to the site plan. The Commission agreed that the
current site plan was insufficient to meet the landscaping and parking requirements.
