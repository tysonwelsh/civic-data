Jeff Seaman, representing Peterson Development, said the Copper Meadows subdivision is on a
difficult property to develop since the south half is a former gravel pit. This project came to the
Commission and City Council last year and was postponed. He thanked the staff who helped to
address the issues that were presented last year. He showed the current and former plans for
comparison and noted the changes. A proposed trailhead at the north was removed after receiving
opposition from the neighbors. Another concern dealt with the proposal to cut into the hillside for
homes in the northwest, but now they are working with staff to keep it open space for trails or
hiking, and it creates a buffer from the existing homes. He pointed out that nowhere in the
development will a backyard fence touch an existing neighbor’s fence. There will be at least 30 feet
of buffer from fence to fence that will be maintained by the HOA. On the east side there is a park
and detention pond that will buffer the neighbors. Another concern was the overall density, which
was 5 units per acre on the first version with the density in the middle of the project. The neighbors
who talked to them asked them to move the denser product to the south, so that is where the new
plan shows townhomes and carriage homes. The overall density is now proposed at 4.2 dwelling
units per acre with a reduction of 58 units that puts it in the low-density category. By working with
staff, the proposal now has minimal impact on the neighbors. Good urban design is being provided
with a variety of housing types, including the ‘missing middle’ housing type. An HOA will be
formed and will maintain the open space. Residents of the new subdivision will not need to go
through the adjacent subdivisions. 8200 South is a collector road. The traffic study showed that
including this community plus the expected growth over the next 20 years, the study area
intersections and project accesses will continue to function with acceptable levels of service with no
additional improvements needed. The project is comparable to the Oneleven project approved this
year that has a variety of housing types, but it is in the Integrated Housing Development zone that
requires a commercial aspect. Commercial uses in the Planned Community zone are at the discretion
of the Council, but this is not a commercial area. He felt that they addressed every concern that was
presented at the previous hearing and asked that the Commission forward a positive
recommendation.

Tayler Jensen stated that the property has gentle slopes on the north moving south to the existing
gravel quarry. He reviewed the meeting history for the project that City Council directed last
December to be redesigned. The applicant has worked with staff since then to address the 17 deal
points identified in the previous submittal. The new lot layout no longer is ‘conceptual’, so the
project will be built as it is shown unless they go through an amendment process. The bulk standards
in the P-C zone allow the applicant to propose variations, which the City Council reviews and
approves, denies, or amends. The applicant has proposed full length driveways, side setbacks were
changed to 5 and 8 feet with a minimum of 13 feet between buildings. He reviewed the built
separations on the wall and fencing plan. Twenty percent of the project must be in open space and
8% of that has to be irrigated and programmed. Slopes greater than 30% are not counted toward the
open space requirement. An 8-foot-wide trail will continue along 8200 South and Stokesly Lane,
which will connect into existing city trails that will continue into Jones Ranch. Internal trails are also
provided. Some building elevation examples were shown, and the standard in the master
development plan states that the same plan or colors cannot be next to or across the street from each
other, which will help to create a sense of place. He explained the proposed changes to the future
land use map and zoning map. The project asks for 318 units at 4.2 dwelling units per acre. There is
an annexation request in process. If the project is approved, the major subdivision and site plan
