DISCUSSION ITEMS


1.     Report and Discussion on the Fort Union Corridor Study.


                                              ***

CONSULTANT:

Dan Smith, representing MHTN, will be presenting the final findings from the Fort Union
Corridor Study. Project initiated in January and final report is anticipated to be delivered in June
2024.

There have been many Public Engagement Opportunities to interact with community members
and get feedback. Opportunities such as Public Meetings, Open Houses, and surveys. There is a
current survey that is ongoing and a Public Meeting tomorrow, May 23, 2024, beginning at 6:00
PM.

Most of the feedback from the community was a desire for more green space and more public
parking. Making the corridor a cohesive area and maintaining the neighborhood quality is a
focus of this project.



PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION MAY 22, 2024.
The corridor has been broken down into three segments to breakdown and take a closer look into
the functionality of each piece. This was vital for studying the different benefits that come from
each area. Each segment’s strengths will be highlighted and combined into one fluid corridor.

Segment 1 (I-15 to State Street) - “Community Gateway”

Concerns: Speeding, pedestrian crossing, congestion, image of entrance to city, crime rates.

104-foot Right-of-way, needing pedestrian refuge. The proposed new layout would have a
pedestrian refuge in the center, three lanes of traffic on each side, a park strip, and amenity zone
on both sides. On one side a side path for a bike lane for example could be added.
Ground uses would be commercial and or residential. Outdoor dining and other amenities to
attract the community. Concentrating the higher residential area on the Northern side, especially
focusing on an HTRZ area.

The TRAX line and surrounding area make up about 4 acres. The current uses are 53% Mixed-
Use, then 36% Residential, and finally 11% Retail. The percentages for Green Space and Parking
will be determined after the study is complete and plans are initiated.

Segment 2 (State Street to 700 E) – “Residential Thoroughfare”

Concerns: Speeding, lack of green space, and pedestrian crossing.

Throughout the area there is not enough traffic to justify traffic lights. The studies show that
Toucan Crossings would be beneficial to the area. This type of crossing is an improvement for
bicyclists and pedestrians that allows for more frequent, safer crossings while sharing the road
with motorized vehicles. This would provide protected waiting areas, bike signals, and motor
vehicle restrictions to reduce accidents.

The area makes up about 1 acre. The current uses are then 16% Residential, and finally 22%
Retail, 30% Green Space and 32% Parking.


Segment 3 (700 E to Union Park Ave)- “Regional Commercial Crossroads”

Concerns: Intersections, ease of travel for pedestrians, and pedestrian safety.

The proposed design would adjust the width of the public right of way area to allow for more
green space, activity areas for commercial businesses, and a designated bike lane. An internal
road system needs to be created to unify all the types of transportation along the corridor. This
highly trafficked area is one of the most discussed segments of the project and most likely to see
the most immediate change.

Development steps:

Phase 1) Establish a grid or connectivity to allow vehicles to move through this area easier.
Internal road network with a unifying element, such as a canal, a bike connection trail, and more
to open the area to traffic.

DISCUSSION:

Chair Erickson asks for clarification on the building of the pedestrian refuge, whether it would be
raised or not. She also wants to know if the park strip would be greenery or other materials.

Mr. Smith clarifies that the results of the study can still have adjustments made depending on the
goals of the community, City Staff, and City Council.

Commissioner Liedtke voices that he favors this layout to provide a much safer pedestrian
pathway for crossing.

Wendelin Knobloch, Planning Director, discusses the outreach that staff has been conducting to
attract a developer to aid in the start of an HTRZ area.

Adam Olsen, Community Development Director, shares a few details of the gateway monuments
that are in the process of design and implementation.

Vice Chair Anderson suggests that the ski bus routes be heavily considered when designing the
Fort Union Blvd area. This is an exceptionally large population that visits Midvale City, and it
would benefit the city to enhance the experiences of the skier/snowboarder travelers.

Commissioner Liedtke mentions the possibility of a designated bus lane.

Mr. Knobloch agrees that the idea of a bus lane would be ideal, however it is not in UTA’s long-
term plan at this moment. The city would need to incentivize and gain more traction with this
idea.

Chair Erickson and Vice Chair Anderson mention the concern that the owners of the private
property around the Canal Trail are not currently incentivized to develop their land. For concern
of crime and heavy traffic, this is going to be a challenge in opening the Canal area.

TOUCAN CROSSING
SEGMENT 02

   TOUCAN CROSSINGS provide crossing
   improvements for bicyclists and pedestrians
   so that “two can” cross without a full signal
   that could lead to more vehicular Byway
   traffic.

   On a Neighborhood Byway, Toucan
   crossings are usually placed where side-
   street volumes do not justify a full
   signal, and are accompanied by turn
   restrictions to reduce vehicular conflicts
   and prioritize pedestrian and bicycle
   crossings.

TOUCAN CROSSING
SEGMENT 02

 DESIGN GUIDELINES
 TYPICAL USES
 • As modification to an existing signal or as a
 new build signal
 • At intersections where traffic diversion off of
 the Byway is a desirable outcome to avoid
 attracting additional motor vehicle traffic
 • To improve intersection safety by prohibiting
 left and through movements which contribute
 to more severe right angle collisions
 • At an intersection within a school zone on a
 walking route

 DESIGN FEATURES
 • Toucan crossings most often feature a single
 direction protected area; however, if space
 allows, a bi-directional protected area can be
 provided
 • Pedestrian crossings can be on the corners or
 may also utilize the center area if space allows
