D. COMMISSIONER COMMENTS:
   1. Commissioner Harvey: I want to publicly thank the team at the Ice Sheet for turning it into a world class
      curling venue. There are probes in the ice measuring the temperature of the ice to keep the ice at even
      temperatures no matter what lane a team plays on. There are magnetic strips on each end of the ice and on
      each stone. The electromagnetic energy from a player’s hand touching the stone will be detected when the
      scratch, or foul line, is crossed, leading to disqualification. The stones come from the same quarry in
      Scotland. The World Curling Championships are taking place every day at 9am, 2pm, and 7pm starting this
      Friday. Tickets are available on visitOgden.com.

     2. Chair Froerer: I spent the better part of last week talking about water. We did not have a lot of snow this
        season. Snow Basin is already closed. We peaked water-wise on March 7th. We have good soil moisture at
        the moment. Weber Ogden drainage will not be at a high water level. 2023 was our highest snow pack in
        history and this year, 2026, will be our lowest snow pack in history. Weber Basin will meet on Thursday
        morning to determine conservation efforts needed for our area. Our reservoirs are at 60%; at this point in
        time in 2021 our reservoirs were 30-40%. If we do not have a 2023 water season next year, it will be even
        worse. I have had calls from some of our mayors saying they do not want to water restrictions that will
        burden our citizens. We are not making more water at this point in time. Now is the time to conserve.
        Because of the heat and lack of water and being a month ahead in our season, fires will become very critical.
        If we don’t look at reality right now and manage our outdoor recreation uses right now, we are going to have
        problems.

    2. Public hearing to exempt Bermuda grass (Cyndodon dactylon) in Weber County from the State of Utah
       Noxious Weed List, so that it is not subject to provisions of the Utah Noxious Weed Law within the
       boundaries of Weber County.

         Bren Edwards (Weber County Weed Control Board Chair): Recently, the state weed board that that
         controls the state noxious weed list had removed a portion of Bermuda. Weber Basin has a big push
         this year for this water-wise grass to be utilized in your grass in your lawns. Even a buyback
         program if you tear out Kentucky blue or other grasses, you can get so much money to put in this
         new hybrid version. We started looking at it from a broader perspective and started considering the

         benefits versus the negatives of Bermuda grass. We worked with those partnerships, Utah State
         Extension and UDAF, and tried to figure out why originally Bermuda was listed on the state
         noxious weed list. We live in a desert state prone to droughts; water is very limited. And Bermuda
         is an extreme drought tolerant grass. Other benefits are Bermuda also helps choke out other
         noxious weeds. As it grows and it builds that mat across the ground and stuff like puncture weeds
         and other weeds can't grow through it. Bermuda was brought to the US in 1750 from Australia and
         New Zealand where it originated. We felt from a producer standpoint, there's a lot of benefits as
         well. As mentioned, the way it grows, it builds that mat in waterways, areas that cattle run on, it's
         very strong and is not going to get walked out like a traditional pasture grass, from an erosion
         standpoint, and it's going to help prevent erosion in those areas. We felt as a weed board that the
         benefits outweigh the negatives. The first step of this process is a recommendation from the County
         Weed Board to the County's legislative body to hold a public hearing on removing this. The next
         step would be as you as the County Commission, as the legislative body, make a recommendation
         to the Commissioner of Agriculture over the state of Utah, who will then take it to the State Weed
         Board for consideration of removal. Then the State administrative code would be updated to
         exempt Weber County. Currently Washington County is the only county in the state that is exempt
         from Bermuda being a noxious weed and we would be the second county in the state. In some
         communications I've had with UDAF, there's quite a few other counties looking at this as well just
         because they're starting to see the benefits versus the negatives with that.

         Chair Froerer: The advantages of water tolerance, erosion control, and staying green in heat and low water
         outweigh the disadvantages.
