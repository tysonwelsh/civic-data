Public Comment

Chair MacKay read the public comment preamble. After which she opened public comment.

Lowell Anderson, of Provo, said the deer population around the Foothill Neighborhood has significantly
increased. With the increasing number of deer, there are now packs of coyotes and cougars, which have
become more prominent in the neighborhood. He shared his concern for the surrounding residents and
suggested the city hire expert hunters to thin the herd. He added that the meat could be donated to the
Food and Care Coalition.

Jeff Kimball, of Provo, stated the deer herd is larger than ever. He also shared he is in favor of any safe
and humane way to thin the herd.

Zach (unknown last name), of Provo Foothill Neighborhood, said he is in favor of thinning the herd.

Michael Coleman, of Provo, said he is supportive of thinning the herd.

Merrill Bateman, of Provo, stated something needs to be done to help the deer and help the families in
the area. He recommended we act quickly and not wait until there is a catastrophe.

Stan Jensen, of Provo, stated this problem exists in other areas of the city as well, not just the Foothill
Neighborhood.

Chair MacKay discussed the Urban Deer Program that was around a couple of years ago that is no longer
active. She shared her appreciation for those that provided comments and promised to take the issue to
leadership to find a solution.

Brian Jones, City Attorney, said there were several changes to the required permit from the Division of
Wildlife Resources that have made the program difficult to continue due to the restrictive regulations.

John Schmidt, of Provo, said he was surprised to hear that only one complaint had been made to the
council. He shared an experience from October that the Police were involved in.

Chair MacKay clarified that the single complaint she spoke of was directed to the council, but there may
be other complaints that have been received by other departments.

Bradley Day, of Provo, reminded the council that he brought a problem to their attention a few months
prior regarding a trash service during early morning hours. He shared his disappointment that no change
has taken place since then and requested some response and/or action from the city.

Jake Holdaway, of Vineyard, thanked councilmembers who have participated in the walk our way tour.
He shared his desire to end the 130-year-old lawsuit to allow a trail to connect from Provo to Saratoga
Springs. He said he is trying to understand the loan that the Lake Commission approved. He invited the
council to a Lake Authority meeting that would be on Friday.

Councilor Fillmore asked about the reason for removing the time restriction for on-site security.

Mr. Ardmore responded that staff felt like the hours of operation take care of the needed security
unless there is an event with alcohol being served or amplified music being played.

Chair MacKay opened the item for public comment.

Barry Finch, of Provo, expressed his gratitude for the progress that had already been made. He shared
his concern for the listed restrictions that are not necessary.

Parker Moses, of Provo, said he is the contractor for Mr. Finch. He mentioned that backyard weddings
are increasingly popular in our city, but they are causing parking issues for neighbors. He shared that
many event centers on farmland have cobblestone to eliminate the mud that gets in tires before leaving
the parking lot. He said there is concern for safety if the road is paved because it could be slick from
mud.

With no other comments, Chair MacKay invited council discussion.

Councilor Fillmore invited Mr. Ardmore to respond to the objections raised by Mr. Finch.

Mr. Ardmore discussed the reasons why staff felt most comfortable with keeping the requirements.

Councilor Shipley asked if the time restriction could be addressed.

Mr. Ardmore stated the hours of operation in the county regulation of their agritourism areas and said
the hours listed were typical for neighborhood noise. He said he does not see any reason to change
them. He added that people who live in the suburbs expect more quiet times than those that live
downtown and are aware of the noise possibilities.

Chair MacKay said living close to the Seven Peaks Waterpark was difficult when they had events that
would last until late hours of the night, especially for the young children who were not able to sleep with
the loud noise.

Councilor Whipple asked if surrounding cities were looked at such as Wadley Farms in Lindon.

Mr. Ardmore admitted that he looked at others but not related to parking or noise.

Councilor Handley said he would feel more confident if more examples were given, either pro or con.

Councilor Shipley agreed with Councilor Handley. He said he would be interested in learning more about
the requirements of other event centers in agritourism zones.

     Motion:      Councilor Handley made a motion to instruct staff to bring back examples of parking
                  requirements in surrounding cities and states that are comparable in size. Councilor
                  Whipple seconded.

Mr. Ardmore presented. He said before now, no one has taken initiative to subdivide these acres which
could be related to the large transmission lines that run through the corridor. He said the plan includes
two single family lots and the others would be twin homes. The Planning Commission concerns included
the variety of housing models and the maintenance of open space, which are both addressed in the
development agreement. He added that the development agreement also caps development at 58
units. He said the owner occupancy issue still needs to be addressed, but the staff and Planning
Commission are recommending approval.

Councilor Shipley asked what the Planning Commission and Development Services staff recommended
to support owner occupancy.

Mr. Ardmore said some examples include restrictions put forth through CCNR’s, additional provisions
added to the development agreement or notes recorded on the plat.

Councilor Hoban asked how the development agreement can be enforced after the development is
completed.

Mr. Jones said the notes on the plat or the recorded CCNR would be included in the development
agreement and would have to be executed prior to the certificate of occupancy being issued.

Councilor Whipple referenced the report of action from the Planning Commission that mentioned
forwarding two concerns to the council, owner occupancy and the natural spring. She wanted to make
sure the developer is aware and has a plan of how to address these concerns. She asked what
addressing the natural spring looks like.

Mr. Ardmore explained a necessary setback would be required to prevent flooding.

Councilor Whipple confirmed the spring is not an amenity, but for safety the setbacks needed to be in
place. She asked for an explanation of what the open space would be used for and what the plan is to
maintain it.

Garrett Seely, representing the Weights who own the property, said they planned around the spring and
would not build basements to prevent flooding. He discussed a manifold system that will collect water
and direct it to an appropriate place. He said the plan is to maintain open areas as they are for wildlife
and the weeds will be kept down around sidewalks and roads. He added that the surrounding residents
were happy to hear that space would be left as is.

Councilor Handley asked Mr. Seely for his opinion about owner occupancy.

Mr. Sealy explained that these will be sold individually.

Andy Flam, Mr. Seely’s partner and co-applicant, said they are selling to a builder who will sell all homes
individually. He added that there is no way for them to control what happens to the homes one or two
owners down the road.

Chair MacKay responded and said with a CCNR, it could be required that the home be owner occupied
for one year after sale.

Both Mr. Seely and Mr. Flam said they would be fine with that stipulation.

Councilor Handley confirmed that the requirement for owner occupancy will be required for one year.

Mr. Ardmore presented. He discussed the current zoning of the parcels and provided an overview of
what the developer had planned for the parcel. He said the development agreement addresses some of
the concerns brought up by staff and the Planning Commission including the use of open space and
future trail networks.

Councilor Shipley asked about the flight pattern over this area.

Mr. Ardmore said his understanding is this parcel is currently within the flight pattern. He added that he
did not feel comfortable addressing other questions.

Bill Peperone, Director of Development Services, explained the plans for the runways and mentioned
the timeframe of the changes are unknown. He added that right now, the parcel is under a flight path
but that could change as the airport expands and grows.

Chair MacKay opened the item for public comment. With none, she called for council discussion.

Councilor Shipley said he hoped for a different project for this area that included river amenities without
a warehouse. He mentioned that this area is ideal for housing options due to its scenic beauty.
Additionally, he expressed a preference for transforming it into a residential space.

Councilor Fillmore asked what amenities the developer had planned with respect to the river.

Norm Avery, developer, said they plan to include three outlook areas, one picnic area, and three
workout areas.

Chair MacKay sympathized with Councilor Shipley.

Councilor Ellsworth shared she wishes our code would implement orienting parking lots away from
natural spots along river.

Councilor Whipple asked if the parking lot would be available for use from the public. She shared her
liking of trails being connected to stores and public places for those that use it to commute.

Councilor Fillmore asked why this parcel was changed from residential to commercial.

Mr. Avery noted that the rise in airport traffic, changes in flight patterns, and increased noise levels
make it less suitable as a residential choice.

Mr. Ardmore added that this parcel is isolated, surrounded by the river and two arterial roads. He said
there is not a lot of connectivity to other neighborhoods.

Keith Morey, Economic Development Director, stated this area has clear boundaries with the river and
arterial roads. He emphasized the demand for commercial spaces and said there are not many options
on the west side. He also shared reasons why this would be a good development from a tax revenue
standpoint.

Councilor Fillmore thanked Mr. Morey for his input and shared his desire to support economic
development within the city.

Councilor Shipley mentioned that there are designated zones in the surrounding areas specifically set
aside for airport industrial purposes. He also explained why he would be voting against this item.

Councilor Handley shared his thoughts on the parcel’s ability to have a corridor large enough for the
public to enjoy if this was zoned as residential.

Councilor Whipple shared her recollection of the safety parameters that would be put in place if it were
residential. She said there are not as many safety concerns if it is an industrial building.

Councilor Shipley expressed a differing opinion, stating that he does not think industrial buildings offer a
higher level of safety compared to residential buildings. He pointed out that industrial buildings in other
cities may not be the most aesthetically pleasing or secure.

Councilor Hoban stated we need homes more than we need another warehouse and said he is more
supportive of residential based on the current housing need. He said revisiting the General Plan might
be a good idea.

Chair MacKay asked what the current zone is.

Mr. Ardmore said the current zone is agricultural, but the General Plan calls for it to be airport related.

Councilor Fillmore suggested continuing to gather more information or provide more certainty.

Councilor Handley indicated that he believed the conversation regarding this matter had already
occurred, and the only obstacle remaining was the development agreement.

Mr. Ardmore verified that in 2022, a decision was made to modify the General Plan, shifting from
residential to airport industrial in anticipation of a project like the current one. He also mentioned that
this is the initial application for the rezoning since that decision.

Mr. Avery said that they are working with the adjacent property to provide connections in a few areas.

Heath Johnston, developer, confirmed that there is a new project moving forward on the property to
the west after the previous one fell through. He said that while they are hoping the council will pass the
item to align with the General Plan change, the developers have strived to keep as much open space for
the public to enjoy.

Chair MacKay recalled that the neighboring property was considering mixed-use development, including
the addition of food establishments. She inquired whether the consideration of mixed use was explored
for their property.

Mr. Johnston explained that their space would be utilized for the transportation of goods, benefiting
from the convenient proximity to the airport and freeway.

Chair MacKay asked if there is anything dictating the distance for public use along the river.
