Electronic Board Meeting Determination

                 Greg Schulz read the following statement regarding the electronic meeting
determination:

               Consistent with provisions of the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act, specifically
Utah Code Ann. § 52-4-207(4), and acting in my capacity as the Mayor of the Magna Metro
Township (“Magna”), I Dan Peay hereby make the following written determinations in support of
my decision to hold and convene electronic meetings of the Magna Metro Township Council
without a physical anchor location:

1.     Conducting Council Meetings with an anchor location that is physically accessible for
members of the public to attend in person presents a substantial risk to the health and safety of
those who may be present at the anchor location.

2.     This determination is based upon the following:

       a.      Pursuant to the Public Health Orders issued by the State of Utah and Salt Lake
       County, the Magna Metro Township has been determined to be classified as being in the
       “Moderate” transmission category meaning significant person-to-person transmission of
       the virus continues to occur in Magna; and

       b.     As low vaccination rates continue to exist in Magna, public attendance at a meeting
       with an anchor location poses high risks of person to person transmission of the virus.

Council meeting held in Magna on April 13, 2021, and future Council meetings as appropriate.
Council staff are instructed to include in the public notice of each Council meeting the information
on how a member of the public may view and make comments during the Council’s Electronic
meetings.

               This written determination is effective until midnight on May 15, 2021, and may be
re-issued by future written determinations of the Mayor at that or any other appropriate time.

RESOLUTION NO. 21-04-03                                                     Date: APRIL 27, 2021

    A RESOLUTION OF THE MAGNA METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL APPROVING AND
    AUTHORIZING MAGNA’S INCLUSION IN THE MUNICIPAL ALTERNATE VOTING
    METHODS PILOT PROJET FOR 2021

                                           RECITALS

         WHEREAS, the State of Utah by its State Legislature has made available a pilot project
for participation in the alternate voting methods for municipal elections beginning January 1, 2019
and ending on January 1, 2026; and

        WHEREAS, House Bills 35 and 75 as passed during the 2018 and 2021 General Sessions
respectively, allow municipalities to conduct nonpartisan races using ranked choice voting in
accordance with the requirements of 20A-4 of the Utah Code and all other applicable provisions
of law, during any odd-numbered year that the pilot project is in effect; and

        WHEREAS, the Magna Metro Township Council, on or before May 10, 2021, must provide
written notice to the lieutenant governor and county clerk stating that the municipality intends to
participate in the pilot project for the year specified in the notice; and

        WHEREAS, Magna desires to contract with Salt Lake County to conduct municipal
elections and does have the resources and capability necessary to participate in the pilot project;
and

       WHEREAS, the Magna Metro Township Council finds that it would be in the best interest
of Magna and of its citizens to hold its 2021 municipal elections by ranked choice voting; now
therefore be it

                                          RESOLUTION

       RESOLVED, the Magna Metro Township Council adopts ranked choice voting for the 2021
municipal elections, and that by this resolution, gives notice to the Lieutenant Governor and to
the Salt Lake County Clerk to enter the pilot project by use of ranked choice voting for its 201
municipal elections; and be it further

        RESOLVED, the Mayor and Municipal Administrator are hereby authorized and directed
to do all things necessary to cause the 2021 Magna municipal elections to be held in accordance
with the ranked choice voting process.

      PASSED AND APPROVED by the Magna Metro Township Council in Magna, Salt Lake
County, Utah this 27th day of April 2021.

comment regarding the following requests:

Application REZ2021-000256 – Ivory Homes is requesting approval of a zone change from A-20
(Agricultural) to P-C (Planned Community) on 78.46 acres of property located on the north side
of 4100 South between 8000 West and 8400 West.

                 Mr. Travis Hair, Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, reviewed
the request stating Ivory Homes is proposing a mixed-use development, with some commercial
industrial and some residential areas. The total density for residential varies from 408 to 816
units, and the non-residential density varies from 217,000 square feet to 1,350,500 square feet.
The density is dependent on whether developers or buyers want to use that industrial space for
industrial or commercial. As that space gets developed into industrial or commercial, the
residential number will decrease. Also, some land is in the over-pressure area, so Ivory Homes
will be restricted by that in terms of the way the subdivisions are laid out.

The P-C zone requires the applicants to list conditional uses as well as permitted uses. The
permitted uses are residential, light commercial, and some industrial, which would be located to
the south close to the manufacturing area near the Northrop Grumman site. The permitted uses
include restaurants, but not drive-throughs. That has been added as a conditional use so the
Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District can look at some of the traffic impacts those would
have.

Then, the development agreement is nearly complete and has been sent to Council Members.
Some things were added to it to clarify the setbacks for both commercial and industrial buildings.

Application #REZ2021-000263 – Gough Homes is requesting a rezone from an A-1/zc
(Agricultural w/zoning conditions) to an R-1-6 (Single-Family Residential) zone on approximately
2.74 acres of property located at 7630 West 2820 South.

                Diana Martinez reviewed the request stating the smallest existing lots in the
proximity of the subject property are 6,000 square feet. Property to the north is zoned R-1-6, with
a zoning condition, and to the east, it is zoned R-1-10. Most of the property surrounding the
subject property is still A-1/zc.

There were a few calls in opposition to this prior to the Planning Commission meeting, and a
person spoke in opposition to it at the Planning Commission meeting. The Planning staff
recommended approval with two conditions: 1) the use be limited to residential single-family
detached dwellings; and 2) the density be limited to six units per acre, or twelve single-family
residential lots.  The Planning Commission also recommended approval with those two
conditions.

               Council Member Pierce asked what the concerns were about this.


                                                13
DATE           TUESDAY                                 APRIL                             27, 2021

               Ms. Martinez stated most of the complaints were from close property owners who
did not want to see more of the small lots that are in the R-1-8 and R-1-6 zones.

               Council Member Prokopis, seconded by Council Member Peel, moved to open the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

               No one spoke in favor of or in opposition to the rezone request.

               Council Member Prokopis, seconded by Council Member Peel, moved to close the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

               Council Member Prokopis stated a lot of people are concerned with the 6,000
square foot lot size, which is what a lot of developers are doing lately. It made more sense to
build mixed use where there were some larger lot sizes too. He asked if the applicant would be
open to developing 8,000 square foot lots.

               Ms. Martinez stated the proposal the applicant brought to the Planning Division
had 6,000, 7,000, and 8,000 square foot units within the 12 lots. The applicant cannot do a
Planned Unit Development (PUD) because the acreage is less than three acres, so he will have
to do a subdivision. That will require certain standards being met and a private road going into
the property.

Public Hearing

            THIS BEING THE TIME heretofore set for a public hearing to receive public
comment regarding the following request:

Application #OAM2021 – Ordinance #21-04-01 to consider amendments to Chapter 15.14 of the
Magna Municipal Code governing special development standards in the Magna Overpressure
Zone. The amendments intend to clarify the boundaries of the area, outline conditions required
for building and developing within the Overpressure Zone and moves this section of code to
Chapter 16.02.

                Travis Hair, Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, reviewed the
ordinance stating a memorandum was sent to the Council last week updating it on concerns that
came up after the Planning Commission meeting, which revolved primarily around the height
restrictions, especially in the industrial areas that are limited to stories. The height restrictions in
place do not work for some of the industrial users interested in coming into the area. Since, the
Magna Metro Township wants industrial uses because they are compatible with the area and
acceptable in terms of the overpressure zone areas, especially in the 0.5 overpressure areas, the
Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (GSLMSD) staff proposed leaving the story
requirements, but taking out the height restrictions. That way, if a company came in that needed
larger machinery or more storage for a warehouse, there would be some allowance for that. The
GSLMSD staff and its legal counsel has been having some discussions with Northrop Grumman
to try to work that out.

           Jay Springer, Legal Counsel, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, stated
the GSLMSD started this process to correct the map because some of Northrop Grumman’s
                                                  15
DATE            TUESDAY                                   APRIL                               27, 2021

facility operations have moved over time. The GSLMSD also wanted to get rid of a parcel-by-
parcel approach. The GSLMSD worked with Northrop Grumman and the Geographic Information
System to get an accurate map that could be carried over into planning. The map is a radius map
from the epicenters. It is generally an improvement, but it will create some parcels that are partly
in a 0.2, 0.3, or 0.5 overpressure areas and partly in a different 0.2, 0.3, or 0.5 areas or even
outside of the overpressure zone, and it is structured so that every part of a parcel that is within
that radius is affected by the regulations of the relevant area. It will also create parcels that have
not previously been designated as being in an overpressure zone, and more properties are
affected by this than there were in the earlier ordinance. Then, some areas that were in one
designated area, may now be in a different designation. The GSLMSD also addressed uses in
the overpressure zone, i.e. subdivision standards, building standards, and increased the
requirements and protections, specifically dealing with the sizes and types of windows that would
need to go in.

Another concern was the density in the 0.3 overpressure areas, which is supposed to be 6 units
per acre, but the ordinance that was drafted had 7,000 square feet per lot with that 6 units per
acre restriction. That restriction will not work once public improvements are taken into account,
so the GSLMSD staff recommended that be reduced to 5,000 square feet. That allows some
flexibility for developers to able to get the public improvements in without having to require really
small lots. That recommendation was not in the Council’s packet.

               Mayor Peay stated it sounded like the Magna Metro Township was getting back
to small cookie cutter houses with the 5,000 square foot lot requirement.

                  Mr. Springer stated there is still the density restriction of 6 units per acre. The
biggest concern was the height restrictions. The initial language in the draft ordinance done in
June 2019, was proposed by Northrop Grumman. That restriction was two stories for the 0.5
overpressure areas and three stories for the 0.3 areas. After working with Northrop Grumman
and the Council, the GSLMSD refined the ordinance by converting floors into height. The
proposed restriction for the 0.5 areas is 30 feet, and it is 40 feet for the 0.3 areas. Northrop
Grumman felt those height restrictions were appropriate. The primary goal is to limit the number
of people being placed in harm’s way during an energetic overpressure incident, primarily from
flying debris, like glass breaking and being thrown, and also damage to properties. Reducing the
surface area that is subject to strong over-force pressure reduces the stress on the structure.
Increased height means increased surface area; and therefore, increased stress loads. Height
restrictions together with story restrictions provide for greater safety of the people that inhabit
such structures.

Under the proposed ordinance, residential structures are not allowed in the 0.5 overpressure
areas, and the 30-foot limitation is only slightly lower than the existing 35-foot building height limit
applicable to the C-1 zone. It should be applied to commercial uses and portions of industrial
structures that are primarily occupied by people, such as office spaces. Northrop Grumman did
not object to increased heights for manufacturing and industrial uses in either the 0.5 or 0.3
overpressure areas if buildings were limited to single-story structures or if portions of structures
were up to 60 feet high. Northrop Grumman did say that in the 0.3 overpressure areas, the
proposed 40-foot height limit should apply to both commercial and residential uses and portions
of industrial structures that house people.
