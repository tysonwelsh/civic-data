John Geilmann said the Council approved 2 resolutions; the City opted out of the social
security system and directed that the money be put in a 401k that is administered by the City,

John Miller said engineering is pursuing contracts for consultant work to get projects out,
specifically the Upland Dr. project. He doesn’t want to send out a Request for Proposal
(RFP) because of the timeline of the project, so he is getting help from consultants from the
UDOT pool and the County’s Right of Way Acquisition pool. John Brems said doing so
would be legal because the consultants were chosen on a qualifications basis and they are in
the pool to use instead of doing a RFP. Council Member Uipi asked if there is a procurement
policy where the City can go back and look at and select consultants or if they were selected
because staff is familiar with their work. Miller said the consultants in the pools meet the
qualifications and he has selected two that have historically been less expensive to work
with. Brems said professional services do not need to go to a RFP process. The Council
consented to Miller’s request for using the consultants.

Mayor Silvestrini said that since John Miller and Frederick Lutze have been hired, the City
should be saving money on contracting engineering needs from the County. The City still
needs to have a contract with the County to cover some other engineering services, so a

consulting engineers to get a competitive price.

5.1 Discussion and Consideration Ordinance 18-09, Amendments to Code
Chapter 19.66 Light Manufacturing M Zone

Francis Lilly went over some recent changes to the ordinance. He said staff did
change the existing structure height from 80 ft. to 100 ft. to accommodate current
structures in the zone. Staff considered the allowable maximum outdoor storage area
and agreed to increase it from 50% to 60% for new manufacturing uses. He
recommends adoption of the draft ordinance, however he brought up a few changes
that were discussed today with the City Attorney. They define the purpose of the light
manufacturing zone referring to C3 zone; C3 zone encompasses any commercial use
in the commercial zones. The outdoor storage of trucks and trailers allowed as
defined by 19.66.050 A (4) was added into the table. A provision that if there are
standard conflicts in the ordinance, that the more restrictive standards apply except as
set forth in 19.66.060. He said that there is a custom, liberal nonconforming use and
noncomplying structure policy that applies to the new M Zone, and another ordinance
change is the extent of nonconforming uses and how they can grow, and a clarifying
statement on any expansion to a nonconforming use.

Mayor Silvestrini said he received a letter from Vincent Rampton on the ordinance.
He then asked if there was any public comment on the ordinance.

Vincent Rampton, 170 S. Main St., expressed concern about the 60% outdoor storage
recommendation. He said that when the amendment was discussed at the last meeting,
the public had requested it to be 80%.

Council Member Uipi moved to close the public comment. Council Member Jackson
seconded. Mayor Silvestrini called for the vote. The motion passed unanimously.

5.2 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 18-11, A Pending Ordinance to
Establish a Town Center Overlay Zone for Properties Located Approx. Between
Highland Dr. & 1300 E. and 3000 S. & 3400 S.

Mayor Silvestrini said he has received feedback from residents to have a Millcreek
town center/s. This particular area is largely commercial, and it would be good area
for the town center. The City has seen potential development inquiries in this area,
and he would like to protect the area to attract the right businesses. Mayor Silvestrini
reached out to Mayor Biskupski to jointly plan this area as some of it is in Salt Lake
City.

Lilly said staff has been working with interested land owners on developing new
mixed use projects. He said there are issues with existing height limits and uses that
may or may not be compatible for a town center and redeveloping that generates a
sufficient tax base to support infrastructure. He said the solution is to come up with a
town center master plan and zone. He said it is a long process to create and has
applied for a transportation land use grant with the Wasatch Front Regional Council
to help develop a code for the proposed town center. He is also working on the
process through the general plan development. Staff sees a town center overlay zone
as a good interim step to set a baseline for guiding what uses, materials, and building
heights Millcreek wants in the area. He said the pending ordinance would impose a
limit on land use approvals to give staff time to work out a proposal. Staff has put
together a rough draft for what the ordinance might look like. The draft he provided
to the Council accomplishes the following: sets height limits; brick and stone be 30
percent of street facing facades; add residential convertible commercial space; similar
standards found in the MD Zone; and prohibiting certain uses, automobile services
stations, sales and rental of vehicles and equipment, telecom facilities such as cell
towers that aren’t stealth, sexually oriented businesses, second-hand dealers including
precious metal recycling, self-storage facilities, tattoo, reiki and massage
establishments, short-term lenders, and drive-thrus as accessory uses to restaurants.

Lilly went over the boundary map on the ordinance. Mayor Silvestrini asked about
the residential property in the boundaries, he doesn’t want it to encroach on single-
family dwellings. Lilly can reconfigure the boundaries.

6.2 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 18-04, the 5" Amendment to the
Unified Police Department Interlocal Agreement

Mayor Silvestrini said the City is a party to an interlocal agreement which gives them
representation on the Unified Police Department Board. The agreement governs the
division of fees for police services and governance of the police department through
the UPD board. The Salt Lake County Sheriff is the head of the Unified Police
Department, but the board wants command staff responsible to the board. He said
there are questions about the impact if the UPD and County Sheriff's Office were to
separate. The Sheriff suggested the concession that she will appoint the chief with the
advice and consent of the board, this gives the board more control over the hiring of
the chief. The UPD Board recommended approval of this amendment and passed it to
the jurisdictions of the UPD for approval.

Council Member Uipi moved to approve item 6.2. Council Member Catten seconded.
Mayor Silvestrini called for the vote. The motion passed unanimously.
