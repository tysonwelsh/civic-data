Proposal 
The applicant, John Sale, representing  Vail Resorts, operator of Park City Mountain, in 
conjunction with the Canyons CVMA are requesting Low Impact Permit  approval for 
r
eplacement of the Sunrise Lift and realignment for the new 10 -passenger Sunrise Gondola at 
Canyons Village. T he new gondola base will be located on parcel RCDA -RC -17-C near the Pendry 
Plaza, between the Lift Hotel and Hyatt Centric and the gondola will terminate near the Red 
Pine Lodge.  The gondola is proposed to be 6,233 feet in length with a vertical rise of 1,104 feet, 
is conceptually designed with eighteen lift towers, and allows for 100% download capability.  
EXHIBIT A
This project will involve multiple parcels and stakeholders EXHIBIT B. The scope of the 
application will include installation of a new base terminal, re -grading near the base 
and surrounding properties, slight realignment of North Escala Court, gondola plaza 
improvements
 and supporting infrastructure,  installation of lift towers, new upper terminal 
and a gondola storage building near Red Pine Lodge  EXHIBIT C
The Sunrise Gondola realignment is planned to be a multi-year construction project. 
• Pha
se 1 will include permitting, planning, and approvals in close collaboration with the
CVMA and Summit County, and other stakeholders.
• Phase 2 will include relocating existing utilities near the proposed base terminal, a slight
realignment of the North Escala Court Road, adding a Canyons Village Shuttle drop -off
zone and clearing the gondola line corridor.
• Phase 3 includes removing the existing Sunrise lift, installation of the gondola, site
grading at the base and top terminals, and construction of the  gondola plaza and
supporting infrastructure.

Background 
The approval of the Canyons SPA plan and exhibits in November of 1999 included provision for 
expansion of the mountain master plan including additional ski lifts and ski terrain.  These 
improvements were originally laid out in a document titled the Ski ’98 plan and then 
incorporated into the Canyons final SPA approval.  An important element of the planning for 
the Canyons SPA approval was an analysis  of the amount of ski infrastructure  necessary to 
match the bed base that was part of the entitlement.  This is referred to in the Global Policies 
and elsewhere in the SPA document as the Comfortable Carrying Capacity or (CCC) for the 
mountain.  Section 2.3.3 of the Canyons SPA discusses the entitlement of this infrastructure and 
specifies the Low Impact Permit (LIP) approval process as the required process for review and 
approval prior to construction.   
Sunrise Gondola is classified as a transportation lift, meaning it cannot be skied back to the base 
terminal directly. Mountain guests would have to utilize other lifts or ski terrain to get back to 
the base area.  Additionally, the  proposed re-alignment of the Sunrise Gondola will be less 
susceptible to wind, versus the existing Red Pine Gondola and Orange Bubble express , requiring 
fewer wind holds and keeping the lines moving and getting guest on the mountain faster. The 
proposed Sunrise Gondola will improve both the current and future demand to access the 
mountain terrain from the Canyons Village core.

Analysis and Findings 
Section 10-3-4(E) of the Snyderville Basin Development Code requires that any use qualifying 
for a Low Impact Permit must meet the following criteria: 
Cri
teria 1 : The use does not significantly increase vehicular traffic, unless the increases are 
consistent with previously approved plans for which appropriate mitigation has been 
contemplated and which has been implemented in an appropriate manner to accommodate 
the proposed amendment.  
Analysis: The proposed Sunrise Gondola will not significantly increase vehicular traffic at 
Ca
nyons Village. Guests will now have an upgraded third option to directly access the 
mountain to the Red Pine area that is near the Forum area and conveniently accessed 
from the surrounding lodging options.  Guests will also be able to arrive via the existing 
shuttle and transit service and the current traffic management plan in the upper village 
will remain in place. COMPLIES
Criteria 2: The use does not significantly increase the demand for parking; unless the increases 
are consistent with previously approved plans for which appropriate mitigation has been 
contemplated and which has been implemented in an appropriate manner to accommodate 
the proposed amendment.
Analysis: The proposed Sunrise Gondola will not significantly increase the demand for 
parking in Canyons Village. This project has two main goals: efficient guest distribution 
across the mountain and improved uphill capacity during peak morning hours. The 
bottom terminal's placement will effectively  mitigate any parking concerns as this 
location will  mainly serve lodging and residential guests  and day  skiers and 
snowboarders who often reach the resort via the Cabriolet lift, consequently minimizing 
potential increases to parking demand escalation. COMPLIES
Criteri
a 3: The use does not intensify the likelihood of pedestrian and vehicular conflicts. 
Analysis: The project effectively addresses pedestrian and vehicular conflicts by 
implementing measures that reduce the likelihood of such conflicts. By incorporating 
prominent wayfinding, designated and accessible crossing points, and dedicated shuttle 
drop- off, the project creates a safe environment for both pedestrians and vehicles. The 
proposed plaza is designed to be fully ADA accessible, ensuring guests of all abilities are 
able to safely and easily access the lift. Overall, the project's thoughtful lower terminal 
design will improve the area’s existing conditions by prioritizing safety and 
minimizing the potential for conflicts between pedestrians and vehicles. COMPLIES

Proposal 
 
The applicant is requesting a Conditional Use Permit for a “Vehicle control gate” use for the 
benefit of the White Pine Ranches Subdivision, located at the White Pine Canyon Road and 
White Pine Lane intersection. The applicant has stated that; “During recent years and most 
notably in the winter of 2022‐3, some patrons of the Canyons ski resort used this private cul de 
sac to gain trespass access to the Tombstone chair lift through the private cul de sac and hiking 
across private lots to gain access to the Canyons. During that time the HOA of White Pine 
Ranches hired a guard to attempt to prevent illegal and potentially dangerous use with limited 
success as no gate or guard house is present. Even with a guard, the trespass use happened in 
complete defiance of the HOAs personnel. Given the nature of the Canyons patrons and the fact 
that all the resorts are struggling to keep crowds down which is at the root of this trespass use.”

Background 
White Pine Ranches Subdivision consists of 12 lots and is located at the White Pine Canyon 
Road and White Pine Lane intersection in the Canyons neighborhood. The subdivision was 
approved as part of two phases. Phase 1 was recorded in 1983 and Phase 2 was recorded in 
1992. All 12 lots are accessible via White Pine Lane, a private cul-de-sac owned by the White 
Pine Ranches HOA. The subdivision is primarily bordered by single-family residential uses and is 
also bordered by the Park City Ski Resort’s Tombstone Lift.  
Analysis and Findings 
 
Per the Snyderville Basin Development Code, “Vehicle control gate” uses are defined as “Any 
gate, barrier, or other mechanism to limit vehicular access on or across the street.” and are 
reviewed under a Conditional Use Permit in the Mountain Remote (MR) Zone.  
 
Section 10-8-12(A) of the Snyderville Basin Development Code states; Purpose: Vehicle control 
gates are generally not appropriate in any zone. In the event that a vehicle control gate is 
necessary to protect the public's health, safety, and welfare, a vehicle control gate may be 
approved in residential zones on private streets as a conditional use. In order to approve a 
conditional use for a vehicle control gate, all applicable findings and review standards as 
required for a conditional use permit in section 10‐3‐5 of this title shall be met. In addition, all of 
the following review criteria shall be met:

Background 
The Elk Springs subdivision is located within the Silver Creek Village Center and was recorded on 
September 7, 2021. It contains 125 lots, intended for detached single family dwellings (EXHIBIT 
A). The development of the subdivision was subject to the requirements contained within the 
Silver Creek Village Center Development Agreement (DA). 
 
The DA contains specific setbacks for each lot. There is a minimum front setback and a 
maximum front setback. The area located between the two setbacks is called the “front setback 
zone”, which is where a certain percentage of the structure must be placed. The setbacks for 
each lot are noted on subdivision plats and are required to match the setbacks noted in the DA.

The SBPC may approve, approve with conditions, or deny a plat amendment based upon 
written findings of fact according to each of the following standards. It is the responsibility of 
the applicant to provide written and graphic evidence demonstrating compliance:  
  
Standard 1: Neither the public nor any person will be materially injured by the proposed 
 
amendment and there is good cause for the amendment. COMPLIES.    
 
Analysis 
The setbacks that are identified in the DA are the setbacks that are required to be noted 
on all subdivision plats within the Silver Creek Village Center. For the majority of the 
parcels, the setbacks in the DA consist of minimum and maximum front setbacks that 
are different to ensure there is a “front setback area”. Staff and the applicant are of the 
opinion that an error may have occurred when the DA was written specific to the lots in 
question. Changing the setbacks will provide consistency with the DA. 
 
Standard 2: No plat amendment shall be approved which results in an increase in density. 
COMPLIES. 
  
Analysis: Density is defined as, “The number of dwelling units per acre with respect to 
residential uses.” This plat amendment would not result in an increase in density.
