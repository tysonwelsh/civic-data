              An additional memo from the Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider
    amendments to the Fiscal Year (FY) 2016-2020 Consolidated Plan (ConPlan). The memo stated as a requirement to receive
    federal funding from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), each year the City's Community and
    Economic Development Department prepares and submits to the Council what is known as an Annual Action Plan. The Annual
    Action Plan (AAP) is used to outline how federal Community Development Block Grant funds, HOME funds, and other federal
    and non-federal program funds will be used by the City in the upcoming fiscal year. When the plan is submitted to HUD in May
    of each year, specific dollar amounts for entitlement grant funds, program income funds, and carry-over funds are estimated for
    the next fiscal year. Part way through the year, after the specific dollar amounts are known or when program income from loans
)   and other programs are received, an amendment must be made to the plan to update the Annual Action Plan budget. Often when
    the mid-year amendment is proposed, changes in funding levels within certain programs and line items are proposed as well. The
    amendments proposed with this item are related to the increased funding to help with the COVID-19 related economic conditions.
    The proposed additional funding for this and other related programs is a combination of shifting funds between programs and

storm drain revenue and refunding bonds, Series 2020
          A memo from the Management Services Department regarding bonding issues came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated City Administration is proposing a refunding of the Series 2012 and 2013 Stonn Sewer Revenue
Bonds. Given current market volatility, refunding will occur only if it is financially reasonable. The Administration is further
recommending that additional bonds be issued in the amounts of approximately $20 Million for Water Utility projects,
approximately $15 Million for Sanitary Sewer Utility projects, and approximately $7.5 Million for Storm Sewer Utility projects.
          Treasurer Johnson summarized the memo and stated this item was reviewed in depth during the March 31 City Council
work session meeting. The proposed parameters resolution authorizes the issuance and sale of revenue bonds as follows:
               •    Issue amount will be not more than $58,000,000;
               • Not to exceed 5.5% interest rate;
               •    Shall not be sold at less than 97% of the total principal amount;
               •    The term of the Bonds will be not more than thirty-one (31) years;
               • Pledged debt service on the bonds will come from Utility fees; and
               •    Designated Officers are authorized to make final decisions regarding final principal amounts, terms, discounts,
                    etc.
          In addition, to provide flexibility to the City and its pricing committee, the parameters are set above the amount
anticipated to be issued. The proposed parameters resolution is also written to allow for any one of the following bond issuance
structures:
               a. Issuance of new money Water, Sewer and Storm Sewer Refunding Bonds under one Indenture;
               b. Issuance of new money Water, Sewer and Storm Sewer Refunding Bonds under one Indenture with Storm
                    Sewer revenues subordinated to the 2012 and 2013 Storm Sewer Bonds, or
               c. Issuance of new money Water and Sewer Revenue Bonds under the existing Water/Sewer Indenture and Issue
                    Storm Sewer bonds under the current Stonn Sewer Indenture.
          Options b and c are available to provide alternatives if it is not economically advantageous to issue storm sewer
refunding bonds. Ms. Johnson concluded that all new funding will be used for water, sewer and storm utility capital projects
outlined in the respective Master Plans.
          Fred Philpot, of Lewis Young Robertson and Burningham (LYRB), then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
discuss financial modeling used to determine rate structure proposals for the City's utilities; LYRB has recommended adjustments
to the City's rate model responsive to current market conditions. This includes foregoing rate increases for FY2021, maintaining
inflationary increases, recommending consideration for future rate increases for the five-year period, reevaluating the proposed
Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) projects dependent upon future rate increases, and reducing interest costs based on current
market conditions via the $40 million in combined bond proceeds. He provided several charts that provided scenario analyses
based on a combination of different options available to the City; bonding can be used as a tool to avoid immediate rate increases,
but consideration of rate policy adjustments or CIP adjustments would be necessary to avoid spending below defined cash reserve
amounts for each utility fund. He concluded by noting current outstanding issues include evaluation of a blended consumer price
index (CPI)/monetary conditions index (MCI), evaluation of future rate increases or adjustments to the City's CIP, and updates to
existing ordinances regulating fee collection.
          Council discussion centered briefly upon the timing of scheduled projects that would be fmanced with the proposed bond

          Laura Lewis, ofLYRB, reiterated Ms. Johnson's summary of the terms of the super parameters resolution; if the Council

for the bonds as follows:
               •    $1.8 million per year in years 2021-2024;
               •    $2.37 million per year from 2025-2032; and
               •    $2.2 million per year from 2033-2045.
          Ms. Lewis emphasized that no rate increases - beyond inflationary increases - are needed to support the issuance of the

Proposed Ordinance 2020-15 adopting the Capital Improvement Plan for
Fiscal Years 2021-2025
          A memo from Council staff regarding the proposed Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) for Fiscal Years (FY) 2021-2025
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated as part of the CIP process, the City Council discusses and considers
projects proposed in the plan, incorporates changes as desired, receives public input and adopts the plan. The CIP is intended to
prioritize capital project funding for the Fiscal Year (FY) 2021 budget. Council Staff will review the proposed FY2021-2025
Capital Improvement Plan (CIP).
          Council Policy Analyst Mabey summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the
Council staffs overview of the FY202 l-2025 CIP Plan. The objectives of the Plan include scheduling, fmancing and constructing
City capital improvements in the upcoming fiscal year and projected for the following four fiscal years. No funding is allocated or
determined as part of this process. CIP projects are those exceeding $30,000 adding to the City's "physical plant", projects
resulting in the acquisition or long-term lease ofland, and/or feasibility studies and evaluations. CIP funding sources include the
General Fund, federal and state grants, B&C road funds, utility enterprise funds, bonds, and other miscellaneous sources (grants,
partnerships, and fundraising proceeds). The CIP Plan identifies City needs, establishes long-term goals for investment, ensures
expenditures are consistent with the General Plan, and informs the budget and sets priorities. Like the budget itself, the CIP is a
policy document that reflects the values and goals of the City (both the Administration and City Council). Ms. Mabey reviewed
the timeline for considering the FY2021-2025 CIP, noting that the schedule has been adjusted in response to the COVID-19
pandemic. The current CIP proposal for consideration includes 45 total projects; 20 new projects are added, 20 have been removed
(eight of which were funded in the FY20 budget), and three existing projects have been significantly increased (defined as greater
than 20 percent in total project cost). The total for all projects included is $106,507,997. She provided a CIP project breakdown by
department and fund, after which she identified Administration's top nine projects, which total $13,089,000:

Proposed Resolution 2020-11 adopting the Five-Year Consolidated Plan
(July 1, 2020 to June 30, 2025): the Neighborhood Revitalization Strategy
Area Plan: the Analysis of Impediments to Fair Housing Choice: and the
Annual Action Plan for the period from July 1, 2020 through June 30, 2021
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the Fiscal Year (FY) 2021-2025
Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) and Annual Action Plan (AAP) came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated City
Administration is proposing the Council adopt a resolution that approves the five-year ConPlan, an updated Neighborhood
Revitalization Strategy Area (NRSA) plan, Analysis oflmpediments to Fair Housing Choice document, and the first year AAP.
The ConPlan is a document the City prepares that serves as an outline for how the City will use federal grant funding to address
housing, community and economic development and other activities for low and moderate-income individuals and business
owners over a five-year period. The ConPlan focuses on the creation of quality and decent affordable housing, creating a suitable
living enviromnent, and expanding economic opportunities for low and moderate-income persons. In addition, the current
COVID-19 pandemic has led to the inclusion of an Urgent Need certification to provide additional assistance to local small
businesses impacted by the pandemic. A major component of the ConPlan is the Neighborhood Revitalization Strategy Area
(NRSA) which prescribes a geographic area in which the City targets housing programs, economic projects and programs, and
public infrastructure improvements. The memo further explained the designation of a NRSA helps the City achieve greater
community revitalization by focusing resources and opportunities in a specific area. The proposed NRSA for the FY21-25
ConPlan has expanded from the previous plan. The new boundaries include the East Central and Central Business District as
contained in the previous plan but now also include the Jefferson Community Plan area. The proposed NRSA encompasses seven
census tracts. The NRSA meets the threshold for low and moderate-income residents and relaxes some of the reporting standards
with which businesses must comply for participation in federal programs. The designation also allows the aggregation ofhousing
units as well as the application of a public benefit standard. Finally, as part of the application and review process, the City is
required to conduct a thorough review of the challenges individuals may face in Ogden that would prevent having fair access to
housing. The analysis looks at housing, economic and transportation conditions in the City as well as private and public sector
policies that may impact housing choice in the community. Much of the information gathered for the Analysis oflmpediments to
Fair Housing Choice (AI) has been cited and included in the ConPlan. The AI provides additional details on other topics like
foreclosures, land use and zoning policies, and lending practices and policies. The AI identifies five major impediments and
provides a strategy goal for each of the impediments. The memo concluded the Annual Action Plan budget consists ofan estimate
of entitlement funding, program income funds, and carry-over funded as well as the Administration's proposal on how those funds
will be expended throughout the next fiscal year.
          Community Development Manager Ogden summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
summarize the ConPlan and AAP documents. The ConPlan is the City's mechanism for receiving Housing and Urban
Development (HUD) funding from the federal government. The City receives annual CDBG and HOME grant funding and if an
activity is not included in the ConPlan, HUD funds cannot be used to fund it. Con Plan documents include the Five-Year plan
(2021-2025); AAP for FY21; Neighborhood Revitalization Strategy Area (NRSA) document; Analysis oflmpediment's to Fair
housing Choice (AI); and the Own in Ogden Program guideline changes. Mr. Ogden reviewed the process for developing the
ConPlan, noting the Council can consider adoption after the May 12 public hearing; funding will be made available on July 1,
2020.
