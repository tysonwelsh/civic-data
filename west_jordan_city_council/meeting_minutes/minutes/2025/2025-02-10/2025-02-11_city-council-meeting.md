 2. PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE
     Becky Condie led participants in the Pledge of Allegiance.


 3. PUBLIC COMMENT
     Chair Lamb opened the public comment period at 7:01 pm.
     Comments:
     Cheneil Hill said she was in attendance because cities needed to take a stand on corruption,
     abuse, and exploitation of religious protections. She believed change started in cities with local
     policies and ordinances that ensured accountability. Ms. Hill spoke of situations in which
     children were exploited and married young, and she was told by a mayor of a different city in
     the County that the topic was an issue for the Legislature. She did not believe she would be
     taken seriously at the Legislature without cities standing behind her. Ms. Hill asked the West
     Jordan City Council to support ordinances that would prevent religious organizations and
     nonprofits from using their tax-exempt status as a shield for criminal activities, and asked the
     Council to stand behind her as she took the fight to the Legislature.
     Ann Marie Barrett, resident of Draper and Manager of Bingham Creek Library, announced
     upcoming programs at the library and spoke of resources available. She noted that the library
     would be closed on President’s Day.
     A commenter, listed online as Utah Corruption, said he was a resident of Salt Lake City and
     asked to be called Phil. He echoed the first speaker’s sentiments, noting he was into cop
     watching, spoke of police harassment of Cheneil Hill whenever she protested, and questioned
     why Chief Wallentine repeatedly sent the same officers to confront Ms. Hill. He questioned why
     a particular officer was still on the force.

 4. LEGISLATIVE UPDATE
     Public Affairs Manager Adam Gardiner provided an update on the current Legislative Session.
     He highlighted House Bills 37, 88, 90, 292, 291, 295, 300, 454 and 465, and Senate Bills 80 and
     96. Mr. Gardiner said electricity and utilities were hot topics at the Legislature that year, and
     answered questions from the Council.

     City Attorney Josh Chandler voiced concerns with Senate Bill 154 regarding attorney client
     privilege. He told Council he would send them more information.

 5. PUBLIC HEARINGS
      a. Ordinance No. 25-09 amending the budget for the fiscal year ending June 30, 2025
         Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck presented proposed amendments to the
         FY2025 Budget, and answered questions from the Council. Council Member Green
         expressed concern with codification services in general, and asked that the City look at what
         newer services and service providers may be available. Council Member Green encouraged
         staff to look into selecting new budget software to transition away from using Excel for the
         City budget.

         Chair Lamb opened a public hearing at 7:43 pm.
         Comments:
         None
         Chair Lamb closed the public hearing at 7:44 pm.

         MOTION: Council Member Shelton moved to APPROVE Ordinance No. 25-09
                 amending the Budget for the fiscal year ending June 30, 2025.
                 Vice Chair Whitelock seconded the motion.
         The vote was recorded as follows:
         YES:       Zach Jacob, Chad Lamb, Bob Bedore, Kelvin Green, Kent Shelton, Kayleen
                    Whitelock
         NO:
         ABSENT: Pamela Bloom
         The motion passed 6-0.

 7. REPORTS TO COUNCIL
       a. City Council Reports
          Council Member Green thanked the Fire Department for inviting the Council to a recent
          awards banquet. He said West Jordan had amazing firefighters who did amazing things,
          and said the City needed to do a better job of telling their stories. He also provided an
          update on the Mosquito Abatement District.

           Council Member Shelton spoke of the opportunity to call bingo at a recent event at the
           Viridian. He reported on a recent Utah Air Quality Board meeting, and spoke of the current
           Legislative Session.

           Council Member Jacob provided an update on the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy
           District (JVWCD), and reported the JVWCD Board was tracking House Bill 81 regarding
           fluoride in culinary water.

       b. Council Office Report
          Policy Analyst and Public Liaison Warren Hallmark said staff looked forward to providing
          training on the Swagit program at the next Council meeting.

       c. Mayor’s Report
          Mayor Dirk Burton reported on recent events and spoke of upcoming events in the
          community. He noted that City offices would be closed on February 17 for President’s Day.

       d. City Administrator’s Report
          Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck reported the West Jordan Passport Office
          serviced 7,149 passports in 2023 (average of 26 per day), with services available every
          day except Sundays. In 2024, the Passport Office provided passport services to 8,934
          individuals (average of 32 per day), and in January of 2025, the Passport Office serviced

            provided a beneficial service. Responding to a question from Council Member Jacob, Ms.

            structure protection ahead of the Palisades fire front, and keeping hot spots in check as
            the fire came under control. Captain Gates said he learned a lot, and it was an incredible
            experience.

            Council Member Jacob thanked Captain Gates for providing the service, and assured
            residents listening that California would reimburse the City for the cost of providing
