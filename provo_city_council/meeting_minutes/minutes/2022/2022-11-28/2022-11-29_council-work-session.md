1.       A presentation from Utah Clean Energy regarding net zero stretch codes. (22-082)
         (14:38)

Hannah Salzl, Planner/Sustainability Coordinator, reviewed energy efficiency policy
considerations as well as the legal context for the use of net zero stretch codes in Utah. She
explained how and why this applies to Provo, as well as the air quality and public health benefits
to these codes. Air quality was identified in the scientific survey for the General Plan as the main
area the public sees as a major concern and would like to see improvement. The new Provo City
Hall was built to be net zero in order to be a standard for the community and a signifier of the
city’s commitment to sustainability. Stretch codes specifically address electrification in the
private sector. Kevin Emerson, Building Efficiency and Decarbonization Director at Utah Clean
Energy, presented and shared more detail on Utah Clean Energy’s program.

Mr. Emerson explained that net zero stretch codes are a voluntary measure to encourage and
incentivize new construction toward meeting a higher standards rather than the minimum
standard required by code. He highlighted examples of stretch codes being implemented in other
Utah cities. He also outlined additional tax incentives, state-level rebates, and grants available for
developers who construct utilizing these stretch codes. It is important to them that the program is
consistent across municipalities to make it easier for developers to adopt and pursue.

Councilor George Handley asked for clarification about what policies they should adopt; Mr.
Emerson clarified what he meant; a zero-energy ready standard would be the standard for cities
to be eligible for the benefits of a program. Ideally having the same standards implemented in
different communities would make it simpler for developers. Mr. Handley also asked Mr.
Emerson to comment on how this might impact development; he wondered if these types of
policies had the impact of discouraging new development. Mr. Emerson said he has not seen that
issue since the program is completely voluntary for a developer. Mr. Handley also observed that
this type of policy could attract more innovative developers.

Councilor David Shipley asked how best to coordinate with other Utah County cities. He asked if
there were any examples of areas of the state that have had an effective coordinated effort. Mr.
Emerson explained this is a newer concept that has not been done extensively; he was not aware
of specific collaborations between governments. He suggested having a formal organization that
represents the interests of cities to convene a group that can bring the communities together, and
to involve individuals with energy policy expertise as well. He noted this could follow a similar
processes as other industries related to cities.

Don Jarvis, Mayor’s Sustainability Committee Chair, noted that he thought the most important
thing to make consistent between cities are the specific standards applied in different cities. Mr.
Emerson identified 6-8 standards that are fairly common in Utah and there are about 3-4 of these
that they would recommend the City look into pursuing. Mr. Shipley expressed support for
looking at a policy at the City for encouraging developers to use the efficiency standards, such as
prioritizing application review, providing a density bonus, or other incentives. Presentation only.

2.      A presentation regarding potential amendments to ADU petition permit process.
        (22-056) (59:42)

Melia Dayley, Policy Analyst, presented. In a Work Meeting in October, the Council had made a
motion regarding an anonymous petition process. The Housing Committee met twice about this
issue and discussed the matter with several City departments to explore the ramifications and
feasibility. Based on those discussions, the Housing Committee does not recommend moving
forward with an anonymous petition process. Ms. Dayley shared the options that the committee
discussed as well as the concerns identified by committee members or City staff. The main
concerns related to the challenges of implementing and operating such a system as well as the
burden on staff when the process was initially intended to be applicant- and grassroots-driven.

Councilor Bill Fillmore commented with his disappointment. He was worried about the negative
impact this would have on neighbors. He thought there were reasonable answers to each of the

someone submits their information to the city, some of this can be redacted such as their address,
but their name is not redacted. Gary Millward, Assistant City Attorney, explained that under
GRAMA, data is presumed to be public unless a later classification determines that the privacy
interest is stronger than the public interest. Mr. Millward gave some insight into how GRAMA
requests are processed and managed. Mr. Fillmore reiterated his previous concerns.

3.      A discussion regarding the proposed new General Plan. (22-011) (1:41:57)

Council Chair George Handley noted that the General Plan is going to the Planning Commission
on December 7, and the Council will officially deliberate and vote on the General Plan after that.
Bill Peperone, Development Services Director, explained that Councilors can send any non-
substantive issues or stylistic/grammatical issues directly to planning staff. For any substantive
changes, a motion and vote of the Council would be helpful in determining the direction.

Brandon Larsen, Planning Supervisor, reviewed the process the city underwent for the
development and review of the General Plan. It has been through several revisions. Mr. Larsen
reviewed the introduction, land use section, and the changes each underwent following the
survey conducted by Y2 Analytics. He highlighted the updates and changes in the land use
chapter. Councilor Katrice MacKay thought this draft of the plan was much more concise and
easier to follow as it placed related topics together instead of spreading them throughout the plan.
Councilor Rachel Whipple indicated that she was going to review a printed copy of the plan in
more detail and submit her comments to staff. Councilor Bill Fillmore agreed that this was a
much better draft; he thought it addressed issues that were underemphasized in the previous
version. Councilor George Handley asked about the State’s feedback about Provo’s moderate
income housing plan. Hannah Salzl, Planner, addressed this question. She noted several minor
corrections that have been made to that document based on feedback from the State; this is
simply an appendix of the General Plan and did not directly impact the General Plan. Mr.
Handley thought the new draft of the General Plan incorporated a lot of public feedback and he
hoped the public felt heard.

Mr. Larsen briefly reviewed the Housing chapter of the General Plan. The Moderate Income
Housing Plan is an appendix to the General Plan which provides additional details about some of
the City’s housing stock. The Economic Development chapter addresses the economic assets of
the airport, educated workforce, tourism, possible areas for redevelopment, and city gateways.
Next Mr. Larsen reviewed the elements of the Transportation chapter, including alternative or
active transportation, parking, handling growth, connectivity, and sustainable transportation
choices. Mr. Handley invited questions from the Council. He asked if they could quantify how
many students live in Provo; Mr. Handley thought it was important to acknowledge in the
General Plan that Provo is home to a university, many students, and many renters. He also
wondered if the General Plan contained specific and clear language to guide policy
determinations, such as evaluating housing types to know what needs specific development
proposals may be fulfilling. Mr. Larsen indicated that neighborhood plans would be a more
appropriate format in which to address the variety of housing types in a neighborhood. Councilor
Katrice MacKay felt that any specifics should be left in a neighborhood plan.

Mr. Handley suggested adding recreation as an important aspect or element of Economic
Development in Provo; many people want to live in Provo due to its parks, mountains, canyons
and trails and we value recreational tourism as an important draw for people to come to Provo.
Mr. Handley also asked what the safety action plan was. Elizabeth VanDerwerken, Council
Executive Assistant, addressed this question; she explained that this was a requirement that the
City fulfilled in order to be eligible to apply for transportation grants through the federal
infrastructure bill. The safety action plan would also be incorporated into the Transportation
Master Plan. Mr. Handley asked some questions about components in the Transportation chapter
of the General Plan. Mr. Larsen explained they had received comments from Public Works about
adding a reference to the safety action plan—Mr. Larsen will review the goals in the
Transportation chapter to see whether there are any actionable goals they can incorporate.

Councilor Rachel Whipple shared comments about the Transportation chapter as well. She noted
language on page 3 that characterized single-occupant vehicles as being the most efficient, which
