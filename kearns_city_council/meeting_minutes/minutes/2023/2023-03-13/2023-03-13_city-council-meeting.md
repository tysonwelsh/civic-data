Stakeholder Updates

Unified Police Department (UPD)

                Chief Levi Hughes reviewed service calls for February. Although they were
higher than the previous year, this was due mainly to snowstorms. Kearns crime levels continue
to trend downward, despite UPD having several officers are on light duty. He followed up on a
citizen concern that was stated during the Community Council meeting and clarified that the
incident in question was a drive-by shooting that took place in South Salt Lake City, which was
outside of UPD’s jurisdiction. The police did not show up immediately because they were not
notified about the incident until the following day.

Chief Hughes reported that the Officer of the Month award had been previously presented to the
family members of Officer Michael Black, who recently passed away while on light duty. He then
went on to present the Detective of the Year award to Nick Lavulo; the Officer of the Year award
to Kick Powell; and the Commander Choice Award to Brent Rogers.

Unified Fire Authority (UFA)

                Captain Wade Russell reviewed several bills that passed during the recent

Legal Updates/Information

Legislative Update

                Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, reviewed some of the
bills that passed during the most recent legislative session.

HB 374: Due to the passage of this bill, the Kearns Metro Township would have two years to
come up with an agreement with the Sheriff for police services.

HB 406: This was related to land use and imposed a 32-foot limit on residential roads, which was
preferable to the anticipated 25-foot limit. It also imposed restrictions on development
agreements. The main concern was that Kearns, as a city, would have to disclose all rights the
developer would give up in entering into a development agreement. If the City failed to disclose
a right, the agreement would automatically become void in relation to that right.

HB 364 and SB 174: These dealt with modern housing. Collectively, they provided a process to
ensure moderate income housing plans were up to snuff. They would also make above-garage
accessory dwelling units (ADUs) a permitted use. City Councils would no longer approve
subdivisions; this will be done by planning commissions or staff.

SB 271: This prohibited cities from regulating fractionally owned homes (which are similar to
timeshares) differently from normally owned homes. Mr. Bracken’s concern for Kearns was the
prospect of multiple families buying a house and all living there at the same time.

SB 43: This streamlined public notice requirements. It set up different classes of notices and
gave different options for fulfilling requirements.

The short-term rental program did not pass and was tabled. There will be an interim study in the
future. There were a couple bills that discussed formulation of new improvement districts, and
these also failed to pass.

                           A SOCIAL MEDIA POLICY

                                           RECITALS

        WHEREAS, Utah Code Ann. § 10-3-717(1) authorizes the Kearns Metro Township
Council (the “Council”) to exercise all administrative powers by resolution, including the adoption
of policies to govern the actions of municipal personnel and the use and operation of municipal
property; and

      WHEREAS, the Political Activities of Public Entities Act (the “Act”), Utah Code Ann. § 20A-
11-1201, et seq., prohibits the use of public funds on certain electoral and other matters; and

         WHEREAS, the Council desires to adopt a social media policy to establish terms of use
for its social media platforms, ensure compliance with the Act, and otherwise ensure that Kearns’
social media accounts are used appropriately for governmental purposes; and

        WHEREAS, the Council has determined that the social media policy attached to this
resolution will serve the best interests of the Kearns Metro Township and the public.

        NOW, THEREFORE, IT IS HEREBY RESOLVED by the Kearns Metro Township Council
that effectively immediately:

   1. The Kearns Metro Townsite webpage (www.kmtutah.org) and the Kearns Metro Township
      Board Facebook page (https://www.facebook.com/KearnsMetroTownship) are approved
      as official Kearns social media accounts.

   2. Kearns’s administrative assistant, ___________________, is designated as the
      moderator and administrator for each of the official social media platforms of the Kearns
      Metro Township:

   3. The Communications Manager of the Municipal Services District is designated as the
      appeal authority for the purposes of the social media policy; and

   4. The social media policy will be posted to Kearns’s website and social media accounts and
      Kearns’s officials, personnel, and agents are instructed and authorized to take all steps
      needed to implement the policy.

       APPROVED AND ADOPTED in Salt Lake County, Utah, this 13th day of March, 2023.
