      1. RESOLUTION APPROVING 2019 MUNICIPAL WASTEWATER PLANNING PROGRAM ANNUAL REPORT –
         RESOLUTION 9-2020
           Chad Meyerfoffer, of County Engineering, stated that the wastewater system report is submitted to the
           State subsequent to the county’s review of the related financial status, the condition, and of any issues that
           may have arisen in the past year. The infrastructure is fairly new and overall looks very good.
           Commissioner Jenkins moved to adopt Resolution 9-2020 approving the 2019 Municipal Wastewater
           Planning Program annual report; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
           Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye
      2. ORDINANCE ESTABLISHING A PROCEDURE TO PROVIDE TEMPORARY RELIEF FROM BUSINESS PERSONAL
         PROPERTY TAX PENALTIES AND INTEREST DUE TO THE COVID-19 PANDEMIC – ORDINANCE 2020-5

           John Ulibarri, County Assessor, noted that the pandemic has had a negative impact on businesses. In an
           effort to provide temporary relief for those in our county, this ordinance defers this interest and penalties
           until 8/15/2020, while encouraging those who can file to do so by the May 15 statutory deadline. He asked
           that this ordinance be adopted on the first reading in order to more quickly disseminate this information.
           Commissioner Harvey moved to adopt Ordinance 2020-5 establishing a procedure to provide temporary relief
           from business personal property tax penalties/interest due to COVID-19 pandemic; Commissioner Jenkins
           seconded.
           Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins– aye; Chair Froerer – aye
      3. CONTRACT WITH ROCKY MOUNTAIN POWER FOR WORK RELATED TO THE RELOCATION OF POWER
         POLES AND POWER LINES IN THE WEBER CENTER’S EAST PARKING LOT

           Scott Mendoza, of County Community Development, stated that utilities need to be relocated due to the
           Weber Center’s parking structure demolition project. Rocky Mountain will remove two power poles near
           the structure, install one new one, and relocate a new power line to Weber Center’s east boundary to
           continue providing power service to adjacent businesses. The contract cost is $11,361.
           Commissioner Harvey moved to approve the contract with Rocky Mountain Power for relocation of power
           poles and power lines in the Weber Center’s east parking lot; Commissioner Jenkins seconded.
           Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins– aye; Chair Froerer – aye
G. COMMISSIONER COMMENTS: The commissioners have received questions as to why the county is still holding
these meetings, why it does not totally shut down, but they are also being asked the opposite. The commissioners
evaluate the situation daily and try to be responsible. Ricky Hatch, County Clerk/Auditor reported that his office
is mostly closed to the public except to handle urgent needs, and he outlined the needed functions that his office
continues to provide. Sean Wilkinson, County Community Development Director, addressed Commissioner
Jenkins’ questions stating that the county has masks for outdoor-working employees but are running low at the
Transfer Station, which closed to the public yesterday. He said that for the most part people are understanding of
this, but they have also received some not so nice comments. Haulers are still picking up garbage curbside, which
has increased at this time. Commissioner Jenkins reported that the archery building is almost complete.
Christopher Crockett, Deputy County Attorney, said that the Governor suspended the regular Commission meeting
requirements and allowed the county to meet electronically. All participants are in separate locations, maintaining
a safe distance, and are able to continue the essential services. He said that this is an example of staying safe but
still moving forward in a time like this because government has a fiduciary responsibility to provide essential
services to its citizens. He emphasized that there are a lot of reasons why government needs to keep functioning.
To add to what Mr. Hatch had said regarding critical functions by the Clerk/Auditor’s Office, the Attorney’s Office
has received a number of questions from the Health Department, which has to be very mindful of people’s rights
throughout this pandemic. A lot of issues and concerns arise, including those related to quarantine, and a lot of
preparation is needed. Human Resource Policies had to be modified to protect employees, the Sheriff’s Office has
to consider how to protect the inmates, etc. Chair Froerer stated that this is a difficult time but that we have the
choice to look on the positive side, consider how to improve, and come out better.
