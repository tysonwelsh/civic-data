        1.2      Mayor’s Report

     The Mayor then reported on recent ribbon cuttings, one of which was at Rely Home Loans
which was celebrating its one-year anniversary. Hot Worx had also recently opened at The
Crossroads near Regal.

Mayor Overson recently attended the ChamberWest golf tournament where she was able to meet
with 192 local business owners. The event was a great success with many relationships built and
connections made.

The 2024 primary election would take place the following Tuesday, with the Council Chambers
serving as a vote center.

The Taylorsville Arts Council had presented two different productions in the last couple of weeks.
One was Back to the Band which was immediately followed by Taylorsville’s Got Talent. Both
shows were held outside on Centennial Plaza and were well-attended.

The Starry Nights on the Plaza program was gaining momentum and continued to be a huge
success. Mars Highway would perform the following Friday evening.

Beginning July 9th, there would be a Farmer’s Market each Tuesday evening on Centennial Plaza.

Mayor Overson had been reappointed as the Chair of the Tourism, Recreation, Culture and
Convention (TRCC) Board. The Board was extremely important to Taylorsville as it had been the
beneficiary of significant grant funds. This included funding for the current improvements that
Taylorsville Park was undergoing. A site plan was available for public review in the Council
Chambers. Applications had been due for 2025 projects the previous week and Taylorsville had
applied for funding to improve the park at 6200 South 3200 West. A decision was expected in
August.

The City Hall campus had recently been repaved. Similar work would soon take place at the
Taylorsville Cemetery and at various public locations throughout the community.

The Mayor had been recently nominated as the Chair of the UFSA (Unified Fire Service Area)
Finance Committee, keeping an eye on taxpayer dollars. She pointed out that three of the five
busiest UFA stations in the valley served Taylorsville and she greatly appreciated the first
responders who watched over the City.

        3.1      Taylorsville Senior Center Report – Mike Potter

            Manager of the Senior Center, Mike Potter, mentioned that the new pickleball courts
by the Senior Center were being used a great deal. He was very excited about the improvements
coming to the nearby park. There was a volunteer recognition event which would take place on
June 27th. He invited the Council Members to stop by.

Mr. Potter then presented statistical data regarding meals served and activities participated in by
the senior community.

       3.2       Victim’s Advocate Report – Sgt. Jake Hill

              Sgt. Jake Hill highlighted a particular Taylorsville case in order to highlight how
important the work of the victim advocates was. He described a case Merari Lopez was involved
in and due to her rapport with a victim, felony charges were filed, the suspect was arrested and
despite initially minimizing the assault, the victim eventually was empowered enough to get help
for herself and her children.

Sgt. Hill then turned the time over to Alex Perez who described the process that she and Ms. Lopez
followed with each victim they assisted. They began by ensuring their safety and then providing
resources whether it be financial help, shelter, job placement services, food assistance, etc. They
also supported them through the criminal justice process.

        6.2      Resolution No. 24-15 A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville Determining the
                 Certified Rate of Tax and Levying of Taxes Upon all Real and Personal Property
                 Within the City for the Fiscal Year Beginning July 1, 2024 and Ending June 30,
                 2024 – Scott Harrington

        Mr. Harrington explained this was the beginning of the Truth in Taxation process. The
certified tax rate needed to be adopted by June 22nd and a public hearing was currently scheduled
for August 13th although that date might change. The city sought a tax rate of .000851 which would
be a 25.06% increase, generating approximately $4.6 million.

In response to a comment from Chair Cochran, Mr. Harrington explained that the City received
the same amount of revenue each year which was why the tax rate had dropped for ten years in a
row —because house values had risen. The tax rate was lowered, yielding the same amount of
revenue.

MOTION:          Councilmember Barbieri moved to approve Resolution No. 24-15, a
                 Resolution Determining the Certified Rate of Tax and Levying of Taxes Upon
                 all Real and Personal Property within the City for the Fiscal Year Beginning
                 July 1, 2024 and Ending June 30, 2025. The motion was seconded by
