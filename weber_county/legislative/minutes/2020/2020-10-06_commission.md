    3. PRESENTATION & DISCUSSION OF INTENT TO ESTABLISH A NEW PROPERTY TAX RATE IN SUMMIT ROAD
       OVERLAY DISTRICT, NOT TO EXCEED $125,000, TO PROVIDE MUNICIPAL-TYPE SERVICES TO PROPERTIES
       IN THE SUMMIT ROAD OVERLAY DISTRICT -- THIS WOULD REPRESENT A 100% INCREASE OF THAT LINE

         Scott Parke, County Comptroller, showed the area map and noted that last week the Commission
         established the Summit Road Overlay District on the very top of Powder Mountain off of the new Summit
         Mountain Road to provide municipal-type services to the residences in that area. This item declares the
         commissioners’ intent to establish a property tax in this overlay district not to exceed $125,000 (for snow
         removal and road maintenance) in that area. The next step is to send out a mailer to each affected property
         owner (there are over 300 parcels, of which about 70 have dwellings), notifying them of the tax increase
         and announcing the November 24, 2020 public hearing at 6 p.m. He recapped snow removal costs to the
         county—about $1,000/mile/year for the lower valley, about $7,000/mile/year for the Ogden Valley, and
         about $50,000/mile/year for the top of Powder Mountain. There a few people up there but they have been
         taking a huge amount of those dollars to maintain that road. An analysis was conducted. The county gave
         them credit for the additional property taxes that they pay because of their larger homes there. The
         additional expense was offset with the additional property tax that they pay and the difference in the
         shortfall is the proposed tax rate, so that they pay for the cost of the service that they receive.
         Commissioner Harvey stated that this is for the new county Summit Mountain Road (previously a dirt
         road that went to the Powder Ridge condos), and is not for the State road, and that this has been well
         vetted, that it is fair, and he supports it. Commissioner Jenkins also stated his support because it takes
         away part of the unfair burden that the other taxpayers have been paying. He said that the commissioners
         felt it had been unfair for the lower and upper valley residents to have been paying up to 50 times more
         for snow removal and subsidizing that area. The $125,000 does not pay the expenses but the
         commissioners felt that it was a fair start. The commissioners have been systematically working to
         equalize services and expenses and make things fair in the county.
         Courtlan Erickson, Deputy County Attorney, stated that the statute that is part of the truth-in-taxation
         process requires that the Commission state at a public meeting that the county intends to levy a tax rate
         that exceeds the certified tax rate, state the dollar amount of and the purpose for that additional tax revenue,
         and the approximate percentage increase. If the Commission wants to do this, it should state Weber
         County’s intent, as written on the agenda: Weber County intends to establish a new property tax rate in
         the Summit Road Overlay District, not to exceed $125,000, to provide municipal-type services to the
         properties in the Summit Road Overlay District. This would represent a 100% increase of that line.
         Commissioner Jenkins moved to accept the statement as read by Mr. Erickson and incorporate it into the
         minutes of this meeting as the statement of the Commission. Commissioner Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Commissioner Harvey – aye; Chair Froerer – aye
H. COMMISSIONER COMMENTS: Commissioner Jenkins is excited with the commissioners’ continued direction
   to bring uniformity to the county, and he appreciates the hard work and service by the other commissioners.
   Commissioner Harvey gave a shout out to Lt. Governor Cox, who after receiving a telephone call from the
   Commission subsequent to the recent severe windstorm, helped Weber County secure a grinder to turn the
   debris into woodchips. In some cases it took the debris down to an eighth of the original volume and helped
   our community a lot. He also noted that our local Stauffer’s Towing came out with their big trucks and started
   pulling up immense root balls, such as the 16-20 foot in diameter one that he saw them standing next to. Chair
   Froerer noted that the county has been in budget meetings over the last couple of weeks, and he very much
   appreciates all of the effort and time involved, and the interest in making sure that the budget, as we move
   forward, is in the best interest of the citizens of Weber County. He couldn’t be more proud of our departments
   and their efforts to deliver top level service with very conservative budgets, as well as to reduce debt as
   evidenced this morning by the paying off in full of the Pleasant View Library Bond.
