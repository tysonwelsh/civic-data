1. Consent agenda
   a. Capital Improvement Plan Amendment, Francom Public Safety Building Virta Training Facility,
      2186 Lincoln Avenue.
      Mr. Brierley reported the Ogden Police Department has an interactive training facility which
      currently is located at the Weber County Sports Shooting Complex. Their desire is to relocate it to
      their current building in order to make it more accessible to officers. The proposal is to locate the
      facility in an existing gym area, but is currently inadequate in size. Their request is to expand the
      gym area in order to accommodate both the training facility and the gym area. The review of the
      Commission is to determine whether the CIP request is consistent with the General Plan, which
      indicates public buildings should be provided to meet the needs of citizens. The use is consistent
      with both the existing zoning and with the General Plan. Staff recommends approval.
       Chief Young responded to questions of the Commission, stating the proposal is to construct a bay
       area for existing gym equipment and allow the 360 degree virtual training facility to locate in the
       gym area. He indicated Ogden is one of few entities where such a facility is available.
       MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to recommend approval of the CIP
               amendment to allow the relocation of the interactive training facility to the Francom
               Public Safety building based on the findings it conforms to the General Plan and zoning
               regulations. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Graf and passed unanimously
               with Commissioners Aaberg, Blaisdell, Graf, Safsten, Schade, Shale, Shinoda,
               Southwick and Sandau voting aye.

2. Work session - Moderate Income Housing Plan
   Mr. Rypien stated the State Legislature adopted SB24 which requesting cities and counties adopt
   moderate income housing element as part of their General Plan and to report how they are providing
   housing for various income levels. Then in 2022 they adopted HB462 providing clarification and
   adding requirements and resources not defined in their previous action. He defined both moderate
   income housing and cost burdened housing and highlighted requirements now in place for Ogden.

  The role of the Planning Commission is to review General Plan amendments and make a
  recommendation to the City Council for its adoption. The item will be considered at the regular
  December meeting relating to moderate income housing as part of the Housing Element of the General
  Plan, including an action plan for implementation and timing for these to be accomplished. As Ogden
  has an identified transit station, a minimum of four strategies are required to be adopted. The draft
  proposal adds five new strategies and amends others as well as a station area plan, with Staff
  suggesting ten new strategies relating to the station plan. He stated an annual report has been
  submitted by the October due date identifying how current strategies are being accomplished and
  reviewed how cities are found to be in compliance and if found not to be in compliance are given an
  additional 90 days. He indicated Weber County recently completed a housing affordability and access
  study which indicates Ogden provides 68% of the County’s moderate-income housing, and three of the
  four fastest growing cities share boundaries with Ogden. Because of lower income levels in Ogden
  City, some moderate-income housing is still a cost burden to our residents. It also found many
  residents of higher incomes are occupying units which could be identified as moderate housing. He
  concluded, indicating while other cities also need to provide low-income services, these are difficult
  without State or Federal participation. He also indicated growth is expected to continue with multi-
  family units being built until construction costs and interest rates are reduced, allowing home
  ownership.
  Commission indicated all housing types are needed as there is not only a variety in income levels, but
  also mobility in the housing market; students need little space and likely live in apartments, as they
  age, more space is desired and then as children arrive and then leave the home, owners may again
  downsize. It was felt the best neighborhoods provide a diversity in income levels. Commission
  expressed concern the low-income levels in Ogden often reduce commercial development options as
  developers are unsure as to whether their investments are productive over time. Commission also
  discussed the incentives for meeting these standards, indicating some smaller cities may not meet the
  regulations as they may feel the benefit for funding of roads is unnecessary. Some communities
  provide little low-income housing and many of the workers need to travel into the community from other
  cities to work. It was indicated while there are now few options available for view lots, there are
  opportunities for redevelopment and infill. It was suggested that different generations may acquire
  different values in terms of ownership and may find density creates a convenience of services such as
  shopping, transit, food choices and other services.

2. Discussion – Make Ogden Downtown Code amendments
  Mr. Brierley indicated many of the questions discussed during the previous meeting will be addressed
  by the consultant, he desired to discuss both social services and drinking establishments. He
  reviewed the purpose of the Plan stating it promotes infill development in the downtown by providing
  employment, improve access ot amenities, social services and transit with a variety of housing types.
  He stated while citizens of Ogden are typically charitable, there may be conflicts between what zoning
  allows and the missions of various charitable organizations. He provided a HUD study talking about
  the effect of concentrated poverty on surrounding neighborhoods, stating poverty levels over 20%
  create negative effects, and identified areas in Ogden where census information identifies
  concentrated poverty. However, when people go outside their neighborhoods for services, ties are
  created and positive benefits occur. While studies are limited, it does appear the concentration of very
  low income housing in one neighborhood can lead to crime, social issues and continued poverty.
  He then indicated the Seager Clinic staff have met with City Staff and have now obtained a business
  license and building permits for their desired work, stating the City will accept their use at the current
  location and they are supportive of the goals of Ogden City in relation to both the Make Ogden and the
  9-Rails Plans. Discussion continued relating to its location and the use of Lester Park by homeless
  individuals and its effect on both the use of the park and the library.

  He then discussed the regulations for drinking establishments, stating the new language is consistent
  with current regulations where three are now permitted on each side of the street in the CBDI zone,
  which will translate to the Commercial-Entertainment zone and two per lineal block now allowed in the
  CBD zone will translate to the Commercial-Mixed-Use zone. Commission expressed some concern
  that the boundaries of the C-9 extends into what is a residential neighborhood surrounding the library

3. Discussion - Interim Motions
   Mr. Brierley indicated when there is some disagreement about items before the Commission, it is a
   good practice to make individual motions relating to parts of the issue, and once the individual motions
   are voted upon, the full item can then be voted upon as the individual parts have been resolved. He
   stated each of the interim motions could be a voice vote and then the final item would include findings
   and would be a roll call vote. It was indicated when there is an even number of members present, a tie
   vote can occur, and if an item is not resolved, it can be continued until a later date.

Mr. Brierley announced the City Council had adopted the East Central Community Plan and the
associated ordinances earlier this week.

There being no additional business before the Commission, the meeting was adjourned at 8pm.
