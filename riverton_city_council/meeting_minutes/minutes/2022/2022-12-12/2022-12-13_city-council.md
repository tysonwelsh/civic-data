Mayor and Council Informal Meeting

WORK SESSION – 5:45 PM

Call to Order

Mayor Staggs called the Work Session to order at 5:50 PM and conducted a roll call.
Councilmembers Buroker, McCay, McDougal, Stewart and Wells were present.

Presentations

       Bangerter Highway Update

Marwan Farah, Utah Department of Transportation Project Manager, presented the Bangerter
Highway update. This presentation is included in the packet.

Councilmember Buroker asked if the acquisitions had begun on 2700 West. Mr. Farah said the
residents are aware and have been contacted. Councilmember Buroker asked about closures
on 13400 South. Mr. Farah said the moving of the utilities will take place before any closures
will happen. As a result of these utilities being moved, there will be lane closures. Residents
will see construction from Winter 2023 to the Fall of 2025.

Councilmember Wells asked about the Rose Creek relocation and if there would be acquisitions
of properties. Mr. Farah said they are widening and putting in curb and gutter and this requires
some acquisitions. Councilmember Wells asked Mr. Farah to estimate when the actual closure

would occur at 13400 South. Mr. Farah said it would be late 2024 through 2025.

Councilmember McDougal asked if they could maintain access for businesses and schools near
the 13400 South intersection. Mr. Farah said there would be interference but they would
maintain access to these businesses and the school during the construction.

Councilmember McCay asked for a copy of the slides to share with the residents. She would
also like to know how the residents would be detoured around the construction. Mr. Farah said
that north and south bound to continue to flow through the intersection. They are likely to utilize
4150 West. However, there is a possibility of roads being closed for 6-9 months.

Councilmember McCay asked if they would direct traffic traveling east to detour north onto
12600 South instead of through the neighborhood to the south of Walmart. These residents are
concerned with traffic being a problem especially since there will be construction on 2700 West
and Bangerter. Mr. Marah said that before they decide traffic detours, they will consult with
local leaders of the City. Mayor Staggs asked them to communicate as far in advance as
possible for these detours.

Councilmember Stewart asked if they have considered punching in at 13200 South and creating
temporary signals to maintain the left turn on the northbound movement to the westbound
movement. This would give business access and moves it away from the construction zone.
Mr. Farah said they have not considered this option but if they could obtain permission from the
City and from Suburban Land Development, they might consider it.

Mayor Staggs asked if the construction on Bangerter includes 9800 South. Mr. Farah said it
does include 9800 South.

         The Hub (Phase 1 of Redevelopment Plan) at the Point

Scott Cuthbertson, Deputy Director of the Point, presented information on the Hub, Phase 1 of
Redevelopment Plan, at the Point. This presentation is included with the packet.

Mayor Staggs explainted that Phase 1 would include 60-70 acres with construction in 2024-
2025. Mayor Staggs asked what the plan was for retail. Mr. Cuthbertson said there would be
retail in Phase 1 but he doesn’t have the specific information on this subject.

Councilmember Stewart asked how the Pointe received ridership numbers and how they were
awarded light rail for this project. Mr. Cuthbertson said he does not have the answer for this
question. Mayor Staggs said that UTA have been a big topic of conversation in Riverton and he
believes the southwest portion of the valley is underserved.

Councilmember McCay asked if they consider any portions of the prison for a museum. She
appreciates their innovation on this project.

Discussion Items

         Community Center Updates

Mayor Staggs explained that the budget for the Community Center auditorium was $369,000
and has cost the City $381,000. The City has secured the TRCC grant for the first-floor
renovation, which will begin in 2023. He and Councilmember Buroker discussed the City
spending a little more to complete the esthetics for the auditorium to make it all come together.

Councilmember Buroker consulted an interior designer for some recommendations. She
presented the report from the interior designer and this is included in the packet.

Mayor Staggs asked the Council to weigh in on these suggestions. He has estimated the
expense to be about $10,000 to $20,000. The Council agreed to spend money to paint areas
to improve the aesthetics.

Sheril Garn, Director of Events and Operations, said the railing would need to be replaced if the
Council wanted it to be a different color. It should not be painted because it will chip. Mayor
Staggs directed staff to find out about the cost of replacing this railing and get back to the
Council.

Adjournment of Work Session

Councilmember McCay MOVED to adjourn the Work Session. Councilmember Stewart
SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for a vote; the vote was as follows: Buroker-Yes,
McCay-Yes, McDougal-Yes, Stewart-Yes and Wells-Yes. The motion passed unanimously. The
Work Session adjourned at 6:52 PM.

increasing and has become severe in some parts of the state.

Mayor/Council Reports

Councilmember Wells – No Report.

Councilmember Buroker said she was at the Midvale homeless shelter a couple of weeks ago.
She confirmed that every homeless shelter is full and they need volunteers to help sort goods.
She thanked Wendy Garvin for her work with the homeless population.

Councilmember Stewart reported that UFA had a meeting today. Mayor Wickers will be taking
over as chair of UFA. Councilmember Stewart has been the chair of the finance committee for
the past 6-7 years and Chair Henderson will now chair this committee. He said the budget cycle
was presented and they are forecasting higher revenues.

Councilmember McCay reported that she attended the Utah League of Cities and Towns
meeting. They talked about HB 462 which is the moderate-income housing bill. She said that
most cities would be in compliance within the next few weeks, however, there are 7-10 cities
that will not comply. She believes they will add a consequence for these cities.

Councilmember McCay said the governor is planning to provide free fares for 2023. She
believes this would only benefit areas that have access to transit. She said they updated the
free fares to include Via and UTA on demand. The southwest valley needs to get ridership up
to justify transit being budgeted for this area.

Councilmember McCay said she has been in contact with the Riverton Youth Council. She said
they have four or five service projects per month within the City and she is impressed they can
help and serve.

Councilmember McDougal – No Report.

Mayor Staggs said he has been meeting regularly with the Southwest mayors since 2018. They
have worked together to show housing statistics in the county’s southwest part. Bluffdale and
Herriman both have about 50% attached housing, Riverton has about 25% attached housing.
He believes the southwest cities are doing their fair share with the housing situation in the
county.

Mayor Staggs said the southwest cities have been successful at moving up the time frame for
completion of Bangerter interchanges. The southwest cities are now trying to advance the
shared priorities of getting funding for Mountain View Corridor and U-111 to become freeway-
type roads. They are looking at ways to improve east/west traffic, looking at improving trails that
run through all of the communities and looking to secure TRCC funds and any other available
funding.

Mayor Staggs thanked staff for putting together another annual Night of Music.

         Annual Financial Report

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, thanked Riverton City staff for all of their help with
the Annual Financial Report. He introduced Shawn Martin from HBME, LLC, which is the firm
that audits the City.

Mr. Martin presented the Annual Financial Report. He explained their audit opinion was that the
City’s financials were materially correct and fairly stated. They did not see any issues with the
City’s internal controls. The findings from last year have been remedied and they did not issue
any new findings.

Councilmember Buroker was impressed with the debt per citizen of $803 which is the best it has
been in 20 years.
