Findings of Fact: 
1. The Deer Crossing Subdivision was recorded in January of 2002.  
2. Antonia Derasaluce-Gorton and James Gorton are the owners of record of Parcel DC-76 (1.04 
acres), located at 8214 N Promontory Ranch Road, Promontory, Summit County, UT.  
3. Stuart Birdt is the owner of record of Parcel DC-77 (1.30 acres), located at 8172 N 
Promontory Ranch Road, Promontory, Summit County, UT.  
4. The proposed Plat Amendment is legally described as “Deer Crossing Lot 77 Amended.”  
5. As identified in Exhibit A of this Staff Report, the proposed Plat Amendment will result in 
combination of Lots 76 and 77 of the Deer Crossing Subdivision and the removal of the 10-foot 
utility easement from the lot line shared by the two lots.  
6. The applicant also proposes to situate a future home on top of what is now identified as a 
drainage easement in which they plan on bridging/spanning the home over the easement.  
7. The applicant proposes to include a required improvement detail on the plat which shows a 
channel.  
8. Note 5 of the Deer Crossing Subdivision states; The combination of two adjacent lots into a 
single lot may be permitted in certain areas as determined by the Declarant in the exercise of 
Declarant’s discretion. If an owner purchases two adjacent and contiguous lots, and the proposed 
house location straddles a lot line, it will be necessary to have the revised building envelope 
approved by the Design Reviewer in connection with the approval of a specific house plan. The 
approval or disapproval of the Design Reviewer may be made in the exercise of its discretion. 
Further, the combination of lots may require the approval of Summit County for a lot line 
adjustment or an amendment to the Plat. Request for such approval may not be submitted to 
Summit County without the prior written approval of the Design Reviewer.  
9. Note 6 of the Deer Crossing Subdivision states; The maximum building floor area of a home 
within Promontory is 10,000 square feet. This may be further limited by the Design Guidelines. 
The maximum building floor area of a home straddling two lots, as described in note 5, may be 
increased to a square footage not to exceed 15,000 square feet if approved by the Design 
Reviewer, in the exercise of its discretion, in connection with the approval of a specific house 
plan. Within Promontory, floor area is measured as provided in the Uniform Building Code, but 
floor area does not count garages or unheated spaces.  
10. The combination of lots 76 and 77 of the Deer Crossing Subdivision is considered to be an 
“estate lot.”  
11. The applicant is requesting to combine the two lots which will result in an increase of the 
allowed livable space from 8,000 sq. ft. to 12,000 sq. ft.  
12. The proposed lot combination would reduce the potential density which could result in less 
demand on County infrastructure.  
13. The applicant has added a height and width clearance requirement which the Engineering 
Department has approved.  
14. The applicant has provided a letter from Promontory supporting the proposed amendment.  
15. Public notice of the public hearing was published in the Summit County News.  
16. Postcard notices announcing the public hearing were mailed to property owners within 1,000 
feet of the subject parcels.  
 
Conclusions of Law: 
1. The proposal complies with the Ordinance.

2. The proposed Plat Amendment will not result in additional density. 
 
Conditions of Approval: 
1. All service provider requirements shall be met.  
2. Per Summit County Council’s finding on September 16, 2009, a lot combination does not 
permit the re-platting of density that has already been designed, platted, and sold to private 
individuals.

family home on the property. The applicant would like to convert the agricultural building 
to a habitable space. A building permit is required, and the applicant must apply for a

6.   Public hearing and possible action for potential amendments to Section 11-6 of 
the Eastern Summit County Development Code to create regulations relating to 
outdoor lighting of buildings and structures. -Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner shared the background. During the November meeting the lighting 
ordinance was discussed. There were questions from the Planning Commission regarding 
language. These questions should be addressed in the Staff Report. Language was included 
regarding lumens and kelvin. Also included was an exception for ornamental porch 
coverings. There was concern that an applicant for a building permit would have to submit 
a lot more paperwork concerning lighting fixtures, so some of that language was taken out. 
This item was noticed as a public hearing. County Council is in favor of the amendments to 
the lighting ordinance. 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
December 6, 2018

Chair Clyde asked whether it makes sense to have separate lighting ordinances for Eastern 
Summit County and Snyderville Basin or just one Summit County lighting ordinance. The 
East side does not currently have the big shopping centers and multi-family dwellings that 
Snyderville Basin has, but in time those distinctions will disappear.  
Agriculture is not included in the proposed changes. There are residential limitations that 
may need to be modified for larger lots. The existing agriculture is grandfathered in, so the 
lighting ordinance would only apply to new operations. If there are any changes to the 
lighting, then it must comply to the ordinance. 
Commissioner Sargent stated that replacement of light fixtures should not require an 
upgrade because a permit is not required. That would become an enforcement issue. 
Director Putt stated that the biggest concern is communication and education for the 
public. The bigger benefits of the ordinance will be on a larger commercial scale. Major 
upgrades will be addressed by enforcement. The County will not be the light bulb police. 
Lighting will be addressed at the building permit stage, with education about the ordinance 
being shared. 
Planner Milliner stated that regulation of agricultural uses is hard to define. 
Commissioner Wilde stated that the ordinance should require downlighting for everyone, 
including agriculture. The building permit process still requires too much detail shown. Just 
requiring downlighting should be sufficient. Commissioner Sargent stated all the 
paperwork is already required by the building permit process and the only addition is to 
show the type of lighting fixture. Planner Milliner stated the requirement of showing the 
lighting fixture is for protection of the applicant, so the builder will not have to change out a
