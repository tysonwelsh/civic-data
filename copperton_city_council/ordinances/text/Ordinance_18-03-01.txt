  AN ORDINANCE GOVERNING THE ISSUANCE, PRECEDURES, AND APPEAL OF
            EVENTS PERMITS AND THE CLOSURE OF STREETS

BE IT KNOWN AND REMEMBERED THAT, the Copperton Metro Township Council (the
"Council") met in a regular public meeting on March 21, 2018.

                                           WITNESS

       WHEREAS, Utah Code Ann. § 10-2a-4 l 4(3) provides that the ordinances of Salt Lake
County ("County") will remain in effect until the "metro township council amends or repeals the
ordinance."

      WHEREAS, the current special events ordinance set forth in Chapter 14.56 of the County
Code does not conform to the needs ofCopperton, its annual events, or potential economic growth;
and

       WHEREAS, it is the goal of the Council to bring autonomy and accountability as close to
the community as possible, especially in administrative function; and

        WHEREAS, Copperton residents have expressed frustration about the process to obtain
special events permits under Chapter 14.56, and more specifically road closure permits for special
events; and

         WHEREAS, the Council desires to adopt a Copperton-specific ordinance that is based on
Chapter 14.56 but is amended to better fit and more accurately reflect the needs of Copperton and
its residents for special events; and

       WHEREAS, the Council desires to adopt a Copperton-specific special events ordinance
that will assign permit application consideration to the Copperton Community Council (the
"Community Council"), with the Council serving as the appeals authority; and

       WHEREAS, the Council desires to adopt a Copperton-specific ordinance that:

           1. Requires events permits to be submitted fourteen days before the regular
              Community Council meeting scheduled to occur prior to the date of the event; and

           2. Requires the Community Council to distribute permit requests to the Unified
              Police Department and Unified Fire Authority to work with the Community
              Council and event planners to ensure public safety; and

           3. Specifies that County-owned facilities may be subject to additional restrictions and
              permits issued through the County's various departments; and
           4. Requires block parties involving street closures to obtain approval of those who
              reside on the proposed stretch of street closure submitted at the time of the
              application; and

           5. Requires the Secretary of the Copperton Community Council to hold application
              copies; and

           6. Requires the Community Council to issue written decisions approving or denying
              an event permit; and

           7. If the Community Council denies an application, authorizes applicants to file an
              appeal in writing with the Council, in which case an agenda item will be added to
              the agenda of the Council's next regular meeting to hear the appeal;

       WHEREAS, if in the future the Council so decides, it may delegate permit review and
approval authority to a permit officer as before or consider compensation for Community
Council members.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE COUNCIL OF THE COPPER TON METRO
TOWNSHIP AS FOLLOWS:

1. The Council finds that the ordinance attached as Exhibit A will promote the prosperity, peace
and good order, comfort, and convenience of Copperton and its residents; and

2. The Community Council will require applicants seeking a special events permit to use the
special permit application form attached as Exhibit B; and

3. The attached ordinance will take effect on April 1, 2018 .

Title 3 - The Copperton Community Council

Chapter 3.01 - SPECIAL EVENTS

3.01.010 - Purpose .

The purpose of this Chapter is to establish permit requirements for special events on Metro
Township streets and sidewalks and on Metro Township property

3.01.020 - Application of provisions.

This Chapter imposes regulatory requirements on certain activities which are held on Metro
Township streets and sidewalks and on Metro Township Property, and which are defined as
"special events" in Chapter 3.01.030.

These requirements will be applied in a content-neutral manner and without discrimination as to
age, disability, marital status, race, religion, sex, sexual orientation, national origin, political
affiliation or other unlawful discriminatory classification.

3.01.030 - Definitions.

For the purpose of this Chapter, the following words will have the following meanings :

"Athletic event" means an organized competitive or recreational event in which a group of fifty or
more people collectively engages in a sport or form of physical exercise, including but not limited
to running, jogging, walking, bicycling or skating, on any metro township street or sidewalk or
upon metro township property.

"Block party" means an outdoor public party put on by residents of a neighborhood that requires
closing down metro township streets, sidewalks or metro township property.

"Community Council" means the Copperton Community Council.

"Council" means the Copperton Metro Township Council.

"County" Salt Lake County .

"Entertainment event" means an organized event involving the preplanned participation of more
than fifty people, having as its primary purpose the entertainment or amusement of a group of
people, including but not limited to parades, carnivals, fairs, concerts, block parties, movie or
television film events, or neighborhood gatherings, on any Metro Township street or sidewalk in
or upon Metro Township property.

"Metro Township" means the Copperton Metro Township.
"Metro Township street" means a public highway (as defined by applicable law) within the Metro
Township, not designated as a state highway.

"Nonpublic forum" Metro Township property that is not designated or traditionally considered a
place for public expression or speech. Examples of non-public forums include county offices and
employee office space, backstage at a theater, or any other Metro Township property that is not
designated or traditionally used for public expression or speech.

"Political event" means an organized event involving the preplanned participation of more than
fifty people, not including an athletic or entertainment event, having as its primary purpose the
exercise of expressive activities of a political nature, including but not limited to speechmaking,
picketing, protesting, marching, demonstrating or debating public issues, on any Metro Township
street or sidewalk or upon metro township property.

"Public forum" means Metro Township roads, sidewalks, rights-of- way and Metro Township
property that has by tradition been used by the public for assembly and expression, or Metro
Township property that has not traditionally been open for public assembly and debate but that the
metro township has opened for use by the public as a place for assembly and expression.

"Sidewalk" means that area between the curb line and the fence line on either side of a Metro
Township street and not including a highway located within an incorporated city or town.

"Special event" means any athletic event, entertainment event, political event, or other organized
event whether held for profit, nonprofit or charitable purposes.

"Spontaneous event" means an event that is occasioned by news or affairs coming into public
knowledge less than forty-eight hours prior to the event and is conducted at a public forum.

3.01 .040 - Permit required.

A. It is unlawful for any person, corporation, partnership, association or other entity, public or
private, to organize and hold a special event without first obtaining a special event permit from the
Metro Township and paying the fees required in this Chapter.

B. It is unlawful for any person, corporation, partnership, association or other entity, public or
private, to impede access to, or cause the closure of, any street or sidewalk in association with a
special event without first obtaining a special event permit and paying the fees as required in this
chapter. Liability under this Chapter will only apply to organizers and will not extend to
participants of a non-permitted event.

3.01.050 - Exemptions from permit requirement.

This Chapter will not apply to:

A. Gatherings, demonstrations, or protests of fifty people or less who gather at a public forum
to exercise their constitutionally protected rights under the First Amendment of the United States
Constitution.

B.   Any meeting or event that is subject to the Utah Open Meetings Act.

E.   A spontaneous event held at a public forum.

3.0 l .060 - Community Council to act as permitting authority.

A. The Community Council is responsible for coordinating and overseeing the review, approval,
and denial of special event permits pursuant to this Chapter.

B. The Community Council will prepare and distribute checklists and informational materials
for use by the public and others regarding the special event permit process. These materials will
be designed to give adequate information regarding special event permits, explain the permitting
process, list those Metro Township officials involved in that process, and explain that:

        I. The Unified Police Department and the Unified Fire Authority may change additional
           fees to cover costs they may incur providing services to the special event, which may
           be based on the number of officers or personnel and amount of support equipment
           required; the date and time of the event; the route location and length of the special
           event; anticipated traffic and weather conditions; the anticipated number of participants
           and spectators; the nature, composition, format and configuration of the event; and the
           estimated time for the event. Applicants will pay any fees charged by the Unified Police
           Department or Unified Fire Authority and the Community Council will verify that such
           fees have been paid before it will issue a special event permit.

        2. Additional approvals and fees may be required from County agencies if the special
           event involves facilities or services the County operates or provides within the Metro
           Township, such as Parks and Recreation, Library Services, Aging Services, or other
           agencies. Applicants are responsible for securing all necessary County approvals and
           paying any fees directly to the applicable Council agency. The Community Council
           will verify that applicants have obtained the necessary County approvals and paid the
           necessary County fees before issuing a special event permit.

C.    The Community Council will assist members of the public in applying for and receiving
special event perm its.

D. The Community Council will distribute, collect, and consider special events permits under
the supervision of the Council.

3.01 .070- Permit application procedure.

A. All applications for special event permits in the Copperton Metro area will be made on a
special event permit application form approved by the Council and will include the following
information:
       I.   Type and description of event;
       2.   Name of the sponsoring entity, contact person, mailing address, email address and
             telephone number;
       3. Proposed date, together with beginning and ending times and road closure times, if
             any;
       4. Proposed location, including barricade plan, traffic detour plan and route map;
       5. Estimated numbers of event staff, security staff, participants and spectators;
       8. Signature of applicant;
       9. If the special event is a block party, the applicant must collect and submit with the
            application form a list of signatures consenting to the street closure from all neighbors
            whose vehicular access to their property is affected by the street closure;
       I 0. For events involving County property or services, evidence that the applicant has
            secured the necessary County approvals;
       11. Whether the applicant is a resident and proof of residency, if applicable.

B. Special event permit application forms may be obtained from the Community Council or
from Community Council Secretary, under the supervision and appellate authority of the Council.

C. Applicants must submit permit applications to the Community Council for consideration and
approval no later than fourteen (14) days before the regular Community Council meeting
scheduled to occur before the date of the event, provided that the Community Council may, in its
sole discretion, accept untimely applications if it determines that special circumstances exist that
excuse the untimely filing of the application and that the Community Council will have sufficient
time to fulfill its responsibilities under Chapter 3.09.

D. Special events that cross or involve multiple governmental jurisdictions are subject to formal
authorization from all relevant governing bodies.

3.01 .080- Permit Fees.

A. There is no fee for Metro Township residents to file a special event application. Each initial
application for a special event permit filed by non-residents of the Metro Township will be
accompanied by a nonrefundable application fee of $5.00.

D.   The following special events will be exempt from the fees set forth in this section:

       1.   Political events;
       2.   Parades ofless than one mile in length;
       3    Events sponsored in whole or in part by the Metro Township; and
       4.   Block parties.

3.01 .090- Coordination with Other Government Entities.

A.      In order to promote, protect and assure the safety and convenience of the public in their
use of Metro Township streets, sidewalks, and other Metro Township Property for the special event
or otherwise, the Community Council will consult with the Unified Police Department and the
United Fire Authority to determine if the special event requires professional peace officers to
provide traffic control, emergency medical services, fire safety, or other services. After consulting
with the Unified Police Department and the Unified Fire Authority, the Community Council will
include any special terms and conditions it deems necessary, in any, in the special events permits
it issues pursuant to Chapter 3.01.0I0(C).

B.       The Community Council is authorized to consult with any other government entity, such
as the Salt Lake County Health Department, if it believes such consultation is necessary to
promote, protect, and assure the safety and convenience of the public in its use of Metro Township
streets, sidewalks, and other Metro Township Property for the special event or otherwise. Based
upon this consultation, the Community Council will require additional terms and conditions it
deems necessary, if any, in any special events permits it issues pursuant to Chapter 3.01 .01 0(C).

C.      If the Community Council requires terms and conditions that will require the applicant to
pay fees or pay for the services of another government entity, it will notify the applicant in writing
of these and any other terms and conditions it may require and will instruct the applicant to make
the necessary arrangements to pay for the fees of the other government entity. Once the
Community Council has verified that the applicant has paid the necessary fees, it will issue a permit
for the special event pursuant to Chapter 3.01.010.

Chapter 3.01.010 - PERMIT APPROVAL AND DENIAL.

A. The Community Council will issue a special event permit to the applicant upon the
Community Council's determination that an application complies with the requirements of this
Chapter. The Community Council will notify all affected agencies of all special event permits
issued pursuant to this Chapter in writing.

B.   The Community Council may deny a permit application for a special event if:

        1.   The proposed special event violates an applicable law, ordinance, policy, procedure,
             or regulation;

        2.   The proposed special event is not consistent with the intended nature and use of the
             requested Metro Township property unless the applicant demonstrates that there is
             no alternative forum by which the applicant may reach the intended audience with
             the same intended message.

        3.   The proposed special event is scheduled at a place and time that will disrupt the use
             of an already approved special event permit.

        4.   The proposed location or facility is not adequate to accommodate the proposed special
              event, or the nature of the event is such that the Metro Township cannot ensure the
              health, safety, and welfare of special event participants or the general public.

        5.   The applicant lacks the legal authority to contract, or to sue, and be sued.
       6.    The applicant has an unpaid debt to the Metro Township or any law enforcement
             agency or personnel for prior costs incurred during a prior special event and the
             applicant has failed to satisfy that debt.

       7.    If applicable, the applicant has not obtained the necessary approvals or paid
             applicable fees required by other applicable government entities under Chapter
             3.01.090 .

C. The Community Council, or the Copperton Metro Township Council under Chapter 3.01.120,
may condition the issuance ofa special events permit on the applicant satisfying certain conditions
that they determine are reasonable and necessary to ensure the health, safety, and welfare of event
participants and Metro Township Citizens in addition to any terms and conditions the Community
Council may require pursuant to 3.01.090. For example, the Community Council may require the
applicant to provide adequate bathroom facilities, security, or post a bond for clean up or other
costs.

D. If Copperton Community Council, or the Council under Chapter 3.01.012, denies a permit
application for a special event, it will provide the applicant with a written notice that:

        I.     Specifies in detail the basis for the denial by citing the law, ordinance, policy and
               procedure, or regulation justifying the denial, and describe how the special event
               is not consistent with the cited provision; and

       2.       Describe how the burden on free speech, if any, brought about by the denial is
                necessary to facilitate the Metro Township's interest in protecting the health,
                safety and welfare of Metro township Inhabitants.

E. The Community Council, in denying a permit application, may authorize a special event
permit at a date, time, location, or route different from that requested by the applicant. An applicant
will notify the Community Council that it will accept an alternative permit within three days of
receiving notice of the alternative permit, but no later than three days prior to the scheduled special
event.

F. The Community Council will have the authority to revoke any permit upon violation of the
terms or conditions for issuance. The Community Council may also revoke a permit in the event
of a declaration of emergency.

G.    The County Sheriff may disperse a special event that is being conducted in a manner
inconsistent with the conditions of a special event permit issued by the Metro Township or if the
event is in-violation of any federal, state or local law.

3.01.01 t - Liability insurance and indemnification.

A. No special event permit will be issued unless and until the applicant has submitted to the
Community Council a certificate of insurance, listing Metro Township as an additional insured,
on an occurrence policy issued by an insurance company authorized to do business in the state,
showing comprehensive general liability and property damage coverage for the event with
minimum limits of: one million dollars for injury or death for one person in any one occurrence;
two million dollars for injury or death for two or more persons in any one occurrence; and one
million dollars for property damage in any one occurrence. Applicants may request an alternative
coverage amount, which the Community Council, or the Council under Chapter 3.01.012, may
award in their sole discretion if they determine that an alternate amount will provide sufficient
insurance coverage for the special event.

B. The following special events will be generally exempt from the insurance requirements set
forth in this section, provided that the Community Council may require liability insurance if it
determines that the special event may subject the Metro Township to potential liability and that
liability insurance is necessary to protect the Metro Township against such liability:

       1.   Political events;
       2.   Parades ofless than one mile in length;
       3.   School events located on, or directly adjacent to, school property;
       4.   Events sponsored in whole by the Metro Township; and
       5.   Block parties

C.     If the Metro Township sponsors a special event, the Community Council and the Council
will ensure that the Metro Township's liability insurance coverage is sufficient to cover the event.

D.     In consideration for the issuing of a special event permit and the use of Metro Township
streets and sidewalks or other Metro Township property, the Community Council will require the
applicant to indemnify, save harmless and defend the Metro Township, its officers and employees,
against any claim for loss, damage or expense sustained by any person on account of injury, death
or property damage occurring by reason of or arising out of the special event.

3.01.012 - Appeal procedures.

Any applicant may appeal in writing an administrative decision by the Community Council to
deny an application for a special event permit, or any decision by the Community Council to
impose a condition on the issuance of a permit, to the Council within seven calendar days after the
date the applicant received written notice of the administrative denial or condition, provided that
the appeal must describe the reasons why the Community Council's decision was in error. Upon
receipt of the written appeal, the Council will include the matter on the agenda of its next regular
meeting. After reviewing the appeal and the Copperton Community Council's decision, the
Council will issue a written decision within four business days of hearing the matter. If the
Community Council accepts an untimely application filed within less than fourteen calendar days
of the event, the Metro Township will utilize its best efforts to timely process the application and
to resolve on an expedited basis any appeals filed, provided that the Metro Township incur no
liability if it is unable to process an untimely application before the date of a special event. A
decision by the Council shall be considered a final decision of the Metro Township and may be
appealed to the applicable district court of the State of Utah within thirty days of the decision
pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 10-9a-801.
3.01.013 - Violations and Penalties .

A violation of this Chapter by an applicant will be a class B misdemeanor. Failure to obtain a
permit or to comply with the terms of a permit as required by this Chapter may also result in an
enforcement action by Unified Police Department or Unified Fire Authority, which may stop a
permitted event and/or may issue citations where event staff or participants violate other state
statutes or Metro Township ordinances, including but not limited to traffic rules and regulations,
disturbing the peace, public nuisance, failure to disperse, trespass, or other health and safety
regulations.
