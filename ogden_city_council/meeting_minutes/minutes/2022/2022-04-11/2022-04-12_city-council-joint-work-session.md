Council, Mayor, and Fire Department Discussion

Fire Chief Mathieu and Deputy Chief Slater engaged in discussion with the Mayor and City Council regarding current
operations at the Fire Department. There was a focus on firework and fire restrictions in the City based upon drought conditions;

Fiscal Year 2022 Annual Action Plan Amendment #3

Community Development Manager Ogden explained City Administration is proposing the Council approve the third
amendment to the FY2022 Annual Action Plan and budget. The proposed amendment specifically addresses the allocation plan
for grant funds related to the HOME Investment Partnerships American Rescue Plan Program (HOME-ARP). The funding related
to this grant has significant restrictions placed on it and may only be used for specific purposes related to addressing
homelessness. As per the city’s Five-year Consolidated Plan, the City itself does not administer any programs related to

homelessness or homelessness prevention. Ogden City, however, is a partner with Weber County and other agencies that do deal
specifically with homelessness issues. The HOME-ARP funding can only be used to serve individuals that meet one of the
following definitions as defined by HUD:
e Homeless.
e Atrisk of Homelessness.
e Fleeing, or attempting to flee, domestic violence, dating violence, sexual assault, stalking, or human trafficking.
e Other populations: specifically where providing supportive services or assistance under Section 212(a) of
NAHA (42 U.S.C. 12742(a)) would prevent the family’s homelessness or would serve those with the greatest
risk of housing instability.
When considering eligible services for the HOME-ARP funding, the following activities may be undertaken and must
benefit the qualifying populations as described:
e Rental Housing
e Tenant Based Rental Assistance
e Supportive Services
e Non-Congregate Shelter
While Ogden City does administer a number of programs related to housing, the City does not administer any programs
that would qualify under the definitions provided by HUD for the grants. As such, the proposal is for the City to publish Requests
for Proposals (RFP) to partner with organizations that do provide the services that would qualify for the HOME-ARP grant
funding. Ogden City Community Development staff performed outreach and consulted with the providers of said services to
determine where the funding may be most beneficial.
Discussion among the Council and staff centered on homelessness resources and guidelines for residents to access
housing vouchers in the community.

Fiscal Year 2023 Annual Action Plan

Community Development Senior Project Coordinator Fuentes reported City Administration is proposing the Council
adopt a resolution that approves the Annual Action Plan and budget for Fiscal Year (FY) 2023. Fiscal year 2023 is the third year
of the five-year ConPlan period. The amounts in the Annual Action Plan budget as approved by the Council are incorporated into
the Major Grants section of the City budget. The five-year ConPlan identifies the City’s priority objectives for the use of federal
grant funding for that period. In each of the five Annual Action Plans for the ConPlan period, the programs the City has adopted
are analyzed based on the priority objective on which the program is based. Anticipated goals and achievements as well as funding
levels are assigned to each priority objective and associated program. The Annual Action Plan budget consists of entitlement
funding, program income funds, and carry-over funded as well as the administration’s proposal on how those funds will be
expended throughout the next fiscal year.

Discussion among the Council and Ms. Fuentes centered on guidelines for use of Community Development Block Grant
(CDBG) funds; job creation activities; target areas for home acquisition and improvement; citizen input regarding the
homeownership assistance programs; and the results of a community-specific housing study and housing affordability.

Proposed City Boundary Line Adjustment between the Cities of Ogden and
North Ogden

Planning Manager Montgomery reported a developer is proposing to build a 142-unit apartment complex near Ogden’s
border with North Ogden City, at approximately 1514 North Washington Boulevard. The developer would like to build on
properties on both sides of the City boundaries but doing so would result in an inconsistent development. Developing in two
different cities would mean having to gain separate approvals for site plans, permits, two different utility providers, and separate
fire/police services. In order to maintain consistency across the development, North Ogden City has requested to adjust city
boundaries with Ogden and absorb approximately 1.6 acres from Ogden. If the Council desires to adjust municipal boundaries,
then a resolution must be passed stating an intent to act. After passing a resolution, and following a 60-day protest period, the
Council may adopt an ordinance approving the boundary adjustment. North Ogden City must follow through with the same
process in order to finalize the boundary adjustment.

Discussion among the Council, Mr. Montgomery, and North Ogden Associate Planner Brandon Bell centered on the
developers plans for the project; current property taxes paid for the property; and actions taken on past boundary adjustment
petitions. There was some hesitancy on the part of a few Council members to approve the boundary adjustment and allow for the
subject property to be incorporated into North Ogden City, while other Council members expressed a sentiment about the
importance of being a good neighbor to surrounding communities. The Council concluded to take some time to contemplate the
implications of the proposed boundary adjustment; they decided to discuss the matter further in the next work session meeting and
asked staff to provide information about the potential increase in the value of the property and the increase of property tax revenue
upon completion of the proposed development.

Council Business

Bus Rapid Transit: Council member Choberka stated she has heard from businesses that are being impacted by the Bus
Rapid Transit (BRT) project and she would like for the Council to consider opportunities for creating a program to which
businesses can apply for financial assistance to compensate them for lost revenue during the duration of the construction project.
Chair Nadolski stated that it would be important to consider the significant fiscal impact of such a program unless the program is
only available to businesses impacted by this one project and not all construction projects in the City.
