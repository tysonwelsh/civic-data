 1.        A presentation of Accreditation for Provo Parks and Recreation. (21-023) (0:10:39)

 2.        Resolution 2020-04 transferring $614,197 from the General Fund to the Airport Fund for
           marketing and an interfund loan payment to the Energy fund and appropriating $124,144 in
           the Airport Fund. (21-018) (20-017) (0:27:21)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Resolution 2021-04 as currently constituted, has been made
                          by council rule.

John Borget, Administrative Services Director, presented. Provo City has agreed to contribute $50,000 in
marketing costs for each new destination Allegiant flies to from the Provo Airport. Allegiant had recently
announced Denver and Orange County as new destinations, requiring a $100,000 contribution from the
City to Allegiant for marketing purposes. This would be funded with a $100,000 transfer from the
General Fund.

Additionally, on April 21, 2020, the Municipal Council approved a resolution authorizing an interfund
loan between the Airport Fund and the Energy Fund for $4,900,526 to purchase land near the Airport.
The loan was for 10 years with a fixed principal payment of $490,053 and a variable interest rate equal
to the monthly Utah State Public Treasurer's Investment Fund rate. The loan was to be paid back with
Airport revenues and if revenues were not sufficient a transfer would be made from the General Fund
for all or a portion of the payment.

The Airport was being subsidized by the General Fund ($141,411 in the fiscal 2021 Budget) and was not
in a position to make all or a portion of the payment, so Administration was recommending a transfer
from the General Fund of $514,197 which includes interest of $24,144.

 3.        Resolution 2020-05 approving a substantial amendment to the program year 2019 Annual
           Action Plan incorporating additional funds for COVID-19 Public Health Response. (21-024)
           (0:37:15)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Resolution 2021-05 as currently constituted, has been made
                          by council rule.

Dan Gonzales, Management Analyst, presented. In response to the pandemic caused by the spreading of
the COVID-19, the federal government provided an economic stimulus package through the Coronavirus
Aid, Relief and Economic Security Act (CARES Act), which made available, at the first tranche, $5 billion
in supplemental CDBG-CV funding to prevent prepare for and respond to the coronavirus.

The funds were administered by the Office of Community Planning and Development (CPD) of the U.S.
Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), which had notified Provo City of an additional
expected allocation of $487,563 in CDBG-CV3 grant funds. To use the funding, the City is required to
amend its PY2019 Annual Action Plan (AAP).

The CARES Act provided flexibilities and waivers for CDBG grantees to make it easier to use CDBG-CV
grants for coronavirus response, including amending the City’s Citizen Participation Plan to suspend
regulatory requirements associated with CDBG funds, such as a reduced comment period, opportunity
to hold virtual public hearings and citizen participation reasonable notices and opportunities to
comment.

A duly noticed Public Comment Period from January 16, to February 2, 2021 was conducted for citizens,
partner agencies, and other interested parties to review and comment on the Plan’s proposed
amendment. Electronic copies of the Plan were available for public review at the City’s website. This
hearing would close the Public Comment Period.

Funding in Provo would be disbursed to the following agencies:
    Community Action and Food Bank
    Rocky Mountain University of Healthcare Professionals
    Utah Regional Housing
    Provo City Community and Neighborhood Services for small business and housing assistance.

 7.        Ordinance 2021-06 amending the Zone Map Classification of approx. 11acres of real
           property, located at 1920 W Center St., from Community Shopping (SC2) to Neigh. Shopping
           Center (SC1) and Medium Density Residential (MDR). Fort Utah Neigh. (PLRZ20200282)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2021-06 as currently constituted, has been made
                          by council rule.

Aaron Ardmore, Planner, presented. The Provo City Development Services department was requesting
a zone change from the Neighborhood Shopping Center (SC2) zone to the Medium Density Residential
(MDR) zone and Neighborhood Shopping Center (SC1) for four parcels of land around 1920 West Center
Street. The subject property was zoned SC2 approximately 20 years ago for a grocery store. Citizens filed
a lawsuit to challenge the zone change and the grocery store was never built. Within the subject
property the Dell Cox Family Partnership own 5.5 acres. Smith’s Food and Drug Centers holds a lease on
the Cox property, and they own the remaining property.

Councilor Harding had requested more information about other SC1 zones in the City and Mr. Ardmore
shared a presentation with the Council to demonstrate what other SC1 zones looked like. (1:26:14)

Keith Morey, Economic Development Director, explained that a west-side grocery store was a priority to
the City Administration and to the citizens of west Provo. The current SC2 zone had a stifling effect on
obtaining a west-side store. Potential grocers were hesitant to move forward with a store if a competing
store could be built on the subject property. The city staff had spent years encouraging the property
owner to move forward with a grocery store. Since it was apparent a grocery store was not going to

happen on the subject property, it was in the City’s interest to rezone the subject property so a grocery
store may be located elsewhere on the west side.

The MDR zone was proposed by staff so that there was not a diminution in property value from the
current zoning and so the area could meet the General Plan goals of a mixed-use village center, while
providing more rooftops to attract retail uses. The SC1 zone along Center Street should provide enough
space for community level retail amenities to build and support the neighborhood.

Since the September 9th Planning Commission, staff had been in contact with the landowners about
what they want done on the property. There had been discussion of waiting for a grocery store or
building residential. The Cox family felt that providing any input on what the future use or zoning should
be was premature and they would prefer to wait to provide input until Smiths/Kroger has provided
more detail on their plans for the property.

Neighborhood Chair Johnathan Hill spoke to the Council. He was glad the City was making these efforts
to bring in a grocer. They understood this was the best way to entice another grocer to the West side.

Mr. Cox, property owner, thought the City had given Smith’s ample opportunity to present a proposal,
and Smith’s had failed to do so. The Cox Family was not opposed to this change. Councilor Ellsworth
asked Mr. Cox what the highest and best use of his property was. He and his sister were not totally in
agreement, but he felt like this was the best option. His sister preferred residential as the safest way to
develop the property. She had concerns about a strip mall on Center Street.

Melanie McCoard, Provo, was happy for this change. She agreed with Mayor’s comments during work
session, Smith’s had held the City over a barrel. There were many people who wanted Smith’s to put in a
store, but it was time to play hardball. They were not meeting their corporate goal to serve
communities.

Scott Yergersen, Provo, had concerns about the rezone. He thought this was the ideal property for a
Wes side grocery store. He said his neighbors would continue to go to Orem, a store off University Ave
on the West side was not going to be effective. He agreed a strip mall was not ideal on Center Street.

Johnathan Hill, Neighborhood Chair, asked if Smith’s would be informed of this rezone and whether
there was an option for a zone change if this zone change took place. Keith Morrey, Economic
Development, said they would do anything necessary to put in a grocery store. They had been in regular
communication with Smith’s, and they were aware of this zone change. If they were to make a request
for a rezone tomorrow, Mr. Morey and the department would be happy to consider the request.

There were no other comments from the public. Vice Chair Shipley closed public comment.

Vice Chair Shipley thought the best use was a grocery store and he was concerned about something else
going in and then the zone change being irreversible. If the owner were committed to being open to
something different, that would be fine, he just wanted a grocery store. At some point someone would
go down the process to build something that fit the zone.

Councilor Harding appreciated Shipley’s concern. Everyone agreed a grocery store would be the best
use. However, status quo was no longer okay. A delay of three to four year for another grocer to build
