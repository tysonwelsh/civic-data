• Safe Haven Project-Based Voucher Request  
o Presented by Russell Opatz, VP of Housing and Support Services at Valley Behavioral Health (VBH). CEO 
Kimball introduced Russell Opatz, who is a former employee of Housing Connect. We get to work very 
closely with him through his position with Valley Behavioral Health. We do third -party property 
management through Choice Property Solutions, and we manage several properties that VBH owns. 

 
2 
 
o Mr. Opatz started we have about a dozen properties where we also provide deeply supportive housing, 
Safe Haven being one of them. VBH built these properties with tax credits and HUD dollars back in the 
1990s. VBH had a very different revenue model than we do now. What mean s is we were able to 
essentially subsidize the rent at these properties, similar to what a voucher program does. Now that we’re 
no longer an MCO and we’re a traditional 501 (c) (3), our funding is like any other non-profit organization. 
We have a dozen properties we’re able to provide and fund supportive servic es through Medicaid. But 
we’re not able to necessarily provide that rent subsidy at these tax credit properties.  
As probably many of you in the room know, Safe Haven is one of the properties Housing Connect manages 
and it has a negative net operating income , and that’s with VBH subsidizing close to $150,000 a year in 
rent subsidy. In front of you, and in the Board book, you have a letter I sent to CEO Kimball and the Board 
last month. There’s 49 units between the two buildings, and they’re located at 550 West 700 South in the 
Granary district. We’ve been working with an architect and have a project scope. We are looking at 54 
units with a rehab – we’ll be adding five (5) units to the site. Our intent is to apply for a nine percent (9%) 
permanent supportive housing application next month for 54 units and to consolidate the projects.  
Valley (VBH) can support the services and fund those. But again, one of the needs, and especially when 
we look at – so there isn’t any debt on the property currently – but as we look at taking on more debt to 
pay that debt service down, we’re going to need a more robust and stable operating revenue source, 
which brings us to Project -Based Vouchers (PBVs). We’ve had conversations with CEO Kimball and Dan 
Nackerman at the city housing authority. The city housing authority has agreed to support 27 PBVs, so 
half of the units; and they will be writing a letter of support for our LIHTC application. 
o The request for Housing Connect is to consider partnering with the Salt Lake City Housing Authority and 
VBH on making the project whole with a subsidy and a letter of support for our LIHTC, with the intent to 
provide PBVs. It will be a competitive 9% PSH project. CEO Kimball clarified the PBVs would be needed at 
the end of 2027  as the certificate of occupancy wouldn’t be until the end of 2027. Chair Bernal asked if 
the vouchers would be contingent on getting the credit funding. I guess the vouchers would help you get 
that, because they would show the financial contribution. Mr. Opatz replied, correct. It’d be a letter of 
Support and Commitment. However, if we did not get awarded then, we could revisit it but ideally, we 
could revisit it. I know we can’t guarantee everything, so that would be the timing. It would be a letter of 
support from the city housing authority and Housing Connect and we hopefully will know this summer.  
o CEO Kimball said we’re not looking for a motion, but an indication from the Board you are supportive of 
us going forward. Given the climate, I wanted to have a thumbs-up or thumbs-down (an indication) about 
supporting project-based applications at this time on this project specifically.     
o Commissioner Ramos asked where does it come from, out of our Continuum of Care? CEO Kimball replied 
it comes out of our Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) budget, and it means that over time, we have 27 less

Chair Bernal reminded everyone that we’ve allowed a certain percentage of our vouchers to be classified

folds that maybe wouldn’t be served well on the mar ked; and the other that we’ve approved in concept

PROPERTY OF THE MONTH 
• Kelly Benson Apartments (KBA) 
CAO Kienast presented.  
o Kelly Benson Apartments opened in April, 2010 ; it’s a nice, little property over in West Valley City. Our 
targeted population is Seniors, over the age of 55, with disabilities, including those who have been 
homeless or who have been homeless, or at risk of homelessness. There are 59 units at KBA: 41 one -
bedroom units; 8 studio apartments; and 11 two -bedroom units. Currently, we have rental subsidies 
through the shelter, plus care and project -based vouchers. We will be seeing the shelter plus care 
vouchers. There's 12 of them. 
o We’re losing the funding for those at the end of June, and we’re working with COO Timmerman and team, 
coming up with ideas how we’re going to cover the gap. 
We have two case managers on the property, who are included in the onsite support team, along with a 
service coordinator. Those are Housing Connect employees funded by a State grant. We have contracts 
with Salt Lake County and the State of Utah. And currently, we look at both of those sources as being less 
stable than they have historically. County funding comes from the general funds, and those at the county 
have been tight. 
CEO Kienast has made comments on the presentation about exposure to risk of the county funding, 
precipitated by the loss of the shelter plus care vouchers. We continue to look for ways to potentially 
mitigate the risks to funding, with our goal of providing stability and independence to seniors with 
disability. 
 
• CEO Report  
o CEO Kimball said there’s a few items I want to highlight today.  
▪ We had two staff, and three Board members attend the National NAHRO Washington Conference. 
The most beneficial thing I came away with was a conversation with NAHRO leadership from 
Adrian Todman, who was the acting HUD Secretary. We talked about what to expect and that was 
focus on your mission, look at your audience, and really talking about the work we do as being an 
economic engine. It’s going to be one of the things that may resonate with the change in 
leadership in DC, and certainly with our state representatives. There was encouragement to look 
at expanding ‘houses’ of partnerships and alliances; and reach out to the League of Cities and 
Towns, and possibly looking at untraditional relationships. 
They discussed trying to figure out how to raise the profiles of housing authorities, and what they 
do, and the supply they have, and there is going to be a fight in terms of how and who gets served. 
The direction of consolidation of housing authorities, possibly up to the state level, which is 
problematic because they’re thinking vouchers and not all of the assets and obligations. We also 
heard about term limits for those we’re serving with work requirements, and looking at 
discontinuing services for mixed families, which is where one of the household members may not 
have legal status. 
▪ We received word earlier in March our Emergency Housing Voucher will be ending at the end of 
2026. I believe that was part of the original approval language for those vouchers. These were the 
vouchers that came out during Covid, and they were targeted towards homeless individuals and 
households who were at high risk of contracting COVID. There are eight (8) housing authorities 
awarded vouchers and  285 active vouchers, and we have 98 households we are looking at 
internally to absorb these households into the Section 8 program. When these vouchers come 
out, they don’t sunset and we usually get them rolled into the base , and that hasn’t happened 
this time. We’re reaching out to other housing authorities to see what they’re doing. We’ve only 
heard back from Cedar City and they’re suggesting a conversation with the State Homeless office.
