CONSENT AGENDA:

•   Minutes from the meeting of September 8, 2022.

•   PC 22-054 Howland Short Term Rental [Conditional Use Permit] for a 2-bedroom short-term
    rental located at 516 East 1000 North in the Traditional Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) zone;
    TIN 05-030-0015 (Adams Neighborhood).

    CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL
    1. All standard conditions of approval will be recorded with the Conditional Use Permit and
       are available in the Community Development Department.
    2. This Conditional Use Permit authorizes a two (2) bedroom short-term rental occupying the
       basement of the single-family home.
    3. This Conditional Use Permit authorizes two (2) people per bedroom with two (2)
       additional individuals for a maximum of six (6) occupants in the STR.
    4. Occupancy for the single-family home portion of the structure shall comply with LDC
       regulations.
    5. A minimum of two (2) parking stalls are required for the STR and two (2) are required for
       the residence. The four (4) total stalls shall be located off-street, outside the front yard
       setback, meet minimum LDC dimensions (9x18) per stall and paved with an approved
       hard surface. Parking shall be reviewed and approved prior to the issuance of a business
       license.
    6. Parking of recreational vehicles, trailers, boats, ATVs or similar vehicles associated with
       the STR shall be legally parked on-site and are not permitted to be parked on-street.


   7. Rental contracts for the STR shall be for 30 days or less.
   8. Rental contracts shall not be offered to the same individual, group, or party for more than
       two consecutive rental contracts in a six (6) month period. The owner shall maintain rental
       records available for review and inspection by Logan City.
   9. Quiet times shall comply with the City noise ordinance of 10 pm to 8am.
   10. A Logan City building/fire inspection is required which may result in building remodeling
       and building permits prior to the issuance of a business license.
   11. A Logan City business license is required before a rental contract can be issued.
   12. The STR shall comply with local and state Building, Health, Fire, Safety, Maintenance and
       Nuisance Codes prior to operating.
   13. Exterior lighting shall be concealed source, down-cast and reviewed and approved prior
       to the issuance of a building permit and shall comply with current LDC regulations.
   14. No signs are approved with this Conditional Use Permit. All signage shall be approved
       and permitted by staff in accordance with the Land Development Code.
   15. No fences are approved with this Conditional Use Permit. All fences shall be approved
       and permitted by staff in accordance with the Land Development Code.

   FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL
   1. The proposed project conforms to the requirements of the Land Development Code and is
      listed as a conditional use in the Use Table.
   2. This STR meets the City’s STR density and location parameters as specified in Chapter
      17.37.130.
   3. As conditioned the use is compatible with the surrounding land uses and will not interfere
      with the use and enjoyment of adjoining or area properties.
   4. The streets providing access and other infrastructure to the subject property have adequate
      capacities and level of service for the conditional use.
   5. The Planning Commission has substantiated the findings for a Conditional Use Permit in
      Chapter 17.42.050.

PUBLIC HEARINGS

PC 22-049 100 West Executive Suites -Continued from the August 25th meeting [Design
Review Permit] Justin Campbell/Jabaa LLC, authorized agent/owner is requesting a Design
Review Permit to construct a new 80,920 square foot, five-story hotel building with parking on the
1st floor and 50 executive suites on upper floors. Located at 162 South 100 West in the Town
Center-One (TC-1) zone; TIN 02-046-0019 (Ellis Neighborhood).

STAFF: Senior Planner Russ Holley stated that this project was continued from last month’s meeting
when the Planning Commission requested additional materials showing the floor plans and a design
for additional fenestration on the ground floor of the front façade, which the applicant has provided.
R. Holley reviewed the applicant’s request for a Design Review Permit as outlined in the Staff Report
as well as an updated plan submitted by the applicant showing shrubbery added along the front of


the building, the facade remains the same. R. Holley also went over the floor plans submitted for
the five floors, the size of the rooms, and the parking garage.

R. Holley went over the concerns that were discussed during the last meeting. Condition number 2
authorizes a 50-room hotel for transient sleeping, specifying that long-term residential use, over 30
days, is not allowed. This condition has not been changed, the application is for a hotel and that is
the land use that will be permitted, with the condition that it will be permitted for short-term occupancy.
Condition number 4 requires 60% fenestration on the ground floor of the west façade. Staff
evaluated the proposed design alterations and felt that the applicant has meet the intent of the
fenestration code language but deferred to the Planning Commission to determine if the 40%
fenestration with the added shrubbery is sufficient.

One public comment received after the deadline was handed out to the commission before the
meeting.

Commissioner Lucero asked if the two doors on the front façade are glass and if they are included
in the fenestration. R. Holley said that they are glass doors and do count towards the 60%
fenestration.

Commissioner Croshaw asked about parking space number 9 and how it is supposed to be accessed
if there is a car parked in the space to the east. R. Holley said that tandem parking stalls are usually
associated with the same room.

PROPONENT: Justin Campbell, the architect on this project asked the commission if they had any
questions.

Commissioner Guth said that the building looks nice and likes how the upper floors are presented to
the street. She feels the fenestration should be more architectural and wondered if the glass above
the doors could extend vertically. Mr. Campbell stated that extending the glass over the doors would
not be an issue.

Commissioner Peterson asked about adding trellising, for a more permanent design to add to the
fenestration, and why they decided to not go with that option. Mr. Campbell said that he did not want
to cover the brick.

Commissioner Lucero asked if condition number 2 was no longer a concern. Mr. Campbell said that
their initial concept was for a non-traditional hotel so they felt that there was a limiting factor in the
language of condition number 2, but after further looking into it, they found that there is a limiting
factor in the building code as well, so moving forward they will be treating this project more like a
traditional hotel.

Commissioner Lucero asked about the size of the rooms. Mr. Campbell said they are trying to create
a studio atmosphere with the rooms. Mohammed Abdullahi, Assistant City Attorney asked if the
units would have kitchenettes. Mr. Campbell said that they didn’t want to add kitchens to the units
yet because they are not sure if all the units will have full kitchenettes, some may just have a small
fridge and a sink.

PUBLIC: Frank Schofield, Logan City School District said that the School District is concerned with
not knowing who the tenant will be for the property. The ability of the owner or tenants to maintain
the property to a sufficient standard will determine the impact the building has on the neighborhood
and the school community. Extended stay hotels have higher crime rates and being near a school
creates disruptions to the school and safety concerns with parents. While extended stay hotels do


play a role in housing within the community, across the street from a high school is not the best
location without better security and gated openings.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Guth would like to see more transparency required on the main floor
to reach part of the 60% fenestration. Commissioner Lucero asked how specific the commission
needs to be on the 20% that is needed to meet the 60% fenestration requirement on the ground
floor. Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director said that if the commission thinks there are
alternatives to what has been presented that would better meet the intent of the code they can require
those, but they also need to remember that the ground floor is a parking garage and there are
structural requirements that have to be met.

Commissioner Lewis said that there are surface treatments that can be added to the solid portions
of the wall that would break it up some. He also agrees with Mr. Schofield that the openings should
be gated somehow to keep people from going in and out.

Commissioner Guth said that the intent of the required fenestration is so that the building is
approachable, welcoming, and has a street presence for interactive use.

Commissioner Lucero asked if the parking stalls that are not very accessible need to be addressed.
An Alternative Parking Plan was approved by the Director for the parking reduction down to 51
parking stalls.

Commissioner Heare asked if one dumpster is enough for this building. R. Holley said that
Environmental addresses this by scheduling more pickups for this site. Commissioner Heare also
said that he is fine with the added landscaping meeting the fenestration requirement.

R. Holley said that as proposed with the added shrubbery (20%) and the existing openings (40%)
they meet the 60% fenestration requirement on the ground floor.

Commissioner Guth would like the 20% split between landscaping and architecture, by adding
additional glass around the entryways or if possible, to the corners of the building. Commissioner
Lucero asked Mr. Campbell to come back up to address where he can add glass. Mr. Campbell said
that meeting the 60% is challenging. The 3 ½ feet of structure on either side of the entry doors is
insufficient as is. He will add as much glass as is possible above the doors and work with the city if
trellising is needed in the openings to meet the 60%.

The commission discussed amending condition number 4 to state that additional landscaping and
metal trellising features and/or glass above both entries to meet the 60%.

MOTION: Commissioner Guth moved to conditionally approve a Design Review Permit for PC 22-
049 with the amended conditions of approval and findings for approval as listed below.
Commissioner Croshaw seconded the motion.

CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL
1. All standard conditions of approval will be recorded with the Design Review and are available
   in the Community Development Department.
2. The Design Review Permit authorizes a 50-room hotel building for transient sleeping land use
   (no long-term residential uses (more than 30 days) are allowed).
3. The alternative parking plan for 51 parking stalls (5.6% reduction) has been approved by the
   Director of Community Development.


4. The proposed ground floor west façade, shown with 40% fenestration, shall have additional
    landscaping and metal trellising features and/or glass above both entries to bring the total up
    to 60% fenestration.
5. Landscaping (shrubbery) shall be used to screen cars parked inside the structure adjacent to
    the two wall openings south of the driveway entrance.
6. Building materials shall match the submitted architectural drawings.
7. Exterior lighting shall be concealed source, down-cast and reviewed and approved prior to
    the issuance of a building permit and shall comply with current LDC regulations.
8. No signs are approved with this Design Review Permit. All signage shall be approved and
    permitted by staff in accordance with the Land Development Code.
9. No fences are approved with this Design Review Permit. All fences shall be approved and
    permitted by staff in accordance with the Land Development Code.
10. Rooftop mechanical and/or building wall mechanical equipment shall be placed out of view
    from the street or screen from view from the street.
11. Weather protection shall be provided above all pedestrian entrances.
12. Prior to issuance of a Building Permit, the Director of Community Development shall receive a
    written memorandum from each of the following departments or agencies indicating that their
    requirements have been satisfied:
      a. Fire – contact 435-716-9515
          • Access issues around the perimeter of the building, aerial apparatus access required. Fire
               Department access insufficient around the perimeter of the building.
          • With a height greater than 30 ft. aerial fire apparatus is required according to IFC D105
               within 15 ft. to 30 ft. on one side of the building, absent of obstructions. A complete fire flow
               analysis shall be provided by applicant. Required fire flow will be determined using the
               type of construction, fire area square footage and if fire sprinklers are installed. The exact
               type of construction may unknown at this time, but is most likely Type II for parking and
               Type IIIB for apartments. This review will be evaluated as such. Subject to change if the
               construction type is determined to be different. According to Table C102.1 a fire flow
               requirement of 10,500 gpm requires 8 accessible hydrants
          • There are existing hydrants in the area. At least one hydrant is required within 100 feet of
               each fire Sprinkler Fire Department Connections (FDC). Discussions required for
               additional fire hydrants.
      b. Engineering - contact 435-716-9160
          • Provide water shares or in-lieu fee for increased demand to City system for the new
               development.
          • Area being developed is less than 1 acre, therefore State requirements for onsite retention
               of the 90% storm, a Storm Water Pollution Prevention Plan, and a Notice of Intent are not
               required. Development must however retain/detain the 100-year storm event per City
               Storm Water Design Standards.
          • It appears that development has made an effort to have access to underground parking to
               align with parking lot access on west side of 100 West. If this is not in alignment with the
               west side access, relocate development access to align with west side access.
          • Dedicate a Public Utility Easement in the landscaped areas between the building and
               property line.
          • No site utilities shown in review documents, will address all utilities for compliance to City
               standards during the building permit design review process.
          • All existing utility service lines to development that are not used with the new development
               shall be capped at the City main line.
          • Remove all existing access approaches to the site and restore curb and gutter to be high
               back curb and park strip areas


        • All new accesses through City right of way shall be a minimum of 8” thick concrete
     c. Water/Cross Connection – contact 716-9627
        • The buildings water main needs to have a RP (ASSE1013) installed and tested on the
          water main after the water meter, as it enters the building before any branch offs or
          possible connections inside or outside of building. Properly sized drain required to serve
          the backflow assemblies dump port.
        • Refer to Utah State Amendment # 608.1.1,608.1.2,608.1.2.1 for installation criteria.
        • All landscape irrigation system’s fed from Logan City water must have a high hazard rated
          backflow assembly installed and tested. All backflow assemblies must be tested within 10
          days of turning in water to them and annually thereafter.
        • Fire suppression systems that are connected to Logan City water (with no added
          chemicals) must have a minimum DC (ASSE1015) installed and tested. Fire risers and all
          B/F assemblies must be installed and tested as per Logan City standards.
        • Project shall comply with all current Utah State plumbing codes, amendments and
          Utah Admin. code R 309-105-12-1 rules and regulations including, but not limited to,
          those pertaining to backflow protection and cross connection prevention, during and
          after construction.
     d. Environmental – contact 716-9761
        • Minimum 60 ft. straight on access required. Approach must be level, no down or uphill
          slopes.
        • Minimum inside measurement for a double enclosure is 24 ft. wide by 10 ft. deep.
          Minimum inside measurement for a single enclosure is 12 ft. wide by 10 ft. deep.
        • Place bollards in the back of the enclosure to protect walls.
        • Gates are not required, however if desired, they must be designed to stay open during the
          collection process.
        • Barrel hinges are suggested for the gates. We need the full 12 ft. clearance so gates
          must be designed to open completely.

FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL
1. The proposed project is compatible with surrounding land uses and will not interfere with the
   use and enjoyment of adjacent properties because of the building design, site layout, materials,
   landscaping, and setbacks.
2. The Design Review Permit conforms to the requirements of Title 17 of the Logan Municipal
   Code.
3. As conditioned, proposed project provides required off-street parking.
4. The project meets the goals and objectives of the TC-1 designation within the Logan General
   Plan by providing services near high-capacity roadways and is designed in way for easy
   circulation of both pedestrian and vehicles.
5. The project met the minimum public noticing requirements of the Land Development Code and
   the Municipal Code.
6. 100 West provide access and is adequate in size and design to sufficiently handle all traffic
   modes and infrastructure related to the land use.
