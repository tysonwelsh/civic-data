Proposed Zone Text Amendments to Multifamily Development Design
Standards in Residential Zones

Planning Manager Brierley explained the Ogden City Planning Division is proposing design standard changes for new
multi-family residential development in residential zones. The proposed ordinance amendments would replace subsection B of
Section 15-13-27, Design Standards for Multiple-Family Dwellings. The original section lists only five standards as they relate to
multi-family design standards. The proposed amendments provide more detail and specificity with regard to building types,
exterior materials, building windows, building orientation, main entrances, parking location and amenities. There are also some
minor changes to the design standards in commercial zones. The changes are summarized as follows:

e Building Types— The proposed amendments provide five types of development of multiple-family dwellings.
These include:

o Single-family rowhouses or townhomes.

o Great house which is designed to resemble a large single-family home and contains between 3 and 8
units.

o =A small apartment building with up to 8 units with a maximum of 2 stories.

o A courtyard apartment with | to 2 stories in a “U” shaped configuration.

o Anapartment building taller than 2 stories where access to units is located within a common interior
corridor.

e Building Exterior Materials — The proposed amendments provide standards for the types of acceptable building
materials and includes brick, stone, wood, stucco or fibrous cement siding that has a texture of wood, stucco or
other materials. The changes provide standards for allowable primary and secondary materials and accents for
the different building types.

e Building Windows — The proposed amendments would allow a glass reflectivity of no more than 20% and
would require at least 10% glass surface for each floor level of the building.

e Building Orientation — The proposed amendments require new buildings to front onto a public street. The
regulations also require main entrances to front onto the public street with the exception of a courtyard
apartment which would face onto the central courtyard. Secondary access may be incorporated into the different
types of buildings.

e Building Main Entrance Requirements — The proposed regulations provide design standards for main entrances
including interior lobbies and access corridors for great houses, small apartments, and apartment buildings.

e Parking Locations — The proposed regulations would allow parking only at the rear of the building for single-
family rowhouses with attached or detached garages or a parking lot. Parking would be limited to the side or
rear of other types of units.

e Amenity Requirements — The proposed changes prescribe allowable amenities and open space for apartment
buildings with more than sixteen (16) units. The proposed amenities are very similar to those approved for the
multi-family units in commercial zones.

e Other Changes to C-2 and C-3 Zones:

o Minor amendment to front yard setbacks for dwelling units: 20 feet for dwellings not along
Washington Boulevard and 15 feet for those fronting Washington Boulevard.

o Amended wording for entrances requiring them to create an enclosed lobby and access corridor to
units within the building.

o Updated wording for fibrous cement siding as an exterior material and a cap of two (2) main exterior
materials.

o Clarification of fagade material offsets and street facing building modules.

Mr. Brierley concluded the Planning Commission reviewed this proposal at its June 1, 2022 and July 6, 2022 meetings
and forwarded a recommendation of approval to the Council. The Commission’s recommendation was made with the findings that
the changes are consistent with the General Plan as articulated in the Planning Staff report attached to this memo.

The Council briefly engaged in discussion of certain amendments to various standards, ultimately concluding to further
explore the potential consequence of approving the amendments in a future work session meeting.

Proposed Modifications to Ogden Youth Council Bylaws
Council Policy Analyst Hallmark summarized the memo and stated keeping the initial purposes and objectives of this
initiative intact, general revisions to the Ogden Youth City Council program can be summarized as follows:

e Youth Council and Youth Leadership Academy are consolidated into a single membership group called the
Ogden Youth City Council (OYCC).

e From the OYCC, seven members will be elected to leadership positions, collectively serving as part of the
Ogden Youth Executive Council (OYEC).

o Youth are elected to leadership positions for one term (one school year = one term) and positions
include:
= Chairperson
* — Vice-Chairperson
= Recorder
= Treasurer
» Public Relations Officer
* Social Officer
* — Service Officer

e No limits on how many youths can be accepted to the OYCC; previously only nine were permitted to youth
council, and an unlimited number permitted to the academy.

e Noterm limits (one school year = one term); if a student wants to participate in OYCC for the duration of their
high school experience for example, they’re no longer restricted to only two years.

e Allmembers of the OYCC, which are not elected/appointed to executive positions, will be assigned to various
committees and/or appointed to deputy positions, as approved by Chair, Vice Chair, and/or program Advisor(s)
and Supervisor.

e OYCC members will have the opportunity to serve as liaisons to various boards and commissions, as
appropriately coordinated.
