1.       An ordinance amending the zone map classification of .95 acres from PRO-R22
         zone to the LDR (Low Density Residential) zone for property located at 193 West
         4800 North, to allow for a 6-unit townhome development. Riverbottoms
         Neighborhood. (PLRZ20210112) (0:10:50)

Aaron Ardmore, Planner, presented. Several structures were demolished in 2014 along 4800
North. In 2018, a project plan was submitted to construct a project on this site. Several single-
family lots were included in the site plan for this project, which were intended to serve as a
buffer between the denser housing units and the surrounding neighborhood. The developer has
returned with a proposal for one of the vacant properties at the site. Councilor Katrice MacKay
asked if the townhomes would be for sale or rent only. Mr. Ardmore indicated that the developer
may reserve a couple of units for personal connections, but the intent was that the units would be
for sale. Councilor Travis Hoban asked about the neighborhood feedback. Mr. Ardmore
indicated that they had received no negative feedback from the neighborhood. Presentation only.
This item was previously scheduled for the Council Meeting on August 23, 2022.

2.      A presentation regarding the economic development of Provo City. (22-066)
        (0:16:00)

Keith Morey, Development Services Assistant Director, presented. Mr. Morey played a video
that highlighted some of the economic development successes in Provo. Provo has been a top-
performing city in the country for two years in a row. Mr. Morey highlighted some of the
accolades and achievements for Provo as a business and community destination. He continued
and explained the branding and marketing campaign for the “Provo Advantage.” They are
developing monthly topics that they are sharing with local community influencers and business
leaders. Mr. Morey shared some of the feedback they have received from the local business and
development community. He highlighted some of the current major projects in Provo which
represent significant investment in the City, as well as some of the future projects they were
expecting to move forward in the coming months. Mr. Morey also reviewed recent and
current/ongoing redevelopment projects along with the projected economic benefits of these
projects. Their outreach efforts include local companies, real estate businesses, and other
community partners in the area. Mr. Morey highlighted several additional efforts, including sales
tax reimbursement agreements, a business resource guide, support for the startup community,
and joint promotional work. Mr. Morey invited questions and comments from the Council.

Council Chair Travis Hoban thanked Mr. Morey for a great presentation and for the work they
are doing to be proactive. Councilor George Handley commented on the redevelopment of the
old city center building and the typical return on investment for something like that. He asked if
the city has been looking at metrics for their return on investment 15 years out, and what would
be the loss if this property wasn’t converted into a successful multi-use project. Mr. Morey
shared some comments on this and indicated they are working on identifying what the current
market is, as well as the impacts of inflation on a potential project. They are preparing a new
request for proposal for the project redevelopment which will include an economic analysis.

Councilor Bill Fillmore also thanked Mr. Morey for his presentation and their great work in
recent years. He agreed with Mr. Handley regarding doing a strong redevelopment project
downtown. Mr. Fillmore also commented on implementing an economic development fund. Mr.
Morey shared some ideas from other municipalities and entities that Provo could potentially use
as ideas for implementing something similar. Mr. Handley also shared some comments on a
possible economic development fund—he liked the idea that these City savings could be used in
a productive way that would generate a return on investment. Presentation only.

3.      A presentation to Council on the 2022 Fireworks Season. (22-048) (1:00:25)

Fire Chief Jim Miguel and Lynn Schofield, Fire Marshal, presented. Chief Miguel thanked Parks

negative aspects of the 2022 firework season. Mr. Schofield also shared details about several
brush fires that occurred in the City. He also highlighted several areas for improvement in future

4.      A presentation regarding the "Safe Streets For All" grant opportunity- Status
        Update & Further Discussion. (22-065) (1:27:50)

Rob Hunter, Engineer, introduced the presentation. Mr. Hunter shared an update on this item,
which was presented to the Transportation and Mobility Advisory Committee at their last
meeting. TMAC is supportive of the grant application and their role in overseeing the
implementation as it is enacted by Public Works. They planned to present the Safety Action Plan
and a resolution for approval at the Work and Council Meetings on September 6, 2022.

Vern Keeslar, Parametrix, also presented. Mr. Keeslar highlighted the components of the Safety
Action Plan and gave a broad overview of each section of the plan. One component of the plan
relates to equity considerations in transportation. Mr. Keeslar reviewed in more detail the equity
considerations and data sources that help inform this evaluation and planning. These metrics seek
to highlight transportation disadvantaged Census tracts within the city. He highlighted some
examples of the types of factors that resulted in that designation. Of the 28 Census tracts in
Provo, 10 of these have four or more applicable categories and are considered transportation
disadvantaged. Mr. Keelsar reviewed additional data that is included in the Safety Action Plan,
including crash and safety data for various streets and intersections in Provo.

Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked if all state roads in the City were exempted from using grant
funds. Mr. Keeslar indicated that the exemption included all state roads, but there were still
potential ways to improve adjacent roads and approaches connecting to state roads using grant
funds. Councilor George Handley asked if the data included collisions with animals; Mr. Keeslar
indicated if the property damage was over $12,000, those collisions were included. Mr. Keeslar
shared a preliminary list of the high-crash locations they have seen in the current data. Councilor
Rachel Whipple asked if they could compare data before and after the safety improvements and
bicycle infrastructure changes were made to Cougar Boulevard. Ms. Whipple asked if that data
showed the project was successful in reducing crashes. Mr. Keeslar indicated they could obtain
this data and share it with the Council. In Provo, 72% of fatalities and serious injury crashes
occur on about 3% of roads. The intent of the Safety Action Plan is to identify serious dangers
and to implement engineering solutions to avoid these types of issues in the future. Councilor
Bill Fillmore shared comments on his participation with TMAC, and how TMAC is involved in
this process. He asked Mr. Keeslar to highlight the timeline for the grant application. Mr. Keeslar
indicated the grant application deadline is September 15. Presentation only.
