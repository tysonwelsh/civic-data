PUBLIC HEARING: November 13, 2018 — 5:30 P.M. RESOLUTION NO. 2018-25 - Declaring
County Interest in Real Property including Parcel Number 06-018-00038 as Surplus Property —
Executive Buttars stated the county owns 20% of the Chamber of Commerce Building where the
Daughters of Utah Pioneers (DUP) Museum is located. The DUP has a no cost 99-year lease t
with the Chamber.

Chairman Erickson opened the Public Hearing and invited public comment.

Sharon Johnson, Director of DUP Museum, reviewed the history of the DUP and Chamber's association.
The DUP understand that the Chamber needs to sell the building and are aware that the lease will
continue when the building is sold, but the problem is once the county surpluses its share, the DUP will
be responsible for property tax on 20% of the building. They do not have the budget to accommodate
that expense and hope to find a way to mitigate the financial impact.

Jamie Andrus, Chamber of Commerce Director, indicated the building has been appraised and the fact
that the DUP will remain goes with the sale of the building, but the lease remains with the Chamber.

Council member Merrill thanked Johnson and Andrus for their cooperative efforts.
There was no other public comment.

e Resolution No. 2018-22 —~ Amending the Cache County Corporation Personnel
Policy and Procedure Manual IX(A)(3) and IX(B)(4) regarding Employee Benefits,
Holidays and Personal Leave — Human Resources Director Adams explained that,
currently, employees can roll over one-third of their accrued personal leave time (PLT)
at the end of the year and many employees try to use PLT during December rather than
lose it. This amends the policy to read that employees can roll over one-third of their
accrued PLT by their benefit eligible date. This will help distribute PLT usage over the
entire year instead of just December. The first part of the Resolution deals with
including animal control officers as able to accrue "specials/in lieu of holiday pay” rather
than have the day off with holiday pay when they do work on holidays. The effective
date will be today, but will not take effect until January.

MEMORANDUM 23 October 2018

To: County Council
Subject: View of the Valley Design Exception request

A request has been made by Douglas Bassett (Ambience Construction and Development) for a
Design Exception on county road 1600 West, to allow access to the View of the Valley Subdivision,
consisting of three additional lots, without road improvements to the paved portion of 1600 West
(Exhibit A).

The Cache County Manual of Roadway Design and Construction Standards (Road Manual) section
1.8 Authority and Design Exceptions, specifies that:
“Full justification and documentation must be provided explaining the reasoning as to why the
roadway standards cannot be met, why an alternative design or construction method can meet the
intent of the roadway standards, and including any other relevant information.”

The View of the Valley Subdivision has been approved by the Planning Commission with the
condition that the proponent obtains a design exception from the Road Manual for the substandard
sections of the access road (Exhibit B).

The submitted request for Design Exception provides the following reasoning:
1. The existing roadway surface was recently chipped and sealed by the county.
2. The Nautica Subdivision (11 additional lots) gains access from 1600 West and was approved.
3. The cost to the proponent to meet the standards is unbearable for the proponent.

The Development Services staff and Director have completed a review of the Design Exception
request and have identified the following:
1. The Road Manual specifies the following:

a. §2.1-A-3 Local Road, Table 2.2 Roadway Typical Sections: Roads with approximately
40 to 1500 Average Daily Traffic (ADT). This includes roadways that have the capacity
for moderate to low speeds and moderate volumes. This category provides a balance
between through traffic movements and direct access. These facilities move both regional
and local rural traffic with emphasis on local movements.

b. Table 2.2 Roadway Typical Sections: Local roads must meet the minimum standards of a
66-foot-wide right-of-way, two 10-foot-wide paved travel lanes with 2-foot-wide
shoulders (1-foot-wide gravel and 1-foot-wide paved) for a total width of 24 feet.

c. §2.4-A-l-c: Development on inadequate roadways is not allowed, and any substandard
sections of roadway access must be improved to meet the minimum standards specified
in the Road Manual.

d. Table A-8 Typical Cross Section Structural Values: The minimum structural composition
for gravel roads requires 14” depth of granular borrow, 6” depth of road base, and paved
roads required an additional 2.5” depth of asphalt.

e. §2.4-A-4-b: The review of requests for development on existing roadways must occur
through the Design Exception process.

future.

impractical.

CACHE COUNTY RESOLUTION 2018 - 22

A RESOLUTION AMENDING THE CACHE COUNTY CORPORATION PERSONNEL
POLICY AND PROCEDURE MANUAL SECTION IX(A)(3) and IX(B)(4) REGARDING
EMPLOYEE BENEFITS HOLIDAYS AND PERSONAL LEAVE

WHEREAS, the Cache County Council on November 27, 2018, ina regular meeting of which
lawful notice had been given, considered amending Sections IX(A)(3) and LX(B)(4) regarding employee
benefits, holidays and personal leave; and

WHEREAS, the Cache County Council finds that it is necessary, appropriate, and in the best
interest of the County and its personnel that the Cache County Corporation Personnel Policy and
Procedure Manual be amended;

NOW THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that the County Council approves the adoption of the
following resolution:

1 Amendments: The Cache County Corporation Personnel Policy and Procedure Manual is hereby
amended as set forth in the attached Exhibit A.

2 Application: The amendment to the Cache County Corporation Personnel Policy and Procedure
Manual shall apply to all current and future county employees.

3 Prior Resolution and Policies: This Resolution and the amendments specified in Exhibit A to
the Cache County Corporation Personnel Policy and Procedure Manual supersede all previously adopted
resolutions and policies to the extent that they are in conflict with the specified provisions of this
Resolution and the attached Exhibit A.
4 Effective Date: For employees with a benefit eligible hire date between November 13 and
December 31, for the 2018 benefit year, this resolution takes effect January 1, 2019. Otherwise, this
Resolution shall be effective immediately upon its adoption.
Adopted by the County Council of Cache County, Utah, this 27th day of November, 2018.

CACHE COUNTY COUNCIL

David L. Erickson, Chairman

Three Options for RU2 and RUS Rezone Requests

As requested by the County Planning Commission and Council, the Development Services staff

has prepared three possible approaches to the RU2 and RUS Zones.

Option 1 reflects the existing approach.

Option 2 is built from the existing approach but with more specific criteria.

Option 3 is a code amendment that adds a sunset clause to the county code for further RU2 or
RUS rezone requests. This option would allow the county to establish a plan for higher
density growth and development following the completion of General Plan.

Option 1 - Existing approach
Ata minimum, each answer to the following questions must be affirmative for a rezone to be
approved:
A. Is the proposed zone consistent with the surrounding residential density?
B. Does the property have existing or potential roadway access that meets the minimum
county road standards, or municipal standards as applicable?
C. Is the property contiguous or adjacent to a municipality?
D. Is the property within the annexation area of a contiguous or adjacent municipality,
and if so has the municipality considered annexation of the property?

Option 2 - Specific criteria
Ata minimum, each answer to the following questions must be affirmative for a rezone to be
approved:

A. Is the proposed zone consistent with a majority of the surrounding residential density
such that more than 50% of the unincorporated properties with a single family
dwelling within a % mile radius are equal to or less than the density prescribed by the
proposed zone?

Does the proposed zone continue an existing pattern of housing development?

Does the existing road network to the property meet the minimum county road

standards, or municipal standards as applicable?

Is the property within the annexation area of a contiguous or adjacent municipality,

and if so has the municipality considered annexation of the property?

E. Is there an inability (due to a lack of services, etc.) or unwillingness by the contiguous
municipality to annex the property?

Option 3 - Code amendment
Title 17.08 is amended to remove the option to rezone to the RUS and RU2 Zones until the
General Plan is complete as follows:

17.08.030: Purpose of Base Zoning Districts
The following provide the purpose(s) of each of Cache County's established base
zoning districts:
A. Rural 2 Zone (RU2):
1. As of January 1, 2019, the option to rezone property to the RU2 Zone will
expire. Existing property that has previously been rezoned to the RU2 Zone
will continue under the rules of that zone.


2. To allow for residential development in a moderately dense pattern that can

allow for rural subdivisions, and to allow for clustering plans larger than a
single parcel. This type of development should be located and designed to not
unreasonably impede adjacent agricultural uses, nor to unreasonably conflict
with the development standards of adjacent municipalities.

To implement the policies of Cache Countywide Comprehensive Plan,
including those regarding improved roadways, density based residential
standards, clustering, moderate income housing and municipality standards.
This zone must be appropriately served by suitable public roads, have access to
the necessary water and utilities, and have adequate provision of public
services.

B. Rural 5 Zone (RUS):

As of January 1. 2019, the option to rezone property to the RUS Zone will
expire. Existing property that has previously been rezoned to the RUS Zone
will continue under the rules of that zone.

To allow for residential estate development in a low density pattern that can
allow for rural subdivisions and smaller scale agricultural uses. This type of
development should be located and designed to not unreasonably impede
adjacent agricultural uses, nor to unreasonably conflict with the development
standards of adjacent municipalities.

To implement the policies of Cache Countywide Comprehensive Plan,
including those regarding agricultural promotion, prime farmlands, improved
roadways, density based residential standards, clustering, moderate income
housing and municipality standards.

This zone must be appropriately served by suitable public roads, have access to
the necessary water and utilities, and have adequate provision of public
services.
