       C.       Public Hearing: Academy Plaza Zoning Text Amendment
                (Legislative Item)
                On the Request of Joe Johnsen of J Development, Representing Dumar, LLC, a
                Request for Approval of a Zoning Text Amendment to the CSD-DPMU (Draper Pointe
                Mixed Use Commercial Special District) Zone to add two new districts for
                approximately 7.7 acres located at 383 West 13490 South, known as Application No.
                2024-0025-TA.       Staff      Contact:      Todd      Taylor,     801-576-6510,
                todd.taylor@draperutah.gov.

City Planner III, Todd Taylor, presented the Staff Report and presented a Vicinity Map showing the
location of the zone and the subject property, which is north of Bangerter Highway and west of I-15.
The Text Amendment will affect the entire zone. An Aerial Map was displayed showing the
Commercial Special District (“CSD”) Zone as well as the subject property. The Parc West
Apartments are located to the west, the Academy Mortgage Office Building to the east, and Bangerter
Highway to the south. The property is designated CSD on the Land Use Map.

Mr. Taylor summarized the text amendments. The applicant is modifying the purpose to add uses to
be included in the proposed districts. The proposed uses being added to the CSD were referenced.
The applicant is proposing to add the uses to the opening statement of the development standards,
and adding standards. For the Office/Warehouse/Limited Manufacturing District the applicant is
Draper City Planning Commission Meeting
April 24, 2025
Page 5

proposing that all the uses be entirely enclosed within a building, that roll-up doors be transparent
glass on the rear of the buildings. The maximum height of the buildings shall be 30 feet and wall
extensions shall be included to screen the truck docks from the surrounding properties. The walls will
be constructed of materials that match the building and landscaping will be planted along the walls.
Secondary pedestrian entrances shall include architectural features such as overhangs, lighting, and
landscaping.

The proposed development standards for the Vertical Mixed-Use District, which is retail and
restaurant under office, prohibits outside storage and the buildings shall not exceed two stories in
height. A mixed-use design is required to have the uses on top of each other. The buildings will face
13490 South, which is north of the subject property. Pedestrian-friendly design is required and will
feature wider walkways within the project, shaded seating areas, and separated pedestrian paths from
the vehicle areas. For the landscape standards, the applicants are proposing to include a 25-foot
landscape buffer when the Office/Warehouse/Limited Manufacturing District is adjacent to
residential. That includes landscaping requirements for evergreen hedges and trees. The parking
standards generally reflect the existing parking standards.

Mr. Taylor reported that the project area will add a District H that separates it from the existing
Academy Mortgage Office building area. The Land Use Master Plan Exhibit will be amended to
include the districts for H. Sample buildings were provided. Staff did not recommend the proposed
uses of limited manufacturing, general printing, and limited wholesale and warehousing as they do
not provide a beneficial mix of uses as indicated in the Purpose Statement of the zone. They do not
provide a high employment density per square foot, which would support existing neighboring uses
and provide employment for the residential. It also would not support the transit nature of the area.
Staff believes it will increase truck traffic and noted that there is currently a Station Area Plan
underway that could potentially include this area within its boundaries. Staff recommended that if
the Planning Commission recommends approval to the City Council that modifications be included
in the proposed text. It was suggested that the proposed uses of limited manufacturing, general
printing, and limited wholesale and warehousing uses be removed from the Table of Permitted Uses
and that the proposed text and exhibits be modified to not include limited manufacturing in the name
of the new district.

Mr. Taylor reported that an e-mail was sent by the Wasatch Residential Group which owns several
apartments in the area including Parc West to the west of the subject site. They expressed concern
with some of the proposed uses. The email was distributed to the Planning Commission Members in
advance of the meeting.

Chair Adams asked if the applicant was present. Mr. Taylor indicated he did not see the applicant.
Chair Adams then opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing
was closed.

Commissioner Shirey stated that it is challenging to make a decision other than that recommended by
staff since the applicant wasn’t there to weigh in.

       D.      Public Hearing: Janisch Home Occupation Conditional Use Permit Request
               (Administrative Item)
               On the Request of James Janisch a Conditional Use Permit to Operate a Home
               Occupation of a Home Office for an Electrical Contracting Business that includes the
               use of a business vehicle that is stored at the property, on approximately 0.30 acres,
               located at approximately 584 West Sunset Crest Way. Known as Application 2024-
               0373-USE,        Staff      Contact:         Nick      Whittaker,      801-576-6522,
               Nick.Whittaker@draperutah.gov.

Mr. Whittaker presented the Staff Report and displayed Vicinity, Aerial, and Land Use Maps of the
property. He explained that the land use is Residential Medium-Density and the zoning is R-3. The
home occupation is to serve as the office to operate electric contracting work. The applicants also
store a work vehicle at the property. The Site Plan showed where that vehicle is stored when not in
use. A floor plan was displayed showing where the home office is to be located. Mr. Whittaker
identified potential impacts were customers coming to the home, associated traffic, customer and
business vehicles parked on the street, noticeable storing of inventory and supplies in yard areas,
nuisance odors, and noise. The proposed mitigation is that no customers may come to the home, only
one business vehicle is to be allowed and it must be parked in the location shown on the Site Plan. In
addition, all inventory, supplies, or merchandise must be stored in an area of less than 250 square feet
located inside of the dwelling. The business or business operations may not create any nuisance,
odors, or noise. A photo of the property was displayed and the area where the work vehicle is to be
stored was identified.

Chair Adams opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing was
closed.

Chair Adams asked if there was any stipulation about the work van being parked behind the fence
line. He also asked if any homeowner can park whatever vehicle they choose to in the third car
parking area. Mr. Whittaker stated that if the van were a personal vehicle, it could be parked in the
third car parking area. Even with the business use, as long as the Conditional Use Permit (“CUP”) is
obtained, he can park it out front in the location identified. It does not have to be behind the fence
line.

Commissioner Ogden thanked the applicant for coming in and working with the City to get things in
order. It was acknowledged that 250 square feet is not very large. It was suggested that that area
potentially be enlarged so long as the van is not parked in the driveway.

The applicant, James Janisch, reported that he has some shelves against a wall where they keep home
wiring and other similar materials. Normally his son drives the van and picks up the materials needed

swimming pools. He confirmed that 250 square feet is large enough. Commissioner Ogden noted
that the applicant has been running his business for some time and there have been no complaints.

Commissioner Nixon pointed out that one of the conditions is that the employees shall be limited to
those residing in the home on the property. Mr. Janisch indicated that his son does not reside on the
property. He comes over each day and parks his truck either inside the garage or where the van is
normally parked. The potential of changing the conflicting condition was discussed.

“Members of the family of the owner who reside in the dwelling may be employed in the home

the van, and parks his truck where the van was parked or in the garage. He then leaves in the van for
work.
