Findings of Fact  
1. The Staples accessory building is located at 795 East Chalk Creek Road, Summit County, 
Utah, in the Agriculture 5 (AG-5) Zone.  
2. There are three structures currently located on the subject property; a) A single family 
residence constructed in 1990. b) A 528 sq. ft. garage constructed in 2000. c) A 1,824 sq. ft. 
accessory building constructed in 2014.  
3. The applicant has stated that the current accessory building is used for storage of the owner’s 
truck, RV trailer, and UTVs.  
4. The applicant is proposing a 532 sq. ft. addition to an existing 1,824 sq. ft. accessory building 
constructed in 2014.  
5. Accessory buildings over 2,000 sq. ft. are a Conditional Use in the AG-5 zone.  
6. The applicant has stated that the 532 addition would be used for private noncommercial 
storage and family and friends gathering.  
7. No accessory dwelling units are being proposed at this time.  
8. All necessary facilities are available on site.  
9. The applicant is proposing plumbing.  
10. The applicant is proposing electrical.  
11. The building is compatible with the existing neighborhood character.  
12. The proposed barn location is located outside of prime agricultural land area.  
13. The findings analyses in the Staples Accessory Building Conditional Use Permit Staff Report 
are incorporated herein. 
 
Conclusions of Law  
1. There is good cause for this conditional use.

Findings of Fact:  
1. The Promontory Development Agreement was approved on January 2, 2001 and was 
subsequently amended in August 2005 and in June 2017.  
2. The Promontory Development Agreement provides for 885 Estate Lots (lots 1 or more acres in 
size) as part of a base density, 716 Incentive Density lots (lots less than 1 acre in size), 300 
Resort Cabin units (2,500 sq. ft. max. units), equestrian facility, up to five golf club houses, and 
other accessory uses on approximately 6,500 acres with no less than 3,900 acres remaining as 
passive open space.  
3. Of the 1,901 total units in the density pool, over 1,300 have been approved.  
4. The Nicklaus Clubhouse, was originally approved in 2014 and Phase II added in 2016.  
5. The facilities within the Nicklaus Clubhouse are a kitchen with chef’s office, main dining 
room, private dining room and dining patios, locker rooms with restrooms and showers and 
shared attendant station, pro shop, a club repair workstation, a restroom and a break room for the 
golf employees, storage, mechanical rooms, and golf cart storage.  
6. Promontory Investments LLC is the owner of record of parcels SS-52 (605.75 acres) and the 
owner is proposing to add a Conference Center to the Nicklaus Clubhouse.  
7. The proposed Final Site Plan is legally described as Nicklaus Clubhouse Final Site Plan Phase 
3.  
8. The existing disturbance area is 2.75 acres. The conference center addition is 0.39 acres and 
the parking area addition is 1.48 acres. The total disturbance is 4.62 acres.  
9. The development parameters for this project are specifically set forth in the Promontory 
Development Agreement.  
10. The Development Agreement delegates decision making powers to the Board of County 
Commissioners for all Final Site Plans. With the change of government that took place, the 
former Board of County Commissioner’s administrative duties diverted to the County Manager 
and the legislative duties diverted to the County Council.  
11. The Final Site Plan is considered an administrative action.  
12. The Eastern Summit County Planning Commission remains as the recommending body.  
13. On February 1, 2018, the Planning Commission held a public hearing regarding the proposed 
conference center and expressed concern regarding the proposed use and the lack of required 
employee housing being provided by Promontory.  
14. On March 1, 2018, a joint meeting was set up between the County Council, Planning 
Commission, and the developer.  
15. During the March 1, 2018 the County Council directed the developer to come up with a plan 
of action on how the required employee housing would be provided.

16. During the March 1, 2018 meeting, the developer’s representative agreed to a 30-45-day 
deadline to come up with a plan for employee housing and return with current employment 
numbers.  
17. On April 4, 2018, Staff received a letter from the developer’s counsel requesting that the 
Conference Center be brought back for a decision, regardless of the housing discussion.  
18. Section 4.7.12 of the Promontory Development Agreement states; 4.7.12 Employee Housing. 
The Developer shall provide for thirty-seven (37) employee households consisting of 82-
bedroom suites of employee housing. Approval of such employee housing units shall be subject 
to the final site plan approval process in this Agreement.  
19. To date, Promontory has provided only two (2) employee households units of the 37 that are 
required per the Promontory Development Agreement.  
20. Section 4.8.4.1.1 of the Promontory Development Agreement states; 4.8.4.1.1 The Developer 
shall provide for thirty-seven (37) employee households consisting of 82-bedroom suites of 
employee housing sited within the Resort, not outside of the Community gates. The employee 
housing shall be clustered together in such a way as to create a high-quality sense of community. 
This employee housing community shall be integrated into the fabric of the Resort Community 
and shall be located within either the Resort Village, Resort Highlands, Middle Valley or South 
Pointe development areas at a location to be determined by Developer. In all instances, employee 
housing should have easy access (walking, bicycling, and internal resort transit) to Resort 
employment generators and provide for reasonable access to South Summit School District bus 
stops at the Community gates (i.e.; shuttle transit bus). All employee housing shall be approved 
pursuant to the site plan review and approval process herein.  
21. The Promontory Developer as part of an administrative amendment in June of 2017 (Third 
Amendment to the Development Agreement aka South Point Agreement Amendment) stated that 
they intend to move the employee housing to the South Point portion of Promontory.  
22. In 2018 Promontory and South Point entered into a Transfer and Assumption Agreement to 
transfer the employee housing obligation from Promontory to the South Point developers. 
Representatives of South Point have publicly acknowledged that they are responsible for 
providing employee housing as required by the Promontory Development Agreement.  
23. To date, neither Promontory nor South Point have provided a specific plan for employee 
housing.  
24. Section 4.8.4.1.1 of the Promontory Development Agreement allows the Promontory 
Developer to transfer the employee housing obligation within the Promontory to the South Point 
in order to construct the employee housing.  
25. Section 4.8.4.1.1 of the Promontory Development Agreement does not state that the 
employee housing requirement (Section 4.7.12) no longer pertains to the entire Promontory 
Development Agreement.  
26. Under Section 4.8.4.1.1 of the Promontory Development Agreement, the County looks to the 
South Point Developer to construct the employee housing.  
27. Deleting a major public amenity such as employee housing from the Promontory 
Development Agreement would require a Substantial Amendment under Section 6.9.1. and the 
change in responsibility is not a change in the actual requirements pertaining to the Promontory 
Development Agreement.

30. Section 4.8.4.1.2 of the Promontory Development Agreement states; 4.8.4.1.2 All employee 
housing shall be produced by the Developer within a reasonable time schedule, to be approved 
by the County, which correlates to the construction of employment generators.  
31. The Eastern Summit County Planning Commission finds that two (2) units is not 
proportional to the buildout of the Promontory development.  
32. As per Finding 30, the County still retains the enforcement power to withhold Final Site Plan 
approvals under Section 4.8.4.3 of the Promontory Development Agreement.  
33. Section 4.8.4.1.2 of the Promontory Development Agreement requires that employee housing 
be constructed on a “reasonable time schedule” which “correlates to the construction of 
employment generators.”  
34. Section 4.8.4.1.3 of the Promontory Development Agreement provides that the Developer 
must provide a “specific plan” for employee housing which is acceptable to the County.  
35. If the South Point Developer fails to comply with the Promontory Development Agreement, 
subdivision plats and final site plans within the entire Promontory Development Agreement can 
be withheld.  
36. During the February 1, 2018 meeting, the Planning Commission expressed concern regarding 
the proposed use and the lack of required employee housing being provided by Promontory.  
37. During the March 1, 2018 joint meeting where Promontory employee housing was discussed, 
the Planning Commission and the County Council found that over 1,300 units have been 
approved and several club facilities operating, and there was enough employment generation 
where it was within a reasonable time schedule to request that Promontory provide employee 
housing.  
38. The applicant claims that they plan on providing employee housing on the South Point 
portion of the Promontory Specially Planned Area.

39. Section 4.8.4.1.1 of the Promontory Development Agreement states that; In all instances, 
employee housing should have easy access (walking, bicycling, and internal resort transit) to 
Resort employment generators and provide for reasonable access to South Summit School 
District bus stops at the Community gates (i.e.; shuttle transit bus).  
40. As it exists, South Point portion of the Promontory Specially Planned Area does not have any

41. Section 4.8.4.2 of the Promontory Development Agreement states; 4.8.4.2 Monitoring. 
Compliance with the foregoing standards shall be ascertained by the County upon periodic 
review.  
42. Section 4.8.4.3 of the Promontory Development Agreement states; 4.8.4.3 Enforcement. In 
the event the County asserts that Developer has failed to provide or bond for the completion of 
any required employee suites, the County may withhold approval of future Final Subdivision 
Plats and Final Site Plans until such time as these standards are met or the County agrees to an 
amendment to the standards of this Section.  
43. The Planning Commission finds that 17 years have passed and over 1,300 units have been 
approved (~68% of density) and that it is within a reasonable time schedule to request that 
Promontory provide employee housing.  
44. Section 4.4.2 of the Promontory Development Agreement states; 4.4.2 Golf Courses. Club 
and Community Facilities and Related Uses. Up to four (4), appropriately sized and sensitively 
located 18-hole, private golf courses (all golf courses must be environmentally sensitive, and 
minimize the use of pesticides, fertilizers, and irrigation water), equestrian and other Promontory 
golf club facilities including restaurants, retail and commercial uses, private clubs and other 
similar uses; and community gate houses, temporary and permanent sales facilities, maintenance 
facilities and other similar uses related to the operation, maintenance, marketing, and sales of 
Promontory shall be permitted…  
45. A Conference Center is not specifically listed as one of the allowed uses within the 
Promontory Development Agreement.  
46. During the May 17, 2018 Planning Commission meeting, the Developer’s representative 
stated that the Conference Center would function only as an amenity for club 
members/homeowners, and not as an independent commercial venture.  
47. With that limitation, it may be appropriate under the category of commercial and private club 
uses allowed.  
48. Public notice of the public hearing was published in The Summit County News.  
49. Postcard notices announcing the public hearing were mailed to property owners within 1,000 
feet of the subject parcels.  
STAFF SUGGESTS AN ADDITIONAL FINDING: The Nicklaus Golf Clubhouse and proposed 
Conference Center are considered employment generators.  
 
Conclusions of Law:  
1. Conference center development is not allowed under the existing Promontory Development 
Agreement without further clarifications on the use.  
2. The proposal does not meet the terms of the Promontory Development Agreement, because 
the Employee Housing has not been provided within a reasonable time schedule which correlates 
to the construction of employment generators.
