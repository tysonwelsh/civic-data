1. Introduction by Council Member Silvia Catten and Millcreek Department Heads
Council Member Catten started the meeting by asking the audience if they received a flyer on
their door and if they had attended a city meeting before. A few people raised their hands.

She brought up the Millcreek District 1 map which goes to the Jordan River on the west side.
She said it is diverse and can be challenging to represent everyone. The Meadowbrook area has
redevelopment going on and she is looking forward to what is coming in the next couple of
years. The District also contains Sunnyvale Park. The City is searching for funding to get park
lighting and improvements for Sunnyvale. The City is also working on trying to get open space
along 600 E. and 3900 S., along Big Cottonwood Creek, and on Main St. Parks and open spaces
are important to Council Member Catten.

A resident said there are a lot of dogs in his neighborhood and they go to the dog park in South
Salt Lake. He wondered if there were better sidewalks in his neighborhood or off-leash space
near the Jordan River Parkway, if it would help residents with dogs and asked if there would be
any such developments. Council Member Catten asked staff if any of the open space they are
working on obtaining could be used for dogs. Francis Lilly said the space on 600 E. and 3900 S.
could be, or there is space down by the Jordan River that the general plan could designate for
that. Council Member Catten said sidewalks are always on the City’s radar.

Council Meeting (quorum) began at 6:13 p.m.

Council Member Catten said the Promise Program has launched in the last year; it is not fully
running yet but there will be opportunities for people to volunteer to work with people in the
community. The City is also starting a Youth Council. She suggested that business owners go to
the City’s website to get involved in the Millcreek Business Council. She also encouraged
residents to sign up for the e-newsletter.

Council Member Catten said she has heard from residents about street lights and speeding. She
said it is expensive to get street lights up and running. She is aware there are dark areas in her
district and asked residents to send her recommendations on where they should go. A resident
said lights on 300 E. are not bright enough. Council Member Catten said the City could look at
changing the bulbs. She has not heard a lot about traffic control lately. A resident asked for
patrol to come to College Street. Council Member Catten said we may need to educate the
neighbors to slow down. The resident said it is not the neighbors that speed, but the commuters.
Another resident asked if there were any plans to widen 4500 S. from 900 E. to 1300 E. Council
Member Catten said 4500 S. is a UDOT road, so the City does not have control over it. She does
not think widening will happen in the next few years.

Council Member Catten said there is a sidewalk plan and smaller district maps on the tables. She
invited the public to write on the maps. She said the City is working on the general plan, so she
encouraged residents to provide feedback. The resident who brought up dog parks said sidewalks
are more important than dog parks. John Miller said the “Walk Millcreek” plan is on the City’s
website for the public to view.

A resident asked why the manhole covers cannot lie flat. Miller said it is hard to manufacture a
24-36” manhole cover and public works does the best they can. When an overlay is done, they
mill out an inch or two of existing asphalt and they should be able to put them back in closer to
the existing lip. He made the analogy of rolling out cookie dough. The baker tries to make the
batches consistent, and it is the same with asphalt. They try setting it high then rolling it low. The
City will work on inspecting them better.

Mark Mumford, resident, asked about the overlay section for the district. He wants to make sure
neighborhood three does not have too high of buildings, with a max of three stories. Lilly asked
if he was referencing the future land use map. Mumford said yes. Lilly said anything was
possible.

Council Member Catten turned the time over to Department Heads to describe what they do.

Rita Lund, Director of Communication and Programs, said she is a long-time resident of
Millcreek. She said she works with the community councils. She works with community events,
including Venture Out, and if residents would like to have a block party and close their street she
needs to work with them on a special event permit. She said she will work with the Youth
Council and works with the Promise Program. She said she assists with the electronic newsletter
and the printed newsletter. She encouraged the residents to submit stories for the newsletters.
Lund referred to her department as the catch-all department.

Kurt Hansen, Director of City Services, said he directs code enforcement/code compliance,
emergency management, building inspection, the City’s fleet, and works with the County for
animal services and parks. He said the County was reactive to complaints with code
enforcement, but Millcreek’s officers are being a little more proactive. A resident, who is a HOA
president, said there is a lot of parking on 300 E. He said the code says that cars cannot park
there overnight in the winter. He said it thankfully has not been enforced very well because his
development does not have a lot of parking. He said there is a new apartment building going in
across the street and asked if the parking enforcement would change. Hansen said parking
enforcement is through Unified Police Department and they have been lax about it. He said the
City is working towards being able to write their own tickets. Hansen said there have been
changes in parking ordinances to require more per unit with new developments. He
recommended to the resident to look at the parking ordinance to consider upgrading the parking
availability in his development. The resident said a neighbor has put up no parking signs in front
of his property and he asked Hansen if it was enforceable. Hansen said unless the sign refers to
City ordinance, then it is not. Another resident said her neighbor has tall weeds growing into her
lot. She said she has been working with code enforcement since May and nothing has happened.
Hansen said he would follow up with her after the meeting.

Hansen said the building department has 2 inspectors that are out doing inspections. They issue a
lot of stop work orders, which get issued because work is being done without a permit. He said
they do courtesy inspections to help residents. He also said the emergency management is
another one of the areas he manages, and Skyler Stratford is the Emergency Manager.

John Miller, Director of Public Works, said the main goals in public works are to maintain the
largest asset, the roads; get residents to and from their home safely; and to actively pursue
alternative funding. The City did get a grant on 900 E. from 3900 S. to 4500 S. that required a
small match with the federal government to make the road look like it does between 3300 S. to
3900 S. He encouraged residents to submit service requests through the Citizen Service Request
portal on the City’s website. If there is an emergency on the roads, call the 24-hour public works
hotline at 385-468-6101. He said there are 4 engineers in his department and one will go out and
inspect what is submitted through the citizen service request.

A resident asked if 3900 S. is a UDOT road. Miller said it is a city road. The resident said there
is a pothole on State St. He asked the resident to submit a request via the website. Miller asked
how many residents have speeding in their neighborhood. He said to address speeding, the City
uses the 3 E’s; education, enforcement, and engineering. A resident asked how long it would take
for the engineering department to have autonomous vehicles. Miller said autonomous vehicles
are already around nationwide, but he has heard anywhere from 10-25 years for autonomous
cars.

Mike Winder, Director of Economic Development, said he is involved with anything increasing
the tax revenue flowing into the City. He has initiated a Business Council and welcomes owners
of businesses of all sizes to get involved. He said the business license administrator found over
200 businesses that were not submitting their sales tax to Millcreek, so she was able to reroute
$70,000 back to the City. He said the state changed the name of Redevelopment Agencies to
Community Reinvestment Agencies (CRA), which he assists in Millcreek. Winder said he
focuses on the three R’s; retention, recruitment, and redevelopment. He said the CRA and staff
are working hard on the proposed city center between Richmond, 1300 E. and Highland Dr. He
said in District | there are several businesses along the Jordan River that the City is looking at to
help. A resident asked if businesses in the old strip malls wanted to retrofit it, is he the one they
would work with. Winder said he is great person to start with. Mumford complimented Winder
on the business survey that went out today to the Business Council. A resident asked about the
old K-Mart property on 900 E. Winder said that property is in the City of Murray.

Francis Lilly, Director of Community Development, said he works on planning functions with
three planners, the general plan, assists the Planning Commission, oversees the SL County
addressing and surveying contract, and oversees the Promise Program. The Promise Program
aligns educators, health care providers, after school service providers, United Way, and the City
on a common set of goals. It will have the players targeted at interventions for the most critical
schools. The interventions are based on 3 promises: strengthen and support local schools to
maximize academic success and envision all Millcreek youth as college graduates; work together
to create a safer, healthier community through resident engagement, support of local law
enforcement, fitness and nutrition programs, and health organization partnerships; and economic
wellbeing, to work to improve income potential for all Millcreek residents who are under or un-
employed, or that have had life experiences that have denied them access to education and
vocational training that will result in higher wages. He is also working on the Community
Development Block Grant program to look at funding for Sunnyvale Park improvements.

Lilly said the City is working on a new general plan adoption and showed the audience the future
land use map. He said the public comment period for the general plan is going on until about
August 25", Residents can review and comment on it at the website, millcreekgeneralplan.com.
He said one of the goals of the general plan is to protect the integrity of the single-family
neighborhoods while acknowledging that the City is growing. The plan would support the
stability of the neighborhoods while allowing for the appropriate redevelopment of the corridors
and creation of town centers. He said one of the town centers is the Meadowbrook area, around
Main St. and 3900 S., and one where City Hall is right now. The Meadowbrook area will see a
40,000 square foot climbing gym and a lot of nearby retail space in the next year.

Jeff Silvestrini, Mayor, said he is a member of the City Council but represents the City in other
capacities. He plans the City Council’s agendas and monitors the contact page traffic on the City
website. He partners with his wife who voluntarily puts together the City’s weekly e-newsletter.
He said in the newsletter, there are links to documents being discussed at the City’s meetings. He
is the Chair of Unified Police Department Board, on the Unified Fire Authority Board, the Chair
of the Fire Service Area which is the taxing district that pays for fire, Commissioner on the
Wasatch Front Regional Council which controls road monies that come from the federal
government, Vice Chair on County Council of Governments which is an entity for the County
distribution of road funds and County initiatives, he is going to receive a seat on the Central
Wasatch Commission which plans the future for the canyons, was appointed to the Federal

Funds Commission which is a committee of legislators that looks at how Utah is going to fund
programs in the future, and he is active in the Utah League of Cities and Towns which is a

Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek did not raise taxes, the property tax is a transfer from
SLVLESA to Millcreek to pay for law enforcement services. He said the City did that to have

Council Member Catten thanked the Millcreek Community council members in attendance. She
said she was on the Millcreek Community Council for several years before she was on the City
Council. She encouraged others to get involved at the community council level. She thanked the
audience for attending the meeting.

The meeting adjourned at 7:19 p.m.

DISTRICT 2 MEETING — 7:30 p.m.
TIME COMMENCED: 7:31 p.m.

1. Introduction by Council Member Dwight Marchant and Millcreek Department Heads
Council Member Marchant started the meeting and highlighted the areas of District 2 on a map.
He said he has lived in District 2 for 45 years. He was interested in running for office to help
maintain the quality of neighborhoods and maintain fiscal strength. He said he has been
appointed by the Council to be the City Treasurer. He said the City Manager has hired all the
new City staff and he is proud of what they have done.

Council Member Marchant said Millcreek Promise is a program to help disadvantaged and the
underserved in the community to help them succeed in life. He said Millcreek, as a city, has the
responsibility to watch out for one another. Millcreek has modeled this program on what South
Salt Lake City has done. The Millcreek Business Council will be meeting at City Hall monthly,
so if residents are interested, they can get involved. He said there is an electronic newsletter that
goes out every Friday that the Mayor’s wife voluntarily puts together free gratis. He said
speaking of free gratis, the City Hall space is being rented at $8 a square foot, the Council and
City Attorney did not accept a salary until the City could afford it, and the vehicle fleet was paid
for with cash. The City is creating a general plan and it only works if the residents get involved.
He said the City Hall Annex is in a town center which could create space for community
involvement and create a tax base. Maliheh Free Clinic will be transferring to Millcreek and
there will be a ribbon cutting on September 12". He promoted Venture Out summer events
which is run by volunteers.

Council Member Marchant said Millcreek has taken over funding of police, which costs
$825,000 a month. He said when the property tax comes through in December, the City can start
paying for that service contract with those funds. He said the best way to improve roads is to get
matching funds with other entities. He said the City is trying to set good priorities. All the
Council Members sit on other boards. He asked a rhetorical question of, “How does a city get
their money?” He said the City is largely run by sales tax. Millcreek also gets a lot of fees for
building permits and such; this week the City received $36,000 in fees. Another option is room

tax. He said if Millcreek could find the right place for hotels, it should be looked into. The City
also gets franchise tax through Comcast Cable and property tax. He said Millcreek has never
charged a property tax until recently because Millcreek dropped out of the Salt Lake Valley Law

Millcreek Department Heads then introduced themselves and explained their duties as was
communicated in the District 1 meeting.

A resident asked Kurt Hansen if there was a citywide communication plan for emergencies.
Hansen said the City is meeting with a person on short wave radios this week and a plan is a
work-in-progress. Mayor Silvestrini said the City is tied into the County’s emergency
notification system and utilizes social media.

A resident asked John Miller what the Public Work’s involvement is with the water system.
Miller said the City’s water is provided by Salt Lake City Public Utilities, Holladay Water and
Jordan Valley. Miller said they coordinate their breaks because it requires cutting into
Millcreek’s roads. The resident asked if it would be possible for Millcreek to have their own
water utilities. Mayor Silvestrini said he has been trying to get appointed to the Salt Lake City
Public Utilities Advisory Commission. He thinks the residents of Millcreek deserve a real vote
on water rates. He has been appointed by Mayor Biskupski to the Commission but is waiting for
the Salt Lake City Council to approve it. He said it is conceivable for the City to take on their
own water systemwide, but it would be complicated to buy it and take it over from Salt Lake
City. Millcreek would have to sort that out and figure out a source of water. The resident asked
about the replacement rate on infrastructure in Millcreek. Mayor Silvestrini said he is looking at
if Millcreek is getting its fair share of maintenance.

A resident said he lives on Scott Ave. and they have had a problem with speeders and high traffic
volume. He said Google maps routes commuters through their residential street because it is the
shortest route between 700 E. and 900 E. He said he also likes the road rough to slow traffic
down. Miller said the City is studying it and he can provide past data to the resident.

Mayor Silvestrini said he is a part time Mayor that works fulltime for Millcreek. The form of
government is a weak Mayor - Council form of government. In that form of government, the
Mayor Chair’s the City Council and has a vote at the City Council. He said the City Manager is
the silent hand, John Geilmann, and he hired all the department heads who run the day-to-day
operations of the City. He told the audience that he is accessible and is in his office at City Hall
every day. He then described the duties he mentioned in the District 1 meeting. He said he
worked on a fireworks bill this year that has been successful in cutting down the number of fires
this season by shortening the number of lawful discharge days and increasing the penalty for

sixteen that happened last year.

Mayor Silvestrini said the Council wants to know what the residents’ priorities are and invited

Council Member Marchant thanked the audience for attending the meeting and encouraged them
to sign up for the City’s newsletter.
