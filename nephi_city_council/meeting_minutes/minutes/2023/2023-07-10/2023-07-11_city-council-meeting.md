ORDINANCE 07-11-2023 TITLE 10 REVISIONS ADOPTED:

 City Administrator Seth Atkinson explained to the council that the Planning Commission has been
working for the past several months on amendments to the Title 10 Land Use Code including:

    ●   Some use changes in select zones to create consistency in terminology between zones.
    ●   Setting minimum lot sizes in all zones, including commercial and industrial.
    ●   Changing minimum lot sizes in select zones.
    ●   Setting minimum frontage requirements in all zones, including commercial and industrial.
    ●   Changing minimum setbacks in select zones for main buildings, and requiring setbacks in all
        zones, including industrial and commercial zones.
    ●   Changing minimum setbacks in select zones for accessory buildings, and requiring setbacks in all
        zones, including industrial and commercial zones.
    ●   Changing the code to have a set maximum height regulation in each zone, not subject to
        planning commission’s recommended approval to city council.
    ●   Adding a new one-half acre zone that allows for animal rights.
    ●   Recommending deletion of the zoning code related to “Planned Residential Developments”, as it
        is in conflict with our “Planned Unit Development” code.


  Mr Atkinson added that the Affordable Housing Task Force made some recommendations to the city.
One of those recommendations was to maintain the 8 units/acre density for the R-3 Zone. This
ordinance includes the recommendation from the Affordable Housing Task Force and also includes a
request from the Planning Commission to lower the height allowance in the R-3 zone to 35’. The
ordinance also removes the Planned Unit Residential (PRD) overlay from the code.

  Additional considerations were given to the R-3 zone for developments that develop vertically. When
the development is required to provide a certain amount of acreage/unit, a vertical development can
leave extensive open space which often turns into a weed patch. Also, acquiring the needed land for the
development can often lead to odd-shaped parcels or “orphan” parcels that help to fulfill the land
requirement for a development but may serve no other practical purpose. The new ordinance will allow
for a discount on land requirements for units that are developed vertically (e.g. on a 1-acre parcel that
allows for 8 units, if 4 of the units are developed vertically, then these 4 units would have a discounted
land requirement. The ground level unit land requirements would stay the same.
   City Planner Shauna Talbot presented an updated zoning map. The council expressed their
appreciation for the new updated map.
   Councilor Worwood moved to adopt Ordinance 07-11-2023 -revisions to Title 10 along with the
summary table. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

assistance for the city’s electrical equipment, including substations. This was needed as the crew hasn’t
had the time nor the expertise to work in some areas on the city’s equipment. Due to the size of Provo
and Spanish Fork, they did not need the same level of technical assistance as some of the other member

DICKERSON AIRPORT AGREEMENT AMENDMENT APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson explained to the council that in January of this year, the Council approved a lease
agreement for a hangar at the airport with Dustin Dickerson. He had mostly constructed his hangar and
is trying to bring power to the building. He had been unable to get Rocky Mountain Power to
expeditiously design and approve a connection to his hangar. He would like to forgo a connection to the
power grid and instead power his hangar through the use of solar panels and a battery. Rust Finlinson,
the Nephi City Electric Superintendent, does not have concerns over a hangar using solar to power their
building. Mr. Dickerson has also filed the appropriate paperwork with the FAA as well.
Mr. Atkinson presented an amendment to the original lease agreement allowing solar panels to be
installed on the hangar and stated that the city attorney had also reviewed the amendment.
    Councilor Worwood moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the agreement amendment with Dustin
Dickerson. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

UDOT RIGHT-OF-WAY, BETTERMENT, AND REIMBURSEMENT AGREEMENTS APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson explained to the council that the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) is engaging
in a road widening project up Nephi Canyon along Highway 132. As part of the project, they want to
move a water line owned by Nephi City. The water line is older and has had problems with leaks in the
past. If the new road alignment was over the old water line, any leaks could compromise the integrity of
the new road. Because of this, UDOT has elected to replace portions of the old water line with a new
water line for the length of the project. The water department has the replacement of this water line,
coming from Bradley Springs, as part of their long-term capital projects list. The UDOT project has
expedited this water line replacement.
   UDOT has broken the road widening into two different projects and as part of one of these projects,
Nephi City would be required to pay $238,335 as part of a Betterment Agreement. UDOT would fund
approximately $1,948,069.75 of the water line replacement. UDOT would also like to compensate
Nephi City for land acquisition to accommodate the road widening in the amount of $20,475.
   Mr. Atkinson stated that although this is an inopportune time to pay for another water project, given
the expenses associated with the Firehouse and Worwood wells, most of the expense will be covered by
UDOT for a project the city would want to complete anyway.
   Councilor Worwood moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the agreements presented with UDOT
concerning the waterline project up the canyon. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion
passed unanimously.

WALL CONSULTANT GROUP APPROVED FOR ENGINEERING SERVICES FOR THE CRIMSON FLAT
DEVELOPMENT:

   Mr. Atkinson updated the council that the city put out a request for qualifications for engineering
services for the public/private partnership commercial subdivision, Crimson Flats. Several engineering
firms responded and the selection committee recommended WCG or Wall Consultant Group as the firm
to complete the engineering on the project.
   WCG is based out of Salt Lake City and has engineers from all over the state. One of the engineers on
their team has worked with the city on numerous transportation projects and was recently part of the
team that put together the city’s interchange transportation study. Due to the proximity of this
development to the south interchange, it was felt that this experience would be helpful. In addition,
WCG has been involved in a number of projects involving storm drainage, which will also be a large
component of the Crimson Flats development. WCG has also been involved with civil engineering for
several commercial developments. An experienced planner was also part of the team which will help
bring ideas for a commercial subdivision from other areas of the state.
   Councilor Ostler moved to approve WCG for engineering services for the Crimson Flat development.
Councilor Parady seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.
