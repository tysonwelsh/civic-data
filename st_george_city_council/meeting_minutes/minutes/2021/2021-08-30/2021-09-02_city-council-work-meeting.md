OPENING:
     Mayor Randall called the meeting to order and welcomed all in attendance. The
     invocation was offered by Reverend Alex Wilkie and The Pledge of Allegiance to the
     Flag was led by Councilmember McArthur.

DISCUSSION WITH THE WASHINGTON COUNTY WATER CONSERVANCY DISTRICT
REGARDING WATER CONSERVATION AND NEW DEVELOPMENT:
     Zach Renstrom and Karry Rathje with the Washington County Water Conservancy
     District provided a PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics: Water
     supply; Population growth; Water Conservation; District recommendations; Water
     efficiency standards; Residential standards; Residential standards: traditional;
     Residential standards: traditional cost; Residential standards: traditional concept;
     Examples; Large lot examples; Residential standards: certified; Residential
     standards: certified concept; Examples requiring certification; and Commercial,
     institutional, and industrial standards.

       During the presentation, the Council also discussed that City code does not require
       front landscaping to be complete prior to Certificate of Occupancy (CO); state law
       dictates when a CO can be held up.

       Councilmember Larkin noted that in a meeting she attended yesterday it was said
       that an accessory dwelling could be metered separately. She asked staff is that was
       correct. Staff indicated they would have to look into that.

       After being asked by Councilmember Hughes, Mr. Renstrom noted the alternative to
       what is being proposed is to limit building as there is only so much water.

       Councilmember Larkin commented there could be a big economic hit if building
       stops.

       Councilmember Hughes commented this proposal is going to change the ability to
       choose landscaping so the County can grow more. He believes using water is not
       necessarily wasting water. Although he wants to be careful in taking away people’s
       ability to have the yard they want, he understands there is only so much water.

      Councilmember Larkin noted that long term, having a large lawn can shut down the
      City 30 years from now.

      Mr. Renstrom likes the proposal since this will not take away water from people as
      they are not using water now as this is for new development. When purchasing a
      large lot, people need to look at other amenities instead of grass. After being asked
      by Water Services Director Scott Taylor, Mr. Renstrom explained that someone
      cannot pay two impact fees if they buy a big lot and install grass.

      Councilmember McArthur asked if that could that be an option. Possibly base impact
      fees on each ¼ acre or ½ acre.

      Mr. Renstrom explained a 5,000 sq. ft. lot uses less water than a 10,000 sq. ft. lot; it
      is not double. If a homeowner wanted to have grass on their entire yard, impact
      fees can be very expensive and then there is still the concern with high water use.

      Energy and Water Conservation Specialist Rene Fleming asked what happens if she
      sells her home that has desert landscaping and the new buyer rips it all out and puts
      in all grass. She asked if there was a mechanism in place to catch that.

      Mr. Renstrom explained the ordinance would only affect homes built after a specific
      date; therefore, they would be in violation of the ordinance.

      Councilmember Hughes noted he believes some people will be okay with the
      proposal as most people can see the value of water.

      Mr. Renstrom shared Santa Clara’s concerns with the fill in lots they have in parts of
      their city.

      Mayor Randall commented that the County will need to go with these restrictions or
      builders won’t be able to build like they are.

      Mr. Renstrom explained water is secured for 50 years with the Lake Powell Pipeline;
      however, using water wisely is always better. The state of Utah is implementing a
      turf buyback program; he believes there is a high probability there will be legislation
      this year. After the District finishes meeting with cities, they will present a proposed
      ordinance the cities can consider adopting. The biggest question being what
      happens if only half of the cities adopt the ordinance – at this point he does not have
      an answer.

      Mayor Randall commented all cities need to be on the same page.

      Water Services Director Scott Taylor commented at some point Mr. Renstrom will
      provide a number of units that can be built before there is no more water based
      upon source size standard. Right now, that standard is .89-acre feet per household,
      per ERU. When Mr. Renstrom comes back with a number, it will be based on a
      standard the City is not at. He would like to see two numbers – how much water can
      be used if water is being used as it is now, based on the current standard as well as
