In response to Council Member Buroker regarding Resolution No. 20-32, Interim City Manager
Ryan Carter explained that in the year 2011, South Jordan identified a limited regional area
within its municipal territory that did not have access to secondary water for irrigation purposes.
To remedy the problem, he said that South Jordan and Riverton entered into an Interlocal
Agreement whereby Riverton would supply secondary water to South Jordan, in a sufficient
amount to supply the neighborhood, through a metered connection at the municipal border
shared by Riverton and South Jordan. South Jordan, in turn, installed a system that delivered
water to its residents in the neighborhood.

Mr. Carter said that the agreement established the terms and conditions by which Riverton City
would allow South Jordan City to install at its sole cost and expense, supply water
interconnection facilities consisting of a meter station and distribution lines connecting to
Riverton’s existing distribution line located at approximately 11600 South 1300 West. He said
the Interlocal Agreement that established the relationship between South Jordan and Riverton
had expired and needed to be renewed.

     16.a        Ordinance No. 20-10 - Rezoning properties located near 4185 West 12600
                 South from PCC (Planned Commercial Center) to C-R (Commercial Regional),
                 John Linton Representing Holiday Oil, Applicant

Development Services Direct Jason Lethbridge explained that the public hearing was for a
Rezone of approximately two-acres located near 4185 West 12600 South. He said the property
was located at the southwest corner of 12600 South and the new 4150 west street, which was
currently under construction. He said the property was currently zoned Planned Commercial
Center (PCC) and the current General Plan designation was Regional Commercial/Office.
Nearby zoning includes PCC to the south and east, RM-14 SD to the west and C-R to the north.
He said the current PCC Zone allowed a large variety of commercial uses, including
convenience stores with gas stations. He said the requested Commercial Regional (C-R) zone
also allowed for convenience stores with gas stations. He said the two zones were very similar
in architectural and site plan standards, and both zones would require a fence to be constructed
along the shared line with the housing development to the west.

Mr. Lethbridge explained that the key difference between the two zones was the minimum lot
size for a standalone site. He said the PCC Zone required 5-acres, and the C-R zone required
2-acres. A site less than the minimum lot size could be developed in either zone if it were part of
a larger master site plan, in this instance Holiday Oil would like to begin development sooner
than the remaining property. There is also a significantly larger setback requirement in the PCC
Zone, as that zone contemplated much larger scale buildings and development. The setback
itself in the PCC was set at 40 feet, which was the same as the Commercial Regional Zone, but
then in a separate section of the PCC ordinance, Section 18.100.070(3), indicates that “No
Page | 6 Minutes – City Council Meeting
April 21, 2020



buildings shall be allowed within 100 feet of a non-compatible zone”. He further explained that,
while the intent of the proposed rezone was not to circumvent any setback requirements, the
additional 60 foot separation requirement was, in staff’s perspective, excessive for the subject
parcel. He said the Commercial Regional Zone was the more appropriate development standard
for a commercial parcel of this size, and for development such as the proposed Holiday Oil.

Mr. Lethbridge said the on March 12, 2020 the Planning Commission held a public hearing on
the rezone application and the Planning Commission voted to recommend approval of the
application.

their concerns.

     16.b        Ordinance No. 20-11 - Amending Sections 18.70.070, 18.85.070, and 18.100.070
                 of the Riverton City Code, Addressing Sidewalk and Frontage Improvements in
                 Certain Commercial Zones, Amendments Proposed by Riverton City

Development Services Director Jason Lethbridge explained that the public hearing was for a
change in the Zoning Text, which was proposed by Riverton City Staff. He explained that the
proposed change was to several similar locations in the code, and was intended to rectify
inconsistencies between the Code and the adopted City Standards and Specifications.

Mr. Lethbridge said that in February of 2018, Riverton City adopted an update to its “Standard

Standard Plans showed road sections with optional meandering sidewalks. Meanwhile, he said
that several sections of Title 18 of the Riverton City Code still called for meandering sidewalks
along the public street frontage of several zones. Mr. Lethbridge said that the proposed Zone
Text Change amended the sections of Title 18 to match the Standards and Specifications.

Mr. Lethbridge said that on March 12, 2020 the Planning Commission held a public hearing to
consider the proposed text amendment and they voted to recommend approval of the
amendment.

Discussion was held and Council Member Stewart spoke of meandering vs. straight sidewalk in
the proposed text amendment, specifically 18.100.070. He said that the Council deliberately
included “meandering” in a PCC Zone. He then recommended that section be excluded from the
proposed text amendment.

     17.a        Resolution No. 20-30 – Approve Negotiations of a Contract with WeLink
                 Communications to Design and Construct a City Owned Network

Mayor Staggs explained that the Riverton City Council adopted a Strategic Plan that features a
set of priorities, with objectives and goals, to improve municipal services from the years 2018 to
2022. He said that by connecting City assets, the City would pay less for telecommunications
services, get faster speeds, and be able to build innovative new methods of delivering services
to residents. He said that he, Council Member Stewart, Ryan Carter and other staff members
met as a committee to review four RFP respondents and WeLink was their chosen vendor.

Interim City Manager Ryan Carter explained that the City Council included $500,000 in the FY
2019-2020 Budget for fiber connectivity of City assets. With that budget item, Council required
staff to bring any contract back for final approval before proceeding. He said the project would
span into the following fiscal year's budget; therefore, upon approval, the Council would need to
include an appropriate amount budgeted into next year’s budget.

Craig Calvert, Operational Director, explained that staff solicited proposals to assist in the
design of a telecommunications network which directly connects critical City-owned
infrastructure. An evaluation committee, consisting of elected officials and employees, have
reviewed responses issued under the RFP and recommended an award of the RFP to WeLink
Communications. He said the construction cost for the city owned network was a not to exceed
cost of $200,000; however, Council Member Stewart requested that a rooftop lattice tower site
be added to the High Tank and also increase the number of 10GB wireless radios to the
network to add redundancy. He also said that $300,000 was not a firm price for the added
feature.

Lengthy discussion was held among the Council Members regarding WeLink and wireless
technology. Council Members expressed their concerns regarding residents who have concerns

research and education regarding firm costs, manpower, return on investment, life of product
and other unknowns. They also requested that a WeLink representative be present at the next
City Council Meeting to address their questions.

     17.b        Resolution No. 20-31 - To Initiate the Process by which the Riverton City
                 Council may Consider Creation of the Riverton Fire Service Area to Fund First
                 Responder Services within Riverton City

Interim City Manager Ryan Carter explained that Riverton City has initiated the process to
withdraw from the Unified Fire Service Area, which historically has served as the means by
which Riverton Residents have paid for First Responder Services provided by the Unified Fire
Authority. He said that, while withdrawal remains pending, the time was right to have a
discussion about whether the Council desires to create a new service area, to serve as a
substitute entity that would levy property taxes to pay for First Responder Services. Assuming
the Council desires to proceed, Staff provided a brief summary of steps that Riverton City must
take to create a new service area, which staff has informally referred to as the Riverton Fire
Service Area.

Mr. Carter said the proposed resolution would be brought before the Council in the meeting on
May 5, 2020. If the resolution is adopted by the Riverton City Council, the following future steps
would occur:

     •    Riverton City will post a notice of a public hearing, once a week for 4 consecutive weeks,
          in a newspaper of general circulation in Riverton City and publish notice on the Utah
          Public Notice Website of a public hearing which will occur on the date of June 16, 2020.
          [See: Utah Code Ann. §17B-1-211]
     •    Riverton City will conduct a public hearing on the date of June 16, 2020, to receive
          public input on: whether the requested service is needed in the area of the proposed
          local district; whether the service should be provided by the county of municipality of the
          proposed local district; and all other matters relating to the proposed service area. [See:
          Utah Code Ann. §17B-1-211]
     •    At the conclusion of the public hearing, Riverton City will consider whether to adopt a
          resolution to approve creation of the Riverton Fire Service Area. [See: Utah Code Ann.
          §17B-1-212].

Council Member Stewart explained that if the above process was not approved, the funding of
the Fire Service Area would be through the City’s General Fund. He said he recommended the
above described process for creating a Riverton Fire Service Area in order to avoid co-mingling
Fire Service Area Funds with the City’s General Fund.

     17.d        COVID-19 Testing

Ryan Carter, Interim City Manager, explained that the City had been taking precautions to
protect employees against COVID-19 but consideration had been given to the expansion of a
process for increased and improved testing for City employees and possibly strategic employers
within the City. He explained that, due to emergency circumstances created by the pandemic,
partnering with other cities for a testing program would require a budget amendment of
approximately $80,000.

Discussion was held regarding the need for the City to provide COVID-19 testing. Mr. Carter
said the program was intended as a pro-active, preventative measure for City employees.
Discussion was then held regarding potential program funding and/or reimbursement options.
The Council agreed to proceed but were hesitant with the City providing what seemed like
health care services to the public. Personal Protective Equipment was discussed and
suggestions given as to how the City could assist local businesses in acquiring some equipment
in order for them to return safely to work.
