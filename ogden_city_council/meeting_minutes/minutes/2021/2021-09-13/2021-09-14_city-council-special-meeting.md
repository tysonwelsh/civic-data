Joint proclamation declaring September 15 through October 15 as
"Hispanic Heritage Month" in Ogden City
         A joint proclamation declaring September 15 through October 15,2021, as "Hispanic Heritage Month" in Ogden City
came before the Council for consideration. Council member Lopez read the Spanish version of the joint proclamation and
Council member Choberka read the English version, after which COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA MOVED TO
APPROVE THE JOINT PROCLAMATION. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER HYER, ALL
VOTING AYE. Council member Nadolski was not present when this vote was taken.
         Chair Blair then presented the proclamation to Dr. Enrique Romo from Weber State University's (WSU's) Office of
Access and Diversity. Dr. Romo thanked the Mayor and Council for this recognition; it means everything to have the Ogden City
Council and Mayor recognize the work being done by student affairs at WSU. The Office of Access and Diversity is heavily
involved in the success of students and this acknowledgment is an acknowledgement of the success of the students. He reported
WSU is working to become an emerging Hispanic serving institution and this recognition aligns with those future goals. He looks
forward to meeting with the Mayor and Council in person to continue the discussion of the work being done by WSU and noted he
appreciates the City's partnership and support.
Public Comments
          Alene Evans stated she is a homeowner in Ogden, and she is here to address the Council regarding property at 550 East
900 North where a church building is being demolished and the property is being redeveloped. A developer has submitted a
request to change the current single family housing zoning to one that would allow multi-family dwellings and increased density.
Ogden City is currently over 50 percent rental properties, and 900 North is the access to a high-density mobile home park; the
traffic onto Washington Boulevard at 900 North is extremely congested, hazardous, and deadly. Rental complexes do not increase
neighborhood home values, nor do they create pride of ownership; transitional housing does not build stability in a neighborhood.
Crime rates are higher at rental prope1ties and the fees for law enforcement and fire personnel are disproportionate and
concerning. Absentee landlords are not invested in the maintenance and well-being of rental properties. Ogden City has recently
experienced a dramatic increase in apartment complexes and high-density housing, and this is a great concern for single family
homeowners. The Plarming Commission and City Council should not feel obligated to change the zoning regulations for
developers in order for them to make more money at the expense of current homeowners that have invested in their
neighborhoods. The area could use nice, affordable homes but not apartment complexes. Ogden City no longer has long range
planning directives for the Planning Commission and City Council to consider when making this type of decision; in the past, the
Planning staff and Planning Commission would have had plans to rely upon. Building codes should impose specific standards to
ensure aesthetically pleasing and safe high-density developments, different than the type ofproject that was recently built on Wall

                  Council member Hyer noted that Ms. Evans was an Ogden City employee for many years until her recent retirement.
                  Judy Lewis stated she is speaking about the same property as Ms. Evans; she has reached out to the entire Council to
        voice her concerns about the proposed zone change and development of the subject property and she heard back from just two

                  Russell James also spoke to the development of the property at 550 East 900 North. He is concerned about building
        another high-density project so close to elementary schools in the area.
                  John Ray also discussed the property at 550 East 900 North; he discussed dangerous traffic issues in the area and noted
        the proposed project will make conditions more hazardous.
                  Don Hughes also discussed the property at 550 East 900 North; he invited the City Council to personally visit the area to
        observe present conditions. Those that live in single family homes have tried hard to make the area a nice place to live and
        allowing this apartment complex will negatively impact those efforts. He acknowledged that the decision the Council is being
        asked to make is a very hard one and he asked them to seriously consider the proposal and the input provided by the residents.
                  Angel Castillo stated she is happy that people are attending City Council meetings and making their voices heard about
        issues that are concerning to them. However, she wished to offer information about the economic and fiscal impact of multi-
        family housing from the Harvard Center of Housing. She stated those that have spoken before her made claims of negative
        impacts ofhigh-density housing, but studies have shown that police activity is no higher in a high-density project than in a single-
        family subdivision and, in many cases, it is lower than in single family areas. The Harvard report also goes on to explain how
        renters are invested in their communities as a homeowner; they are twice as likely to socialize with their neighbors as homeowners
        and equally as likely to take part in structured social groups, have an interest in politics, identify closely with their town or city,
        and are only slightly less likely to attend religious services. In conclusion, the study undermines the notion that apartment
        residents somehow do not care about or do not involve themselves in the community in which they live. She wants to make sure
        that the Council is considering all points of view and data. Most important is the type of housing that is built on the subject
        property; the median home price in Ogden has increased by 33 percent in recent months and the City needs to adjust zoning
        standards to allow for smaller units for purchase and develop a plan to filter high density housing throughout the entire City.
        Ogden needs to be reactive rather than proactive in providing different housing options, both for rent and purchase.

        Council member Comments
                  Council member Stephens thanked those who addressed the Council this evening; when considering zoning petitions, he
        tries to detennine if the proposed project will strengthen or stabilize a neighborhood and benefit the area in which it will be
        located. He then stated he has received several emails about any policies or mandates from the City regarding mask wearing in
        City facilities or even local schools. He asked Mr. Johnson to address that issue. Mr. Johnson stated the only thing Ogden City can
        regulate is mask wearing in City buildings, but Administration has chosen not to mandate masks at this point. Those who are not
        vaccinated against COVID-19 are encouraged to wear masks when in City facilities. The City has no control over local school
        policies; the Utah Legislature passed a law earlier this year that prohibits any govermnent entity from issuing a mask mandate for
        schools, with the exception of the Health Department upon declaration of a health emergency mandate. This would give a local
        County Commission the authority to determine whether to impose a mask mandate in line with such a declaration.
                  Council member Hyer stated he also appreciates the input from those who spoke during the public comment period and

        Castillo provided study data regarding high density housing, it is possible to fmd a study that supports nearly any position. The
        residents who live in that area are ve1y aware of the present conditions and their input is relevant. He hopes the Council will listen
        closely to their concerns and the information they relay regarding the flavor of that area of the community.
