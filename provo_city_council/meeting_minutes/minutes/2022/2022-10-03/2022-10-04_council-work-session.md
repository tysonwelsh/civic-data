Mills explained they worked with the Division of Wildlife Resources, who are also involved in
coordinating between the different agencies to address any impacts that may develop. Mr.
Fillmore also asked about potential mosquito abatement. Mr. McCulley said that mosquitos are

5.      A discussion regarding a proposed Timpanogos Golf Club Transfer (22-074)
        (0:29:36)

Scott Henderson, Parks and Recreation Director, presented. He provided some background
information on this request and on the operations of the golf course during the pandemic. During
this time, the Parks and Recreation was also working toward national accreditation. Mr.
Henderson reviewed some of the challenges at the golf course, including construction to
accommodate a private development. He highlighted additional benefits of the golf course.

Brett Watson, Golf Course Manager, presented on the current operations at the golf course. The
golf course is currently self-sustaining, with over 100,000 rounds played in the last year. They
are on pace to reach $1.8 million in revenues this year. Next year will be the 100th anniversary of
golf in Provo. Sustainability is a focus at the golf course with native plant areas and water reuse
that are important to the golf course operations.

John Borget, Administrative Services Director, explained the operating transfer, which relates to
operations in the year 2020. The City’s auditor had recommended that the City transfer funds to
the golf course to mitigate the revenue impacts of construction in 2020. The Administration
reviewed some options—there is some funding in the Legacy CIP which has not been spent and
could be an appropriate use for this, as the revenue loss is directly related to the pandemic.

Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked what other funding mechanisms could be used; Mr. Borget
said the other option would be to utilize general fund balance. Councilor George Handley asked
with the revenues are going up as quickly as they are, whether there was a way to cover the gap
with golf course revenues. Mr. Borget explained that the revenues were important for continued
investment in the golf course; due to the investment that is being made there, funding that 2020
shortage would negatively impact their budget. Mr. Handley said his biggest reservation was that
there are other important uses for Legacy CIP funds; he was concerned with whether this funding
could be used towards the completion of the Rock Canyon Master Plan and roundabout at 1450
East. He is hesitant to commit the funds for the golf course transfer until they have figured out

7.      A presentation regarding towing ordinances (22-076) (1:57:48)

Brian Jones, City Attorney, and Mark Eyo, Council Intern, presented. Mr. Eyo researched about
towing of delivery vehicles; he outlined the results of his research including the differences in
the code for controlled versus uncontrolled lots. Some cities designate a specific grace or waiting
period of 15 or 30 minutes before a tow can happen.

Councilor Rachel Whipple had thought about having properties designate a location for short-
term use such as delivery drivers. Brian Jones, City Attorney, noted that Provo does not have a
distinction between controlled and uncontrolled lots. He indicated that one of the existing
requirements for properties that do patrol tows, is that those areas have designated visitor
parking. Councilor David Shipley asked if the current code had a grace period; Mr. Jones
explained that with the way city code is written and fees are charged, towing companies are
essentially incentivized to connect vehicles to a tow truck as quickly as possible. Mr. Shipley
noted there are some issues with Ms. Whipple’s proposal; he wasn’t sure if it was realistic and
there are some areas of the city that do not permit on-street parking at all. Mr. Shipley liked the
idea of a grace period for delivery drivers or individuals making brief stops to pick up or drop
someone off similar to what is in the Ogden city code.

Mr. Jones outlined a few issues; Ogden’s code is not specific to delivery vehicles. Instead, it
simply creates a grace period for all vehicles. He outlined some potential complications with this
specific provision. Councilor George Handley asked Mr. Jones for clarification on his thoughts
on short-term parking. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth commented with some anecdotal
experience of delivery vehicle parking in different styles of housing she has lived in in Provo.
Councilor Katrice MacKay thought this discussion was about apartments; she felt 30 minutes
was too long but that 15 minutes was appropriate.

Mr. Jones presented on towing signage requirements and fees. He noted that Provo distinguishes
between patrol tows and request tows. Mr. Jones reviewed the differences between the state code
and city code on patrol tows. Any sign that meets state code would meet Provo city code, except
for requiring that the fees are listed on the sign. Listing the fees is problematic now that the city
code refers to a specific percentage of the state fee. The City code on request tows is also similar
to state code in many regards, with several slight differences. Mr. Jones recommended repealing
the City code provisions, as the state code is much more detailed and exhaustive; the provisions
are not in conflict, but as written it is a bit redundant and inaccurate.

8.      An ordinance amending Provo City Code Section 4.01.010 regarding the process for
        making appointments to the civil service commission to align with state code. (22-
        009) (2:42:40)

Brian Jones, City Attorney, presented. The State code designates the Council as the appointing
authority for the Civil Service Commission, while City code indicates the commissioners are
appointed by the Mayor with the advice and consent of the Council. This code amendment
simply amends City Code to reflect the process dictated by state code.

Mr. Jones noted they have encountered some issues recently with the Civil Service Commission,
as the state code does not designate a process or ability to appoint alternate members. Mr. Jones
is working with Provo’s state representative to run a bill to resolve this. He also intends to
discuss with Provo’s state representative some issues regarding the appointment process for the
Metropolitan Water Board. Regarding the Civil Service Commission, state code has specific
