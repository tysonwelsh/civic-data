PART TWO: Social Capital and Brokerage

Bonding and Bridging Social Capital

* Bonding social capital (cohesiveness) is associated with ties to close friends or family, dee
interpersonal relationships, and a resulting sense of “closeness” (Besser 2009; Putnam 20
* Bonding social capital is often associated with reciprocity and solidarity (Putnam 2000). Economica
bonding social connections can act as a protection or buffer for those who are already economicall
vulnerable by roviding access to childcare, a source of easily obtainable loans, and other resource
needed to address problems in daily life {8son and Farnsworth 2011). A lack of bonding social capit

could make it more difficult for individuals or families experiencing economic hardship to get by

(Sherman 2006).

* Bridging social capital (economic connectedness) has been described as relationships or
associations with others outside of one’s immediate social group or across social classes,
racial or ethnic groups, or organizations.

* Bridging social capital is generally viewed as linkages to external assets by providing access to resou
such as information and job referrals (Putnam 2000). It is seen as constituting norms of looking out
from one’s immediate social group and bridging “across social groups” (Putnam 2000). Bridging soc
capital helps individuals get ahead.

* Institutional brokerage

* An institution that arranges interpersonal connections between actors to create bonding and bric
social capital.
