Z-6-2020
DR Horton
4600 South 5600 West
Zone Change from A to RM, R-1-10 and C-2
11 acres


        David Lewis with DR Horton has submitted a zone change application for an 11 acre
        property at 4600 South 5600 West. The property is currently zoned A (agriculture,
        minimum lot size ½ acre). The proposed zoning is a combination of RM (multiple unit
        dwelling residential), R-1-10 (single unit dwelling residential, minimum lot size 10,000
        square feet) and C-2 (general commercial). The property is designated low density
        residential (3 to 4 units/acre) for the west portion of the property and mixed use for the
        east portion of the property. According to the General Plan document, the mixed use
        designation at this location includes general commercial and medium density residential
        (7 to 12 units/acre).

        Surrounding zones include M (manufacturing) to the south and east and R-1-7 (single
        unit dwelling residential, minimum lot size 7,000 square feet) to the north. Surrounding
        land uses include a Dominion Energy utility installation, Union Pacific railway and
        vacant land south of the railway to the south; commercial uses including self-storage, an
        orthodontics office, fast food restaurant and an auto service business to the east and
        single unit dwellings to the north. The subject property is vacant.

        The applicant’s concept plan includes a 98-unit, 2 story townhome development on 8.2
        acres, a 4-lot subdivision for single unit dwellings on 1.7 acres and a 1.1 acre commercial
        pad in the southeast corner. The density of the townhome portion of the project is 12
        units/acre. For parking, the townhome portion shows 4 stalls/unit (2-car garage plus 2
        spaces in the driveway) plus 16 visitor spaces. The attached concept plan and
        development agreement proposal from the applicant provide details on the proposed
        project. Also included are elevations and a rendering of the proposed townhomes. More
     details about the proposal are covered below under the development agreement
     discussion. Where this application involves three different zones, this report includes a
     section for each of the three zones requested.

     Included with this report are several emails from residents of the neighborhood to the
     north. At the time this report was completed, staff had received eight emails that were in
     opposition to this application and one that was in favor. Also included with this report is
     a summary of the resident comments along with brief staff responses in red.

     RM Zone

     According to the City’s zoning ordinance, zone changes to the RM zone can only be
     considered by the City if the following four conditions are met:
1.   For properties without existing Structures which are proposed to be developed, the
     property must be a minimum of one acre and the Density must not exceed 12 units per
     acre. For properties with existing Structures proposed to be redeveloped, the property
     must be a minimum of 20,000 square feet and the Density must not exceed 20 units per
     acre. For properties that are west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West
     Valley Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station or the Redwood
     Junction light rail station, the Density may exceed 20 units per acre;
2.   The property shall meet any one of the following three requirements: (i) have access and
     Frontage on a Street with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet (for properties
     under two acres) or 100 feet (for properties two acres or larger) as indicated on the Major
     Street Plan; (ii) be adjacent to existing multifamily residential development on two sides;
     or (iii) be located west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West Valley
     Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station, or the Redwood Junction light
     rail station;
3.   A Development Agreement must be proposed with the zone change application that
     addresses Dwelling Unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, Landscaping, and project
     Amenities; and
4.   A traffic impact study prepared by a registered traffic engineer is required for projects
     with 100 or more Dwelling Units.
     This application meets the first condition in that the property is over one acre and has a
     proposed density at 12 units/acre. For the second condition, the subject property has
     access and frontage on 5600 West, which has a planned right-of-way width of 106’. On
     the third condition, the applicant’s development agreement standards are addressed later
     in this report. The fourth condition does not apply because project is under 100 units.

     As the Commission evaluates this application, the appropriateness of the uses and the
     quality of the proposal are primary considerations. The points below may help the
     Commission when considering the appropriateness of the uses:
   The one stub street to the north will be terminated in a cul-de-sac for the new single unit
    dwellings. All of the townhomes will have direct access to 5600 West without going
    through the neighborhood to the north.
   This section of 5600 W is a five-lane arterial street that is serviced by UTA bus routes 47
    and F556. An express bus route is planned on 5600 West by 2030 from the Old Bingham
    Highway Light Rail Station to the Salt Lake International Center, then to the Salt Lake
    International Airport and finally to downtown Salt Lake City. This project is replacing
    the bus rapid transit project that was previously planned between 2700 S and 6200 S.
   Average annual daily traffic (AADT) for this section of 5600 West for 2017 was 28,000.
    A five lane arterial can handle a traffic volume of 26,500 at a level of service (LOS) C
    and 30,500 at an LOS D. Traffic along 5600 West is actually projected by Wasatch Front
    Regional Council (WFRC) to decrease in the future as the Mountain View Corridor
    draws commuters off of 5600 West. WFRC’s AADT projections for this segment of 5600
    West are 24,000 for 2030, 20,500 for 2040 and 22,500 for 2050. According to the
    Institute of Transportation Engineers, townhomes generate 5.81 trips per day.
   The main comment expressed by residents to the north is that the property should be
    developed as single unit dwellings and not townhomes. As pointed out earlier, the
    General Plan does calls for medium density residential and commercial. This designation
    is consistent with other major intersections along 5600 West including 2700 South, 3500
    South, 5400 South and 6200 South.

    If the Commission believes that townhomes are appropriate here, the quality of the
    townhome proposal should then be considered.

    Dwelling Unit Sizes
    All of the proposed townhomes are 3-bedroom units. The minimum area set forth in the
    City’s ordinance for a 3-bedroom townhome is 1,400 sq. ft. According to the applicant,
    all units will meet the minimum area. For comparison, other recently approved townhome
    developments like Erin Hills (3627 S 560 W) and Cobalt Landing (3579 S Redwood Rd.)
    have larger minimum areas ranging from about 1,600 sq. ft. to over 2,000 sq. ft.
    depending upon the type of unit. Cobalt Landing also included basements.

    Exterior Materials
    As seen in the townhome elevations and rendering, the proposed exterior materials are
    stone, fiber cement siding and stucco which are allowed materials in the City’s ordinance.

            Architecture
    The architecture will be subject to the City’s multi-unit residential design standards.
    Assuming this application is approved, a comprehensive review of the building plans will
    verify compliance with the design standards. Based on a preliminary review of the
    elevations and rendering, staff noticed the following:
         The City’s design standards for multi-unit residential require architectural
            variation between buildings for projects with more than four buildings. As
       proposed, the only variation in buildings is the number of units in a building (4, 5
       and 6-unit buildings) and colors. Other recent projects, like Erin Hills, Cobalt
       Landing and the Villages at Westridge (5610 W 5400 S) had more substantial
       variation.
      Additional window treatments will be needed on the rear or alley side elevation.
      The minimum roof pitch for pitched roofs is 6:12. This will need to be verified on
       the construction drawings.
      The code requires a patio or balcony with a minimum area of 60 sq. ft. This will
       also need to be verified on the construction drawings.
      The street facing side façades will be very prominent in this development. While
       these façades are better than those on the opposing side, they are still fairly plain.
       Staff recommends adding features like larger windows, more windows, vertical
       fiber cement siding in the gable, roof articulation and/or another pop-out or some
       other form of relief.


Landscaping/Open Space
The proposed open space amount (53%) is above the required 50% specified for planned
unit developments (PUDs).

Project Amenities
The City’s multi-unit residential design standards require projects with 76 to 99 units to
include at least three amenities from a list included in the ordinance. The following
amenities are ones proposed by the applicant that count toward the three required: tot lot,
courtyards, walking/exercise trail and outdoor fire pit/fireplace.

In addition to the architectural concerns raised above, staff is also concerned about the
proposed concept site plan and fencing. While site plan issues are usually addressed
during the subdivision review process, the final layout will impact decisions about
fencing and architecture so staff is raising them now.

Having townhome units face 5600 West as shown on the applicant’s concept plan would,
in staff’s opinion, look better than a wall and some landscaping. However, 5600 West is a
5-lane, busy and noisy street. Other recent townhome developments along 5600 West and
2700 West (also 5-lanes) installed or will be installing a wall and some landscaping along
the street and then have the sides of the townhomes to the street. A townhome owner of a
unit facing 5600 West may prefer the privacy and noise attenuation of a wall. For this
reason, the second staff alternative for approval below mentions a 6’ masonry wall along
5600 West. The second concept from the applicant shows how this could be
accomplished.

The first concept plan also shows a building along the west side of the townhome
property where the garage side of the building is fully exposed, meaning there is no
    building on the opposite side of the street. Either the back of this building would need to
    be enhanced significantly or the layout changed so that it is no longer exposed. The
    second concept plan from the applicant includes the same number of units but in a
    different configuration.

    As a minimum, the ordinance requires a 6’ chain link fence along railroad rights-of-way.
    To create more privacy and durability, a 6’ masonry wall could be considered as an
    enhancement in the development agreement.

    If the Planning Commission supports this application, the following development
    agreement provisions are recommended based on the applicant’s proposal as well as
    suggestions from staff:

   The maximum number of units shall be 98. The development shall be platted such that
    each townhome unit is located on its own lot.
   The minimum size for all units shall be 1,400 finished square feet above grade excluding
    the garage.
   Exterior materials shall be fiber cement siding, stucco and stone.
   All townhomes shall be built substantially like the renderings provided by the applicant
    with the exception noted below. All requirements in Chapter 7-11 Part 4 of the Zoning
    Ordinance (multi-unit residential design standards) shall be met. This means that the
    proposed townhomes may need to be revised as part of the subdivision approval process
    to meet the ordinance standards. The street side elevations shall be modified to include at
    least two of the following treatments: larger windows, more windows, fiber cement
    siding in the gable, roof articulation and another pop-out or some other form of relief.
   At least four different exterior color schemes per building shall be used to provide
    variation between buildings throughout the community. To provide additional variety
    between buildings, an alternative set of elevations shall be developed and built for the
    front and street facing façades. The details of the alternative set of elevations shall be
    determined during the subdivision review process.
   All townhomes shall have a 2-car garage.
   Project amenities shall include a tot lot, courtyards, a firepit and a walking/exercise trail.
   An entry feature shall be included at the entrance. The details of the entry feature shall be
    determined during the subdivision review process.
   All units shall include a driveway with a minimum depth of 18’.
   A 6’ tall vinyl fence shall be installed on the north property line. A 3-rail fence shall be
    installed along the residential frontage of 5600 West. A chain-link fence shall be installed
    along the railroad.
   Upon completion of all of the on-site improvements, the developer shall provide a letter
    to the City from an independent engineering firm which certifies that all of the on-site
    improvements are completed as shown on the plans approved by the City. For the
    purposes of this provision, on-site improvements shall include landscaping, irrigation,
    private streets, sidewalks, parking, lighting, entrance features, fencing, playground
    equipment, firepit and any other outdoor recreational amenity.
   All new homes built on the four lots on the R-1-10 zoned property shall meet the
    requirements in Part 2 of Chapter 7-11 of the West Valley City Zoning Ordinance.

    One concern expressed from a neighbor in a phone call to staff was that the proposed
    townhomes do not have basements. This limits storage space and leaves no room for
    additional living space. Some townhome projects within the City have basements while
    others do not. All of the homes along the south side of 4535 South to the north have
    basements. According to County Assessor data, the average area of these homes is 1,771
    square feet including the basements (finished or unfinished).

    R-1-10 Zone

    The ordinance allows properties under two acres to be rezoned to the R-1-10 zone. As
    seen in the concept plan, the proposed R-1-10 zone area is 1.7 acres. Given the existing
    stub street and R-1-7 zoning to the north and the General Plan designation of low density
    residential, staff supports the R-1-10 zoning as proposed.

    C-2 Zone

    The C-2 zone includes a minimum district size of 5 acres. An individual parcel within the
    C-2 zone can be as small as 20,000 sq. ft.; however, the property must be adjacent to
    other C-2 zoned parcels that together total 5 acres. The Planning Commission recently
    approved an ordinance change (ZT-4-2020) that would allow property zoned M
    (Manufacturing) to count toward the minimum C-2 district size. At the time this report
    was prepared, the Council had not yet considered this ordinance change. If the Council
    approves the ordinance change, the proposed C-2 zoning for this application would be
    eligible to be rezoned.

    The area proposed for C-2 zoning is the closest to the signalized intersection at 4700
    South. It is also across the street from storage units and commercial uses like a fast food
    establishment, orthodontist office and auto repair. As pointed out earlier, the General
    Plan also calls for general commercial uses. Two other approved townhome
    developments in the City, the Villages at Westridge and Erin Hills also included a
    commercial component. For these reasons, staff is supportive of the proposed C-2 zoning.

Ken Johnson resides at 5613 West Cape Vista Way. Ken asked what the detention
area depicted in the concept plan would be used for. Steve explained at this stage

Bradly Worth, residing at 5678 West 4510 South. Bradly stated he agrees with
