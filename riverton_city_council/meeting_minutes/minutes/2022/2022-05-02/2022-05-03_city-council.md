        Since Fiscal Year 2015, total budgeted expenditures for the City have only grown by

Mayor Staggs reported that several large projects had been funded with his proposed budget:

Culinary Water
    Installation of a reverse osmosis system on the City’s Green Well to improve water
       quality that will meet or exceed the quality of water provided by the Jordan Valley Water
       Conservancy District (JVWCD). Salt Lake County has signaled interest in contributing $3
       million toward the project in exchange for a special water rate for the Riverbend Golf
       Course. This investment, combined with a contribution of $650,000 from the City using
       ARPA funds, will allow the City to re‐engage the Green Well. This innovative project
       could supply nearly half of the City’s culinary water needs, reducing our dependency on
       JVWCD water, and allowing us to keep culinary water rates low for the foreseeable
       future.
    The City has budgeted funds to replace aging water lines on 1830 West and 11800
       South.

Pressurized Irrigation (Secondary Water)
    $3 million ($1.5 million this year and $1.5 anticipated next year) of ARPA funds have
      been budgeted to install secondary (pressurized irrigation) water meters on the
      remaining two-thirds of connections without a meter. The City anticipates the State of
      Utah paying for 70% (approximately $8.8 million) of the total cost of this project using
      funds the Utah State Legislature set aside in House Bill 242 for this purpose. The City
      has already made application for those state funds. It’s likely this project will span the
      next two years.
    Proposing the City make significant investments to improve pressurization and filtration
      of the secondary irrigation water system. $850,000 has been budgeted for the addition of
      a pump station, pump repairs, and improved filtration.
    Currently in negotiations to purchase the balance of secondary water shares the City will
      need through build‐out. Proposing paying for those shares over a 5‐year period, paying
      20% each year starting in Fiscal Year 2022‐2023.

         Administrative Law Judge Discussion

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained he would introduce the new administrative code that
would affect code enforcement violations. This code would provide one central location for the
City’s options for pursuing a code enforcement problem and would put an administrative law
judge in place to enable the City to streamline things for most types of cases.

Mr. Carter said the remedies are threefold: the ability to issue a civil citation and fine, the ability
to abate nuisances, and the ability to proceed in a classic fashion and have the case heard
before the Riverton Justice Court judge. Under these three approaches, the City will have
various options to go after code enforcement issues. The most common remedy would be to
issue a civil citation and have a person come before an administrative law judge which can
happen quickly.

Mr. Carter believes the City should step up the administrative enforcement of these issues,
abate the nuisances and assess liens on the property. He would like to bring this back for
approval at the next City Council meeting. The City would need to hire an administrative law
judge and he believes the code enforcement officers are capable of handling these cases. He
asked the Council to look at the fees which are similar to fees in surrounding cities.

Councilmember McDougal asked about the fee schedule and whether multiple fees would be
assessed if there are multiple charges. Mr. Carter responded that there are penalties for each
charge. However, he explained that the City would consider what it would take to abate the
problem and this is not a fixed fee. The civil fee is for administrative enforcement and then the
City would charge for actual costs to abate the problem.

Councilmember McCay asked how the new fee structure compares to the original fee structure.
Mr. Carter responded that there were no fees set out for administrative fines in the old code.
This is the first time that the City has had these fees and these fees were determined by looking
at surrounding cities. Mr. Carter explained that one of the provisions in the new code would
deny an applicant seeking development of a property if they have outstanding code
enforcement violations.

         Ordinance No. 22-14 – Cardenas Rezone, Rezone of approximately 2.62-acres
         located near 1768 West 13400 South from RR-22 to R-4. Applicant-Juan Cardenas,
         with Del Sol Properties

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained this was a public hearing for a
Rezone application brought by the applicant for 2.62-acres located near 1768 West 13400
South. The application includes four (4) properties that form an “L”-like shape with frontage on
Redwood Road and on 13400 South. The applicant included a preliminary site plan which
shows the two existing homes facing Redwood Road remaining, while the underused rear
portions of the property would be accessed by a new private lane coming from 13400 South
Street. The preliminary site plan is only conceptual, and would be subject to further review,
applications and city approvals prior to the land being subdivided or any construction starting.

Mr. Lethbridge said the subject property is currently zoned “RR-22” which is a single-family
residential zone with half-acre lots and animal rights. All the adjoining properties are also zoned
RR-22, although many of them are less than half-acre, something that is common in older areas
of Riverton. The General Plan, which was adopted in August 2020, designates the entire area
as Medium Density Residential. This designation allows for 10,000-sf lots or larger, and the
requested R-4 Zone is consistent with the General Plan.

Josh Black, Civil Solutions Group, representing Juan Cardenas, explained that the rezone
extends to Redwood Road and includes the existing homes. Mr. Lethbridge agreed that the
rezone extends to Redwood Road.

Councilmember McCay asked if the 2.62 acres include the existing homes’ lots. Mr. Black
replied that it does include the existing homes and the new subdivision is about 1.75 acres.

Juan Cardenas, applicant, explained that they are trying to improve the neighborhood and make
it look better. He knows that lots are very expensive and he would like to be able to give his
children lots to build their own homes.
