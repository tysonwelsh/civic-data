31          1.1     (Project CUP-24-015) Request by Amanda Martin to Operate a Home Business
32                  (Pet Care for Two Dogs Maximum) at 2426 East Sarto Avenue.
33
34   City Planner, Ian Harris presented the Staff Report for the home business and indicated that it is
35   located in the neighborhood to the east of Brighton High School. The property is zoned R-1-8 Single
36   Family. The applicant, Amanda Martin, is requesting approval to operate a pet care services business
37   by appointment in her existing home. Services would include daycare and boarding for 1 to 7 nights.
38   The business will operate seven days per week. To mitigate any issues pets may have with fireworks,
39   the business will be closed on July 4th and July 24th. The applicant will limit bookings to a maximum
40   of two pets at a time. In rare cases, they might accept a third pet based on its behavior and ease of
41   care. In such instances, they will allow three pets, which is the maximum number of pets allowed per
42   the Pet Licensing Ordnance 8.16.120. Parking is available in the applicant’s driveway for drop-offs
43   and pickups, which would be scheduled after 6:00 a.m. and before 9:00 p.m. the parking and yard
44   areas were identified on the Site Plan.
45
46   One public comment was received that was forwarded to the Hearing Officer for review. Mr. Harris
47   reported that the presentation and Staff Report should answer the questions posed in the comment.
48

 1   The property is zoned R-1-8 Single Family, and Home Occupations with Clients are considered
 2   Conditional Uses. The proposed business is secondary and incidental to the use of the property as
 3   the applicant’s permanent, full-time residence. Staff found that the application is Code compliant and
 4   recommended approval subject to the Conditions outlined in the Staff Report.
 5
 6   Ms. Martin reported that she has a thorough intake process and monitors animals for behavior and
 7   health. With rare exceptions, she is always at home. In response to a question from Hearing Officer
 8   Johnson, she confirmed that all pets are required to be up-to-date on their vaccinations. Dogs are
 9   evaluated for behavioral issues and how they interact with other pets. Ms. Martin also confirms if
10   they are spayed or neutered and reviews any health issues, vaccination records, and emergency
11   contacts. She monitors the schedule regularly to ensure that there are no conflicts such as two dogs
12   arriving at the same time. She provides a mixture of daycare and boarding services. She receives a
13   lot of boarding requests over the holidays and daycare requests on ski days. She normally only
14   accepts clients two weeks per month.
15
16   Hearing Officer Johnson clarified that City Ordinance allows a maximum of four total pets.
17   Ms. Martin stated that she prefers to only have one pet at a time. However, she has a large clientele.
18   If she knows the dogs, she will take up to three as long as one is a daycare client who will go home
19   in the evening. Some families have two dogs, and in that case, she might board three dogs at once.
20
21   In response to a question from Hearing Officer Johnson, Ms. Martin confirmed that her yard is fenced
22   with a locked gate. Pets are monitored at all times when they are outside, and pet waste is removed
23   multiple times per day.
24
25   Hearing Officer Johnson opened the public hearing.
26
27   Kathy Potter asked for clarification on whether approving the application would affect the current
28   residential zoning. Hearing Officer Johnson clarified that home businesses are an allowed use in any
29   residential zone, and the application was being reviewed as a home business in the R-1-8 Single-
30   Family Residential Zone. It was not a zoning change. Anyone can obtain a Business License for a
31   home-based business anywhere in the City as long as the predominant use of the property is as their
32   residence. Ms. Potter stated that she is Ms. Martin’s neighbor, and Ms. Martin was a very respectful
33   and responsible neighbor. There was a lot of barking at first but Ms. Martin has done a very good job
34   of vetting her clientele and addressing the issue.
35
36   Brian Holt asked if Ms. Martin had written approval from the owner of the property. Hearing Officer
37   Johnson confirmed that the owner’s consent has been given and it is a requirement of the licensure
38   process. Mr. Holt’s understanding was that City Ordinance requires home business to be conducted
39   within the home. He expressed concern that the dogs will be allowed outside at night and stated that
40   there should be parameters around when the dogs are allowed out. Most residents in the area are
41   retired and loud dogs can be very disruptive. Hearing Officer Johnson stated that the business must
42   be conducted primarily within the home and any use of yard space must be clearly secondary to the
43   yard being used for residential purposes. For example, home daycares are allowed to have recess
44   time in the yard as long as the yard is not being utilized 100% for that business purpose.
45
46   Gary DeBernardi reported that he submitted questions via email, and he was satisfied with the City’s
47   response. It is a very small area with one way in and out. He assumed that Conditional Use Permits
48   are considered on a case-by-case basis but he was concerned that approving this permit will open the
     Cottonwood Heights Administrative Hearing – 10/02/24                                               2
 1   area to additional Conditional Uses and it will be difficult to deny other permits once that precedent
 2   is set. Hearing Officer Johnson clarified that Conditional Use Permits are defined at the state level.
 3   Residents are entitled to Conditional Use Permits anywhere in the City where they are an option as
 4   long as the proposed use does not create a negative impact that cannot be mitigated through
 5   conditions. The City is allowed to impose conditions but only in very rare cases can it deny a
 6   Conditional Use Permit. It is not an issue of setting a precedent, but rather of property rights. If the
 7   property has the right to a Conditional Use Permit, they are entitled to go through the process. In
 8   response to his question, Hearing Officer Johnson stated that if Mr. DeBernardi wished to open an
 9   automobile mechanic shop in his garage, his application would be considered based on business
10   hours, noise potential, traffic generation, and other factors.
11
12   Suzanne Hyland reported that Ms. Martin has taken care of her 12-year-old dog which does not do
13   well in commercial boarding facilities. She has witnessed how attentive Ms. Martin is to her clients
14   and mitigating nuisances. Cottonwood Heights allows up to four domestic pets but Ms. Martin’s
15   request was to keep dogs below that limit. She was very impressed with Ms. Martin’s intake process
16   and how aware she is of the nuisances that could be created. It is also very expensive to live in
17   Cottonwood Heights and side hustles are becoming a necessity. She was impressed by how
18   enterprising and creative Ms. Martin is. She brings dogs into her home instead of having dogs of her
19   own. She also engages her family in their care, so she is teaching her children empathy for animals
20   and how to be responsible for them. Ms. Martin is a responsible business owner, a wonderful mother,
21   and a wonderful caretaker, and her business is everything the City should want in a home business in
22   Cottonwood Heights.
23
24   Kathy Mather lives behind Ms. Martin. They have had some interactions with her and find her to be
25   an extremely nice neighbor. They are home all day and their biggest concern is barking. In the past,
26   dogs have barked at them when they went into their backyard. She asked what mitigation measures
27   could be taken so that they can enjoy their backyard and still allow Ms. Martin to have a business.
28   Hearing Officer Johnson stated that whether it is someone else’s pets or your own, the City has an
29   Excessive Barking Ordinance. If there is an ongoing issue, it could be addressed.
30
31   Ms. Martin reported that in the area three other homes have dogs that bark, so she hoped that neighbors
32   can tell where the barking is coming from. If she has a new dog, there will be barking until the dog
33   gets used to the property. Once they calm down and are familiar with the home, that helps mitigate
34   the barking. She is very cognizant of it. She lived next door to someone that she had to report,
35   including times and dates of excessive barking, and she is doing everything she can to avoid those
36   issues.
37
38   There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.
39
40   Hearing Officer Johnson thanked the members of the public for their comments and Ms. Martin for
41   her thorough proposal. His concerns were the use of the yard, the number of pets, vaccinations, and
42   ensuring the animals are the right fit to avoid situations that can happen when those controls are not
43   in place. He reported that substantially more conditions would be placed on this business than if
44   anyone simply kept four dogs on their property and he was comfortable that the potential negative
45   impacts will be mitigated through the recommended conditions. Conditions are subject to ongoing
46   review, so if a condition is not being followed, the City can take corrective action. Nuisances like
47   excessive barking are investigated by the Code Enforcement Division.
