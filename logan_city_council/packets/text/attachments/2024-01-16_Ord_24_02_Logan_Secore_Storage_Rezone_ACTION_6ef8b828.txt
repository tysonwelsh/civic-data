Huddleston/Skyler Hartman, authorized agent/owner are requesting a zone change of a 4.43-acre parcel
from Industrial Park (IP) to Airport (AP) to build a new 70,000 square foot self-storage facility located at
1950 North 1000 West; TIN 04-076-0015 (Bridger Neighborhood).

STAFF: Russ Holley, Planner reviewed the request for a rezone of a 4.43-acre parcel from Industrial Park
(IP) to Airport (AP) to build a new 70,275 square foot self-storage facility.  The Airport zone is the only
zone that permits self-storage land uses.  In October 2021 a conditional use permit was approved for
Infinite Storage (previous project name) that expired in October 2022.  In March 2022 the Logan City
Council adopted a code amendment prohibiting new storage units in all zones except for the Airport zone.

PROPONENT: Skyler Hartman discussed his situation and explained why he thought the rezone was a
good idea. He expressed issues with completing the sewer design and the site design. He said most
storage units facilities in the city are near capacity and that the demand for more is currently existing.

PROJECT

BACKGROUND
On October 14, 2021, the Logan City Planning Commission (PC# 21-050) conditionally
approved the Infinite Storage (previous project name) entitling up to 900 commercial storage
units on the 4.43-acre parcel. As per the Logan City Land Development Code (LDC) 17.51.010
and listed on the Design Review and Conditional Use Permit, the approval expired one year
from that date. (October 14, 2022). On March 1, 2022, the Logan City Council adopted a code
amendment ordinance prohibiting new commercial storage units in the IP zone. The applicant
submitted for a building permit on May 23, 2023, nearly six months after the expiration date. The
applicant cited informal correspondence with water and sewer City personnel as the reason for
the delay. On July 21, 2023, Walter Keane submitted an appeal application to the Logan City
Land Use Appeals Board. The Land Use Appeals Board upheld staff's denial of the project and
determined the project to be expired. The Appeal is now going through the District Court
process with the hope to overturn the Land Use Appeals Board decision.

The applicant is now also proposing a rezone to the AP zone with the intention to acquire a new
permit and entitlement for the self-storage project now named Logan Secure Storage.

GENERAL PLAN
The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP), adopted in 2008, identifies this property as Industrial Park
(IP). The General Plan, a nonregulatory visioning plan, describes IP areas as being for large
employment and production centers. Residential is not appropriate and site development
standards will emphasize large setbacks and ample landscaping. The IP designation is
generally located in the northwest quadrant of the city.

The Airport (AP) FLUP designation is specifically intended to promote the development and
enhancement of the Airport. The AP designation is also intended for commercial uses typically
associated with Airports, such as hotels, restaurants, and similar. The AP designation should
continue to foster the relationship with Utah State University and their aviation instructional
efforts.

Land Development Code (LDC) - Zoning
The IP zoning district no longer permits or conditional permits "self-storage" or commercial
storage units. In the spring of 2022, the adoption occurred with the debate centered on
commercial storage units being too land consumptive for most zoning districts and the land use
provides very few jobs and employment opportunities as compared to other IP land uses.
Currently, the AP zone is the only district that conditionally permits commercial storage units in
the City. Site development standards include ample setbacks and landscaping to help mitigate
and buffer the typically stark building designs and layouts.

Setbacks
The Land Development Code (LDC) requirements for setbacks in the AP zone are as follows
(as measured from property lines):
Front:                      10'
Side:                       20'
Rear:                       10'
Parking (front/rear)        20'/15'

The following setbacks are proposed for the building (as measured from the exterior property
lines of the project site, at closest points):
Front (west):               45'
Side (north):               40'
Side (south):               58'
Rear (east):                60
Parking (front):            30'

As proposed, the project meets minimum setback requirements of the LDC.

Lot Coverage
The LDC 17.10.070 establishes a maximum lot coverage of 60% (building(s) footprint) in the AP
zone. The total project site is approximately 4.43 acres in size and with a 70,275 SF building
footprint(s), the lot coverage would equal approximately 40%. As proposed, the lot coverage
complies with standards in the LDC.

Building Design
The LDC 17.12.060 requires high-quality and aesthetically pleasing building design in industrial
areas of town. Minimum articulation and material mixes are required for better variety and
interesting developments. Blank or featureless walls are limited to no more than 40-foot lengths.
The proposed storage buildings are approximately 240' wide and vary in depth at 40'-110'. The
proposed material for the storage units is concrete masonry units. As per commercial storage
design requirements, the buildings are oriented so that the storage unit bays do not face the
surrounding street. The front building is internally accessed, with the remaining rear buildings
accessed from exterior garage-style doors. The front elevation is proposed with alternative
metal façade materials rather than the AP required 40% transparency. As conditioned, with
enhanced landscape screening and the Planning Commission determining the approval of the
alternative materials, the project meets the requirements of the LDC.

Parking Stall Requirements
The LDC does not prescribe parking ratios for this land use and suggests an alternative parking
plan based on the project details. Parking for exterior storage units occurs directly adjacent to
the unit in the 34' wide drive aisle. Parking for this land use is temporary in nature, as users
either load and unload items and quickly exit the property. For the front interior storage units,
nine (9) stalls are shown on the submitted site plan. Based on the project layout and unique
land use, staff considers the proposed parking areas adequate to mitigate overflow parking onto
adjacent public streets.

Useable Outdoor Space and Landscaping
The LDC 17.10.070 requires 10% landscaping and 10% useable outdoor space in the AP zone.
The LDC 17.32 generally describes useable outdoor space as outdoor areas that are designed
for the benefit and enjoyment of the residents and their guests. Useable outdoor space may
include public gathering areas, patios, decks, walkways, plaza's, active recreational areas, and
natural open space areas. The project site for this proposal is approximately 192,000 SF and
would require 38,400 SF of both landscaping and outdoor space. The submitted concept
landscaping plan shows approximately 40,000 SF of area for landscaping and open space
meeting the LDC requirement as submitted.

Lighting
The LDC 17.30.090 requires adequate lighting that adds aesthetic quality and improves safety
while mitigating unnecessary glare, sky glow and light trespass. The LDC limits freestanding
pole height to 32 feet and luminaire fixtures on buildings and canopies to be concealed source,
down-cast and shielded from neighboring properties. Light measurements are required to range
between 0.5 – 4.0-foot candles, so areas are sufficiently safe, but not excessively bright. As

SUMMARY
Given the FLUP designation of IP and the 2022 code amendment prohibiting new commercial
storage units in the IP zone because of their land consumption rates and lack of larger
employment opportunities, staff recommends following the FLUP and denying the rezone
request. It is unfortunate that the applicant let the 2021 entitlement lapse and expire, but that is
how the Logan City land use regulation and zoning is established. Typically, land use authorities
establish expiration dates to prevent old and outdated surprises from popping up unexpectantly.
The nearest AP zoned parcel is currently over 2,000 feet away and would create a small island
of AP zoning that is not contiguous with the actual Airport.
