         A Petition to the Planning Commission to Forward a Positive Recommendation to the
         South Salt Lake City Council for an Ordinance Amendment Establishing Uniform
         Regulations for All Auto-Related Uses Regarding Outdoor Storage, Increasing the
         Minimum Lot Size, Minimum Lot Frontage, and Minimum Interior Display
         Requirements for Car Dealerships, Adding a Definition for Luxury Vehicle, and Updating
         the Land Use Matrix.

         ACTION ITEM
         Applicant: South Salt Lake

Ahead of the Staff Presentation, Chair Carter reviewed the rules for the public hearing. When the public
hearing takes place, those interested in sharing comments can approach the podium, state their name,
city of residence, and provide comments on the application. It is not a question-and-answer period, but
questions can be posed. Those questions may or may not be addressed following the public hearing.
Comments will be limited to three minutes or less. He noted that there is no need to repeat comments.

Mr. Cawley presented the Staff Report and stated that the item before the Commission is for the Auto-
Related Uses Code Amendments. He shared a summary of the changes that have been made:

     •   Expanded definition of Luxury Vehicle;
     •   Inclusion of metal materials in fencing requirements;
     •   Reduced lot and site requirements for new or expanding dealerships;
     •   Commercial Repair Services will remain in the Commercial General District;
     •   Addition of Automotive Restoration in Business Park District;
     •   Shielded storage for dealerships that provide secondary services.
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Regular Meeting                                       8
November 7, 2024



Mr. Cawley shared additional information about the lot and site requirements for new or expanding
dealerships. Originally, there was a three-acre minimum proposed but that has been reduced to 1.5 acres
based on the feedback received. The current standard for lot frontage is 200 feet and that is proposed
to remain. The purpose and intent of the ordinance is to clarify requirements and regulations that already
exist in the code. There is a table in the Staff Report that identifies what each of those items is and
where they are located in the code. For example, outdoor storage and parking standards.

Outdoor storage is currently prohibited under the ordinance. Nothing new is being added there, but it
is being clarified across all auto-related uses what those standards are. The same is true with the parking
standards. Vehicle and vehicle inventory of operable vehicles needs to be organized in designated
parking stalls. Parking surfaces must be paved, which is stated in two locations in the ordinance.

One of the conversations Staff has been having with community members has to do with non-
conforming dealerships. This is when a land use that existed previously was allowed, but there were
later changes made to the ordinance. When that happens and the previously allowed use is no longer
permitted, it is then considered non-conforming. Mr. Cawley clarified that the use can still exist as is
in the location as long as it continues to meet the standards of the Code. Once that use changes, the use
would no longer be grandfathered in. The businesses that exist currently can continue to operate as long
as they maintain an active Business License and adhere to the health, safety, and welfare standards.

If dealerships are looking to improve their facilities (for example, paving, structural upgrades, or
combining parcels) the existing owners have the right to do that. Those applications would be
considered on a case-by-case basis and work could be done with the City throughout the Building
Permit process. Since dealerships are a conditional use, if there is expansion, there will likely need to
be an amendment made to the CUP. Through that process, the dealerships can continue and expand.
The ordinance ensures that businesses can thrive and that existing dealerships are not displaced.

The Director of Finance and the Community and Economic Development Director looked into the
economic impacts that auto-related businesses have in the City. There are a total of 228 auto-related
businesses. Of those businesses, it was found that nine are not licensed by the City, so work is being
done with those businesses. 25% of the businesses are not reporting sales tax. That could be due to
wholesalers or even a lag in reporting. Of the 25% that are not reporting sales tax, 31 of the businesses
are auto-repair and six are used car sales. Mr. Cawley noted that the average sales tax by type of auto
business was also examined. He shared a chart that outlined the number of businesses, the average sales
per square foot, and the high and the low sales per square foot. For 2023, the City sales tax revenue
based on the various auto-related uses that are listed was approximately $4.9 million.

Staff then looked further into the sales by square feet numbers. It was identified that 70% of dealerships
have sales greater than $50 per square foot. 83% are greater than $25 per square foot. 16% have less
than $25 per square foot. Of all these dealerships, six are generating 64% of the sales tax revenue.
Mr. Cawley discussed some possible next steps for the amendments. Depending on the outcome of the
Planning Commission Meeting, the intention is to take this to the City Council on November 13, 2024.
