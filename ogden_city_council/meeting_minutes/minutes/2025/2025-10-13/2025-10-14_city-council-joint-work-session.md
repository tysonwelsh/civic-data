          The purpose of the joint work session was to receive Visit Ogden reports regarding the economic and tourism impact of
arts and culture and the economic impact of baseball and softball tournaments; discuss a proposed Public Art Project – Olympic
Mural at approximately 2450 Kiesel Avenue; discuss a proposed rezone at 768 West 24th Street; discuss proposed Zone Text
Amendment for the MU Zone; discuss the MU Zone Project Master Plan for 768 West 24th Street; receive an Administrative
Update; discuss Council/Board business; and hear Council/Board comments.

Visit Ogden Reports
          Economic and Tourism Impact of Arts & Culture: Visit Ogden President/CEO Toliver used the aid of a PowerPoint
presentation to discuss the economic and tourism impacts of arts and culture in Weber County; Ogden and Weber County have
made a name for themselves with their vibrant arts and culture scene, bringing thousands of visitors to the area to enjoy arts
experiences. This identity is a strong driver of economic development for Ogden and the county as it draws in residents, new
businesses, and out-of-town visitors. The presentation defined the various arts and culture sectors in the community and
communicated that the total impact of all the sectors in 2024 was $356.1 million, with 2,416 annual jobs supported. In 2024,
Ogden’s arts and cultural activity produced over $7.4 million in tax revenue for local municipalities, the county, and the state.
Weber County and local municipalities capture approximately $3.5 million annually. Additionally, $4.4 million of the fiscal
impact is attributable to the arts and culture sector, and an additional $3.0 million is due to visitor spending. For a copy of the
presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and Ms. Toliver centered on the sources of the data used to determine the impact of
tourism on Ogden’s economy.
          Economic Impact of Baseball and Softball Tournaments: Ms. Toliver used the aid of an additional PowerPoint
presentation to highlight the economic impact of baseball and softball tournaments hosted in Ogden City during the summer of
2025; the presentation listed all tournaments held in the City and concluded the total economic impact of all events was
$5,042,441. The presentation also illustrated additional non-monetary benefits of hosting tournaments in Ogden City; for a copy
of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          The Council and Ms. Toliver discussed several baseball and softball events that span Weber and Davis Counties and
those tournaments that plan to return to the area in future years. They also discussed the efforts of City staff to perform
improvements at the facilities used for the tournaments; Ms. Toliver indicated these improvements have made it much easier to
recruit events to the community.

Proposed Public Art Project – Olympic Mural at approximately 2450
Kiesel Avenue
          Arts Administrator Buckley explained the Ogden City Cultural Services Division is requesting Council approval to
allocate $110,000 from the Public Art Fund for a proposed Olympic mural on Kiesel Avenue. Of this amount, $62,500 represents
the city’s base contribution to the mural itself, $22,500 is budgeted as contingency to cover potential shortfalls in partner
contributions, and $25,000 is reserved for mural lighting. The project, developed in partnership with Visit Ogden, Snowbasin,
MarketStar, and the MarketStar Foundation, is estimated to cost $210,000 in total, with the balance expected from partner
contributions and in-kind support. If approved, the mural would be designed and installed beginning in 2026, with the intent of
marking Utah’s 2034 Winter Olympics.
          Discussion among the Council and Ms. Buckley centered on the process for selecting an artist or artists to complete the
mural project and the need to update the Arts and Culture Master Plan to drive future public arts projects.

Proposed Rezone – 768 West 24th Street; and
Proposed Zone Text Amendment – MU Zone; and
MU Zone Project Master Plan – 768 West 24th Street
          Senior Planner Rypien explained the request before the Ogden City Council involves three coordinated actions for the
property at approximately 768 West 24th Street: (1) a proposal to amend the zoning code to establish site-specific standards for a
new Mixed-Use (MU) zone; (2) a proposal to rezone four parcels from single-family (R-1-5) to MU; and (3) a proposal to have
the Ogden Redevelopment Agency adopt a master plan for the property, which is located within the Trackline Redevelopment
Project Area. The petitioners, Tyler M. Gray & Teresa McCandless, have proposed developing 16 three-story live/work
townhomes with ground-floor commercial uses and residential units above. On May 7, 2025, the Planning Commission considered
the proposal and recommended approval with conditions to ensure design quality, maintain multiple land uses, and align with the
West Ogden Community Plan’s vision for walkable mixed-use development along 24th Street. Mr. Brierley noted this project was
considered by the Planning Commission on the same night that the recently adopted West Ogden/ 24th Street Interchange District
Plan was considered. The petitioner’s request was developed and reviewed by Planning staff and the Planning Commission with
the previously established West Ogden Community Plan adopted in 2017. It should be noted that the proposal also generally
follows the goals and policies of the new district plan adopted in 2025. He presented images and renderings to illustrate the
petitioner’s design concept.
          The Council engaged in discussion with Planning staff regarding adjustments that have been made to the project proposal
since the applicant first approached the City and interactions between the proposed development and other vital projects in West
Ogden.

RDA Administrative Update
          Community and Economic Development Deputy Director Sawyer used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide a
Redevelopment Agency (RDA) Administrative update. The presentation referenced the RDA Annual Summary for 2024; creation
of an RDA project dashboard; review of the application process for the Housing Fund Allocation Policy; the status of specific
projects; and project area performance information. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the
meeting.
          Brief discussion among the Board and Mr. Sawyer centered on the value of the dashboard tool.

Council/Board Business
          Council member Graf noted he was contacted by a local boy scout troop that wants to perform a flag ceremony at a future
meeting; he asked Council staff and Council leadership to calendar the item.
          Council member Graf then discussed ongoing efforts of the Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC) to work with a
consultant to host an Active Transportation Implementation Academy over the course of several months, from December 2025 to
April 2026. He discussed the information to be presented at the Academy and noted the WFRC is seeking assistance from local
government officials and leaders who would be interested in the program.
          Council member Myers asked about the status of the 21st Street pond, specifically related to resident access/utilization of
the pond. Chief Administrative Officer Brown stated City Administration is investigating different concepts for the pond and
plans to meet with the Goode Company regarding some changes to the pond that will provide increased opportunity for public use.
          Vice Chair Hyer discussed ongoing conversations about the future of the Weber County solid waste transfer station; he
feels a detailed discussion of the matter would be helpful to the entire Council and asked Council staff to calendar the matter for a
future work session. This led to discussion among the Council regarding existing contracts that restrict cities to using only the
Weber County solid waste transfer station.
          Vice Chair Hyer then reported that he attended a recent meeting of the Weber County Commission during which the
City’s transferrable development rights near the Ogden Valley wellfield were discussed; he felt the Commission was supportive of
the City’s position, though the recommendation they received from the Ogden Valley Planning Commission was not favorable.


         The meeting adjourned at 6:10 p.m.
