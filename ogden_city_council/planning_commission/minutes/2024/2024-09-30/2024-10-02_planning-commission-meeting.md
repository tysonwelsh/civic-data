3.Conditional Use Permit, Wildlife Rehab Center outdoor mews, 332 Washington Blvd.

Petitioner Buz Marthaler co-founder and Chair of the Wildlife Rehabilitation Center in Northern
Utah. He stated it has been a long process of them trying to open their business since June of last
year and he are hoping today will be the end of that. First there was a 30-foot setback portion, but the
area behind it is the old Fire Station owned by Ogden City. It is currently on the Mayor's Desk for
signature to go to City Council to be rezoned as commercial, which Buz thinks will change some of
the requirements for them there. So, they would rather not put any more expenses on that before they
find out where that goes, as part of it was removing pavement and adding a chain link fence and slats
on the east, which are already there. The portion on the south side that the city had to extend 20 feet a
few months ago was left without slats and so he thought the city should be the one to put those in.
Also, there has been an issue with an individual since the day they were evicted or removed from the
animal shelter, who complained that there was a problem with odors. He believes the complaint was
more vindictive than anything as there was no citation or report made. They do use left over food
scraps daily for their animals, but they are frozen and not thrown out until the day of garbage pick-up.
So, they are asking if the city can hold off on enforcing restrictions unless they receive a
substantiated noise or odor complaint, because as stated before, there are no odor issues, and the
noise is at a minimum. If they are forced to remove any animals (all are Avian, from Hummingbirds to
Eagles) due to these assumed accusations, they may have to end up euthanizing them as they have
nowhere for them to go.

Assistant Planning Director Mr. Simpson begins by clarifying this isn't for the Wildlife
Rehabilitation Center itself, but by city code, whenever there is an animal hospital that also does
outdoor kenneling, it triggers a conditional use requirement, and so that's what we're looking at today.
So, some things we need to find are, does the use create detrimental impacts that cannot be
mitigated? Is the use and site compatible with the surrounding development? Is the project consistent
with the general plan, and does it comply with land use ordinances? To give a breakdown, it’s about a
0.3 acre piece of property. It does have an L shape to it, and the location of the mews themselves
would be in the leg of that L shape, so not visible from Washington Boulevard. Historically, this
property's function is multifamily and some general office commercial uses, so when they located at
this location, it is a permitted use in the zone. Again, the only thing we're really looking at is the mews,
or what code calls outdoor kennel, single parcels. So, there isn't any combinations or subdivision
processes that need to occur (site images shown). The application includes that the mews are clean
and maintained regularly, and there shouldn't be any impact to surrounding properties. They have

4.Final Group Dwelling. The Sycamore on 28", 642 28" St

Petitioner Jake Weber who lives at 1728 Roberta St, SLC, UT is the Civil Engineer that is
working on the Project. They are doing a 7-unit townhome development with two different buildings.
Four units will be facing 28" St and then three units in the back. It does have amenities planned for
some open space area and adding a concrete retaining wall at the west property boundary.

Planner Haylie Hales stated that this group dwelling is in the R-3 East Central Zone. It has a
single family home on the west side of the property and the rest of the surrounding homes are
multifamily (photo shown). So the findings for their proposed seven unit group dwelling is that it
meets all of the preliminary comments that needed to be addressed. Some of which were, that they
show an electric vehicle charger in every garage, add four foot sidewalks leading to all of the units to
the common areas, show the location of the AC units and the gas and electric meters and that they be
screened, meet the required balcony and patio sizes, have a maximum six foot landscape area
excluding sidewalk between the unit and any hard surface parking or access lanes, required material
changes on the building projections, provide a sign and landscaping details for the entry of the
development, provide all the amenity requirements, put a vinyl fence on the side of the property that
has the multifamily dwelling, with the concrete wall on the west side. Since all of these have been
added and addressed, staff recommends approval of the proposed final group dwelling, subject to
satisfying the Engineering review comments, which include the submittal of a complete monitoring
and maintenance plan, and then to ensure that the water and sewer lines have at least a ten foot
horizontal separation.

things that were on the plat. Since that time there have been a lot of changes with phases 1 and 2

Assistant Planning Director Mr. Simpson began that what they were looking at was an
amendment to a PRUD and residential unit development project that was started in 2016. He pointed
out that due to the time length, by the code, there have been changes that warrant amendments to the
approved PRUD plans to ensure the PRUD requirements are being met, specifically as it relates to
what we're looking at changing, reasonable access, active open space, landscaping and fencing, so to
kind of hit on it. So when you do a PRUD, you plat it so that the individual building footprints are
owned individually. It has been partially developed as indicated. Phases one and two have had
buildings put on them but common area spaces have not been completed. Phase three has had one
home built, but as indicated, the amendments that need to be done. Phase four is yet to be built.
Although it is in phases, he pointed out that it is all one development, so everything has to interact
with each other, be cohesive and meet the PRUD plan requirements altogether (maps/pictures
shown). The common area is the land around all the units owned in common by the entire
development. But limited common area affords certain rights to the owners of the pads adjacent to
that limited common area. They include driveways, patio decks, things of that nature. Some can even
be closed in those limited common areas with approval from the HOA and permits from Ogden City.
Phases one and two amendments from 2022 have expired and have reverted back to the platting and
trail location. (pictures shown). With those changes, and the addition of phases three and four, there

total of 62,400 square feet of active open space which means an additional 21,900 square feet is
required. Staff is recommending in meeting the code, that trail connections be made to Harrison

area spaces, and provide that reasonable access through the development to the public streets. Staff

6.Public Hearing Rezone, CP-3 to R-4, 2869 Grant Ave.

Petitioner Race Ostler stated that a couple months ago, the planning commission discussed in
detail the removal of the multifamily component of the CP-3 zone, and as an alternative, the
commission recommended that landowners within the CP-3 zone who were still interested in building
multifamily property to simply apply for a petition to rezone according to what makes sense
according to the residential surroundings. He believes it makes sense to be rezoned, and R-4 and they
would like to put four townhomes in the quarter acre space that would be individually owned to fit
with the general plan.

Planner Damian Rodriguez displayed slides and added that additionally, there are two vision
strategies that can be progressed with the approval of the proposed, when staff's recommended
conditional overlay is applied. The first vision would be to integrate new multiple family and single
family development into the surrounding areas through context sensitive design, and also explore the
use of incentives to enhance and increase home ownership, and that second vision staff recommends
a conditional overlay with this rezone, and that conditional overlay would limit any multifamily units
developed at the property to needing to be subdivided for individual sale and ownership. Both visions,
with some buffering are consistent with the general plan. And the proposed zone R-4 is among the
most prevalent zones in the community. Staff finds that the zone and the land uses that are permitted
in the zone do reflect the prevailing character of the Jefferson Community to the largest extent
possible and the intended use is identified as an ideal use for a vacant inner block parcel, and with the
applicable development standards, staff finds that the rezone will be compatible with the surrounding
single family homes. So, given those findings, staff recommends a conditional overlay be applied to
the R-4 zoning that would require the unit to be divided, the units to be divided for individual
ownership, and the item be tabled to allow staff and the applicant to negotiate a development
agreement and return to the Commission for further considerations.

Petitioner Ostler agreed with tabling it to negotiate.

Kerry Wayne a local resident for about 40 years said the lot has been an eye sore and would like
to see a single family home there maybe because he has two businesses there with no parking and cars
are parked in front of the lot all of the time.
