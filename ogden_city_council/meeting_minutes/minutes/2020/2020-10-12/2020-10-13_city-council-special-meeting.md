Proposed Ordinance 2020-53 amending Municipal Code Subsection 12-2-2
to revise regulations regarding refuse carts on narrow streets
          A memo from the Public Services Department regarding proposed adjustments to Engineering Standards pertaining to
refuse carts came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Administration is proposing that a roadway width
requirement of at least 20 feet be adopted for placement of refuse bins. The Administrative transmittal describes that city refuse
trucks cannot effectively and safely operate on narrow streets, alleys, or private lanes. This change is anticipated to limit refuse
trucks from providing service to ve1y narrow streets, alleys, and private lanes. Residents are to instead place bins at the curb of the
nearest street with a pavement width of twenty feet (20 ') or greater. This proposal also includes a minor change to remove refuse
cart serial numbers from the utility bill, due to billing software capabilities. A work session was held to discuss this and other
proposed city engineering standard amendments on September 1, 2020. In discussing this proposed ordinance amendment, the
Administration described that approximately 130 homes on 19 roadways are anticipated to be impacted by this change.
Administrative Staff described that if approved the change would occur incrementally over the period of a year. Council members
requested that this amendment be considered at a later time after impacted residents can be notified. A mailed notice was sent to
all property owners anticipated to be impacted by this change. Assistant Deputy City Engineer Nelson summarized the memo and
used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to present aerial photographs of streets in the area to which this proposed ordinance
amendment would apply; on streets with no shoulder, on-street parking could reduce tlie passable road width to six or eight feet.
This widtli is not sufficient for refuse trucks.
          Chair Choberka referred to the discussion that took place earlier in the work session and noted that the Council plans to
table action on this ordinance until further communication with impacted residents can occur. Additionally, tlie Council has asked
for information regarding alternate trash collection schedules and the distinction between a narrow roadway and a cul-de-sac. She

Proposed Resolution 2020-28 authorizing disposition of unclaimed firearms
in the possession of the Ogden Police Department: AND
Proposed Resolution 2020-29 authorizing the disposition of unclaimed
currency and confiscated firearms in the possession of the Weber Morgan
Narcotics Strike Force for maintenance and operational costs
          A memo from Police Department came before the Council to consider the disposition of unclaimed property held by the
Ogden City Police Department and the Weber Morgan Narcotics Strike Force. The memo stated Utah law provides for the
disposition offireanns no longer needed as evidence and outlines specific standards for the disposal of unclaimed/confiscated
firearms. An agency may choose to dispose of unclaimed/confiscated firearms, but in doing so is required to sell the fireanns to a
federally licensed fireanns dealer and apply the proceeds from the sale to a public interest use, or to destroy the fireann if unfit for
sale or if the firearm is associated with a notorious crime. Before an agency applies the proceeds to a public interest use, the
legislative body must provide pennission to do so and designate and approve the public interest use. Agencies are also required to
comply with Utah Procurement Code. The Strike Force intends to sell the fireanns and appropriate the proceeds and currency for
building maintenance and repair. Pursuant to an inter local agreement, Ogden City is responsible for fiscal operations of the Strike
Force, including budgeting, purchasing, and contracting functions. As a result of this agreement, the Weber County Attorney's
Office has detennined that the Ogden City Council consider this resolution as proposed.
          Deputy Police Chief Young summarized the memo and stated the Strike Force board approved for disposition of property
and use of abandoned funds and revenue from the sale of forfeited firearms. This letter indicates approval ofusing these funds for
maintenance and unfunded operational costs. The actual cost breakdown will require final approval of the Strike Force Executive
Board.
          Council member Hyer inquired as to the process employed by the Police Department to ensure that any transaction
involving the sale of firearms by the City is legal. Deputy Chief Young reiterated that the City is required to sell firearms to a
federally licensed fireanns dealer; the City does not cmTently have a contract with such a dealer and, therefore, it will be necessmy
to publish a request for proposals (RFP) to select a qualified dealer.
          Chair Choberka then introduced in writing proposed resolution 2020-28, entitled:
                    "A resolution of the Ogden City Council authorizing disposition of unclaimed firearms in the possession of the
                    Ogden Police Depaitment."
