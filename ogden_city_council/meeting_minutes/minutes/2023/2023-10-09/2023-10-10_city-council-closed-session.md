Proposed Zone Text Amendment — High Impact Use Standards in the C-
2/CP-2 Zones
Senior Planner Rypien explained Administration is proposing amendments to what are considered high impact uses in the
C-2 and CP-2 zones. High impact uses are those uses that may have impacts related to noise, traffic, lighting, and vibrations
associated with them. As proposed, any use that is designated as a high impact use would have to comply with additional
mitigation measures such as site access, building openings, noise studies and noise dampening measures, limited hours of
operation, placement of loading/unloading docks, and outdoor lighting. In addition, the Administration is proposing enhanced
requirements for both laundry-style, or automated drive through, carwashes and manual carwashes. The proposed changes are
specific to the C-2 and CP-2 zones with special focus on high impact uses adjacent to residential zones. Administration posted a
notice of pending ordinance on May 18, 2023. The pending ordinance restrictions expire on November 14, 2023. Mr. Rypien
presented a map that identified C-2 and CP-2 zoned properties that would be subject to the proposed amendments.
, There was discussion among the Council and Planning staff regarding appropriate amendments to the zoning ordinance
that will give the Planning Commission the ability to restrict uses for which perceived nuisances cannot be mitigated properly in
order to protect residential properties. Mr. Rypien presented a map illustrating the location of all existing car washes as well as a
. map identifying properties for which a car wash application could be approved if the new standards are adopted; hours of
operation will be imposed on car washes located near residential properties. The Council indicated they are comfortable
proceeding with consideration of the proposed ordinance amendments at a future meeting.

Proposed Nuisance Fire Alarm Ordinance

Fire Chief Mathieu stated he is seeking approval of an ordinance authorizing him or his designee to issue notices, ( }
warnings, and citations to buildings owners and tenants where a false or nuisance alarm has been triggered. Warnings would be
given for the first and second offenses; fines would be assessed for repeat offenses. He noted the Fire Department frequently
responds to false fire emergencies where the activation of an alarm system is the result of mechanical failure, lack of proper
maintenance, malfunction, improper installation or other unknow causes not fire related; on one unique situation, the Fire
Department has responded to the same building 50 times over the past year. Dispatching fire apparatus and personnel to non-
emergency calls burdens limited resources and may limit the ability of the Department to respond to actual emergencies. The cost
of aresponse to a false or nuisance alarm can cost thousands of dollars and is not an appropriate or efficient use of taxpayer funds.

The Ogden City Fire Department currently has no authority to encourage alarm system repairs or replacement or fine building
owners for repeated false alarms. Chief Mathieu summarized the proposed ordinance amendments intended to help the
Department curb nuisance alarms in the community.

Discussion among the Council and Chief Mathieu centered on problems or occurrences that could lead to a false alarm,
after which the Council concluded they are supportive of the ordinance amendment moving forward to a meeting for
consideration.

Proposed Amendments to the Fire and Property Maintenance Codes

Fire Marshal Brown used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to present proposed changes to the City Fire Code to
align with the State Construction Code and the State Fire Code, as required by the State of Utah. The Administration will also
review proposed changes to the City Property Maintenance Code.

Proposed Compensation Commission Ordinance

Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reviewed the proposed ordinance establishing an Elected Officials Compensation
Commission. Appointed members of the Commission will make recommendations regarding the annual compensation--both
salaries and stipends--for the City’s elected officials. The proposed ordinance addresses the Commission member appointment
process, identifies preferred expertise of the appointees, establishes the terms for filing vacancies, establishes rules under which
the Commission will operate, and sets forth the Commission’s powers and duties.

The Council engaged in discussion regarding topics such as bias of any member of the Commissioner or the Commission
as a whole; and whether creation of the Commission will help the Council to achieve transparency relating to the matter of
deciding compensation of elected officials.

Council Business

Flag Display Ordinance: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith facilitated discussion among the Council regarding
standards for the flags that are allowed to be flown on the City’s flag poles: United States Flag, Utah Flag, Ogden City Flag, and
Police and Fire Department flags. Council member Hyer suggested the Council consider an ordinance that more broadly addresses
flag displays to avoid situations that could be politicized in the future or be divisive. The Council briefly discussed the matter and
Ms. Eller-Smith indicated that Council staff can research whether there is a State law or regulation governing flag display.

Leadership Selection: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith stated it is her understanding from past discussions
of this matter that the Council will select leadership in February of 2024 rather than in January. She facilitated discussion
among the group regarding the amount that the Chair and Vice Chair should receive in compensation; the Council
acknowledged that at least 10 percent higher than other Council members is appropriate.

Council Comments

Council member Lopez stated he wished to publicly apologize for the comments he made during last week’s City
Council meeting regarding the arrangement the City has reached with former Chief Administrative Officer Mark Johnson for
consulting services. He noted he has apologized to current Chief Administrative Officer Brown but wished to apologize on the
public record.

The meeting adjourned at 5:21 p.m.
