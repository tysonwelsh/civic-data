General Plan Update — Initial Discussion
The Ogden City General Plan was last rewritten in 2000. The initial work for the development of the plan began in
October of 1998. The process and the eventual name of the General Plan was the Involve Ogden General Plan. Since that time,
there have been several significant updates to the plan, including an update of the Open Space and Recreation section, Chapter 10,
in 2010, an Annexation study amendment in 2016, amendments related to the Bicycle Master Plan in 2016, updates to the
Transportation section of the plan related to the Transportation Master Plan in 2019, and most recently an update related to the
State’s requirements for a Moderate Income Housing Plan in 2023. In addition to these more significant changes, several smaller
amendments have been made and community plans and corridor plans have been updated or rewritten in that time. The typical
lifespan of a community’s general plan can be as short as ten years but usually not longer than twenty years. Ogden’s General Plan
as a whole is now over twenty years old and discussions have been increasing over the last few years to rewrite the plan. Funding :
of nearly one million dollars for a rewrite was included in the Fiscal Year 2024 budget that includes funding for a consultant as
well as additional temporary full-time staff.
Council Deputy Director Symes used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the project to perform
comprehensive updates to the City’s General Plan. He discussed the State law requirements pertaining to a municipal General
Plan, including the elements that must be included in the document. The City has several existing community plans and corridor
plans that are part of the City’s General Plan; significant master plans also part of the General Plan and many of these master plans
have been adopted within the last 10 years, so they are not as old as the General Plan as a whole.
Senior Planner Rypien then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the scenario-based planning concept, '
which allows for consideration of community values, inclusion of stakeholders and the public, data gathering, technical analysis,
and implementation. He provided examples of planning directives from other communities that have used the scenario-based
) planning concept and highlighted Salt Lake City’s General Plan for its Depot District, which includes different scenario evaluation
tools. He used an online planning tool to illustrate how different layers can be placed on the map of Ogden City to include
different land use types and development concepts. He then discussed the urban planning model, which includes identification ofa
study area, consideration of regulatory models and development models, and a reporting dashboard. Scenario based plans work
seamlessly with the other ArcGIS apps like StoryMap, Hub, and Survey123. He presented a preferred scenario map with metrics
for Layton City; the map is an illustration of Layton’s future as shaped by the Envision Layton participants and stakeholders.
Preferred scenario maps can become a city’s future land use map. He feels this type of map is user friendly and easy to
understand. He asked for feedback from the Council regarding this type of process and concepts discussed and the feedback
provided by the Council was largely favorable.
