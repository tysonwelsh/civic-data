Mayor Hall reported on the following:

       She attended both nights ofthe Rodeo, which was a sold-out event. It was enjoyed by all.
       She recently met with the Neighborhood Plan Group Section Leaders. They meet monthly
       to discuss the City's Emergency Plan. They were preparing for a City-wide communication
       drill in September, which was National Preparedness Month.
       The Mayor recently met with several residents during Meet the Mayor.
       She and the City Engineers met with the Division ofNatural Resources("DNR"). The City
       applied for American Rescue Plan Act("ARPA")grant money for secondary meters. The
       Legislature recently passed legislation requiring all secondary water to be metered. The
       meters were expensive but the City was able to obtain funding to help deifray the costs.
       The Mayor recently attended the play Les Miserables. It was unique in that it involved all
       students. Their voices were amazing and the sets were incredible. It was the first
       performance by the lead actor who portrayed the main character, Jean Valjean.
       Congressman Burgess Owens visited Bluffdale and brought lunch for the Youth City
       Council. He spoke to them while they ate lunch and gave them great advice. The Mayor
       appreciated him taking time out ofhis busy schedule to meet with the Youth City Council.
       Congressman Owens planned to do a service project in Bluffdale City every year.
       The recent Council of Mayors("COM") Meeting took place in Taylorsville. One of the
       topics pertained to a bill that was passed regarding the homeless overflow shelter. The
       Mayors had been trying to find places for the homeless to be sheltered during the winter.
       Bluffdale City submitted a site for consideration. The shelter will be temporary and moved
       throughout the valley each year. She did not expect Bluffdale's submission to be chosen
       this year due to the distance from mass transit.
       Each year the Mountain West Chamber of Commerce holds a golf toumament for
       scholarships, which are awarded to students in the Herriman,Riverton, and Bluffdale areas.
       Mayor Hall was able to attend and met the donors who contributed to the scholarships. She
       was also able to golf with various members of City Staff.
       The Utah League of Cities and Towns("ULCT") would be holding its Legislative Policy
       Committee("LPC")Meeting to discuss upcoming potential bills.
       She traveled with City Planner/Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell to the
       Point ofthe Mountain Transportation Meeting for updates on what they are working on.
       Mayor Hall reported that she serves on the Wasatch Front Regional Council's("WFRC")
       Regional Growth Council. They were performing studies related to future growth. An
       Open House was scheduled to take place in Saratoga Springs to receive feedback and input
       on growth in that part ofthe Valley.
       The Council of Government("COG") met in West Jordan recently where they discussed
       refugees, water conservation, and other significant issues.
       The Mayor recently attended a dinner with the Governor where she met 95 other mayors.
       This was the first time in the history of the State that many mayors were able to meet. It
       was a great opportunity to collaborate with other mayors.

Mr. Bosserman discussed the referendum process and stated that it is a complicated area of the
law. An application was received on Monday that started the timer. They now have 20 days for
legal counsel to issue an opinion on whether it would be referable to the voters. For instance, if
the application is timely filed and complies with the Code. In conjunction with that, the Budget
Officer would also issue an opinion addressing the fiscal and legal impacts of repealing the law
that was passed. After those opinions are issued, everjdhing will run through the local Clerk. The
Recorder's Office will take the application, conduct a preliminary review, and issue a packet and
signature page for the sponsors of the application. The sponsors will take the page to citizens in
an effort to meet a threshold number ofrequired signatures in the City. Once those signatures are
obtained, the packet will move onto the County. The County will have 10 days to verify that the
signatures are fi*om registered voters from within the City. The packet will then come back to the
local Clerk and the Recorder's Office will verify that there are the proper number of signatures.

Mr. Bosserman reported that this was a unique circumstance where there is a property tax
referendum running up against an election. He noted that the General Election is scheduled for
November. There will likely not be a determination from the Recorder's Office by the time ballots
are required to be printed and sent out,so Utah Law requires that it be placed on the ballot whether
the sponsors meet the signature requirement or not. This was a safety precaution established in
Utah Code. If the threshold signature requirement is not met, even though it is still on the ballot,
those votes would not be counted. There would be discussions with City Staffto ensure that all of
the referendum-related deadlines are met.

Council Member Kallas asked about the threshold numbers. Mr. Bosserman explained that for a
city of 10,000 and 30,000 or a city of the fourth class, signatures fi*om 11.5% of residents are
needed. There was also a separate requirement that 75% of the voter participation areas reach
11.5%. This means that there need to be four districts that are fairly equal in size. Three of the
four districts need to meet that 11.5% amount. Mr. Reid reported that based on the maximum
timeline, it could be mid-November before the City knows whether the signatures were all
received. The intention was to move forward the timeline where possible on the part ofthe City.
It was clarified that the 11.5% needs to be made up of active voters. This was defined as residents
who have voted in one ofthe last three elections.
