CALL TO ORDER
Chair McConnehey called the meeting to order at 5:30 p.m.

BUDGET DISCUSSION
Danyce Steck presented the Mayor’s proposal for the FY 2021 Budget: 24% Revenue ($2.3
million); 44% Expense ($4.3 million); 32% Reserves ($3.2 million). She acknowledged that the
Committee had expressed a desire to alter the percentages. At the May 28, 2020 meeting, the
Committee requested discussion of overtime, outside committees, operational cost reductions,
Council contingency, and the City website.

Ms. Steck presented FY 2021 Budget expenses reduced by Staff since the May 28 meeting in the
categories of ‘overtime’ and ‘operational cost reductions’ totaling $342,552, and reported that
$50,000 for Police Department training was restored to the Budget.

    • Outside Committees
Ms. Steck stated the Arts Council was allocated $66,150 in the FY 2021 Tentative Budget.
Committee Member Whitelock emphasized that every City department was asked to accept budget
cuts, and stated that the Arts Council needed to be approached about reductions before a decision
was made. Committee Members Worthen and Whitelock agreed that all outside committees should
be approached about budget reductions.

Committee Member Lamb commented that cutting relatively small amounts from outside
committees would not result in the substantial reductions needed. Committee Member Green
responded that if other departments were accepting cuts, outside committees also needed to accept
reductions. Committee Member Whitelock suggested requesting each of the outside committees
to submit a budget with a 5-10% reduction. Ms. Steck agreed to reach out to outside committees
with that request.

   • Council Contingency
Committee Member Whitelock said she would be willing to cut the attorney expense from the
Council Contingency Budget if the City Attorney’s Office agreed to assign an attorney to the City
Council.

Chair McConnehey said he would prefer for the Council Contingency Budget to increase to
maintain ability for Council control. He suggested moving economic development funds to the
Council Contingency Budget to ensure Council collaboration. Ms. Steck responded that the
Council maintained control with approval of budget amendments. Chair McConnehey repeated
that he would prefer the Council to remain engaged in economic development.

Committee Member Worthen said she believed the Council Committee should consider a 10%
budget reduction just like other departments. Committee Member Green said he would like to see
a list of unfunded budget requests that could be funded if money became available. He said he
believed the Council Committee needed an operational contingency to allocate to operational
needs as they arise. Ms. Steck commented that contingency funds were already coming from
reserves for the FY 2021 Budget. She said departments did not submit lists of unfunded requests
for FY 2021 because of the direction to cut their budgets.

Committee Member Lamb agreed with a suggestion made by Korban Lee to shift a position from
the City Recorder Office to the City Council Office to provide support, and reduce the Council
Contingency Budget by half, since a portion of the funds were included in the Contingency Budget
to pay for Council Office support. Committee Member Worthen agreed. Committee Member
Whitelock suggested a closed session for continued discussion. Committee Members Whitelock
and Worthen repeated the belief that Council Contingency Budget should be reduced.

Four Committee Members indicated approval for reducing the Council Contingency Budget from
$150,000 to $100,000. Committee Member Pack said he would prefer to define Council Office
staffing before deciding how much to cut from the Contingency Budget. Korban Lee suggested
the Committee cut the Council Contingency Budget by 10% to show solidarity with other
departments, with further discussion about staffing at a future meeting.

Committee Member Green suggested cutting the Council travel budget by 10%. The Committee
unanimously agreed with cutting the Council Contingency and the travel budget both by 10%.

    • City Website
Ms. Steck explained the need for an improved City website, and requested the Council approve a
one-time budget expense for the City website out of the IT Fund. The cost was estimated to be
$100,000-$120,000; the FY 2021 Tentative Budget set the allocated amount at $150,000 to provide
contingency. Committee Member Pack said he assumed an improved website would save the City
money in reduced staff time. Ms. Steck agreed.
Committee Member Green said $120,000 seemed like too much. He emphasized that change
orders to increase the cost should not be allowed. Chair McConnehey said it would be nice to be
able to quantify the benefit that such a significant expenditure on the City website would bring.
Mr. Lee said he would put together an operational analysis of what a new website would provide.
Chair McConnehey commented that the Committee could use better agenda management
software in addition to a better website.
A majority of the Committee indicated support for the expense reductions proposed by Ms.
Steck. A majority of the Committee indicated support for the department budgets as proposed.

Mr. Lee commented that a utility transfer would strengthen the franchise tax portion of the
General Fund revenue base. Ms. Steck explained that rates for sewer, storm water, and solid
waste utilities would all be increased by 5%. She stated the utility transfer would, in essence, be
rent for using City-owned property to provide services in the right-of-way.

Committee Member Jacob said he believed it was important for the public to fully understand the
proposed utility transfer. The Committee and Staff discussed possible public education efforts.

Committee Member Jacob said he would like the City to adopt an ongoing policy to hold the
property tax rate stable, with a truth-in-taxation hearing when necessary. Committee Member
Green said he did not disagree philosophically, but pointed out that the utility transfer in FY
2021 would result in savings for a majority of utility users. Given the uncertain economic times,
he said he was not in favor of a property tax increase in FY 2021.

Committee Member Worthen stated she was in favor of a utility transfer and not a property tax
increase in FY 2021. She left the meeting at 7:35 p.m. Committee Member Whitelock said she
would not support a property tax increase in FY 2021.

Chair McConnehey said he was not in favor of a utility transfer, and would prefer a property tax
increase in FY 2021. He commented that the utility transfer would affect tax exempt entities,
said he believed they were designated tax exempt for a reason, and said he felt the City should
not try to circumvent the tax exempt status with a utility transfer. Acknowledging that a majority
of the Committee appeared to support a utility transfer, Chair McConnehey said he did like that

       Four Committee Members indicated support for a utility transfer and not a property tax increase

       HIGHLANDS SPECIAL ASSESSMENT AREA
       For the Highlands Special Assessment Area, Committee Member Green proposed:

          •   Keeping the rates as they stand today (remove the proposed increase from the Tentative
              Budget); and
          •   Prepare a report to evaluate the current level of service, financial needs of the district and
              any other relevant elements. The report should include:
                 o Brief History
                 o Service Provided
                 o Historical Revenues/Expenses
                 o Financial Projections
                 o Any other pertinent information

       A majority of the Committee indicated support for the proposed direction to Staff.

       ADJOURN
       At 7:57 p.m., Committee Member Whitelock moved to adjourn the meeting. Committee
       Member Green seconded the motion, which passed by unanimous vote (6-0).
