36          Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District Presentation
37
38   Sherrie Ohrn, Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District Board of Trustees, presented
39   information about the water supply and other water issues. This presentation is included in the
40   packet.
41
42   Ms. Ohrn explained that to recover from the drought, Utah needs another year like this year.
43   She talked about the importance of water in the Great Salt Lake. She urged citizens to continue
44   conservation efforts.
45
46   Ms. Ohrn said there was potential legislation that would take away taxing from the Jordan Valley
47   Water Conservancy District and said this would have a large financial impact on the district.
48   This legislation would affect the bond rate for the District and this would in turn affect
49   conservation grants and efforts. Instead of passing the bill, the legislature decided to do a study
50   for the next couple of years to analyze all of the data and how the bill would affect different
51   agencies.

33             Discussion of Lot Coverage Standards
34
35   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, said there is a current ordinance that states
36   that the maximum coverage is 30%. The maximum coverage refers to “buildings,” meaning it
37   has a roof supported by columns and walls. The 30% lot coverage maximum has been a
38   concern for residents because of the reduction in lot sizes. The reduction in lot sizes has led to
39   a trend towards larger homes and additions to homes which causes concerns for residents
40   when they aren’t able to build what they would like to build. He said that swimming pools are
41   considered part of the 30%. He asked the Council if they would like to change the maximum
42   coverage to 40% and exclude swimming pools and pavement.
43
44   Councilmember McCay said she errors on the side of personal property rights and would like to
45   increase it. She suggested a maximum lot coverage of 45%.
46
47   Councilmember Pierucci said that he also supports personal property rights. He asked what
48   percentage of the City is part of a special district that would not be included in this standard. Mr.
49   Lethbridge estimated that about 25% of the City is in a special district, mostly on the western
50   end of the City. Councilmember Pierucci supports at least 40% on the maximum lot coverage.

26             Ordinance No. 23-09 – Dissolving the “Redwood South Neighborhood
27             Redevelopment Project Area”
28
29   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the Riverton City
30   Redevelopment Agency in 1997 voted to create the "Redwood Road South Neighborhood
31   Redevelopment Project Area", which encompassed areas of the Riverton City downtown from
32   12600 South along Redwood Road. The area's purpose was to support and facilitate
33   development and redevelopment within that project area. All such project areas have a defined
34   timespan, and this project area expired in 2017, though the project area itself remains in place
35   until formally dissolved. There are no remaining contractual obligations associated with the
36   project area, nor is there any outstanding indebtedness connected with it. The Riverton City
37   Redevelopment Agency adopted a resolution dissolving the project area, which is required by
38   State Code to be implemented by ordinance by the Riverton City Council.
39
40   Mayor Pro Tem McDougal called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public
41   Hearing.
42
43   Councilmember Pierucci MOVED that the Riverton City Council adopt Ordinance No. 23-09
44   – Dissolving the “Redwood South Neighborhood Redevelopment Project Area.”
45   Councilmember McCay SECONDED the motion. Mayor Pro Tem McDougal called for
46   discussion on the motion. There being none, he called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as
47   follows: McCay-yes, McDougal-yes, Pierucci-yes and Wells-yes. The motion passed
48   unanimously.

50             Ordinance No. 23-07 – Residential Chicken Ordinance, Adopting Amendments to
51             the Residential Chicken Ordinance
     Page | 7 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     April 4, 2023




 1   Mr. Lethbridge explained that the Riverton City ordinance currently allows up to 6 residential
 2   chickens to be kept on residential property of 10,000-square feet or larger. 10,000 square feet is
 3   ¼ acre. Based on recent requests by residents and inquiries by the City Council. Staff has
 4   reviewed various approaches to allowing chickens on lots below 10,000 square feet. One
 5   method was a ‘sliding scale”, used in Herriman and other communities. The sliding scale allows
 6   for keeping a progressively smaller number of residential chickens in lots of different sizes. The
 7   zoning results in residents of a 5,000-7,999 square foot lot being allowed to keep four (4)
 8   chickens, and residents of an 8,000-10,000 square foot lot are allowed to keep six (6).
 9
10   Mayor Pro Tem McDougal called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public
11   Hearing.
12
13   Councilmember Pierucci MOVED that the Riverton City Council adopt Ordinance No. 23-07
14   – Amending 18.135.090 Animal and Fowl created a sliding scale regulating the number of
15   allowed residential chickens by lot size and amending the required minimum distance for
16   keeping chickens as described in Exhibit A. Councilmember Wells SECONDED the motion.
17   Mayor Pro Tem McDougal called for discussion on the motion. There being none, he called for
18   a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: McCay-yes, McDougal-yes, Pierucci-yes and Wells-
19   yes. The motion passed unanimously.
