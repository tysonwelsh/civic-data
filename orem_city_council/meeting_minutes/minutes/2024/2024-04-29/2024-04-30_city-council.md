WELCOME AND INTRODUCTION

Mayor Young welcomed those present. He asked those at the table to introduce
themselves. Those in attendance are members of the ASD administration, ASD School Board,
Orem City Council, and the Orem City Manager. The City Council and ASD representatives
chose to have public comment after the discussion and only if time permits.

SAFETY ISSUES

Ms. Wilson started this discussion by thanking the City of Orem for their coordination
with Orem Elementary during construction of the water tank. She spoke about concerns that
were shared with her at the intersection of 400 South and 600 West. During construction, that
intersection became a four way stop. There have been requests to keep that intersection a four

way stop.

Ms. Hacken asked for an update on plans for how to slow down traffic on 1600 N near
the Northridge Elementary school, as well as Windsor Elementary. Mr. John Dorny, Traffic
Engineer, spoke about Northridge and Cascade Elementary Schools. He reported that at Cascade
safety ballards have been added, there will be adjustments made to the timing of the hawk
signal, unused bus parking will be removed to help with parent pick up, and next year two
crossing guards will be added. The moving of the hawk signal will be phase 2, The hawk signal
will be removed if a signal is put in. In Mr. Dorney’s discussion with the school there have been
many ideas generated, including color coded pick up times, For the Northridge Elementary, Mr.
Dorny met with them and gave them a variety of options, including narrower striping lanes,
removal of parking in key locations, and use of a possible undeveloped land. Mr. Taylor Forbush,
Traffic Operations Section Manager, addressed the possible 4 way stop near Orem Elementary.
The plan is to conduct a traffic study once the construction is complete.

Ms. Millett asked Mr. Dorny to speak about the area near Bonneville Elementary. He
reported that at 1200 West and 1360 North, west of Bonneville Elementary, that the sidewalk
ends southbound on 1200 West. The City is designing a high visibility crosswalk with added bike
lanes. Mayor Young spoke about the creation of the ‘tiger’ traffic team in Orem to address traffic
issues.

RECOMMENDATIONS OF SPLIT OPTIONS

Superintendent Farnsworth reported that recommendations from the board are option 3, a
two way split of the district and option 4, a three way split. These two options will be further
studied prior to presenting to Utah County. The public hearing meetings are scheduled for June
11 and June 25. Then the board has scheduled a July 12 meeting to determine what, if any,

propositions to put on the ballot. He reported that there are other cities who have entered into
interlocal agreements to start that same process ASD is doing. Orem, Lindon, Pleasant Grove,
and Vineyard are the only four cities that do not have an interlocal agreement.

Ms. Wilson was hoping that interlocal agreements would not be signed and that the
district, as a whole, would vote on the two proposed options by ASD. ASD plans to put forth
their recommended district splits. The interlocal agreements only allow citizens in the cities that
signed the agreement to vote on that type of split. Mr. Farnsworth explained that there are
statutory guidelines/laws with respect to competing initiatives. He expects the county to clarify if
they allow competing initiatives, and if so, what happens if they both pass. Mayor has heard that
if there is a conflict, then the legislature would likely create a priority at that point. Mr. Earl, City
Attorney, explained how voting works when cities have an interlocal agreement versus a district
initiated split. Ms. Millett is in favor of an interlocal agreement that allows cities in those areas to
vote on the split, instead of the district as a whole determining the split.

Ms. Hacken pointed out that ASD employed MGT to do a feasibility study. As part of
that study, public input was requested and obtained. In every cluster, the majority of the people
that responded wanted option 1. Option 1 was not to split the district. Those same people wanted
to see the school district split on the ballot for voters. There is support to stay together and

support for taking a look at the options of a split. Mr. Macdonald is concerned that the ballot
could become confusing for the voters. Ms. Hacken clarified that the 45 days to study is more for
citizens to study the information and give input, ASD has already run the study. Ms. Bateman
said the study included maintenance and operations numbers. The further study brings in the
capital planning, property owned, new builds needed, seismic concerns, and renovations. Mr.
Sundberg explained this is an internal study concerning capital plans. Mr. Farnsworth said this
will likely be the capital plan that is annually updated divided into the different areas. Ms. Millett
and Mayor Young would like to see more information given to the public including conditions of
current schools and more than a snapshot of maintenance and operations costs.

Mr. Lambson asked ASD what the public engagement process looks like going forward.
Mr. Farnsworth assured the group that they will fulfill the statutory requirements, they will have
the disclosure of the boundaries, the capital maintenance and operation numbers and all of the
variables that are required. They will provide additional information for the public so they can
look at what those options would mean for them. Then there will be opportunities for feedback
with two public hearings. The plan is to articulate the complete feedback process on May 14.

Mayor Young echoed Mr. Macdonald’s concern that the ballot may be confusing to
voters. Mr. Farnsworth believes that the county should give some clarification on this. Others
have the same concern.

Mr. Spencer asked the board members that represent cities that signed interlocal
agreements, the reason they chose to sign. Ms. King expressed that the cities were concerned
about representation, the need to build schools faster, the need to have bonds pass, and a level of
disconnect. Ms. Bateman similarly reported that representation was important for her cities.
Other concerns include a two way split, the failure of the bonds in a larger district, differing
priorities for differing geographic areas, and the inability to come to a consensus in a large
district. Ms. King brought up that interlocals require more public hearings that allows for more
public engagement. Ms. Beeson explained that the American Fork area bleeds into the Lehi
boundaries and that joining that interlocal made sense for their community. She says the mayors
and council members in her area take comfort that they are taking part in the process together.
Ms. Lincoln spoke of the difficulty of living in an area that is growing so fast that needs can not
be met. The failure of the bond was difficult for her area.

Mayor Young asked the board members to speak to why the City of Orem should or
should not do an interlocal agreement. Ms. Wilson believes a three way split is the most
disruptive, the most expensive and the most disadvantageous to the students. Ms. Bateman thinks
the biggest reason for an interlocal is that each area would have a say on their area, the reason
not to do an interlocal is to allow citizens to vote on the options put forth by the school district.
Ms. King said that an interlocal will only work if it is collaborative. Mr. Clement reiterated that
interlocal will allow only your community to vote and no interlocal allows for a district wide
vote. City council members discussed pros and cons of an interlocal agreement. Ms. Hacken
explained that a split is a 3 year process.

WINDSOR ELEMENTARY

Mayor Young spoke about the three schools that have been shut down. He is concerned
about the possibility of Windsor being closed. He would like to see that school stay open during
this process. Ms. Wilson confirmed that there is a pause on closing schools or creating magnet
schools until the 2025-26 school year. This will allow for the school district reconfiguration to
play out.

DUAL LANGUAGE IMMERSION (DLI) MAGNET SCHOOL AT ORCHARD
ELEMENTARY

Mr. Spencer is concerned that kids that live in the area of Orchard Elementary will not be
able to attend the local school if they are not in the DLI program. They will need to be sent to a
school further away. Ms. Millett is concerned that the DLI magnet school could force the closure
of Windsor by removing their DLI program. Ms. Wilson spoke with the principal at Orchard
Elementary and she is in support of the DLI program. Ms. Hacken brought up the challenge of
DLI classes becoming smaller with the older classes. She spoke about reduced student
enrollment in some schools and the problems that come from low enrollment. The Windsor
Elementary area would like to see Windsor rebuilt and then Northridge students could come to
the new school. The Northridge community is not sure that is what they want. She explained
that many discussions need to happen, but first the reconfiguration needs to be solved.
