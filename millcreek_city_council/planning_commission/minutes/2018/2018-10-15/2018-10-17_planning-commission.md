1.1 Consideration of SD-18-012, Sidewalk Exception Request for a One-lot
Subdivision Location: 2138 E. Millstream Lane Applicant: Emily Bullock Planner:
Robert May
Robert May said the item was a two-part application; a preliminary subdivision plat and
sidewalk exception. The property is on a corner and consists of about 8,700 square feet |
and the subject parcel of 5,000 square feet is a remainder piece of land due to the
introduction of Millstream Lane in 1953. The second part of the request involves the
requirement of sidewalk with developments within the City. Residents do have the right
to request an exception and bond for it. Although Millcreek Code requires these
improvements for all developments, it is understood by Millcreek staff that the
installation of all improvements at the time the development occurs is not necessary.
Sidewalk is mostly absent from Millstream Lane and other streets in close proximity. The
lot complies with the minimum area and width requirements in the zone and will consist
of a single-family dwelling. Staff recommended a deferral agreement between Millcreek

and the property owner by recording it with the subject property that runs with the land in
perpetuity. May said he received one phone call about the property and he explained the
zoning requirements. The recorded deferral agreement would place the responsibility on
the property owner that when the installation of sidewalk becomes required, the owner of
the property would be responsible for the costs of the sidewalk improvements and related
costs. Staff found that the proposed subdivision and requested sidewalk exception should
be approved.
Commissioner Mumford asked who pays for the sidewalk in the deferral agreement. May
said whomever is the current property owner. Commissioner Mumford said he drove
through the area and there is a development on the next street that has random sidewalks
throughout. May said he does not know when sidewalk will be installed on the street but
at least a deferral agreement will account for it later. Commissioner Mumford said he
would be ok with the exception if the property was in the middle of the street, but this is
different safety-wise because it is on a corner. May said he is not the expert, he is basing
the recommendation off of engineering’s input.
Chair Stephens opened the public hearing.
There were no comments.
Chair Stephens closed the public hearing.
Commissioner Allen said the deferral concept creates a domino effect. As a father, he
enjoys utilizing sidewalks for safety concerns and is not in favor of the deferral.
Commissioner Carlson said he lives in this neighborhood and believes that neighbors like
the character of the neighborhood. He said putting in a section of sidewalk all by itself
does not make sense and it is unsightly. Commissioner Booth said if this were a
transitional property between neighborhoods then he would like it, but it is an old and
established neighborhood and the partial sidewalk would be disruptive. Commissioner
Allen said the rule is that every subdivision requires a sidewalk and if there should be an
exception then there should be something exceptional about the subject property.
Commissioner Mumford said it makes sense to start sidewalk on a corner than in the
center of the street. Commissioner Healey said he is in favor of the subdivision and the
exception and putting in partial sidewalk would not look aesthetically pleasing.
Commissioner Carlson, in connection with application SD-18-012, moved to grant the
request for a One-lot subdivision at 2138 E. Millstream Lane. Commissioner LaMar
seconded. Chair Stephens called for the vote. The motion passed unanimously.
Commissioner LaMar said perpetuity seems unfair for future property owners. He recommended
a time limit on the deferral.
Commissioner Healey, on SD-18-012, moved to make a recommendation to the Mayor to

1.2 Consideration of CU-18-017, Conditional Use Permit for an 8-unit Mixed Use
Multifamily Development Location: 2780 E. 3300 S. Applicant: Joe Johnsen
Planner: Erin O’ Kelley
Erin O’Kelley said the applicant previously rezoned the property to C-2 with a
development agreement. There was a lot of resident feedback prior to the rezone, so staff
worked on the detrimental effects. She read through the approved development
agreement. She said she has not received any neighborhood feedback since the
development agreement was approved. The Millcreek Community Council unanimously
recommended approval. Staff recommended approval of the conditional use permit
subject to the following conditions:
1. The project will comply with all conditions in the development agreement.
2. Prior to receiving final conditional use approval, the applicant shall certify that
existing utility services have the capacity to accommodate additional demand induced
by the development, or that the services will be upgraded to meet the additional
demand. This includes fire safety issues, any issues reviewed by the fire official will be
addressed during technical review.
3. Applicant will seek separate building permits for all required structures and signs
according to Millcreek Code.
4. The applicant shall obtain final approval from Salt Lake County’s Traffic Engineer
that all anticipated traffic impacts or concerns of the proposed development have been
mitigated.
5. The applicant shall provide sufficient parking-lot light illumination. No light source
(light bulb, fluorescent tube, or other direct source of light used to illuminate a parking
area) shall be visible beyond the property line including the alley (ROW) of any off-
street parking area as required by Section 19.80.030 of the zoning ordinance.
6. Any modifications to these approved plans must be approved by the Planning
Department, in writing, prior to the changes being implemented in the field.

1.3 Consideration of CU-18-016, Conditional Use Permit for an 18-unit Mixed Use
Townhome Development Location: 3672 S. 900 E. Applicant: Lotus Klass, LLC
Planner: Blaine Gehring
Blaine Gehring said the C-2 zone allows for a mixed use development as a conditional
use. The development is for eighteen 3-story townhomes with two-bedroom apartments
and a two-car garage. The property is currently occupied by Utah Water Gardens.
Gehring went through the site plan, landscaping plan, and building elevations. The

Millcreek Planning Commission Meeting Minutes 17 October 2018 Page 5 of 9
building is contemporary and will exceed the minimum depth and frontage requirements
in the zone. The site has a 40% open space requirement and the developers are doing
41% as well as putting in 70 parking stalls though only 42 are required for the density.
The Millcreek Community Council recommended approval though there was concern
that there was not enough guest parking. The parking includes two in the garage, two on
driveways of thirteen units, and eight guest parking stalls. Planning staff recommended
approval of the conditional use permit subject to the following conditions:

e The applicant shall provide a project parking ratio of 2 stalls per residential unit,
plus 0.33 stalls of visitor parking, for a minimum total of 42 parking stalls.

e The applicant shall provide the proposed exterior detailing/finishes as provided
for in the application drawings.

e The development will be limited to the eighteen two-bedroom units and one
commercial office space as proposed on the plan.

e The front yard is 25’, the side yards are to be 8', 14.5’ and 15’ and the rear yard is
25".

e The proposed building height is no more than 35’.

e All service and mechanical equipment must be screened by landscaping or other
methods as set forth in section 19.77.070 of the zoning ordinance.

e Signs will require separate building permits and staff review for zoning/land use
compliance. No light source (light bulb, fluorescent tube, or other direct source of
light used to illuminate a parking area) shall be visible beyond the property line of
any off-street parking area as required by Section 19.80.030 of the zoning
ordinance.

e Any modifications to these approved plans must be approved by the Planning
Department, in writing, prior to the changes being implemented in the field.

e Landscaping shall be installed in compliance with Chapter 19.77 Water Efficient
Landscape Design and Development Standards. Any revisions to the landscape
plan package shall be reviewed and approved in writing by the director or
designee prior to commencement of construction. Re-certification of compliance
with the requirements of the landscaping ordinance shall be provided by the
qualified professionals who prepared and submitted the plan revisions.

e The applicant will comply with all requirements established through the technical
review process prior to receiving final conditional use approval.

e The applicant will comply with all requirements of the Unified Fire Code, subject
to approval by the Fire Marshal.

e The applicant shall install “No Parking” signs in the fire access lane.

e The applicant shall obtain a complete review for an approved building permit and
shall continually comply with the requirements of Millcreek City, Salt Lake City |
Department of Public Utilities, and the S. L. City Suburban Sewer District.

e The applicant and its successors shall properly and continually maintain all
required landscaping, fencing, buildings, and roads.

e All items of the staff report.

1.4 Consideration of ZM-18-011, Rezone from R-2-10 to R-1-21 Location: 2254 Alva

Circle Applicant: Laurel Osborn Planner: Blaine Gehring

Blaine Gehring said the applicants are seeking to rezone two parcels to do a boundary

line adjustment for their lots. The two parcels had duplexes on them which have been

demolished. In order for a lot line adjustment to occur to reconfigure and enlarge their

lots and not have a split zoning, the two former duplex lots need to be rezoned to R-1-21

to match the zoning of the current lots. The East Millcreek Community Council

unanimously recommended approval of the rezone. Planning staff recommended

approval of the rezone.

Commissioner Allen asked if the boundary line adjustment would come before the
Planning Commission. Gehring said no, staff will do that. Commissioner Booth asked

about the R-1-21 zone. Gehring said they would be half acre lots, not multi-family. He

said there were two emails received from the public, one in support and one against. The

property currently has farm animals and the resident’s comment that was against said

they did not want any more animals on the property. Commissioner Allen asked if

increasing the size of the lot increased the number of animals allowed. Gehring said it

would if they amended the conditional use permit. Commissioner LaMar asked if the

conditional use would stay with the land if it gets rezoned. Gehring said yes, the animals

come before the Planning Commission.

Laurel Osborn said the new property is for a yard expansion. She said the conditional use

three types of animals at a time and there are limits on the types. Commissioner Allen
