6:00 PM WORK MEETING (Fort Herriman Conference Room)

Chair Andy Powell called the meeting to order at 6:03 p.m.

1.   Commission Business

     1.1. Review of City Council Decisions – Michael Maloy, Planning Director

Planning Manager Spencer provided an overview of the discussions at the City Council meeting. He
mentioned a concept that Commissioner Rypien and his partner have been working on, referred to
as the "missing commercial" or "micro flex" space. This concept aims to provide affordable, small-
scale commercial spaces for businesses transitioning from home-based operations to brick-and-
mortar establishments.

Commissioner Ferguson arrived at 6:09 p.m.

He explained that these spaces would resemble warehouses with offices and potential storefronts.
He noted that there are limited areas of commercial and manufacturing space left in the city, and
they have identified properties that might be suitable for this type of development.

Commissioner Rypien elaborated on the concept, describing it as a "maker space" or "hobby cave"
that would fill a gap in the market for businesses looking to expand beyond home occupations. He
mentioned that similar spaces in other areas have long-term leases and are in high demand.

Commissioner Garcia compared the concept to developments near Slackwater, noting the retail-like
frontage. Commissioner Anderson mentioned a similar "innovation center" concept at the Mountain
View Village.

Planning Manager Spencer stated that they would be working on an ordinance to address concerns
such as permitted uses, architectural recommendations, and reduced setbacks.

    1.2. Review of Agenda Items – Planning Staff

Planner Hoadley mentioned that for item 4.1, one of the conditions of approval regarding the rock
wall had already been addressed in plans received that day.

For item 4.2, Chair Powell noted that the preliminary plan for a 3-lot commercial subdivision
appeared straightforward.

Planning Manager Spencer explained for item 4.3 he would be including an additional provision
requiring fencing to be at least vinyl or better quality, due to the visibility of the site from Porter
Rockwell Boulevard and the future Mountain View corridor. The Main Street Meats development
includes a large freezer as part of its 16,500 square foot building, which is utilized for the storage of
frozen meat. This facility primarily focuses on warehousing, packaging, and distributing frozen meat
products. The frozen meat arrives at the facility in boxed form, with the business ensuring a clean
and efficient process through daily USDA inspections.

    1.3. Review and discuss Land Development Codes, Standards, Policies, and best practices for
         Herriman City – Michael Maloy, Planning Director

Planning Director Maloy discussed upcoming conferences and training opportunities for
commission members. He mentioned a few possible events. He offered to send out emails with more
information and asked interested commissioners to let him know if they would like to attend.

He also mentioned a recent discussion with Commissioner Anderson about safe walking routes and
pedestrian safety concerns in the community. He suggested inviting someone from the engineering
team to discuss safe walk route regulations and the city's work with school districts on this issue in
a future meeting.

A training video was shown with discussion following.

2. Adjournment

Commissioner Garcia moved to adjourn the meeting at 6:58 p.m. Seconded by Commissioner
Ferguson and all voted aye.

   4.1. Review and consider a Conditional Use Permit request for the South Hills Dual Pump
        Station, located at 15320 Mountain View Corridor R-2-15 Residential Zone.
        Applicant: Jonathan Bowers, Herriman City (authorized agent)
        Acres: ±3.95
        File No: C2025-010

   https://www.herriman.gov/uploads/files/5264/Item-4.1-Staff-Report-C2025-010.pdf

Planner Hoadley presented the proposal for a conditional use permit for the South Hills Dual Pump
Station. She explained that the project is located in the southern part of the city, across from
Academy Park. The site plan includes an access road leading to a pump station, which will go around
an existing tank. Landscaping will be provided along the road to help shield the building and vehicles.

The proposed building will be relatively short and shielded by an 8-foot-tall precast wall. The Zoning
and engineering departments did not have any major issues with the project. She noted that the
third recommendation in the staff report had already been addressed by the applicant, who
submitted new plans showing the rock wall continuing to the end of the residential use.

Public Utilities Engineering Manager Bowers provided additional information. He clarified that the
March 19, 2025 Planning Commission Meeting Minutes                                     Page 4 of 7

pump station would be adjacent to residential uses, approximately 20 feet away. He assured the
commission that significant sound attenuation would be included in the project. He also mentioned
that while the adjacent residential subdivision layout was not yet approved, they anticipated it
would be close to the pump station.

Commissioner Oberg asked about access to the area, particularly concerning potential safety issues
with children retrieving stray balls. Engineering Manager Bowers confirmed that the area would be
fenced around the perimeter except for the frontage, which would have a precast wall. He stated
that access would only be along the maintenance road.

Commissioner Rypien inquired about the funding for the improvements. Engineering Manager
Bowers explained that the costs specific to the pump station and site would be reimbursed by the
city but fronted by the developer.

Commissioner Ferguson moved to approve item 4.1 Review and consider a Conditional Use Permit
request for the South Hills Dual Pump Station, located at 15320 Mountain View Corridor R-2-15
Residential Zone. With staff’s recommendations 1. Applicant shall acknowledge receipt of and
agree with all recommendations from the city. 2. The parcel for the pump station shall be
included and approved in a plat associated with the development. Either Panorama Plat A, or
Panorama Backbone Roadways Plat. 3. Extend the proposed eight (8) foot tall masonry fence on
the west property line to the north property line, following City code in regards to height within
the front yard. Any lighting in the project be directed away from adjacent residential
development.

Commissioner Garcia seconded the motion.

        Applicant: Larry Myler, Herriman 73 Partners LLC (property owner)
        Acres: ±5.803
        File No: S2024-064

Planner Hoadley presented the preliminary plat for a 3-lot commercial subdivision located in the
northern portion of the city off of 12600 South. She noted that soil contamination from a previous
development had been mitigated across the entire site. The plat shows 3 lots with one road
providing access, and an easement between lots 3 and 4 connecting to an easement between lots 1
and 2.

She stated that the proposed plat meets zoning and engineering requirements. The staff
recommendations included asking for a landscape plan, which may come in as individual site plans
are submitted.

Brandon Hansen, representing the applicant, was present to answer questions.

Chair Powell opened the public hearing.

David Thompson, a resident whose townhome backs up to the property, inquired about the planned
structures and facilities for the site.

Commissioner Garcia moved to close the public hearing, Seconded by Commissioner Oberg and all
voted Aye.

Applicant Brandon Hansen provided additional information. He explained that the property is zoned
C-2 and that a commercial company is under contract to create buildings similar to what is already
in the area single-story structures. He noted that they don't have anyone under contract for the
remaining portion of the property at this time.

Commissioner Garcia moved to approve item 4.2 Consideration of a Preliminary Plat for a three
(3) lot commercial subdivision known as the Garden Plaza Subdivision, Plat 3, located generally
at 12500 S Herriman Main St in the C-2 Commercial Zone. With staff’s recommendations 1.
Receive and agree to the remaining corrections from all City departments. 2. Provide a landscape
plan for all street right of ways and park strips which complies with City ordinance. This can be
provided at time of Site Plan/Conditional Use permit application.

Commissioner Rypien seconded the motion.

   4.3. Consideration of approval for a Conditional Use for Main Street Meats at 2342 W Porter
        Point Cove in the M-1 Manufacturing Zone.

        Applicant: Jon Galbraith w/ Element Design Collective (Authorized agent)
        Acres: ±2.53
        File Number: C2024-150

   https://www.herriman.gov/uploads/files/5265/Item-4.3-Staff-Report-C2024-150.pdf

Planning Manager Spencer presented the application for a conditional use permit for Main Street
Meats, located off Porter Rockwell Boulevard in the southeast part of the city. The proposal is for a
16,500 square foot, 40-foot tall building for frozen meat warehousing, packaging, and distribution.
The site plan includes 62 parking stalls and 34% landscaping.

He noted that staff were recommending the privacy fence be at least vinyl or better, rather than the
chain link with slats allowed by ordinance, due to the visibility and architectural nature of the area.
He also mentioned some landscaping shortfalls that needed to be addressed, including the need for a
landscape architect-stamped plan and ensuring 50% live growth coverage in certain areas.

Applicant Richard Faddis, representing Main Street Meats, expressed appreciation for the city's
cooperation and excitement about coming to Herriman. He confirmed that the vinyl fence
requirement was not a concern. He explained that the retail component would be small, with about
12 feet of counter inside, primarily serving walk-in customers. He clarified that their main business
would be serving restaurants and hotels.

In response to questions, Richard explained that the meat comes to the warehouse in boxes, with
very little mess. He emphasized that the operation is clean, with third-party inspections and daily
USDA oversight.

Commissioner Rypien moved to approve item 4.3 Consideration of approval for a Conditional
Use for Main Street Meats at 2342 W Porter Point Cove in the M-1 Manufacturing Zone. With
staff’s recommendation 1. Comply with department requirements. 2. Comply with Landscaping
requirements. A. LA Stamp B. 50% live growth C. Along Porter Rockwell Blvd (work w/ UDOT).
3. Different texture/color/material for ped crossing. 4. Screening fencing not be chain link;
vinyl or better.

Commissioner Fenn seconded the motion.
