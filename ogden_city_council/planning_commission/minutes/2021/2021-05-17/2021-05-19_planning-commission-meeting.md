Special Meeting
1. Revised CBD Building Exterior, for mixed use building at 144 Historic 25th Street in the CBDI zone.
    (Attachment 1)

    Mr. Montgomery stated the Commission had reviewed the façade plan for this building in April at which
    time the parking requirement was waived. Since that time, the developer has been working with Ogden
    City to have the ability to use parking owned by the RDA, and the City has worked out a development
    agreement to allow the use of shared parking if the applicant would be willing to change the building
    material on the upper floors to be more consistent with the historic character of the street and with
    Ogden’s downtown area. A new façade has now been submitted which shows a continuation of the
    brick appearance on the upper floors as well as a continuation of the window pattern on the two upper
    floors. Pilasters are proposed to break up the mass of the structure and Staff feels the design is more
    compatible with both the Historic District and the Make Ogden Plan. Staff recommendation is for
    approval, subject to the development agreement being approved. He also commented the Landmarks
    Commission Technical Review Committee has reviewed the new façade and has approved it and that
    approval will be scheduled to be ratified at the next Commission meeting.

    Mr. Stratford clarified the previous approval would remain valid in the event the development
    agreement is not fully executed.

    Commissioner Schade commented the proposal is now a better design than the previous design and

Work Session
1.  Discussion Items:
   a. Parking Study and suggested Ordinance changes
      Mr. Montgomery stated a downtown parking study has recently been completed as a partnership
      with Ogden City and Wasatch Front Regional Council. The intent is for the study to be used state-
      wide with a toolbox of ideas to be prepared. This study included both Ogden City and South Salt Lake
      City. Over the past twenty years, Staff has periodically conducted counts of parked vehicles in the
      downtown, and this information was made available to the consultant. The study was completed in
      January 2020 and information is prior to the COVID shut-down, so it is anticipated the information is
      accurate. He stated there are three types of lots in the downtown: free public parking, private paid
      parking lots and some regulated by government such as Federal and State agencies. Some areas of
      on-street parking have time limitations while others do not. The counts indicate most lots are
      typically below capacity with 40%-50% occupied, with more than 85% of on-street parking utilized.

     Counts were taken at various time periods, early morning, daytime and early evening. He further
     reviewed the findings of the Study and asked Commissioners to consider whether parking is a good
     utilization of land in the downtown. He suggested consideration might be given to reducing the
     parking requirement for commercial buildings as these often appear to be mostly empty. Curbside
     parking also may be mis-used due to lack of enforcement of time limits. Some areas of curbside
     parking are underused while others appear always to be full. The study suggests paid parking may
     be warranted where parking is now being over-utilized. Other lots in the downtown are not monitored
     and not well managed. Discussion continued relating to the effectiveness of existing parking lots
     and the ability of users to access businesses as well as mis-use of public parking spaces by
     businesses with the installation of A-frame signs identifying use for pick-up only. The perception of
     safety is an ongoing concern with the use of parking within parking structures and the need for
     parking to be inviting and convenient. Commission discussed changing trends as well as whether
     paid parking might create revenue for upkeep of existing parking. The City is researching technology
     as well as monitoring what is occurring in other areas to better regulate parking.

b. East Central zoning provision on painted brick.
   Mr. Montgomery stated recent trends have been to update homes by painting brick, with owners having the
   perception it increases the curb value and protects deteriorating brick. While the City adopted ordinance
   language in 2009 prohibiting painted brick in the East Central community, it continues to be an enforcement
   issue. Concerns with painted brick include loss of integrity of the original material, loss of shadow lines
   created with varying brick and mortar colors, paint can increase deterioration of original material and any paint
   will need to be maintained and repainted on a regular basis. Because no permits are required for exterior work
   to buildings, work often is begun before the City is aware or is done on weekends when enforcement is not
   available. Once painted, it is not only costly to remove, removal often damages the brick as the paint has
   adhered to its surface. He stated many brick buildings had been painted prior to 2009 and these are allowed
   to be re-painted over the existing paint surface. He also acknowledged the Utah Historical office allows tax
   credits for repainting if the building had already been painted but not for brick that has not previously been
   painted. Mr. Stratford indicated enforcement becomes a challenge as it is difficult to tell whether original
   painting was done prior to 2009, and if repainted, there is no evidence that the building had not already been
   painted.

     Commission asked if there are ways the public can be better educated or that properties could be better
     protected, suggesting a notice might be recorded on the deed, informing realtors, social media or the City
     newsletter. It was suggested most owners are not aware it is illegal to either paint brick or to cover brick with
     vinyl siding. Commission also expressed concern that painting of brick occurs in other communities and
     whether the regulation could be applied City-wide. Mr. Stratford stated the regulation is applicable to the East
     Central Community due to its historic nature and could only be applicable to areas where most of the homes
     were constructed prior to 1950. Commission asked Staff if they had researched procedures in other historic
     areas within Utah such as The Avenues or Sugarhouse. Commission felt the original brick color is often more
     appealing than painted brick, but acknowledged owners may be concerned with the City regulating what they
     want to do with their property. Commission felt the regulation should include appropriate consequences,
     requiring the building to be restored or a lien placed on the property, indicating owners often prefer to pay fines
     than to pay the cost of restoration. Commission felt it is important to preserve the historic neighborhood and
     suggested better education might help.
