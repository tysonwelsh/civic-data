Z-1-2022
West Valley City
3846 S 2200 W
Zone change from R-1-8 to A
1.31 acres

This application was continued during the Planning Commission meeting on February 23, 2022.

West Valley City has submitted a zone change application for a 1.31-acre parcel located at 3846
S 2200 W. The current zone is R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000
square feet) and the proposed zone is A (Agriculture, minimum lot size ½ acre). The current
General Plan designation is low density residential (3 to 4 units/acre).

Last year, City staff proposed General Plan and zone changes to several parcels as application
GPZ-4-2021. The purpose of the application was to address HB 1003 which was passed by the
Utah Legislature last year. Among other things, HB 1003, removed cities’ ability to regulate
most building design standards for a one to two family dwelling where no development
agreement exists. Most vacant properties in the City planned for residential use either have a
development agreement that includes building design standards or they are within an agricultural
zone where staff anticipates a development agreement upon a future zone change. The properties
included in GPZ-4-2021 did not have a development agreement and were zoned R-1-6, R-1-8, R-
1-12, and RM. The properties zoned R-1 could be developed as single unit dwellings without
many of the City’s building design standards.

Staff’s original list of properties affected by GPZ-4-2021 included the subject property. Upon
hearing about the proposed zone change, the owner of the subject property agreed to a
development agreement where any new homes would follow the City’s previous design
standards. Given the owner’s willingness to enter into a development agreement, his property
was removed from GPZ-4-2021. However, despite several attempts from staff, the owner of the
subject property has not executed the development agreement. For this reason, staff is proposing
to rezone this property once again.

During the Planning Commission meeting on February 23, 2022, the property owner asked if the
development agreement could be amended so that he has some assurance that he could build
homes on 8,000 square foot lots in the future. Staff discussed this issue with Brandon Hill in the
Legal Department who provided the following recommended language to be added to the
development agreement:

       “Lot sizes shall be a minimum of 8,000 square feet. Developer shall be entitled to
       develop single family homes on 8,000 square foot lots provided all other requirements of
       the West Valley City Municipal Code and this Agreement are satisfied.”

Staff sent this language to the property owner for his review. At the time this report was
prepared, staff had not heard back from the property owner.


Staff Alternatives:

      withdraw the application when the property owner signs the development agreement.

Z-5-2022
Northrop Grumman Innovation Systems, Inc.
Amend the zoning map by reducing the number and changing the location
of Overpressure Zones.

Northrop Grumman Innovation Systems, Inc. has submitted a zone change application to reduce
the number and change the location of Overpressure Zones. A brief history of the Overpressure
Zones and an explanation of the proposed changes is below. Included with this report are two
letters from Northrop Grumman’s counsel to support this request.

Northrop Grumman along with the federal government own thousands of acres of property in the
southwest portion of West Valley City in the area generally between 6400 W and SR-111 and
between 4100 South and 5400 South. On this property, Northrop Grumman and its predecessors
of Hercules and Alliant Techsystems have manufactured and tested products for space
exploration and national defense for decades. Given the hazards associated with these operations,
Northrop Grumman’s predecessor Hercules expressed concerns throughout the 1970s about
potential injury and damage to persons and property as urban development expanded to the west
side of the valley closer to their operations.

To address these concerns, Northrop Grumman’s predecessor Hercules, Salt Lake County, and
later West Valley City conducted several studies during the late 70s and early 80s. As
recommended by these studies, West Valley City adopted Overpressure Zones in 1983. A copy
of the 1983 ordinance to create the Overpressure Zones is included with this report. These
Overpressure Zones included building regulations like structural reinforcements and tempered
glass to protect structures in the event of an accidental explosion.

The regulations in the Overpressure Zones vary for Zones A, B, and C. Zone A includes the most
stringent regulations as these areas are closest to Northrop’s high hazard manufacturing
activities. Overpressure Zone C includes the least stringent regulations as these areas are furthest
from Northrop’s high hazard manufacturing activities.

Northrop Grumman continues to handle the same types of energetic materials that resulted in the
creation of the Overpressure Zones. However, as indicated in their zone change application,
operational changes at Northrop provide the explanation for the requested changes to the
Overpressure Zones.

The first and most significant requested change is the removal of the southeastern most
Overpressure circles. According to Northrop’s application, the company has shut down and
eliminated the rocket motor manufacturing facilities in that area as a part of its overall operations
consolidation efforts over the last several years. In Northrop’s view, this eliminates the need for
the southeastern most Overpressure circles. Staff has included aerial photos from 2009 and 2019
to show demolition that has occurred in the southeast portion of Northrop’s property.

Staff asked what would become of the southeastern portion of the Northrop property. The
applicant sent the following response:

       “Northrop Grumman plans to continue its existing operations far into the future in
       support of the federal government programs it supports for our nation’s defense and
       space launch programs. Nevertheless, over the last several years, one of Northrop
       Grumman’s goals has been to consolidate its facilities to reduce overall operating costs.
       Northrop Grumman is aware that neighboring property owners are looking to develop
       their properties and the proposed zone text change will allow our neighbors to safely
       build using more affordable building standards.

       While development plans for the southeast portion of the Northrop Grumman property
       remain uncertain, Northrop Grumman has no plans to utilize the southeast portion of the
       property for rocket motor manufacturing in the future. Northrop Grumman also
       understands that the contraction of the overpressure zones will result in Northrop
       Grumman Grumman’s inability to use the southeast portion of its property for rocket
       motor manufacturing in the future and has advanced this application with that
       understanding.

       On an ongoing basis, Northrop Grumman will continue to evaluate potential options for
       its unused land which may include retention as buffer property, given that a small portion
       of the property in the southeast portion is completely outside of any overpressure circle,
       or other complementary future development options.”
The second requested change is the relocation of the center and western circles. According to
Northrop’s application, these changes more closely align with the actual location of Northrop’s
high hazard manufacturing activities.

Staff received the following email inquiry from Edward Woolford regarding this application:

       “The overall calculated distance of the defined overpressure zone seems to be static and
       unchanged from the original calculations. Although, don’t overpressure zone calculations
       have a direct correlation to change in elevation? I only ask because the newer locations
       appear to have a 200’ to 400’ modification in elevation which potentially could increase
       the diameter of the required overpressure zone? Did Northrop Grumman recalculate the
       required Overpressure Zoning overlay pursuant to the new data points or just shift the
       zone based upon a static calculation?”

Northrop Grumman’s Safety Director provided the following response:

“The original master plan consultant for the county, Jack Reed, of Sandia National Labs
addressed a similar question in the original study commissioned by the County. Topography for
this range from the explosion site as excerpted below is not a factor. Overpressure does change
with elevation based on air density and would be a factor for the much higher pressures we
would experience on plant when compared to the standard predictions which are conservatively
set for high density air at sea level. Nominal changes in topography from one part of the plant to
the other would not be a significant factor for even the on plant high pressure exposures.”




Included with this report is an explanation video prepared by staff that shows the changes
between the current and proposed Overpressure Zones. No changes are proposed to the
Overpressure Zone regulations.

At the time this report was prepared, staff had received two letters in opposition to this
application. These letters are included with this report.

Discussion:      Steve Pastorik presented the application. He played a five-minute video
                 explaining the applicant’s proposal. Steve displayed images of the subject
                 property from 2009 and 2019 depicting buildings that have been demolished. He
                 then illustrated the buildings that Northrup Grumman is planning to remove in the
                 future. Steve said that the buildings that have been and will be removed in the
                 southeast corner no longer housing energetic materials. This is why the applicant
                 is proposing to eliminate the circles in the southeast corner. Steve noted that he
                 had received two letters in opposition of this proposal, one letter was part of the
                 Commission’s packet, and he read aloud a letter in opposition during the pre-
                 meeting. He had also received an email from a resident asking if changes in the
                 topography would impact the blast radius. Steve stated that the original study
                 indicated that the slight elevation difference would not cause a significant shift in
                 the overpressure zones.

                 Chair Meaders said that the letter from Northrop Grumman states they are no
                 longer manufacturing energetic materials in the southeast corner of the subject
                 property, nor will they in the future. She asked Steve what holds them to keep
                 that promise. Steve said that he discussed what would become of the property
                 with the applicant, and they do not have any plans. Steve explained that in the
                 ’70s, Northrup Grumman’s predecessor, Hercules, had approached the County
                 and the City requesting the addition of overpressure circles. Northrup Grumman
                 is stating there is no longer a need for those circles to remain. He said the zoning
                 permits a manufacturing use, and perhaps they can discuss a development
                 agreement that would add another level of assurance for the Commission.

                 Commissioner Winters asked if new construction would require the same
                 permitting process. Steve said yes, all new construction is required to have a
                 building permit.

                 Lyndon DeYoung, Sr. Director of Contracts for Northrop Grumman Innovation
                 Systems, Inc., said he would like to address the questions presented during the
                 meeting. He stated that they have been working for the past five years to reduce
                 their facilities footprint, to decrease the cost of utilities and maintenance. Mr.
                 DeYoung explained that they no longer have any energetic materials in the
                 southeast corner of the property. He said that there had been many requests to
                 reduce the size of the Overpressure Zones because it is much more costly to build
                 a structure meeting the building requirements within those zones.

                 Mr. DeYoung said they are regulated by the Federal Government and DOD safety
regulations. He explained that the Federal Government would shut their
operations down if they did not comply with those regulations.

Commissioner Winters said that he assumes any new or increased number of
energetic materials would need to go through the same regulations and auditing
procedures. Mr. DeYoung noted that is correct. Commissioner Winters asked if
their license is limited to the quantity and type of energetic materials used. Mr.
DeYoung said that it is, and the Federal Government sites each building to ensure
they are not exceeding that limit.

Tim Flack, an employee of Hexcel Cooperation, said that his company is affected
by the Overpressure Zones. He asked Steve if the ordinance was staying the
same. Steve explained that the regulations are not changing, just the Overpressure
circles. He noted that most of Hexcel would be out of the overpressure zone if this
proposal is approved. Mr. Flack asked Steve to clarify the proposed changes to
the Overpressure Zones.

Freddy A Espinoza, a resident of West Valley City, asked if these changes to the
Overpressure circles were derived from a recent research study. Mr. DeYoung
explained that they have been manufacturing the same product using the same
materials since the ‘70s. He also stated that Northrup Grumman’s safety
organization had looked closely at and compared their operations today to the
processes when the ordinance was first put into place and did not find any
significant changes requiring a new assessment. They did reassess the central
location of our current operations and made the appropriate adjustments to the
circles.

Mr. Espinoza stated that he does not feel comfortable with the location of the
Overpressure Zones using data from forty years ago and feels a more recent
analysis should be done.

Katrina Long, representing the property owner of 4319 S. 8460 W., explained that
this property is changing from Zone C to Zone B, increasing building
requirements. She asked why there would be an increase when the area impacted
by the Overpressure Zones is decreasing. Mr. DeYoung said that when the center
location of hazardous materials was reassessed and adjusted, that could have
caused the zone to change, requiring higher standards. Steve added that our
ordinance only pertains to West Valley City. Where this property could be
located outside of West Valley City, Chair Meaders asked the applicant to
continue this conversation after the meeting.

Commissioner Woodruff said that he is comfortable with the proposed changes
after the discussions during the study meeting and this meeting. He believes the
reduction in the Overpressure Overlay Zones could impact the liability of
Northrup Grumman. However, he does not see this proposal negatively
impacting the surrounding residential properties.
