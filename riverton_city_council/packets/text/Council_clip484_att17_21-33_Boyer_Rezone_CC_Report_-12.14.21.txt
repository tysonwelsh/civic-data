Subject:         Ordinance 21-33, Boyer Rezone, Proposed rezone of approximately 8 acres located
                 at 12761 South 3600 West from C-PO/EHOV-SD to M-1, Light Industrial, Applicant-
                 Spencer Moffat, representing the Boyer Group



On November 18, 2021, the Planning Commission held a public hearing to review this application.
Minutes from that meeting are included below. The Planning Commission voted to recommend
denial of this application, with the following recommended motion:

I move the City Council DENY Ordinance #21-33, proposed rezone of approximately 8.0 acres of land
located at 12761 South 3600 West, thus keeping the zoning as C-PO/EHOV-SD, Professional Office with
an Elderly Housing Overlay and Specific Development Designations.

Background

This is a public hearing for an application to rezone approximately 8.0-acres of land located at 12761
South 3600 West. Spencer Moffat, representing the Boyer Group, has requested a rezone of
approximately 8 acres of property located at 12761 South 3600 West. The property is currently zoned C-
PO/EHOV-SD, Professional Office with an Elderly Housing Overlay and Specific Development
Designations allowing residential senior housing. The applicant is requesting a rezone to M-1, Light
Manufacturing, with the proposed use as “Office/Flex Warehouse Space”.

The property in question is located on 3600 West on the north side of Water Street. It has been the
subject of many rezone applications and much discussion over the past few years. In February of 2021
the City Council passed Ordinance 21-02 rezoning the property to C-PO/EHOV-SD, Professional Office
with an Elderly Housing Overlay and Specific Development Designations, a copy of that ordinance is
attached to this report. The General Plan Designation for the property is Business Park and Light
Manufacturing, which corresponds to the both the existing and requested zoning.

The applicant has provided a conceptual site plan showing three “flex-office buildings”. This hearing will
NOT be reviewing or approving the design or layout of the site, however it is helpful to see how a site may
layout. Flex-office space is built to accommodate a variety of commercial, office and industrial uses.
The applicant suggested it would be possible to see a start-up software company, next to a dance studio,
next to a Heating and Air Condition office, next to a medical supply company all in the same building.

Notices for hearing were sent to property owners within 1,000-ft of the subject property. Email comments
submitted by the public are attached to this staff report.

The Planning Commission recommended denial of this rezone application, maintaining the Elderly
Housing Overlay on the property. The City Council may accept that recommendation and vote to deny

      B.     REZONE, A REQUEST FOR A REZONE OF APPROXIMATELY 8 ACRES
             OF PROPERTY LOCATED AT 12761 SOUTH 3600 WEST. APPLICANT-
             SPENCER MOFFAT, REPRESENTING THE BOYER GROUP.

Vice-Chair Lefgren opened the public hearing.

Mr. Prestwich presented the Staff Report and stated that the above request is for a rezone
of approximately eight acres of property located at 12761 South 3600 West. A property
rendering was displayed. The Concept Plan includes a flex warehouse with a storefront and
garage doors in the rear. The underlying zone is Commercial Professional Office with an
Elderly Housing Overlay. He reported that the proposed M-1 zone fits within the General
Plan for Business Park or Light Manufacturing and is an appropriate zoning for that
designation. Properties within 1,000 feet have been noticed and written comments were
included in the packet.

Vice-Chair Lefgren pointed out that the Overlay Zone was changed in early 2021 and the
City Council went ahead with an overlay that was counter to the Planning Commission
recommendation. It was noted that the neighbors would prefer commercial over residential.
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                                  5
November 18, 2021

The applicant, Spencer Moffitt from The Boyer Group, stated that they understood that the
Elderly Overlay Housing Zone had been approved and the previous developer has no
interest in developing the property when they were denied their requested density. They
were reminded of the history of the property and decided on Light Industrial. He clarified
that there will be truck traffic associated with the buildings during business hours as they are
larger facilities. These types of buildings are typically 32 feet in height with a parapet to
conceal equipment.

Jason Woodbury expressed concern with property access and asked that any traffic not go
through his neighborhood.

There were no further public comments. Vice-Chair Lefgren closed the public hearing.

Zoning issues were discussed. Mr. Prestwich stated that M-1 and CPO zoning differ in terms
of uses, architectural requirements, and signage allowances. CPO allows only minimal uses
for retail and almost no manufacturing. Surrounding properties were detailed.

Commissioner Breinholt recalled the agreement with the nearby plaza regarding trucks
accessing Water Street and asked if there is signage. Mr. Prestwich confirmed that signage
is posted.

Vice-Chair Lefgren stated that he has been involved with this project from the most recent
application and recalled that the Planning Commission forwarded a recommendation to the
City Council with a different proposal. He was inclined to leave it as-is and allow the City
Council to change it back.

Vice-Chair Lefgren moved to recommend DENIAL of PLZ-21-4009 Rezone of 8.0-acres
of land located at 12761 South 3600 West, thus keeping the zoning as C-PO/EHOV-
SD, Professional Office with an Elderly Housing Overlay and Specific Development
Designations. Commissioner Cannon seconded the motion. Vote on motion:
Commissioner Brown-Nay, Commissioner Cluff-Nay, Commissioner Cannon-Aye,
Commissioner Breinholt-Aye, Vice-Chair Lefgren-Aye. The motion passed 3-to-2.

Dear Planning Commission/City Council:

I am writing this in response to the rezone request for M-1 zoning on the property to the North of
Stampin Up. I would have liked to have been at the Planning Commission meeting in person, but
travel plans prevent me from being there. Instead I will share my thoughts here. For your
information and reference, I live in the Castlewood neighborhood directly to the East of the subject
property.  

This property has been a contentious issue between Stampin Up and the surrounding
neighborhoods for the better part of the last 4 years. Several proposals have been made that have
all included high density residential development with densities between 8 and 14 units per acre.
The neighbors have stood united and strong against this type of development, and have made their
opinions known through meeting attendance and signed petitions. The Planning Commission and
City Council have mostly supported the residents in their opposition to these rezone requests. The
most recent proposal earlier this year brought a rezone request for high density elderly housing.
Residents were opposed to that rezone request, and expressed support for no more than 4 units per
acre. The developer was requesting zoning for 10 units per acre. The City Council granted what they
thought was a reasonable compromise of 6 units per acre with an elderly overlay, which is now the
current zoning. Residents were not entirely pleased with this outcome, but were grateful the City
Council did not approve a density any higher than 6 units per acre. The outcome of that rezone was
something residents had worked for over a period of years, and have accepted that it’s not exactly
what we wanted, but was a good compromise with the property owner and the city.  

Throughout this process, residents have attended several meetings with developers, representatives
of the property owner, the City Council and Planning Commission. I personally have been in ten or
more meetings over the last four plus years to address these zoning and development issues.
Through all of these meetings the message from residents has been clear. We do not want
residential development that is of a higher density than the surrounding neighborhoods, but we are
willing to accept the medium density that it is currently zoned for. My concern is that the property
owner may still be pushing for higher density. In a recent Facebook post on our neighborhood page,
Councilwoman McCay mentioned that in her discussions with the Planning Department, one option
being considered is to “throw Stampin’ Up a bone and increase density by 1 to 2 units an acre.” To
throw Stampin Up a bone and increase the density would go against everything the residents have
been fighting for over the last 4 years, and what the City Council approved only 9 months ago. 6
units per acre is a good fit for that property, and has widespread support from our neighborhood.
The M-1 zoning currently being considered is not a good fit for that area. When it was originally
zoned commercial and light manufacturing in the past, there was very little residential development
in the area. Having said that, if the alternative is high density residential, I would prefer the
commercial development. My concern is that if this rezone request for M-1 is denied, there seems
to already be discussions among the city staff to give Stampin Up an increase in density up to 8 units
per acre. This should not happen!    
I would encourage the Planning Commission and City Council to keep the property at the current
zoning of commercial with the elderly overlay at 6 units per acre. This was the zoning the property
owner asked for 9 months ago, and what was approved by the City Council. This is a compromise
that the residents of the Castlewood neighborhood can accept. We cannot accept what seems like
and endless push by the property owner to have high density housing on this property. This has
been denied several times by both the Planning Commission and the City Council. Please do not
consider “throwing Stampin’ Up a bone” by raising the density above what has already been
approved.      
Thank you for your service to Riverton City and its residents and for your consideration on this issue.
