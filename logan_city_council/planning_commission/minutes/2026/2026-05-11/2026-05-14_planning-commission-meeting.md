PC 26-017 Elevate at 12th [Design Review] Daniel Berger/DSD West Logan LLC, authorized
agent/owner is requesting a design review permit to construct two mixed use buildings located at
1137 West 200 South & 1136 West 130 South, in the Commercial (COM) Zone; TIN 05-107-0021; -
0014, in the Ellis Neighborhood.

Staff: Aimee Egbert, City Planner, reviewed the request for a design review permit for two new three-
story mixed-use. Each building includes five ground-floor hotel/motel units, one ADA-accessible long-
term rental unit, and five residential apartments on both the second and third floors. In total, the
development includes 10 hotel/motel units, 2 ADA-accessible long-term units, and 20 multifamily
residential units, for a combined total of 32 units between both buildings.

A. Egbert explained that the Land Development Code (LDC) requires mixed-use developments within
the Commercial zone to dedicate at least 80% of the ground floor area to commercial use. As
proposed, each structure provides approximately 78.5% of the ground-floor area as commercial.

A. Egbert reviewed the LDC transition standards for commercial and mixed-use development located
adjacent to residential zoning districts. The standards require front setbacks to match the adjacent
residential zone when facing or abutting residential property. The proposed project is located within
150 feet of the adjacent NR-6 zone and is therefore subject to the transition area standards and a 25


foot setback from 200 South. Staff determined that the development generally complies with building
height, setbacks, and overall site design. However, the placement of a large dumpster within the
setback adjacent to the NR-6 zone may not be consistent with the requirement to maintain a
residential-compatible front-yard character.

A. Egbert also reviewed the building transparency requirements. Commercial buildings are required
to have provide a minimum of 30% transparency along ground-floor street frontages and exposed
sides, while upper floors must provide 20% transparency. As proposed, building one provides
approximately 26-27% transparency along the east and west ground floor façades. Building two
provides approximately 28% transparency on the street-facing ground-floor façade, with exposed
east and west façades ranging from approximately 25–27% transparency.

The applicant is proposing 51 on-site parking stalls and 5 additional off-site stalls on the adjacent
property to the west. A. Egbert stated that the applicant will be required to submit an alternate parking
plan for approval by the Community Development Director.

Proponent: Danny Berger stated that the project is intended to provide affordable one-, two-, and
three-bedroom housing units within the area.

Public: Jeannie Simmonds asked about the parking requirement for hotel/motel units compared to
short-term rental units.

Commission: Commissioner Doutre said she did not see the benefit of reducing the required amount
of ground-floor commercial space.

Commissioner Maughan commented that the project appears primarily residential in nature and
asked how the short-term rental units would be enforced.

Russ Holley, Community Development Director, stated that the project would be conditioned to
require the commercial units to be constructed and licensed as commercial uses.

Commissioner Maughan asked how the parking requirements differ between residential and
commercial uses. R. Holley explained that residential parking requirements are 1.5 stalls per studio
or one-bedroom unit and 2 stalls per two-bedroom unit. Hotel/motel uses require 1 stall per unit plus
4 additional stalls.

A. Egbert added that the ground floor commercial/short-term residential units would require a total of
10 parking stalls if classified as short-term rental units and 14 stalls if classified as hotel/motel units.

Commissioner Doutre suggested that the Commission review the appropriateness of hotel/motel
uses within the mixed-use zone during a future code discussion.

Commissioner Doutre also stated that the proposed dumpster should not be located within the
setback adjacent to a residential zone.

MOTION: Commissioner Doutre made a motion to conditionally approve a Design Review Permit
for PC 26-017 subject to the amended conditions of approval and the findings for approval as listed
below. Commissioner McNamara seconded the motion. The motion was approved unanimously.

PC 26-018 Public Works Campus Phase 2 [Design Review] Kevin Blalock/Logan City, authorized
agent/owner is requesting a design review permit to construct phase two of the new Public Works
Campus which consists of the demolition of the existing structures and constructing a two-story
administration building, two vehicle storage buildings, and two covered storage areas. Located at
950 West 600 North in the Public (PUB) Zone; TIN 05-062-0017; -0004, in the Ellis Neighborhood.

Staff: Aaron Smith reviewed the request for phase two of the Public Works Campus. He explained
that the project includes a new administration building, two vehicle storage buildings, and two covered
storage areas. This phase is part of the larger Public Works Campus development, which also
includes the fleet maintenance building, vehicle wash building, and Parks and Recreation vehicle
storage building approved in phase one.

A. Smith reviewed pedestrian circulation through the site and stated that sidewalk connections should
be provided from the administrative office to the adjacent streets and internal pathways, as reflected
in condition number three.

A. Smith reviewed the proposed landscaping plan and noted that phase One included a condition
allowing landscaping requirements to be met across the entire campus. The combined phases
require approximately 158,000 square feet of landscaping and usable outdoor space. Staff reviewed
the landscaping plan submitted for the phase one building permit and the site plan submitted for
phase two and determined that the overall campus development provides approximately 152,000
square feet of landscaping area, satisfying approximately 96% of the requirement.

The applicant requested that the Planning Commission consider a 10% reduction in landscaping
requirements. Staff considered the scale, landscaping placement, use, and overall landscaping area
proposed for both phases and determined that the design provides an adequate amount of
landscaping. A. Smith also noted that phase one includes substantial plant materials exceeding
minimum requirements.

A. Smith explained that the Land Development Code does not permit more than 20 parking stalls in
a row without a landscaping break. The westernmost row of the administrative office parking lot
contains 30 stalls without a break. Given the generous planting strips and substantial landscaping
buffer to the west, the Planning Commission would need to determine whether this requirement
should be waived.

The vehicle storage buildings and bulk material storage building do not include architectural detailing,
or wall breaks on the north and south elevations. The buildings are 80 and 88 feet wide and are set


back a minimum of 65 feet from 600 North and 83 feet from 500 North. Staff indicated that, due to
the significant setbacks and extensive landscaping buffers along both streets, it may be reasonable
to allow the landscaping to substitute for wall breaks.

Proponent: Paul Lindhardt, Public Works Director, stated that the City worked to make the project an
example for future developments along 1000 West through increased setbacks and enhanced
landscaping buffers.

Public: No public comments were made.

Commission: Commissioner Heare said he supported the landscaping reduction because the
overall project substantially meets the landscaping requirement.

Commissioner Doutre stated that condition five could be removed due to the additional landscaping
provided along 1000 West.

The Commission discussed condition number two regarding wall breaks and determined that
perimeter landscaping would provide sufficient screening for the blank walls.
