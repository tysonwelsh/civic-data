self-sufficiency and neighborhood revitalization. She said they were a status of Public Body

and Affordable Housing regarding rental properties.

Ms. Kimball addressed questions from Council Members and Council Member Buroker spoke in
favor of the Housing Authority and said a lot of Riverton residents could benefit from the
program.

    b. Unified Fire Authority January – October 2020 Report

    a. Ordinance No. 20-25 - Amending Section 18.90.010 Table of Commercial Uses, and
       18.85.030(12) Permitted Uses (in the C-R Zone), Regarding Distance Limitation for
       Certain Uses

Council member McCay recused herself from the following discussion and vote:

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that Riverton City’s Table of
Commercial Uses Zone, and the Commercial Downtown, Gateway, and Regional zones,
currently included language limiting convenience stores with gas stations and standalone gas
stations to a maximum of two within 1,000 feet. He said that Staff has brought up the question of
whether this restriction is one the City wishes to maintain in its Code, as development patterns
with gas station uses in the City has not brought this issue to light for many years. He said the
language was apparently originally adopted in part to address concerns with 12600 South and
1300 West, where for a time there was the potential for three service stations at that
intersection. He said that, as gasoline service stations are much more difficult to convert to other
uses or retrofit, one likely concern at the time this language was originally adopted was avoiding
what may become unstainable competition resulting in vacant and difficult to redevelop
properties.

Mr. Lethbridge said that the Planning Commission, in reviewing the existing language, voted to
recommend maintaining the limitation as it currently stands. However, he said Staff included an
alternative amendment that would keep the maximum of two within 1,000 feet unless stations
were separated by a major roadway, one of at least seven lanes. He said this would limit
situations where more than two stations could exist within 1,000 feet to areas where traffic
volumes could make multiple stations more sustainable, and also where the separation across
such a large right-of-way could make the uses less impactful to one another.

Mayor Staggs opened a Public Hearing and called for public comment; there being none, he
declared the Public Hearing closed.

Council Member Stewart said the proposed ordinance affected his district and there was more
need than he originally thought, especially traveling west. He said his concern was regarding
preservation of the downtown area; however, he supported the recommended motion.

Brief comments were given by City Attorney Ryan Carter and other Councilmembers in support
of the following motion:

Councilmember Stewart MOVED that the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 20-25 -
Amending Section 18.90.010, and 18.85.030(12) Distance Regulations on Gasoline
Service Stations, a max of two within 1,000 feet unless separated by a roadway of at least
seven lanes. Councilmember Buroker SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for
discussion on the motion; there being none, he called for a roll call vote and the vote was as
follows: Councilmembers Buroker-Yes, McCay-Recused, McDougal-Yes, Stewart-Yes, and
Wells-Yes. The motion passed.
