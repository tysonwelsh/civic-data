E. PRESENTATIONS:

    1. PRESENTATION OF WEBER COUNTY HERO AWARD TO LAWANNA SHURTLIFF

         Commissioner Ebert said that a call had been issued for nominees of heroes in the county. George
         Hall read his nomination for Ms. Shurtliff stating that she had been his High School teacher during a
         difficult time and her challenge for him to do better stayed with him all through his life. She was the
         kind of teacher that influenced the lives of many and her caring and concern for individuals made her
         such an admired and respected political figure during her tenure representing Weber County in the
         House of Representatives. Ms. Shurtliff was pleased to know that she influenced Mr. Hall’s life.
         Chair Gibson stated that Ms. Shurtliff continues to influence so many individuals. He served with
         her in the House of Representatives for four years and recognized that she was a person to emulate,
         who cared deeply about the community as a whole. She was presented with a plaque.

    2. PRESENTATION ON LEADERSHIP

         Spencer Holliday, Student Body President, Ogden Preparatory Academy, gave the speech that he had
         given recently on leadership. He said that leadership is about taking action, helping others, striving
         to make a difference in the lives of others and about taking responsibility. He added that a true
         leader admits mistakes, has the wisdom to learn from them and the courage to change.

    3. PRESENTATION ON WEBER COUNTY EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT

         Chair Gibson became well acquainted with Lance Peterson, County Emergency Manager, as they
         worked on the severe 2011 flooding in western Weber County. Mr. Peterson worked tirelessly for
         weeks with little sleep. He found Mr. Petersen to not only be an exemplary employee, but one who
         is a person of great character who truly loves what he does. He expressed gratitude to Mr. Peterson
         for his hard work and dedication over the years. Mr. Peterson explained the various facets of county
         emergency management and the various types of disasters. He works to create a framework to
         prepare for and reduce the vulnerability to hazards. He noted that the county has spent the past four
         years repairing and recovering from the 2011 flood. Emergency Management is the key link to get
         through the disaster process with the state and federal governments and bring in resources. He works
         with all 15 cities in the county, the University and the two hospitals. Some accomplishments under
         Mr. Peterson include creating a hazmat response capability, Weber Metro USAR (Urban Search &
         Rescue), working with CERT (Community Emergency Response Team) and with the northern Utah
         six-county Northern Utah Regional Homeland Security Coalition. The commissioners expressed
         gratitude to Mr. Peterson for the tremendous amount of work that he does.

G. ACTION ITEMS:

    1. PRESENTATION FROM THE UTAH STATE FIRE MARSHALL’S OFFICE REGARDING THE WILDLAND
       URBAN INTERFACE ORDINANCE
       Ted Marshall, Utah State Fire Marshall, noted that the Wildland Urban Interface Code had not been
       meeting the counties’ needs. He presented a few amendments relating to definitions, structures,
       water supply and driveways stating that they came with approval of the State Forester and the State
       Fire Marshall. The amendments give the local jurisdictions more control. They are not applicable in
       areas with paved roads, fire hydrants, water systems, etc. He recommended that the Fire Marshall
       present to the Commission what changes the Weber Fire District feels are applicable to the county.

    3. CONTRACT WITH BOWEN CONSTRUCTION FOR WATER SYSTEM UPDATES AT WEBER MEMORIAL
       PARK PROJECT – CONTRACT C2015-145
       Jennifer Graham, County Recreation Facilities Director, stated that one of the two springs at the Park
       that provide the drinking water was declared under direct influence of ground or surface water and
       had to be abandoned. A study was conducted. She explained that water will be pumped (and
       chlorinated) out of the operational spring to a higher elevation and will gravity flow back to the
       distribution system.
       Commissioner Bell moved to approve Contract C2015-145 with Bowen Construction for water
       system updates at Weber Memorial Park; Commissioner Ebert seconded.
       Commissioner Bell – aye; Commissioner Ebert – aye; Chair Gibson – aye

    4. AGREEMENT TO ELIMINATE AND DISSOLVE THE NORTHERN UTAH REGIONAL LANDFILL
       AUTHORITY (NURLA) – RESOLUTION 27-2015, CONTRACT C2015-146
       Gary Laird, of County Solid Waste, stated that about eight years ago NURLA was created, mainly to
       look for a northern Utah regional landfill and consisted of Logan City, Wasatch Integrated Waste,
       Weber County and Box Elder. Weber County purchased Box Elder landfill. Box Elder withdrew
       from NURLA. A new entity has been created.
       Commissioner Ebert moved to adopt Resolution 27-2015 approving Contract C2015-146 to eliminate
       and dissolve the Northern Utah Regional Landfill Authority; Commissioner Bell seconded.
       Commissioner Bell – aye; Commissioner Ebert – aye; Chair Gibson – aye

     7. UPDATED TRAVEL POLICY 9.0

        Lynn Taylor, of the County Clerk/Auditor’s Office, stated that the Clerk/Auditor’s Office has been
        working on items 7–10 for over six months to ensure they are compliant with State and county
        regulations. Changes include that the county will now use GSA (General Services Administration)
        rates for per diem and a per diem for local vicinity travel was added.
        Commissioner Bell moved to approve the updated Travel Policy 9.0; Commissioner Ebert seconded.
        Commissioner Bell – aye; Commissioner Ebert – aye; Chair Gibson – aye

     8. UPDATED BOARD OF EQUALIZATION (BOE) POLICY 12.4 – RESOLUTION 28-2015
        Lynn Taylor, of the County Clerk/Auditor’s Office, stated that the resolution expands the scope of
        electronically filed appeals. Some changes to the policy included that property owners can appeal on
        the classification basis and modified deadlines mirror the Utah State Tax Commission’s.
        Commissioner Ebert moved to adopt Resolution 28-2015 approving the updated Board of
        Equalization Policy 12.4; Commissioner Bell seconded.
        Commissioner Bell – aye; Commissioner Ebert – aye; Chair Gibson – aye

     9. UPDATED TAX RELIEF POLICY 12.5
        Lynn Taylor, of the County Clerk/Auditor’s Office, stated that this policy had substantial revisions
        after closely collaborating with other counties in an effort to standardize how the counties conduct
        tax relief. Amendments include the process for selling a property receiving tax relief, increasing the
        allowable savings amount for the indigent abatement to $7,500, and the U.S. Health and Human
        Services’ poverty guidelines have been adopted in part.
        Commissioner Ebert moved to approve the updated Tax Relief Policy 12.5; Commissioner Bell
        seconded.
        Commissioner Bell – aye; Commissioner Ebert – aye; Chair Gibson – aye

    10. FUND BALANCE POLICY 5.1
        Scott Parke, County Comptroller, presented this new policy stating that the county issued refunding
        bonds at the end of 2014 and Standard & Poor’s favorably viewed the county’s overall financial
        health and future outlook. They recommended that the county formally adopt a fund balance policy.
        It sets a minimum acceptable fund balance level for each of the county’s significant funds and
        ongoing monitoring/reporting requirements.
        Commissioner Ebert moved to approve Fund Balance Policy 5.1; Commissioner Bell seconded.
        Commissioner Bell – aye; Commissioner Ebert – aye; Chair Gibson – aye
