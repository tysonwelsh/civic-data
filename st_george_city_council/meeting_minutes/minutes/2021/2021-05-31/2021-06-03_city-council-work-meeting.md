DISCUSSION ON DESERT COLOR WATER USAGE PLAN:
     City Manager Adam Lenhard introduced Rob Behunin and David Clark with Desert
     Color.

      Mr. Behunin provided an update on the development of Desert Color. He stated that
      he hopes the community vision continues and mentioned that Big Shots will soon
      break ground in the area - it will be a great amenity for the community. With
      regards to the lagoon, things have been delayed due to Covid; however, they will
      continue with construction. Their due diligence tells them they will be able to have
      their own water system by the end of the year or sooner. Currently, the well water
      can be used for other things. They are committed to installing a 300 gallon a minute
      pump, running the pump for 16 hours a day. Their proposal is to purchase about 4
      million gallons of usable water from the City for the lagoon. He believes this is the
      amount needed until December. They are pretty comfortable that they can do more
      than a one-on-one exchange with the well water and what is proposed to be
      purchased from the City. In tandem, they have some purple hydrants in the area
      and will commit to put in more to completely eliminate the use of construction water
      and will continue using well water. Once the lagoon is completed, they will have
      excess water. At the end of the day, they will bring more water to the project than
      they propose to purchase from the City. They will have scheduled maintenance
      where they will take the system off line and perform maintenance. He noted they
      would like to enter into an agreement to purchase water from the City for backup in
      case of a failure.

      Councilmember Larkin asked what the problem was initially. She asked why the
      initial system was not adequate for their water needs.

      Mr. Behunin explained the water samples did not reveal how bad the water actually
      was. When they drilled the wells, they found that the water is much worse the
      farther north you go and the farther south, the water is better.

      Councilmember Curtis inquired if the investment could be in jeopardy if water
      evaporates with current water accessibility.

      Mr. Behunin replied yes, if they cannot get water in the lagoon, they will void the
      warranties. He believes the lagoon is a community investment.

      Councilmember McArthur asked Mr. Behunin what they can offer in exchange for the
      water provided by the City.

      Mr. Behunin explained they will be able to exchange more than 1 for 1. For the 4
      million gallons they propose to use, they will bring back a significant amount.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard mentioned construction water being used in Desert
      Color is culinary water.

      Mr. Behunin replied yes, the majority of the water they use is culinary water. He
      noted this is not different than other contractors. Getting the two ponds in will help
      with the pressurization and gravity feed.

      Councilmember Larkin commented if they are saving more culinary water in this
      move, it can be justified. She noted this is the worst timing with the drought.
      Desert Color is a great community, but there has been push back from citizens who
      are concerned with the lagoon.

      Mr. Behunin stated they can put a meter on the water to show what they are using
      and what is being put back into the area. He noted it were not for Covid, they would
      not be here requesting this proposal.

      After being asked by Councilmember Larkin Water Services Director Scott Taylor
      explained it would be tough to put a cap on the water purchase. He believes staff
      has to work with the developers to make sure they are maintaining and replacing as
      needed. Staff has a good idea of equipment and lead items; if it looks as though
      they are not making a good faith effort, they can be shut down.

      Mr. Behunin stated they can plot and provide a schedule an estimate of what is going
      to be needed prior to using the water. If the development is ever sold or out of their
      hands, they would work with the City to help assure things are done the way the
      Council wants. He noted SITLA is a partner and that will never change after which
      he noted this is a long-term project.

      Water Services Director Scott Taylor explained it could be to the discretion of Water
      Services to shut them off if the developers are not making the good faith effort to
      repair or replace.

      Mr. Behunin would commit to the standard without hesitation. If they are not doing
      things in good faith, they do not deserve the water. They did not want to put the
      City in this position.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard agreed that he wished the pump was in and things were
      going as planned. He is excited about the conversion of the construction water.
      There will be some nuances as not all of the water will be able to come from
      secondary irrigation; however, the math is pretty simple. Water Services Director
      Scott Taylor verified the number and it is about 10 million gallons a month for
      construction water. He noted since the watering trucks are not licensed, they cannot
      be driven on the streets. Mr. Behunin will need to install a hydrant so they can fill up
      as needed. He thanked the developers as they are the first to offer this proposal.

      Councilmember McArthur commented he believes this is a great trade because of
      what the developers are doing with the secondary water.

      Mr. Behunin explained they have a process of taking reuse water turning it into
      potable grade water.

      Water Services Director Scott Taylor stated staff will need to make sure the
      agreement does not have a set percentage of how much secondary water versus
      culinary water is used; the developers and staff need to work together in good faith
      knowing what the end goal is. He noted Desert Color has been great to work with.
      He asked about the proposed treatment plant.

      Mr. Behunin explained they will find a way to haul off the brine stream. They would
      like to have this project complete by September/October, December at the latest.

      Councilmember Curtis commented he would like to see the project succeed from a
      community investment standpoint.

      Councilmember McArthur agreed.

      Mr. Behunin noted they have another builder on board. The water amenity is
      consistent with the local and state water conservation plan. He believes the water
      amenity is saving quite a bit of water since homeowners will not need to build a
      private pool in their backyard.

      The meeting adjourned at approximately 4:40 p.m.
