Conditional Use Permit

3. 16C23 - Public Hearing and Consideration of a Conditional Use and Site Plan Amendment to File
#42C14 to Construct a New 2,245 Square foot Drive-Through Restaurant and 33,992 Square Foot
commercial building in the Legacy Plaza Shopping Center at Approximately 1758 West 5400 South,
Taylorsville, Utah. (Jim Spung/AICP/Senior Planner)

3.1 Mr. Spung presented this item. File #16C23/CUP-000339-2023. He explained that Rolland Lee,
RTLee Design on behalf of David Werts, Legacy Real Estate, is requesting review and approval of a
conditional use permit and site plan amendment to File #42C14, related to the commercial master plan for
the Legacy Plaza Shopping Center at 1758 West 5400 South in Taylorsville, Utah.

3.1.1 The applicant’s proposal includes the construction of two new commercial buildings on
undeveloped land within the center. Key changes proposed from the existing master site plan
include: (1) New drive-through pad adjacent to the western drive aisle; (2) New nose-in parking
along the south facade of the building; (3) Angled parking in lieu of 90-degree parking along drive
aisle adjacent to existing Planet Fitness building; (4) New 90-degree parking along the northern
property line.

3.1.2 The Legacy Plaza Shopping Center is approximately 19.19 acres and includes 8 lots within the
“Legacy Plaza at 54h Commercial Subdivision.” The master plan area of the Legacy Plaza Shopping
Center is approximately 8.22 acres and includes four separate parcels and five buildings. The
property has direct vehicle access to 5400 South as well as Redwood Road.

3.1.3. The Proposed Land Use Map in the General Plan sets the foundation for all land uses within the
City of Taylorsville. The land use plan identifies general land use classifications and in some cases
the intensity within the classification. Each land use classification on the Proposed Land Use Map
corresponds with compatible classifications on the official Taylorsville Zoning Map.

3.1.4 The property is currently designated as “Regional Commercial” on the Proposed Land Use Map.
The Regional Commercial (RC) designation is for those areas that contain large shopping centers
of regional significance, such as the Family Center at Taylorsville located on Redwood Road.
Properties that carry an RC classification commonly provide a wider array of services such as major
department stores, specialty shops, and professional offices. RC districts generally serve an area
within a 5-to-15-mile radius, which usually extends beyond the boundaries of Taylorsville. RC
districts are usually located adjacent or near intersections of major transportation corridors. The

3.2 FINDINGS:

3.2.1 The application (File #16C23/CUP-000339-2023) was initiated by Rolland Lee, RTLee Design.

3.2.2. The applicant is requesting Planning Commission review and approval of a conditional use permit
and site plan amendment to File #42C14, related to the commercial master plan for the Legacy
Plaza Shopping Center.

3.2.3. The Legacy Plaza shopping center is 16.19 acres and is currently made up of 8 lots and 9 individual
parcels (Parcel IDs: 21103790350000; 2110390320000; 21103790340000; 21103790310000;
21103790330000; 21103790260000; 21103790300000, 2113790290000; 21103790250000).

3.2.4 The applicant’s proposal includes the construction of two new commercial buildings on
undeveloped land within the center (between the existing Planet Fitness building and Building D),
as illustrated in Exhibits H, |, and J.

3.2.5 Salt Lake County tax records indicate there are two separate parcels (6A and 6B) within Lot 6. The
creation of these parcels has not been reviewed or approved by Taylorsville City and will require a
subdivision plat amendment.

3.2.6 | The subject property is in the Community Commercial (CC) zoning district.

3.2.7. The subject property is in the “Regional Commercial” designation in the City’s General Plan.

3.2.8 | The applicant’s proposal is consistent with the City’s General Plan.

3.2.9 The application has been reviewed by City departments and review agencies. Prior to final
approval, City Staff and review agencies will continue coordinating with the applicant to address
all outstanding review comments.

3.2.10 Specific development standards that merit further discussion and consideration from the Planning
Commission have been listed in a table in the “Land Development Code Analysis” section of the
Staff Report.

3.2.11 On June 30, 2023, a public notice was mailed to all affected entities and property owners within
300 feet of the subject property.

3.2.12 Section 13.23.240(A) of the Taylorsville Land Development Code (LDC) requires that prior to
development in a shopping center, the Planning Commission review and approve a master plan.
An approved master plan will inform future development applications for subsequent subdivision,
conditional use, and site plan reviews.

3.2.13 Staff and review agencies have identified conditions of approval to be considered by the Planning
Commission.

3.2.14 The Planning Commission is the decision-making authority for preliminary non-administrative
conditional use permit review. Final review authority is granted to the Director, unless otherwise
directed by the Planning Commission.

3.3 CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL:

3.3.1 The applicant coordinates with City Staff and reviewing agencies to address all outstanding review
comments and to determine compliance with applicable standards, prior to final approval.

3.5 APPLICANT ADDRESS: David Werts. Mr. Werts said he is glad to be back before the Planning
Commission. This project started in 2013. He advised the biggest change in the site plan is the size of the
buildings. Smaller size buildings are more popular with tenants now.
3.5.1 Commissioner Quigley asked if they had prearranged tenants coming in and if Mr. Werts could
give any input as to who they will be. Mr. Werts said Unit A would be a Chipotle Restaurant, Unit
D — Five Below, Unit U — Ulta Beauty, and the last one is a Petco Store. Commissioner Quigley
asked if the Chipotle Restaurant would be for pick-up only as indicated and Mr. Werts said that
was correct for now, with a future option for a dining-in area to be included. Commissioner
Quigley then wanted to know what the landscape area would consist of, and Mr. Werts said it is
planned to be xeriscape.
3.5.2 Commissioner Willardson expressed concerns about the drive-up order box and the noise
it would generate. Commissioner Quigley advised if that becomes a problem, it can be addressed
by the Planning Commission. Mr. Spung volunteered that the Planning Commission could
add a condition of approval to limit the noise level emanating therefrom. Mr. Werts said it is about
200’ from the property line and the ordering board would be at the south end of that unit, so he
foresaw no noise problem. Commissioner Willardson said his concern was that at night, the sound
carries. Commissioner Quigley said that the drive-up window would be facing 5400 South and
that would channel the sound direction to the south rather than towards the neighbors.
3.5.3. Commissioner Wright had questions about parking, i.e., on the west side parking is 90
degrees which is fine, but the angled parking is not. He wondered if that parking could be placed
somewhere else. Also, he wondered what was the reason for the number of parking stalls right
next to the building. Mr. Werts said what is driving it is “space”. All retailers require certain
frontage, and they just ran out of space to fit in the parking lot. That the angled parking complies
with the City Code, and they tried to preserve that same parking. There is more than enough
parking but retailers like the more parking the better.
3.5.3.1 Commissioner Quigley asked if the applicant couldn’t put a sign there that says “Right
Hand Turn Only” as they exit. He felt that could eliminate some of that potential. He
asked if the future tenant would have any problem with that. Mr. Werts said the parking
spaces are designed 24’ wide, which should allow for sufficient room for backing out.
Angle parking clearly is easier to back straight out and head north. His intent was to be
able to fit everything in while adjusting for all the Code requirements.

3.5.3.2 Commissioner Wright wanted to know if the parking on the west side was
necessary. Mr. Werts advised they are needed because by doing the angle parking they
had already lost five spaces. Commissioner Wright said the angle from a traffic pattern
would make it difficult for patrons to back out and go south. Mr. Werts noted that they
have already signed the lease with Chipolte which approved the site plan. Mr. Lee
Rollands (developer) inserted via Zoom that because of the loss of parking, they are right
on the count, needing 200 stalls and having 204 now and by losing the angle parking they
would be under the Code requirement.

3.5.3.3 Commissioner Wilkey expressed that she felt the angled parking is going the wrong
direction because traffic flow will need to cut across the traffic to get into that. Then to
back out of it, they would back out across the traffic to go the right direction. Mr. Werts
felt that 90% of the traffic would be entering from the south, so all customers will be able
to pull into those spaces easily and head out to the north. It is a two-way road.
Commissioner Wilkey said that her main concern was that heading into the parking
space, one would have to cross over the lane to get to it. Usually, the angle goes with the
traffic. She said the patrons would enter and head north and to get a parking spot they
will need to cross over a lane to park. Then when they get out, they will need to back out
over a lane to get going again. Mr. Werts added that is the same situation in every drive
aisle in the parking lot and all comply with Code.
3.5.3.4 Commissioner Wright felt that is not a safe approach. He wondered if the Code
could be changed to revise this specific side of the property because of it being a traffic
issue. Mr. Rollands suggested making this a Code variance. The reason for doing
this is because starting at the Planet Fitness site, there needs to be 6 feet of landscape to
be able to meet setback requirements. If they obtained a variance instead of having
it 6 feet, reduce it down to 4 feet and then they would still be able to do the 18’ head in
parking and be able to make that work. The 1%’ needed to have 90-degree parking
could be made up eliminating a foot of landscaping and a half a foot on the other sidewalk
would make it work.
3.5.3.5 Commissioner Quigley asked what the exterior of the building was made of. Mr. Rollands
said it was brick/cinder block. Commissioner Quigley wondered if there were any designs
for pedestrian traffic included. Mr. Werts said all along the building is a 6’ wide landscape
buffer. Mr. Rollands suggested some ways they could make up the necessary footage
needed for compliance, which would directly affect the Chipolte site. Commissioner
Quigley said his concern for that would be for the larger size trucks accessing there.
However, that most cars today are compact and smaller and would fit in there fine. Mr.
Werts agreed, saying that would still allow a smaller buffer between the building and the
curb.
3.5.3.6 Mr. Spung allowed that the Planning Commission does not have the authority to grant a
variance as part of this review. The Code says one option is it does allow for compact
parking spaces, up to a certain percentage in these types of centers. He believed
the size of those type parking spaces is 16’, which would shave off 2’. There is no way to
eliminate the 6’ setback requirement from curb to building or the 5’ landscape
requirement but the length of the parking stalls could vary along the western edge that
would meet the Code. Those are things that could possibly be worked on with the
applicant to make this work better. Mr. Werts wanted to confirm that it is 6’ that
is required along Planet Fitness, to which Mr. Spung said the requirement is for the
distance from any driveable surface to the building. Any building, anywhere must be at
least 6’ from anywhere that can be driven or park, which is generally provided by a 6’
sidewalk and in this case there is no sidewalk so it would be a 6’ landscape area. Staff can
explore options with the applicant to see if a 16’ parking depth would accommodate 90-
degree parking there rather than angled.

3.5.3.7 Commissioner Wright asked if there were a way to address the number of parking
stalls, with the possibility of reducing that number. Mr. Spung said he would have to look
up that possibility in Code. Commissioner Wright talked about the brick on the existing
buildings there and the fact that a little of that has been incorporated into the new
buildings but wanted to know how to incorporate more. Mr. Werts said they are
using the same tan stone that is along the bottom of Buildings A, B, C, and D. As far as
the facades, national chain companies dictate the colors and prototypes of structures.
Commissioner Wright asked if that includes the entire facade or a portion. Mr. Werts
replied the part they have the most control over is right next to Chipolte, where those
buildings are the exact copy of Buildings A, B, and C. The brick columns on the Five Below
store has two brick columns and Petco has corporate colors and Ulta has the orange colors
with white block in their columns. He added that most national chains have their own
signature building designs, which they require on their new stores. Commissioner Wright
discussed possible changes in the type of brick he would like to see included and Mr.
Werts explained why that was not possible with these national stores.
Commissioner Wright then commented that this site has been vacant and blighted for a
long time now. He felt this was a significant location and important for the City to be able
to implement the design element that the City would hope for and not have it become
just another strip mall. The Planning Commission is able to push for that and he would
like to see additional considerations given to that. He felt this is a great opportunity to
have these tenants locate here but felt it would be nice to do a little more to provide for
continuity of that existing development on the site. Mr. Werts responded by saying look
at other prominent malls which used to be very consistent in their exteriors have now all
gone to a more tenant focus. For example, Fashion Place Mall in that their exteriors now
look very different than before because they want to distinguish themselves with more
engaging architectural appeal in order to attract customers. These new tenants do know
the prominence of this particular location and want to make it work. He asked that the
Commissioners take note that newer centers all look exactly like this one will.
3.5.3.8 Mr. Spung gave the figures for parking as the requirement for Unit A (Chipolte) — 30
spaces, and 170 spaces for the remaining stores. So, 200 would be required and they are
providing at least 202 with the site plan shown this evening. The Code does allow for the
Planning Commission to reduce the parking count. Section 13.24.080 says that reduction
may be allowed by the Planning Commission for retail businesses and shopping centers
over 80,000 square feet. Mr. Spung personally felt that retailers prefer to have a lot of
parking available for any day, any time.
3.5.3.9 Commissioner Russell then asked about the fencing requirement and Mr. Spung advised
a 6’ masonry or concrete wall adjacent to residential is required. There is an existing wall
in place now, which is old and would need some repair in spots but actually does meet
the Code presently.
3.5.3.10 Commissioner Young asked about the east facing wall by Planet Fitness and
wondered if it would be right up against Chipolte. He wanted to know of any plans for
beautification of that wall. Mr. Werts said there were no plans to change that at the
moment. Presently it is a gray cinder block wall. There has always been a plan to
have a parking lot between Planet Fitness and whatever was to be constructed.
Commissioner Young commented that there seems to be a lot of parking available in that
area, to which Mr. Werts agreed, saying that Planet Fitness wants there to be a lot of
