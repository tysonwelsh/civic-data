     Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council

     Requesting a Zone Text Change to Sections 11-2-109, 11-4-110, and 11-5-103 to

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

        City staff, as directed by the City Council, is proposing an ordinance text
        change to amend Sections 11-2-109 and 11-4-110 of the sign ordinance to
        remove the regulation exemption for noncommercial banner signs and to
        prohibit signs containing content which is not protected by the U.S.
        Constitution or the Utah Constitution. Staff is also proposing to modify
        several standards for temporary on-premises signs in Section 11-5-103.

        The first change relates to noncommercial banner signs. The current code
        in Section 11-2-109 exempts noncommercial banner signs from meeting
        any signage regulations. The proposed change will remove this exemption
        so that noncommercial banner signs must meet the same sign regulations
        that commercial banner signs are required to meet. As a point of
        information, Section 11-1-103 already allows for any noncommercial
        message to be substituted for a commercial message on any sign type
        permitted by the code.

        The second change adds another item to the list of prohibited signs in 11-
        4-110. Signage containing content which is not protected by the United
        State Constitution, or the Utah Constitution will be prohibited. Such
        content would have to be quite sexually explicit in nature or be something
        that incites violence or makes specific threats against an individual to not
        be protected under either constitution.

        The first two changes are being proposed at the request of the City
        Council. The other proposed changes to Section 11-5-103, dealing with
        temporary on-premises signs, are being suggested by City staff. These
        changes will remove formatting inconsistencies between different
        temporary sign types in the code and will also modify several standards
        for temporary signage.

 Mayor Lang opened the Public Hearing. There being no one to speak in favor or
 opposition, Mayor Lang closed the Public Hearing.

ORDINANCE 24-31: ENACT SECTION 2-1-114 OF THE WEST VALLEY CITY
MUNICIPAL CODE TO ENACT CERTAIN REQUIREMENTS CONCERNING
THE CONSENT AGENDA
Mayor Lang discussed proposed Ordinance 24-31 that would enact Section 2-1-114 of the
West Valley City Municipal Code to Enact Certain Requirements Concerning the Consent
Agenda.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Currently, most items on the City Council's agenda are subject to individual
       discussion and approval, regardless of their nature. This process can be time-
       consuming, especially for routine and non-controversial items that do not require
       extensive debate. Implementing a consent agenda for such items would allow the
       City Council to focus on more significant and contentious matters, thereby
       enhancing the overall efficiency of the meetings.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and she called for a motion.

       Councilmember Fitisemanu moved to approve Ordinance 24-31.

RESOLUTION 24-120: APPROVE THE PURCHASE OF LAPTOP COMPUTERS
AND RELATED EQUIPMENT
Mayor Lang presented proposed Resolution 24-120 that would approve the Purchase of
Laptop Computers and Related Equipment.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       IT replaces the oldest quarter of Police computers annually. This year, the
       department proposes purchasing ruggedized laptops for the patrol cars instead of
       standard models. The ruggedized laptops offer increased durability without a
       significant price increase over previous purchases. Funds are already allocated in
       the existing IT budget for this replacement cycle.

       Typically, an accidental damage warranty is added to laptop purchases. However,
       trial use of ruggedized laptops has shown they are durable enough to forgo this
       additional warranty, even in the demanding environment of patrol vehicles. The
       quoted price for the ruggedized laptops is comparable to previous non-ruggedized
       laptop purchases. Keeping computers up to date reduces the downtime for officers,
       enhances cybersecurity, and improves performance for critical police applications.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and she called for a motion.

       Councilmember Christensen moved to approve Resolution 24-120.

       Councilmember Fitisemanu seconded the motion.

RESOLUTION 24-121: APPROVE A PROFESSIONAL SERVICES AGREEMENT
BETWEEN THE CITY AND PARAMETRIX CONSULT, INC. FOR THE
PREPARATION OF A MAJOR STREET PLAN AND IMPACT FEE FACILITIES
PLAN
Mayor Lang presented proposed Resolution 24-121 that would approve a Professional
Services Agreement Between the City and Parametrix Consult, Inc. for the Preparation of a
Major Street Plan and Impact Fee Facilities Plan.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       This resolution authorizes a contract with Parametrix to update the City’s Major
       Street Plan and the City’s impact fee facilities plan for roads in order to update the
       City’s road impact fees. Parametrix will inventory the City’s current facilities,
       identify current levels of service and project facility needs to continue those levels
       of service over the next ten years.

       Impact fees are fees charged as part of new development to allow the City to
       provide facilities that will serve such development. Impact fees allow cities to build
       new facilities that are necessary to keep a consistent level of service as new
       development occurs. Impact fees cannot be used for maintenance of facilities.

       The last updates to the City’s road impact fees started in July of 2019. Since that
       time, the cost of improvements has increased substantially, effectively reducing the
       City’s ability to maintain our level of service. Other information used to calculate
       the road impact fees has changed in recent years. Examples of such data changes
       include traffic volume increases and new facility construction.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang there were no further questions from members of the City
