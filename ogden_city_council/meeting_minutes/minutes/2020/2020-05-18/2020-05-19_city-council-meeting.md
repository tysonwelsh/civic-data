Proposed Ordinance 2020-17 adopting the tentative budget for Fiscal Year
2020-2021
          A memo from Council staff regarding the Fiscal Year (FY) 2020-2021 tentative budget came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated each year during the first Council meeting in May, the Mayor is required to present a proposed
budget for the upcoming fiscal year. The Council accepts the proposed budget for review and, through a series of work sessions,
reviews the proposal over the next two months. A tentative budget is adopted after holding an initial public hearing. A second
public hearing is held at the time of adoption of the final budget. During the budget hearings, the Council outlines their proposed
changes to the budget and accepts public input. Once the final public hearing is closed, the Council makes its changes and adopts
the final City budget. By State law, a budget must be adopted by June 30. An exception to the timing is allowed if the Council is
considering a property tax increase.
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide
the Council staff review of the Mayor's proposed tentative budget for FY 2020-2021. She indicated the City Council has adopted
fmancial principles to govern the budget process; these principals include conservative revenue estimates; a balanced budget;
employee salaries as a budget propriety; strategic planning; and reserving at least 10 percent of the City's General Fund revenues
in fund balance. Top priorities for the City Council include fmancial sustainability and maintenance of essential services. The
Mayor's proposed budget does not include a proposal to increase property taxes; in the past the Council has discussed pursuing an
annual review of the property tax rate and associated revenues, but that process was not enacted this year due to the COVID-19
pandemic. However, it is important to understand that the City's property tax revenues have not kept pace with inflation; the
City's current tax revenue is $40 million, which is just 2 .41 percent greater than the City received 20 years ago in 2001. The fact
that the City can continue to operate and provide quality services with such a small increase in revenues is due to great
management and dedication of City employees. Proposed staffing changes in the tentative budget include the creation of six new
positions that are grant funded, one position being renamed, and two positions reclassified. Ms. Eller-smith then provided a
comparison of the FY20 adopted budget with the FY2 l proposed budget; the FY21 budget will be $4.4 million less than the FY20
budget. She reviewed the Council's budget strategic initiatives for economic development, community safety, recreation, City
image and reputation, and fiscal sustainability and transparency and highlighted projects and expenditures included in the budget
that are responsive to these initiatives. She then indicated Administration has provided a proposed schedule A, including minor
changes to the Mayor's proposed budget since it was presented to the Council. Schedule A includes an adjustment to the
Information Technologies (IT) fund to decrease the use of fund balance by$ 155,850 and an adjustment in the major grants fund to
shift funds to increase the microenterprise loan program funding capacity by $100,000 and the Quality Neighborhoods program by
$30,475. The Council may direct additional changes to the budget utilizing Schedule A-1. She concluded by reviewing the
timeline for adopting the fmal budget, which should culminate by June 23, 2020.
          Council member White stated that she has been a proponent of reviewing the City's property tax rate each year; she
understands why it will not be reviewed this year, but she would like to discuss the option of setting a date for performing a
thorough review of the adopted budget sometime in the next three to four months to consider amendments based on more clear
