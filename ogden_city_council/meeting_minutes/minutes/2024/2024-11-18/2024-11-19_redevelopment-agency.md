Proposed Resolution 2024-8 approving and authorizing the Executive
Director to execute a Land Exchange Agreement with Lotus 1900 Lincoln,
LLC for the redevelopment of land located at approximately between Wall
Avenue and Lincoln Avenue on 18th Street, Ogden Utah, and
approximately 1890 Lincoln Avenue, Ogden, Utah
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed Land Exchange Agreement
came before the Board for consideration. The memo stated Ogden City Administration is proposing to transfer 4.09 acres of
Redevelopment Agency owned property to Lotus 1900 Lincoln, LLC in exchange for 2.45 acres of property owned by Lotus for
the development of Phase Three of the Riverwalk development in the River Bend Redevelopment Area. The proposed land
transfer would exchange Redevelopment Agency owned property on the block between Wall Avenue and Lincoln Avenue south
of 18th Street for property owned by Lotus located east of Lincoln Avenue south of 18th Street. The properties are within the
Ogden River Redevelopment Area and are subject to the design standards of the Ogden Bend Redevelopment Master Plan and
Design Guidelines and the Mixed Use (MU) zoning ordinance. The Redevelopment Agency Board recently approved amendments
to the Ogden Bend Master Plan to accommodate the proposed land transfer in conjunction with amendments to the MU zoning
ordinance approved by the Council.
          Community and Economic Development Executive Director Johnson summarized the memo and stated other elements of
the agreement include timelines for all necessary city approvals, timelines for construction and completion of the project, and
environmental remediation. Per the agreement, the Redevelopment Agency would reimburse the developer for any environmental
remediation cost above $360,000 if a First-time Homebuyers Investment Zone (FHIZ) were to be created and money available.
This initial cost has been calculated into the overall value comparison of the properties. It is anticipated that the proposed
development will need to comply with all zoning requirements of the MU zone including an additional development agreement
containing the full detail of the proposed development. Based on the MU zoning ordinance, a development agreement will need to
be created for the development. Based on the requirements of the MU zone, that agreement would be signed and executed by the
mayor and not the Redevelopment Agency Board. Mr. Johnson also used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to orient the Board
to the concept plan for the proposed development, which conforms with the Ogden Bend Master Plan, the Alta Survey and
appraisal for the subject properties, and the conditions of closing on the land exchange. For a copy of the presentation in its
entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Public Services Executive Director Anderson also used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the relationship
between the proposed development and the section of the Ogden River that has been restored in recent years. For a copy of a
presentation in its entirety, see the information meeting for the packet.

Proposed Resolution 2024-9 approving and authorizing the Executive
Director to execute a Land Transfer and Development Agreement with
Parkway Group, LLC for the redevelopment of land located at
approximately 1820 Washington Boulevard, Ogden, Utah
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a Land Transfer and Development
Agreement (LTDA) came before the Board for consideration. The memo stated a multi-family residential and commercial
development located at approximately 18th Street and Washington Boulevard is being developed which is anticipated to include up
to 110 units in two 4-story residential buildings and a small commercial building where the Pioneer Heritage building
(Piccadilly’s building) was located. The overall project approvals have included a rezone to C-3/CO, a street vacation of River
Drive east of Washington Boulevard, both of which were approved by the Council on September 26, 2023, property sale and
transfer from the City, Redevelopment Agency, and the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) to the Parkway Group, and a
participation and incentive agreement which were approved by the City and Redevelopment Agency on November 23, 2023. At
the time of the previously approved LTDA on November 23, 2023, three UDOT properties remained unacquired by the City. The
properties are now under contract by the Redevelopment Agency and are proposed to be included in the overall land transfer to the
Parkway Group. This action is to include the three remaining UDOT properties in an updated LTDA. In addition to the three
UDOT properties, the updated land transfer and development agreement specifies that the Parkway Group will be dedicating
certain property within the riparian zone on the west bank of the Ogden River to the City as well as an update to the project
description to include two four-story multi-family buildings with a combined total of up to 110 units. The previous agreement’s
description was two five-story buildings with a total unit count of up to 115 units.
          Economic Development Manager Meess summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to offer a
conceptual plan for the project; she compared the updated concept plan including the three additional parcels with the former
concept plan that did not include the three additional parcels. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information
packet for the meeting.
          Board Member Hyer stated he has been impressed with the developer of this project and their intent to enhance the area
in a public/private manner. The river users and pedestrians will experience great improvements to the area, and he hopes other
developers will follow suit. Board member Graf agreed.
          Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2024-9, entitled:
                   “A resolution of the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency approving and authorizing the Executive Director to
                   execute a Land Transfer and Development Agreement with Parkway Group, LLC for the redevelopments of
                   land located at approximately 1820 Washington Boulevard, Ogden, Utah.”
          ON A MOTION BY BOARD MEMBER HYER AND SECONDED BY BOARD MEMBER GRAF,
RESOLUTION 2024-9 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – BOARD
MEMBERS BLAIR, GRAF, HYER, VICE CHAIR WHITE, AND CHAIR RICHEY. VOTING NO – NONE. Board
members Choberka and Myers were not present when this vote was taken.

Executive Director Comments
         Executive Director Nadolski reminded the Board of upcoming events included in the Union Station 100th Anniversary
Celebration. He also encouraged everyone to vote for Ogden’s Christmas Village in the best holiday light display contest hosted
by USA Today. He then apologized to Vice Chair White for the lack of complete information regarding the proposed Land
Exchange Agreement with Lotus but thanked the Board for ultimately supporting the resolution.

Board member Comments
         Vice Chair White stated that she feels the City needs to start re-thinking what types of projects can be sustained by the
community; Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) deals may be the best a developer can do at this time, and there are many
people in need of LIHTC housing, but at some point in time it will be necessary to look at the housing stock in the community. A
great emphasis has been placed on creating a thriving downtown area, but when it is surrounded by residences that cannot spend
money in downtown Ogden, the commercial entities are actually being dealt a disservice. The Board is repeatedly told that low-
income housing is the only type of housing developers can afford to build right now, or the only product on demand, but it is
important to consider the long-term implications and consequences of the types of developments that have been and continue to be
approved. Council member Hyer agreed, but noted there are some areas in which single-family housing is not appropriate or in
demand; in the case of the Lotus proposal, he feels this is a developer that is truly trying to work with the City to achieve common
goals and he does not believe the City was ‘painted into a corner’ for this proposal.


      There being no further business to come before the Agency, BOARD MEMBER HYER MOVED THE MEETING
ADJOURN AT 7:28 P.M. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY BOARD MEMBER GRAF, ALL VOTING AYE. Board
members Choberka and Myers were not present when this vote was taken.
