2. Request for Change of Nonconforming Use, from architect office to a general office at 2726
   Harrison Boulevard.

   Mr. Matt Lund stated he desires to operate a small trophy shop, and felt the busines would be
   similar to the previous use as an architect office. He stated the business had been in South
   Ogden, and he had purchased the business four years ago, and the sales has turned to being
   mostly by email or internet-based. While there will be a small showroom, there are seldom
   times when customers would come in and browse. Online sales are typically delivered, and
   there is no active sales force for the business.

   Ms. Corbridge stated this building has operated as a professional business for over 28 years
   and is a nonconforming use in the residential zone. As such, any change of use requires

                                              ~2~
Planning Commission approval. She stated the Commission would consider whether the new
use is more intense than the existing use. The use as a trophy shop functions much the
same as a business office, with a similar number of clients or customers as the architect
office, but will have both office space and a small showroom. No changes to the building or
site is proposed and the parking lot will remain as it currently exists. A new sign is proposed,
which will use the existing sign structure, but will be slightly larger. While the applicant is
requesting the sign have internal lighting, Staff feels this is not appropriate given the
surrounding residential area. The company has provided three sign options, with the second
sign being much larger than the existing sign, and the third option containing reflective
lettering to be illuminated by spotlight. She also indicated lawn signs have been placed on
the property which are not legal and must be removed. She stated as this is a residential
area, the sign should not be illuminated. Staff recommendation is for approval subject to the
temporary signs being removed and approval of the smaller sign with no illumination. The
business will be required to also obtain a sign permit.

Commissioner Safsten asked about the approval process for changing use for an established
nonconforming use. Ms. Corbridge responded this change of use was identified when a new
business license application was requested. If the use were to again change in the future,
Planning Commission approval would again be required. Commissioner Stoker asked why
Staff is recommending the sign not be illuminated and asked if spotlights might be allowed.
Ms. Corbridge responded this is a residential area and there are no lights along this stretch of
Harrison except what is typical for residential uses. Discussion continued relating to both
external and internal lighting and their potential effects on the surrounding residential area. It
was suggested a downward facing light from the top of the sign would be preferable to that
of a light shining from the ground onto the sign. Commissioner Southwick noted the previous
sign had no lighting and the use of reflective lettering would allow cars coming along Harrison
to see the sign. Commissioner Schade stated this property has a long history and its original
use was as a home.

Mr. Robert Bateman, 2708 Harrison stated he has lived in this area for a long time and knows
the history of this building. He stated it was originally a single-family home, owned by Mr.

he wanted to be assured the use did not turn into a retail use now or in the future. He also
asked how the busines had begun operation prior to the approval being obtained by the
Planning Commission.

Ms. Corbridge stated when the business application was reviewed, she had the
understanding the building proposed was a commercial style building in a residential zone,

When she later found it was not a commercial style building, she contacted the owner and

3. CBD Site Plan, for Fourplex at 123 Binford Street.

  Mr. Josh Green stated they are under contract to purchase this property and desire to
  construct housing for the downtown area. The proposal is for a fourplex.

  Mr. Spencer stated the review of the Commission is to determine first that the proposal meets
  the Ordinance requirements of the CBD zone, and then to assure it is consistent with both the
  General Plan and the recently adopted Make Ogden Plan for the downtown area. Because it is
  within ¼ mile of a transit stop, required parking may be reduced to 1.5 spaces per unit. He
  stated most buildings in the CBD are brick, and as the review of the Commission is to assure
  new construction is compatible with existing architecture, brick should be the main building
  material, The Make Ogden Plan limits the use of stucco as a building finish, and it’s use on the
  ground floor is not compatible with the downtown area. Staff suggests the ground floor be
  finished with brick, cement fiberboard or concrete and the material be continued on all four
  sides of the building. He stated the porch entryways need to be better defined, with a covering
  oven the entrance door, which may be accomplished with a canopy, awning or recessed door
  entry. A pedestrian path should be provided from the units to the street and a detailed
  landscaping plan is required. Lighting is allowed to provide security, but must be shielded
  from the sky so as to not extend beyond the property or attract attention. Staff also suggests
  the project be serviced with an amenity space within the green area which may be as simple

                                               ~4~
as some seating benches or tables, but could include other items such as a fire pit, barbeque,
or game tables. Staff is also concerned the 24’ access into the garages does not allow enough
room for turning into the garages, and would recommend the aisle width be extended to 26’.
The applicant is proposing the property to be surrounded with a 6’ vinyl fence which is not
appropriate in the downtown. Staff has provided some options of both metal and wrought
iron fences which would be more acceptable in the downtown area. Staff recommendation is
for approval subject to the following conditions:
1. Additional wood appearing concrete fiberboard be installed on the second and third floors
     as proposed by Staff.
2.   Architectural concrete, brick or stone be installed on the first floor, to be continued on all
     four sides of the building
3.   Stucco is not allowed and should be replaced with either base material (brick, cement,
     stone) or cement fiber board.
4.   Entrance features to the building be provided which may include a recessed entrance,
     pitched roof, awning, canopy or other approach. Final design to be approved by Staff.
5.   Provide additional details on the landscape plan to ensure pedestrian scale interest and
     compatibility in the CBD zone as recommended by Staff.
6.   Include pedestrian scale lighting plan on west side of building.
7.   Provide amenity in the landscaped area to the south of the building.
8.   Install a masonry or metal fence around the perimeter of the property.
9.  Provide a dumpster enclosure at rear of development with gated enclosure to match
    building material.
10. If and when the right of way along Binford Street is resolved the applicant or property
    owner at the time will work with the City on any reconstruction of the sidewalk, curb and
    gutter.

Mr. Josh Green stated he had prepared some slides in response to the recommendations of

4. Public Hearing, Petition to Rezone, from R-1-5 to M-1 property at 1751 Pacific Avenue.

  Mr. Sam Hunter stated this is residentially zoned property but is mostly surrounded by
  industrial uses. While there are some homes on Pacific A, these also have industrial uses with
  the homes. His desire is to combine the three parcels and construct a building to contain 2-4
  shop spaces for light industrial use such as contractor storage, machine shop or maker space.
  He stated the existing right-of-way on Pacific is very narrow and the existing traffic is primarily
  truck traffic gaining access to the existing industrial uses along the street.

  Ms. Olson stated this area has historically been zoned for industrial uses, but following the
  adoption of the Gibson Area Plan, much of the area had been rezoned as residential. These

                                               ~6~
parcels had remained zoned M-1 at that time but a petition was submitted in 2019 to rezone it
to residential, which was supported by the Gibson Plan. While the proposed development at
that time did not occur, the parcels have remained as residential, and much of the overall area
is slated as a redevelopment area for the City to construct housing. When considering
rezoning of property, the Commission is to review the Community Policies as well as the
Community Plan. These policies indicate definite edges should be created between types of
zoning, and also to consider if a particular area is in transition. She stated while Pacific
Avenue is narrow, it provides an edge between the industrial area and the residential area to
the east, while if these lots were residential, there is no definite edge as industrial zoning would
back onto residential uses. While there is a mix of industrial and residential uses along Pacific
Avenue, the area is in transition and it appears to be becoming more viable for residential use,
and the Gibson Plan supports residential development in this area,. Staff recommendation is
for denial of the petition.

While there was no public in attendance, the Commission noted they had received an email
from a property owner Vickie Robbins, expressing support of industrial zoning rather than
residential zoning, as well as expressing concern with dust in the area due to the vacant
property and the continued use of dirt driveways. She also was concerned with the existing
width of Pacific Avenue.

Mr. Sam Hunter stated the use of Pacific Avenue is typically for industrial and truck traffic, and
is not a good location for homes.

As there were no additional comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to
close the public hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Garner and passed
unanimously.

Commissioner Schade stated while the Commission had approved these parcels to be
rezoned as residential a few years ago, no development occurred, which might indicate
residential use is not viable. He stated there are industrial uses on both ends of this property
as well as across Pacific Avenue. He felt this is not a good location for residential
development. Commissioner Garner agreed due to the narrowness of Pacific Avenue, it does
not serve as a buffer between types of uses, and felt there is no future for residential
development as industrial uses are on both ends. Commissioner Safsten asked if the property
were rezoned as part of the Gibson Plan. Ms. Olson indicated while it was not included in the
rezoning of the Gibson Area when the plan was adopted, when a petition was submitted in
2019 to rezone to residential, it was found to be supported by the Gibson Plan. She also
indicted the City has been acquiring property in this area and anticipates a viable residential
neighborhood, indicating the subdivision plat previously reviewed showed Pacific Avenue
being widened to a 60’ right-of-way. Chair Graf noted the Gibson Plan also supported a bicycle
path to connect this area to the Ogden River Parkway and the FrontRunner, which would be a
benefit for residential uses. Commissioner Blaisdell commented it appears the long-range
plan for this area supports residential zoning.

5. Conditional Use Permit, for waste transfer station at 3027 Midland Drive.

  Mr. Stratford reminded the Commission and the public that this is a conditional use permit
  review, and as such the use is allowed under Utah State law so long as any anticipated
  impacts of the use can be mitigated. While neighboring property owners may not be in favor
  of the use as a transfer station, the concern of the Commission is to identify potential impacts
  of the use and assure there are ways they can be mitigated.

  Mr. Brent Bateman stated his client desires to work with the City and provide a service as both
  a recycling center and waste transfer station. The surrounding area is zoned for heavy
  industrial uses and this is a convenient location. He stated the owner made an application for
  a waste transfer station in 2014 which had never been taken to the Commission for approval,
  and this review is under the 2014 Ordinance, and determination is whether the request meets
  those requirement

  Commissioner Blaisdell asked about what is recycled in relation to what goes to the transfer
  station. Mr. Bateman answered household recyclables are sorted and baled, with recycled
  material sent to the recycle facility and what is not recyclable sent to the transfer station. Mr.
  David Rawson stated glass is recycled and made into road base, stating glass is not stored
  with other recyclables. Commissioner Boykin asked about items that re not recyclable. Mr.
  Rawson responded items become non-recyclable if they are wet or contain liquid, stating wet
  paper and plastic jugs are not recyclable. He also discussed other items such as construction
  materials and indicated there has to be a market for recyclable items or they become residue,
  stating if items in the blue bin are clean, they typically can be recycled.

  Mr. Montgomery reviewed the site plan stating a new scale is proposed, and circulation
  patterns within the site identified. A new building is proposed to contain the waste transfer
  operation. He reviewed the history of the use stating the waste transfer station ordinance was
  adopted in 1997 when it was anticipated Weber County would be building a new transfer
  station in Ogden. While the ordinance was adopted, the County built their facility outside of
  Ogden. Then in 2015 an amendment was made to the Ordinance to change the stacking
  distance for scales. Then in June 2018 a pending ordinance was adopted relating to transfer
  stations, and in November 2018 language was adopted which eliminates the use of transfer
  stations in Ogden City. Because the owner had made an application in 2014 to allow a transfer
  station which had not been reviewed by the Planning Commission, this review is based on the
  2014 Ordinance.

  He reviewed the details of the site plan, stating while there are two vehicle lanes leading to the
                                               ~8~
scale, there is no indication as to how these would que up into one lane at the scale.
Engineering is requiring a traffic study be completed to determine impacts created by
increasing the users and to avoid conflicting traffic patterns of various users. It appears there
is a conflict at the entry between garbage trucks and personal vehicles accessing the site.
Other circulation patterns within the site also need to be better defined.

He then reviewed other Ordinance requirements, stating the site shows a setback of 9’ 6”
where the Ordinance requires a 25’ setback. The Ordinance also requires five trees per 1000
square feet, which translates to a total of 405 trees while 61 are now existing. Areas within the
site not being used are required to be landscaped and buildings must meet the design
requirements of the Ordinance and some variety of design is required. No outside storage is
allowed, and hours of operation are limited to 7am until 7pm, while the recycling ordinance
now governing the use allows opening at 6am. He stated this does not govern on-site work
but is limited to the receiving of materials. A storm water detention facility also is required as
well as a financial guarantee for work to be completed. Staff feels the 2014 Ordinance has
adequate requirements to be addressed to govern a waste transfer station and of these can be
satisfied, a conditional use permit may be issued. Because no final drawings are provided to
address the Ordinance requirements, Staff feels no approval should be given until these
requirements can be satisfied. He also clarified no receiving of waste material will be allowed
until conditions are satisfied and construction and site work is completed

Mr. Paul Wheelwright expressed concern relating to the proposed building as it shows open
buildings and questioned whether these are able to be enclosed by a garage door or other
means. He also asked how the site is monitored to assure everything is contained inside
buildings. He wanted to be assured no debris or other materials would affect surrounding

Mr. Parker Bartels stated the site is cleaned nightly and stated the business desires to be a
good neighbor and part of the community and to provide a service to Ogden City and the
neighboring communities.

Mr. Merrill Marshan stated the use is important to Ogden City in that both recycling and waste
transfer can occur at one location. He stated the operation maintains a clean work space and
desires to bring recycling back as a benefit to the community.

Mr. Brent Bateman stated the owner desires to be a good neighbor, stating garbage is
removed from the site daily. He stated the process for recycling is extensive, and the ability to
provide both recycling and waste transfer at one location is a benefit to Ogden City. He stated
the owner has reviewed the conditions identified by Staff and desires approval at this time
subject to those conditions being satisfied..

Commissioner Garner expressed concern that not enough information is provided in order for
the Commission to make a decision at this time. Commissioner Southwick stated while the
applicant may have a vested right for consideration, the Commission needs to know all
impacts can be mitigated prior to granting approval. He expressed concern that the site plan
presented does not comply with the 2014 Ordinance and felt it is in the best interest of both

                                            ~9~
  the property owner and the City that a clear site plan and proposal is important prior to
  approval for a conditional use permit is granted.

              passed unanimously with Commissioners Blaisdell, Boykin, Garner, Safsten,

6. Proposed Amendment to Conditional Use for revisions to conditions for recycling center at
   3027 Midland Drive

  Mr. Brent Bateman stated this action is dependent on the waste transfer station approval, but
  the applicant feels the recommendations of Staff is acceptable.

  Mr. Montgomery stated any action relating to the waste transfer station will supersede this
  action, but one of the conditions for the amendment is that if there is no approval of the waste
  transfer station, that a time period be established to guarantee work is completed. Mr.
  Stratford suggested the Commission discuss the item and could identify alternative findings if
  necessary but would allow the process to continue.

  Mr. Montgomery explained the Planning Commission had granted approval of a revised
  conditional use permit in November 2018 at which time 14 conditions of approval were
  identified and time frames established for their completion which have since passed but not
  all work has been completed. This request is to revise or eliminate some of those conditions.
  He stated the conditions of that approval are in effect until such time as a proposed transfer
  station is constructed, but would be superseded by the new conditional use permit once it is
  constructed and operational. Until that time, care needs to be taken to protect adjacent
  properties from the potential impacts identified by the Planning Commission at that time. He
  reviewed the revisions requested by the applicant, stating while they would not be necessary if
  the waste transfer station is constructed, they are still appropriate in the event the transfer
  station is not completed within the allowed time period of the conditional use permit approval.
  As the deadlines have passed, it is appropriate to identify a new timeframe, and the
  recommendation of Staff is that all conditions of approval be completed within three months
  after the conditional use permit approval expires or if for some reason the waste transfer
  station is not constructed, the items should be completed prior to September 30, 2021.

  Mr. Stratford stated it is reasonable to extend the timeframe as recommended by Staff,
  indicating until a new building is constructed there is concern of debris which may exist in
  open areas.

  Mr. Bateman indicated netting is not necessary as items will be contained within a building
  and is an unnecessary expense, but the owner is willing to go forward with the
  recommendations of Staff.

  MOTION:     A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to amend the conditions of

               approval for the recycling center based ion the findings the revisions create a
               more manageable time frame under present review processes that can be met
               and still provide the mitigation of impacts so adjacent properties are not
               detrimentally impacted. Approval is subject to the recommendations of Staff
               extending the deadline until installed within three months of expiration of a

7. CBD Site Plan, for Riverwalk, 240 W. 20th Street.

  Mr. Joe Torman introduced his engineer, Jeremy Draper and architect John Simeander. Mr.
  Draper stated this residential project reflects the theme of the Ogden River as well as the
  Redevelopment District. Native species of trees and shrubs are proposed, with some
  mounding between buildings. A decorative fence will shield the parking from the public street.
  A trail system connects to the City’s trail network and amenities within the project include
  game tables, a barbecue area, clubhouse, picnic tables and benches as well as a par course
  exercise trail system. The buildings proposed are to be a net zero development with solar
  power panels on the roof of each building and is to be energy star rated. Units will have
  laundry facilities, storage and buildings will be serviced with four-stop elevator and high-end
  finish materials.

  Mr. Spencer stated the project proposes four buildings as well as a maintenance building.
  Two will be apartments and two will be townhome style buildings with ground floor interior
  parking. A storm drain easement area is identified, and the owners are working with
  Engineering staff to assure drainage is adequate. He stated some dimensions are not shown
  and it is unclear as to whether the buildings meet setback requirements. The applicant is
  proposing a private street, to be dedicated as a public street in the future, so a temporary fire
  turnaround is required until that dedication occurs. As the dimensions of the street are
  unclear, the setbacks of the buildings may or may not meet the Ordinance requirements. The
  fire turnaround needs to provide a 100’ diameter, which may require the removal of some
