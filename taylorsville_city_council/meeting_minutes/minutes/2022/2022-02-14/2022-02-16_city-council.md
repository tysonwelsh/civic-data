1.      WELCOME, INTRODUCTION AND PRELIMINARY MATTERS

call was conducted, wherein all council members were present.

        1.1      Opening Ceremonies – Pledge/Reverence – Council Member Knudsen
                 (Opening Ceremonies for March 2, 2022 to be arranged by Mayor
                  Overson)

Council Member Knudsen directed the Pledge of Allegiance. He then took a moment to describe
a recent instance when one of his children had made a kind gesture towards another. He reminded
those gathered how important it was to be helpful and kind to one another, particularly when there
was so much negativity in the country. We could all be helpful and positive to make the world a
better place.

        1.2      Mayor’s Report

Mayor Kristie Overson expressed her concern about the two-alarm fire that had broken out that
afternoon at the Macey’s grocery store. Although she did not yet have much information about
the incident, she indicated how grateful she was for the firefighters out of Stations No. 117 and
No. 118 as well as other Unified Fire Station, TVPD officers and Murray Police who also assisted
at the scene.

The Mayor commented on Alex Brotherson who volunteered at the West Valley Animal Shelter
which was partly owned by Taylorsville. Mr. Brotherson was the brother of fallen West Valley
Police Officer Cody Brotherson. With the help of police officers as far away as Tennessee, Alex
had raised more than $7,000 to purchase pet food for the West Valley/Taylorsville shelter. The
Fallen Hero network provided the semi-truck that was used to deliver the food to the shelter.

That month was Free Fare February with the Utah Transit Authority. The previous week Mayor
Overson had the opportunity to take Trax to meet other elected officials. She enjoyed having the
opportunity to compare notes about the various transportation experiences each had that day, and
she was pleased that all meeting participants had agreed to take public transportation to the event.
From their meeting location they proceeded to the Salt Lake County Government Center for a
demonstration involving an electric bus. She hoped that people would take advantage of the
opportunity to use the entire UTA transit system for free through the end of the month.

As always, things were moving quickly at the legislature and earlier in the day there had been a
meeting of the Senate Economic Development and Workforce Services standing committee. The
committee had discussed HB38 which had been drafted to address the huge problem of catalytic
converter thefts. The bill was the brainchild of ChamberWest and was sponsored by both Sen.
Karen Mayne and Rep. Ryan Wilcox. The bill passed out of the committee with a unanimous
favorable recommendation.

Mayor Overson explained that she had finished interviewing candidates for the Citizen Advisory
Board for the TVPD and expressed appreciation to Jay Ziolkowski who had coordinated the
interviews. There was a great pool of candidates, and she was now deliberating to determine who
would serve on the board. That decision would soon be made and reported to the city council at a
future meeting. The board would become functional as of July 1 and having the board was a
commitment that had been made when the new police department was created.

The Mayor reminded the council that a public “needs” hearing had been held in December for
CDBG (Community Development Block Grant) funding that was available. There would be an
additional hearing in March with a funding decision in April. Along a similar line, there were still
some funds available for the Taylorsville Mortgage Assistance Program. The funds were provided
by the Community Development Corporation of Utah and were intended to provide temporary
mortgage assistance for Taylorsville households impacted by COVID-19. Further information

about the funding would be provided in the March edition of the Taylorsville Journal and would
also soon be pushed out on social media platforms.

City administration and staff met with Volta representatives earlier that day and received an update
on the project design. Plans would soon be officially submitted to the planning department for
review.

She and other city representatives had also met regarding future improvements at Taylorsville Park
as well as a future park at 3200 West 6200 South. They reviewed plans and were aware that as

future. Mayor Overson pointed out that COVID had taught us how important outdoor, open and
green spaces were in our lives

Speaking of COVID-19, Salt Lake County COVID numbers were trending downward.
Taylorsville would be hosting a vaccine clinic in the city hall parking lot the following day. If
someone needed a ride to get the vaccination, they could call 2-1-1. This was a great opportunity
to offer a service to city residents if they needed it.

In honor of the Winter Olympics currently taking place in Beijing, City staff had hosted their own
tabletop Olympics earlier in the day by participating in miniature/tabletop makeshift events such
as snowboarding, ski jumping, curling, luge and bobsled. There was also a “name that flag” contest
involving identifying a variety of flags from other nations. The Youth Council also participated
in this event, and it was a good opportunity for everyone to take a brief break from their work
responsibilities and have some fun with one another. The mayor expressed her great appreciation
to Kris Heineman, Jean Ashby, and Jen Andrus for organizing the event.

3.        REPORTS

          3.1     Y2 Analytics Taylorsville City Survey Results – Kyrene Gibb/Emily Schill
                  Gray

         Kyrene Gibb summarized the most recent resident survey.

     •     746 residents scattered throughout the city responded to the survey with a margin of error
           of less than 4 percentage points
     •     Overall, most of the survey respondents said that the city was headed in the right direction
     •     Their greatest concerns were traffic, managing growth and crime
     •     Affordable housing was also on the minds of residents

     •   The preferred source of information continued to be the Taylorsville Journal although
         social media was becoming more popular than in previous surveys
     •   Overall, the new police department was viewed positively in the city
     •   Residents were largely happy with their neighborhoods although there was some room for
         improvement
     •   Residents preferred single-family homes in their own neighborhoods but felt that mixed-
         use developments could be in other neighborhoods in the city
     •   9 out of 10 respondents approved of the job being done by the city’s elected officials
     •   45% of residents felt the city had improved in the last five years while 1/3 of the residents
         report no change.
     •   Location and accessibility to the rest of the Salt Lake Valley were the things respondents
         liked most about Taylorsville
     •   Traffic, mitigating the effects of growth, and crime were the top issues of concern to survey
         respondents
     •   Overall, most of the respondents indicated that the Taylorsville Police Department was
         professional and responsive
     •   Respondents indicated that the police departments’ top priorities should be traffic issues,
         vandalism, and burglaries/robberies
     •   25% of respondents were not happy with the way their neighborhoods looked
     •   Respondents preferred that development/growth in their neighborhood be focused on
         single-family residents with large yards. They felt multi-family housing or mixed use
         development would work better in other neighborhoods besides their own
