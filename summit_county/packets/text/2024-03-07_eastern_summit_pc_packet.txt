Recommendation 
Staff has reviewed the application for compliance with all standards in the Eastern Summit 
County Development Code (Code) and found that it meets the minimum required for approval. 
Staff recommends that the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission (ESCPC) review the 
proposed Lewis View Subdivision, consider the issues outlined in this report , conduct a public 
hearing, and vote to forward a positive recommendation to the Community Development Director, 
pursuant to the findings of fact, conclusions of law and conditions of approval, found in this staff 
report.  
 
Project Description 
Project Name: Lewis View Subdivision 
Applicant(s):   Preston Tholen 
Property Owner(s):  Preston Tholen (JT) 
Location:   Unaddressed 
Zone District:   Agricultural 10 (AG-10) 
Parcel Number and Size: NS-227-F, containing 20 Acres 
Type of Process:  Subdivision Consisting of Three Lots or Less  
Final Land Use Authority: Community Development Director 
 
Proposal 
The property owner, Preston Tholen, is requesting consideration of a two (2) lot subdivision on 
Parcel NS-227-F that consists of approximately 20 acres (Exhibit A). This parcel is currently 
zoned AG-10. The parcel is not currently addressed. The proposed access is off of Lewis View 
Way. Lot 1 will be approximately 12.24 acres and lot 2 is proposed to be 8.00 acres.  
 
 
 
5
Vicinity Map 
 
  
 
Background 
Parcel NS-227-F is considered a conforming parcel located in the AG-10 zone.  The applicant 
proposes to divide Parcel NS-227-F into two (2) lots, per Eastern Summit County Development 
Code Section 11-4-5(B). The parcel is currently vacant. 
 
A joint variance application was filed in 2021 including parcels NS-227-F, NS-227-L, and NS-227-
H for a driveway grade variance in order to improve the access from East Wanship Road to 
parcels NS-227-F, NS-227-L and NS-22-H.    
 
On June 24, 2021, the Summit County Board of Adjustment granted a 5% variance to the first 
1,425 feet of the existing driveway, with conditions, allowing for a maximum of 17% grade.  The 
variance was recorded on all parcels involved in the request and all property owners agreed to 
the conditions of approval Exhibit B. An encroachment permit was issued for all improvements 
permitted as part of the 2021 variance.  To date, the work is yet to be completed but the 
encroachment permit is still valid.  
 
On December 6, 2021, an application was received for the proposed two (2) lot Lewis View 
Subdivision.  Staff routed the application to service providers for review and comment.   
 
The Summit County Planning Commission received another final subdivision application for a 
two (2) lot subdivision known as Western Sky Subdivision, located near the subject subdivision 
site, in February 2022. The Eastern Summit County Planning Commission conducted a public 
hearing on October 5, 2023 (Agenda and Minutes) for the Western Sky Subdivision. During that 
meeting, after review of the application and consideration of the public input, the Commission 
6
requested that Staff provide additional information related to several items, some of which 
were brought up again for this subject subdivision application. The related items include: septic 
feasibility and confirmation of road classification of East Wanship Road. At the December 7, 
2023 Eastern Summit County Planning Commission meeting (Agenda and Minutes), Staff 
returned with the information requested. Ultimately, the Commission voted unanimously to 
approve the two (2) lot subdivision. Staff has included the information provided at that meeting 
again below: 
  
1. Septic Feasibility 
Rocky Pace from the Summit County Health department submitted a septic feasibility 
letter dated October 12, 2023, confirming that they conducted multiple perc tests on 
site, and all passed the necessary requirements for consideration for individual systems 
Exhibit C. They do not foresee any issues with the utilization of individual wastewater 
systems, but also did not guarantee issuance of permit until all required documentation 
for permitting is submitted. As previously discussed, appropriate notes are shown on 
the plat to remind the individual property owner that they will need to demonstrate 
compliance with the Summit County Health department standards prior to issuance of a 
building permit. 
2. Confirmation of East Wanship Rd road classification 
Steve Dennis, County Engineering office confirmed that East Wanship Road is 
designated as a Class B County Road Exhibit D. Summit County currently plows and 
maintains the road. Mr. Dennis could not confirm exactly when it was originally 
designated a public road, but did state that it has been shown since the last major 
update in 2018. Additionally, I have attached the current Summit County Road map, as 
of November 30, 2023. 
 
Analysis and Findings 
Development Code 11-4-5(B) identifies the Community Development Director as the final land 
use authority for subdivisions consisting of three or less lots. Because Staff received several 
timely written objections, a Public Hearing before the Planning Commission is required. 
 
The Planning Commission may recommend to the Community Development Director approve, 
approve with conditions, or deny the proposed Subdivision based upon written findings of fact 
according to each of the following standards (see 11-4-5). It is the responsibility of the applicant 
to provide written and graphic evidence demonstrating compliance. 
 
Standard a: All the land required for the density needed to create the lots within the 
subdivision, including a remnant parcel, which on its own would not be large enough to qualify 
for any density, shall be contained within the boundaries of the final subdivision plat. COMPLIES 
 
Analysis: NS-227-F is 20.0 acres in size and is located in the AG-10 zone district.  The base 
density is one unit per twenty (20) acres and the minimum lot size is one acre. The

in the subdivision boundaries.

of the applicable zone at the time. N/A

required density.

VICINITY MAP
1. All lots within the proposed subdivision are buildable, any further subdivision of such lots, whether by deed, 
bequest divorce decree, or other recorded instrument shall not result in a buildable lot until the same has been 
approved in accordance with the Eastern Summit County Development Code.
2. The owners of property within the Eastern Summit County recognize the importance of agricultural lands and 
operations and small rural business enterprises. It is recognized that agricultural lands and operations and rural 
business enterprises have unique operating characteristics that must be respected. (Owners of each lot platted 
in this subdivision / the owner of the residence constructed upon this lot) has been given notice and recognizes 
that there are active agriculture lands and operations and rural business enterprises within Eastern Summit 
County and acknowledge(s) and respect(s) that, so long as such lands and operations exists, there may be 
dust, noise, odor, prolonged work hours, use of roadways for the purpose of herding / moving animals, and 
other attributes associated with normal agricultural operations and rural business.
3. Utilities shall have the right to install, maintain, and operate their equipment above and below ground and all 
other related facilities within the Public Utility Easements identified on this plat map as may be necessary or 
desirable in providing utility service within and without the lots identified herein, including the right of access to 
such facilities and the right to require removal of any obstructions including structures, trees and vegetation that 
may be placed within the PUE. The utility may require the lot owner to remove all structures within the PUE at 
the lot owner's expense or the utility may remove such structures at the lot owner's expense. At no time may 
any permanent structures be placed within the PUE or any other obstruction which interferes with the use of the 
PUE or any without the prior written approval of the utilities with facilities in the PUE.
4. All lots within this subdivision must meet all building permit requirements at the time of building permit 
issuance.
5. Questar approves this plat solely for the purpose of confirming that the plat contains public utility easements. 
Questar may require other easements in order to serve this development. This approval does not constitute 
abrogation or waiver of any other existing rights, obligations or liabilities provided by law or equity. This approval 
does not constitute acceptance, approval or acknowledgement of any terms contained in the plat, including 
those set forth in the Owners Dedication and the Notes and does not constitute a guarantee of particular terms 
of natural gas service. For further information please contact Questar's Right-of-Way department at 
1-800-366-8532.
6. All buildings shall meet the requirements of the current International Fire Code and the 2005 or newer 
Wildland-Urban Interface Code at the time of building permit issuance.
7. Water has not been approved for this site. It shall be the responsibility of each lot owner to demonstrate that 
water of adequate quantity is available for each lot prior to the issuance of a building permit. This shall be 
accomplished with a memorandum of decision from a state engineer for a private well or a written commitment 
from a municipality or private company. 
8. Septic or sewage has not been approved for this site. The property owner shall demonstrate that the property 
can adequately support a septic system per state/county requirements or has access to an operational, 
approved sewer system prior to the issuance of a building permit though an approval letter from the County 
Health Department.
9. Each primary residence located in the subdivision shall have a minimum two car garage and outdoor parking 
space for a minimum of three vehicles. Unscreened outdoor storage is prohibited. All livestock fences shall be 
installed so as to be wildlife friendly. 
10. All exterior materials (including fencing) must be in darker earth tone colors that blend in with natural 
surroundings. Highly reflective colors as found in the white, yellow, light red, light grey and light tan tones are 
not permitted.

3.02 SITE PREPARATION AND ACCESS
A. WALK SITE AND IDENTIFY LOCATIONS OF LIMIT OF WORK AND
ENVIRONMENTALLY SENSITIVE AREAS. ESTABLISH CONSTRUCTION
STAGING AREA, LOCATED BEYOND ENVIRONMENTALLY SENSITIVE AREAS.
B. INSTALL CONTROL MEASURES AS SHOWN ON THE DRAWINGS,
INCLUDING THOSE DEFINING THE LIMIT OF WORK.
C. LIMIT VEHICULAR TRAFFIC TO AND FROM SITE TO MINIMIZE
TRANSPORT OF SEDIMENT.
3.03 CLEARING, GRUBBING, AND STRIPPING
A. SCHEDULE GRUBBING AND STRIPPING TO OCCUR IMMEDIATELY PRIOR
TO EARTH DISTURBANCE. DEPENDING ON SITE AREA, CONSIDER
MULTIPLE GRUBBING PHASES, SEQUENCED TO TAKE ADVANTAGE OF THE
EROSION PREVENTION POTENTIAL OF EXISTING VEGETATIVE COVER.
B. MINIMIZE THE AREA OF EXISTING VEGETATION REMOVED WHEREVER
POSSIBLE.
C. LOCATE AND SIZE STOCKPILES TO MINIMIZE EROSION POTENTIAL,
TAKING ADVANTAGE OF TERRAIN SLOPE AND ASPECT, WHERE
APPROPRIATE.
D. PROTECT VEGETATION, INCLUDING ROOT SYSTEMS, BEYOND LIMIT OF
CLEARING.
E. PROCESS TIMBER, STUMPS, SLASH, AND BRUSH SO AS TO PROTECT
ENVIRONMENTALLY SENSITIVE AREAS AND INSTALLED CONTROL
MEASURES. PROPERLY DISPOSE OF EXCESS OFF SITE. PRIOR TO ANY
WORK, OBTAIN SPECIFIC APPROVAL FROM PROPERTY OWNER FOR
BURIAL OF STUMPS ON SITE.
F. ON-SITE SOIL IS PROPERTY OF OWNER. COORDINATE WITH OWNER
REGARDING COMPENSATION TO OWNER PRIOR TO EXPORT OF ANY SOIL
OFF SITE.
3.04 EXCAVATION FOR BUILDING FOUNDATIONS AND
UTILTIES
A. DEVISE AND INSTALL CONTROL MEASURES ADEQUATE TO HANDLE
DISCHARGES AND TRAP SEDIMENT FROM FOOTING SUMP AND WELL
POINT PUMPS PRIOR TO EXCAVATION.
B. ARMOR SUMP PUMP DISCHARGE LOCATIONS TO PREVENT EROSION
AT POINT OF DISCHARGE AND AREAS DOWNSTREAM.
C. IF FOUNDATION EXCAVATIONS GRADE TO DAYLIGHT ON THE LOW
SIDE, DEVISE AND INSTALL CONTROL MEASURES TO HANDLE SURFACE
AND GROUNDWATER FLOW FROM EXCAVATION LOW POINT.
D. BACKFILL UTILITY TRENCHES AS SOON AS PRACTICABLE TO PREVENT
FLOODING, SLOUGHING, POTENTIAL OVERFLOW, AND REPETITIVE EARTH
DISTURBANCE.
3.05 SITE GRADING
A. STOCKPILE EXCAVATED MATERIALS TO BAFFLE OVERLAND RUNOFF,
AVOIDING THE CREATION OF LENGTHY PATHS OF CONCENTRATED
RUNOFF.
3.06 STORMWATER MANAGEMENT SYSTEM
A. THE STORMWATER MANAGEMENT SYSTEM INCLUDES, BUT IS NOT
NECESSARILY LIMITED TO, ALL PERMANENT DETENTION / RETENTION
BASINS, DISCHARGE STRUCTURES / WEIRS, CULVERTS, OPEN
CHANNELS, CURBS, GUTTERS, PAVED SWALES, CATCH BASINS, DRAIN
MANHOLES, DRAINAGE PIPES, ROOF DRAIN MANIFOLDS, WATER QUALITY
SWALES, SEPARATORS, AND SIMILAR STORMWATER RUNOFF
CONVEYANCE, TREATMENT, AND STORAGE FACILITIES.
B. INSTALL STORMWATER MANAGEMENT SYSTEM COMPONENTS
STARTING AT THE DOWNSTREAM END AND PROCEED UPSTREAM. WHERE
POSSIBLE, COMPLETE INSTALLATION OF PERMANENT DETENTION /
RETENTION BASINS PRIOR TO BEGINNING WORK ON UPSTREAM SYSTEM
COMPONENTS.
C. INSTALL CONTROL MEASURES AT FINISHED UPSTREAM AND
DOWNSTREAM PIPE ENDS AS SOON AS POSSIBLE AFTER COMPLETION
OF PIPE RUN. SUCH MEASURES INCLUDE, BUT ARE NOT NECESSARILY
LIMITED TO, RIPRAP, CHECK DAMS, HAY BALE / SILT FENCE BARRIERS,
AND VELOCITY DISSIPATORS.
D. AT THE END OF EACH DAY OR WHEN RAINFALL IS EXPECTED, PLUG
UPSTREAM END OF PIPES / DAM OPEN CHANNELS OR OTHERWISE
REDIRECT POTENTIAL RUNOFF AND PREVENT FLOW FROM ENTERING
PARTIALLY COMPLETED SYSTEM / SYSTEM COMPONENTS.
E. WHERE PORTIONS OF A NEW SYSTEM ARE TO BE ACTIVATED PRIOR
TO COMPLETION OF THE ENTIRE SYSTEM, EMPLOY CONTROL MEASURES
TO PREVENT SILT AND DEBRIS FROM ENTERING THE SYSTEM. EMPLOY
SILT SACKS OR FABRIC ON CATCH BASIN INLETS, AND PIPE AND
CULVERT OPENINGS. EMPLOY CHECK DAMS AND TEMPORARY SEDIMENT
BASINS UPSTREAM OF AND ALONG OPEN CHANNELS, SWALES, AND
DITCHES TO TRAP SEDIMENT UPGRADIENT OF ENVIRONMENTALLY
SENSITIVE AREAS.
F. REMOVE TRAPPED SEDIMENT AND DEBRIS FROM ALL SYSTEM
COMPONENTS AFTER COMPLETION OF INSTALLATION, AND AGAIN AFTER
THE ENTIRE SITE IS PERMANENTLY STABILIZED BY ESTABLISHED
VEGETATION, FINISH LANDSCAPE MATERIALS, PAVED SURFACES,
AND/OR ROOF AREA. REMOVE TRAPPED SEDIMENT AND DEBRIS FROM
DETENTION / RETENTION BASIN BOTTOMS SO THAT FINISH BOTTOM
MATERIALS / INFILTRATION FUNCTION CONFORM TO DESIGN.

Background: 
For larger development projects, Summit County (County) has required a Transportation Impact Study 
(TIS) to evaluate the impacts that the development will have on the surrounding roadway system.  
However, there has never been a set of rules or guidelines to follow for when a TIS is required and what 
it should include.  The Summit County Transportation Division reviewed several different TIS guidelines 
from multiple sources to create our own set of guidelines. 
 
Guidelines:

projects.  The guidelines will provide a technical approach that will benefit both the consulting 
community and County.  This document establishes protocol for TIS and mitiga tion based on 
transportation planning and engineering practices.  This is not an exhaustive list and requirements can 
change based on Summit County Planning Commissions and County Council comments throughout the 
planning process.

vehicle trips during the weekday AM or PM peak hour.  Table 1 shows the approximate land use size

Transportation Impact Study Guidelines 
Introduction 
This document is meant to assist applicants when assessing transportation impacts from 
development projects.  The guidelines will provide a technical approach that will benefit both 
the consulting community and Summit County (County).  This document establishes protocol for 
transportation impact study (TIS) and mitigation based on transportation planning and 
engineering practices.  This is not an exhaustive list and requirements can change based on 
Summit County Planning Commissions and County Council comments throughout the planning 
process. 
Review Process 
TIS should be submitted to the County with the submittal of the development application as 
action will not be taken on the application until final approval of the TIS. Along with the TIS, the 
intake checklist should be completed and included with the submittal. 
The TIS shall be prepared by a professional engineer registered in the State of Utah. All work 
shall be stamped and signed. The applicant is responsible for hiring the engineer to perform the 
TIS. 
If a TIS submittal is deemed incomplete or outdated, the County will not initiate a review until 
all components are submitted. The engineer and applicant will be notified of the deficiencies in 
the submittal. This notification shall in no way constitute a review of the TIS. Once the TIS 
submittal is deemed complete, the County will provide, in writing to the engineer, applicant, 
and lead land use agency, an estimated timeline for the review. The review time needed will 
vary depending on the number of traffic studies in the “queue” and the complexity and 
accuracy of the submitted study. 
 
66
Study Requirement Threshold 
A TIS will be required prior to the approval of a project at the time of the application if the 
project will generate 25 or more net new vehicle trips during the weekday AM or PM peak hour 
or other analysis hour at the discretion of the Transportation Planning Director.  The 
Transportation Planning Director also reserves the right to require or not require a TIS for any 
other reason.   
Table 1 shows the approximate land use size that would generate 25 new vehicle trips during 
the weekday PM peak hour.  In addition to the weekday PM peak hour, the weekday AM peak 
hour should also be reviewed to estimate the trip generation of the proposed development.  
Proposed developments may take credit for vehicle trips generated by existing land uses to be 
replaced on the project site or pass by trips. 
A full listing of trip generation rates can be found in the latest version of the Institute of 
Transportation Engineers Trip Generation Manual.

Project Scoping 
 
Scoping is the first step when it is determined that a TIS is required for a development.  The 
consultant should meet with the Summit County Transportation Planning Division to discuss the 
items that are best suited for the type and level of analysis that will be necessary for the 
project.   
• Description of the proposed development’s land uses 
• Anticipated construction schedule 
• Preliminary site plan 
• Project location and site access 
• Estimated vehicular trip generation and mode share assumptions 
• Project trip distribution 
• Proposed study intersections 
67
• Proposed traffic volume growth rates 
• Any other relevant project-specific information 
 
Study Time Period 
 
In the Snyderville Basin area where there is seasonal traffic, the TIS should reflect a typical 
weekday during the typical winter (January to mid-March) period.  If traffic counts are taken 
outside of the typical winter weekday, then a seasonal adjustment factor may be used.  The 
seasonal adjustment factor will need to be approved by the Transportation Planning Division.  
At some intersections, it may be appropriate to increase or decrease specific movements (e.g., 
those which would or would not be affected by seasonal traffic) by higher percentages than 
others.  However, the total entering intersection volume should be adjusted consistent with the 
seasonal adjustment factor.  The methodology used to factor intersection traffic volumes should 
be described in detail in the TIS. 
 
In the rest of the county the TIS should reflect a typical weekday.  The required study time 
periods include the highest one hour of adjacent street traffic during the AM (8-10 a.m.) peak 
and PM (3:30-5:30) peak hours. 
 
Project Trip Generation 
 
Site-generated trips for all modes of travel (e.g., vehicular, walking, bicycling, and transit) 
expected for the proposed development should be estimated using the trip rates and 
methodologies described in the most recent editions of the ITE Trip Generation and ITE Trip 
Generation Handbook. 
 
When available, additional information collected from surveys of similar developments, local 
mode share data, or US Census data can also be utilized to aid in calculations of trip generation 
and mode split estimates. Assumptions used to determine the trip generation (including transit 
and non-motorized transportation mode splits), as well as and supplemental backup data, 
should be clearly described and documented in the project scoping memo for approval and in 
the completed TIS. 
 
Project Trip Distribution and Assignment 
 
The project trip distribution should be determined using the best available information for the 
area with project trips assigned to the roadway network using realistic estimates.  Where 
applicable, the latest version of the Summit/Wasatch travel demand model should be used for 
trip distribution. The methodology and assumptions used to develop the project trip 
distribution should be outlined and documented in the scoping memo and the completed TIS. 
 
 
 
 
68
Traffic Volumes 
 
The TIS horizon years should correlate with the buildout of the project, development phasing, 
and/or Summit County Long Range Transportation Plan (LRTP) phases.  Traffic Volumes should 
be developed for the following analysis scenarios: 
 
• Existing 
• Year of project opening (if different from existing) without project generated traffic 
volumes 
• Year of project opening with project generated traffic volumes 
• Five years beyond year of project opening without project generated traffic 
• Five years beyond year of project opening with project generated traffic 
• Larger projects may be required to match the different phases of the Summit County 
LRTP 
 
If a project is anticipated to be completed in phases, each anticipated phase should be 
evaluated before full buildout.  Future traffic volumes should be developed using annual growth 
rates determined by the Summit-Wasatch travel demand model for a baseline. 
 
Traffic Operations Analysis 
 
Traffic operations at study intersections should be analyzed using the most recent Highway 
Capacity Manual methodology.  Peak hour factors should be calculated and applied to the 
intersection as a whole, whereas heavy vehicle percentages should be applied by approach or 
individual movement. Each study intersection’s level of service (LOS) and average vehicle delay 
should be reported. The County requires an operational standard to be maintained at a LOS C 
for all County roads and intersections and a LOS D for State roads. 
 
In addition to vehicular LOS, the 50th and 95th percentile queue lengths and available queue 
storage should be reported for each intersection.  At intersections where the 50th percentile 
peak hour queue exceeds the available storage, the traffic operations analysis should be 
reviewed to determine if spillback queueing affects the traffic operations at other intersections 
within the roadway network. 
 
Mitigation measures should be examined for intersections the exceed the LOS standard or 
produce queues that affect traffic operations at other intersections. Software files for all analysis 
and mitigation scenarios should be submitted to the Transportation Planning Division along with 
the TIS. 
 
Site Access Analysis 
 
The site access analysis should include the following: 
 
69
• The connection of how each vehicular site access connects to the existing roadway 
system and the LOS at each access point should be evaluated.  
• The access points for people walking and biking to the proposed development should be 
described.   
• The location of commercial vehicle access for loading/unloading and deliveries should 
be evaluated. 
 
Traffic Safety 
 
Historical crash data should be summarized at each intersection for the previous five-year 
period using records from the Numetric database. Crash patterns should be identified and 
described along with anticipated changes that could occur with the proposed project’s 
development. For any proposed roadway improvements, a safety analysis using crash 
modification factor countermeasures as outlined in the AASHTO Highway Safety Manual should 
be conducted to compare the predictive safety of different types of treatments that could be 
implemented at the location. 
 
On roadways with higher speeds, turn lanes and deceleration/acceleration lanes may be 
required for safety even if the LOS thresholds are met. 
 
Non-Motorized Transportation 
 
An analysis of the pedestrian and bicycle system should be documented, shown on a site plan, 
and include all the following that apply: 
 
Pedestrian System Analysis: 
• Completeness of the pedestrian network on site 
• Connectivity of the site pedestrian network to the surrounding existing network 
• Accessibility to nearby shared off-street paths 
• Direct pedestrian routes rather than circuitous routes between pedestrian destinations 
• Gaps in the area’s existing pedestrian facilities that could prevent or discourage 
pedestrian accessing the development
