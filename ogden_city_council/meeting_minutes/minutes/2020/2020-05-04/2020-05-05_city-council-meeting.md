Proposed 2020 Ogden-Hinckley Airport Master Plan
          The City contracted with Airport Development Group in August 2018 to develop a proposed 2020 Ogden-Hinckley
Airpmt (Airport) Master Plan (Plan). The Plan will serve as a guide for the continued development of the Airport as well as
meeting requirements from the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) to receive federal funding. The Plan covers six basic areas
including inventory, forecasts, facility requirements, development alternatives, implementation, and an airport layout plan. This is
the first comprehensive master plan developed for the Airport since 1994. The Plan will be accompanied by updates to the
Transportation element of the City's General Plan. Those amendments will be addressed in a separate item for consideration.
          Airport Manager Bryant Ganett made a presentation to summarize the Plan, the purposes of which include the following:
               •   Define Big Picture/Vision for 20+ Years;
               •   Respond to industry, air transportation system, and community trends;
               •   Justify to the FAA the need for development projects; and
               •   To be eligible for FAA funding up to 90.63 percent, a project must be shown on the approved Airport Layout
                   Plan (ALP).
          The community outreach program utilized to facilitate development of the Plan entailed a user/pilot survey, two public
infonnation workshops/open houses that were publicly advertised, five Planning Advisory Committee (PAC) work session
meetings over an 18-month period; and communication via newsletters, email, and a dedicated webpage. He reviewed the
membership roster for the PAC and explained there is representation from many different sectors or stakeholders that have an
opinion about what should be occUITing at the Airport. He discussed the components of the Plan, which include an Airport
Geographic Infonnation System (AGIS) survey; inventory of existing on and off airport conditions; forecasts of aviation demand;
facility requirements; development alternatives; plan implementation proposals; and ALP drawings. He gave an overview of
forecasting information, which requires FAA approval. Forecasts cover activities regarding passenger totals, based aircraft, and
aircraft operations for the base year, short term (2023), intermediate term (2028), and long term (2038). Relative to facility
requirements, the Plan addresses parking for additional aircraft needs, the total additional apron area required, hangar inventory
and condition, additional hangar needs, terminal space, and terminal parking for the same shmt tenn, intermediate term, and long-
term ranges. Long-term development considerations include:

used to determine project funding eligibility for the FAA and other funding sources.
          There was discussion among the Council and Mr. Garrett centered on the necessary hangar or terminal improvements at
the Airport to generate revenue sufficient to justify the investments called for in the Plan document. Mr. Garrett stated it is his
philosophy that if the City continues to do what it has done in the past, the same results will be seen at the Airport; however, if the
City wants to erase the operational deficit for the Airport, it is necessary to make changes relative to the investment level and
policies governing the Airport. He has studied 27 airports and 26 of them assumed the Fixed-Base Operator (FBO) role, resulting
in generating a profit after a five-year time frame. He recommends that the City create a five to six-year phased plan to move
toward the FBO model. Dana Hartshorn, consultant with Airport Development Group, noted that the east side of the Airport is
nearly built out and in order to achieve the higher level of aircraft activity desired and necessary to result in revenue generation,
the City needs more hangars and the areas north and south of the terminal area are prime for that type of development.
          Discussion shifted to the long-term business plan for the Airport and whether the City should focus on commercial
passenger travel or material shipping air service. Community and Economic Development Director Christopulos stated he does
not want to eliminate the possibility of commercial air traffic at the Airport and he will continue to aggressively recruit
commercial carries, but other attempts should also be made to salvage and improve upon the current assets at the Airport. This can
be accomplished through landlord/tenant arrangement for hangar space. Mr. Garrett agreed and indicated he has no doubts that the
Airport can be profitable. He likened the facility to Business Depot Ogden (BDO) and asked if anyone in the City would have
believed BDO would be successful as it now is when it was first being turned over to the City from the Federal Government.
          Chair Choberka thanked Mr. Garrett and representatives of Airport Development Group for the information provided
tonight.

Airport Terminal Roof and Infrastructure Planning
          The City Council has been asked to consider a CIP amendment to incorporate a new project into the current plan: Project
AR005 - Airport Terminal Building Improvements. This is an airport terminal roof improvement project to address leaks
throughout the building. This also addresses restaurant area improvements with efforts undeiway to reincorporate a restaurant and
space for a new rental car business. The Administration is currently working with the Bickering Sisters and Hertz Rental Car, with
an initial desire to complete this project in Spring 2020, this timeline in being adjusted due to COVID-19.
          Planning Manager Montgomery explained the Planning Commission approved a unanimous, 8-0, recommendation to
amend the CIP finding that the AR005 project is consistent with the City's General Plan to continue to improve, promote and
expand utilization of the Airpmt. No public comments were provided during the Planning Commission's review and
consideration.
          Mr. Garrett presented photographs of the airport terminal to illustrate the disrepair throughout the facility that will be
addressed by this project. Repairs have been made in the past, but the quality of those repairs is not sufficient to ensure a long life
of the building's roof. Eventually the roof will fully fail and there will be extensive damage to the interior of the building, which
will be very costly for the City and any user located therein.
          Comptroller Stout summarized the funding sources that have been identified for the project, including $100,000 in the
Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) Fund Critical Contingency account and Business Depot Ogden (BDO) lease revenue; the total
appropriation will be $320,000. Council member Nadolski asked about the remaining balance of unappropriated BDO lease
revenue available to the City. Ms. Stout summarized the planned transfers from unappropriated BDO lease revenue, noting that if
this proposal is approved, there will be $395,000 available. If that amount remains unappropriated, it can be carried forward to the
next FY as unappropriated available funding.
          Council Policy Analyst Mabey stated that Administration is proposing that the project be included in the current year
CIP, but given impacts COVID-19 has had on the timing of projects and Council actions, it may be appropriate to include the
project in the CIP plan for FY 2021-2025, adoption of which will be considered by the Council in the near future.
