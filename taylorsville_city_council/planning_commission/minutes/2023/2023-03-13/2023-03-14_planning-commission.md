                                            GENERAL MEETING – 6:00 P.M.

CITIZENS/GUESTS PRESENT: In person and via Zoom. Bob Knudsen and Ernest Burgess.

Discussion Item

    1.   Mr. Jim Spung presented this item. This meeting consisted of review of the Taylorsville Land Development
         Code Update Project – Chapter 13.03: Use Regulations.                  Mr. Spung went over his power point
         presentation and the latest draft of the Chapter which included the most recent notes therein. In general,
         tonight the discussion revolved around: (1) Consolidation and reorganization of all use-related standards
         into one chapter; (2) Creation of a new table of uses for all zoning districts; (3) Updating levels of permission
         for uses; and (4) Updating definitions and use-specific standards. He went over the current use lists and
         addressed the new consolidated use table. He discussed housing diversity and diversified housing options
         which included (1) To allow a variety of housing types; (2) Removal of barriers that prevent development
         of housing at different price points; and (3) Consideration of new incentives to encourage housing variety
         in targeted areas.

    2.   The majority of the ensuing discussion included Mr. Spung saying that an Administrative Conditional Use is
         staff level review and Director approval, with Non-administrative Conditional Uses going before the
         Planning Commission. Mr. Spung went into detail with review of the Use Table in the document, focusing
         on the changes made therein to consolidate and make it more user-friendly.

    3.   Mr. Spung advised Staff will be working on all overlay zones, beginning with one for the Jordan River
         Corridor, including design requirements before incorporation into the general standards.

    4.   Another important element discussed was the need for diversified type housing in Taylorsville. That right
         now the only diversification is in what size the apartments can be. Commissioner Quigley was of the
         opinion that is determined by what generation is being dealt with. Younger people are satisfied in renting,
         which gives them more flexibility in where they live. Older adults like the stability of owning their homes.

         house payments to buy. The problem in Taylorsville is how to provide affordable housing which includes a
         variety of options for customers. Commissioner Quigley felt that taxation is the biggest driving force and
         felt it would be helpful if Congress could make it possible to write off a portion of the tax interest and
         increase the Standard Deduction amount. Commissioner Wright added that it is also important to provide
         housing where people want to stay and build a community. Commissioner Wendel agreed that younger
         people today are less interested in buying than just renting for the time being.

    5.   Mr. Spung explained in detail the new direction being taken for the tables within the chapter. He showed
         the Commissioners the easiest way to navigate the tables and answered their questions in that regard.

    6.   Mr. Spung felt it would make sense to center development where public transit is available. He realized it
         is hard to envision because there is no master plan in place for it right now. Commissioner Quigley said
         that the Commission is trying to give the City Council directional tools but the Council will still drive what is
         finally done. Mr. Spung likened that to the Commissioner supplying a pallet of many colors to the City
         Council for them to choose which ones they prefer.

    7.   Commissioner Wendel asked about short-term rentals and Mr. Spung advised that portion of the update
         has not been completed but there will be a “place holder” left for it in the new Ordinance.

    8.   Mr. Spung said that affordable housing is on everyone’s mind. That there are different approaches to take
         for that, one being giving some type of incentive to do it or don’t give them the choice by adding a certain
         percentage must be affordable housing. It depends on how aggressive the City Council wants to be.
         That permanent and affordable housing are both important. The problem is a concern over having too
         many short-term rentals to compensate for the loss of permanent housing.

    9.   Commissioner Wendell suggested the problem of having too many too close together could be regulated
         like day care. Mr. Spung added that there could also be a limit on the number of occupants allowed. That
         would help eliminate there being events hosted which would change the character of the neighborhood.
         Also, to say the structure must remain looking like a family home could be written in the standards.

    10. Commissioner McElreath felt a homeless research center also needs to be addressed.            Mr. Spung said
        the City can be proactive and make the rules or have the City Council give direction on that. Mr. Spung said
        that specific use has not been included in the document yet, however, that can be done right now.
        Commissioner Quigley said he understood the premise but did not want to go too far and encourage that
        particular use in the City right now. Mr. Spung suggested not saying they are “not allowed” but rather
        would only “be allowed”when. Staff and the Planning Commission need to make specific recommendations
        in this regard. Mr. Spung said that Staff will continue to work on that issue, along with the affordable
        housing one. He said another land use issue not addressed is Cannabis-Marijuana use. Commissioner
        Quigley also suggested the need was great for more open space and pocket parks throughout the City.

    11. Mr. Spung then reviewed the different types of retail sales groups within the City as being (1) Small (such
        as Carriage Square cubicles); (2) Medium-large (Walgreens); (3) Large- (Supermarket); and (4) Big box (Wal
        Mart). The new Code could include standards for each different size. Commissioner Wright suggested
        putting small retail shopping areas within residential zones. Mr. Spung said that would work in certain
        types of areas but would require zone changes and compliance with building codes. The intent is to let the
        market figure out what works.

    12.         Commissioners made several suggestions regarding the tables in the document, including how many
          car washes are needed in the City, places where self-storage areas are allowed, dealing with commercial
          uses in residential areas, Accessory Dwelling Units, allowances for having farm animals, portable storage
          units (POD’s), Tattoo parlors, etc. Mr. Spung compiled a list and will bring back suggestions for discussion
          at a future Planning Commission meeting. This completed the discussion for this evening.

    13. Commissioner Wendel then asked if there were any training sessions scheduled that the Planning
        Commissioners could attend and Mr. Spung advised he would check on that. He added that Mark McGrath
        (Long Range Planner for Taylorsville) had suggested a new book for the Commissioners to read titled “The
        Color of Law”. That is a history of zoning laws and descriptions. Mr. Spung will look into that as a possible
        book club event for the Commission.

ADJOURNMENT: By motion of Commissioner Wendel, the meeting was adjourned at 08:35 p.m.
