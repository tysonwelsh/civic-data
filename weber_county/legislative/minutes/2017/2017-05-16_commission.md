E. PRESENTATION ON THE WEBER COUNTY ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES
   Sarah Swan, Administrative Services Director, noted that her department is comprised of Human Resources
   and Information Technology. She expressed thanks for the Commission and her staff’s support. Their main
   focus is to change the corporate culture at the county and to move into the digital age. She highlighted recent
   employee events as well as planned future ones. Administrative Services has been working with the county’s
   benefit broker to move toward an online benefit enrollment platform. The county has partnered with UCIP to
   utilize their bank of online training. They are updating the Personnel Policy manual and developing a
   performance management process. Chair Ebert noted the tremendous amount of work already accomplished
   in a short time. Commissioner Gibson introduced Shana Hunter, a valued employee since 1982, as the
   employee of the month. Ms. Hunter is retiring this year.

   5. FIRST READING OF ORDINANCE AMENDING THE ALCOHOLIC BEVERAGES ORDINANCE FOR PINEVIEW
      & CAUSEY RESERVOIRS.
       Holin Wilbanks, County Public Affairs Director, noted that usage of Pineview Reservoir and surrounding
       beach and trail areas continues to grow and there are increased safety concerns. Last year the county
       formed a community advisory team comprised of a cross-section of users. One of their recommendations
       is to limit alcohol to designated areas. Another of their concerns is the issue of trash in/around the
       Reservoirs, the majority of which is related to alcoholic beverages. Lt. Brandon Toll, of the County
       Sheriff’s Office, pointed out that the majority of incidents of lewdness, fights, DUIs with fatal crashes,
       drownings, etc., in Ogden Valley’s recreation areas are alcohol related. The U.S. Forest Service has been
       a great partner in this. District Ranger Sean Harwood stated that this item had been well described. Doug
       Allen, Huntsville councilmember, applauded this ordinance and the Commission’s work.
       Commissioner Gibson moved to approve the first reading of the ordinance amending the alcoholic
       beverages ordinance for Pineview and Causey Reservoirs; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
       Commissioner Gibson – aye; Commissioner Harvey – aye; Chair Ebert – aye
   6. FIRST READING OF AN ORDINANCE AMENDING OVERNIGHT WATERCRAFT USE ORDINANCE FOR
      PINEVIEW RESERVOIR.
       Holin Wilbanks, County Public Affairs Director, noted that last year the county adopted the overnight
       anchor permit for the Reservoir. This ordinance allows for a season option to that permit, a good benefit
       for multiple night users. Chair Ebert stated that reports from Ogden Valley residents is that they have
       seen a tremendous difference in the behavior and self policing since the ordinances were adopted. Lt.
       Brandon Toll, of the County Sheriff’s Office, concurred stating that multiple boaters have told marine
       deputies that it has turned out to be an enjoyable and quiet experience on the lake.
       Commissioner Gibson moved to approve the first reading of the amending the alcoholic beverages
       ordinance for Pineview and Causey Reservoirs; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
       Commissioner Gibson – aye; Commissioner Harvey – aye; Chair Ebert – aye
H. PUBLIC COMMENTS (all relate to the above Reservoir items):
   Steve Luger, of Uintah, one of the boaters, said that having the law enforcement at Pineview did not help but
   the county starting actions last year was enough to allow boaters to start self-policing, which had the most
   impact, and the boaters should get the credit. Chair Ebert noted that the community had seen tremendous
   change in behavior and that it involved self policing. Mr. Luger said that the Commission took many email
   addresses last year relating to this item and had indicated that they would keep the boating community
   involved and informed, however, the boaters received emails on July 1 at about 4:50 p.m. regarding what
   would be occurring on Tuesday, July 5, which was not timely at all.

   Mr. Luger requested that boaters be informed in a timely manner. Commissioner Harvey said that there will
   be more timely communication. He agrees with today’s ordinances; he picks up the alcoholic beverage trash
   all the time. He also agrees with the season pass so boaters do not have to go back every night to renew the
   pass. He said that last year a commissioner had felt intimated on the Sheriff’s boat when boaters were acting
   in a threatening manner, but he could find no one who had been a part of that. Commissioner Gibson stated
   that it is obvious there have been problems there and feels that they are on the right path to correct them and
   that there has been progress. He asked Ms. Wilbanks to make sure that there is early communication.
   Charles Gordon, of Layton, is an avid Pineview boater and runs the Pineview Boating facebook webpage. He
   is concerned with the overall fees—there is a fee to camp for out of towners, a fee to put their boats in, the
   overnight fees, etc. For a regular boater, like himself, who boats every other weekend it is costly, about $300
   a season just to be able to stay overnight on his boat. Last year he never saw a deputy sheriff on the water at
   night or that his boat was checked for the overnight pass. He asked where all that money is going; if he is
   paying all of this money for the deputy sheriff to be there then one needs to be present. He would like to see
   reasonable fees and to get what he is paying for. Last year he was attacked violently on his boat by a drunk.
   He filed a complaint with the Sheriff, pointing out the boat, the person’s name and witnesses. The deputy did
   not want to confront the drunk because of the many boats around there. Mr. Gordon understood not
   confronting the person that night but he has talked with the deputies since and after one year it still is not
   addressed. He stated that self policing has helped a lot. Chair Ebert noted that Lt. Toll was present and
   would make sure those issues were addressed. County fees were based upon service call needs and law
   enforcement staffing, but this was implemented very late in the year and that is when the fees took effect. He
   noted that there are Forest Service launch mandated fees that are not related to the county’s fees.
   Doug Allen, of Huntsvile, said that the county is doing a great job following up on this issue. New
   regulations take adjustments and awhile to get known. Permits are a record of who is out on the Reservoir.
   The population of Utah has grown tenfold since 1958 when the Reservoir was put in, and to his knowledge,
   with the exception of the one boat ramp at Anderson Cove and the Spring Creek parking area there has not
   been a single addition to the recreation facilities in the Ogden Valley. In 1975 signs appeared at the mouth of
   Ogden Canyon and on Hwy 89 that say, “Ogden Valley Recreation Area.” These are State recreation signs
   but in the entire Upper Valley there is not a square inch of State owned/administered land. It is all either
   Forest Service or private. When boats, snowmobiles, etc., are registered a lot of money is being paid to the
   State and he wondered if some of it can be tapped into to help with law enforcement funding to help keep
   people safe. He expressed appreciation for the good work on these issues and stated that Huntsville residents
   are very happy with the efforts. Chair Ebert noted that the Forest Service is adamant about self inspection for
   invasive species and there is a requirement to get onto Pineview. Holin Wilbanks, County Public Affairs
   Director, will post this requirement and other information on the Pineview Boating website. Commissioner
   Gibson complimented the deputy sheriffs on their hard work.
