      2. Administrative Reports
         2.1. Discussion of the 10-year Capital Improvement Plan – Kyle Maurer; Director of
              Finance and Administrative Services | Trevor Ram, Management Analyst | Bryce
              Terry, City Engineer
      Finance Director Kyle Maurer presented the updated 10-year Capital Improvement Plan (CIP)
      to the Council, explaining that staff had recommended modifications based on revised
      revenue projections for impact fee and general capital project funds. Director Maurer
      detailed the critical situation regarding park impact fees, which were projected to be about
      $10 million less than initially estimated. Transportation impact fees were deemed sufficient,
      though they might require reevaluation after a forthcoming study involving the Olympia and
      Panorama developments. Director Maurer noted the General Capital Project fund lacked a
      dedicated funding source, highlighting a significant challenge for future budgeting.

      Director Maurer provided insights into the process staff used to analyze and prioritize
      projects, including reviews with various departments, meetings with the CIP committee, and
April 9, 2025 City Council Meeting Minutes                                              Page 3 of 9


      discussions with Engineering and Public Works. This comprehensive review ensured that all
      factors, such as safety, system need, external funding, preservation of existing assets, and
      community enhancement, were considered in the decision-making process.

      He outlined several significant proposed changes for major park projects. For Mountain Ridge
      Park, an initial $1.5 million allocation was suggested, with the elimination of a $3.4 million
      Phase 2. History Park saw a reduction in funding from $4.4 million to $2.5 million,
      emphasizing the need to seek additional TRCC grants to bridge the gap. Jackson Park's
      funding faced a substantial cut from $8 million to $4 million, necessitating a complete
      redesign to match the available budget. The proposal included having the Friends of Herriman
      fund the jump park portion of the Juniper Canyon project, highlighting community
      collaboration in project financing.

      For general fund projects, Director Maurer explained that many, including the 7300 West
      extension phase 3 and certain park and facility projects, were proposed to be deferred or
      eliminated due to inadequate funding. The use of CDBG funds for the Community Center roof
      replacement was suggested as a potential source for addressing specific needs.

      Extensive discussion among the Council highlighted several concerns. There was a strong
      emphasis on the need for dust mitigation on roads like 7300 West, given its public status
      despite being unsealed. The council debated the high costs associated with restroom facilities
      and splash pad replacements, considering the balance between building quality parks versus
      more basic parks in multiple locations. The importance of planning for long-term
      maintenance and replacement costs for City amenities was emphasized, with suggestions to
      phase larger projects to match available funding.

      Councilmember Shields stressed the inconsistency in the allocation of funds, with a reference
      to understanding the discrepancies in impact and general fund allocations. He questioned
      whether the funds should focus on developing fewer high-quality parks or maintaining a
      broader but less developed network of park facilities. The Council agreed there was a need
      for a balanced approach that considers current public demand while planning for future
      maintenance and replacements.

          9.2. Consideration to Proceed with a Rose Creek Floodplain Update – Jonathan
               Bowers, Public Utilities Engineering Manager
      Public Utilities Engineering Manager Jonathan Bowers offered an overview of the Rose Creek
      Floodplain. He explained that Community Development Director Blake Thomas serves as the
      floodplain administrator for Herriman City and is required to adhere to the guidelines and
      requirements set forth by the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) as well as the city
      code for development within floodplain areas. Manager Bowers highlighted that Rose Creek
      was primarily designated as a Zone A floodplain, characterized by its conservatively
      estimated boundary based on nominal data and lacking detailed study. He noted that although
April 9, 2025 City Council Meeting Minutes                                            Page 6 of 9


      some sections of the creek had undergone more thorough analysis by developers which
      resulted in narrower floodplain designations, most of Rose Creek maintained a broad and
      general floodplain estimation.

      Manager Bowers also discussed a tentative study conducted in 2014, which suggested that a
      detailed investigation could potentially remove around 60 parcels from the floodplain
      designation, thus alleviating some of the regulatory burdens associated with being located
      within designated floodplain areas. He emphasized that while this study would result in a
      more accurate delineation of the floodplain, it would not alter any existing flood risks but
      provide property owners and city planners with a clearer understanding of floodplain
      boundaries.

      Manager Bowers elaborated on how structures situated within the floodplain are considered
      non-compliant under current regulations, which imposed limitations on the extent of
      improvements or additions that homeowners may wish to pursue. He highlighted the financial
      implications for properties within the floodplain, including the typical requirement of
      acquiring flood insurance, which often comes at a significant cost. The proposed detailed
      study, estimated at a cost of approximately $32,000, held the potential to remove around 58-
      60 parcels from the floodplain, thereby offering relief to property owners from certain
      regulatory constraints and potentially lowering their insurance premiums.

      The Council discussed the potential benefits for residents, noting the reduced regulatory
      requirements and possible cost savings on insurance premiums as significant advantages of
      proceeding with the study. They acknowledged that accurately identifying the true location
      of the floodplain was valuable information for property owners, as it could enable them to
      make informed decisions about property improvements and insurance needs.

      Councilmember Ohrn moved to proceed with a study to update the Rose Creek Floodplain.
      Councilmember Shields seconded the motion.

      The vote was recorded as follows:
      Councilmember Jared Henderson               Aye
      Councilmember Teddy Hodges                  Aye
      Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn                  Aye
      Councilmember Steven Shields                Nay
      Mayor Lorin Palmer                          Aye

      The motion passed unanimously.

          9.3. Approval of a Memorandum of Understanding between Herriman City and Land
               Reserve, LLC – Wendy Thomas, Assistant City Manager
      Community Development Director Blake Thomas presented information on the
      Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) between Herriman City and Land Reserve, LLC,
      which was part of a property swap involving open space and potential athletic complex space.
      He highlighted key terms of the MOU like Land Reserve would convey park property to
      Herriman, including a 3/4-acre piece near Olympia. The amendments to the Creek Ridge
      Master Development Agreement (MDA) included reducing the commercial acreage
      requirement from 7 to 3.7 acres, removing the requirement for the City to spend $2 million
      on a park, allowing 27 single-family residential units in a designated area, and restricting
      development in the park area to single-family residential or open space/parks. The City would
      be responsible for paying rollback taxes to the county, and a covenant not to sue Kennecott
      Copper was included. Director Thomas provided a summary of unit counts and changes in
      different areas of the development, mentioning that the agreement ensured trail access and
      right-of-way for 6400 West.

      The Council discussed the flexibility built into the agreement regarding commercial
      development and unit counts. Councilmember Ohrn addressed concerns about what the
      MOU would entail and stated, it was not a formalized agreement. It would clarify the
      understandings of discussions that have happened to give the parties the framework for a
      future agreement to consider.

      Councilmember Henderson moved to approve the Memorandum of Understanding between
      Herriman City and Land Reserve, LLC. Councilmember Shields seconded the motion.

      Councilmember Jared Henderson                Aye
      Councilmember Teddy Hodges                   Aye
      Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn                   Aye
      Councilmember Steven Shields                 Aye
