2.3) Public hearing and recommendation of Avante Recovery Center’s request for review of
a Development Code amendment to Chapter 12 changing the exception for group homes to
allow up to 16 residents.

    Jacob Curtis presented information on agenda item. Commissioner Everett inquired whether
    the exception was for new facilities or current facilities. Jacob stated it would only apply to
    new facilities, not rebuilds, and clarified that it was restricted and would not include
    outpatient per City Code.

    Commissioner Hemmert clarified that this was the only residential facility of this type.

    The applicant was present and did not have any questions or comments.

    Commissioner Hemmert opened public hearing on agenda item 2.3

    Commissioner Hemmert advised that there were two comments that were received and
    reviewed via email.

    Ricky Evans submitted public comment that read “Hi Teisha I understand that avanta
    recovery is applying for more residents. I have a few concerns for the planning commission.
    First I understand that they want to increase to 18 residents which the code I believe only
    allows 16 and if they go over that it changes to an institution and they would have to make
    changes to the building. Second I am concerned about the parking for staff and visitors. We
    are in a cul-de-sac and parking is a mess as it is as I have had people park in front of my
    residence to visit them. Third thing are they going to increase the staff that stays there at
    night or is it going to stay status quo? I want to make sure our concerns are addressed so if
    there is anything else I need to do please let me know. Thanks”.

    Ellis Bodily submitted public comment that read “Adding additional residents to this facility
    is not a gain to the public health, safety and welfare of our neighborhood. It actually adds an
    increase in the safety concern for residents who live in our neighborhood. Allowing the
    number of residents housed at this facility to be doubled will directly affect the number of
    cars coming into the cul-de-sac. Physicians, clinicians, healthcare workers as well as visitors
    for 16 residents will double the amount of traffic than was originally proposed by Avante. I
    see no positive benefit to the community by increasing the number of residents allowed at
    this facility. The obvious issue with increasing the number of residents in this facility is
    directly related to the parking requests listed above. 1 stall per 4 residents equals 4 stalls to
    be available at all times for anyone who is at the facility to visit or care for said residents. On
    street parking only allows for 2-3 cars, without blocking any mailboxes, and on-site parking
    is limited to the same amount or less. There is also a concern for enough space for cars to
    either turn around in the parking lot to exit back onto the street and/or backing out onto the
    street. This is a direct safety issue to any children or residents of the neighborhood who may
    be nearby. The bedrooms in this facility are not large enough to be considered for a “multi-
    occupant” status. The privacy allowed residents in a “multi-occupant” room will not be
    conducive for optimal rehabilitation. There is also the question of whether the “cost” of
    treatment for each resident will decrease due to a shared bedroom? If Avante reduces
    treatment costs per resident, so they can house 16 people, will the “high end” clientele, they
    promised the neighborhood, participating in their rehab facility decline? The “feel” in our
    neighborhood will be directly affected by the addition of 8 more residents! Avante handed
    out pamphlets at the “neighborhood open house” which clearly stated that they will be
    offering various types of “outpatient” services including “Partial Hospitalization” (20 hours
    per week), “Intensive Outpatient” (9-19 hours per week) and “General Outpatient” (1-8 hours
    per week). Where will all these people be parking? All of these “outpatient” services will
    additional neighborhood traffic as well as parking issues to the already limited space.
    Our neighborhood has not been provided with any data or statistics which validate the claim
    that that research exists showing group therapy is more effective with more individuals in
    the home. This claim needs to be backed up with more evidence-based data than just “1
    letter from 1 individual” who may very well be a stakeholder in this facility. I’ve been a
    resident in Lehi for over 27 years and have lived in 3 different areas of this city. I have also
    been a Registered Nurse for 15 years working in the area of Public Health. I take the health
    and safety of all individuals very seriously. Having this facility in my neighborhood is not the
    optimal choice for me but I do see the great need for rehabilitation services in our state.
    I’ve worked in the Long-Term Care setting and watched owners admit many residents that
    did not meet facility requirements. This was done to increase the income of the facility when
    actual qualified applicants were not available.
    The owners of Avante have promised our neighborhood that they will only admit residents
    that would be considered “high end clientele” yet that is a very subjective term. If the
    number of residents is increased from 8 to 16, I can see all kinds of rule bending happening
    to keep this facility making profits. The bottom line here is all about the money and income
    this facility will provide for the owners, not the actual rehabilitation of its residents.
    I petition you to uphold the initial accommodations that were approved for The Avante
    Recovery Center and help to maintain as much normalcy as possible in our neighborhood.
    Thank you so much for your service to Lehi City and its residents. It’s been a wonderful
    place to live for our family and for our neighbors as well. Please feel free to contact me with
    any questions or concerns you may have.

    Commissioner Hemmert closed public hearing on agenda item 2.3

    Commissioner Hemmert stated he used to be a medical courier as part of the medical group
    at Provo Canyon District. He stated that these facilities can do great things and are needed.
    He stated after reviewing all material and operations and the character of the surrounding
    area that an impact will be felt by adjoining properties. He was unsure of the gain of the
    public’s welfare and safety. He expressed that he did see a community benefit, but the
    applicant bears the burden and that this is a necessity to the exiting accommodations.
    Commissioner Everett echoed Commissioner Hemmerts statements. Commissioner Nielsen
    stated he was concerned about parking and increased traffic and whether the burden of proof
    had been met. Commissioner Hereth stated he felt like it was two pieces but did not feel like
    with the code amendment that a burden of proof was met. Kim Struthers stated that it was an
    important distinction, but that the applicant was asking for a specific amendment and if there
    were general concerns it could still be part of the consideration and accommodation
    allowance. Commissioner Ellis stated that maybe the size of the footprint be taken into
    consideration and will be more acceptable. Commissioner Hemmert stated that the applicant
    needed to demonstrate a need or a gain. Commissioner Nielsen stated he felt there was a
    justification for the benefit of the community by providing resources for those that need
    them.

    Commissioner Hemmert stated there is already an existing code for the and there is nothing
    that prohibits these provisions under the current code. Commissioner Eyre stated there was
    nothing that prohibits the development, but with the current code he felt that he would be
    alright with the footprint. Commissioner Hemmert stated the code does come into play, but it
    is existing and will need a conditional use permit and is larger than what the current code
    allows. Commissioner Bunker stated he has reviewed the material and that it is a large
    footprint. He thinks that it could be beneficial. Commissioner Nielsen stated there could be
    more people in the facility and wasn't sure if the item was grandfathered in, and if so, would
    they be able to accommodate the increased traffic and other issues that may arise.
    Commissioner Nielsen asked if that would need to apply for conditional use and may need
    general code change. Commissioner Hemmert stated that the comments the Commissioner
    have made have been persuasive. Commissioner Everett stated homes can be larger and
    smaller in each neighborhood and opportunities need to be presented and taking it at code
    only is taking the wrong direction. Commissioner Nielsen asked for clarification of the code.
    Kim Struthers stated it could be drafted in the language of the City Code and that it was
    something that could be amended with a tiered system. Ross Dinsdale asked if you could
    specify square footage per person. Commissioner Nielsen asked the Commission could
    forward a positive recommendation with amendments. Commissioner Ellis stated he would
    rather see a tiered system. Commissioner Everett stated the language in the code and DRC
    comments should be considered for this item. Commissioner Nielsen asked about resident to
    staff ratio and if this would be within reason. Craig Chambers stated that even if it passes it
    still must be safe and pass through other avenues for safety. Commissioner Everett asked
    about other developments and if other cities had encountered this issue. Jacob stated that no
    other cities were looked at. Craig Chambers mentioned that the comments in Teams needed
    to be part of the record.

Motion: Commissioner Nielsen moved to forward a positive recommendation to City Council of
Avante Recovery Center’s request for review of a Development Code amendment to Chapter 12
changing the exception for group homes to allow up to 16 residents.; including all DRC comments
and findings stated in the report.

Vote: Commissioner Ellis, yes; Commissioner Everett, no; Commissioner Hemmert, no;
Commissioner Nielsen, yes; Commissioner Bunker, yes; Commissioner Hereth, yes;
Commissioner Eyre, yes. The motioned passed 5-2.
