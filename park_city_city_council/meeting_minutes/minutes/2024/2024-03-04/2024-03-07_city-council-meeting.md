Kimball Junction Environmental Impact Study Update by UDOT: 
Becky Stromness, UDOT Project Manager, reviewed the goals for this project, including 
the improvement of travel times on SR224, the reduction of vehicle queues on the I-80 
off ramps, and the improvement of pedestrian and bicyclist mobility and accessibility in 
the area. She stated Alternatives A and C were advancing through the process at this 
time. She explained the screening process for the alternatives that helped the team 
decide which alternatives to make priorities. 
 
Alternative A would have a split-diamond interchange with intersection improvements, a 
bus rapid transit (BRT) lane planned at Olympic Parkway, and a pedestrian crossing.

1. Gordo Property Update: 
Erik Daenitz, Economic Development Manager, and Ryan Blair, Environmental 
Regulatory Program Manager, presented this item. Daenitz displayed a proposed 
concept design for the Gordo property and explained it showed the possibilities for the 
property. He reviewed the history of the property acquisition and the mix of zoning. He 
noted Council supported soil remediation for that property. They also supported a study 
for a regional park and ride and favored public/private partnerships for a project. 
 
Daenitz stated the City entered into a partnership with Deer Valley in December. Blair 
indicated the City received approval for excavation work by the Department of 
Environmental Quality. Daenitz stated there needed to be public input before a full 
development was initiated. He also recommended issuing an RFP to get a sense of the 
market. Blair detailed the corrective action plan for soil cleanup and noted he wanted 
the most restrictive clean up so the City could have the greatest options for the property 
moving forward. He estimated the cleanup would begin in May and would cost 
approximately $4 million.  
 
Daenitz stated this site was previously considered for a Public Utilities building and 
retention pond. The construction of 3Kings Water Treatment Plant eliminated the need 
for a facility here. A park and ride was also considered for the site. The current proposal 
included 1,500 parking stalls, 266 housing units, 50,000 square feet of mixed-use 
space, 100,000 square feet of garage, transit, public safety support and/or commercial, 
and 90,000 square feet of community recreation on the garage rooftop. He indicated a 
site evaluation was performed for park and rides and the Gordo property scored the 
highest. He noted none of the structures were more than four stories high. Daenitz 
stated public financing tools could be used if there was a certain ratio of mixed-use and 
affordable housing.    
 
Mayor Worel asked if RFP respondents could generate their own design. Daenitz stated 
the current design was just an example and requirements could be put into the RFP. 
Council Member Rubell asked if the soils on the parking portion of the site could be 
capped, instead of removed. Blair stated the entire site would need to be remediated 
according to the corrective action plan. Council Member Rubell asked if the plan could 
be redefined to which Blair stated he could go back and talk about a specific location for 
parking and those associated requirements. 
 
Council Member Dickey asked if there was a recommended mix ratio of affordable and 
market rate housing for this project. Daenitz stated the City should look at the number of 
units needed for a public/private partnership to work. Then comply with the state’s 
requirements associated with the housing and transit reinvestment zone, which was that 
there could not be short-term housing and 10% of the housing had to be affordable. 
 
Council Member Ciraco referred to the cost analysis on the property and asked if the 
monetization of the current public works facility had been considered in evaluating the 
cost of the use. Daenitz stated that had not been considered. Council Member Parigian

 2. Review the Dining Deck Program: 
 Jenny Diersen, Special Events Manager, reviewed the Council’s direction to assess the 
dining deck lease fees and not create a hardship on the Arts Fest. She stated the 
program had been in effect for 13 years. It was a complex program and took nine 
departments to implement. There were nine dining decks on Main Street in the summer. 
Dining deck fee options included eliminating the fees, which would require a public 
 benefit analysis, and eliminating the permit fees. She noted eliminating fees would not 
 be an incentive for additional decks and it could encourage other organizations to 
 request fee waivers. The restrictions set up for the dining deck program prohibited 
 additional restaurants from participating. 
  
 Diersen also discussed the Art Festival booth fee for restaurants with dining decks and 
 stated one option, in exchange for waiving deck fees for restaurants, was for the City to 
 waive the $10,000 Kimball Art Center (KAC) City services fee during the festival, and to 
 pay KAC $2,000 to make up the difference in lost revenue. 
  
 Mayor Worel asked if additional dining decks would be constructed on Main Street if the 
 barriers were eliminated, to which Diersen indicated waiving fees could help, but a 
 bigger incentive would be to eliminate the operational restrictions on dining decks. She 
 noted the two-meal requirement was a big deterrent to restaurants as they considered 
dining decks. The HPCA also looked at Main Street restaurants and most had space for 
 outdoor dining.

Rename the program from Special Service Contract to Public Service Contracts

 Why we are recommending this: 
  • The Budget Policy describes the program as Public Service 
   Contracts
  • The existing terminology is confusing (e.g. “Special Service 
   Contracts,” “Regular Special Service Contracts,” “DEI Special 
   Service Contracts,” etc.)
  • We want to avoid confusion by using simple, clear, and 
   consistent terminologyPUBLIC SERVICE CONTRACTS
Contracts with nonprofit organizations for services consistent with the needs and 
goals of Park City
    • Not grants or donations
    • Provision of these services is common for municipalities
    • Utah Code 10-8-2 allows municipalities to fund services 
      that promote safety, health, prosperity, moral well-being, 
      peace, order, comfort, or convenience
    • Utah Code 10-7-85 allows municipalities to appropriate 
      funds for the support of the artsRECOMMENDATION #2

Re:  Annexation Petition and Development Plan for Parcel SS-104-B 
     (Council Meeting March 7, 2024,  New Business Item 1) 
      
This letter is to state our opposition to the Annexation Petition which was made public on 
February 28, 2024 and which is scheduled to be voted on in the March 7 Council meeting.  
The Petition involves both the annexation of the Summit County parcel just east of Iron 
Canyon Drive, combining that with a narrow parcel which runs along Iron Canyon Drive, 
and then subdividing the resulting property into 3 lots.  The property proposed to be annexed 
is the square parcel labeled SS-104-B shown within the white border on the attached parcel 
map.1 
 
We do not oppose the landowner building a residence on Parcel SS-104-B nor its annexation 
by Park City.  However, we oppose the development plan proposed in the Petition and urge 
the Council to either deny the Petition or at least postpone its vote until a more suitable plan 
is presented.  
 
We live at 2554 Aspen Springs Drive and have resided there for 30 years.  Our house 
overlooks the portion of Iron Canyon Drive that is adjacent to the proposed development.  In 
those 30 years there has never been a year in which we have not witnessed at least one 
vehicle slide-off from the road during the winter, in some cases the vehicle overturning.  
This is in spite of the 15 mph posted speed limit and a sign warning of icy conditions.  This 
has occurred on both sides of the road though usually on the south side of the series of  "S" 
curves adjacent to the highlighted parcel. 
 
This stretch of road is narrow, has no curbs or sidewalk, has blind curves and is hazardous to 
both drivers and children who walk up it from where the bus stops on Payday Drive.  We 
believe allowing the construction of three driveways onto this section of road, and even one 
driveway, would result in this road being even more hazardous than it already is.

We also oppose the development plan because the Petition proposes to subdivide and build 
houses on a portion of property that has long been regarded as common area.  The areas 
contained within the blue-green outline on the attached map represent Parcel IC-MISC. It 
stretches south to Payday Drive where there is a monument sign designating the entry to the 
Iron Canyon subdivision and across much of the northern portions of the subdivision.  It 
serves to fill in the areas around the boundary of the subdivision. 
 
This parcel has been regarded as common area since the inception of the Iron Canyon 
subdivision in 1983 even though title was held in the name of the developer, the Boyer 
Company.  In December, 2022 the Boyer Company deeded the parcel to a joint venture and 
on the deed it stated "NOTE: THIS PROPERTY WAS DETERMINED NOT TO BE 
COMMON AREA."  After almost 40 years of being treated as common area, this parcel is 
now claimed to be private property.  There is no explanation of how this determination was 
made. 
 
Also attached is a page which shows an enlarged map of  Parcel SS-104-B and the portion of 
Parcel IC-MISC (shaded) which is proposed to be combined and subdivided into 3 lots.  This 
would actually result in 4 parcels since the rest of Parcel IC-MISC is not proposed to be 
included in the resulting lot split.  Nowhere in the documents submitted is there a discussion 
of this parcel being common area or the eventual destiny of the remaining land included in 
Parcel IC-MISC.  For example, does the joint venture intend to remove the Iron Canyon 
monument at the bottom of Iron Canyon Drive if it now claims that property is no longer 
common area?  The absence of any mention of this issue and how it was determined that the

average size of 1.65 Acres.  That report points out that the lot sizes of the proposed

this requirement.
