#3 Rocky Mountain Power Transmission Line Rebuild Conditional Use Permit 
Zetterquist reviewed the staff report for the Rocky Mountain Power Transmission Line Rebuild 
Conditional Use Permit (CUP). 
Staff and Commission discussed storm water plans and if emergency access is required. 
Steve Liechty representing Rocky Mountain Power introduced himself. 
Wayman Stodart, an attorney representing a property owner, raised concerns with the scope of the 
project, concerns with the width of the poles, and what is in the increase of the yield and the impact and 
concerns with clarifications on the easements. 
Harrild stated the first condition covers some of the concerns raised. 
Richard Merrill commented on concerns regarding increase of voltage in the lines and impacts from 
exposure to the increase of electrical output. 
Tony Nielson commented on possible health concerns, property devaluation, and the amount of 
electricity going over their property. 
Mr. Liechty commented that voltage will not increase, poles will be 5-8 feet taller and most will be 
wood, and stated the conditions regarding access will be followed. 
Sands asked about ground disturbance. 
Mr. Liechty responded ground disturbance will be minimal and heavy equipment should not be needed. 
Christensen asked about contact info for people to contact him. 
Mr. Liechty gave his number. 
Tim Nielson asked about larger conductors and what guarantee there will be that there won’t be an 
increase in voltage after the work is done. 
 
4 March 2021                       Cache County Planning Commission Minutes                        Page 4 of 9 
 
Mr. Liechty stated that an increase in voltage would require Hyrum City to upgrade their system. If they 
make that request there are processes to go through to upgrade. 
Harrild commented that he can’t speak to hypotheticals and laws that can change. The real question is 
health impacts and how those are addressed. 
Watterson asked if the company would be opposed to a condition that if voltage were to be raised above 
46kVs you would come back before the Commission. 
Mr. Liechty stated yes. 
Watterson commented that a condition regarding an increase in voltage over 46,000 would have to come 
back before this Commission and that would help allay some of the concerns that have been raised. 
Mr. Liechty stated that is typically what has been done in the past for other projects in other areas 
because that also requires an upgrade in structure. 
Harrild asked if there are random fluctuations in the voltage. 
Mr. Liechty responded +/- 1-2%. 
Harrild asked Mr. Liechty about easement requirements. 
Mr. Liechty stated he didn’t know; he is not the one that handles that but it is something they can let the 
County know. 
Christensen informed the public that there are 10 conditions that the applicant has to meet before 
construction can occur. 
Watterson motioned to approve the Rocky Mountain Power Transmission Line Rebuild Conditional Use 
Permit with the 10 amended conditions and 3 conclusions; Sands seconded; Passed 4, 0.

#4 Cache Valley Compost Facility 
Zetterquist reviewed updated staff information for the Cache Valley Compost Facility. 
Issa Hamud informed the commission that the surveying of the property has been completed and more 
information has been made available. 
Gary Vance from JUB Engineers commented on the history of the project, the letter submitted that 
addressed the concerns raised at the last meeting. 
Leland Myers former general manager of Davis Central Sewer District reviewed his PowerPoint 
presentation on composting and biosolids. 
Christensen asked when the homes next to the Davis facility were built. 
 
4 March 2021                       Cache County Planning Commission Minutes                        Page 5 of 9 
 
Mr. Myers stated some were built before the facility and some were built after the facility. 
Watterson asked why wait until complaints about odors to put membranes on. 
Mr. Myers stated they don’t own the membranes but they monitor the piles religiously to handle the 
odor. 
Watterson asked about air pollutants from the biosolid compost. 
Mr. Myers responded there are pollutants that come off but they can be managed. 
Watterson asked about odor precursors. 
Mr. Myers responded yes and the meters measure all or some of those and a delusion of odor plan. The 
Davis facility also has a 24-hour phone number available for people to call if there is an odor problem. 
Olsen asked if Logan is going to be similar to the plan described. 
Mr. Myers commented that Davis did it the cheapest way it could to eliminate the problem. Logan is 
going into this with the intent to build it right the first time and utilize these processes. 
Christensen asked about travel distances for smells. 
Mr. Myers responded turbulence mixes the odor into the air; odor isn’t easy to track. Mild odors may be 
there, but shouldn’t offend people other than what the most sensitive smellers can detect. The top odor is 
what people smell. Davis has equipment that emits odor neutralizers to help with the smell. 
Tom Willmore asked about how inversions affect odors. 
Mr. Myers states the slight movement of air that makes odors worse. In Davis the odor is stopped from 
going off the property; if operated correctly any plant should be able to do that. 
Mr. Hamud commented that Logan wants to be good neighbors and Mr. Myers will be on the design 
committee for the facility. Logan City wants to meet all the needs of the concerned citizens and work 
through their concerns. 
Sands asked about the current process for the compost facility at the landfill now and if it is similar to the 
process that will be used. 
Mr. Hamud stated yes the process is similar to what is happening now and Logan City wants to address 
and work with the public on their concerns. Logan City is willing to put together a committee that is 
made up mainly from the citizens concerned to work through the concerns. 
Christensen stated that smell is one of the biggest concerns. One suggestion made has been to use the 
Eliason property rather than the proposed property which is two miles away. 
Mr. Myer stated odor is relative to the operation rather than the distance. What is needed is a process to 
address the concerns before they happen and then smell can be mitigated before it becomes a problem. 
Most smells can be traced back to where they are coming from and being addressed.

Christensen asked if Logan City has looked about doing this out of Clarkston. 
Mr. Hamud stated it is more complicated than just hauling the material. The waste from the water 
treatment facility has to go somewhere. 
Mr. Myer commented that taking the waste to a land-fill is not a good option because then the odor is 
concentrated. 
Mr. Hamud stated all the green waste for the valley comes to the Logan location landfill. 
Watterson reminded the public that a public hearing has been held but the Commission has allowed 
comments at other times in the past. 
Eve Davies commented, as a representative of Pacific Corp. and Cutler Reservoir, with concerns 
regarding water quality, wanting more information regarding a detailed design plan and project details, 
and drainage of water from the property going to Cutler Reservoir. She requested that the project be 
continued until more details regarding impacts to water quality and details looking into other options and 
locations be made. If the project is approved, she suggested that water quality checks be included as a 
requirement. 
Mr. Myer commented that he has several ties to the water quality community along the Wasatch front 
and the amount of phosphorus in the ground water. 
Ms. Davies commented on phosphorus in the water column and sediments. 
Mr. Hamud commented that the new facility will increase the quality of water discharge and the 
composting facility is not supposed to discharge any water at all. Storm water runoff will be mitigated in 
the design and will not run off but will be contained. 
Watterson asked about containing storm water. 
Mr. Hamud commented on how the storm water will be contained. 
Mr. Vance commented on storm water runoff and State requirements for storm water runoff. 
Mr. Hamud stated all of it will be addressed in the design phase. 
Mr. Wilmore commented in opposition of the composting facility due to the impact of odors from the 
facility and subsequent land devaluation; as well as Blue Springs impacts, wetlands, irrigation concerns, 
and Logan City not talking to the Benson residents first. 
Katie Fuller commented in opposition of the rezone and that Logan City contains their own waste in 
their boundaries and other concerns. 
John Drew, Mayor of Providence City, commented in favor of the proposal and pointed to the good 
program that Hyrum City runs that does this same thing on a smaller scale. 
Jeff Kunzler commented in opposition of the project due to wetlands, runoff, odor, and trust issues 
between Benson citizens and Logan City.

Commissioners discussed the reasons for denial such as lack of information regarding alternatives to this 
type of composting, no information regarding an alternative location, public clamor, that this is a gateway

approve the rezone and open the possibility for a conditional use permit.
