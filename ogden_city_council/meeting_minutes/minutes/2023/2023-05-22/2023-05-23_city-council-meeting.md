Council member Hyer inquired as to the reasoning behind the current municipal boundaries. Mr. Brierley indicated that
when reviewing the history of the property, he found that the properties were intended for development by the Internal Revenue
Service (IRS). The City of Marriott-Slaterville did not exist at the time that Ogden’s boundary was first created, and the IRS
wanted their properties to be part of a City, so they annexed into Ogden City. The development of the property never occurred,
and the properties are now owned by the Weber School District.

Council member White asked if there is any property tax value for the properties in their present condition. Mr. Brierley
answered no because they are owned by a non-profit entity. Council member White asked if the City would have the ability to
capture any property tax value of the properties were sold to a for-profit entity and developed in a manner that generates tax
revenue. Mr. Brierley stated that if the property is within Marriott-Slaterville, taxes will be collected through them, though
Mariott-Slaterville does not have a property tax. He has spoken with the City Attorney’s Office, and they are not aware of any
reversionary clause that would allow the City to stake a claim for any property tax generated by the property. Council member
White then inquired as to the ownership of the property that is in between the two properties subject to the boundary adjustment.
Mr. Brierley indicated that property is owned by the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT). /

Council member Nadolski asked if the elected officials at Marriott-Slaterville are supportive of the boundary adjustment.
Mr. Brierley answered yes and reiterated they actually proposed the boundary adjustment to Ogden City.

Proposed Resolution 2023-13 declaring the intent to adjust the current
boundaries of Ogden City and South Ogden City by relocating their mutual
boundary from the south side of Country Hills Drive to the centerline of
Country Hills Drive to Gramercy Avenue to 925 East

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider a resolution
declaring the intent to adjust current boundaries between Ogden City and South Ogden City. The memo stated South Ogden City
is requesting a boundary adjustment for a section of Country Hills Drive between Gramercy Avenue and 925 East. The adjustment
would shift the boundary from the south side of the Country Hills Drive right-of-way to the centerline of the right-of-way. The
request is the result of South Ogden City’s approval of an apartment building close to the property line and concerns Ogden City
has with liability for issues related to access in and out of the development.

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and presented a map to orient the Council to the area subject to the
proposed boundary adjustment. The result of the boundary adjustment would be to move the common boundary from the south
side of Country Hills Drive to the centerline of the road. This issue arose due to South Ogden’s permitting of an apartment
building on Country Hills Drive for which the applicant should have requested access and encroachment permits from Ogden
City. That did not occur, and the complex has been built, but Ogden City staff has concerns about the location of the building and
visibility issues created by the development given that it is located on a vertical and horizontal curve. There is also insufficient

. buffering between the building and the roadway and Ogden views the building as creating some liabilities; staffhas discussed the
_~ boundary adjustment in order to shift liability to South Ogden City. South Ogden would also be responsible for plowing one side
of the road if the boundary adjustment is approved. He concluded the action before the Council tonight is to adopt the resolution

of intent and set a public hearing for August 8, 2023 to take final action on the proposal.
