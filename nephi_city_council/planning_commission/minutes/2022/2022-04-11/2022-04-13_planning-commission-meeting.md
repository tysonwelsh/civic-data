BLAKE & GULLEY SUBDIVISION PRELIMINARY AND FINAL PLAT APPROVED , LOTS VACATED IN POWELL
PARK AND RED CLIFFS SUBDIVISIONS:

   City Administrator Seth Atkinson explained that when the Powell Park subdivision was approved it
was in anticipation of owners who were in the Red Cliffs Estates Plat A subdivision to the west would
purchase the lots in Powell Park, and then merge the two lots together to make one larger lot to get
more than a half-acre of property to be allowed to have animal rights.
     Mr. Gulley (who owns lot 10 of the Red Cliff Estates subdivision) has purchased lots 10 and 11 in the
Powell Park subdivision, and Mr. Blake (who owns lot 12 in the Red Cliff Estates subdivision) has
purchased lot 9 in the Powell Park subdivision. Mr. Gulley and Blake would like to take lots 9, 10, and 11
from the Powell Park subdivision, split them down the middle and attach this property to their lots 10
and 12 in the Red Cliff Estates subdivision.
   To amend these subdivisions, the Utah State Code requires that the affected lots in both the Powell
Park and Red Cliffs Estates Plat A subdivisions be first vacated, and then a new subdivision created
showing the two proposed lots in the Blake and Gulley subdivision.
   Commissioner Fran Peterson asked the question if the lots are vacated in the Red Cliffs Estates Plat A
and Powell Park subdivisions are they no longer held to the CC&Rs that were recorded for those
subdivisions?
    Mr. Atkinson confirmed this would be the case. The City’s intentions of approving the Powell Park
subdivision were to create larger lots that would allow animal rights to be reinstated on those lots on
the east side of the Red Cliffs Estates subdivision. The CC&Rs of this subdivision prohibits animal
ownership currently, so doing this will allow this to happen. The subdivision directly to the east, Sun
Ridge Ranches, does allow animals.
   The question was asked if the owner of lot 11 in the Red Cliffs Estates subdivision, who did not elect
to purchase the ground behind him from the Powell Park subdivision, was aware that his neighbors to
the north and south of him can now place animals surrounding him.
    Neither the City, nor the applicant, Mr. Gulley, has spoken to this individual and could answer this
question, but the land that was subdivided into Powell Park subdivision had animal rights on it until the
recent subdivision, so this would not be a large change in use in this location.
     Commissioner Heather Robertson clarified that this process of vacating and amending these two
subdivisions will have to happen every time lots in the Powell Park are to be attached to lots in the Red
Cliffs Estates subdivision.

   Ms. Talbot responded that this would be the case if they want to combine them to obtain animal
rights.
   Commissioner Brough asked if the access easement behind all the lots in the Powell Park subdivision
would still be in effect?
    Ms. Talbot indicated that the access easement is still being shown on the new Blake and Gulley Fields
subdivision plat, so will still be available.
 Commissioner Robertson voiced concern that the other items listed in the Powell subdivision
development agreement would also still apply here.
   Ms. Talbot and other Commission members also shared that concern, and she suggested that they
ask the City Attorney to clarify before the City Council approves the project. Mr. Atkinson agreed.
Mr. Atkinson explained that the Commission is considering both the preliminary and final plats for this
new subdivision concurrently, because there are no additional utilities or infrastructure being placed.

Motion: The motion was made to vacate lots 9,10, and 11 of the Powell Park Subdivision contingent on
the City Attorney be consulted to ensure the Powell subdivision development agreement conditions
would still apply.

Made By: Karl Brough            Seconded: Cory Thompson          Outcome: Passed Unanimously

Motion: The motion was made to vacate lots 10 and 12 of the Red Cliff Estates Plat A Subdivision

Made By: Cory Thompson          Seconded: Heather Robertson Outcome: Passed Unanimously

Motion: The motion was made to approve the Blake & Gulley, two-lot, Subdivision Preliminary and Final
Plat contingent on a signed development agreement for the lots that would contain the same conditions
as previously existed for the Powell Park subdivision development agreement.

Made By: Cory Thompson          Seconded: Kent Heap              Outcome: Passed Unanimously

REED RIDGE PLAT “A” VICINITY PLAT WITH A REMAINDER PARCEL APPROVED:

    Mr. Atkinson presented a vicinity plan for a commercial development that is zoned HC-1 Highway
Commercial. It is either a six or seven lot subdivision. There are two lots on the west side of the
development that there has been some concern in DRC regarding whether there will be access to those
lots so as to not land-lock them. Mr. Parkinson is showing two cross-over easements coming between
some lots that would bring some access There are six lots to the north of the Maverick gas station that
is going in, and one potential lot to the south of the Maverick along Main Street. They are proposing
two accesses along Main Street, which is a UDOT right-of-way, and so these accesses will need to be
approved by UDOT. They generally should line up with accesses across the street for better traffic
control, which is what the city would like to see, but UDOT is the ones who will dictate the location, and
how many accesses and their width.
    Mr. Parkinson explained that he only wants to subdivide the six lots right now.
    Ms. Talbot stated that this clarification from Mr. Parkinson will mean that this is a phased
development then, and the remainder parcel will be required to subdivide at a future date before it can
be sold.
   Commissioner Brough asked if there will be access easements shown between the lots not just the
two legs coming in off the accesses.

  Mr. Parkinson stated that there won’t be access easements there, it will probably just be utilities
because there will be sewer that needs to run back there, and you wouldn’t want sewer to run over an
access point.
  Mr. Atkinson stated the city would probably like to see a sixty-foot access easement not the thirty foot
being shown even though it will not be either a private or public road.
  Mr. Parkinson stated that they are envisioning fast food businesses on the front where there will be
shared parking lots.
  Mr. Atkinson explained that this is one reason cross-over easements would be necessary so access
between locations could be freely accomplished.
  Ms. Talbot agreed stating that this would be a reason cross-over easements that are sufficient should
be required to access all pertinent areas of these properties. She suggested like a horseshoe coming up
and over and out down the middle of all the properties. This would help the city because when the
future developed site plans of those lots come in that is what the city would require of those future
developers anyway, and it is much simpler now when the subdivision is being done, and we only need to
deal with one landowner not several to accomplish the same thing. This does not mean we are asking
the developer to build a road to city standards, just allow for ingress and egress of the common future
owners of the entire site.

   Mr. Parkinson assured the commission that they are going to make sure the utility easements and
cross easements would be there to not leave land islands so that consumers will be able to access from
side to side. It’s just hard right now because they don’t know exactly who the tenants who will purchase
will be. They want to do the bare minimum right now until they know what will be going in there. They
certainly want to try and used shared parking lots that people can just drive across not city street right-
of-way on the interior of the project.

Commissioner Fran Peterson asked what the dotted lines at the north of the subdivision boundary are
showing.

Mr. Parkinson’s engineer (Ludlow) explained that this is a disputed area of land.

Mr. Parkinson will need to clear up any boundary line disputes before this subdivision can be approved
and recorded through the city.

Commissioner Fran Peterson asked if any city street is planned for the north side of this subdivision, and
Mr. Atkinson said that 1300 North further to the north of this property would go in at some point as
there is an easement there already for an irrigation line. Comm. Peterson asked if the city has discussed
the coordination of the roads in this location with Mr. Parkinson. Mr. Atkinson said the city has met and
discussed the coordination of roads in that area since the city owns to the north and the west of this
parcel, but Mr. Parkinson did not want to coordinate the parcels, so the city is not anticipating a
connection right now. The Commission talked a bit more on circulation for traffic around this project.

Motion: The motion was made to approve the Vicinity Plan for the Reed Ridge Plat “A” six-lot
subdivision, (Phase 1) with a remainder parcel.

Made By: Kent Heap              Seconded: Cory Thompson          Outcome: Passed Unanimously

LOVELESS ESTATES FINAL PLAT APPROVED:

Mr. Atkinson stated that Loveless Estates subdivision has just a few outstanding items listed by the DRC
that need to be addressed by the developer prior to the final being approved if it goes to City Council,
including showing the power pole on 200 W. to ensure it will not conflict with any building sites being
proposed, cleaning up the boundaries of the property, shifting a few of the buildings to ensure side
yards are met, eliminating some cross-hatching on the plat used to delineate common open space etc.
They need to show location and type of fencing. A reimbursement district can be set up for the
developer to recoup their cost of installing 200 West down to 700 North, a much needed and beneficial
road extension that will be wonderful the flow of traffic in and out of this development.

Ms. Talbot told the Ludlows that they will need to number the open space lots for the County Recorder
to be satisfied to record the plat. There should likely be three lots labelled and will need to be legally
described.

Mr. Travis Ludlow stated that they are in the process of getting the gaps quiet- titled, and that process
will take a minimum of 90 days, they would not be able to record the plat or sell the lots before the
quiet-title process is complete anyways, but that most all of the other items could be cleaned up on the
plat before the Council meeting next Tuesday if the Commission would be able to approve based on
that.

Comm. Ann Peterson said they had helped to clear up her concerns she had about approving with their
list of outstanding items.

Motion: The motion was made to approve the Loveless Estates 75 lot Subdivision – Final Plat with the
contingency that all items on the DRC notes are addressed before the City Council approves the plat.

Made By: Kent Heap               Seconded: Karl Brough            Outcome: Passed Unanimously

BARBAROSA SUBDIVISION FINAL PLAT APPROVED:

  Mr. Atkinson explained that this is the final plat of the Barbarosa Nephi 2 lot Subdivision that is being
brought tonight concurrent with a site plan for Wendy’s. They are planning on pulling their utilities from
the north, and the City is willing to let them cross the road before this is completely approved at their
own risk because UDOT is doing a mill and fill project right now, and once that is done they may not let
anyone cut the road for some time. This will save the developers time and money and be better for the
City and UDOT as well. They do have a few items to clean up from the DRC notes still outstanding
before it goes to City Council.

There was concern from the council if there is an access to lot 2 or not. Ms. Talbot said the last
conversation with Ken Price for Wendy’s he said they were letting them keep their current access on
both lots. The staff will check with the developer and Wendy’s tomorrow to confirm if that is still the
case as it doesn’t appear to show that on the plat. If there is not an access onto the highway, then a
cross-over easement will have to be put in place between the lots before this subdivision is approved.
The site plan could be approved, but not the subdivision without access to the second lot.

Comm. Brough said that the plans state that lighting will be in accordance with their “photometric plan”,
but no plan seemed to be included. It would be good to see the candle lighting and height and direction
of lighting for the site plan.

Motion: The motion was made to approve the Barbarosa Nephi 2 lot Subdivision – Final Plat with the
contingency that either an access to highway 132, or a cross-over easement be provided for Lot 2 and
updated on the plans.

Made By: Ann PetersonSeconded: Heather Robertson                 Outcome: Passed Unanimously

PUBLIC HEARING FOR CHANGES TO TITLE 10 OF THE NEPHI CITY CODE:

 The Commission discussed proposed changes to 10.16 Off Street Parking and Loading of the Nephi City
Code. They talked about what percentage a structure could be enlarged before it needed to provide
additional parking and adhere to the updated code. Staff was directed to find out if the IBC or the ADA
has any direction on that. They also discussed if parking plans should be required at building permit time
for anything other than a single-family dwelling and Commission liked that idea. They discussed that
any future parking lots should be designed to not allow backing out onto arterial or collector class
streets except for single-family dwellings. A couple of other items on parking were discussed.

Commission Chairman Ann Peterson opened the public hearing up for public input. No public input was
given.

Motion: The motion was made to continue the public hearing of changes to Title 10 – Land Use
Regulations of the Nephi City Code 10.16 Off Street Parking and Loading to the next regularly scheduled
Planning Commission on May 11, 2022

Made By: Ann PetersonSeconded: Heather Robertson Outcome: Passed Unanimously

FARM ANIMAL DISCUSSION:

of talk around town about this issue. Ms. Talbot suggested if they did want to make changes to the code
that the chickens be allowed on a conditional use basis not on a permitted use basis. Councilor
Worwood agreed with Ms. Talbot’s suggestion.
