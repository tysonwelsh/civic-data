Findings of Fact  
1. On August 11, 2016, Cindy L. Schumann submitted a Sketch Plan application for a 
proposed minor subdivision on Parcel CD-152. 
2. Parcel CD-152 is located at 4580 North Woodenshoe Lane, Peoa, Utah.  
3. Parcel CD-152 contains 2.57 acres.  
4. Section 11-4-8.B.2 of the Code states: “In the highway corridor (HC) zone, a lawfully 
created "lot of record" that is not within a previously approved subdivision plat that is two 
(2) acres in size or larger will be considered for one additional lot under the minor 
subdivision of property review process.” The zoning for Parcels CD-152 is primarily in the 
HC with a very small sliver within the AP.  
5. The property is subject to the provision in Section 11-4-8, B.2 of the Eastern Summit 
County Development Code that allows a parcel in the HC zone that is 2 acres or more to be 
split into two (2) lots.  
6. Parcel CD-152 is a lot of record.  
7. Parcel CD-152 has an existing residence on what will become Lots 1 of the subdivision.  
8. There will be a total of two (2) lots within the boundaries of the subdivision.  
9. There is an existing driveway to serve Lot 1.  
10. Each lot has legal access, reviewed and approved by the Summit County Engineering 
Department, as conditioned.  
11. Lot 2 will be serviced by an individual well, to be reviewed and approved prior to 
building permit issuance.

12. A Septic system is proposed for Lot 2. Perc tests have been completed and the Eastern 
Summit County Water Conservancy Special Service District has reviewed and approved the 
use of septic systems on December 8, 2016.  
13. The proposed subdivision is not located on a hillside.  
14. There is not a 100-year floodplain within the proposed subdivision.

22. Lot 1 and 2 both contain at least 1 acre.  
23. Parcel CD-152 is owned by Cindy L. Schumann and the entirety of the lots is included in 
the subdivision.

Conclusions of Law  
1. All new lots created have been clustered to the maximum extent possible.  
2. Access to the lots have been consolidated to the greatest extent practicable and 
reasonable  
3. Each lot has legal access through a recorded right of way.

1. Discussion regarding Master Plan Developments; Resort Commercial (RC) and 
Family Estate Subdivisions. 
 
Planner Milliner opened the work session. The discussion began with the Family Estate 
Zone. The Legal Department has concerns with the Family Estate Zone. The first concern 
has to do with not being able to sell the property for five years to no one other than a family 
member. The Legal Department states, “If we were to enact something like this, then we 
would at a minimum need to ensure that we have crafted a rational basis for such a 
restriction. Which frankly we do not know what that is. If you look at the purpose 
statement of this section, we talk about providing affordable housing for ‘family members’ 
and a ‘traditional way of life.’ These may be good reasons, but then it also smacks of 
violations with equal protection (i.e. singling out one person or group of persons from 
among the larger class of those similarly situated on the basis of a tenuous justification that 
has little or no merit).” The question for the commission is whether to move forward with 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
January 19, 2017 
Page 8 of 18 
 
 
 
some amendments trying to find how to make the Family Estate Zone legally defensible or 
try for something different. 
Attorney Strachan stated it is not dead in the water. It needs to be tightened up. The history 
of Family Estates is to protect agrarian ways, to let a farmer pass on their property to their 
children. Other states have done this. Virginia has laws that allow communities to do this. 
The Commission would have to come up with language and reasoning as to the purpose 
and why it is necessary for our community. The way it is drafted now does not do that. 
Another concern is loopholes can easily be found to get around these rules and regulations. 
There are potential legal issues the way it is written, but nothing that could not be 
overcome. 
Commissioner Ure asked if a bank would lend the money for something that is zoned as 
Family Estate. If the home were to go into foreclosure and no other family member wanted 
the house then the bank would have to sit on that house for five years. Commissioner 
Sonntag said he thought they talked about needing an exception for foreclosures. Attorney 
Strachan stated it was talked about but it is another concern. If this were to go before 
County Council these would be some of the concerns that would be brought up as potential 
issues at that level as well.
