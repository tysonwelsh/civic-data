Discussion Items

         City Noise Ordinance and Common Code Enforcement Issues

Justice Goble, Resident of Riverton City, addressed the Council regarding the City's noise
ordinance, specifically a section that restricts the operation of off-road machinery, snowmobiles,
and snow blowers within 800 feet of a dwelling. The resident explained that this ordinance,
originally adopted from Salt Lake County, may not be practical for the City. He became aware of
the ordinance after a neighbor reported his children riding dirt bikes in his backyard. He
proposed that the City consider modifying the ordinance to align with neighboring cities like
Herriman and Bluffdale, which do not have blanket distance restrictions but instead prohibit off-
road vehicles in specific locations, such as public parks or protected lands.

         Business Licensing Code

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, provided an overview of the City’s business license code, a 67-page
document that requires updates to remove outdated provisions. He cited alcohol licensing as an
example, noting that the City's proximity requirements for alcohol sales are already dictated by
state law and do not need to be duplicated in local code. The revision process is not intended to
change existing policy but rather to align the code with state regulations and remove
unnecessary provisions. Given the size of the document, he suggested two possible
approaches for review: forming a small committee of Councilmembers to examine sections in
detail before presenting updates to the full Council, or breaking the document into chapters and
reviewing them one at a time.

Councilmembers suggested bringing the business licensing code back in sections to be
reviewed over a few Council meetings.

Citizen Comment

Mayor Staggs called for public comments;

Eric Veenendaal addressed the Council regarding concerns about a speed dip installed on
Lampton View Drive. He reported that large vehicles crossing the dip generated significant
noise and vibrations, which could be felt throughout his home. The disturbances were frequent,
especially during busy traffic hours, sometimes waking his children. He requested that the City
consider alternative solutions to address speeding and consider filling in the dip due to concerns

Stacy Conologue confirmed that large vehicles traveling over the speed dip caused noticeable

it.

         Ordinance No. 25-05 – Joseph White Text Amendment, Proposed Amendment to
         Section 18.90.010, Table of Commercial Uses to allow for a Reception Center as
         a Conditional Use in the C-N Zone.

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that this was an application for a
text amendment to the City’s ordinance, specifically asking for a change to the table of
commercial uses. The applicant is requesting that reception centers be allowed in the
Commercial Neighborhood (CN) zone, which currently does not permit them.

However, since this is a text amendment, the change would apply to the entire Commercial
Neighborhood zone, not just the applicant's property. Mr. Lethbridge pointed out that the CN
zone is relatively limited in the City, mostly along Redwood Road and 12600 South, with a few
properties further down around 2700 West.

The Planning Commission held a public hearing and, after receiving input from both the
applicant and others, forwarded a recommendation for denial of the text amendment.
However, the Planning Commission also suggested an alternative—rezoning the applicant’s
property to Commercial Gateway, which would allow the reception center use and could be
more appropriate for the Redwood Road corridor. This alternative suggestion was not part of
the motion for the text amendment since rezoning was not included in the applicant's request.

Alice White, representing the applicant, provided context about her business, Wasatch Arts
Center, a dance studio on Redwood Road. She explained that her business acquired a building,
which previously housed Pet Planet. Since Pet Planet is no longer operating, Alice intends to
use the space as an extension of the dance studio. Additionally, she plans to offer the space as
a community venue for events such as meetings and music recitals. She acknowledged that
while the space is not large, it is beautiful and would be a valuable community asset.

Mayor Staggs expressed support for the use, noting that the property has good access off
Redwood Road and is surrounded by other commercial properties. He acknowledged concerns
about integrating such uses into residential neighborhoods but felt this particular location was
appropriate for the proposed use.

Councilmember Buroker suggested denying the text amendment to avoid integrating reception
centers into neighborhoods, but recommended supporting a zoning change in the future, as the
area is more suitable for a gateway designation. This would allow the reception center to align
better with the property's surroundings.

Councilmember Pierucci expressed excitement for the project, citing a lack of reception spaces
in the area and supporting the idea of pursuing a zoning change instead. He encouraged the
applicant to return with the rezoning request.

Councilmembers McDougal and McCay also voiced support for the idea, particularly for
accommodating events like dance recitals, and Councilmember McCay suggested waiving
some fees for the applicant if the rezoning was pursued to make the process more affordable.

Mayor Staggs directed staff to reimburse the fee for the zoning change application.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.

Councilmember Buroker MOVED that the City Council DENY Ordinance No. 25-05 –
Joseph White Text Amendment, Proposed Amendment to Section 18.90.010, Table of

Discussion/Action Items

         Resolution No. 25-08 – Resolution of Support for HB 81, Eliminating Salt Lake
         County Regulations to Fluoridate Drinking Water

Mayor Staggs shared that he had previously reached out regarding House Bill 81, which seeks
to eliminate the mandate for fluoridation of culinary water systems. The bill has already passed
out of committee with a favorable recommendation. He explained that Salt Lake County, along
with Davis County, is one of the only counties in the state with this mandate. He emphasized
concerns about the questionable benefits of adding fluorosilic acid to the water supply. The
resolution supports the bill and expresses general approval of its current form. If the bill passes,
the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District, which supplies water to half of the City, would
stop adding fluoride to the water, reducing both the cost and obligation of doing so.

Mr. Carter highlighted a potential risk with fluoridation, referencing an incident in Sandy where a
power outage caused a pressurized system to release an unintended, concentrated dose of
fluorosilicic acid into the water supply. This imbalance resulted in fluoride reacting with metal
pipes, potentially leading to lead and copper contamination in the water. He pointed out that
such events can go unnoticed for an extended period, making it difficult for residents to
understand the cause of any issues until much later.

Councilmember Haymond MOVED that the City Council approve Resolution No. 25-08 –
Resolution of Support for HB 81, Eliminating Salt Lake County Regulations to Fluoridate
Drinking Water. Councilmember Pierucci SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for
discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows:
Buroker-yes, Haymond-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes and Pierucci-yes. The motion passed
unanimously.
