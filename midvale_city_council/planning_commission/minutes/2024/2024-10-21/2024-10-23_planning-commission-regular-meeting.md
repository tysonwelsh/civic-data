APPLICANT:

Charles Allen, a Parametrix representative, highlights the main points of Midvale City’s first
ever adopted Transportation Master Plan. A Transportation Master Plan is a guiding document
that sets forth transportation projects and policies. It focuses on the next 10 years. This plan also
includes a Capital Facilities Plan as part of the roadway projects to come.

Midvale City is largely built out, which brings the focus to finding a balance between vehicular
traffic and active transportation modes. A public survey was conducted to gather information on
modes of transportation used. This also gathered information on general concerns; including
pedestrian and biker safety and air quality.

Existing plans combined with concerns of the public and City Staff helped to create goals for
projects moving forward. The four goals that came of this were: Increasing quality of life
through human-powered and motorized access, support human-powered access through
neighborhoods, city centers, and redeveloping areas, improve community cohesion through
active transportation, and create safe and connected pathways to ease movement throughout the
city.

Existing Roadway Conditions are measured in Level of Service (LOS) for traffic flow quality.
Midvale ranks as LOS D, meaning it is approaching unstable flow. Crash data was observed to
verify where the most danger exists on Midvale’s roadways. Most accidents occur around Fort
Union Blvd. and the I-15 junction areas. The active transportation network looks at facilities
dedicated to pedestrians and bikers. There is an absence of this infrastructure and there will need
to be a lot of change in this aspect.

Future conditions measured Roadway LOS in 2034 without any improvements. A 10-year
outlook and further in the future can help understand roadway capacity needs that can be
implemented to avoid these transportation issues to come. Wasatch Front Regional Council
(WFRC) has a Regional Transportation Plan that will modify and widen existing roadways
throughout Midvale City and nearby municipalities. There are seven projects recommended by
Parametrix to help address capacity needs and update the current roadway conditions in order of
priority. After these suggested projects are completed, LOS 2050 looks much more acceptable
for flow of traffic standards.

Mr. Allen goes through his presentation and explains a few of the diverse types of active
transportation projects and roadway cross-sections that are proposed in the Transportation
Master Plan. There are a lot of variables that go into the different projects and modifications to
different sections of the roadways. Please refer to the attached presentation for a more thorough
understanding.

Project cost estimates of the entire outlook of the updates suggested are broken down by funding
sources, such as WFRC, Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT), and federal grants. Impact
fees are a one-time fee required for new development to offset the capital cost of the project.

DISCUSSION:

Commissioner Liedtke asks the difference between a buffered bike lane and a separated bike lane.

Mr. Allen clarifies that a buffered bike lane is typically a painted barrier versus a separated bike
lane that is normally a physical, vertical structure such as a median.

Commissioner Edwards questions the drive behind the East side of 7800 S around 1000 East.

Mr. Allen explains the possibility of a project at 7800 S because around this area, the center turn-
lane ends. This creates an area that can manage lower-capacity traffic and therefore is a place for
a future project to ease traffic flow.

Chair Erickson clarifies that it may be necessary to bleed out into neighboring cities so that harsh
cut-off in design elements at the city limits are not so noticeable.

Wendelin Knobloch, states that there is already some collaboration with Murray City for example
to smoothen out the transition.

Mr. Allen explains that on both Holden and Center Street there is an active transportation plan to
change the capacity of the area. Narrowing of streets, protected bike lanes, and more active
sidewalk space are all going to be implemented.

   1.   View 78 Concept Plan, located at 795 W Ivy Drive

        David Dennison, representing the Gardner Company, discusses a brief history of the
        project. There were a lot of comments and suggestions given throughout the process from
        City Council, Planning Commissioners, City Staff, and residents. The Gardner Company
        brought on Wasatch Residential Group to help redesign the community and create a new
        development plan that is better for everyone.

        Adam Langford, representing Wasatch Residential Group, refreshes the Commissioners on
        the former and current projects that their company is a part of. Wasatch Residential Group
        maintains and manages their own properties. This ensures that the capital improvement
        money is saved from the start and the property is well-kept. The site-plan is updated to
        apply many comments and suggestions given to Gardner throughout the process. Please
        refer to the October 23, 2024, Meeting Packet for reference images.

        Mr. Langford describes the setup as being a mix of three-story townhomes and apartments.
        Community amenities are also included such as a pool, clubhouse, and outdoor area. He
        describes the elevation of the roofs, being flat and not blocking any of the neighbors’ views.

        Vice Chair Anderson questions if there is an HOA on this project.
