                                             Staff Meeting

Electronic Board Meeting Determination

                 Greg Schulz read the following statement regarding the electronic meeting
determination:

               Consistent with provisions of the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act, specifically
Utah Code Ann. § 52-4-207(4), and acting in my capacity as the Mayor of the Magna Metro
Township (“Magna”), I hereby make the following written determinations in support of my decision
to hold and convene electronic meetings of the Magna Metro Township Council without a physical
anchor location:

1.     Conducting Council Meetings with an anchor location that is physically accessible for
members of the public to attend in person presents a substantial risk to the health and safety of
those who may be present at the anchor location.

2.     This determination is based upon the following:

       a.     Pursuant to the Public Health Orders issued by the State of Utah and Salt Lake
       County, the Magna Metro Township remains under a state of public health emergency
       related to the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, and significant person-to-person
       transmission of the virus continues to occur in Magna; and

       b.        All other facts noted in the August 7, 2020 Letter of Determination are still in effect.

Ordinance

            THIS BEING THE TIME heretofore set for a public hearing to receive public
comment regarding the following request:

Ordinance 20-02-01 amending Chapter 19.69: Planned Community Zone of the Magna Municipal
Code of Ordinances.

               Jay Springer reviewed the Planned Community Zone (PC) Ordinance stating he
worked with the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (GSLMSD) staff on revisions
regarding acreage, reducing the size, the size caps on the different categories of PC zones, and
some conforming updates, at the direction of the Council. They also made some clarifications
that they did not feel substantively changed the content, intention, and use of the PC zone. The
Magna Planning Commission has discussed the amendments and recommended approval.

The PC Zone Ordinance that was originally adopted required a minimum of 200 acres for a
development to use this. The amendment to the ordinance reduced the minimum acreage to 75
acres, with 200 acres at the larger end. The difference between the two sizes is that light industrial
and manufacturing are permitted uses at 200 acres, but between 75 and 200 acres, those uses
are conditional uses and they would have to be incorporated into a development agreement that
the Council would approve, and then, go to the Planning Commission for approval.

                Council Member Pierce thought the Council should pull this item because it was
a time certain item, and the Council did not carry it out that way.
.
                Mr. Springer stated he was not concerned about that because the standard is to
have the discussion as soon as is practical after the time certain.

               Greg Schulz stated he has been monitoring the board, and no one has left the
meeting.

               Paul Ashton stated he was okay too since no one has left the meeting.

               Council Member Prokopis, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to open the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

               No one spoke in favor of or in opposition to the ordinance amendment.

               Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to close the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

             Council Member Hull, seconded by Council Member Peel, moved to approve
Ordinance 20-02-01. The motion passed unanimously.

            Mick Sudbury thanked those who have been working on the Magna Main Street
cleanup. Something needed to be done about that.

Magna Water District

               Clint Dilley, General Manager, Magna Water District stated the Magna Water
District Board of Trustees has been working on a master plan, which is nearly complete, and it
has adopted a facility plan. It also increased impact fees, but those costs to developers will be
offset by no longer needing to pay for secondary water dedication. The board combined the
culinary water and secondary water impact fees. Those impact fee changes will go into effect in
April. The board chose not to pursue rate increases in 2020 because of the pandemic recognizing
the economic hardship that would put on people in the community, and it delayed increases that
were to go into effect until January 2022. However, it is looking at around a 5 percent yearly
increase on water and sewer bills. It has sent out flyers and will be doing so again. There will
also be a public hearing for that in April.

As far as staffing, the Magna Water District filled quite a few vacant positions throughout the
summer, and has hired an in-house inspector, as well as an accountant to help out with the
controller on the financial side. It is also going to hire two new union employees to perform some
pro-active and preventative maintenance functions in the operations departments -- one in the
water side department and one in the sewer department. It will be taking resumes until next
Wednesday, March 3rd, for those positions.

The Magna Water District has some upcoming projects that will impact traffic. It is doing a sewer
collection repair project in two locations – 8400 West near Western Drive, and 8400 West near
3370 South. The biggest project it will be doing is a culinary water line replacement along 3500
and a secondary water line along that same corridor between 8000 West and Magnolia Drive,
continuing up on Montclair Street. Contractors are being prequalified for that. That project will
require some additional traffic control measures and some noticing above and beyond what is
typically required. He anticipated that project would be bid and awarded in March and completed
by August.
