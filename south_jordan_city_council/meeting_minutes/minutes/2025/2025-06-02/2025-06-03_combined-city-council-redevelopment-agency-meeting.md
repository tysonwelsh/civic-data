Mylee Wong, a third-year member of the Youth Council and current Youth City Council Mayor,
addressed the City Council to share highlights from the past year. She expressed appreciation for
the opportunity to serve and noted that this has been her most meaningful year of involvement.
Ms. Wong explained that the Youth Council is composed of students in grades 9 through 12 who
are interested in learning about government and contributing to the community through service
and teamwork. She emphasized the value of the program, citing its educational opportunities and
hands-on experiences. She reviewed prepared presentation (Attachment A).

Mayor Ramsey provided background on the history of the South Jordan Youth City Council,
noting that it was previously managed by community volunteers. As those volunteers stepped
away, the City faced a decision about whether to continue the program. Ultimately, the City
chose to transition the Youth Council into an official city program, allocating staff time and
appointing a council liaison. Mayor Ramsey expressed strong support for this decision,
emphasizing the value the Youth Council brings to the community and highlighting the
leadership opportunities it offers young residents. She commended the Youth Council members
for their service and contributions throughout the year and concluded by leading a round of
applause in appreciation for their efforts.

                D.4.     South Valley Chamber of Commerce annual update. (By South Valley
                         Chamber of Commerce President & Chief Executive Officer, Jay Francis)

South Valley Chamber of Commerce President & Chief Executive Officer Jay Francis
introduced South Valley Chamber of Commerce Managing Director Jamie Jensen. Mr. Francis
reviewed prepared presentation (Attachment B) providing an overview of the Chamber’s
activities, programs, and accomplishments. He explained that all initiatives are guided by the
principles of Connect, Educate, Advocate, and Grow, and events are planned to meet at least
three of these goals. Among the highlighted programs was Women in Business, which held ten
events in 2024, including a Junior Women in Business mentorship program for high school girls
that culminated in a graduation and scholarship awards. The Business Institute included the
Business Accelerator, which graduated 26 participants, and Leadership South Valley, a 10-month
program offering hands-on exposure to various industries and government operations. The
Chamber also hosted bi-monthly business boot camps focused on practical tools for small
businesses and partnered with Salt Lake Community College’s "Everyday Entrepreneur"
program to guide aspiring business owners, helping some to successfully launch and others to
pause before investing prematurely. In addition, the Chamber hosted "Let’s Do Lunch" and
"Meet the Member" events, alternating monthly. These gatherings provided networking and
promotional opportunities, featuring speakers such as the Lieutenant Governor and executives
from the Utah Jazz and Zions Bank. Programs like Chamber 101 and Connect After Hours
offered training and informal networking opportunities, respectively, while executive forums and
“In the Know” panels addressed timely topics such as legislative updates, tariffs, and regional
development. He highlighted the Chamber’s committees, including Ambassadors, Government
Affairs and Public Policy, and Small Business, which contributed to initiatives such as tracking
legislation, organizing a Washington D.C. advocacy trip, and selecting Small Business of the
Year awardees. The Chamber also hosted three golf tournaments and a Shark Tank-style
competition in partnership with FBLA and DECA, engaging high school students and featuring
well-known entrepreneurial judges. The Titan Awards continued to grow, moving to a larger
venue to accommodate increased participation, and honored community and business leaders,
including South Jordan’s Small Business of the Year recipient. In 2024, the Chamber conducted
five ribbon cuttings in South Jordan, welcomed 196 new members, 19 of which were South
Jordan-based, and reported that over 100 South Jordan businesses participated in Chamber
events. He concluded by expressing appreciation for the City's continued partnership and
support.

Mayor Ramsey expressed appreciation for the South Valley Chamber's efforts, noting that many
of their contributions, such as support for the Jordan and Canyons Education Foundations and
involvement with Envision Utah, extended beyond what was highlighted in the presentation. She
acknowledged the Chamber’s growth into a strong regional presence and a valuable force for the
business community in Utah. Mayor Ramsey thanked the entire Chamber team for their ongoing
support of both large and small businesses in South Jordan and throughout the region,
emphasizing the City's gratitude for their continued partnership.

                         City, Utah made taxable by law for year 2025. (By CFO, Sunil Naidu)

CFO Sunil Naidu presented the final amended city budget, explaining its purpose and statutory
requirements. He emphasized that the city must adopt a tentative budget, make it available for
public inspection, and hold a public hearing, which was occurring during this meeting. He
clarified that the final budget includes all major components; the General Fund, other
governmental funds (such as special revenue), and enterprise funds. He noted that this budget
also includes the adoption of the certified tax rate for real and personal property, which is
calculated by the State Tax Commission in conjunction with the County Assessor’s Office. The
city is not proposing a tax increase or pursuing the Truth in Taxation process this year. He
provided a high-level overview of the budget components, including a summary page for public
reference (Attachment D). He explained that while sales tax is the largest single revenue source
for the General Fund, its growth has been volatile, ranging from a 30% increase in past years to a
current growth of 2–3%. He also noted that personnel costs are the largest General Fund
expenditure, supporting approximately 400 employees. He outlined the structure of the
Enterprise Fund, which operates like a business by collecting user fees for services such as
water. He mentioned outstanding debts, including bonds issued for the Public Safety Building
and Fire Station, as well as Daybreak-related special assessments. He highlighted the city's
success in securing approximately $60 million in grants and legislative appropriations over the
past few years, crediting the mayor and council for their efforts. He acknowledged there are
additional funds secured through partnerships like UDOT that are not reflected in the city’s
