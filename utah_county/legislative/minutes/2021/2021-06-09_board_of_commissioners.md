23. ADOPT A RESOLUTION IMPLEMENTING WATER CONSERVATION MEASURES TO
ADDRESS THE DROUGHT SITUATION IN UTAH COUNTY CONSISTENT WITH THE

EMERGENCY DECLARATION ADOPTED BY UTAH COUNTY
-Dave Shawcroft, Attorney

COMMISSIONER LEE - THIS IS TO ADDRESS THE DROUGHT SITUATION WE HAVE.
WE DID PUT FORWARD THAT DECLARATION OF EMERGENCY THAT WE ARE IN A
DROUGHT. WE REALIZE THAT IT LOOKS LIKE THE GOVERNOR HAS SENT
INDIVIDUALS TO SPECIFICALLY SAY PRAYERS IN COMMISSION MEETINGS WHICH
IS FANTASTIC. I’M GLAD THAT HE’S GOING THAT AND THIS ONE IS A FOLLOW-UP
MEASURE ON OUR PART TO SHOW THAT WE'RE NOT JUST STATING IT BUT WE'RE
ACTUALLY GOING TO DO THINGS THAT WE HAVE CONTROL OVER. WE DON’T
HAVE CONTROL OVER THE CLOUDS OR ELSE WE WOULD MAKE IT RAIN, BUT WE
DO HAVE CONTROL OVER SPRINKLERS THAT ARTIFICIALLY MAKE IT RAIN AND
ALSO OTHER MEASURES. I WANT TO EMPHASIZE THE OTHER MEASURES
BECAUSE THERE WAS A LOT THAT WE TALKED ABOUT THERE AND I WANTED TO
GIVE YOU A CHANCE RICHARD TO TALK ABOUT WHAT WE'RE LOOKING AT
IMPLEMENTING AND THAT THERE'S PROBABLY MORE IN THE FUTURE AS WE
LOOK AT THIS AND WORK DOWN THROUGH IT.

RICHARD NIELSON, PUBLIC WORKS DIRECTOR - AFTER THE CONVERSATION
WITH YOU COMMISSIONER LAST WEEK, I DID REACH OUT TO MY DIVISION
MANAGERS THAT OVERSEE BOTH THE GROUNDS AROUND OUR COUNTY
FACILITIES AND IN OUR PARKS THAT WE'VE MANAGED THROUGH THE COUNTY.
NOW, THESE ARE JUST COUNTY-OWNED FACILITIES - THE COUNTY BUILDINGS,
COUNTY PARKS. IT DOESN'T AFFECT THE CITIES SO THOSE ARE ON THEIR OWN.
WE'VE LOOK AT IT AND WHAT YOU'LL SEE IN THE RESOLUTION IS THAT WE'VE
GOT A PLAN THAT WE THINK WILL HELP US REDUCE OUR WATER USAGE BY 35-
40%, IT’LL VARY FROM LOCATION TO LOCATION. IT’LL DEPEND ON WHAT THE
SPRINKLER SYSTEMS ARE CAPABLE OF AND WHAT THE SPRINKLER SYSTEM
ACTUALLY WATERS. ONE OF THE THINGS YOU'LL SEE IN THE RESOLUTION IS
THAT THE TREES AND THE PERENNIAL ITEMS THAT ARE A LARGE INVESTMENT
AND TAKE MANY YEARS TO ESTABLISH THAT WE DON’T WANT TO RUN THE RISK
OF LOSING THOSE, WILL BE THE PRIORITY FOR THE WATERING WHERE
SOMETHING LIKE THE TURF GRASS IS A LOWER PRIORITY. IT CAN GO DORMANT
AND AS YOU SEE SOME OF THE PARKS AND THE FACILITIES HERE, YOU MAY SEE
SOME BROWN GRASS IN AREAS AND THAT’S OKAY.

ONE OF THE THINGS THAT WE ARE GOING TO LOOK AT HERE ESPECIALLY ON
THIS CAMPUS, WE HAVE THE FREEDOM FESTIVAL THAT TAKES PLACE HERE ON
THIS CAMPUS. WHAT YOU'LL LIKELY SEE IS JUST AHEAD OF THAT AND JUST
AFTER THAT YOU'LL SEE US DOING SOME FAIRLY HEAVY WATERING ON THE
TURF GRASS JUST BECAUSE IF WE LET IT GO REALLY DRY AND THEN PUT ALL
THAT FOOT TRAFFIC ON IT, WE WILL DESTROY IT. THAT WILL BE A LITTLE BIT
OF GIVE AND TAKE HERE AS WE GO THROUGHOUT THE SUMMER, BUT WE ARE
LOOKING AT TRYING TO DO THAT 35-40% REDUCTION IN ALL OF OUR WATER
USAGE WHERE WE CAN ON ALL OF OUR LANDSCAPING.

What’s the challenge?

* Current water sources are not able to supply enough water for future growth
in the canyon - which includes commercial and residential

* In 2019 the NFSSD had to impose a building moratorium for the entire
canyon prohibiting all development that requires a water connection

* Sources are adequate for existing connections; however, when factoring in
drought conditions even existing connections could be at risk

* During shortages, water policies put priority to residential connections
puta commercial at risk. This could have a direct impact on tax revenue
rom Sundance and other commercial users in the canyon

* In order for NFSSD to counter the drought and limited existing water sources,
an additional source is needed (a new well)

* To build and create infrastructure for this new well, already high water rates
would need to increase even more
