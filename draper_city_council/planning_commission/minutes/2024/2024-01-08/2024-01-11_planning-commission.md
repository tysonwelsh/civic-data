       F.      Public Hearing: City-Initiated Subdivision Ordinance Update (Legislative Item).
               On the Request of Draper City, a Request for a Text Amendment to Titles 9 and 17 of
               the Draper City Municipal Code in Order to Address Recent Changes to the Utah State
               Code Concerning the Processing of Subdivision Applications. Known as Application
               2023-4645-TA.          Staff    Contact:         Todd    Taylor,    801-576-6510,
               Todd.Taylor@draperutah.gov.

Mr. Taylor reported that the proposed text amendments were brought forward due to recent changes
in State Code, primarily due to Senate Bill 174, which was passed in 2023 and included new
subdivision regulations that become effective on February 1, 2024. Staff also included other changes
and related provisions to deal with the functionality of the Code. The major changes were presented.
Changes were made to definitions in both Title 9 – The Zoning Code and Title 17 – The Subdivision
Code. In Title 9, the definition for “Lot Line Adjustment” was updated and the definition for
“Subdivision Amendment” was added. The intent was to indicate that lot line adjustments no longer
require subdivision plat amendments.

In Title 17, the definition for “Concept Plan” was updated as a result of major changes. The definition
of “Family” was deleted because it was not consistent with a more recent definition that was included
in Title 9. The definition for “Planning Commission” was also updated to designate the Planning
Commission as the Administrative Land Use Authority, which is a new term in State Code. The
definition of “Zoning Administrator” was also updated to be consistent with Title 9 and the update
referenced the “Development Review Committee” definition in Title 9. Title 17 of the Subdivision
Code contains some of the major changes. Preliminary Plats are still being approved by the Planning
Commission at a public meeting rather than a public hearing. Final Plats will now be approved by
the Zoning Administrator. Section 17-10-80 includes major changes in the review process for
subdivisions. The initial review of Preliminary Plats is required to be completed within 15 business
days. Final Plats are required to be reviewed within 20 business days. They are limited to four
reviews for each application. Requirements were also added in terms of the staff review and what is
provided to the applicant. The new requirements do not apply to property located within a geological
hazard zone.

Mr. Draper reiterated that Subdivision Plats that come before the Planning Commission will not be
public hearings but will be public meetings and will not be opened up to the public for comment.
Staff will bring them forward for discussion and approval or denial. Going forward, Final Plats will
be signed by Ms. Jastremsky as the Zoning Administrator. The primary issue is that there are now
four reviews allowed for an applicant. The current average is five or six.

Mr. Taylor next addressed Section 17-10-85 - Public Notification. Public noticing was removed from
State Code, so this section was amended to require noticing for Subdivision Plat Amendments and
Minor Subdivisions that request deviations. Staff was proposing that the mailed notice to property
owners be reduced from the current 400 feet to 300 feet, which is commonly seen and consistent with
the cities of Lehi, Riverton, and South Jordan. In addition, State Code no longer requires notices to
be posted on-site. It was proposed that notices be mailed to property owners going forward except
for instances when a proposal is to vacate a street or municipal utility easement, which is the sole
instance where on-site notice is still required.

To Section 17-120, the State Code change requires a new Appeal process. After the fourth review, if
the City has not provided any response within 20 business days, the property owner can request an
Appeal. If the Appeal is related to subdivision improvement plans, there is an appeal panel based on
State Code provisions. If the Appeal is based on just our Subdivision Ordinance Review, the Appeal
would go to the Appeal and Variance Hearing Officer.

Chapter 2 pertains to Concept Plans and goes from a formal application process that was approved
by the Planning Commission to an optional pre-application process to provide early feedback to the
applicant. Guidance is provided by the Development Review Committee. Per State Code, once
requested, a Pre-Application Meeting must be scheduled within 15 business days of the request.

To Chapter 3 - Preliminary Plat, State law now allows for a concurrent submittal of a Final Plat with
a Preliminary Plat Application. Text was added related to that. In Chapter 4 for Final Plats, they
allow for the concurrent submittal, and the review procedure was clarified. To Chapter 8 – Minor
Subdivision, the City can decide what the requirements are. The intent is to mirror to the extent
possible the Final Plat procedure where the Zoning Administrator would provide the approval if there
are no deviations. Deviations would go to the Planning Commission for approval at a public hearing.
The review restrictions were referenced for consistency.

In Chapter 9 – Plat Amendments and Property Boundary Adjustments, Subdivision Plat Amendments
will still be approved by the Planning Commission. Joining lots will still require a Plat Amendment.
The separate noticing section was removed and combined. For Lot Line and Parcel Boundary
Adjustments, approvals are through the Zoning Administrator. This is to ensure compliance with the
land use ordinances. The applicant would then record both the City's Notice of Approval and the
Boundary Line Agreement with the County.

Commissioner Fowler recalled instances where members of the public claimed to have never received
a mailed notice and wondered why they were not finding them. Ms. Jastremsky explained that the
intent is to eliminate signs posted on properties and do just the mailed notice. In most cases, when
people claim to have not received a notice they are outside the mailing radius, did not check their
mailbox, or the address the County Recorder has on file is incorrect. Commissioner Fowler felt there
was still some benefit to on-site notice and considered it more important than a mailed notice. Other
Commission Members agreed. The importance of transparency was mentioned. Commissioner
Fowler felt that eliminating on-site notice gives the impression that they are removing a layer of
transparency. The preference was for the on-site notice to remain.

Mr. Draper pointed out that the mailed notice is not the only notice. It is still posted on the City’s
