     DISCUSSION – Rocky Mountain Power North Orem Transmission Line Project

 Mr. Lange of Rocky Mountain Power reviewed the proposed project that would be within the city
boundaries of Orem. He indicated it would run west of the freeway along 1600 north, east to the
foothills and then runs south to the Spanish Fork substation. There is about 5 miles that need to
be rebuilt due to growth of the system. They are working with both UDOT and the City of Orem
in order to increase the size of the capacity. He continued to state this project will be done by an
outside contractor that will work with both UDOT and the city to ensure the proper permits and
notifications to residents are done.
Mr. Fowler, Project Manager presented the general scope of the work and the timeline as to the
work being done. He indicated they will be changing out poles along roadways and will only be
moving traffic around the work site without having to close the area down completely. They intend
to work on the high traffic areas in the very early mornings, so not to impede traffic. They
anticipate the build to be done by the end of May.

        DISCUSSION – Future of Hillcrest Park

 Mr. Bybee presented some background information regarding the project of the Hillcrest Park
property and those involved. Ms. Benson of Landmark Design reviewed the three possible options
which include:
   • Retain the school in its entirety, using the building for classes or offices and open area for
       the park amenities. Mr. Smith of Method Studios reviewed the pros and cons of retaining
       the building in its entirety and what would need to be done in order to update the building
       to comply with current building and safety code issues and estimated costs.
   • Retain a portion of the school and increase the recreational use area and allow for an open
       lawn area. Improvement to the existing pavilion, restrooms, and playground. Mr. Smith
       continued to review what portions of the school would be retained and remain functional.
   • Removal of the entire school, opening up the area for an entirely recreation property and
       green space.
Ms. Benson stated all configurations would include a well and a detention area.

     RESOLUTION – Site plan approval of Springwater Pointe at Sleepy Ridge located
     generally at 1900 West Golden Pond Way in the PD15 zone.

Mr. Bench stated on May 22, 2019, the site plan for Golden Pond at Sleepy Ridge Phase 7
was approved by the City Council with eight twin home units. Units 1-6 were approved as
two-story twin homes (33’-3” high) and Units 7-8 were approved at one story units (24’-8”
high). These units are currently under construction with no changes proposed. The applicant
now desires to amend the existing site plan to create three additional units, Units 9-11, to the
south of the site. The three new units will be two-story with an overall height of thirty-three
feet three inches (33’-3”). The overall density of the revised site plan will be 5.88 units per
acre. The PD15 zone, Area C, allows a density of eight (8) units per acre. Access to the
development will no longer be from Golden Pond Way as previously approved, but from a
new access at Sleepy Ridge Drive at approximately 1850 West 1000 South. A new
Homeowners’ Association (HOA) “Springwater Pointe at Sleepy Ridge” will created for the
development and it will not be a part of the existing HOA. The proposed buildings will be
stucco and brick/rock front. The roof will be architectural shingles and standing seam metal roof
accent. At 33’-3”, the height is under the maximum height of 35’ allowed in the PD15 zone.
Each unit will have the required two spaces and there will be seven visitor parking spaces (one-
half space is required for each dwelling). No parking will be allowed in the emergency vehicle
turn around area. The proposed landscaping and overall plan comply with the requirements of the
PD15 zone for deciduous trees, evergreen trees, shrubs and grasses. Access from Sleepy Ridge
Drive will require the installation of a small public street (Springwater Lane) across a portion of
city property. The new street will be installed by the developer. Revisions to the golf course
including the relocation of a tee box and additional landscaping around the new street will be
installed by the golf course management team. The City Council is the final approving authority
for the site plan.

Mrs. Lauret stated she is currently living in the area and is pleased with the proposal and feels it
will work better than what is currently in place.

Mayor Brunst moved, by Resolution, to approve the site plan of Springwater Pointe at Sleepy
Ridge located generally at 1900 West Golden Pond Way in the PD15 zone. Mrs. Lauret
seconded the motion. Those voting aye: Richard F. Brunst, Jeff Lambson, Debby Lauret, Tom
Macdonald, Terry Peterson, David Spencer, and Brent Sumner. The motion passed.

Ms. Lewis of Lewis, Young, Robertson & Burningham reviewed the amounts being bonded and
the projects in which they will go towards. These are long term Capital Improvements that will
be in place twice as long as the bond. The parameters are the maximum that is allowed, and they
try to be a little over for flexibility. The parameters resolution set the par at $45 million dollars,
the maximum term of 30 years, the maximum coupon rate at 5.5% and the maximum discount rate
at 3%. They believe the par amount will be a less once they compile all the information making it
more in the range of $30 million dollars, final maturity around 25 ½ years, underwriters currently
have a maximum coupon rate of 4%. This would give the city an annual debt payment of
approximately $1.86 million dollars. She feels the market going forward is going to stay steady at
least for the near future and interest rates are currently below constructions rates.

Mr. Patterson of Gillmore Hale reviewed information regarding a current State loan for a sewer
project currently held by the city that would need to be combined into the one utility bond.

General discussion ensued regarding funds available to put towards the project and the interest
rates that the city can receive due to the cash funds put towards the project.

Mayor Brunst wanted to thank the residents of Orem for their support in going with the pay as you
go and the amount the city has been able to save and not take out a bond during that time affording
them to take out a bond for needed projects with cash towards the project and receiving low interest
rates.
