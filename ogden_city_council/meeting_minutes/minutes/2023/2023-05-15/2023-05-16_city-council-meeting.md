Proposed Ordinance 2023-24 amending the Municipal Code by amending
Section 1-5-4 to amend provisions pertaining to a city flag

A memo from Council staff regarding the proposal to adopt a new Ogden City flag came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated this consideration comes after a selection process that narrowed a field of 215 submitted designs
down to 10 before being presented to the Council. Each Council member provided their top five rankings of the top 10 to narrow
the field to three designs. In the work session of March 14, 2023, the Council requested recommendations from the Youth City
Council, Ogden City Administration, and the Design Review Committee (DRC) before further consideration. All three bodies
recommended the three-stripe design with a circle in the middle broken up by mountains on the inside. The Council decided to
provide $750 to both the artists of the two designs from the final three that were not selected in addition to the $1,000 provided to |
the artist of the winning design.

; Communications and Public Engagement Coordinator Brandon Garside summarized the memo and Ellie Gomez, Ogden

“Youth Council Member, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the process that was followed to select a new
Ogden City Flag; this project was inspired by Utah's initiative to design a new State flag. The Ogden Youth Council launched a
competition to design a flag for Ogden City. The Youth Council received 215 total designs, 81 of which were from local students.

The review process consisted of an initial review by the Youth City Council, which forwarded 105 designs to the DRC for further
eee

consideration. The DRC was made up of representatives of Ogden City Council; Arts, Culture, Events (ACE) employees; the .
Diversity Commission; Ogden Downtown Alliance (ODA), Visit Ogden; and Ogden business owners. The DRC narrowed the
field of designs from 105 to 32 and requested variations for some of the designs; in the DRC’s second review of the designs, each

member was given five votes, and the top 10 designs were moved forward for final consideration. In the final review step, the

DRC ranked their top 10 designs. The designs were then forwarded to the City Council, which ranked their top five designs out of

the top 10; the top three designs remained based upon those rankings and the Council requested a recommendation from Ogden

City Administration, the DRC, and the Youth City Council. Of the final three designs, the Ogden City Administration, DRC, and

Youth City Council all recommended the same flag: .

The design comes from Cody Hockin and consists of three horizontal stripes with a circle in the center broken up by
mountains. Proposed Ordinance 2023-24 describes the flag as follows:

“The Ogden Flag design consists of three horizontal stripes with a circle in the center. The top and bottom stripes are
aqua blue, the same color found in the 2016 Ogden City Brand Style Guide (hex code 00acd4), with a white middle stripe. The
circle is broken up into two pieces, top and bottom, with the top half of the circle shaped to appear as the sky behind three
mountain peaks, which are part of the white stripe passing through the circle. The center peak is taller than the two on each side,
which are the same height. The bottom half of the circle has a small peak in the middle that breaks up the white stripe a third of
the way into the circle. The color of the circle is navy blue matching the 2016 Ogden City Brand Style Guide (hex code: 003469).

As defined by the artist, the focal point of this design centers on an iconic white mountain range that symbolizes the
snowy Wasatch Mountains loved by Ogden residents. This three-peak mountain range is set in a dark blue circle that creates a
high-contrast image to draw the eye into the design. A dark blue triangle is centered below the tallest peak. This represents the |. /
individual or the family that is here in Ogden. The dark blue circle creates the letter “O” for “Ogden.” Symbolically an “O”
resembles community, connection, or a collection of people. The mountain range breaking outside the “O” signifies the
welcoming and inclusive nature of the people that live in Ogden and the culture of tourism. The colors match the color scheme of
the brand identity for Ogden. Blue in flag design can represent freedom and determination, with the white symbolizing a sense of
peace and harmony.”

Proposed Ordinance 2023-23 amending the Fiscal Year 2022-2023 budget
by increasing anticipated revenues and appropriations in the amount of
$4,033,825 in order to close out multiple Capital Improvement Projects and
transfer excess funds to other CIP projects and recognizing additional :
revenues for equipment purchases
A memo from the City Comptroller regarding proposed Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) project amendments and
associated Fiscal Year (FY) 2022-2023 budget amendments came before the Council for consideration, The memo stated the
proposed budget amendment will increase the FY23 City budget by $4,033,825. In the General Fund, the proposed budget
amendment recognizes revenues for police operations and appropriates those revenues to police uniform wages and benefits to
provide an additional Sergeant over school resource officers ($380,200). General Fund contingency funds are being re-
appropriated to purchase five bullet-proof vests for code enforcement officers ($10,000). B & C Road contingency funds are being
reappropriated to cover the bond payment for the ESCO project ($480,950). In the CIP Fund, multiple CIP projects are being
closed out with any remaining funds being transferred to cover overages in other completed projects. Funds from an unexpended
Airport project are being transferred to the CIP Fund for street reconstruction projects ($214,550). Remaining funds from
’ completed CIP projects in the Airport, Water, Sanitary Sewer, and Storm Sewer are also being re-appropriated to other projects.
Internal Services funds - IT and Fleet - are recognizing revenue from current operations and appropriating those funds for needed
equipment.
Comptroller Stout summarized the memo and presented a spreadsheet detailing the proposed adjustments to revenues and
appropriations in the FY23 budget; she stated in the CIP Fund, contingency funds are reappropriated to provide additional funds
for Union Station draining upgrades ($150,000), the Fire Station #3 construction project is being closed out and the remaining
funds transferred to cover an overage on the Station #4 remodel project ($16,153). Remaining funds from the completed
Trackline Bike Park project are being transferred to the Centennial Trails project ($32,257). Several park projects have been
completed and any remaining funds are being transferred to cover overages in other completed park projects. Funds from the .
General Park Improvement CIP project are being used to cover additional overages required to close out those projects ($11,038).
Funds from an unexpended Airport project account are being returned to the CIP Fund for future street reconstruction ($214,550).
In the Airport Fund, funds from completed CIP projects are also being re-appropriated to cover additional CIP projects
($200,132). The Golf Course Fund is receiving RAMP funds to complete building improvements ($87,325). In the Utility Funds,
fund balance is being transferred to fund water master plan projects ($2,000,000), a lead and copper pipe inventory ($525,000),
and CIP funds are being reappropriated to fund additional sewer master plan projects. Excess funds from completed CIP projects
are also being re-appropriated to fund additional storm sewer master plan projects and additional river restoration projects. The ‘
Fleet Fund is recognizing revenue from the sale of assets ($106,650) and a donation ($20,000) to purchase additional equipment
for Public Services, Police, and Management Services. The IT Fund is recognizing revenue from internal users and appropriating
those funds for equipment purchases ($700,000).

Proposed Ordinance 2023-25 adopting the proposed Tentative Budget of
Ogden City for Fiscal Year 2023-2024

A memo from Council staff regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2023-2024 tentative budget came before the
Council for consideration. The memo stated on May 2, 2023, Mayor Mike Caldwell presented his proposed FY 2023-2024
budget; each section of the budget will be reviewed in greater detail over the course of the coming weeks and months, culminating
with potential adoption of the budget on June 20, 2023.

Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide
Council staffs analysis of the tentative budget. She discussed Council staff’s participation in the budget preparation process,
which includes an analysis of the City’s financial principles, for which there was one change this year to increase the maximum
fund balance from 10 percent to 20 percent. The Mayor’s proposed budget does not include a recommended tax increase and the
most significant items in the budget include the following:

, e Employee Cost of Living Increases
o One percent Cost of Living Adjustment (COLA);
o Four percent merit increase;
e Nine New Positions (Net)
e General Plan Update (~$1 M) ,
e Union Station Property Purchase ($780,000)
e Swift Environmental — FINAL ($300 K)
e Recreation Improvements ($750 K)

Ms. Eller-Smith then provided a year-over-year comparison of the FY24 proposed budget with the FY23 adopted budget;
the total increase in General Fund revenue is 4.71 percent and a 0.45 percent increase when considering all funds in the total City
budget. She summarized the proposed staffing changes under the nine new employment positions, proposed changes to wage and
benefit costs (5.14 percent increase overall) and offered explanations of the manner in which the budget conforms with primary
directives of the City’s Five-Year Strategic Plan relating to economic development, community safety, recreation, City image and
reputation, and fiscal sustainability and transparency. She also provided a list of CIP projects included in the proposed budget,
with a total FY24 funding request of $5 million. One key project not included in the CIP is the Marshall White Community Center
project; this project will receive $1 million in Business Depot Ogden (BDO) lease revenue with the remainder of the project cost
coming from bond issuance. There will also be a bond for the Central Business District parking structures, and the Ogden Canyon
Water Line project is not included in the CIP because City Administration is still considering funding options for the nearly $87

million funding estimate. She concluded that to date there are no recommendations for changes to the budget via a Schedule A
document from Administration, but the Council may direct changes through a Schedule A-1 document at the time final adoption
of the budget is considered.

Vice Chair Richey referenced the listing of year-over-year General Fund revenues for proposed FY24 and adopted FY23
budgets; he asked ifyear to date tax collection matches budget projections. Ms. Eller-Smith noted that she does not have access to
final tax collection data and Council staff has only included year-to-date receivables through the end of March. Chief
Administrative Officer Johnson added that City Administration will be able to provide a quarterly update to the Council staff
office in the coming days and Ms. Eller-Smith’s information can then be updated; he noted sales tax revenues have leveled off.

Chair Choberka asked if the budget includes sufficient funding to provide all employees with a four-percent merit
increase even though all employees may not be eligible to receive four percent. Mr. Johnson stated that employees who are
already at the top of their pay range will not receive the full four percent and that is accounted for in the budget recommendation.
Ms. Eller-Smith added that all employees will receive the one percent COLA and their pay range will be adjusted to reflect that
increase.
