 5.      A presentation regarding the Redevelopment Agency of Provo City Corporation.
         (23-045) (1:32:09)

Brian Jones, City Attorney, shared some legal foundations on this presentation. The Council, as a
legislative body, has the ability to create and dissolve community redevelopment agencies. He
noted that right now, the Council is meeting as the Council and not as the Governing Board of
the Redevelopment Agency. Any discussion items for the Governing Board would need to be
deferred to one of their meetings. Mr. Jones shared several definitions from state law which
define redevelopment agencies, project areas, and other related terms. Mr. Jones also outlined
some of the duties and privileges of the redevelopment agency. One of the key distinctions is that
the City cannot gift property, but the RDA has the ability to gift property. They are governed by
the bylaws, which can be amended by the governing board. Councilor Katrice MacKay asked
about the Mayor’s role in the RDA board meetings. Mr. Jones indicated that her role is the same
as when she is in a Council meeting; she does not vote in the governing board meetings but is
permitted to be there.
Councilor David Shipley asked about how the RDA is funded and where that is established. Mr.
Jones shared details on this but noted that generally their funding comes from the sale and
purchase of property, as well as the tax increment financing. Bylaws do not have provisions over
funding. There are specific provisions that are slightly different but not many.
Mr. Shipley asked if Provo has established any ongoing funding for the RDA.

Keith Morey, Development Services Assistant Director, explained that once the city sets up a
RDA area with a sale of land or tax increment financing, those funds can still be used in the city
but the money that came from it is intended to go back into the RDA projects. Those
requirements are loose so the city could find beneficial use for the city with those funds. If funds
are going to be used for other things in the city, there needs to be a correlation with the RDA
projects. They do collect funds for the RDA projects that they set aside. Keith said they will have
some discretionary funds from the sale of some property at market rate. He believes that they can
transfer property to the RDA but may need to look at that to be sure.

 13.     A presentation of the FY2024 Tentative Budget and Council Priorities (23-008)
         (4:20:09)

Justin Harrison, Council Executive Director, presented. He shared a list of the Council’s
priorities and highlighted the funding status of these priorities. The long-range plans were
identified as a high priority by the Council and Mr. Harrison gave a status update on these plans.
The General Plan was updated earlier this year; the Council will be able to adopt the updated
Downtown Master Plan at the Council Meeting that evening. Several additional plans are in
progress with Development Services, including the Conservation and Resiliency Plan and the
Gateways Plan. The Canyons and Hillsides Plan and River and Lakeshore Plan also both kicked
off at the end of last year, and staff anticipate those will be brought to the Council later this year.

The next priority the Council included was a Provo-specific housing audit or study, which has
been discussed extensively in Work Meetings. Staff are currently working on some internal
efforts to identify relevant data points. Stan McShinsky, GIS Analyst, has been incredibly helpful
in facilitating this process in order to help the Council realize some of their goals related to this.

The five-year staffing plan for Police and Fire is still being developed by the Administration.
There are several positions that have been funded in the tentative budget and Mr. Harrison gave
details about these. There are two additional patrol police officers that were unfunded in the
budget but have been requested.

IS and Cybersecurity enhancements is another Council priority. Mr. Harrison provided details on
a number of supplemental requests that were submitted for the budget and which have been
addressed with funding in the tentative budget. The one critical unfunded ask was for a Cayenta
software analyst.

For employee retention, the tentative budget includes a number of relevant items, including a 4%
cost of living adjustment and other Human Resources initiatives related to the pay study and
merit increases. For the priority of city debt reduction, Mr. Harrison gave updates on the City’s
debt. There is currently only one variable interest rate as part of the City’s debt portfolio.
Additionally, there could be opportunities to addressing this outstanding debt through CDBG
allocations.

A Provo app is an ongoing project and a priority of the Mayor as well. Staff are doing internal
cleanup and a redesign of the City website, which will make the process easier moving forward
and a Provo app will be forthcoming in the future.

Plan has partial funding in the proposed CIP budgets for fiscal year 2024. The Rock Canyon
trailhead project costs roughly $3 million. About $2 million has been funded through grants, and
$500,000 will come from the City as a match to $500,000 of community fundraising. In FY2025,
a roundabout for the Rock Canyon trailhead parking lot entry has been funded in the CIP.
