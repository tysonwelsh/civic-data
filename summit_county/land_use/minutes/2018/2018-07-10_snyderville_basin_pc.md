Planner Caus said this application is for a 300,000 gallon water tank to be located at the 
top of The Colony.  It is behind the last lot of the entire development.  He displayed a 
slide that indicated the general location of the tank.  The tank will be placed 
underground and the area will be re-vegetated.  Staff recommends approval as outlined 
in the Staff Report.  The applicant had no comments.  
 
The public hearing was opened.  No comments were made and the public hearing was closed. 
 
COMMISSION COMMENTS AND QUESTIONS          
 
Commissioner Stevens asked if any part of the water tank will be above ground.  
Planner Caus said there will be vents above the ground.   
 
Commissioner Fine noted the tank would be buried with a 3:1 fill slope.  What does that 
mean?  The applicant answered that means it will be a fairly gentle slope.  
Commissioner Fine asked if this water will be for culinary use only or will it also be 
used for fire hydrants?  The applicant said most of the capacity is for fire suppression.   
 
Commissioner Fine noted the Staff Report states that no outdoor storage is permitted.  
Planner Caus replied that means that equipment may not be stored outdoors.  
Commissioner Cooke asked if there will be any trails that will be impacted or re-routed.  
The applicant responded there are no trails nearby.   
 
Commissioner Harte made a motion, which was seconded by Commissioner Stevens, 
to approve the application as outlined in the Staff Report and as follows.  All voted 
in approval.

4. Public Hearing and possible action regarding potential amendments to Sections 
10-8 General Regulations of the Snyderville Basin Development Code to allow for 
the operation of Mobile Food Trucks and Mobile Food Courts in Certain Areas of 
the Snyderville Basin – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner  
 
Planner Milliner said this meeting is to review the proposed amendments to the mobile 
food business ordinances that have been proposed.  Planner Milliner reminded the 
Commission there have been a couple of meetings previously held on this item.  The 
Commission said they would like to go over a couple of items before any action is taken 
on the amendments.   
 
Planner Milliner explained some changes have been made to the ordinance per the 
Commission’s request.  One change is the number of trucks allowed in a mobile food 
court.  A previous discussion considered how many trucks will be allowed on a site.  It 
was decided to eliminate the number listing the maximum amount of trucks.  This will 
be decided when the Commission reviews the site design.   
 
Planner Milliner requested that an up or down voted be sent to the County Council, 
after the public hearing and discussion.       
 
The public hearing was opened.  No comments were made and the public hearing was closed.  
 
COMMISSION COMMENTS AND QUESTIONS

that Summit County uses.  It has also been adopted by the State of Utah.  Chair Peck said 
if it is the same thing, she would rather have the local Code listed.   
Chair Peck suggested a few changes to be made to the document.

FINDINGS OF FACT 
1. Chapter 1 of the Snyderville Basin General Plan notes that social and economic 
diversity and its associated demands are encouraged for the long term health of 
the Basin. 
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission 
July 10, 2018 
Page 6 of 8 
 
2. In furtherance of this goal,  §10-1-1(D) of the Summit County Code (“Code”) 
provides that within the Snyderville Basin Planning Area tightly knit, neighbor 
friendly, town, village, and resort centers shall be permitted in designated 
locations.  These centers shall be designed to maintain and review a sense of 
place and foster a feeling of belonging.    
3. Individuals have reported that mobile food businesses are important 
contributors to a community’s social scene, as they become impromptu 
gathering spaces. 
4. Qualifying provisions and criteria included in the proposed language are 
designed to ensure that the food trucks are compatible with surrounding uses, 
and are respectful to the natural and built environment.  
5. The proposed Code language does not remove any existing restrictions that 
would unduly affect nearby property owners. 
6. Food trucks are small businesses and are generally local. 
7. Small businesses have proven to be an important component in Summit County’s 
local economy. 
8. As currently drafted, the Summit County Code does not set forth specific criteria 
or a process for the regulation of Mobile Food Businesses or Mobile food Courts. 
 
CONCLUSIONS OF LAW 
1. The amendment is consistent with the goals, objectives, and policies of the 
General Plan.   
2. The amendment will not permit the use of land that is not consistent with the 
uses of properties nearby. 
3. The amendment will not permit the removal of the then existing restrictions 
which will unduly affect nearby property. 
4. The amendment will not grant special favors or circumstances solely for one 
property owner or developer.  
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission 
July 10, 2018

5. The amendment will promote the public health, safety, and welfare better than 
the existing regulations for which the amendment is intended to change.
