         The purpose of the joint work session was to review the agenda for the Special City Council meeting scheduled to begin
at 6:00 p.m., discuss the proposed 2026-2030 Five-Year Consolidated Plan and Citizen Participation Plan; discuss a Council
member initiative regarding Lester Park and Lorin Farr Pool updates; discuss proposed Adams Community Reinvestment Area –
Nine Rails Arts Programming; proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget Amendment – Adams CRA Arts Programming; proposed
Amendment – WonderBlock Land Transfer and Development Agreement; proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget Amendment –
WonderBlock Bond Interest Income; discuss Council/Board business; and hear Council/Board comments.

Agenda Review
        Chair Richey and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the Special City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Proposed 2026-2030 Five Year Consolidated Plan
          The work session discussion is intended to inform the development of the upcoming Fiscal Year (FY) 2026- 2030
Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) so any changes the Council desires can be incorporated into the Plan. This discussion will meet the
intent of the Council’s Budget Guideline adopted with the FY2024- 2025 budget.
          Community Development Program Compliance Administrator Fuentes and Community Development Director Smith
used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to facilitate Council discussion of the proposed 2026-2030 ConPlan, which is the
mechanism for the City to receiving HUD Entitlement Funds. Along with the ConPlan, the City is required by HUD to prepare:
              • Citizen Participation Plan
              • An Analysis of Impediments to Fair Housing Choice (AI)
              • Priority Needs Assessment (in ConPlan)
              • Five Year Consolidated Plan (ConPlan)
              • Annual Action Plan (implements 1st year of the ConPlan)
             • Neighborhood Revitalization Strategy Area (NRSA) – (optional)
         For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.

Proposed Adams Community Reinvestment Area – Nine Rails Arts
Programming
          City Administration has proposed that the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) enter into an eighteen month “pilot agreement”
with Ogden Contemporary Arts (OCA) to manage and expand the arts programming beyond the Dumke Arts plaza into the Nine
Rails District. Funding for the contract is proposed to come from the Adams Community Reinvestment Area (CRA) budget. The
Administration is hoping that the experience with the pilot agreement will inform future art policy for other areas of the City. The
Board initially discussed the proposal on August 20, 2024, and expressed concerns about whether tax increment funds could or
should be used for arts programming as it would limit the available funds for other economic development projects in the CRA.
Board members were also concerned about how the other participating taxing entities would view the expenditures. Some Board
members also felt that a public process to select a provider would be more appropriate than sole sourcing the contract for arts
programming services. In response to Board concerns, City Administration asserts that this agreement and funding will allow
OCA additional time to build organizational capacity to sustain current and future programming levels.
          Economic Development Director Meess and Arts Administrator Buckley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
address the concerns expressed by the Board in their August 20, 2024, work session meeting. The presentation focused on the
appropriateness of using tax increment for arts/events programming and any precedents for this type of agreement, as well as the
potential for the program to create a return on investment for the Agency. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the
information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among Ms. Meess and Board members centered on other funding options for the proposed agreement;
whether it would be appropriate to pursue a public request for proposals (RFP) process for this program and agreement. There was
also extensive discussion about the philosophy of using RDA funds for arts programming. Chair Richey concluded Council
leadership will take the matter under advisement and determine when to bring an action item to the Board regarding the proposed
pilot agreement.

Proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget Amendment – Adams CRA Arts
Programming
         Finance Director Sorensen explained this item corresponds with the previous agenda item; City Administration is
proposing a budget amendment of $120,000 to increase the Fiscal Year (FY) 2025 Redevelopment Agency (RDA) budget to fund
extended arts programming in the Nine Rails Creative District. The proposed amendment would recognize funds from the Adams
Community Reinvestment Area (CRA) fund balance and appropriate those funds to a new area specific program account to fund a
contract with Ogden Contemporary Arts (OCA). Administration requests the Board hold a public hearing on October 1, 2024, to
consider the proposed budget amendment.

Proposed Amendment – WonderBlock Land Transfer and Development
Agreement
         Community and Economic Development Executive Director Johnson explained the current proposal is to approve the
First Amendment to the Amended and Restated Land Transfer and Development Agreement, which includes updates to the
phasing and naming of phases, detailed financial commitments and processes, changes to the invoicing and payment procedures,
and clarifications on ownership and representation for the WonderBlock Project. Mr. Johnson used the aid of a PowerPoint
presentation to expound on the terms of the proposed agreement. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information
packet for the meeting.
         Chair Richey concluded the proposed amendment will be presented to the Board in their October 1 meeting for
consideration and action.

Proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget Amendment – WonderBlock Bond
Interest Income
         Finance Director Sorensen explained this item corresponds with the previous agenda item; Administration is proposing
an amendment to the Fiscal Year 2024-2025 budget to recognize $4,000,000 in interest income on the Series 2023 $64,220,000
Sales Tax and Tax Increment Revenue Bonds. These bonds were issued in December 2023 to fund the Agency’s portion of the
WonderBlock project. The additional revenue will be used to fund development costs related to the project. Excess funds, if any,
will be used for debt service.
         Chair Richey concluded this item requires a public hearing and can also be scheduled the October 1 Redevelopment
Agency (RDA) meeting.

Council Member Initiative – Lester Park and Lorin Farr Pool Updates
         Council Member Graf has proposed an initiative to discuss the use of $10 million of ARPA (American Rescue Plan Act)
funds to make improvements to Lester Park and Lorin Farr Pool. Council member Graf used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation
to provide the details of his proposal; the presentation focused on the history of Lester Park, which was named for Mayor Lester
Herrick, who was elected as Ogden’s Mayor seven times. He compared Lester Park to Chandel Park, located in Detroit, Michigan;
“The $14 million overhaul is the largest so far among a wide range of Detroit Parks and Recreation Department construction and
renovation projects across the city getting attention largely with the aid of federal American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) dollars.” He
noted Lester Park is Ogden’s “Central Park’, located on the OGX route and in the Nine Rails Arts District. It is within walking
distance to downtown Ogden and is surrounded by a changing neighborhood with growing population. People currently are not
using the park because they feel it is unsafe, unwelcoming, and outdated. He expounded on ideas for addressing safety and to
activate the park again. The project is in line with the City’s Strategic Plan directives relating to economic development, City
image and reputation, community safety, and recreation. Council member Graf concluded this is the final year of the current
Strategic Plan and an opportunity to make an investment that will cap the plan with an exclamation point. He proposed the
Council approve the project in an effort to create a new, revitalized park that will serve Ogden and the people that live here for a
long time to come. For a copy of Council member Graf’s presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Council member Choberka agreed that investment in the park is needed, and she appreciated Council member Graf’s
engagement on this matter.
          Mayor Nadolski stated that he wants to be involved in presenting this project, which he believes is important to the
community. He discussed the past surrounding this project and indicated he feels there are some great opportunities available to
the City to help the community activate the space in a way that is organic and natural, and that helps unite the neighborhood. He
also discussed the need for Lorin Farr pool updates, but wanted to keep that project separate from the Lester Park project. He is
also focused on other needs in the community and emphasized the need to prioritize short- and long-term goals/projects for the
City.
          Council member Blair stated that it would be helpful for the Council to have information regarding the Capital
Improvement Plan (CIP) projects planned for Lester Park and Lorin Farr; he would also like to understand the amount of funding
that has been allocated to the two projects.
          Discussion among the Council centered on other park renovation projects in Ogden and other communities and the
success of those projects; opportunities for protecting the City’s investment in the park to ensure that blight does not creep into the
area in the near future; and the intent of the 2016 design concept contest regarding the Park.
