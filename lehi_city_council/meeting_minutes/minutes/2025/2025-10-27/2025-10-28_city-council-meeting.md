3) 20 Minute Citizen Input

Burrough Keesler is a longtime Lehi resident. He wanted to thank Mayor Johnson for his many
years of service, and especially for his advocacy for transportation. He thanked the city council
for their service and mentioned several sidewalk projects he’s seen completed that helped create
safe routes to school. He also thanked Lorin Powell and city staff for the work on 600 East. He
appreciates the parks that have been created and improved.

Delay Layton mentioned a road that narrows unexpectedly and discussed the danger it causes for
drivers, as there is a lot of speeding along that road.

Rachel Anderson shared worries over head-on collisions that could result from the new flex lanes
on Pioneer Crossing. She mentioned other places that use movable barriers for their flex lanes.

José Zunia is a union representative for carpenters. He explained that it is difficult for young
people to make it in the trades with how many subcontractors there are. He encouraged the
council to look to smaller developers for their projects.

Max Engling shared concerns over sidewalks ending on major roads. He thinks that constructing
more sidewalk would make life easier for both unhoused people, and the general population of
Lehi.

7) Public Hearing and Consideration of Ordinance #64-2025 adopting an Impact Fee
     Facilities Plan, and impact Fee Analysis, and Amending Impact Fees for Power .

Dean Lundell presented the item with Suzie Becker. Becker defined an impact fee as a one-time
fee charged to offset capital costs associated with new developments. They can only cover costs
of system improvements, not project improvements. Two documents are used to determine
Impact Fees, the IIFP and the IFA, which gather and analyze information respectively. Becker
explained that Lehi is growing quickly and will need many new facilities in the upcoming
decade. She shared a presentation on the data she had analyzed.

Councilor Condie asked about a specific figure on the megawattage chart. Becker clarified it for
him.

Lundell explained that Impact Fees have gone up 28% since the last time they were analyzed,
and mentioned that it almost exactly matches inflation, which sits at 29%.

Mayor Pro Tempore Albrecht opened and closed the public comment period.

Motion: Councilor Condie moved to approve Ordinance #64-2025 adopting an Impact Fee
Facilities Plan, and impact Fee Analysis, and Amending Impact Fees for Power. Councilor
Hancock seconded the motion.

Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Yes; Councilor Hancock, Yes;
Councilor Newall, Yes; and Councilor Stallings, Yes. The motion passed unanimously.


8) Consideration of Resolution #2025-77, approving the UAMPS Fremont Solar Project
     Agreement.

Joel Eves presented the item. He began by addressing some questions from the Council.

Councilor Stallings asked if the price for solar energy was reasonable. Eves confirmed that it is
compared to other places that are doing solar. He explained that Lehi is in an ideal spot to
receive tax incentives that will be going away in the future. Cou ncilor Stallings asked if there
would be overproduction, and if the price would stay good. Eves said there would still be some
risk, but the battery attached to the project would help to hedge against that. Currently solar
energy is between $38-50 per megawatt. This is very good pricing. Eves clarified that tax credits
are an added benefit, but not a major deciding factor.

The Council asked what would happen if the city passed on the agreement. Eves said that there is
more interest than there are services available, and that the project would go on to another group.
Councilor Stallings asked what the benefits would be for the city. Eves said the biggest benefit
would be price surety. Based on personal experience, he believes prices will go up over time.

Eves presented a slideshow explaining the setup of the project, as well as the solar profile.

Councilor Condie asked where the project would fit into the city’s energy portfolio. Eves
explained that many of these types of projects are presented, but not many get started. This

be used at any time. It will be a daily market-driven decision.
