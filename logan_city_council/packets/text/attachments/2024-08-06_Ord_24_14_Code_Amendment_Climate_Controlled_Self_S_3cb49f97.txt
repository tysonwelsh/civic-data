PUBLIC HEARING:

PC 24-025 LDC Climate Controlled Self Storage [Code Amendment] David Adams and Daren Jackson
request an amendment to the Land Development Code to allow climate-controlled self-storage units as a
conditional use in the Commercial Services (CS) zone.

Staff: Russ Holley, Logan City Planner, reviewed the request to amend the Land Development Code
(LDC) to conditionally permit "Climate Controlled Self Storage" in the Commercial Service (CS) zone. The
request is to create a new land use table category for this type of land use. The existing LDC land use
table has a "storage, commercial self-storage" category that only allows that use in the Airport (AP) zone.
The applicant defines the new land use as buildings that are climate-controlled, no larger than 33,000 SF
in footprint size, and less than three stories in height. The applicant did not define what a climate-
controlled building would be.

The proposed request would allow an applicant to build multiple 33,000 SF one-story buildings and sprawl
them out over numerous acres, it does not prevent the development of the typical commercial self-storage
project.

The commercial self-storage use was eliminated in the Commercial Service (CS) and Industrial Park (IP)
zones in 2022 because they are land consumptive, provide minimal employment opportunities, and do not
contribute to the City's tax base.

R. Holley pointed out that the proposed code language would allow an applicant to build multiple 33,000
SF one-story buildings and sprawl them out over numerous acres. It does not prevent the development of
the typical commercial self-storage project.

The CS zone is one of the smaller land areas within the City with fewer acres available for new
development, let alone commercial self-storage types of uses.

Commissioner Peterson asked what zoning other Cities allow storage use in, and if any area of the Airport
zone can be developed for a use other than airport related uses.

R. Holley said that every City regulates and defines commercial storage differently. Typically, Cities have
an Industrial type of zone where commercial storage is allowed, however, some Cities do not allow
Commercial Storage at all. R. Holley said that there are many uses allowed in the Airport zone, and the
Airport also leases out land for private use.   Mike DeSimone said there is an Airport Land Use Plan that

Proponent: Darren Jackson addressed each of the findings for denial listed in the staff report.
   • Land consumption concerns are addressed by capping the storage buildings at 33,000 SF.
   • Retail space on the lower level with storage above offers more employment opportunities.
   • The retail space would contribute to the City's tax base.

Mike DeSimone said that before continuing, the commission needs to first decide if the proposed use is

Commissioner Heare said the applicant needs to present a comprehensive code amendment that
addresses minimum height and retail space requirements.

Mike DeSimone said the applicant can take the proposed amendment before the Municipal Council to see
if they are interested in entertaining the idea of allowing climate-controlled storage as a use before the
proponent drafts an updated amendment.

Motion: Commissioner Lucero made a motion to forward a recommendation of denial to the Municipal
Council on a code amendment for PC 24-025 with the findings for denial listed below. Commissioner
Heare seconded the motion.

FINDINGS FOR DENIAL
1.    Utah State Law authorizes local Planning Commission to recommend ordinance changes to
      the legislative body (Municipal Council).
2.    The proposed Code Amendments are not consistent with the Commercial Services description
      in the Logan City General Plan.
3.    The proposed amendments would further the problem of land consumptive projects that
      provide little employment and tax benefits for City residents.
4.    Areas zoned for Commercial Service are better suited for projects and land uses that provide
      better employment opportunities.
5.    No public comment has been received in support of the proposed amendments.

PROJECT INFORMATION
The proponent is requesting to amend the Land Development Code (LDC) to conditionally
permit "Climate Controlled Self Storage" in the Commercial Service (CS) zone. The request is to
create a new land use table category for this type of land use. The existing LDC land use table
already has a category "Storage, Commercial Self-Storage" and only allows that land use in the
Airport (AP) zone. The applicant loosely defines the new land use as buildings that are climate
controlled, no larger than 33,000 SF in footprint size, and less than three stories in height.

The application mentions a possible project including climate controlled self-storage building
with additional retail space. Conceptual renderings show this building on the corner of 200 West
and 1400 North, but this application is solely for a code amendment and would impact all CS
zoned property within the city.

BACKGROUND
On March 1, 2022, the Logan City Municipal Council adopted a code amendment ordinance
prohibiting new commercial storage units in all zones in the City with the exception of the AP
zone. The debate around this code amendment, it was coupled with RV Campgrounds and
Impound Tow Yards, was that these land uses are all too land consumptive and offer little to no
employment and economic benefits to the city. Commercial self-storage facilities are land
consumptive, provide minimal employment opportunities, and provide very little, if any, sales tax
benefit.

CURRENT PROPOSAL
The proponent is requesting a code amendment to LDC Table 17.11.030 to conditionally
(Conditional Use Permit) allow "Climate Controlled Self Storage" in the Commercial Service
(CS) zone. Along with the proposed new land use category, the applicant defines the new land
use as buildings that are climate controlled, no larger than 33,000 SF in footprint size, and less
than three stories in height. No new definition or amendment to the definitions section of the
LDC is specifically proposed. The request is accompanied with a letter from the applicants,
Dave Adams and Christian Wilson, explaining why this new land use category is aligned and
compatible with the aesthetics of commercial service neighborhoods. They quote the General
Plan description of the CS zone and how it is designed to fill a need in between general
commercial and industrial areas of the city. Light manufacturing, warehousing, wholesaling, and
similar uses that support construction trades are ideal for the CS zone. The application also,
includes market studies stating the need for more self-storage units and how climate controlled
units yield a higher rate of return on investment.

SUMMARY
The main reasons commercial self-storage land uses were eliminated in the CS and IP zones in
2022 was because they are very land consumptive and eliminate important land from future
uses, they lack employment benefits with typically only one employee per complex, they don't
contribute to the City's tax base, and they don't contribute to furthering a sense of "place" in
Logan. By not using the available vacant lands in these zones for commercial self-storage, the

land area could go towards other, more intensive land uses with a greater tax and employment
base. This request of climate controlled self-storage does address the concerns about land
consumption by capping building size to 33,000 SF, but with no other limiting factors, an
applicant could build multiple 33,000 SF one-story buildings and sprawl them out over
numerous acres. The language is vague enough that the interpretation does not prevent the
development of the typical commercial self-storage type of project.

The CS zone is one of the smaller land areas within the city as compared to IP and AP. Logan
City has an approximate total of 12,000 acres of zoned land. The AP zone has 616 acres, the IP
has 1046 acres, and the CS zone has 503 acres. Based on this, the CS zone has fewer acres
available for new development, let alone commercial self-storage types of uses. The CS zone
fills a gap between the Commercial zone and the Industrial zone by permitting a wide range of
overlapping uses. Because the CS zone is typically located closer to the built areas of the City,
altering City policy towards a prohibition of commercial self-storage in most zones, reduces the
potential for future land uses that provide a more significant impact for the overall city, both in
terms of tax base as well as future employment opportunities that are conveniently located near
existing services. Not only is staff is concerned with the lack of clarity with this proposed code
amendment, but is also concerned about a deviation in City policy regarding this specific type of
land use. For these reasons, staff would recommend denial of this proposal.

Market Demand

A previous study conducted by Box Pro in January 2023 established that
the climate controlled self storage is severely underserved in the greater
Logan market. Whereas approximately 33% of self storage supply in the
western United States is climate controlled, only 7-10% of Logan's supply
is climate controlled. This translates to climate controlled self storage in
the Logan market being approximately 216,000 NSF undersupplied.

Land Use Recommendation

The subject project should be a three-story climate-controlled
development with a portion of the ground level dedicated to retail use.

Using other examples of multistory climate controlled self storage
facilities in the greater Salt Lake market, the project should expect to
achieve 50% building coverage, which results in a building footprint of
[arrow] 38,115 SF. At three stories, the total building is 114,345 gross square
feet. At an 85% efficiency, the project should produce 97,650 total net
rentable square feet including 91,650 net rentable square feet of self
storage, which should capture 42% of the projected market undersupply.

The retail portion should account for approximately 6,000 NSF or 6% of
the total project rentable square feet. This space can be divided into five
retail spaces that are approximately 24x50' each.

The remainder of the rentable space—91,650 NSF—should be dedicated
to climate controlled self storage.
