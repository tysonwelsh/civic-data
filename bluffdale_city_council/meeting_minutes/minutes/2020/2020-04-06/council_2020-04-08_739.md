4.     COVID-19 Briefmg; Staff Presenters, Battalion Fire Chief Warren James and Natalie
       Hall.

Emergency Program Manager, Natalie Hall reported on the Emergency Management response to
the earthquake that recently occurred in Bluffdale. The Engineering Department inspected all City
facilities and reported back formally to Salt Lake County. Ms. Hall also noted that City employees
were moved out of the Public Works Building on Redwood Road. The only damage that occurred
within the City was a crack on Boulden Falls Road and minor stucco cracking in nearby residences.
There was no other damage reported.

Ms. Hall stated that the primary challenge with the earthquake was sending out timely and accurate
information due to false rumors about larger, subsequent earthquakes. She commented that the
rumors constituted more of an emergency than the earthquake.

Information on the City's response to COVID-19 was also provided by Ms. Hall. She reported
that the City of Bluffdale made an emergency declaration in addition to the declarations made by
Salt Lake County and the State of Utah. The City is currently at Level III Activation, which
consists of monitoring the situation. Battalion Fire Chief, Warren James was also added to
Emergency Support Function No. 4 (ESF #4) - Firefighting. Chief James was working with Chief
Roberts on planning and working on the Incident Action Plan.

6.       PUBLIC HEARING - Consideration and vote on a resolution adopting the Tentative
         Budget for the 2020-2021 Fiscal Year and establishing the Time and Place for the
         Public Hearing on the Final Budget for the 2020-2021 Fiscal Year, staff presenters,
         Bruce Kartchner and Stephanie Thayer. (LBA & RDA FY2020 Tentative Budget
         discussion held in coniunction).

Finance Director, Bruce Kartchner provided the following details for the FY2021 Tentative
Budget:

     •    The budget must be approved by the first Tuesday in May for the fiscal year, which begins
          July 1, 2020 and ends on June 30, 2021.
     •    Conversations to discuss changes to the budget were expected and significant changes
          could be made based on the Council's direction.
     •    Once the tentative budget is passed, staff provides input and additional changes are made.
          The final budget is to be passed by June 22, 2020. Mr. Kartchner recommended passing
          the final budget at the first meeting in June.
     •    If the City Council requests a property tax increase, a Truth in Taxation Public Hearing
          must be held, which usually takes place in August.
     •    During the year, the Council may amend the budget as often as needed. A final budget
          amendment is typically made by the end of June.
     •    Mr. Kartchner explained that the budget is created based on various expenditure requests
          and modifications from department heads, prior year data, carryovers, and projections for
          the coming year based on current events and trends. He noted that revenue projections
          can change significantly depending on various factors, including the impact of COVID-19
          on the economy.
     •    The General Fund consists of two primary types of revenue including property, sales, and
          franchise taxes and non-tax fees, licenses, permits, and grants. The tax items are recurring
          revenue but can fluctuate based on economic variables. Some of the non-tax items are
          one-time revenues and some are recurring, such as garbage fees.
     •    Mr. Kartchner noted that he and Accountant/Human Resources Director, Stephanie
          Thayer have a specific model they use to provide the budget format and are required to
          ensure that the budget is balanced. Mr. Kartchner reported that he and Ms. Thayer have
          organized volumes of data and worked with Mr. Reid to finalize the details.
     •    Other funds aside from the General Fund include the Water Fund, which acts more like a
          business and funds that are limited in scope such as impact fees, RDA funds, and LBA
          funds.

       •    The budget originally included a personnel cost increase adjustment of3.5% for a Cost of

            keeping the merit increase at 1%.
       •    Mr. Kartchner explained that COLA and merit are provided as separate figures because

            range adjusts upward or downward, whereas adjustments for merit are for employees that
            are already within a specific range.

budget amounts are carried over to the upcoming fiscal year and adjustments are made based on
estimated revenue projections and department requests.

project where staff believes the budget will be by the end of the year. The last column consists of
requests to be added to the tentative budget.

Dave Kallas referred to Page 3 of the revenues and the re-appropriation of fund balances and asked
why the actual year-to-date projections were left blank. He thought the Council budgeted
approximately $900,000 as a transfer. Mr. Kartchner stated that staff budgets for all potential costs
but there are various events that occur that impact the expenditures. The departments are tightly
monitoring their costs and, as a result, the projected expenses are lower than anticipated.
Mr. Kartchner noted that staff is projecting an excess of $370,000 and that is why the amount is
zero; however, the costs related to COVID-19 have not been figured in, and the excess amount
will likely be adjusted.

Dave Kallas asked why the excess is not shown in what was budgeted. Mr. Kartchner noted that
it is reflected on the left side as $161,600.00. He explained that when the budget was amended in
February, actual revenues were higher than expected and staff addressed the excess accordingly.
The excess of $370,000 leaves the City with $1.975 million in unappropriated funds, which will
be available for future use.

Mr. Kartchner explained that the City is required to have no less than 5% and no more than 25%
in unappropriated fund balance. The current balance is approximately 12.5%. The target has been
18% and Mr. Kartchner may recommend increasing the balance and carrying it into next year to
provide flexibility in 2021. Council Member Kallas asked if the City has ever had to draw from
the funds. Mr. Kartchner replied that it has not done so in the last two years. He noted that, as
values increase and the economy increases, the City has been able to cover expenses with revenues
that have exceeded the budget.

fireworks since people can view them from home.
