2.1   Lourdes Ramos, City Treasurer, reported that the semi-annual City Deposit
      and Investment Report is presented every six months to the Utah Money

      presented was in December 2020. Ms. Ramos presented a chart depicting the
      City’s balances, investments, and bonds to be paid.

3.0   Discussion: Internal Accessory Dwelling Units (IADU)

3.1   Jennifer Jastremsky, planner, stated that the presentation being shown tonight
      was presented at the Planning Commission last week. The Planning
      Commission forwarded a positive recommendation and no formal requested
      changes were made. One of their concerns was that if a private lane was wide
      enough to accommodate on-street parking, then it should be included on the
      IADU map. Additionally, they discussed the question that if a neighborhood
      with larger lots had room for off-street parking, then should it be on the map
      if the road was narrower.

3.2   In response to a question from Councilmember T. Lowery, Ms. Jastremsky
      explained that there would have to be specific findings of why a deviation
      would be allowed. Staff would assess what types of conditions or findings
      would be appropriate.

3.3   Councilmember F. Lowry asked what streets were being assessed in the
      proposed ordinance. Ms. Jastremsky explained that public streets included
      anything 60 feet wide, which was the City’s standard. The majority of private
      streets were between 20 and 25 feet wide.

3.4   Mayor Walker said there were already a lot of ADUs in the City, and by creating
      this ordinance the City would be providing a way for them to exist legally and
      be better supported. He mentioned Holladay had a similar ordinance in place.
      Ms. Jastremsky said staff could reach out to Holladay to get more information
      on its ordinance. The question was raised as to whether or not a business
      license would be required for ADUs. Ms. Jastremsky said no; the City was
      instead requiring a land-use permit.

3.5   The Council then discussed the process that would take place before the
      formal adoption of an IADU ordinance. Fire Chief Clint Smith briefly discussed
      emergency access. There was further discussion on the matter.

3.0   Recognition: Mountain Point Medical Center recognizing Draper City Fire
      Department

3.1   Katie Flores, Chief Nursing Officer at Mountain Point Medical Center,
      recognized members of the Draper City Fire Department. She explained that
      the Amazing Save Awards were given to EMS and Police Departments that had
      provided amazing life-saving efforts to the members of the Draper City
      community. Tonight, Ms. Flores would be presenting two such awards.




                                                                         Page 3 of 6
      The first award was in reference to an incident that took place on March 25,
      2021. Medic Engine 22-BC-21 and Medic Ambulance 21 were dispatched on
      the jogging path where a patient had collapsed and was in cardiac arrest.
      When they arrived, CPR was being performed by a bystander. The City’s EMS
      personnel then arrived on the scene and took over, subsequently transporting
      the patient to the Mountain Point Medical Center where continued care and
      interventions took place. The patient was able to be discharged home nine
      days later. For this Amazing Save, the Mountain Point Medical Center
      recognized Battalion Chief Paramedic Robert Lambert, Captain Paramedic
      Steve Darlington, Firefighter/EMT Connor Carpenter, Firefighter/Paramedic
      Juan Pasillas, Firefighter/Paramedic Justin South, Firefighter/EMT Donovan
      Meniiz.

      The second award was in reference to an incident that took place on April 25,
      2021. Company 22 was dispatched to a patient with complaints of breathing
      problems. Upon arriving they found that the patient was unresponsive. They
      began CPR, and during the second round of CPR, the patient regained
      consciousness. The patient was then transported to Mountain Point Medical
      Center for continued care and intervention. The patient was discharged home
      eight days later. For this Amazing Save, the Mountain Point Medical Center
      recognized Captain Paramedic Steve Darlington, Engineer/Advanced EMT Tee
      Hover, Firefighter/EMT Tony Runco, Firefighter/EMT Connor Carpenter,
      Firefighter/Paramedic Klae Beck.

      Ms. Flores thanked the Draper City Fire Department for its service.

6.0   Action Item: Draper Community Foundation 2021 Agreement, approval of
      Agreement #21-175 with the Draper Community Foundation.

6.1   David Dobbins, City Manager, explained that every year the City funded the
      Draper Community Foundation. In the past, the City had given $155,000. He
      presented the agreement which included a list of items that the Foundation
      was responsible for. He reviewed the annual reporting process.

6.2   Mayor Walker asked questions about the trail program, to which Mr. Dobbins
      stated the program would begin with one trail and three signs.

      Councilmember F. Lowry asked if the amount was annual, to which Mr.
      Dobbins said yes.

      In response to a question from Councilmember Green, Mr. Barker stated that
      the signs fall under the category of government speech. The actual signs were
      13 by 10 inches in size.

6.3   Councilmember T. Lowery said it was great to have other revenue sources
      coming in for the maintenance of these trails. Councilmember F. Lowry added
      that having these signs, as adopted by an organization or company, would
      show pride in the trails as well.

      Mayor Walker agreed and noted he had built a sign as part of an Eagle Scout
      project.
