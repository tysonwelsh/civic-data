   1. AMEND COUNTY LAND USE CODE SECTIONS: DEFINITIONS (§101-1-7), GENERAL PROVISIONS (§102-1),
      NATURAL HAZARDS OVERLAY DISTRICTS (§104-27), SUPPLEMENTARY AND QUALIFYING REGULATIONS
      (§108-7) AND HILLSIDE DEVELOPMENT REVIEW PROCEDURES AND STANDARDS (§108-14) TO CLARIFY
      THAT THE PLANNING COMMISSION IS NOT THE ONLY LAND USE AUTHORITY OVER PROJECTS WITH
      NATURAL HAZARDS, AND TO PROVIDE CLARITY, REMOVE REDUNDANCIES, AND INCLUDE PROCESS STEPS
      AND APPEAL PROVISIONS FOR NATURAL HAZARDS REVIEWS – ORDINANCE 2016-17

         Charles Ewert, of the County Planning Division, noted that a public hearing was held on 9/6/2016 on
         the proposed changes, however, the commissioners needed some questions addressed prior to adopting
         the ordinance, including whether the changes will streamline the process for the applicant. He
         responded that it does and outlined three primary things that streamlines the development review
         process, i.e., it takes natural hazards consideration away from the hillside development review process
         and puts the private market more at the forefront for offering development pattern recommendations on
         geologic hazard areas. For a more thorough process, the ordinance calls for geologists, civil engineers,
         geotechnical engineers, etc., to look at each other’s work and verify that they have built/designed to the
         best of the industry’s practices.
         The ordinance is not affected by the new maps; the new maps have the same application to the old
         ordinance as to the new ordinance. He then showed both the old and new maps. The new maps have
         the best available information to help review/determine where natural hazards exist. Regarding the
         question of how many more property owners are affected by the new map, he responded that this is
         difficult to address. In an attempt to explain the total land acres affected by the recent map update or the
       total lots affected by it, planning and GIS staff tried to find a method of displaying/quantifying the
       information. However, because the earlier map is a non-digitized image, they have not been able to find
       an efficient method to do that. He noted that the new and better information from UGS is a result of
       various projects, including the hydrogeology study done for the Ogden Valley.
       This ordinance takes natural hazards/geology out of the hillside development if there is no such issue
       thereby expediting the building process for developers. Mr. Ewert addressed the commissioners’
       questions, including that the county will now be looking at geotechnical and civil evaluations to verify
       compliance with the geologist’s recommendations. He stated that the hillside review still applies if the
       land has more than 25% slope and to lots that have hazardous soil-types. Additionally, if questionable
       work is submitted, the ordinance gives the County Engineer full discretion to request that the scope of
       work be reviewed again and address issues. David Wilson, Deputy County Attorney, noted that the
       county relies on third parties for the certifications.
       Commissioner Ebert moved to adopt Ordinance 2016-17 amending the Weber County Land Use Code to
       update and clarify provisions related to natural hazards regulations and provide clerical edits; Chair Bell
       seconded.
       Commissioner Ebert – aye; Chair Bell – aye
