 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
       a. Discussion and Progress Report on the 5600 West Old Bingham Highway Station Area
          Plan
          Senior Planner Tayler Jensen provided an updated regarding the 5600 West Station Area
          Plan (SAP) which was set to be adopted by the end of the year. He hoped to obtain Council
          feedback prior to presenting it to the public. Mr. Jensen emphasized that the city did not
          have control over any of the land in the area and infrastructure improvements would be
          but the responsibility of developers. The SAP would provide a framework for future
          infrastructure and development. He reviewed the project timeline and goals with the
          Council.

          Consultant Emily Burrowes presented the project vision, which included light industrial,
          mixed-use, and high-density developments. A major feature in the plan was the
          "Brickline," which will serve as a key connector between the TRAX station and Bingham
          Creek Regional Park. The plan included crosswalks, bike lanes, and enhanced accessibility
          to create a destination area. Street sections were shown to highlight safety measures for
          pedestrians, and ideas for event-based street closures were discussed.

          A portion of the area falls within South Jordan, which has its own gateway plan and chose
          not to coordinate on this project. However, a public survey would be offered to collect
          feedback after the SAP was made available to the public and surrounding communities.
          Policy recommendations include the use of the TSOD overlay district for density and land
          use adjustments. Council Member Green asked if closing the corridor to automobile traffic
          had been considered. Ms. Burrowes said the option could be explored.

       b. Discussion of possible amendments to the City of West Jordan Firework Restriction
          Map for 2025
          Fire Chief Derek Maxfield expressed gratitude to the Council for having this discussion
          early and thanked GIS Administrator Clint Hutchings for preparing an interactive map. He
          provided a brief history of fireworks restrictions in the city, noting that while some
          Council Members have advocated for a total ban, the current map, restricts fireworks
          primarily on the west side and has been in place for several years. He mentioned that one
          of the biggest challenges was enforcement and safety. Referring to the request to review
          and recommend changes to the firework restriction map for 2025, he proposed moving
          the restriction to U111, as it serves as a clear boundary and would make enforcement
          easier.

          The Council looked at the Unified Fire Authority (UFA) Firework Restriction Map and
          discussed various issues surrounding fireworks. Council Chair Jacob pointed out
          inconsistencies with no restrictions in open fields areas like the one near Albertson's
          property, while less dangerous residential areas faced restrictions.

          Council Member Green suggested designating a specific location, such as Ron Wood Park,
          where residents living in a restricted areas could enjoy their fireworks. Council Members
          discussed the idea of banning fireworks completely west of U111. Chair Jacob suggested
          that educating the public might be a more effective approach rather than restriction.
          Council Member Green proposed holding individuals liable for the costs of putting out
          fires caused by fireworks suggesting it may encourage more caution when using
          fireworks. While the idea had some merit, City Attorney Josh Chandler felt that identifying
          causation could be tricky. Council Member Shelton expressed support for extending the
          restrictions to U111, while Council Member Green voiced concerns about certain areas,
          including 9400 S and 6400 W.

          Council Member Bedore noted that there was no regulation regarding the size of
          educational signs at fireworks stands and suggested that better signage or flyers could
          help educate the public. There was discussion around potential legislation to require
          more stringent education and restrictions at the point of sale. However, Council Member
          Whitelock expressed reluctance to burden small business owners with the responsibility
          of providing that information, suggesting instead that the city take on the task of
          educating residents.

          Council Office Director Alan Anderson suggested Council refer the map to staff for
          adjustments based on direction that evening and bring back the 2025 Firework
          Restriction Map for review and approval sometime in March or April 2025. Chief Maxfield
          thanked the Council for the discussion.

       c. Presentation of proposed Veterans Memorial Park Master Plan
          Parks Project Manager Tyler Crawford showed a list of priorities for Veterans Memorial
          Park provided by the Council and presented a comprehensive plan for park expansion,
          featuring a central loop, splash pads, play areas, expanded rodeo grounds, gardens,
          monument parks, and improved traffic flow. The plan also addressed event parking
          demands, the relocation of baseball fields, and established a food truck plaza.

          Council Member Green raised concerns about the extensive asphalt proposed and
          stormwater management. He suggested a multi-use space that could include tennis or
          basketball courts. Mr. Crawford acknowledged that parking was a top request but noted
          the possibility of integrating these ideas. Council discussed increasing the visibility of the
          fairgrounds, relocating the baseball fields, potential multi-use spaces, and Council
          Member Whitelock proposed the idea of adding an amphitheater in areas labeled for
          mounding/screening.

          Mr. Crawford noted that roads in the proposed plan were designed to improve traffic flow
          through the park. Mr. Lee added that adding the proposed parking lots would allow for
          the closure of Ring Road to use the area for events and recreation, allowing it to become
          an amenity.

       d. Discussion regarding deferred maintenance costs of city road surfacing
          Assistant Engineer Krista Reister explained the Pavement Condition Index (PCI) where
          road conditions are rated from of 0 (failed) to 100 (new), noting the city’s average PCI at
          53. She shared various levels of road deterioration and associated repair costs
          emphasizing the need for preventative maintenance and presented a plan for pavement
          preservation in West Jordan.

          Ms. Reister said maintaining the current level of spending for asphalt preservation would
          bring the mean PCI from 53 to 33 over the next ten years. The unfunded roadway
          maintenance backlog would increase from the current $124 million to $255 million in ten
          years. She pointed out that increasing the budget now, even by a small amount, would
          save the city millions of dollars over the next decade. Ms. Reister said staff could continue
          to look at funding scenario options, and suggested residents could be polled regarding
          desired level of service.

          The Council and staff discussed road maintenance methods, advancements, and costs. Ms.
          Reister said the City saved a lot of money by doing much of the maintenance work in -
          house, but some projects needed to be contracted out. Council Member Green asked if the
          city could stretch available dollars by investing in equipment and crews. Chair Jacob
          asked how much would need to be in the budget to maintain the current PCI of 5 3. City
          Administrator Korban Lee asked if it would be more fiscally responsible to fund
          maintaining current level of service, or to fully fund the required maintenance.

          Mr. Lee suggested scheduling another meeting to discuss realistic options for increasing
          resources available.

           Senior Assistant City Attorney Patrick Boyce explained the State controlled alcohol
           licensing, and the City issued permits. Mr. Boyce recommended simplifying Title 17 to be
           more user friendly with clearer authority for the Licensing Department. He said the
           proposed amendments reduced Title 17 from around 33 pages to 8 pages and asked the
           Council for direction.

           Council Member Green expressed support for the simplified Title 17 and shared the
           opinion that Title 17 needed to refer to Title 16 regarding license revocation. Mr. Boyce
           agreed that reference to Title 16 needed to be added.

           Council Member Whitelock suggested changing language in 17-2-4E to clarify temporary
           closure versus permanent closure. The Council discussed extending the closure timelines
           from the current 10-day regulation to a 30-60 day period. Staff would investigate related
           State provisions. Council Member Whitelock suggested clarifying acceptable
           identification for alcohol permits to align with state definitions. Council Member Bloom
           noted that State regulations did not recognize foreign forms of identification for purchase
           of alcohol. A majority of the Council appeared to support bringing proposed amendments
           to a future Council meeting with changes discussed that evening.

        f. Discussion of proposed amendments to Title 7 – Parking
           Senior Assistant City Attorney Patrick Boyce presented proposed amendments to Title 7
           regarding parking and explained proposed changes to the fee schedule. He said many of
           the proposed amendments would bring Title 7 into compliance with State Code. Council
           Member Green expressed support for the proposed amendments.

           Mr. Boyce spoke of enforcement highlighting the need for additional personnel and time
           to implement the program. The Council discussed proposed amendments and advocated
           for public education. Council Member Whitelock noted necessary grammatical edits and
           planned to coordinate with staff after the meeting.
