    George City Code as it relates to parking and setbacks in the PD-C (Planned

ZONE CHANGE/ORDINANCE:
     Consider approval of an ordinance changing the zone from C-4 to PD-C
     (Planned Development Commercial) on property located on the north-east
     corner of Tabernacle and 100 East.

      Planer Dan Boles presented the request to change the zone from C-4 to PD-C on
      property located on the northeast corner of Tabernacle and 100 East. He provided a
      PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics: vicinity map; zoning map;
      conceptual site plan; elevations; general plan map; parking. The Planning
      Commission recommended approval with conditions that if approved, the triggers for
      reevaluation of the parking on the site are the 3rd floor is built out or prior to the
      existing County office building being occupied.

      City Attorney Shawn Guzman commented on the number of stalls for parking. The
      County pointed out, the existing uses will not change and a lot more is being done
      remotely. Regrading the 192 stalls, he asked that the Council to allow for a variance
      of between 6-10 stalls. The plan for the electricity infrastructure is currently being
      designed.

      Washington County Commissioner Gil Almquist explained the older building has done
      well; however, there is a need to expand. There was no interest in leaving the
      downtown area.

      Councilmember Larkin commented she supports the parking reduction. She inquired
      about the HCP museum. Commissioner Almquist replied that the museum will have
      space on the first floor as will the tourism office.

      City Attorney Shawn Guzman and Commissioner Almquist noted the City will be
      allowed to spade and replant three (3) large oak trees.

      Commissioner Almquist commented the County is not borrowing any funds to
      construct the new building. They have using a local contractor who has committed
      to using as many local sub-contractors as they can.

APPEAL OF DECISION BY THE SHADE TREE BOARD:
     Consider an appeal of a decision of the Shade Tree Board to deny the
     removal of trees on St. George Boulevard that are protected by ordinance.

      Parks Manager Shane Moore explained JMI is coming in to appeal a decision made by
      the Shade to remove two (2) mulberry trees at 275 East St. George Boulevard at the
      old Spectrum newspaper location. These trees are protected by ordinance. JMI
      appeared before the Shade Tree Board in June to seek approval to remove the trees
      and replace them with a different species for visibility. The Shade Tree Board voted
      unanimously to deny the removal.

      Steve Jennings with JMI stated they purchased the spectrum building and are trying
      to clean up the landscaping around the building. They never received a letter from
      the Board on their decision and was encouraged to take the appeal to the Council.
      He provided a photo of the current landscape. At the time, they did not have a
      landscape plan and they now do. He spoke about a proposed landscape plan. They
      would like to remove the trees to replace them with other trees to meet or exceed
      the City’s landscape ordinance. In looking at the Shade Tree Board’s mission, to
      protect rare, endangered, or historical trees. They don’t feel like the trees fall under
      those categories. After being asked by Councilmember Larkin if these trees could be
      incorporated into the proposed plan, Mr. Jennings commented the trees are very
      large and overgrown.

      Mayor Pike noted the landscape plan is in the packet. He commented he loves the
      trees and the shade they offer.

      Mr. Moore noted the Board tries to work with the businesses. Typically, they offer
      their services and expertise to incorporate trees into a proposed plan. He feels these
      trees are a valuable part of the urban forest. The Board was adamant that they
      would love the trees to stay. After being asked by Councilmember Smethurst, Mr.
      Moore stated the trees are too large to be moved.
