 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
       a. Discussion of West Jordan Justice Court
          Council Office Director Alan Anderson reported that Judge Ronald E. Kunz had announced
          his retirement in January 2025. He turned time to Justice Court Administrator Jim Peters
          and Deputy Justice Court Administrator Kim Zimmerman, both local residents.

          Justice Court Administrator Jim Peters presented options for the future of West Jordan’s
          Justice Court following Judge Kunz’ retirement. The options included: dissolving the
          Court, partnering with a neighboring jurisdiction, or continuing to operate the Court.

          Mr. Peters said notice would need to be provided to the Judicial Council by July 1 at least
          two years prior to the effective date of dissolution of the Court. The Judicial Council could
          shorten that time frame if deemed warranted. If the court were dissolved, it would be
          unlikely be restored easily, under 78A-7-123(2)(b) they would need to demonstrate a
          strong need for is reinstatement. Partnering with a neighboring jurisdiction would
          require approval of the Judicial Council. He also noted that upon termination of the
          partnership, and with Judicial Council approval, West Jordan could resume operating its
          own court.

          The process to continue operating the West Jordan Court, was outlined as follows: the
          Administrative Office of the Courts (AOC) and West Jordan would advertise the vacancy
          for at least 30 days and possibly 45 days, ideally at the same time the AOC would form a
          Nominating Commission of five individuals which would conduct interviews (45 days),

            followed by a public comment period (at least 10 days), and the Mayor would select a
            finalist (30 days) with Council ratification (30 days). The whole process can take six
            months. The AOC would then provide a new judge orientation, and the Judicial Council
            would certify the new judge.

            Mr. Peters outlined the required advertising process and membership of the Nominating
            Commission found in 78A-7-202(3). Responding to a question from Vice Chair Lamb,
            Judge Kunz said his retirement date was flexible if the City chose to continue the Court
            and moved forward with finding a new judge in a timely manner. Mr. Anderson said City
            Code was already in place if the Council chose to continue the Court, and commented a
            judge pro tem could be appointed in the interim if needed. Mr. Peters would like to
            minimize the gap between Judge Kunz and a new judge as much as possible.

            The Council discussed benefits of continuing with a judicial court in West Jordan. Vice
            Chair Lamb and Council Members Whitelock, Green, and Bedore spoke in favor of
            continuing the West Jordan Court. Council Member Shelton mentioned knowing a
            neighboring city who eliminated their judicial court because they felt the city was
            subsidizing cases from outside the city, however, he would be happy to accept the will of
            the Council.

            Judge Kunz said he took pride in the service that had been provided in West Jordan and
            while caseloads were down recently, he believed the City owed it to residents to provide
            the service. Police Chief Ken Wallentine said approximately 416 hours per year would be
            added to Police Department personnel costs if West Jordan relied on the County for court
            services. He appreciated the relationship between the West Jordan Court and Police
            Department and felt the people were best served by being able to come to West Jordan
            Justice Court.

            A majority of the Council supported the continuation of West Jordan Justice Court.
