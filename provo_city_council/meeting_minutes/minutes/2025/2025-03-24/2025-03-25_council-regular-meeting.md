2         A presentation recognizing the service of Greg and Valynn Baum (25-007) 0:08:57

Mayor Kaufusi presented a comprehensive overview of the "My Hometown" initiative, which is a unique
partnership between Provo city, the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, volunteers, and
residents of the South Freedom Pioneer Park and Dixon neighborhoods. The collaboration extends to
businesses, nonprofit organizations, schools, and other faith groups, all working together to revitalize
these communities. This effort includes various programs such as classes at the Community Resource
Centers, service days focused on beautifying and repairing homes, and community events that foster
interaction among neighbors. In the past year, 1,648 residents took part in classes at the Community
Resource Centers, with offerings that ranged from English as a second language and American Sign
Language to financial management and basic home repair. Volunteers completed 35 home
improvement and beautification projects in the target neighborhoods, with over 1,450 volunteers
contributing their time and effort. Additionally, 5,300 residents participated in My Hometown
community events. In total, 2,279 volunteers dedicated over 45,664 hours, which, when valued at the
national volunteer rate of $29.50 per hour, amounts to $1,347,088 in contributions. Mayor Kaufusi took
a moment to recognize the volunteers in the audience, who shared their time, skills, and experience
teaching classes, organizing service projects, and planning social events that continue to bring neighbors
together.

Mayor Kaufusi took a moment to recognize Greg and Valynn Baum for their exceptional contributions to
the My Hometown Pioneering Program in Provo. Under their leadership, Provo has become a model for
other communities, including Orem, with multiple mayors seeking guidance on how to implement the
program. Mayor Kaufusi emphasized that Provo’s success has drawn attention from the Church, which
has referred other communities to learn from their efforts. To honor their leadership and commitment,
Mayor Kaufusi awarded Greg and Valynn Baum the Mayor’s Award, a rare recognition, acknowledging
their vital role in making the My Hometown program a success. She praised their service and leadership,
noting that their contributions have greatly lifted the community and set a high standard for others to
follow.

Mr. Baum expressed his gratitude, beginning with recognizing Mayor Kaufusi for her unwavering
support from the very beginning of the My Hometown initiative. He acknowledged her leadership and
the contributions of the city liaisons, Tara Riddle and Kendra Herbert, who have been instrumental in
the program's success. He highlighted how, starting with the Pioneer Park and South Freedom
neighborhoods, the initiative has expanded to include the Dixon community and will soon add the Provo
neighborhood in January 2026. Mr. Baum emphasized that none of the progress would have been
possible without the support of Provo city. He shared that over 9,000 residents and volunteers have
participated in the program since its launch in April 2022. He also spoke about the tangible impact the
program has had on residents, citing examples of individuals who have learned English and improved
their employment opportunities, such as the current PTA president at Franklin Elementary, an
immigrant from Peru who couldn’t speak English when she arrived but is now leading her school’s PTA.

Public Comment 0:27:58

Chair Garrett read the public comment preamble and opened the public comment period.

John Hale, of Provo, shared a deep personal story about the motivation behind starting his sober living
facility in Provo. John recounted the struggles of his younger brother, an athletic individual who had
been captain of his football team in Mesa, Arizona. His brother’s life took a tragic turn after a motorcycle
accident, leading to years of pain, addiction, homelessness, jail time, and near-death experiences. John
shared how his brother, who had once been a faithful assistant to the bishop, reached a breaking point,
crying out in despair during a prayer to God. This moment deeply impacted John, and he later learned
that his roommate, Joey, also struggled with addiction, despite appearing to be a stalwart member of
the church. Joey expressed his desire to one day start a sober living program, focusing not on the
financial aspect but on the opportunity to help others. Inspired by this, John and Joey began
brainstorming how to make this dream a reality. Unfortunately, Joey's dreams were derailed when a
heartbreak led him into depression and relapse. John’s commitment to the cause became even more
personal when he received a call from Joey’s family, asking him to check on his roommate. Tragically,
John found Joey in a devastating situation, an image that he described as haunting and pivotal in
transforming his vision of a sober living program into a concrete mission.

Laurine Hall, of Orem, expressed her deep concerns about the development along the Provo River,
particularly the rezoning of the land for the construction of three large warehouses. She painted a vivid
picture, asking the council to imagine a favorite family destination, only to be disrupted by the presence
of large, unsightly warehouses nearby. Laurine described the river as a sanctuary for her family, likening
it to a peaceful, pastoral place in England. She expressed concerns about the potential environmental
impacts of the development, including its effects on water and soil quality, the native plants, and the
mature trees that provide shade for those who enjoy walking along the river. Laurine shared stories of
people from surrounding areas, like Mapleton and Pleasant Grove, who come specifically to walk along
this section of the river due to its natural beauty. She also highlighted the importance of preserving
wildlife and the overall human experience in the community. While she expressed hope that the
landowners might sell the land and retire, Laurine urged the council to consider alternative locations for
the warehouses and suggested the land could be better used as a beautiful Provo reserve.

With no other public comments, Chair Garrett closed public comment.

6         A resolution approving lease purchase financing for two fire engines. (25-033) 0:42:29

     Motion:      An implied motion to approve Resolution 2025-13, as currently constituted, has been
                  made by council rule.

Mr. Borget provided an overview of a tax-exempt lease agreement for financing the purchase of fire
apparatus. He explained that for several years, the city has used this method, which is technically a loan,
to secure favorable rates for funding fire equipment. The Fire Department plans to enter into a tax-
exempt lease-to-purchase agreement with Webster Bank, which offered the lowest bid of 4.12% after a
competitive process with seven other bidders. The loan amount is $2.7 million, with an effective date of
April 1, 2025, and a final maturity date of April 1, 2033, making it an eight-year loan.

Chair Garrett opened public comment. With none, he invited a council discussion.

Mr. Jones noted that an email had been circulated about updated exhibits. He pointed out that the
resolution itself also needed to be updated because the amount was slightly incorrect. He asked Kevin to
display the updated figure on the screen and clarified that, while it was a technical adjustment, the
proper procedure was to make a motion to amend the resolution. The corrected amount was
$2,707,500, and he wanted to ensure the number was on the record. He then requested that someone
make a motion to amend the resolution to reflect this updated figure.

     Motion:      Councilor Bogdin made a motion to update the language in the resolution to reflect
                  the correct amount. Seconded by Councilor Whipple.

Chair asked if there was any discussion on the motion. With none, he called for a vote on the motion.
     Vote:        The motion passed 7:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, Handley, Hoban,
                  MacKay, and Whipple in favor.

Chair Garrett called for a vote on the implied motion as amended.

     Vote:        The motion passed 7:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, Handley, Hoban,
                  MacKay, and Whipple in favor.
