Presentation by Utah Division of Multicultural Affairs
           The Council received a presentation from the Utah State Division of Multicultural Affairs. Representatives from the
Division were originally invited to present their Leading by Inclusion campaign to the Council. The on-going pandemic has
refocused their efforts on addressing gaps and barriers faced by underserved communities.
           Chair Choberka recognized the members of the City's Diversity Commission who were present this evening, after which
she invited Nubia Pefia, Director for the Utah Division of Multicultural Affairs (MCA), to address the group. Ms. Pefia provided
an overview of the resources available through MCA for marginalized populations during the COVID-19 Pandemic. Marginalized
populations have reduced access to technology, which results in them receiving a great deal of misinformation; they also face
housing insecurity, reduced access to medical resources, language barriers, fear of deportation, and several other negative
circumstances. The Governor's Office has chosen to create a Multicultural Advisory Committee that will focus on addressing
these needs for underserved communities. She then engaged in discussion with members of the City Council, City Administration,
and the City's Diversity Commission about resources that ai·e available to underserved residents in the Ogden community in light
of the pandemic. There was a focus on the difficulty that some residents with limited income are experiencing when trying to gain
access to health care and other resources needed to support them during a quarantine or shelter-in-place order; many low income
residents do not have access to technology or internet service needed to easily receive information about health directives or to
facilitate home-schooling for their children. The group discussed opportunities for pooling resources and coordinating efforts in
communicating important health information and updates to all residents in the community.

    Proposed Defense Depot Reuse (DOR) Zone Building Height Amendments
              Council Deputy Director Symes explained a petition has been filed by Boyer Company to amend the height limit for
    buildings within a designated area in Business Depot Ogden (BDO). The proposal would increase the maximum height limit for
    buildings north of 400 North and east of Depot Drive from 50 feet to 90 feet. The area proposed for increased height is located
    within the Defense Depot Reuse (DDR) zone. The DDR zone is classified as one of the City's manufacturing and industrial zones.
    The petitioner has indicated an in increase in demand for taller buildings within BDO.
              Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the implications of the
    proposed ordinance amendments. He presented an aerial map to orient the Council to the area of BDO that is subject to the
    proposed amendment; it is located in the northeast quadrant of the installation. At present, the current maximum building height in
    the DDR zone is 50 feet. The petitioner would like to increase the maximum building height to 90 feet in the area north of 400
    East and east ofDepot Drive; this area is bordered by agricultural/wetland property to the east and the Weber County Fairgrounds
    to the north. Planning Commission considerations included the following:
                   •   Some manufacturing uses require additional building height.
                   •   The area is far from residential properties and the Ogden Nature Center.
                   •   Other manufacturing zones in similar situations have no height limit.
                   •   Unlike most of the BDO, this area has historically been vacant property.
                   •   The change will accommodate and support growth in the BDO, which is consistent with the General Plan
                       (amusement ride manufacture company needs additional height to develop prototypes and fill orders).
              The Commission voted unanimously to recommend approval of the proposed ordinance amendmentto increase building
    heights from 50 feet to 90 feet in the area of the DDR zone located north of 400 North Street and east of Depot Drive.
              Council member Stephens wondered if this proposed change could lead to additional proposals to increase the maximum
    building height in other areas ofBDO. Mr. Montgome1y stated he does not believe there are other undeveloped areas that have the
    same criteria as the subject area and would warrant increased maximum building heights.
              Chair Choberka inquired as to how this proposed amendment was advertised. Mr. Montgomery stated that there was no
    requirement to mail personal notification to individual prope1ty owners; however, the City has notified Harrisville City of the
    proposed amendment, given that the installation borders properties in that city.
              Mr. Symes indicated Council staff will include action items on the May 5 and May 12 City Council meeting agendas for
    this issue.
