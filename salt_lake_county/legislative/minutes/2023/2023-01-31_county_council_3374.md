2. CITIZEN PUBLIC INPUT
Dr. Brian Moench  spoke on behalf of Utah Physicians for a Healthy Environment in 
opposition to a proposal to log Northern Utah ’s forest as a solution to delivering more 
water to the Great Salt Lake.  Research indicates that killing trees to increase flow to 
streams does not have a consistent result, and in fact, the opposite may occur. If 
increased flow does occur, it is minor, temporary, and mainly happens in the form of 
increased flooding, and it does not increase water storage.  Claims that forests are 
tinderboxes because they are overgrown and unhealthy and logging is a cure is largely a 
myth and has been debunked by numerous independent experts.  In fact, Dr. David 
Lindenmayer, author of 48 books on forests, said logged forests always burn at greater 
severity than intact forests.  The climate crisis is the main driver of western forest and fire 
vulnerability, not tree density or excessive fuels.  In fact, dense tree areas act as 
windbreaks, retain moisture, and decrease forest temperatures.  Furthermore, burning the 
biomass after trees are thinned not only releases carbon into the atmosphere, but also 
sacrifices a critical pathway for carbon absorption long into the future.  Then, when trees 
are burned while there is still snowpack, the carbon falls onto the snow, warming it and 
accelerating snowpack loss.  Forest thinning is a waste of taxpayer money; government 
should focus water conservation by hardening home requirements.  Utah Physicians for a 
Healthy Environment is asking the Council to formally reject this proposal.

3.3. Mayor
Mayor Jennifer Wilson  stated she had two items regarding County employees 
stepping up.  First, Catherine Kanter, Deputy Mayor of Regional Operations, and 
Visit Salt Lake worked together to get a shuttle service up Little Cottonwood 
Canyon.  That was launched, and to date, reports showed that 780 of the 800 seats 
available were filled and that locals and tourists were happy with it.  She also 
acknowledged the Sandy City police who escorted the buses up to the front of the 
line.
Secondly, due to the cold temperatures, Parks and Recreation opened Central City 
Recreation Center last night after closure to 40 unsheltered individuals.   There were 
efforts made throughout the evening to go out and find people who needed shelter .  
Martin Jensen, Director, Parks and Recreation Division said last night was calm and 
and that people left peacefully the next morning before the recreation center 
opened, and some of them helped clean up.  Parks and Recreation staff members 
volunteered, along with people from Salt Lake City and Unsheltered Utah.  Tonight, 
they are opening it again, and there is capacity for 90 unsheltered individuals.

RESOLUTION NO. 6052
A RESOLUTION OF THE SALT LAKE COUNTY COUNCIL 
CONDITIONALLY AGREEING TO THE CREATION OF AN ISLAND 
FROM THE PROPOSED LARK AREA ANNEXATION BY SOUTH 
JORDAN CITY
WHEREAS, South Jordan City was served with a notice of intent to file a 
petition to annex what the petition sponsor has titled the Lark Area 
Annexation, as shown on the map attached as Exhibit A, which if accepted 
by South Jordan City could lead to a portion of what is currently 
unincorporated Salt Lake County being annexed into South Jordan City; and
WHEREAS, the proposed annexation is being pursued under and controlled 
by Utah State Code Sections 10-2-401, et seq.; and 
Page 7 of 14
County Council Meeting Minutes January 31, 2023
WHEREAS, currently, the proposed annexation would create an 
unincorporated island and pursuant to Utah Code Section 10-2-402(1)(b)(iii)
(B); an unincorporated area may not be annexed to a municipality if the 
annexation will create an unincorporated island unless the County and 
municipality otherwise agree; and 
WHEREAS, Herriman City has accepted an annexation petition titled the 
Rana Toro Annexation, as shown on the map attached as Exhibit B, that 
could successfully annex the island prior to or simultaneously with the 
effective date of the Lark Area Annexation and, thus, there would in fact not 
be an island created by the proposed Lark Area Annexation as illustrated on 
the map attached as Exhibit C showing the combined Lark Area and Rana 
Toro annexations; and
WHEREAS, the sponsors of the Lark Area Annexation assert that the Rana 
Toro Annexation will be competed before or simultaneously with the Lark 
Area Annexation; and 
WHEREAS, although the County ’s consent may ultimately not be required 
because the proposed annexation may not result in the creation of an 
unincorporated island, the annexation process which is intended to allow all 
interested parties to review the proposed annexation to determine if it is 
lawful and has sufficient community support could be needlessly 
complicated due to questions regarding the County’s consent;
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, THAT:
Although the consent of Salt Lake County may ultimately not be required, to 
avoid any complications and uncertainty, Salt Lake County conditionally 
agrees to the creation of the unincorporated island shown in Exhibit A that 
would result if South Jordan City accepts, certifies, and approves, the 
proposed Lark Area Annexation and is otherwise able to meet the 
requirements of Utah State Code Sections 10-2-401, et seq ., and successfully 
annex the proposed annexation area.  However, if Salt Lake County 
determines that Herriman City will be unable to successfully complete the 
Rana Toro Annexation and annex the unincorporated island before the Lark 
Area Annexation is scheduled to be finalized, Salt Lake County may, at its 
sole discretion, conduct a full review of the proposed annexation and 
withdraw its conditional consent to the creation of an unincorporated island.
