7. Joint Work Session with the Planning Commission pertaining to the General Plan
Update (4:30 p.m.)
The Planning Department presented on the General Plan land use element update. They
identified areas of stability and areas of change and they wanted to focus on the areas of change.
Transportation issues will also be discussed as they work through the process. Lehi is automobile
oriented, but they could consider providing other forms of transportation such as biking, walking,
and transit. The Council, Planning Commission, and staff were assigned to different groups for
further discussion and input on where they would like to see mixed use development, open
space, transit, and other land use elements.

11. Consideration of Preliminary subdivision approval for the Parkview Cottages, a 66-unit
    residential development located at 300 West 3200 North.
    Brad Mackey with Ivory Development was representing the applicant. He stated that this is the
    plan they’ve been working on for the last year.

    Councilor Condie stated that there was some concern about the aesthetics on lot 101 and the
    direction the house is oriented. The current orientation of the proposed home would be to the
    north instead of facing east onto 300 West. Mr. Mackey stated that the backyard and side yard
    setbacks would be applied differently. There was further discussion on the home orientation
    and its relation to the current neighboring homes. Mr. Mackey stated that this is a standard
    corner lot configuration. Councilor Koivisto stated that a PUD is very restrictive and doesn’t
    believe that the lot conforms to the PUD requirements. Councilor Albrecht stated that they
    should avoid cars backing onto 300 West for safety concerns. Mayor Johnson suggested that
    they build a smaller footprint on lot 101 so that the yard setbacks are more uniform with the
    home to the south. Mr. Mackey stated that they can do a smaller footprint on lot 101.

    Councilor Hancock inquired about adding no parking zones along road B to provide a better
    line of sight. He also inquired about the additional parking that was part of the concept plan.
    Mr. Mackey stated that the DRC asked them to remove parking in parcel F. Mr. Struthers stated
    that there was some concern with people backing up onto road D as others were turning into
    the development. He stated that they felt that was a conflict. Mr. Struthers stated that Ivory
    meets the required parking without the parking stalls. Mr. Mackey stated that they are happy
    to put them back in and they can add them closer to lot 141 so there isn’t a conflict with the
    intersection.

    Councilor Koivisto inquired about the open space requirements for the PUD. Mr. Struthers
    stated that the requirement is 15% open space. He said that the detention pond should not be
    counted toward the open space. Mr. Mackey explained how they matched the open space
    requirements even if they take out the detention pond. He feels that they’ll still meet the
    requirements of open space if they add more parking stalls. Councilor Koivisto stated that the
    PUD also requires that the open space be contiguous, and she interprets the open space on this
    site plan as remnants. Mr. Struthers stated that they have a diagonal of open space through the
    property. He said it comes down to the definition of contiguous. Councilor Koivisto stated
    that she feels this doesn’t meet the definition of contiguous. She said there are very strict
    requirements with a PUD and they need to be followed. Mr. Struthers stated that contiguous
    can be defined as near or next to.

    meet all required setbacks.

    Chris Bowerbank stated that there are different interpretations of the code and there needs to
    be a definition that everyone agrees on before they move forward with a vote. He suggested
    that the item be tabled until the terms are better defined.

    Rachel Rehart stated that she was also concerned they are not meeting the setbacks on all the
    adjacent properties. She said they haven’t used this zone consistently and was concerned about
    the precedent being set and felt they should follow all the requirements of a PUD. She was
    concerned with the transition from the smaller homes to the larger lots. She was concerned
    about the choice of trees that may obscure the view and create a safety concern.

    Michelle Stallings stated that she was concerned with the types of trees and suggested they
    keep the landscaping to a minimum around the ingress and egress to make it more visible for
    safety. She asked that there be no parking near the entrances for safety. She also asked that
    they follow the development code for setbacks here.

    Mayor Johnson stated that he believes they follow the code. He said there may be different
    interpretations of the code, but they’ve done PUD’s and PRD’s for a long time and he doesn’t
    believe they’ve been inconsistent in their interpretation. Mr. Wood stated that he hasn’t had a
    chance to look at the code sections. He clarified that a development agreement becomes the
    land use law that applies, so if there’s something that makes sense for the project that’s not in
    strict compliance with the code, then that is one avenue they could take. He also said that if
    there’s a section of the code that’s ambiguous, then they look at the intent of the code section
    and does the way it’s applied meet that intent.

    Councilor Koivisto stated that she doesn’t feel comfortable moving forward with the setbacks
    and the ambiguity of the language. She stated that a PUD is very strict, and she doesn’t want
    to set a precedent for not following the rules.

12. Consideration of Ordinance #01-2020, the Innovation Pointe General Plan Amendment,
    changing 16-acres located at approximately 1441 West Innovation Way from Business
    Park to Commercial.
    Kent England was representing the applicant. He stated that they are changing 40 acres, not
    16. He said the Commercial zone fits better here as described in the submitted report. He stated
    that this is the direction from staff. Mayor Johnson explained that originally this was planned
    for just business, but as the city has grown, he believes it’s better to allow this area to have
    both business and commercial. He stated that allowing business only creates issues with traffic
    because the peak times are all the same, but with the Commercial zone, they can also add retail
    which could offer restaurants for people to walk to and fluctuates the peak times. Mr. Struthers
    stated that Commercial provides a better fit for what they’d like to see in that area and allows
    for more mixed uses which can reduce traffic. Mr. England stated that they want to add office
    buildings, but they do not have a use for the property on the east side. Councilor Condie stated
    that the Commercial zone allows the area to be more vibrant rather then just office space.

    Mr. England stated that they plan to build an office building north of the elementary school
    and then the Commercial zone would allow them more flexibility for the other properties.
