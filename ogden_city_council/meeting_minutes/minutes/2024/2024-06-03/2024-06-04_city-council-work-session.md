Agenda Review
        Chair Richey and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Ogden Pioneer Stadium Sponsorship Naming Agreement
         Parks and Cemetery Manager Stewart presented a proposal for a Sponsorship Naming Agreement with the George S. and
Dolores Doré Eccles Foundation. Under the terms of the Sponsorship Naming Agreement, the Foundation will recognize the
recent contribution of $800,000 to construct improvements to rodeo grounds as well as the millions of dollars donated over the
course of more than forty years—since 1982—to fund the Ogden Pioneer Days rodeo and improvements to the stadium. In return,
the City will grant naming rights for the stadium to the Foundation for a term of 75 years or until such time as the stadium is no
longer used as a public stadium space.
         Discussion among the Council and Mr. Stewart centered on the terms of the naming agreement, timing of future
improvement projects at Ogden Pioneer Stadium, and historical improvement projects at the stadium.

Fiscal Year 2023-2024 Third Quarter Financial Report
          Comptroller Sorensen used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the Fiscal Year 2023-2024 Third Quarter
Financial Report. The Report provides information about the City’s financial position as of March 31, 2024. The Comptroller’s
office is estimating that tax receipts will be at or slightly below budget due to the flattening of sales tax revenues. Franchise tax
revenues are exceeding estimates, which will offset most of the reduced sales tax revenue. Departmental expenditures are tracking
at or below 75 percent for each departmental budget. This indicates that expenditures are in line with projected budget
expenditures for the year. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          There was discussion among the Council and staff regarding specific expenditures and revenues identified in the report.

Fiscal Year 2023-2024 Third Quarter Capital Improvement Plan Report
          Comptroller Sorensen used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the quarterly Capital Improvement Plan (CIP)

Proposed Ogden City Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget
          Fire Fees: Fire Marshal Brown explained the City requires permits and inspections for private fire protection systems.
Inspections of these systems are tracked and performed by a third party. Ogden Municipal Code 9-2-8 currently states that the fee
for a third-party inspection may not exceed $12 per inspection. The Fire Department has proposed to increase the fee from $12 to
$15 per inspection. The $12 fee has not been increased since the ordinance was last amended in April of 2017. The Fire
Department is also proposing to add language to the ordinance that allows the fee to be increased annually based on consumer
price index. Other fees that have been recently amended to include the annual increase based on consumer price index are water
hookup and refuse collection fees.
          General Fiscal Year 2025-2029 Capital Improvement Plan Discussion: Council Deputy Director Symes explained the
current proposal is to have the Council review the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2025-2029 Capital Improvement Plan (CIP), make
any changes the Council sees as necessary and approve the plan along with the final FY2025 annual budget. He cited a list of CIP
projects proposed for FY2025 funding by Department and fund, concluding that FY2025 funding totals $117,324,400. He noted
there is an action item on tonight’s meeting agenda to set a public hearing for June 18 to take final action on the CIP.
          Council staff and representatives of the Comptroller’s Office engaged in discussion regarding the prioritization and
ranking of projects included in the proposed CIP.
          General Fiscal Year 2025 Budget Discussion: Council staff facilitated discussion among the Council regarding the
tentative Fiscal Year (FY) 2025 budget. There was a focus on compensation packages for City employees, including
increased benefit costs; changes in staffing models for Departments that utilize seasonal, part-time employees; and viability
of utilizing volunteers in the Fire Department.
