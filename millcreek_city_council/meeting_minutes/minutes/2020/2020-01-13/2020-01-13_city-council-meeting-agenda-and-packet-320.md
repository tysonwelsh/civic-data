1.4 State of the City Address; Mayor Jeff Silvestrini

Mayor Silvestrini said the City started with nothing 3 years ago and the Council met
in the Mt. Olympus Improvement District with no staff or money. He said John
Brems volunteered to be the City Attorney and Leslie Silvestrini was the City
Recorder until one was hired. He thanked John Geilmann and city staff for City Hall.
He said today the budget was $25.5 million and was balanced. The Council had
fulfilled campaign promises of leaving the Municipal Services District and Salt Lake
Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA). The Council contracted with
Unified Police Department (UPD) and increased Millcreek’s police force by 6
officers without a tax increase to residents. The Council had updated ordinances,
acquired open space on Grandeur Peak, and obtained an AA+ bond rating to bond for
park space. The Council had implemented measures for sustainability with the City’s
fuel efficient fleet, installing electric car charging stations at City Hall, providing
incentives for green building for future planning, requiring solar panels for buildings
in the city center, were exploring options with 2019 H.B. 411, and ran a rain barrel
program for residents. The Council had increased Millcreek’s profile on boards and
commissions in the State, including the Wasatch Front Regional Council, Council of
Governments, Central Wasatch Commission, and the Utah League of Cities and
Towns.

He announced that the City was working on a cultural facilities inventory from which
a master plan would emerge to promote arts and culture in Millcreek and was
working on an active transportation plan with other cities in the Salt Lake Valley.
Millcreek was recognized by the University of Utah’s David Eccles School of
Business for ethical leadership. The Council had good public engagement on the

property tax shift for law enforcement services and listened to the public on the blight
study. Millcreek started a business council for local businesses and the Promise
Program. The Promise Program helps to lift up those less fortunate, ensure children
have the opportunity to attend college or trade school, builds community, and bridges
divides. The Council has been fiscally responsible without a tax increase and operated

The Mayor said that while he was campaigning, he learned that residents were happy
with what the Council had been doing. Residents had expressed concern about air
quality and sustainability. They wanted more open space and liked the idea of
economic rejuvenation and a city center. They would like to see housing affordability,
quality growth, infrastructure to be improved and maintained, a transparent and open
government, and they wanted to be heard and involved. The Mayor said he heard
rumors while campaigning. The first was that the City Center was about building a
new city hall. He said the City’s current City Hall space was under a lease agreement
for another 7 years though the landlord could terminate the lease to redevelop the
property. The purpose of the City Center was about addressing housing and
rejuvenating economic development. There had not been a conversation about a city
hall yet, but when there is one, he promised that there would be public engagement on
it. Another rumor he heard was that the City had too much debt. Millcreek has a loan
for borrowing that was take over from what SLVLESA was doing to pay for police
services. The borrowing occurs twice a year and it is paid off every year as a
revolving line of credit, but Millcreek has reduced the borrowing by almost $3
million from what SLVLESA borrowed. The debt is a $20 million bond for the city
center open space property acquisition and construction. Millcreek has the second
lowest debt per capita of any city in Salt Lake County. The debt will be repaid by the
additional taxes generated in the city center area when it is redeveloped, not out of
residents’ pocketbooks. He said he heard that the City had condemned property for
the City Center. Millcreek does have the power for eminent domain for park space
but has not used it; every property acquired had been through a negotiated transaction
based on an appraised value. Staff had engaged in significant efforts to relocate
businesses in the city center. He was proud that Plowgian Auto was relocated to
Holladay in a location twice as big as they had before. He said if the residents wanted
more park space, all the City could do was buy it.

He pledged that the Council would be even more collaborative, transparent and open
during his term in office. He said he hosts Meetings with the Mayor on Friday
afternoons by appointment for all residents. He said he would like to start doing town
hall meetings throughout the City.

The Council and staff were able to bring $24 million in grant funding to Millcreek.
With that, residents would see the reconstruction of 3900 S., three sections of
sidewalks completed on 3300 S. and a section on 700 E. by RedMaple Road; curb,
gutter, and bike lanes on Main Street from 3900 S. to the Murray boundary;
improvements to 900 E. from 3900 S. to 4500 S.; and Mt. Olympus would lose the
“Jupiter Jump.” He said there was still a big problem in Millcreek; the storm sewers.
When the piping rusts out and collapses, it creates an immediate emergency that

from input from focus groups and prior meetings with residents. Mayor Silvestrini said a

Forestry Standard which says what kind of tree is to be planted where in the City Center
and it would include maintenance standards. Staff was consulting with a landscape
architect team to include a tree expert.

2.1 Public Hearing on Opening and Amending the Fiscal Year 2019-20 General
Fund Budget and the Fiscal Year 2019-20 Capital Improvement Project Budget
Laurie Johnson said the second amendment included an increase in the General Fund
budget by $362,688 and to the Capital Improvement Fund budget by $3,723,597. The
changes in revenue reflected an increase of $250,000 in mass transit tax, $60,000 in
engineering permits, $150,000 in Class C Road funds, $20,000 in park impact fees, and
$6,953 in Promise Program donations. She showed the Council an updated staff
organizational chart.

Johnson went through the General Fund expense changes. The Legal Services budget
increased by $5,000 to cover professional services expenses. The City Recorder budget
decreased by $20,000 due to lower than anticipated election costs. The Building Services
budget increased due to code compliance inspector positions added to the department
along with applicable expenses and increased contracted building inspection costs. The
Communications and Programs budget increased by $39,000, however personnel costs
were decreased due to a position change and lower printing costs. The “professional
services” line item increased for the Cultural Facilities Master Plan and the “events” line
item increased to cover costs not fully paid in the previous fiscal year. The Emergency
Management budget increased by $10,000 to cover additional benefit expenses. The
Information Center budget decreased by $20,000 due to lower personnel costs. The
Economic Development budget increased to cover additional personnel expenses relative
to changing a previous code compliance position to a field officer. The Business License
Administration budget increased to cover additional personnel expenses. The Finance
Department budget increased by $28,500 to cover additional personnel and professional
services expenses. The Non-Departmental budget increased by $91,236 to cover
additional banking charges and additional fund balance. The Legislative Policy budget
decreased as code compliance personnel moved to another department, but the IT/Facility
Manager and Legislative Policy Analyst positions were added. The Facilities budget
decreased due to various line items being realigned. The Planning and Zoning budget
increased to cover adding a Planner II position (formerly Planner I) and additional
professional services expenses. The Promise Program increased in the amount of
donations received. Mayor Silvestrini pointed out that the Promise Program Manager had
been able to obtain a lot of grant funding for improving Sunnyvale Park. The Public
Works budget decreased by $124,000 but personnel expenses were increased due to the
addition of a GIS Technician. The contracted services line item decreased as expenses
were offset by increased Class C Road fund projections. The Class B & C Road budget
increased in the same amount of projected Class C Road funds. Johnson said at the end of
FY 2019 the General Fund budget reserve was at 18.91% and the projected balance for
the end of FY 2020 was 17.77%.

Johnson said the Capital Improvement Project (CIP) revenue budget increased
dramatically. She said $100,000 of grant match funds were from development
agreements to go towards the Mountair project and $70,000 was reimbursement for the
CATNIP grant. The “use of fund balance” line item was anything that had not been spent
during the previous fiscal year that gets carried forward to the current fiscal year, so $3.5
million would be available to be used for other projects. Council Member Marchant said
in 2019, the interest earnings were $185,000 more than what was received in 2018.
Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek moved excess funds into the CIP budget to avoid

3. Business Matters
3.1 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 20-01, Approving an Interlocal
Cooperative Agreement with Salt Lake County with Respect to Oblique Imagery
(Aerial Photography)
Mayor Silvestrini said the interlocal agreement was a contract with the County
Surveyor’s Office for drone photography and aerial imagery of the City. John Miller said
the agreement was an opportunity to get imagery at 3-inch accuracy that could be used
for preliminary conceptual design at a minimal price ($3,000) and was a value for what
would be received.

Mayor Silvestrini asked for public comment. There was no comment.

Council Member Jackson moved to adopt Resolution 20-01, Approving an Interlocal
Cooperative Agreement with Salt Lake County with Respect to Oblique Imagery. Council
Member Catten seconded. Council Member Catten asked if $3,000 was the total price. John
Miller confirmed and said it was the price for the whole city. The Recorder called for the vote.
All Council Members voted yes. The motion passed unanimously.
