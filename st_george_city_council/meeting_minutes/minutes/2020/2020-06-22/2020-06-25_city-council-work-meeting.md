OPENING:
     Mayor Pike called the meeting to order and welcomed all in attendance. The invocation

DISCUSSION REGARDING COMMERCIAL DEVELOPMENT IN SUNRIVER:
     Darcy Stewart provided a map of various commercial development parcels in Sun
     River. He asked the Council for their suggestions. He showed and outlined a map
     detailing the various commercial development parcels. There is a C-2 zone, 7-acre
     parcel to the north that will potentially be an RV storage;

      Mayor Pike reiterated what Mr. Stewart stated: 1) there is a C-2 zoned, 7-acre parcel
      to the north for a potential RV storage; 2) potential for RV sales and service center on
      the north end of the C-2 zoned, 23-acre parcel (near the freeway); and 3) residential-
      like apartments on any C-2 zoned property.

      Community Development Director John Willis commented the overall master plan
      outlines this area as community commercial – commercial for neighborhood, day to
      day, uses. The master plan refers back to the City code for specific uses. RV sales
      and service does fall under the auto/car sales sections in the ordinance listing specific
      streets this is allowed on. If a request involves modifying the C-2 zone, it would apply
      to the entire City.

      Mr. Stewart stated that one of the discussions was regarding the uses they envisioned
      20 years ago. He asked if the Atkinville PD can be abandoned and rezone on a case
      by case basis.

      Mr. Willis noted the benefit of the master plan is that the entire area was looked at,
      not each parcel individually.

      Councilmember Larkin stated it is challenging to work with something that is 20 years
      old. She doesn’t think it makes sense to adjust the C-2 zone; she would rather look
      at possible zone changes. Neighborhood grocery stores will not go to an area where
      there are not enough rooftops.

      After being asked by Councilmember Randall, Mayor Pike noted there are two storage
      unit facilities out that way.

      Mayor Pike noted he is concerned with the grade of the parcel. He wondered about
      the prominence of it with regard to the entrance to the City and those living in the
      Riverstone development. With regards to the current lighting in the area, he feels it is
      too bright.

      Mr. Stewart commented that the Mancaves and mini storage front Bluegrass were
      landscaped to look like buildings, not storage. He hopes to continue with the same
      façade.

      City Attorney Shawn Guzman spoke about putting an age restriction on the
      apartments. The developer can put add an age restriction if they want that type of
      community. He asked the Council if they want this to be a requirement and noted it
      would be hard to enforce. After being asked by Mayor Pike, he explained staff would
      have to look at the ordinance as it may have to change to allow the RV sales and
      service. There is not as much need for retail space in the area as was needed 20
      years ago.

      Mr. Stewart explained the age restriction is a federal guideline. Every two years, they
      have to submit information to the agency; they keep SunRiver very age restricted.
      They would be required to do the same with the apartments. Regarding the 11-acre
      parcel, he is not sure how many apartment units there will be as the conceptual plan
      is not done yet. He will return to a work meeting before he proceeds. He has heard
      that certain businesses were not aware of the extent of Desert Color; 9,000 rooftops
      are needed before they will consider coming to the area.

      After being asked by City Manager Adam Lenhard, Mr. Stewart stated that he has met
      with some neighborhood representatives; they spoke about what types of businesses
      they would like to see in the area.

      Mayor Pike welcomed comments from those attending.

      Resident Ron Brown asked if there any type of single-family homes with nursing or
      care in the facility, similar to Beehive Homes, being proposed. Mr. Stewart replied this
      would be a component after the apartment component.

      Bobbi Wan-Kier with the Arts to Zion, noted they missed the deadline for the RAP tax
      grant; however, they have since submitted an application and was told that they were
      included. She thanked the Council for their support.

      Councilmember Larkin explained the process for approving RAP tax funding. The Arts
      Commission met this morning to make recommendations.

      Mr. Brown noted most of the galleries are closed due to COVID.

DISCUSSION REGARDING THE ENERGY SERVICES RESTORATION PLAN:
     Energy Services Director Laurie Mangum presented a PowerPoint presentation
     covering the following topics: video of a new transmission line; Central to St. George
     transmission lines; identified critical locations; traffic signals; and capacity.

PRESENATION OF THE 2020 ANNUAL FRAUD RISK ASSESSMENT:
     Administrative Services Director Trevor Coombs pretend the 2020 annual fraud risk
     assessment, a new audit requirement. This assessment is something that the
     financed department does currently. The City has never received any complaints in a
     former manner. He provided a copy of the assessment and outlined the questionnaire
     that will be submitted.

REPORTS FROM MAYOR, COUNCILMEMBERS, AND CITY MANAGER:
     City Manager Adam Lenhard noted there is not time for a closed meeting; however, he
     will email the Council with a proposal regarding the sale/purchase of property.

RIBBON CUTTING AT BLACK HILL VIEW PARK:
     The Mayor and Council attended a ribbon cutting ceremony at the Black View Park.

RIBBON CUTTING FOR THE PICKLEBALL COURTS AT VERNON WORTHEN PARK
     The Mayor and Council attended a ribbon cutting ceremony at the pickleball courts at
     Vernon Worthen Park.
