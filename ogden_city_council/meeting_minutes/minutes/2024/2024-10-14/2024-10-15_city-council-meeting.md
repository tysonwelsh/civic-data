Proposed Resolution 2024-12 adopting a Citizen Participation Plan that
outlines how the City will engage and involve the public in the U.S. Housing
and Urban Development (HUD) Five-Year Consolidated Plan planning
process
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the Citizen Participation Plan for the
2026-2030 Five Year Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the
Administration is proposing the Council adopt a resolution that approves the Citizen Participation Plan that will set forth the
outreach and public participation efforts in conjunction with the development of the Five-year Consolidated Plan for fiscal years
2026- 2030.
         Community Development Director Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
summarize the proposed Citizen Participation Plan. Ogden City is a Housing and Urban Development (HUD) Entitlement City
and receives annually Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) and HOME Investments Partnership Grant funds. To
receive these grants, the City must comply with HUD regulations in developing a Citizen Participation Plan. The Citizen
Participation Plan includes:
              • Involving the public in the planning and reporting of CDBG and HOME Grants
              • Consultation with stakeholders
              • Outreach to citizens
              • Community and Economic Development’s Citizen Advisory Committee to review HUD Plans and make
                  recommendations to City Council and the Mayor
              • Public Hearings to Adopt HUD plans
              • 30-day public comment period to provide citizens and stakeholders an opportunity to review HUD plans and
                  comment prior to adoption
              • A notice in the paper announcing the 30-day public comment period
              • Consideration of all comments received regarding HUD plans
              • Procedures to follow to respond to citizen input
         The City has updated the previous Citizen Participation Plan to include the following new HUD requirements:
              • Additional outreach efforts to include alternative public involvement techniques (QR codes, surveys, use of
                  internet, focus groups, etc.)
              • The Citizen Participation Plan is to be adopted at a public hearing.
              • The Analysis of Impediments (AI) to Fair Housing Choice is to follow the same citizen participation plan
                  procedures as other HUD plans, which includes adoption of the AI at a public hearing.
         Mr. Smith concluded the Citizen Advisory Committee met and recommended approval of the Citizen Participation Plan
on September 5, 2024. The Citizen Participation Plan was reviewed along with the Annual Action Plan (AAP) Fiscal Year (FY)
2025 Amendment #1 and the introduction to the five-year Consolidated Plan at the City Council work session on September 10,
2024. As required by HUD, Community Development is submitting the Citizen Participation Plan for adoption by City Council.
         For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.

Proposed Ordinance 2024-28 prohibiting new multiple-family dwellings,
single-family rowhouses, and duplexes in the C-2/CP-2 and C-3/CP-3
zones
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed zone text amendments
limiting multiple-family dwellings in C-2/CP-2 and C-3/CP-3 zones came before the Council for consideration. The amendment
proposes to prohibit new multiple family dwellings, single-family rowhouses, and duplexes in the C-2/CP-2 and C-3/CP-3 zones.
The Planning Commission heard the proposal on July 3, 2024, and made a recommendation to approve the proposed amendment.
On May 28, 2024, the City issued a notice of pending ordinance to prevent new applications for multiple-family dwellings, single-
family rowhouses, and duplexes in the C-2/CP-2 and C-3/CP-3 zones from being submitted until the proposed ordinance
amendment was voted on. Utah Code (10-9a-509) allows a municipality to reject land use applications for 180 days, if the
municipality initiates pending legislation in a manner provided by local ordinance, and if the proposal would be prohibited under
the pending ordinance. The pending ordinance period of 180 days will expire on November 24, 2024.
          Planning Director Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
proposed text amendments; the presentation focused on Ogden’s housing goals and the Quality Neighborhood’s Initiative as the
basis for the proposal. There was information regarding the current housing stock in the community and household income levels,
as well as recent permitting trends (2020-2023) for different housing types: multiple-family housing permits represent 62 percent
of the total housing permits issued for that time period. Mr. Brierley displayed a map of downtown Ogden illustrating existing
multi-family zones and commercial zones, as well as other areas in close proximity to Ogden’s downtown that could potentially
be used as multi-family housing developments in the future. Mr. Brierley concluded the Planning Commission’s recommendation
is to adopt the proposed zone text amendments, with the understanding that this is the beginning of the conversation surrounding
housing needs of the community, the amendments will lead to possible zone changes, and the amendments could impact the
General Plan update project. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          There was a brief focus on a map provided by Planning staff to identify C-2/CP-2 and C-3/CP-3 zones in the City and
public transportation routes; Council and staff discussion centered on development opportunities for specific properties if the
proposed ordinance amendment were approved.

          Dan Schroeder stated he is currently serving on the Citizen Advisory Committee for the General Plan revision project. He
stated he hopes there is no one in the room who actually believes the City is in danger of running out of commercial space, that
there is plenty of room to build more housing in the City’s R-3, R-4, and R-5 zones, or that the City’s commercial corridors are
such awful places to live that no one would have a valid reason for voluntarily choosing to live there. Those arguments are
inconsistent with the facts. He stated he believes this proposed ordinance is rooted in a desire for more control over housing
developments; adding new restrictions and more difficult rezoning processes for a landowner to follow will drive up construction
and housing costs, which will also drag down the economy. It also sends a message that Ogden City is arbitrary and capricious in
its land use policies. He is concerned that this action would not be temporary in nature and would, instead, be a long-term decision
that would impact the issuance of building permits in the City for a number of years.
          Benjamin Zach discussed housing affordability in Ogden City and indicated that approval of the proposed ordinance will
further limit affordable housing, even if it is in a high-density housing development. He referenced recent housing projects that
have occurred in commercial areas and noted that many of them are actually walkable and beautiful. He also noted there are a
number of vacant parcels of land along Washington Boulevard that have not been used for commercial purposes; it is a fact that
there is not a shortage of commercial property in the community. He would hate to see all housing pushed to the side or limiting
all high-density housing to specific neighborhoods of the City. Allowing mixed-use development in commercial areas will create
more housing opportunities.
          Salvoy Miller stated he appreciates Mr. Brierley’s plan to meet the needs of residents living in high density housing
areas, but he did not hear a plan for dealing with vacant commercial properties that are vacant, dilapidated, and in serious
disrepair. He believes that lack of interest in these properties means they are not ideal commercial areas, and it would be more
appropriate to allow the highest and best use of those properties. If developers are interested in building a housing development or
mixed-use development on a property that has been vacant for years, it should probably be allowed because there is no
commercial market for the properties. He supports mixed use development that has a commercial element on the ground level and
residential above but requiring a parcel to be 10-acres in size in order for mixed-use zoning to be granted is unreasonable. He
recommended the Council vote to deny the ordinance amendment.
          Linda Sanchez also recommended the Council not adopt the ordinance; one of the complaints she most commonly hears
is that housing in the community is not affordable. The City should not be enacting regulations that further limit housing access,
especially for the homeless population or seniors. She is not opposed to senior apartments, but it is not proper to restrict the
development of a property to one that will only serve a certain age or demographic. She referenced a television documentary that
examined the development of apartment buildings in a very condensed areas; the apartments were eventually torn down because
the project was too dense and did not include sufficient green space. Many of the residents who lived there were forced to live on
the streets.

been working to get financing that would make the housing units on his property as affordable as possible. He has received a $4.1

The current proposal does not address affordable housing and delaying his project will only cause construction and housing prices
to increase even further. When the pending ordinance was enacted, it forced him to pause his project. He asked why the City
would put such a restriction on someone who already has the right to develop their property. His application has been delayed and
he believes the City has directly attacked affordable housing.
