         3.1.3     Adequate parking on this site is a concern. A recent site visit confirmed that areas designated for
                   landscaping were being used for vehicle parking – primarily within the 12-foot landscape area along
                   the western property line. Staff is concerned that approving the expansion of service operation
                   capacity may increase the number of vehicles on-site; however, the site appears to not have
                   adequate parking to service current capacity and operations. The applicant is proposing to remove
                   the existing landscape area along the western property line and replace it with additional asphalt and
                   parking; however, it is not confirmed that additional parking will absorb the increased demand by the
                   expansion. Staff recommends the Planning Commission consider these constraints and ask the
                   applicant to provide more details regarding the proposed site layout and expansion and how current
                   and future parking demand will be met.

         3.1.4     Regarding landscaping, Chapter 13.25 includes required landscaping standards for commercial
                   properties. There are two seemingly conflicting standards in this section that require staff
                   interpretation. Section 13.25.020(B)(2) says, “Areas not visible from the street shall have one
                   landscape area in the amount of one hundred square feet for every seventy-five linear feet of
                   property line not visible from the public right of way. Section 13.25.020 (E ) says, “Adjacent to
                   Residential Districts: A minimum ten foot (10’) width of landscaping shall be provided on an
                   applicant’s property, including a combination of trees and shrubs (evergreen and deciduous), and
                   ground covers shall be provided to create a buffer for the adjacent residential district.” While one
                   standard allows landscaping to be consolidated and relocated to more visible areas of the site,
                   another standard requires a minimum 10-foot landscape buffer between commercial uses and
                   adjacent residential zoning districts. The City’s legal department’s opinion is that because there is an
                   existing 8’ high block wall around the adjacent perimeter, the added 10’ of buffering would be of no
                   benefit because back residents would not see it. 1:30:34 Therefore, Staff has deferred to Section
                   13.25.020(B)(2) as previously noted, which says, “Areas not visible from the street shall have one
                   landscape area in the amount of one hundred (100) square feet for every seventy five (75) linear
                   feet of property line not visible from the public right of way.” The Applicant is proposing to install
                   more landscaping in the middle of the site and elsewhere. Also, any trees displaced during this
                   renovation would be moved to other areas on the site. Staff still has a concern with the amount of
                   parking on site in that there is a high demand for parking and wanted to know if the expanded
                   operation would need more parking. 1:30:42

      4.           2G20 - Recommendation to the City Council for a General Plan Map Amendment for the Property
                   Located at 4202 South Riverboat Road from “Business Park” to “Medium-Density Mixed-Use”. Jim
                   Spung/AICP/Senior Planner

     4.1 Mr. Spung presented this item. Ivory Development is requesting to change the proposed land use map
         designation in the City’s General Plan for the property at 4202 S. Riverboat Road from “Business Park” to
         “Medium-Density Mixed-Use.” The applicant is seeking this General Plan Map amendment with the intention
         to develop 142 townhome units, two commercial buildings each with a 20,000 square foot footprint, a
         fitness/daycare center, a playground, and a multi-purpose field.

           4.1.1     This item was presented to the Planning Commission on May 12, 2020 and was tabled with the
                     following motion, “I move that we table File 2G20 until we get further information regarding the
                     small area studies that have either been proposed to City Council or intended to be completed in the
                     near future and that we would strongly encourage the applicant to continue to have discussions with
                     Staff and to advance some of their thoughts and processes based on tonight’s discussion.”

           4.1.2     The subject property is within one of several designated master plan study areas currently underway
                     as part of a larger General Plan update effort. The City has contracted with a firm to lead a master
