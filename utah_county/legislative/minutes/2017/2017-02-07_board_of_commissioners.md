(Track A, 09:18)
Lindon resident and retired Utah County Commissioner Larry A. Ellertson explained the policy of Utah Retirement
Systems (URS) requiring him to resign from all positions effective December 31, 2016, at the end of his term as
commissioner. Mr. Ellertson expected to be re-appointed at the end of a 60-day window to comply with URS, but
his name is not included in this proposed resolution of nominated individuals for the Board of Trustees of the
Central Utah Water Conservancy District (CUWCD). He asked the Board to reconsider their list of nominations, as
the Utah State Governor generally allows two or more terms for those serving well on the CUWCD; Mr. Ellertson
has yet to complete his first term.

Utah County Civil Division Chief Deputy Attorney David Shawcroft and Commissioner Ivie discussed appointment
recommendations. When Commissioner Ivie stated there is currently no elected official serving on the CUWCD,
Larry Ellertson corrected that current Salem City Mayor Randy Brailsford is on the Board. The commissioners and
Mr. Shawcroft questioned whether Mr. Ellertson would be risking his retirement benefits if they recommended his
appointment to the Board. Mr. Ellertson reviewed the process of nominating an individual, including approval from
the governor and the senate, pointing out it is unlikely that would all occur before his 60-day window ends on March
3, 2017. Commissioner Ivie also commented on a proposed senate bill addressing taxing entities and suggested it
would be in the best interest of the county to have a current commissioner on the CUWCD. Mr. Ellertson repeated
Mayor Brailsford is an elected official residing in Utah County. Commissioner Ivie responded he would support
Mr. Ellertson being included in the next round of nominations, to occur in January 2018.

            Commissioner Ivie made the motion to adopt the resolution nominating
            individuals to be presented to the Utah State Governor for appointment to the
            Board of Trustees of the Central Utah Water Conservancy District as detailed
            in Consent Agenda Item No. 12. The motion was seconded by Commissioner

                                         PUBLIC COMMENTS
(Track A, 27:20)
Lindon resident and retired Utah County Commissioner Larry A. Ellertson stated he is very disappointed in the
Board’s decision on Consent Agenda Item No. 12, regarding nominations to the Board of Trustees of the Central
Utah Water Conservancy District. Mr. Ellertson expressed the decision was made before this meeting took place,
which is acting oppositely of the need for transparency which is often preached by the commissioners. Mr. Ellertson
stated their reasoning to not include him in the recommendation to the Utah State Governor is not well-founded, and
he would have appreciated the opportunity to continue serving in that capacity. Commissioner Lee responded that
the Board does discuss board appointments during closed sessions, but no actions are taken during those meetings.
UTAH COUNTY BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS

(Track B, 00:01)
Utah County resident Mark Allen stated he applauds this Board’s efforts to allow new people to serve the
community, and applauds their transparency. Referring to the retired Commissioner Ellertson, Mr. Allen stated he
has been unsuccessful for two years in gaining transparency from the Board. Mr. Allen presented his points of
concern for the commissioners to address, including the need for updated maps in American Fork Canyon and to
correct the water and soil quality problems in Tibble Fork. He spoke of the impacts of sediment in the communities,
stating the issue is a “ticking time bomb” and the Utah Division of Water Quality is not interested in protecting
public health. Mr. Allen expressed concern in re-labeling Critical Environment Zones, which are intended to
preserve watersheds. He also believes citizen input on county decisions are limited because of the Commission
meeting times and the lack of cameras in the Commission chambers; Mr. Allen would like this to be fixed. Lastly,
he spoke again of water and soil quality related to the Yankee Mine and repeated his concerns that the stated
Division of Water Quality will not help the people.
