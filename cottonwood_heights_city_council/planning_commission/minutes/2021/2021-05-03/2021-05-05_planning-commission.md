27          1.2     (Project ZTA-19-001)   Sensitive Lands Evaluation and Development
28                  Standards (SLEDS) Ordinance - Senior Planner Andrew Hulka.
29
30   Mr. Hulka reviewed the Sensitive Lands Evaluation and Development Standards (“SLEDS”)
31   Ordinance and stated that it is primarily a district in the City that designates areas that contain
32   natural hazards. He reported that sensitive lands require additional engineering reports and
33   approvals from the City Engineer or Geologist depending on the hazard. Benefits include
34   protections that preserve the environment, wildlife areas, and natural beauty. A map detailing land
35   hazards was displayed. He explained that the SLEDS Chapter of the Ordinance is highly technical
36   and no specific recommendations have yet been prepared. It was staff’s intent to make the
37   Commission aware of what is being considered and receive feedback.
38
39   Community and Economic Development Director, Michael Johnson, reported that the amendment
40   process began over one year ago when they contracted with Geostrata Geotechnical Engineering.
41   The intent of the amendment is to ensure that all recommendations, requirements, and references
42   to State and National standards are current. Data is continuously changing and staff wanted to
43   make sure that the Ordinance references the most up-to-date information.
44
45   Mr. Hulka stated that the standards contained in the zoning ordinance apply to all lands within the
46   designated Sensitive Lands District. They may also apply to areas outside of the district if the



     Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 05/05/2021                                        2
 1   Director determines there is some sort of hazard present. The current Sensitive Land maps were
 2   referenced. Mr. Johnson confirmed that areas with increasing slope severity are required to
 3   complete a Slope Stability Study prior to the issuance of a Building Permit. Areas that have been
 4   identified as having possible liquefaction were identified. Mr. Hulka stated that additional hazards
 5   include rock falls, flood plains, and shallow water.
 6
 7   Mr. Hulka presented an illustration depicting how a review takes place. He explained that the
 8   process can occur as part of the rezone of a property or as part of a Building Permit review or site
 9   plan review depending on the project, application, scope, and hazards. The process begins with a
10   submission of an application and a meeting with the Design Review Committee (“DRC”)
11   consisting of representatives from the City’s Engineering Department, Public Works, Unified Fire
12   Authority (“UFA”), Planning and Zoning, and other entities depending on the hazards that are
13   present on the site. Mr. Hulka explained that they identify all of the hazards and work required to
14   review and analyze those hazards. They then conduct an extensive review process. If approved
15   and all items are addressed, the Hazard Report moves onto the final review.
16
17   Mr. Hulka stated that additional sections of the Code are more policy-related and are located in
18   the Development Standards and Controls. The Ordinance specifies that slope areas in excess of
19   30% may not be developed and density calculations are detailed. Frequent questions were
20   reviewed. Mr. Johnson explained that standards in the single-family section specify that every lot
21   must have at least 3,500 square feet of buildable area and be an area with slopes that are 30% or
22   less, contiguous, and minimum dimensions of 50 feet. The lot must be designed to be located
23   within 250 feet of a public street. He indicated that impervious standards are stricter in the
24   Sensitive Lands area with a maximum allowable coverage of 30% that includes roofs and private
25   driveways.
26
27   Cut and fill slopes were next addressed. Mr. Hulka noted that the Ordinance requires the hillside
28   not be cut into or a slope filled that exceeds 12 feet and a slope ratio of 2-to-1. Mr. Hulka explained
29   that the Sensitive Land area contains multiple street requirements. Cul-de-sacs may not be longer
30   than 600 feet, streets must be designed in consideration with the hillside, and trail access to public
31   lands must be provided. The entire development cannot include the impervious streets to cover
32   more than 20% of the entire site with a maximum grade of 12%. He reported that there is new
33   legislation expected to be enacted this year that will prohibit local governments from regulating
34   items like exterior color, exterior materials, roof structures, residential permits, and may affect
35   enforcement moving forward.
36
37   Mr. Hulka explained that the Riparian Corridor and Water Shed Protection Ordinance referenced
38   in the Sensitive Lands Chapter states that the protection requirements are located in Chapter 17.31
39   of the Ordinance. However, they do not exist and were not previously included. Staff was
40   researching the original intent for work on a new ordinance and emphasized the importance of
41   areas near creeks.
42
43   Commissioner Wilde asked where the ordinance originated and suggested that Chapter 17.31 be
44   replicated. Mr. Hulka confirmed that the ordinance came from Salt Lake County’s Ordinance for
45   the Foothill Canyon Overlay (“FCA”).

40          3.1       (Project SPL-21-001) A public hearing to receive comments and take
41                    possible action on a request from Savory Swig Stores, LLC for site plan
42                    approval of a new drive-thru soda shop (Swig) at 2029 E. Fort Union Blvd.

44   Associate City Planner, Samantha DeSeelhorst presented the staff report and stated that the
45   request is for a site plan for a new drive-thru soda shop, Swig Soda, to be located at 2029 East
46   Fort Union Boulevard in the Dan’s Market parking lot. A map of the property was displayed.



     Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 05/05/2021                                        5
 1   The site history included several establishments. The property is located in the Regional
 2   Commercial Zone (CR Zone) where a drink shop is a permitted use. The zone requires a Master
 3   Development Plan to be reviewed and approved by the Planning Commission. Ms. DeSeelhorst
 4   reported that the use also falls in the Gateway Overlay Zone, which requires a Certificate of
 5   Design Compliance from the Architectural Review Commission (“ARC”). The request was
 6   reviewed by the ARC at its April 22, 2021, meeting. Changes were recommended with respect
 7   to the building architecture. The ARC will rereview the updated elevations at their next meeting.
 8
 9   Ms. DeSeelhorst reported that a Traffic Study was conducted and reviewed by the City Engineer.
10   The study recommended realigning the access to the site from Fort Union Boulevard and is
11   aligned with Ponderosa Drive. The Traffic Study also included an analysis of four existing Swig
12   locations in Salt Lake County. It was determined that the average peak drive-thru queue is 13
13   vehicles with that highest observed at 16. The site plan was designed with a curbed queue to
14   accommodate 13 vehicles with an overflow for up to 16.
15
16   Parking was next discussed. City Ordinance requires six to seven stalls for the proposed use and
17   the plan includes 30 available stalls, which are shared with Dan’s Fresh Market.
18   Ms. DeSeelhorst reported that the stalls are not generally used and may be the result of COVID-
19   19. More use was expected to be seen as restrictions are lifted. She was confident, and it was
20   staff’s recommendation, that with the 30 plus stalls, the six to seven required could be
21   accommodated even with increased use. Staff received verification of the property owner’s
22   consent for the application.
23
24   Ms. DeSeelhorst reviewed the Landscape Plan and stated that the applicant has proposed to
25   remove three existing site trees, add three new site trees, and plant a variety of shrubs and plant
26   material. The CR Zone requires the front yard of commercial properties to be landscaped and
27   maintained with live plant materials. Ms. DeSeelhorst explained that the ARC reviewed the
28   plan and had no requested changes. The development standard setbacks were met in full and
29   the building height was well below the 35-foot maximum. The lighting did not comply with
30   signage lighting and exceeds the Outdoor Lighting Ordinance requiring no more than 10 foot
31   candles at any point on the site to be illuminated. Ms. DeSeelhorst indicated that the menu board
32   exceeds that requirement with 71.5-foot candles and the applicant is working through a revision
33   to bring that into compliance.
34
35   The dumpster was also not in compliance and is located 34 feet away from residential. It was
36   noted that the requirement is 50 feet. She confirmed that the rooftop equipment is located within
37   a steel enclosure as required. The project was found to meet the development standards with the
38   exception of the foot candles and dumpster enclosure. Those technical corrections would be
39   resolved administratively prior to the issuance of the Building Permit. Staff will also confirm
40   the issuance of a Certificate of Design Compliance from the ARC as well as the existence of a
41   Shared Parking Agreement with Dan’s Fresh Market.
42
43   Two public comments received prior to the meeting were provided to the Commission. Staff
44   recommended approval.
