Proposed Resolution 2021-8 scheduling a public hearing to allow public
input regarding proposed transfer from the City's Utility Enterprise Funds to
the General Fund
          A memo from Council staff came before the Council to consider a resolution scheduling a public hearing to allow public
input regarding proposed transfers from Enterprise Funds to the General Fund. The memo stated as required by Utah State Code
§ 10-6-135.5, a city that is proposing to transfer funds from an enterprise fund to another fund for a purpose not directly related to
the goods or services provided by the enterprise fund must provide notice of the transfers to the users of the enterprise fund. The
city must also hold a public hearing on the transfers prior to adoption or the amendment of the City's budget. In Ogden, a
budgetary transfer from the City's Water, Sanitary Sewer, Storm Sewer, and Refuse Utility enterprise funds to the General Fund
has been done for many years; however, the requirement to provide notice of the transfers and to hold a separate public hearing is
a recent change to state law. These utility funds are accounted for as enterprise funds in Ogden and are operated with user fees
charged for goods and services. In the proposed fiscal year 2022 budget, the City is proposing a transfer from these utility funds
into the City's General Fund to act as the collection of franchise and property tax. In order to comply with state law requirements,
a public hearing on the proposed transfers is scheduled for June 22, 2021.

Fiscal Year 2022-2026 Capital Improvement Plan. Proposed Ordinance
2021-28 approving the Capital Improvement Plan for Ogden City for the
Fiscal Years 2022 to 2026
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed Capital Improvement
Plan (CIP) for Fiscal Years (FY) 2022-2026 came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the City's process for
reviewing the five-year CIP underwent significant changes in the last year. Previously, the CIP was reviewed months before the
budget process and rankings from both Administration and Council were used to inform the budget development process. With the
recent change, the CIP is now being reviewed alongside the budget making it easier for the Council and Administration to align
the plan for capital expenditures with the actual budget. The CIP will be reviewed in conjunction with the budget for the fiscal
year 2022 budget process. The CIP is reviewed and adopted each year as a planning tool for major capital investments in the
City's physical plant over a rolling five-year period. Each year's plan is updated based on the changing needs of the City, funding
opportunities, projects completed and other similar factors. During this year's budget process, proposed capital projects will be
reviewed alongside each department's proposed budget and in context with the overall needs and resources available to the City
through the budget process.

 Proposed Ordinance 2021-31 amending the Municipal Code to address
changes to annual inflation adjustments to City utility fees- Water, Sanitary
Sewer. Storm Sewer and Refuse
         A memo from the Management Services Department regarding amendments to the Consumer Price Index (CPI)
calculation came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated as a follow-up to previous discussions on the proposed
amendments to the CPI calculation, and as part of the overall review of the FY22 proposed budget for Public Services, the Council
reviewed the proposed blended rate CPI proposal at the June 1, 2021 work session. Representatives from Lewis Young Robertson
& Burningham, the City's fmancial advisors, as well as the City's Administration presented details on the proposed CPI
amendment. In addition, an update on the general state of the City's utilities was presented as part of the budget review. As
proposed, the annual consumer price index (CPI) increase built into the utility rates structure would be amended to include a
blended rate. This blended rate would use the establish CPI for the portion of the utility's budget roughly equal to operations and
maintenance costs and a construction cost index for the portion of the utility's budget roughly equal to the capital investment
costs. The blended CPI would apply to the Water, Sanitary Sewer and Storm Sewer utilities. Refuse would continue to use the
standard CPI as that utility has very few capital investments costs.

             Proposed Ordinance 2021-30 adopting the budget for Ogden City for the
             Fiscal Year July 1, 2021 to June 30, 2022
                      A memo from Council staff regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2022 Tentative Budget came before the Council for
             consideration. The memo stated each year during the first Council meeting in May, the Mayor is required to present a proposed
             budget for the upcoming fiscal year. The Council accepts the proposed budget for review and, through a series of work sessions,
             reviews the proposal over the next two months. A tentative budget is adopted after holding an initial public hearing. A second
             public hearing is held at the time of adoption of the fmal budget. During the budget hearings, the Council outlines their proposed
             changes to the budget and accepts public input. Once the fmal public hearing is closed, the Council makes its changes and adopts
             the fmal City budget. By State law, a budget must be adopted by June 30. An exception to the timing is allowed if the Council is
             considering a proposed property tax increase. At this time, Administration is not recommending a property tax increase and is
             asking the Council to adopt the Tentative Budget along with proposed Schedule A changes to the budget.

Update and review of communications plan for the proposed Honorary
Street Name Designation of 2nd Street from Century Drive to Wall Avenue
as "Bingham Ft-Chief Little Soldier Ln." extended from the June 1. 2021
meeting
          Council Deputy Director Symes explained Anna Keogh submitted a petition to assign an honorary street name for a
portion of 2nd Street between the Union Pacific railroad tracks and Washington Boulevard. The initially proposed honorary street
name was "Bingham Fort Lane" in honor of Bingham Fort, which was located in that area. The area included in the proposal was
shortened based on a recommendation from the Planning Commission to between Century Drive and Wall A venue. The proposal
was amended further to include the name of Chief Little Soldier. The alternative proposal was to assign the honorary name as
"Bingham Ft-ChiefLittle Soldier Ln." At the Council's May 25, 2021 meeting, a vote was taken on the proposal which resulted in
a tie vote of3-3. Since four Council members did not vote in favor of the proposal, the petition was denied. At the June 1, 2021
meeting, the Council voted to reconsider the item and further voted to extend consideration of the item to a later date. Discussions
with the Council on a desired review process will be discussed at the June 15, 2021 work session. Council Rules and Norms -
Extended Items Based on procedures in the Council's Rules and Norms, the Council voted to extend this item. An extension,
unlike a tabling, does not require a date certain for when the item will be brought back for consideration. The Council's Rules and
Norms do state, however, that Council Staff will provide an update and present a communications plan for the item at the
Council's next regular meeting. He noted that Council staff has discussed appropriate changes to the communications plan for the
item and has detennined to include a discussion item on the June 15 work session agenda to give the entire Council the ability to
discuss the manner in which they would like to proceed with the matter. He added the Diversity Commission has invited him to
provide them with an update regarding the matter at their meeting scheduled for tomorrow.
          Council member Hyer indicated Ms. Keogh would like to attend the Diversity Commission meeting as well and he asked
that Mr. Symes reach out to her to extend an invitation. Mr. Symes stated he will confer with Ms. Keogh and noted virtual
participation will be allowed in the Diversity Commission meeting and he will send instructions to the Council for participating if
they so choose.

Public Comments
          Angel Castillo referenced the work session meeting held earlier in the evening and thanked the Council for actively
engaging in discussion of the importance of water conservation. She understands it will be difficult to quickly develop a program
that can solve the City's water supply issues, but she understands the Council has communicated to Administration a directive that
residents not be fmed for brown lawns for the remainder of the growing season. Some people may fear receiving such a citation
from the City if they allow their landscaping to go donnant and it is important for the City to communicate that type of
enforcement action will not be taking place this fall.

Administration Comments
          Chief Administrative Officer Johnson indicated Administration has given direction to the Code Enforcement Division
that they not issue citations to residents whose lawns are not being watered, although residents are encouraged not to let their
lawns completely die. Administration is developing a communication piece that will provide residents with tips and advice for
keeping their lawns alive while also conserving water. Administration is also concerned about the current drought conditions, and
he noted that Pineview Reservoir is at just 52 percent of the normal level for this time of year; additionally, the temperature of the
water is at the highest it has been for this time of year, which means that it is susceptible to algae blooms. This is a dire situation
and the City is monitoring it closely. He then noted that while residents will not be cited for brown lawns, they are still at risk of
being cited if they allow weeds or other vegetation on their property to become too tall and unkempt. The City's Water Manager
will attend the next City Council work session to discuss the communications item with the group and discussion the manner in
which the infonnation will be communicated to residents.

Council member Comments
         Council member Stephens stated that along with sending information regarding water conservation to residents, the City
should also send information about fireworks restrictions. Mr. Johnson stated that the Fire Chief will attend the work session next
week as well to discuss fireworks restrictions; many residents have suggested that the City ban fireworks this year, but the City
does not have the legal authority to do so.
         The Council briefly discussed ongoing communication efforts regarding the drought conditions and measures for
conserving water, and opportunities for individual Council members to promote water conservation.
