    2. PUBLIC HEARING REGARDING A PROPOSAL TO AMEND WEBER COUNTY CODES §104-5, §104-6, §104-7,
       §104-8 & §104-25 TO REGULATE THE CULTIVATION & PROCESSING OF MEDICAL CANNABIS, AS REQUIRED
       BY STATE CODE & TO PROVIDE RELATED MINOR EDITS TO CLARIFY INTENT AND CONSISTENCY.

         Commissioner Jenkins noted that this is for hemp and the item is required by State Code. Charles Ewert, of the
         County Planning Division, noted that the State adopted regulations governing medical cannabis and requires
         the county to offer locations in certain zones where it may be cultivated and processed. If the county has a
         manufacturing zone and an agricultural zone, it has to allow cultivation/processing in at least one of each of
         those zones. The county has four manufacturing and four agricultural zones, the heaviest intensity of uses of
         each of these is the M-3 Zone, which primary use is for aerospace engineering and the A-3 is intended to be the
         heavier side of agriculture. These two were reviewed for the effect that allowing medical cannabis would have
         on them, but the Commission would have to decide whether it should be exclusive to these zones. Mr. Ewert
         was contacted by four individuals who wanted to know about allowing it in the M-1 Zone and to locate on
         Rulon White Blvd., but this zone was not looked at too closely. This proposal explicitly allows this to occur in
         the M-3 and A-3 zones.
         Mr. Ewert referred to State Code requirements including that medical cannabis cultivation is required to be
         completely grown inside in an enclosed facility that is manned by 24/7 security. It prohibits siting of a medical
         cannabis cultivation and processing facility within 500 feet of a residential zone. In Weber County, all of the
         agricultural zones allow residences, as do two of the manufacturing zones (including the M-1) and it is not clear
         whether the allowance of residences make them as much residential as they are agricultural or manufacturing.
         The proposed amendment plainly states that the A-1, A-2, and AV-3 zones are primarily agricultural and
         residential, and that the A-3 zone is primarily an agricultural zone. The Planning Commission wanted this in
         the A-3 Zone and with large buffers of at least 100 foot setbacks from any property line between these facilities,
         which are going to be industrial in nature, even if they just grow/dry there, and if there is a residence there or
         one locates nearby in the future, that a berm with trees or a masonry wall be installed. The 2003 West Central
         Weber General Plan recommends eliminating residential uses from the M-1 Zone.
         The State requires that if an operation is within a certain proximity of residences that there be scrubbers on their
         exhaust outputs to keep the smell down. The M Zones do not have this requirement. There isn’t too much
         concern currently in the A-3 Zone, which is very far out west and allows heavy uses. To allow medical cannabis
         processing in the M-1 Zone, the county would need to delete residential uses as being allowed. Commissioner
         Jenkins had visited several of these operations in Denver and found only one thing offensive—the smell, which
         is very, very heavy and instantly upon coming into the industrial parks that very heavy smell permeated the
         entire parks and the buildings. Thus, it is very important to him that the facilities be properly ventilated. Mr.
         Ewert said that this proposal does not suggest exhaust scrubbers in the manufacturing zone (only in the
         agricultural zones due to the residential use), but that he could add that language to the ordinance. He addressed
         Chair Froerer’s questions including that this is listed in the AV-3 Zone as a conditional use, that the county
         could apply those requirements to the M-1 Zone or some lighter version of it, and that the current ordinance
         could be arguable that medical marijuana growing operations could occur in any A or M Zone. Commissioner
         Harvey’s concerns relate to Mr. Stapley’s comments and Commissioner Jenkins’ concerns and wants to be very
         careful and to protect the existing customers in the industrial parks and the pertinent residents.
    3. PUBLIC HEARING REGARDING A PROPOSAL TO AMEND WEBER COUNTY CODE §106-2-4 TO ALLOW LOT
       AVERAGING TO OCCUR IN SUBDIVISIONS IN THE AV-3 ZONE, AND PROVIDE RELATED MINOR CLERICAL
       EDITS.

         Charles Ewert, of the County Planning Division, noted that lot averaging was added to the county’s code about
         1½ years ago and so far it appears successful in the A-1 and A-2 zoned subdivisions. The Planning Commission
         does not want to see the acreage reduced below one acre nor the width narrower than 100 feet. Chair Froerer
         noted that lot averaging is allowed in the lower valley but has not been allowed in the Upper Ogden Valley.
