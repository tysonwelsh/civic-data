a 9000 South Roadway Extension; 9000 South — 6400 West to U-111 (Bacchus Highway);

Preliminary Subdivision Plat to dedicate property (approximately 13.5 acres) for an
extension of 9000 South from 6400 West to U-111 (Bacchus Highway); Ben Seastrand,
Gardner Company (applicant) [#29953]

Cole Peterson, Gardner Group, was in attendance representing the applicant and was available to

answer questions.

Tayler Jensen clarified that the item was noticed as preliminary and final approvals, but staff is only

recommending preliminary approval at this time and staff approval for the final plat. The plat is 13.5

acres to be dedicated to West Jordan to allow for the extension of 9000 South from 6400 West to U-

111 highway. It is shown on the 2040 Master Transportation Plan and is anticipated to be transferred

to UDOT as an arterial road. The State has given money for the construction.

Based on the analysis and findings contained in the staff report, staff recommended that the Planning

Commission approves the Preliminary Subdivision Plat for the 9000 South Roadway Extension with

the conditions and requirements of approval listed.

I. Approval of the Subdivision is conditional on UDOT approval of a 134’ right-of-way.

2 The detention pond located at the northwest corner of 9000 South and 6400 West shall be

maintained by the Dry Creek Highlands HOA and / or the Jordan School District.

Matt Quinney asked why West Jordan is responsible to build the road when it is going to be transferred

to UDOT.

Tayler Jensen said West Jordan will take over other roadways from UDOT, so it will be a trade, The

road will be built to UDOT standards. He said there is a change to recommended condition #1 to

remove the reference to an exhibit that could limit options with the UDOT agreement. The new
condition reads, ‘Approval of the Subdivision is conditioned on UDOT approval of a 134’ right-of
way.”

There was a discussion regarding how the developer could be held accountable to condition #2. There

could be on a plat note or another type of document. West Jordan will only contribute to 5% of the

stormwater that will go into the pond, so the utility department did not want to take on the
responsibility of maintenance. This road is associated with Dry Creek Highlands development and the
condition will be noted there as well.

Kent Shelton asked if the interchange between New Bingham Highway and 9000 South will be treated

similarly to how it was treated at 7800 South near the airport.

Todd Johnson said it will be fairly similar.

Ammon Allen opened the public hearing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

MOTION: Jay Thomas moved to approve the Preliminary Subdivision Plat for the 9000
South Roadway Extension, approximately 13.5 acres located between 6400 West
and U-111 (Bacchus Highway) with the recommended conditions and
requirements of approval listed in the staff report including changes to the
conditions. The motion was seconded by Matt Quinney and passed 7-0 in favor.

3. Text Amendment — PRD and Residential Lot and Bulk Standards; Amend the 2009 West

Jordan Municipal Code Section 13-5C Planned Residential Development (PRD) zone, and
Code Section 13-5B-3 Residential Lot and Bulk Standards; City-wide applicability; City
of West Jordan (applicant)

Scott Langford said this item was directed by the City Council in March 2023 as a result of recent

developments, such as Welby West located in the PRD zone. Some council members were concerned

that the PRD zone was not as in alignment with the PC zone as it could be. One concern was the depth

of driveways and potential for larger vehicles to be an impediment to pedestrians if the vehicle were to

extend over the sidewalk. An ad hoc Council committee discussed the amendment, and a draft was

presented to the full Council on June 21, 2023. He listed the four points that the amendment focused
on. 1) The preamble to the PRD zone reduces the minimum acreage from 20 acres to 10 acres. 2) The

Council wanted to retain the discretionary language already in the code to have flexibility to approve a

plan with less than 10 acres. 3) To help alleviate the concern about driveway length, the front yard

setback was increased to 22 feet minimum. As that was discussed, they also looked at R-1 zoning
districts. Cul-de-sacs and knuckles currently have a 20-foot minimum setback. The Council supported

a 22-foot setback on those lots for consistency. Staff considered how that might affect the building

permit process, but the adjustment of two feet didn’t appear to cause much alarm. However, this

amendment could make it necessary for some home plans to be modified slightly. 4) There are other
amendments that bring the PRD into greater compliance with the PC zone.

Based on the analysis and findings contained in the staff report, staff recommended that the Planning

Commission forward a positive recommendation to the City Council for this application.

Trish Hatch asked why the Council asked for a reduction from 20 acres to 10 acres.

Scott Langford said as currently written, there is a very small window of where a PRD could be

applied. When the Welby West project was brought to the City Council there was some angst that it

was a significant deviation from the 20-acre minimum. This change will resolve some of the concerns
of using their discretion for a smaller project.

Duncan Murray said the City Council had the discretion already, but this will make it easier for them

to make the decision in the future without making an exception.

Matt Quinney asked if these new standards would have affected the Welby West development if it had

been in place at the time of approval, such as fewer lots.

Tayler Jensen said all of the setbacks in Welby West are 22 feet minimum.

Scott Langford said sometimes it is best to have a baseline starting point. Putting a number in the code

eliminates the need to negotiate, which is part of what was explained to him.

Ammon Allen opened the public hearing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

MOTION: Matt Quinney moved, based on the information and findings set forth in the staff
report and upon the evidence and explanations received today, to forward a
positive recommendation to the City Council for this application to Amend the
2009 West Jordan Municipal Code Section 13-5C Planned Residential
Development (PRD) zone, and Code Section 13-5B-3 Residential Lot and Bulk
Standards. The motion was seconded by Kent Shelton and passed 7-0 in favor.

4. Text Amendment — Daycares; Amend the 2009 West Jordan Municipal Code Section 13-
5F-2 to allow Day Cares in the M-1 Zone as a Permitted Use; City-wide applicability; City
of West Jordan (applicant)
Larry Gardner said this amendment was initiated by a majority of the City Council from the
Committee of the Whole meeting. The Council would like to see day cares in the M-1 zone. Day cares
are currently prohibited in the M-1 zone but are allowed in all other zoning categories. The definition
already exists and is not being amended. The amendment would allow them as a permitted use in the
M-1 zone.
Based on the analysis and findings contained in the staff report, staff recommended that the Planning
Commission forward a positive recommendation to the City Council for this application.
Ammon Allen asked what the practice was for other cities.
Larry Gardner said they are allowed in Salt Lake City, South Salt Lake, and Murray.
Ammon Allen opened the public hearing.
Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.
MOTION: Matt Quinney moved, based on the information and findings set forth in the staff
report and upon the evidence and explanations received today, to forward a
: positive recommendation to the City Council for this application to Amend the
2009 West Jordan Municipal Code Section 13-5F-2 to allow Day Cares in the M-1
Zone as a Permitted Use. The motion was seconded by Trish Hatch and passed 7-0
in favor.
MOTION: Jay Thomas moved to adjourn.
