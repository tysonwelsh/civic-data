Comeau will present Life Saver Awards to Ogden Fire employees Raymond

Communications Plan for Proposed Noticing Amendments. Update and
review the communications plan for the proposed noticing amendments
extended from the March 14, 2023, meeting. bt

A memo from Council staff explained a proposal was presented to the Council regarding changes to the newspaper
noticing requirements for certain land use related items. At the Council’s March 14, 2023 meeting, the Council voted to extend
consideration of the proposed amendments with direction that information regarding noticing costs and newspaper subscribership
and circulation be presented. As per the Council’s Rules and Norms, the item was extended. An extension, unlike a tabling, does
not require a date certain for when the item will be brought back for consideration. The Council’s Rules and Norms do require that
Council staff provide an update and present a communications plan for the item at the Council’s next regular meeting.

Council Deputy Director Symes summarized the communications plan that has been prepared by Council staff; some
information requested by the Council during their March 14 meeting can be easily accessed, but Council staff would like to take
two months to perform an audit of the City’s noticing process to track which notices are being published in the newspaper, the
cost of noticing, other locations that notices are being posted, and how quickly and consistently notices are being posted on the
State’s Utah Public Meeting Notice Website. Additionally, Planning staff will provide a sign-in sheet at Planning Commission
meetings to ask participants how they were notified of the agenda item they were interested in to determine how many people are
getting notices from the newspaper. Council staff anticipates being prepared to provide additional information in a few months
from now.

Heath Satow thanked the Council for listening to the input provided by citizens, even of some of the comments made
may sound accusatory or negative; the public cannot be expected to know all information, especially information that is discussed
in closed sessions, and that can cause frustration that leads to toxicity. Citizens can only affect change through elections, but for
the years during elections the public can only ask questions and provide input and hope they are heard. He addressed a few items
that were originally included on the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) agenda for this evening; there is a single company that is
slated to develop at least four major properties that Ogden City has pledged to them, and these projects could total upwards of a
half-billion dollars or more. This includes the development of Union Station, which is the touchstone of the community’s history.
Additionally, there were to be updates to the developer selection process tonight due to ongoing concerns about the transparency
of that process, but yesterday these items were removed from the agenda, likely due to the Board’s concerns to place so much of
the City’s future into a single basket. While he appreciates the care taken to address these situations, he feels itis prudenttotakea
step back and reassess the situation; he understands many projects are brought to the Board very late in the process and they have
little time to make decisions and that is why he has encouraged them to contact outside experts, such as Urban3, Weber County,
and commercial retailers who have financial interest in the projects. By doing so, the Board could gain new insight and possibly
avoid identifiable problems that may have been overlooked. As elected officials, the Board has a covenant with the citizens of
; Ogden to prioritize their wellbeing and fulfilling this promise requires a willingness to change course when new evidence comes
: > to light. It is a sign of strength, not weakness, to reassess and make adjustments when necessary. He asked the Board to take time
\ and seek out as much outside expert opinion as they can gather; doing so will help them to make the best possible decisions for the i
community and ensuring it continues to thrive and grow as the heart of Weber County. :
Travis Pate discussed historical actions in Ogden City associated with the creation of the Redevelopment Agency; he also .
discussed the importance of utilizing all options available to provide public notice of pertinent actions being considered by the
Council and the RDA. Just because something is allowed by Jaw, does not mean it should be done; the State of Utah has changed
noticing requirements, but that does not mean Ogden City should reduce its efforts to provide adequate public notice of important
actions. He has found historical notices regarding the Union Station when it was first dedicated as a museum, and if the City were
to changes its noticing processes now, such notices would no longer be provided and available to tell the history of the City in the :
future. There have been problems with notification in the past and the City Council needs to take ownership of those matters. He :
then added that requests for qualifications (RFQ) documents should only be published after a request for proposals (RFP); the City
is shoehorning inappropriate projects into neighborhoods in the City where they do not belong, and he encouraged the Council to
take a step back and evaluate accordingly.
Teresa Bramwell discussed what happened in a recent Planning Commission meeting when she was seeking permission
to rebuild her home at 25" Street and Monroe Boulevard; she was disturbed by some of the things that happened to long term
property owners who were seeking approval from the Planning Commission to simply use their property, including placing a
tenant in their commercial building or even acting as a tenant in their own commercial building. These people own one property, :
but they are being classified as developers and being required to meet an entire host of development requirements; one property
owner was required to tear up his parking lot and replace half of it with grass and install an irrigation system for the new grass.
This person simply wants to use his property to clean cars and sell them at a wholesale auction. He cannot believe the hoops that
individual property owners/taxpayers are being required to jump through in order to use their property. It broke her heart to see
that people are being forced into a classification and spending a great deal of money just to use their own property, yet true
developers are being allowed to take property that is owned by the City of Ogden and make millions of dollars on that property
while ignoring zoning laws. Developers are getting rights that individual property owners will never have and that is not right.
Laura Lewis referenced a note on her most recent water bill regarding the Wonder Block project; the messaging was
quite confusing and references ‘defense ecosystem’, but she could not find information about what that is. The only thing she
S could find online was a reference to defense contractors who help subsidize manufacturing and development. There is another
) quote about the developing bringing high paying jobs to the community, but from what she knows of the development, it includes
retail, office space, grocery, boutique hotel, and other service-oriented businesses, which typically provide low paying jobs and
she asked what will be included in the development that will entail high paying jobs.
Mike Adlay stated the federal government will meet tomorrow to make decisions about interest rates; taxable values for
real estate are based on one-year arrears of sales that occurred prior to the assessor’s evaluation. He asked the Council to be aware
that things will drastically change as real estate values are impacted by interest rates; as rates climb exponentially away from a
certain market, there is a very real negative impact on values. When interest rates were below three percent, property values were
unrealistic, and this is exactly what happened in 2008 before the market crashed. As rates climb above five percent, there will be a
negative impact on residential values, and this is what is causing many banks to be in default on loans they have made. He is .
starting to believe that something similar to the crash in 2010 will be happening soon.
Tereas Holmes discussed the history of the Union Station and efforts of many volunteers in the community to locate
museums in the facility in order to save it; she is concerned about the museums being removed from the Union Station at this time
and she hopes the City is committed to preserving them.
Angel Castillo referenced discussions during tonight’s work session meeting; the My Hometown Ogden group shared
great information about their cause, and she suggested that Ogden City follow their lead and share information about important
resources available to the community. She noted that there is an imminent threat of flooding in the community this spring and she
encouraged the City to begin preparing to respond and help residents respond to protect their own property. She also referenced
the Airport Community Reinvestment Area (CRA) and noted the State of Utah is evaluating how cities are using tax increment
financing (TIF) in these types of project areas. Ogden City has been listed on a webpage maintained by the State as having
provided no evidence that project area plans and objectives have been tracked. She is not saying that something has been done
wrong, but until the City’s ‘house is in order’ and the Council can verify that all reporting is being properly handled, the City
should pause future projects or extensions of existing project areas.

Council member Nadolski asked Community and Economic Development Director Cooper to answer Ms. Lewis’s
question about the ‘defense ecosystem’. Mr. Cooper noted that Ogden City has been home to a manufacturing base for the
aerospace and defense industries located at Hill Air Firce Base (HAFB). The ecosystem is a community of like-minded businesses
working together to achieve the same goal. The City’s role is largely based on initiatives and geographic location to HAFB and
there are private companies with high paying jobs that are interested in locating in the Wonder Block project.

Council Member White asked Mr. Cooper to also address the information communicated by Ms. Castillo. Mr. Cooper
stated the City met with auditors from the State of Utah over a two-week period and shared with them all the financial information
for current RDA areas; the auditor chose to hone in on the Hinckley Airport redevelopment area and the City disagrees with their
findings because the City is able to point to tax increment collection and spending for a building that is located adjacent to the
runway at the Airport. However, the City has spoken with the auditor and has learned things that can be done differently to ensure
that paperwork is in order. There is nothing out of order and he does not believe it is appropriate to pause Redevelopment Agency
activities.

Acting Chair Richey stated he was grateful to hear from My Hometown Ogden in tonight’s work session and encouraged
residents in the community to look into opportunities to serve with that entity.

Council member Blair stated that the Council was able to attend the recent Police Department and Fire Department
banquets and it was great to hear about the heroic efforts of the employees of those Departments. He appreciates the mentality of
the leaders of those Departments and their dedication to performing high quality service to residents; he was glad to hear from the
Ogden Regional Medical Center about the fact that they have noticed the efforts of first responders as well.

Council member Hyer stated that he was not at the Planning Commission meeting referenced by Ms. Bramwell, but he
has attended hundreds of Planning Commission meetings; as a Planning Commissioner he participated in the review and
development of land use code and community plans and the group weighs proposals very carefully and does not distinguish
between developers and single property owners for the purposes of applying land use regulations. It can be difficult to impose
regulations that will be costly and burdensome, but it is necessary to treat everyone the same; he would be interested to hear if
developers are not being required to abide by land use laws, but he suspects that is not the case.

Council member Nadolski stated that he would like to receive a copy of the audit referenced by Ms. Castillo and
addressed by Mr. Cooper. He understands that the audit considers best practices and not necessarily law, but it is important to be
as transparent as possible when considering future redevelopment projects in order to help to restore public trust that has been lost.
