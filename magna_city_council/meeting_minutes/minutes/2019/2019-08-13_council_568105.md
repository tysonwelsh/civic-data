    Possible amendments to the 8400 West project

                 Ms. Crystal Hulbert, Salt Lake County Public Works Engineering, reviewed the
proposed project, which entails putting in some wider medians and trees along 8400 West in an
effort to slow traffic. The wider median would be installed north of the railroad tracks all the way
to Elk Point. The Department of Transportation and the developer to the south of the railroad
tracks will also be installing a median there, which the Council does not have a say on. Also, a
Hawk light will be installed for pedestrians to cross 8400 West, and it has to be away from
intersections in order to meet best transportation practices. Amendment options for the Council

to consider are to 1) keep everything the same, 2) remove the median completely to allow left
turns, although UDOT is still not going to allow left turns, or 3) keep the median, but open it up for
emergency personnel.

              Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to open the
meeting for public comment on the 8400 West project. The motion passed unanimously.

                Jeff Gatzemeier, pharmacist and owner, Oquirrh Mountain Pharmacy, stated his
business is located in the Magna Medical Building at 3665 South 8400 West, and it will be affected
by this proposal, as will emergency vehicles trying to access the property. The Utah Department
of Transportation (UDOT) installed a porkchop there, so traffic coming from the north has to make
a U-turn into the parking lot. Because of the wider median, drivers will have to make the U-turn
500 feet down the road. Also, because of the crosswalk being moved further south, drivers will
have to have several interactions with that intersection and crosswalk. If the median was cut back
far enough to allow a U-turn on the north side, that would alleviate some of the problem. He was
also open to an access option through the north side of the parking lot. The most important issue
is making sure it is safe.

                Kim Sim stated her house is on the west side of the street directly across from the
medical building where all of the U-turns will be taking place. That will impact her being able to
get out of her driveway safely, particularly during rush hour, unless she turns right.

                 Heidi Jeffries stated she works at the medical office building and was also
concerned about the proposal. Ninety percent of patients and staff come from the north, so they
are going to hit that crosswalk twice. Then, when they make the U-turn, they will have to watch
for traffic in two different directions. Cutting the median back and allowing people to make a U-
turn closer to the parking lot is probably the safest bet for drivers as well as kids crossing the
street.

              (Inaudible) Higgins stated she works in the medical building and asked why the
porkchop had to be there. Other businesses along that road do not have one. She was also
concerned that putting in trees will affect visibility.

                Tristan (did not give a last name) stated her biggest concern was how the
residents living in the three large neighborhoods were going to get out of their neighborhoods in
the mornings or afternoons. There will be a lot of congestion, and there are already near accidents
now from people cutting into traffic.

              Dawnell Despain asked how long it would take to get another access. The
property where that could go has been vacant for years.

                Council Member Peel stated the development there has been delayed, but once
it is done, an access can be put in.

               Linda (inaudible) stated everyone has to come from the north now because the
Exodus Health Care area is being blocked. That is not safe. It would be difficult for an ambulance
to access the urgent care there. It is critical to get that access opened up.

             Council Member Hull, seconded by Council Member Prokopis, moved to close
public comment. The motion passed unanimously.

              Lisa Zundel, Traffic Operations Engineer, UDOT, addressed some of the
concerns as follows:

   •   When the developer of the property on which the medical building is located developed
       the property, the developer and owner acknowledged that the access would be right in
       right out only. UDOT is not considering removing the porkchop.
   •   Research shows that this type of crosswalk is much safer for people crossing, and that
       medians are a safety benefit and reduce crashes.
   •   U-turns will increase the risk of residents being able to get out of their driveways, but there
       is a risk with making a left turn out of the driveway too.
   •   UDOT does have height requirements for trees, as well as the mature trunk size, which
       cannot be larger than four inches. There will also be setback requirements from the
       crosswalk.
   •   Crashes do occur at locations like this, so UDOT tries to avoid such conflicts.
   •   Not allowing a left turn with a left turn further back is to avoid crashes Head-on crashes
       take place in conflict offsets like this.
   •   UDOT would be very supportive of an access into the Arbor Park Kennecott property.

                Council Member Prokopis asked why a left turn here was any more dangerous
than a left turn out of other locations nearby.

              Ms. Zundel stated anytime conflict points can be consolidated into traditional four-
legged intersections, driver expectancy becomes higher. Drivers are looking for left turn
movements at those intersections, but that is not always the case with a driveway access.

             Council Member Peel stated the median will have to be moved no matter what to
allow emergency vehicles.

               Ms. Zundel stated emergency vehicles can go over medical curbs.

              Capt. Allen Norton, Unified Fire Authority (UFA), stated the curbs are damaging
to the UFA vehicles and the U-turns are difficult to maneuver. The UFA drivers accessing the
property would probably turn left into the right only turn.

             Ms. Hulbert stated UDOT could rip up what is there now and install a mountable
median for emergency personnel.

                Council Member Prokopis stated he was inclined to move the island back for
emergency vehicles, but allow other traffic to continue as it has for a couple more years. Then,
revisit the proposal once the Arbor Park commercial property is developed and an access installed
there.

Citizen Public Input

                Randy Peet stated he had been getting irrigation water from the ditch near his
property for 30 years, but has not gotten it since a development started going in adjacent to his
property. The original development plans showed irrigation water being piped through to his
property, but that developer sold the property, and now the new owner is telling him the ditch was
abandoned.

                Paul Ashton stated the developer is obligated, per statute, to make sure water is
provided to the property there unless the canal company abandoned the ditch. The canal
company is the entity that should be watching to make sure that is done. He suggested sending
a letter to the canal company demanding to know what it has done to provide water there and
asking that it be put on record if it did not abandon the ditch. He also suggested the Council look
at the subdivision plat to see if it says anything about the ditch or the irrigation water. If the
development has not been completed and the developer is in breach for not providing the water,
some things can be withheld, such as certificates of occupancy.

Unified Police Department

                Lieutenant Ken Malone, Unified Police Department, reviewed the quarterly report
for April, May, and June for the Magna Precinct, highlighting the following matters:

   •   Copperton and Southwest Community cars were moved to the Magna Precinct this
       quarter, and those statistics are included in this report. That move affected response
       times, in that those officers were often dispatched to the south end of the valley. Response
       times within Magna remained consistent.
   •   With Riverton withdrawing from the UPD, the Magna Precinct was overstaffed by one
       officer for a period of time, but it is now down by one officer.
   •   Magna Precinct responded to a lot of violent crimes, several of which were aggravated
       domestic type situations. Canine and the gang unit are part of violent crimes too, and
       there are a lot of gangs that frequent the Magna area. However, if a report is not filed,
       the response is not documented in this report.

              Council Member Prokopis stated the Special Victim Unit in this report lists 109
sex crimes against children. That is one sex crime a day against a child.

                 Lieutenant Malone stated that is how many times a call was received and
reported, but it does not reflect whether a crime was confirmed. The Magna Precinct receives a
lot of reports from the Department of Children and Family Services (DCFS) and has to look into
all of those. A crime is not necessarily substantiated in all of those case; oftentimes, they are due
to a neighbor calling DCFS. He also reported that the Magna Precinct is getting ready for the Das
Energi Festival at Saltaire this Friday and Saturday, which a lot of concert goers will be attending,

RESOLUTION NO. 19-08-01                                                      Date: August 13, 2019

       A RESOLUTION OF THE MAGNA METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL APPROVING
       A FIVE-YEAR SERVICE CONTRACT BETWEEN MAGNA AND ONSOLVE, FOR
       USE OF THE CODERED ALERT SYSTEM

                                            RECITALS

A.     The Magna Metro Township (“Magna”) is a municipality pursuant to Utah Code §§ 10-2a-
401 et seq.

B.    The Magna Metro Township Council (the “Council”) is the municipal legislative body for
Magna pursuant to Utah Code § 10-3b-501.

C.    Onsolve, LLC (the “Company”) is a provider of an emergency alert and information
dissemination system known as CodeRED.

D.     One of the key components required in the development of the Magna Emergency
Operations Plan is the establishment of a community emergency alert and information
dissemination system.

E.    Based on referrals from some local communities and the Emigration Metro Township,
Magna contacted representatives of Onsolve LLC to inquire about contracting to use Onsolve’s
CodeRED alert system.

F.     Based on discussions with Company representatives, and Onsolve’s presentation to the
Council on May 28, 2019, the Council directed staff to pursue a five-year contract with the
Company for CodeRED.

DATE           TUESDAY                               AUGUST                             13, 2019

G.     The Company responded with the attached contract for services (Attachment “A”).

H.     Use of CodeRED will allow Magna to effectively communicate important information to its
residents during emergencies and other important non-emergency incidents.

                                         RESOLUTION

       THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED, the Council hereby approves the 5-year service contact
between Magna and Onsolve LLC, hereinafter known in Attachment A with exhibits, and
authorizes the Mayor to sign the contract to affect the same.
