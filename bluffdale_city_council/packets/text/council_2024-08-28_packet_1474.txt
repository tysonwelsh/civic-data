 2   Camp Williams was established at the turn of the 20th century. Bluffdale was settled in the 1880s
 3   and Bluffdale and Camp Williams have coexisted for over a century. Bluffdale is proud to support
 4   veterans and active service members at Camp Williams and Bluffdale City. It recognizes that Camp
 5   Williams has a vital mission to continue training active service members to protect the people of both
 6   the State of Utah and the United States. As Bluffdale works to accommodate the current development

 8   facilitating their mission. Those sentiments are echoed by the legislature.
 9
10   In the most recent legislative session, the State passed House Bill 256 regarding military-compatible
11   Land Use amendments. The bill requires municipalities to collaborate with military installations
12   within their jurisdictions and adopt a Compatible Use Plan for all areas located within 5,000 feet of
13   military installations. The plan must be adopted by July 1, 2025. In addition to developing and
14   adopting the plan, Bluffdale is also required to provide notice to the Utah Department of Veterans
15   and Military Affairs ("UDVMA") of any Land Use applications, with the exception of Building
16   Permits, received for properties within that 5,000-foot buffer. That includes zoning amendments,
17   General Plan amendments, and site plans. The UDVMA then has a 90-day response period in which
18   they will provide a written response stating whether they find the proposal to be compatible or
19   incompatible with Camp Williams' mission. Once the response is received, the City is able to move
20   forward and take final action on the application. The statute also requires the City to consider its
21   Compatible Land Use Plan when making those decisions.
22
23   Ms. Tubbs presented a map of the affected areas of Bluffdale, which covers the southern half of Perry
24   Farms and south of 15200 South, as well as a portion of the gravel pits and Camp Williams itself.
25

29   The process also included representatives from Camp Williams and the Utah National Guard, as well

                                     Traffic calming incorporates the combination of physical measures to reduce the negative
                                     effects of motor vehicle use, alter driver behavior, and improve conditions for non-motorized
                                     street users such as pedestrians and bicyclists (ITE 2016). Traffic calming measures
                                     encourage vehicles to obey posted speed limits as they move through different roadways
                                     (Parham 1998). The objective of traffic calming is to reduce motor vehicle speeds, noise,
                                     and collision frequency and severity; increase the level of safety for non-motorized users
                                     such as pedestrians and bicyclists; and enhance the street environment (ITE 2016, New
                                     England Section 2000). In addition to these objectives, speed reduction measures serve to
                                     increase quality of life, create safe and attractive roads, help to reduce the negative effects
                                     on the environment due to motor vehicles, and promote walking, cycling, and transit use (ITE
                                     2016).

                                     The purpose of this manual is to provide general information about several speed reduction
                                     measures used in the United States. This manual presents several speed reduction
                                     measures with their proper definition, effectiveness, relative cost, applications, and
                                     advantages and disadvantages. The manual also includes two decision matrices: one to
                                     illustrate the effectiveness of each measure and the other to show the potential impacts of
                                     installing each measure. This manual also includes a suggested planning process to
                                     address speeding issues, as well as a flow chart and a project prioritization form to help
                                     decide the appropriate traffic calming measure.

VERTICAL SPEED REDUCTION MEASURES
Vertical speed reduction measures are applications that adjust pavement elevation over
short distances and cause vehicles to slow. The vertical changes in elevation are always less
than six inches at the street centerline (DelDOT 2012). Motorists who travel over these
speed control measures above the appropriate speed limit will experience “unpleasant
bumping sensations” (PennDOT 2012).

Vertical speed control measures are effective in reducing speed and diverting unwanted
traffic from neighborhood streets. If the location receiving vertical measures is used by
transit or emergency vehicles, or regularly requires snow removal via snowplows, it is
important to plan and design the placement of vertical measures to appropriately
accommodate those vehicles (DelDOT 2012).

Bluffdale is willing to implement the following vertical speed reduction measures:

   •   Raised Crosswalks
   •   Raised Intersections
   •   Textured Pavement

Information regarding these measures can be found in the appendix.

Due to the negative impacts of speed bumps/humps, they are not suitable for what
Bluffdale City is trying to accomplish with traffic calming on its roads. Information regarding
the negatives of speed bumps/humps can be found in the appendix.

                                     HORIZONTAL SPEED REDUCTION MEASURES
                                     Horizontal speed reduction measures are applications that cause drivers to slow by altering
                                     vehicle direction. The applications hinder a driver’s ability to drive in a straight line by
                                     creating a horizontal shift in the roadway; the shift forces drivers to slow down to safely
                                     navigate the measure (PennDOT 2012).

                                     These measures are primarily used in areas where speeding is a concern. However, they
                                     may also be effective in diverting traffic from neighborhood streets and improve pedestrian
                                     safety by reducing the width of the crossing (DelDOT 2012).

                                     Bluffdale is willing to implement the following horizontal speed reduction measures:

                                        •   Realignments
                                        •   Chicanes

Stop Signs should not be used for speed control
Definition: Stop signs are primarily used to manage right-of-way at an intersection. In the
past, they have also been used as a traffic-calming measure (or also called speed reduction
measures). For example, stop signs have been used to slow down traffic in certain areas to
make them less attractive to through traffic (NCCHPP 2011). However, MUTCD states that
“STOP signs shall not be used for speed control” (MUTCD 2023).
Effectiveness: A study of nine residential two-way stop sign installations in Portland, Oregon,
indicated that stop signs are not very effective in reducing speeds. Before-and-after studies
on all 36 legs of the intersections indicated that the 85th percentile speeds remained the
same on 19 legs, (53%), increased by 2 mph or more on four legs (11%), and decreased by
2 mph or more on 13 legs (36%) for an average decrease of 0.5 mph (Parham 1998).
Application: Stop signs should only be used when warranted by the MUTCD. As with any
other traffic sign, drivers need to recognize the need for a sign, or they will eventually begin
to disregard the sign that they feel is unnecessary. This problem is especially dangerous with
regards to stop signs. The Utah MUTCD states the following regarding the excessive use of
stop signs (MUTCD 2011):
       Section 2A.04 Excessive Use of Signs: Regulatory and warning signs should be
       used conservatively because these signs, if used to excess, tend to lose their
       effectiveness.
       Section 2B.04 Right-of-Way at Intersections: Yield or Stop signs should not be
       used for speed control.
       Section 2B.07 Multi-Way Stop Applications: Multi-way stop control can be
       useful as a safety measure at intersections if certain traffic conditions exist.
       Safety concerns associated with multi-way stops include pedestrians,
       bicyclists, and all road users expecting other road users to stop. Multi-way
       stop control is used where the volume of traffic on the intersecting roads is
       approximately equal.

POTENTIAL IMPACT MATRIX
Speed reduction measures are used to address speeding issues; however, they can have
other effects in the surrounding area. These effects depend on the type of measure used
and its physical characteristics such as size and material. Based on the research done,
seven potential impacts were determined that apply to all measures: available shoulder
space, emergency response vehicles, drainage, noise pollution, pedestrian protection, snow
removal, and bicycle safety. This section explains each potential impact and shows a final
decision matrix including all the measures and potential impacts.
In general, the symbols used in this matrix consist of hollow and black circles.

   ●     Positive impact

detailed information about the impact of a specific measure, refer to the vertical horizontal,
narrowing, and non-construction speed reduction measures sections in the beginning of this
manual.
Available Shoulder Space
The available shoulder space category refers to whether a road has sufficient space to place
a specific speed reduction measure in an area. In other words, it refers to the space that a
specific measure will need to be built on a particular roadway/intersection. Available space
is often a limiting factor since some measures require a considerable amount of road space
to be put in place. Chokers and bolb-outs, for example, require far more road space than
most traffic calming measures; they increase the sidewalk width and decrease the road
width to slow drivers and allow for safer pedestrian crossings.
The symbols used to reflect available shoulder space are a hollow circle and a black circle.

                                     1. Initiate the Study
                                     A study should be initiated involving all parties that would be affected by the implementation of a
                                     speed reduction measure in the area of study. In this stage of the process, parties involved, study
                                     area, the scope of work, and any constraints (budget, schedule, site conditions, etc.) should be
                                     identified and agreed upon. At the study initiation, the following tasks should be done:

                                            Identify the need for a speed reduction study: The need for a speed reduction study could be
                                            identified by neighborhood residents or organizations, and they should submit a formal
                                            petition to ensure consistency and objectiveness. A speed reduction study could also be
                                            identified by the city and municipal staff assigned to work on speed reduction issues. If the
                                            reported issue has already been identified and studied previously, and site conditions and
                                            traffic patterns have not significantly changed in the study area since the previous study, the
                                            concerned resident is notified of the results of the previous study. No further study will be
                                            performed unless study area conditions have changed, as determined by the City Engineer.

                                            Define study scope and study area: The study area should be clearly defined before any data
                                            collection and further investigation occurs to ensure that key objectives, constraints, and
                                            deliverables are identified and agreed in advance. The study or project area should include
                                            the study street, cross streets on either side of measure, any street that relies on the study
                                            street for access, the two parallel local service street, and any other local streets that may be
                                            affected by the speed reduction measure to be implemented.

                                            Develop a Team: The City Engineer may choose to form a team consisting of City Staff and/or
                                            a consultant engineer with experience in speed reduction measures depending on the extent
                                            and nature of the project.

                                            Meet with the team: The City Engineer and team should meet to ensure that all the parties
                                            agree and are aware of the issues and scope of work. In this meeting, the team should
                                            develop a detailed work program, schedule, and budget for the study.

                                     may be appropriate to address such issues. The following steps are recommended to identify the

plan according to what the area needs. The following steps present a layout for a successful plan:

       Examine arterial streets: Traffic operations of adjacent arterial streets should be examined to
       ensure there are no operational deficiencies which might be negatively affecting traffic in the
       neighborhood. If there are problems on the adjacent arterial streets, then modifications
       should be made to eliminate or reduce the traffic problems that occur on neighborhood

       planning process can reach its objective.

       Examine neighborhood streets: Consider the effects of speed reduction measures on

including:

   •   Discomfort for vehicle occupants: Speed bumps/humps can cause discomfort to
       passengers inside the vehicle, particularly when driven over at higher speeds. This
       can be particularly bothersome for those with back or spine issues.

       components.

Memo
To:            Mayor and City Council
From:          Stephanie Thayer, Finance Manager
Date:          August 23, 2024
Re:            Proposed Changes to the Consolidated Fee Schedule




The City charges a variety of fees for services it provides. The amount of a given fee is
calculated based on the underlying cost to the City. The underlying cost to the City may
include directly attributable costs such as the cost of purchasing a thousand gallons of water,
and the underlying cost may include allocated costs such as the labor and equipment costs
incurred to maintain the water system.

All the fees are detailed in a document we publish on our website called the Consolidated Fee
Schedule. Our staff use this schedule to ensure we are consistent when charging for services,
and residents and others working with the City use the schedule to plan and estimate their
costs for various activities.

Since the fees are set to cover the City’s associated costs, department heads and other key
staff review the fees at least once a year to determine whether the fees need to be increased
or decreased to cover changes in the underlying costs. Typically, this is done as part of the
annual budget process, but events and factors may at times require a mid-year adjustment.

Not all fees change every year. There are times when the change in underlying costs does not
warrant a change in the fee. But, generally speaking, as our costs change, we modify the Fee
Schedule.

Some of the most notable changes for consideration in this proposed Consolidated Fee
Schedule are as follows:

        •   Water fees were increased to reflect the increase in the contract cost of water from
            Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District;


                                                                                       1|P a g e
       •   Garbage fees were increased at the same rate as increases in the contract with
           Waste Management;

       •   Water meter installation fees and hydrant meter rental fees were increased due to
           an increase in the cost of the meters;

       •   A new Public Safety Fee (pursuant to Ordinance 2024-13) is being instituted to
           cover the increased costs from the expansion of service levels in the fire
           department and police department. This is a specific charge for a specific service.
           This fee is reported in the Fire and Police Protection Fund and may not be used for
           any purpose other than providing Public Safety Services. The amount of the fee
           was determined by an independent fee study based on the number of calls for
           service in the various zones of the City. In other words, the fee is proportional to
           services to be provided in each particular zone. The fee study calculated that a
           homeowner in a residential zone could be charged as much as $47.75 per month to
           pay for services provided. However, staff is recommending the City reduce the
           residential zone fee to $9.25. Similarly, the fee has also been reduced for the other
           zones in the City. The recommended fee is projected to generate the total Public
           Safety fee revenue included in the fiscal year 2024/2025 budget;

       •   Three sections were moved from the “Streets” section to the “Engineering” section
           since the engineering department administers the fees in those sections;

       •   Fees for using our community rooms were updated to better differentiate them
           based on their capacity and amenities, as well as to help the front office better
           administer the reservations;

       •   The landscaping bond fee was removed to align with changes in Utah State law as
           well as the SWPPP violation fee as sometimes new legislature comes along that
           changes how we must comply.

We recommend you adopt the Consolidated Fee Schedule with the proposed changes. We
further ask you to set the effective date as September 1, 2024. The City’s monthly invoices are
sent at the beginning of the month. The delayed effective date will allow staff the time to
implement these changes in the billing system.

If you have any questions, please let us know.

       INTRODUCTION
       EFG Consulting (EFG) developed a police service fee for Bluffdale City (City) that recoups the cost of police
       service. The purpose of the fee analysis was to create a nexus between the use of police service by specific
       users and cost of providing that service. The following provides a summary of the findings, methodology
       for the analysis, and options evaluated in the analysis.


       SUMMARY OF FINDINGS
       EFG provides the following two scenarios of fees based upon the amount of cost recovery desired by the

       METHODOLOGY
       The primary principle that guided this analysis was creating a clear link between the cost of providing
       police service to each user type. This methodology has been tested by the Utah Supreme Court in its
       decision in Larson vs. Pleasant Grove City. Cody Deeter was the manager of the transportation user fee
       analysis for Pleasant Grove City that was later challenged and upheld by the Supreme Court.

       EXPENSES
       The methodology utilized to make this critical link was the cost per police call. This started with the 2025
       budgeted cost of service. The majority of the City’s cost for providing police service is the contract with
       the City of Saratoga Springs which is 87% of the budget. The other expenses are supplies, equipment, and
       shop charges.

     CALL DATA
     The next step in developing the fee was to determine the total number of calls for service. This
     information was provided by Saratoga Springs and analyzed by Glen Busch with Bio West. In 2023, there
     was 11,292 calls for service. Of those, approximately 50% were calls that cannot be attributed to
     demand at an address. This includes traffic related calls, calls outside City boundaries, calls on City
     property, or calls to Camp Williams. The demand for these calls were included in the base fee and
     spread across all users equally.

     The remaining calls were geocoded to each zone within the City and then rolled up to three categories:
     Commercial, Residential, and Apartments. These three areas have a unique user profile.

     BASE FEE ANALYSIS
     The base fee was calculated by taking the total budget for 2025 ($3,310,974) and multiplying by the
     percent of unallocated calls (50.7%). The base fee thus needs to collect $1,678,944 over 5,270 users.
     This equates to a base fee per month of $26.55 per user.

     FEE PER TYPE
     The fee per user type was calculated by taking the proportion of the budget allocated to calls per zone
     ($1,632,030) and spreading that over the calls within each user category. The following table
