    Mark Owensby, Deputy Fire Chief, presented a PowerPoint Presentation
    summarized as follows:

              -   Objectives
                      o The purpose of the response analysis is to determine the
                          effectiveness and efficiency of the West Valley City Fire
                          Department during emergency response as it relates to industry
                          best practice, and general expectations.
                      o Determine Effective Response Force (ERF), and Response time
                          analysis.
                      o Placement and distribution of Fire Stations, Apparatus, and
                          personnel.
              -   ERF
                      o (ERF= Minimum amount of staffing and equipment, determined
                          through Critical Task Analysis (CTA), required to mitigate
                          emergencies within the community. Also referred to as “First
                          Alarm”.
                      o Critical Task Analysis (CTA): Determination, through risk
                          assessment, of tasks required to be completed by emergency
                          responders in order to successfully mitigate an emergency of a
                          given severity and type.
                      o ERF for a House fire. 17-21 personnel.
                      o ERF for apartment complex, Commercial or Industrial
                          occupancy. 20-25 personnel.
              -   Call volume heat map
              -   90th Percentile Response Time chart
              -   4 Minute travel time WVC
              -   4 Minute travel time mutual aid

B.    ANIMAL SHELTER ADOPTION HOURS
      Mayor Lang stated that she received an email from a gentleman who was unable to
      adopt a pet due to the limited hours of adoption services at the animal shelter. She
      indicated that she forwarded this email as well as staff’s response and asked if the
      Council had any direction. Councilmember Whetstone stated that it appears the
      shelter is at capacity and it would be a matter of increasing staff or extending hours.
      Mayor Lang suggested extending hours later into the evening during the week.
      Nicole stated that this is a possible option but noted that it would be difficult. She
      indicated that there normally needs to be about an hour and a half for the adoption
      process and if the hours are extended to 6 PM, for example, there would need to be
      enough staff present until approximately 7:30 PM to cover phones and the front
      door as well as any other potential walk-ins requesting adoption meet and greets.
      Nicole stated that staff can add/remove hours as needed but noted that there isn’t a
      budget for overtime so there would need to be some trimming around the edges in
      one way or another to accomplish this. She indicated that this is a very difficult,
      emotional job and has one of the highest turnover rates so keeping the animal
      shelter fully staffed has been a challenge. Councilmember Harmon questioned if
      adoption hours is a common complaint. Nicole replied that this is the first concern
      that has been reported to administration or the Animal Shelter. Mayor Lang stated
      that she has had people express direct concern to her in the community. Mayor Lang
      expressed concern about phone calls not being answered at the shelter as well.
      Councilmember Harmon suggested allowing adoptions once or twice a month on
      Saturdays at the shelter instead of off-site. Nicole replied that the City can do this
      for a few months and then report back on how it goes. The Mayor and Council
      agreed that this would be a good first step. Councilmember Whetstone asked if
      appointments are well utilized. Nicole replied yes and indicated that this began
      during COVID and has been well received by the community. Councilmember
      Huynh asked if there is a certain period during the day where more adoption

      C.     CODE ENFORCEMENT DISCUSSION
             Mayor Lang and the Council agreed to go over the list of “Proposed Procedures of
             Enforcement for Select Codes” that was emailed by Wayne Pyle to the Council
             following the January 31, 2023 Code Enforcement Discussion. Nicole Cottle,
             Acting City Manager, stated that this document was produced as a result of the
             Council’s discussion and is meant to verify that staff understood the direction
             provided by the Council during the January 31, 2023 City Council meeting. The
             following items were discussed as follows:

             Outside Storage/Solid Waste: Items constructed for inside use stay inside.
             Discarded, spent, broken, or useless items are disposed of properly.

             Front setback: Enforce

             Side Setback: No enforcement behind solid fence. Enforcement without solid fence.

             Rear Setback: Regardless of fencing, no enforcement unless imminent or current
             health hazard such as deteriorating organic waste with odor.

             Enforcement Process: Issue Courtesy Notice with minimum 10 days covering 2
             weekends. The City will seek a court order through the NOV process to enter and
             remove all offending outside storage/solid waste items. The City will not issue
             Administrative Warnings or Citations.

             Lang asked if broken equipment or items not in use would be in violation.
             Councilmember Harmon replied that under this proposal, the City would not check

 Enforcement Process:

 60% Violations: The City will seek a court order through the NOV process to
 remove excess parking and maneuvering materials to achieve the 60% minimum
 landscaping requirement. The City will not issue Administrative Warnings or
 Citations

 30% Violations: No enforcement. The City will not seek a court order through the
 NOV process to install live plant material or to apply water, fertilizer, etc., in order
 to achieve the 30% minimum live plant material requirement. The City will not
 issue Administrative Warnings or Citations for properties that do not achieve the
 30% live plant material requirement.

 6” Rule: Issue Courtesy Notice with minimum 10 days covering 2 weekends. The
 city will seek a court order through the NOV process to remove grass and weeds in
 excess of 6” high.
                          OR

 Issue Administrative Warning with minimum 10 days covering 2 weekends. Any
 subsequent day thereafter in which the property is in violation, the City will issue
 an Administrative Citation, not to exceed 2 Administrative Citations per calendar
 year. No case is submitted to court, and the City may not enter and abate.
