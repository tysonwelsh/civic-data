         Unified Fire Authority Standards of Cover

Chief Wade Watkins presented Unified Fire Authority’s Standards of Cover. This presentation is
included in the packet.

Mayor Staggs commented that Riverton City’s total response time is about 10 minutes and that
tracks with urban response times around the valley. He also commented that the average
standard is about 17 firefighters per incident.

money should be included in the upcoming budget for larger repairs.

Councilmember Buroker asked if there is a capital facility improvement plan. In response, Kevin

improvements in the RFSA budget.

the current standard of cover and said this standard is included in the budget.

mutual agencies could respond.

drills are conducted regularly at all of the local schools. Councilmember Buroker said it would
be helpful to see a report detailing the fire inspections that happen at Riverton businesses.


Discussion Items

         Update Regarding Transportation Issues and Literary Opportunities

Mayor Staggs explained that he was appointed in February 2022 to be on the UTA Advisory
Board. The three trustees are empowered to handle the budget and the local advisory board
does not have much authority. In the south end of the valley, UTA has pulled out all bus routes
and implemented the Via app for transit. Salt Lake City and the Southwest cities of South
Jordan, Riverton, Herriman and Bluffdale have roughly the same population which equates to
each of these two areas representing 17% of the county population. However, in comparing
Salt Lake City to the Southwest cities, the bus stops are 32% compared to 2%, the rail stations
are 48% compared to 5%, the riders are 50% compared to 2%. Sales tax revenue for mass
transit totals $260 million for Salt Lake County, $75 million comes from Salt Lake City, $27
million comes from the Southwest cities. In the operational budget, the expenses are shown by
mode and it was determined that the costs in the southwest are only $6 million whereas, Salt
Lake City is $103 million in costs.

Mayor Staggs explained that he would like to encourage the Southwest neighbors to reach out
to the trustees while they are considering the budget this year about these discrepancies.

Councilmember McDougal said that UTA has a reduction in fee for Salt Lake City residents who
ride mass transit. Every year the disparity will increase and the Southwest cities will not get the
services they need unless something changes. The growth is tremendous in the Southwest
cities and mass transit needs to be an option to help with traffic issues.

Councilmember Stewart said there are other funding mechanisms that UTA uses that should be
researched. He tried to get a bill passed that would require UTA to be transparent on reporting
and Carlton was able to get it killed.

Councilmember McCay expressed appreciation for the Mayor’s information on UTA. She
explained she found similar disparities in the way that TRCC funding has been distributed over
the past years. She will continue to fight for cities that have been underfunded and hopes that
Riverton will receive about $1 million in TRCC funding. She would like to apply for TRCC
funding every year and partner with surrounding cities on projects. She said the cities need to
have a 50% match in order to get funding.

Mayor Staggs explained that the Salt Lake County Library System Board consists of 9
members, only one of which is elected, County Councilmember Snelgrove. He provided an
excel spreadsheet and other supporting documents for both Salt Lake County libraries and the
Murray library that are included in the packet.

Mayor Staggs said the County was contemplating tearing down the Riverton library and building
a 30,000 square foot facility which would cost $38 million. He plans to give Riverton residents
additional information so they can approach their elected officials and provide comment.

Councilmember Stewart asked for budget information for each branch in the Salt Lake County
Library System.

Mayor Staggs asked that Item 14 c be pulled from the Consent Agenda because they have

Mayor Staggs explained that Councilmember Stewart will give notice to the City Council that he
will be leaving on December 31 given that he was elected to the County Council. Mayor Staggs

Public Hearings

         Ordinance No. 22-22 – The Master Development Agreement for Riverton Ranch
         Estates, establishing requirements, standards, and parameters for development of
         approximately 3.2 acres located at 13040 S Redwood, and including approval of an
         eight (8) lot private lane subdivision on that property

Mr. Lethbridge explained this item was continued from the September 20, 2022 City Council
Meeting. Riverton City is proposing approval of a Development Agreement establishing
requirements, standards, and parameters for development of approximately 3.2-acres located at
13040 S Redwood Road, including approval of an eight (8) lot subdivision of that property, with
all lots accessing a private lane.

Mr. Lethbridge said the property is currently zoned R-3-SD, a single-family residential zone, with
1/3 minimum lot sizes. The zoning and general plan designations were previously approved by
Riverton City. The subdivision accesses Riverton Ranch Road via a private drive, and the
applicant has proposed a gate as part of the development. The lane and configuration and
location of the gate has been reviewed by the Riverton City’s Unified Fire Authority
representative.

Ryan McDougal, Applicant, commented that he wanted to continue the hearing until the
development agreement was correct. He said the redlines he submitted are still outstanding
and he would prefer to have 100% of the redlines signed off before entering into the master
development agreement.

Councilmember McCay said she would like to have the gate on lot 104 removed. Mr. McDougal
said the gate was placed there to minimize the traffic going in and out of the neighborhood.

Mr. Lethbridge explained the redlines were clean up items, the conditions are small and will be
completed by the engineer and sent to the applicant tomorrow.

Councilmember Buroker said that approving this project at this time would move the project
along quicker and said that continuing the public hearing would be very confusing for residents.

Mayor Staggs asked if the redlines would significantly impact this development. Mr. Lethbridge
said no.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments;
