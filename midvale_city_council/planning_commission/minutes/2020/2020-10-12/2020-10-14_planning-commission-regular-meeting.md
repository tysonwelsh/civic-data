AMENDMENT; MODIFYING DEVELOPMENT STANDARDS IN JORDAN

BLUFFS SUBAREAS 1-3. ZONE; ADAM LANKFORD/WASATCH

DEVELOPMENT (APPLICANT); ALEX MURPHY, PLANNING DIRECTOR

(STAFF).
Mr. Alex Murphy stated that in 2019, the City adopted a zoning ordinance for Subareas 1-3 of the
Jordan Bluffs zone. This ordinance established goals for the district, review process requirements,
and standards for commercial, residential, and mixed-use development. To date, two projects have
been approved under this ordinance (Holmes Homes’ Jordan Bluffs Condo project and Zions
Bank).
Wasatch Residential Group is working on a third project in this area, a soccer training facility. As
part of this project, they are requesting the addition or modification of several ordinance
requirements. Staff is also using this opportunity to propose modified lighting and signage
standards following the recent changes that were adopted in Subarea 4.
This text amendment proposes the changes to the following sections:
17-7-10.12.8 Fencing and screening standards.
This section includes changes to fencing requirements on the cap slope and around athletic
facilities.
PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION OCTOBER14,2020,

The current zoning ordinance is written to prohibit all fencing on the slope. However, the ordinance
also allows solar panels and recreational uses on or near the slope. Some of these uses may have
security concerns that warrant fencing. New language has been added to allow limited fencing to
address security concerns when approved by the Community Development Director.

The current ordinance limits athletic fencing to 14’ in height and requires a minimum of 50%
opacity for portions of fencing over 7’ tall. The proposed changes increase the allowable athletic
fencing limit to 20’, to better address the possibility for soccer or other balls to leave outdoor fields,
and lowers the opacity height to 6’.

17-7-10.12.9 Outdoor lighting standards.

This section includes changes to the lighting standards for all uses in the zone. As proposed:

e Section A is renumbered for clarity.

e Lighting standards for outdoor athletic facilities are added. These facilities would be
allowed taller lights than other uses but are still subject to the fixture type, trespass, output
limit, and other requirements of the zone. Operational times are limited to 7:00 a.m. to
10:00 p.m. to match the County’s noise ordinance time limits.

e Uplighting would be allowed up to a defined limit of lumens, based on property area,
provided no light trespass occurs across property lines.

e Street lights would be exempted from the 100,000 lumen/acre limit.

e String or bistro lighting would be exempted from the cutoff, down direction, and shielding
requirements.

17-7-10.12.13 Signage.

The proposed changes to this section would allow temporary and freestanding signage. As with
the JB Sub 4 zone, temporary signage was not originally approved when this zone was adopted.
As proposed:

e Banner signs up to 80 SF would be permitted on building walls and fences. Banner signs
would also be allowable as primary signage for up to 6 months after a business license or
certificate of occupancy, after which time a permanent sign would be required.

e Other temporary signs (balloons/inflated displays, flags, feathers, etc.) would be permitted
for up to 30 days per quarter. A temporary sign permit would be required for each display
period. A defined number of signs would be permitted (1 per 50’ of street frontage) and
signs would be limited to 12’ in maximum height.

e Grand opening signs are redefined as new occupancy signs to remove possible confusion
regarding content. Signs for new occupancy periods would be allowed for up to 18 months
or 30 days after the final Certificate of Occupancy for a project, which ever comes later.

e A new sign type, freestanding signs, is proposed. These signs are intended to be used for
flagship developments in the project area and would be limited to the largest properties in
the zone (over 15 acres in size) and have greater separation from other signs. Other
requirements include maximum height and area calculations.

5. SUBDIVISION PROCESS AND ORDINANCE UPDATE; JASON BINKS, SENIOR
PLANNER, AND ALEX MURPHY, PLANNING DIRECTOR (STAFF).
Mr. Murphy stated that as background the Housing Plan was approved last year. This plan called
for improvements and modifications to the subdivision process. This is partially in an attempt to
reduce the administrative burdens that exists in the current process and should theoretically reduce
the cost. Therefore, Staff wanted to have a discussion on the process of subdivisions. He added
that Mr. Jason Binks, has put together a presentation on Subdivision Analysis to go along with the
forgoing discussion. The presentation will review the process as a whole, what the state of Utah is
presently doing and comparing other opportunities.
Mr. Jason Binks stated that this year City Council adopted a moratorium on Master Plan
Developments in single family residential zones and so staff is working on options and are in the
process of narrowing them down. One of the options is to look at the root of the procedures and
Staff therefore decided to look at a comparison of what the State Code states vs. what the Midvale
Codes states to identify any gaps and differences.
Mr. Binks started the comparison by looking at Authority and where the subdivisions process is
addressed in State Code. He said that those codes can be found in Title 10, chapter 9a, Municipal
PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION OCTOBER 14, 2020

Land Use, Development and Management Act and part 6, Subdivisions. In Midvale code it is found
in Title 16, Subdivisions, Chapter 4, General Provisions and section 40, Large Subdivisions. In
looking at authority, Mr. Binks stated that the Midvale subdivisions application process currently
meets all State code requirements. He added that in looking at City’s around the State, many have
designated the Planning Commission as the land use authority and City Council has no additional
input. As Midvale code stands currently, it does not explicitly designate a land use authority. It |
does however identify reviewing bodies and specifies that City Council has final approval. In
Midvale’s subdivision process, the Economic and Development department staff reviews the
submissions and provides recommendations to the Planning Commission. The Planning
Commission then reviews it, takes comment and makes their own recommendation on it and then
it gets passed along to City Council for their final review and approval.

The fact that there not a designated land use authority can be very confusing to the developers and
the public.

In looking specifically at Midvale’s large and minor subdivisions processes, Mr. Binks stated that
for large subdivisions, meaning more than 3 lots, there are 3 public meetings and 2 notification
requirements. For Minor Subdivisions, meaning 2-3 lots, there are 2 public meetings and 2
notification requirements. He added because the final is approved by City Council for both large
and minor subdivisions, Midvale City Council is essentially the land use authority in this case.
Often the comments and requests for changes made in the preliminary hearings are not made until
the final plat review.

Mr. Binks stated that because of the requirements attached to the Midvale’s Subdivision process,
Midvale has more restrictive subdivision requirements than the State. There are pros and cons to
this. Pros include more oversight and involvement and the cons are increased time and cost for
everyone.

In looking at other municipal codes, Mr. Binks notes that Salt Lake City designates the Planning
Commission to approve the preliminary plat and the final plat is approved by the Mayor. Salt Lake
County designates the Planning Commission as the land use authority on Subdivisions. Overall,
in both Salt Lake County and Salt Lake City, the Council is not involved at all with subdivisions
and Salt Lake City does not even take comment on the applications that meet code.

In conclusion Mr. Binks added this comparison has provided some suggestions on ways to moved
forward. Many of the suggestions provided revolve around the designation of the land use
authority, the use of public comment and regulations for administrative review.
