         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the interlocal agreement between the
Redevelopment Agency (RDA) and Weber County pertaining to the Airport Community Reinvestment Area (CRA) came before
the Board for consideration. The memo stated on June 6, 2023, the RDA Board approved the plan and budget for the Airport
Community Reinvestment Area (CRA). The Board approved the survey area in February 2019. The area consists of all airport
property within Ogden City as well as properties north of Hinckley Drive and east of Airport Road. The proposed project area
collection period is 25 years commencing on January 1, 2023. The total proposed budget for the project area is $119,316,079 with
Ogden City, Ogden School District, Central Weber Sewer Improvement District, and Weber County participating. At the June 6,
2023, meeting the Board approved interlocal agreements between the RDA and Weber County, Ogden School District, Central
Weber Sewer Improvement District, and Ogden City. In the time since the adoption of the interlocal agreements, Weber County
has chosen to revise its participation from 90 percent to 75 percent and would restrict the use of the county’s contribution to
infrastructure only. The action for the Board would be to adopt an updated interlocal agreement.
         An additional memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed participation
and incentive agreement with Williams International regarding an expansion to their facility came before the Board for
consideration. The memo stated the draft participation and incentive agreement would incentivize the Company’s expansion with
the use of up to $22 million in tax increment financing (TIF) support. The incentive would include $17 million to support new
electrical infrastructure with an additional $5 million to support the new jobs. An additional $5.5 million of RDA funds would be
committed by the Agency to construct a new 24” waterline at the Airport. The incentive would be paid annually post-performance
until the Airport CRA expires in 2048 or until the maximum incentive is reached, whichever is sooner.
          Economic Development Director Meess summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to illustrate
the participation structure of the taxing entities involved in the Airport CRA. She used an additional presentation to summarize the
terms of the Williams International participation and incentive agreement. She first reported that Williams International is
considering expanding their operations - the company produces gas turbine engines for small jets and cruise missiles - and is a key
supplier for national defense and has been operating in Ogden since 1878. Their proposed expansion is 100,000 square feet, will
bring more than 300 high wage jobs to the area, and generate increased tax increment financing for the next several years. Ms.
Meess discussed some of the challenges Williams International has faced as part of their expansion, including labor and other
operating costs that are higher than at other sites and electrical infrastructure upgrades are required for the Ogden site. They have
received an approved maximum of EDTIF incentive from the State of 30 percent of new state tax revenues for 20 years. Their
expansion is estimated to generate $31.25 million in local TIF by 2047. The proposed incentive to be offered to Williams
International is $27.5 million structured as follows:
               • Direct post-performance incentive
                        o $17M to support electrical infrastructure improvements
                        o $5M for job creation and job training activities
               • Public infrastructure investment
                        o $5.5M for new 24” waterline loop from airport well
          For a copy of Ms. Meess’ presentations in their entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Chair White referenced the information in the presentation regarding labor and other operating costs being higher in

business recruitment project; Ogden is still more affordable than other states in terms of operating a business, but many states in
the southern part of the country are much more competitive in terms of labor, operating costs, and utility costs.

Proposed Resolution 2025-5 authorizing the Executive Director to execute a
real estate purchase contract to purchase real property from Weber
Housing Authority located at 2325 Madison Avenue
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed real estate purchase
contract (REPC) for 2325 Madison Avenue came before the Board for consideration. The memo stated the Board will consider a
resolution authorizing the Executive Director to execute a REPC with Weber Housing Authority (WHA) for purchase of the
Aspen Cove assisted living facility at 2325 Madison Avenue. The purchase price of $2.2 Million represents the appraised value of
the property plus verified expenses that WHA has expended on the project.
          Community Development Director Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
summarize the proposed property acquisition; the Quality Neighborhood Initiative is the basis for the property acquisition and the
property and building could potentially be used as an infill project with up to 12 lots. The appraised value of the property is $2
million, and the negotiated sales price is $2.2 million; this takes into account WHA’s expenses on the property to date. If the RDA
approves the REPC this evening, the due diligence period will expire on March 20, 2025, and the transaction can close on March
28, 2025.
          Board member Choberka indicated she is aware that some residents have reached out to City Administration regarding
potentially identifying the subject property as a historic landmark and she asked Mr. Smith if he has a position on that. Mr. Smith
stated he is aware a formal application has been submitted, but he has limited understanding of the process for historic
preservation. He is aware that the application will need to be submitted to the Landmarks Commission and ultimately the City
Council. Board member Choberka inquired as to the timeline for that process. Mr. Smith stated he is unaware, but the timeline
does not follow the timeline for the execution of the REPC and closing on the property. Chair White wondered if the Board should
delay action on the proposed REPC until clear information regarding the application for historic preservation of the property is
available. Community and Economic Development Executive Director Johnson stated it is his understanding the application was
filed today, well after the proposal regarding the REPC was submitted to the Board. He deferred to the City Attorney to provide
information regarding any conflicts between the two actions and the timing of such. City Attorney Williams stated he does not
have the facts of the matter for purposes of analysis, but he does not know of anything that would stop the Board from approving
the proposed resolution tonight. Mr. Smith added that the due diligence period will provide the City additional time to explore the
historic preservation application. Acting Chair Hyer added that it is his understanding that the proposed REPC was being ‘fast-
tracked’ in order to provide the WHA with a clear resolution to this matter and he also does not see any reasons to delay the
current proposal. Executive Director Nadolski agreed; the most damaging thing that could be done tonight would be tabling the
proposal because that would cause additional problems for the WHA. He would prefer that the Board and the Agency move
forward in good faith at this time.
          Board member Choberka stated she feels she needs to declare a conflict of interest; she provided some information
regarding her experience related to this complicated issue and living in the neighborhood where the subject property is located.
She supported the WHA project because it was developed by experts and would serve a particular population that she has worked
very closely with over several years. The population consists of mentally or physically disabled individuals that are unhoused or
have a challenge becoming stably housed. People in her neighborhood have asked her over the course of her years of service to do
something to help those who are living unsheltered in the community. She has considered many options for solving the problem
and she has always communicated to the public that the City is working on the matter, but when there was a recent ballot initiative
to expand the Weber County jail to provide additional space, the community voted no. She does not condone everything that was
in the County’s proposal, but it would have been a start towards creating some mental health support at the jail. She also wants to
represent the renters in the community that the City has heard from; several of the people who would be a good fit for the WHA
project have been taken advantage of by their friends and families in their current housing situations. Because the City is not
supporting the WHA project, those people will continue to live in unstable housing in the East Central neighborhood. Until a new
location for the WHA project is found, problems will continue because there is nowhere else in the County to house the
individuals who would have received support. On the topic of the proposed REPC, she plans to recuse herself from voting because
she does not feel she can vote on the continuation of a program – the Quality Neighborhood Initiative – that benefits her directly.
She appreciates the idea of wanting to do good and having the best intentions for the community when the Initiative was initially
created, but over the past decade as she and her family have worked very hard to restore a historic home on Jefferson Avenue and
having chosen to live there for a variety of reasons; the value of her home has increased substantially because of the success of the
Initiative. After receiving the most recent appraisal of her property, she found it had increased by over three times its original
value, or $600,000. This means her property tax bill has increased, but that is not too big of a deal for her based upon her own
professional circumstances. However, most others in her neighborhood have not experienced that kind of change in income,
though their property values have increased. The Quality Neighborhood Initiative has worked and made it possible for many to
achieve home ownership, but she has also wondered about those that have been displaced over the years by the same Initiative.
She asked when enough will be enough; when will the need to artificially increase property values be enough. The Board has
heard that the next neighborhoods to be the focus on the Initiative are on the northern and western sides of the community and she
hopes the Agency will ensure that those already living in those neighborhoods will benefit directly from the Agency’s efforts. She
stated her street has changed over the years, having gone from 95 percent renter occupied to 90 percent owner-occupied single-
family homes. Some of those homes have returned to rentals in the same decade and that is because some homeowners have been
able to purchase additional properties and now rent them out. She has even speculated that these people have been able to leverage
their investment in her neighborhood to make this possible. This makes her realize that she is benefiting from the efforts of the
Initiative in a disproportionate way, and she cannot, in good conscience, condone the rhetoric that vilifies renters, those who live
below the poverty level, or those who do not have a stable home. Some may argue that property owners have taken on the risk of
investing in real estate, so they deserve to gain financially, but they are already benefiting from the efforts of the City.

Executive Director Comments
         Executive Director Nadolski expressed his support for all individuals to speak their mind and provide their point of view,
even when that means he is being criticized for his actions or decisions; he understands that many in the community are struggling
and he has tried to focus on the things he can control, which includes how he responds to situations. He referenced Mr. Wayne’s
comments about the conditions along his road; he has reviewed an aerial map of the area Mr. Wayne lives in and found wetlands
surrounding his home. He also personally surveyed the areas with standing water along 1200 West and did not find any mosquito
larvae there. His conclusion is that the mosquitos are coming from the natural wetlands in the area. Executive Director Nadolski
indicated he has spent every day of his job living up to the commitments Ogden City has made, and he continues to work with
Weber Area Council of Governments (WACOG) and leaders in Marriott-Slaterville on transportation projects that would address
Mr. Wayne’s concerns. He referenced an article from the Standard-Examiner covering the meeting during which the Weber
County Commission allocated WACOG transportation funding to the Marriott-Slaterville project. He suggested that Mr. Wayne
address his own city elected officials if he does not like what is happening on that road. He concluded he has spoken with Mr.
Wayne so many times and no longer finds those conversations constructive. He then referenced the acquisition of 2325 Madison
Avenue and the City’s continued efforts to improve the neighborhood around the property; he firmly believes other cities in the
County need to assume some responsibility for providing supportive housing. He stated that some cities have stepped up, while
others have not, but it is not Ogden’s responsibility to bear the entire burden of supporting the homeless population of the entire
County. He has had many tough conversations with many in the community about this issue and he apologized if the tone of his
comments caused pain to others. He then briefly invited Deputy Public Services Assistant Executive Director/City Engineer
Nielsen to provide the Board with an update regarding recent dirty water conditions associated with the Ogden Canyon Waterline
Project and bringing the treatment plan online. Mr. Neilsen stated there was some heavily discolored water last Thursday and it
was evaluated to determine the source of the issue. The problem has been resolved and public information about the situation has
been published.
         Executive Director Nadolski then concluded by reporting the City has hired a homeless medical advocate, who has made
a great impact on the community.
