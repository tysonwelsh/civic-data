Planning Manager, Caitlyn Tubbs, presented the Staff Report and stated that Ordinance 2025-15 was
a request to amend the Development Agreement between Perry Investments, LLC, and Bluffdale for
The Centrum, and Ordinance 2025-16 was to amend Section 11.110.130 SD-X “The Centrum at
Bluffdale” Zone.
The proposed changes included the following:
e The addition of 66 units to the entitled apartment count.
e One additional story, for a total of five unit levels and two parking levels, and a maximum
height of 90 feet.
e A reduced parking ratio of 1.6 stalls per unit.
The Centrum is located at the intersection of Porter Rockwell Boulevard and 14600 South. The
Rockwell Ridge Business Park is east of the subject property, and the Marketplace at Independence
housing development is to the south. The Site Plan was reviewed, indicating existing retail space for
businesses such as Domino’s Pizza, Starbucks, and Jersey Mike’s. The Applicant was ready to begin
development of the approximately three-acre residential portion of the project.
The original Centrum Development Agreement and SD-X Zone were adopted on January 12, 2022.
A total of 236 units were entitled at that time. The agreement and zone were amended on October 12,
2022, to revise the architectural design guidelines and add 16 units, for a total of 252 units. The new
application requested an additional 66 units, which would bring the unit count to 318.

Approved and proposed elevations were reviewed. No changes were proposed to the architectural
style or materials. The amended elevations include one additional level of dwelling units for a total
of five, in addition to the two structured parking levels.
A total of 252 units were entitled. At the required parking ratio of two stalls per unit, the required
number of stalls was 504. The current standard would require 636 parking stalls for the proposed 318
units and the applicant requested approval of 509 stalls. In support of the deviation, the applicant
submitted studies of their other developments in Utah and neighboring states that indicate the average
number of cars per adult is 0.79. At the Planning Commission’s direction, Staff reviewed parking
standards for neighboring cities and determined that Bluffdale’s standards for multifamily
developments are in line with those communities. The Planning Commission held a public hearing
on June 4, 2025, and forwarded a positive recommendation to the City Council for approval of both
ordinances.
Staff found that:

e Development Agreements and Ordinance Amendments are legislative items for which the
Council has broad discretion.

e The additional units would benefit the City’s annual housing report as approval would add
higher-density housing near a future job center.

e The Utah Department of Transportation ("UDOT") will install a traffic signal at the
intersection of Porter Rockwell Boulevard and Rising Star Way once the development is
completed.

With regard to parking, Mr. Finlinson noted that it is in their best interest as the owner and operator
of both the retail and multifamily projects to ensure adequate parking, and all their retail leases include
that requirement. Perry Commercial typically holds a property for over 25 years, so they must
consider both what works now and in the future. They analyzed their current apartment portfolio to
determine how much parking each project provides, as well as how many parking stalls are actually
utilized. Mr. Finlinson noted that the other projects are suburban and include a different mixture of
units than The Centrum. Because The Centrum is Bluffdale’s extension of The Point, they believe it
will be more walkable. It will also provide a different unit mix from their other projects, which are
primarily one- and two-bedroom units.
To analyze parking requirements, several factors were considered. Their existing portfolio does not
include studio apartments and averages 1.4 cars and 1.57 adults per unit. The Centrum project will
consist of 24% studio, 46% one-bedroom, and 30% two-bedroom units. Parking requirements were
calculated by considering portfolio averages and the actual unit mix in the project, as well as rumored
parking at The Point. The requested 1.6 stalls per unit exceeds the 471.50 stalls indicated by their
analysis.
Additionally, because Perry Commercial also owns the retail center, parking requirements can be
enforced if necessary. The apartments will have assigned stalls in the parking structure and all tenants
will be required to register their vehicles with the leasing office. Minor overflow surface parking will
be available near the leasing office for potential tenants and guests. A Shared Parking Agreement can
be put in place with the retail development, which will have peak demand between the hours of
9:00 AM and 5:00 PM Peak demand for the multifamily development will be between 5:00 PM and
9:00 AM. In the event of conflict, Perry Commercial is confident in its ability to enforce parking
requirements.
Mr. Finlinson reported that UDOT acquired a 39-foot portion of the corner lot for a new right-of-way.

developments and their associated traffic studies, they have noticed that the traffic studies indicate
the need for fewer parking stalls than City Code requires. Traffic impact and parking analyses will
also be required as part of the Site Plan application, so the Council will be able to garner additional
information at that time.

was comfortable with the proposed parking. Perry Commercial does not want parking problems, and
parking will also be important to the type of tenants they want to attract.
