Mayor’s Budget Message for FY 2027 Budget Presented to City Council May 5, 2026
A Commitment to Disciplined Stewardship
I want to thank the citizens of Logan, the City Council, and our dedicated City departments for their
collaboration in establishing this balanced budget for Fiscal Year 2027.
Budgeting is the art of prioritizing limited resources. While we may not have every resource we desire,
we are committed to maximizing the impact of every dollar we do have. In our General Fund—which
supports essential services like Police, Fire, Public Works, and Parks—we continue to utilize Target
Based Budgeting. This collaborative approach provides departments the flexibility to address their
most critical needs while remaining strictly aligned with the strategic objectives set by the Mayor and
Council.
Economic Outlook and Revenue Strategy
While we remain hopeful that current local and national economic uncertainties will stabilize, we are
projecting that sales tax revenue— our General Fund’s most significant revenue source, will remain
relatively flat for FY 2027.
To balance this volatility, we rely on property tax as a vital stabilizing force. It is important to clarify
a common misconception: property tax revenues do not automatically rise alongside increases to
property values. Unless the City adjusts the levy through the "Truth in Taxation" process, the total
revenue from existing taxpayers remains flat.
We believe that small, incremental adjustments are better policy than implementing large, disruptive
rate corrections. Therefore, we are proposing a 3% increase to the property tax levy. This modest
adjustment is specifically earmarked for two critical needs:
   1. Public Safety: Funding one additional Police Officer position.
   2. Infrastructure: Covering the rising costs of citywide street lighting.
Strengthening Essential Operations
To maintain the high level of service our residents expect, this budget proposes strategic additions to
our workforce:
   •   Police Department: As mentioned above, we propose funding one police officer position
       through a property tax increase.

Councilmember Dahle asked when questions regarding the budget would be most appropriately
addressed.

Mr. Anderson responded that detailed budget discussions and questions would likely be best suited for
the upcoming budget workshops scheduled for Tuesday, May 12 and May 26. He added that
Councilmembers are welcome to ask questions during regular meetings as well.

Councilmember Simmonds asked for clarification regarding proposed staffing additions in the budget
and inquired whether any reductions or cuts were being considered to offset the added positions,
noting that the proposed property tax increase represented the primary source of additional revenue.

Mr. Anderson clarified that several of the proposed position increases were funded through enterprise
or dedicated funds rather than the General Fund. He explained that the additional 911 positions would
be funded through 911 service charges paid by other cities, while sewer treatment positions would be
funded through utility revenues.

Mr. Anderson further explained that the primary General Fund position under discussion was an
additional police officer position funded through property tax revenues. A fire department position
would be funded through targeted budgeting allocations and partially offset through reductions in part-
time staffing, resulting in consolidation of positions.

Regarding potential budget reductions, he noted that departments prioritize expenditures each year due
to limited funding availability, but no major elimination of services or programs have been identified.

Councilmember Simmonds requested clarification regarding discussion of a proposed police officer
position, questioning how a position that had not yet been added could be described as being
“eliminated.”

Mr. Anderson explained that the wording referenced was required statutory language associated with
the Truth in Taxation process. The City is required to identify the department for which the proposed
property tax increase would be used and describe the operational impact if the increase is not
approved. The proposed property tax increase would fund an additional police officer position, and if
the increase were not approved, the City would be unable to fund and add that position.

Councilmember Simmonds asked how funds previously allocated toward airport-related expenses
were being utilized within the current budget.

Mr. Anderson responded that the funds were not redirected to a specific item but instead remained
within the General Fund and were absorbed into overall General Fund operations and expenditures. He
added that the expense had simply been removed from the expenditure side of the budget.

Councilmember Simmonds asked for additional clarification regarding approximately $100,000 in
airport-related expenditures that had been removed from the budget.

Mr. Anderson explained that revenues and expenditures were calculated proportionally across
departments within the General Fund, and that without the removal of the airport expense, departments
collectively would have received approximately $100,000 less in funding allocations. He added that
the proportional allocation breakdown is available and could be provided to the Council for review.

Councilmember Dahle requested further explanation regarding proposed funding for lighting
improvements, questioning the prioritization of lighting expenditures in relation to other budget
considerations such as employee wages and retention of police personnel.

Mr. Anderson explained that the General Fund supports a wide range of core municipal services,
including public safety (police and fire), public works, parks and recreation, community development,
transportation infrastructure, and street lighting.

He clarified that street lighting is also a General Fund expense and is provided through the City’s
electric utility system. Unlike typical utility customers, most streetlights are not individually metered;
instead, they are connected directly to the City’s electrical infrastructure. The electric department
estimates the annual energy and maintenance costs associated with street lighting, and the General
Fund reimburses those costs through an internal accounting allocation rather than monthly utility
billing.

Mr. Anderson noted that this arrangement ensures the City accounts for the full cost of providing street
lighting services, even though usage is not individually metered.

Councilmember Lee-Koven asked for clarification regarding the approximately $54,000 allocated for
street lighting costs, inquiring whether the amount was based on a cost study and anticipated usage or
metering assumptions. She also requested a year-over-year comparison of the street lighting line item
within the General Fund and asked whether the current year reflected an increase in costs.

She further asked, if there was an increase, what factors were driving the higher cost, such as
additional lighting needs, expanded infrastructure, or changes in energy usage or rates.

Mr. Anderson explained that the street lighting cost study is updated approximately every three to four
years, rather than annually due to the complexity of the analysis. In the interim years, the City makes
incremental adjustments based on known utility rate increases.

He noted that the current increase in the street lighting allocation is driven by several factors, including
utility rate increases over the past several years, the identification of additional streetlights that were
not previously accounted for in earlier studies, and new streetlights added through recent development
and growth.

Mr. Anderson further explained that in the prior study period, the estimated cost was approximately
$216,000, and the updated estimate is approximately $290,000. The increase reflects both updated
usage data and expanded infrastructure over the intervening four-to-five-year period.

Mr. Anderson noted that there has been a change in personnel within the Electric Department since the
prior street lighting cost study was completed, and the staff member who previously conducted the
analysis is no longer with the City. Staff explained that new personnel were involved in updating the
study and reviewing the calculations.

He stated that the study was reviewed multiple times internally to verify accuracy due to the
significant increase in estimated costs. He acknowledged concern over the approximate 25% increase
and confirmed that the figures were rechecked to ensure they were correct and reflected current
conditions, including updated rates and system data.

Councilmember Lee-Koven suggested that the City may benefit from a future discussion regarding
street lighting technology and energy efficiency, noting that when cost increases are identified, it may
be worthwhile to evaluate whether more cost-effective or efficient lighting solutions are available. She
stated that while the current discussion focused on budget figures, a separate review of lighting
technology and potential improvements in efficiency could be valuable for long-term cost
management.

Councilmember Simmonds asked for clarification regarding what happened to previously budgeted
funds that had been used to cover portions of street lighting costs under prior accounting methods.

Mr. Anderson responded that those amounts had been reduced in the corresponding budgets as the
costs were consolidated into a single accounting structure for street lighting. The net increase in
funding was approximately $80,000, with $54,000 of that being requested through property tax
revenue. He confirmed those figures and explained the relationship between the consolidated costs and
the requested funding source.

Councilmember Lee-Koven noted it was her first budget process and acknowledged the importance of
understanding what issues should be discussed in the current meeting versus future budget sessions.

Mr. Anderson responded that each department would be reviewed individually during budget
meetings, and additional opportunities for questions would be available both during department
presentations and at the conclusion of the budget process.

A Councilmember noted for public context that Logan City represents approximately 12% of the
overall property tax burden paid by residents and suggested that this information may be helpful for
public understanding of the proposed budget and property tax discussion.

The Councilmember also referenced prior year budget materials comparing property tax rates among
similarly sized municipalities in Utah, noting that Logan City’s rate compares favorably and is
relatively low in comparison. The Councilmember stated that such comparative data is useful for
understanding the City’s position in a broader statewide context.

Mr. Anderson commented on ongoing budget pressures, specifically identifying the rising cost of
employee healthcare as a significant and recurring factor affecting baseline budget expenditures. He
indicated these broader context items may be helpful for public communication and understanding of
the budget discussion.

Mr. Anderson explained the procedural requirements associated with the Truth in Taxation process
and the proposed property tax impact schedule. He noted that if the Council wished to decrease the
proposed property tax rate, it would be permissible under current notice; however, if the Council
intended to significantly change or increase the proposal, the City would likely be required to re-notice
the hearing and restart portions of the process prior to the statutory deadline.

He clarified that changes to the proposal could be made within a limited timeframe, but that substantial
revisions particularly those altering the identified funding uses would likely require additional noticing
and reprocessing. Mr. Anderson also explained that while the public hearing remains a required step in
the process, adjustments to the proposal may still be possible depending on the Council’s final
direction and timing.

Council discussion followed regarding the interpretation of “substantial changes,” procedural
requirements, and the flexibility of adjusting funding allocations (such as shifting between public
safety positions).

Mr. Anderson acknowledged the complexity of the statutory framework and noted that the process can
be procedurally challenging due to state requirements and the structure of municipal governance.

He added that further discussion and clarification would continue during the upcoming budget
meetings.

Linda Johnson of Logan requested clarification regarding the proposed Center Street safety bollards.

Mayor Anderson clarified that the proposed Center Street safety bollards are not fully retractable
systems. Instead, they are removable bollards intended to allow for controlled access while
maintaining flexibility for operational needs. He noted that fully retractable systems were considered
but were not feasible due to cost constraints.

Mayor Anderson further explained that the bollards are intended to enhance public safety during large
downtown events by preventing unauthorized vehicle access into closed portions of Center Street. The
system would reduce the need for a large police presence currently required to monitor for potential
vehicle entry into event areas.

Staff further clarified that the bollards are permanently installed in the roadway but can be inserted or
removed as needed for event control. When not in use, they would be stored at the Public Works
facility. The system is designed specifically for the Center Street corridor and requires installation
infrastructure in the pavement to ensure stability and effectiveness.

There were no further comments and Chair Johnson closed the public hearing.

Consideration of a proposed resolution designating a Closed Area to Ignition or Use of
Fireworks – Resolution 26-14 – Robert Lacroix, Assistant Chief of Operations

Assistant Chief Robert Lacroix addressed the Council and presented Resolution 26-14 regarding the
annual designation of restricted areas for fireworks use within the City. He noted that the resolution
includes the same restricted area map used in previous years.

Assistant Chief Lacroix explained that the Fire Department plans to increase public outreach and
social media messaging beginning in mid-June to educate residents on fireworks restrictions and fire
danger. He emphasized that, given current conditions including low snowpack, reduced moisture
levels, and ongoing drought concerns, the City is anticipating an elevated wildfire risk this season.

In response to questions, Chief Lacroix clarified that while the State regulates the sale of fireworks,
municipalities are only able to restrict their use in designated areas and cannot prohibit sales. He
reiterated that restricted zones remain consistent with prior years and that no changes to those

Councilmembers raised questions regarding whether any adjustments to restricted areas could be
considered, and staff confirmed that the current boundaries would remain in place.

Chair Johnson asked, have we considered allowing “ground only” fireworks.

Assistant Chief Lacroix confirmed that the City has not considered allowing “ground only” fireworks
in restricted areas, noting that expanding allowances could lead to increased use of aerial fireworks
and additional enforcement challenges.

Councilmembers discussed recurring concerns from residents regarding fireworks activity in
neighborhoods near restricted zones, particularly during the Fourth of July period.

Assistant Chief Lacroix reiterated that fireworks restrictions remain necessary due to prior incidents
and ongoing enforcement challenges within restricted areas.

Assistant Fire Chief Lacroix also noted his long tenure in fire service and shared that this would be his
final fire season prior to retirement on June 5. He emphasized continued concern about wildfire risk
this year due to dry conditions and an anticipated active fire season.

The proposed resolution will be an action item at the May 19, 2026 Council meeting.
