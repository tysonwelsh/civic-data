3.     Text Amendment - Transit Station Overlay Infill Projects; Amend the 2009 City Code
       adding Section 13-6G-18 permitting limited flexibility for small infill developments in the
       Transit Station Overlay District; City-wide applicability; City of West Jordan (applicant)
       [#22442]

Mark Forsythe said the proposal is a result of back and forth discussions with the City Council, staff,
and the Jordan Fields developer due to conflicting interpretations of the ordinance. In order to move
things forward, the land use subcommittee agreed to changes that allow the City Council to have
limited flexibility in applying some of the ordinances in the TOD. The ordinance was originally
designed for larger, multi-phased transit-oriented developments such as Jordan Valley Station TOD.
The ordinance allows the City Council to waive specific ordinances for small infill of seven acres or
less within the TOD. The second section provides boundaries and guidelines, suggested by the City
Council, as to what the waiver does not apply to. Larger properties cannot be split into smaller pieces
of less than seven acres with the purpose of obtaining a waiver. The third section says that exemptions
are only granted on the individual merit of the project and cannot be used as a precedent for other
properties. Fourthly, it requires that the modified sections shall be listed in the master development
agreement. He showed a map of parcels within the TSODs. About half of the parcels with development
potential in the TSODs would be affected by this ordinance, which is about 70 acres.

Matt Quinney opened the public hearing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

It was clarified that obtaining a waiver doesn’t change any of the procedural steps in the approval
process regarding public hearings, etc. It is just to provide flexibility for parcels less than seven acres
that may not fit the terms of the current ordinance.

Jay Thomas was concerned that it will be an end-run for some developers to get around the zoning
laws.

Kent Shelton said a developer can’t subdivide a property into smaller parcels for the purpose of getting
an exemption. He asked if it is subdivided and then sold to a new owner, would that owner
automatically become eligible for the exceptions or would they be disqualified.

Larry Gardner said the key phrase in the text is that the ordinance ‘may be’ waived by the City
Council. The Council and staff will be able to determine if a property has been subdivided
intentionally for that purpose. The applicant will have to convince the City Council of why it is a good
idea to waive the requirements. It is not an automatic approval.

Corbin England felt that it is well thought out and there are built-in protections.

MOTION:        Corbin England moved, based upon the information and findings set forth in the
               staff report, and upon the evidence and explanations received today to forward a
               positive recommendation to the City Council adding 13-6G-18 to the 2009 City
               Code which permits the City Council to allow limited flexibility to small infill
               developments within the TSOD. The motion was seconded by Kent Shelton and
               passed 6-1 in favor with Jay Thomas casting the negative vote.

4.    Text Amendment - Amend the 2009 City Code Section 13-5C of the Planned Development
      Zones regarding open space requirements, Master Development Agreement requirements
      and residential density; City-wide applicability; City of West Jordan (applicant) [#22440]

Ray McCandless said this text amendment was initiated by the City Council to allow for flexibility to
waive certain open space and density requirements in the planned development zones.

13-5C-2H allows the density in the PRD zones to be modified by 15% of the overall density
requirement. The open space requirements in the P-C Zone can be reduced from the required 20%
open space to a minimum of 17% gross area requirement. Any modification or waivers shall have
specific findings from the Council that it is in the best interest of the City. Any waiver or modification
only applies to the specific development and does not set a precedent for future development.

13-5C-2B removes the WSPA references and the requirement for a master development agreement.
The master development plan is still required for a zone change to a planned development designation
and must be part of the complete application to the Planning Commission prior to consideration by the
City Council.

Larry Gardner clarified that in order for the Council to approve a modification, they have to make the
finding that it is in the best interest of the city. The Planning Commission is a recommending body,
but they will be reviewing the items more intently in the future with this amendment.

Matt Quinney suggested that they could dissect a master development plan as part of a training topic in
the future.

Matt Quinney opened the public hearing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

MOTION:        Trish Hatch moved to forward a positive recommendation to the City Council to
               Amend the 2009 City Code Section 13-5C of the Planned Development Zones
               regarding open space requirements, master development agreement requirements
               and residential density. The motion was seconded by Pamela Bloom and passed 7-0
               in favor.

5.    Text Amendment – Development Procedures; Amend the 2009 City Code Section 13-7C
      and 13-7D as it relates to the 20-day provision; City-wide applicability; City of West
      Jordan (applicant) [#22460]

Alan Anderson, Council Director, said there are four sections of code that require a time delay between
a Planning Commission recommendation and the City Council meeting. The Council wants to put the
full responsibility on the developer to have a complete packet for the Planning Commission that will
then be sent to the City Council. Section 13-7C-4 has two notes that the Council feels can be deleted.
The Council will set timelines within their policies and procedures, which already has the requirement
Planning & Zoning Commission Meeting Minutes
October 20, 2020
Page 7

for a complete packet. Section 13-7C-5C deletes items 3 and 4 regarding timing. Section 13-7D-4
deletes the same notes and Section 7D-5B2 is deleted regarding the 20-day spacing between Planning
Commission and City Council meetings. The idea is that the Planning Commission meeting will not be
held until there is a full packet. The process will then continue to the City Council with all of the same
information. He said there have been times when the Council doesn’t have all of the materials to make
a final decision, so this puts the responsibility on the applicant to have all of the information prior to
the Planning Commission meeting.

Duncan Murray said the main Council rule is that a complete packet is required ten business days or
two-weeks prior to the meeting.

Alan Anderson said the council office would like to provide the City Council with enough time and
information to read and ask clarifying questions of staff ahead of time so that they can make a good
decision for the City. The Council feels that the Commission represents the recommendation of the
executive branch of government to the legislative body, and it is hard to have a full recommendation if
all the information is not provided from the beginning.

Larry Gardner wanted to make it clear that the Development Department does not accept incomplete
applications as defined by State Law. In the past, the Planning Commission was given enough and
adequate information to make a recommendation. The City Council now wants to see everything
completed before they look at it, rather than just having enough information to make a decision. So, the
Planning Commission will be seeing more complete information than they had in the past.

Duncan Murray agreed that this amendment is talking about what the City Council considers to be a
complete packet.

Matt Quinney opened the public hearing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

It was pointed out that the text shown tonight was an updated version that reflects the City Council’s
comments at their work session. It was presented to the public tonight and can be voted on.

Ammon Allen said if they recommend Option B, they need to delete the superscript 1 and 2 in the table
heading on both charts. He asked if there is a definition of ‘soonest available meeting’.

Duncan Murray said that is for the City Council and staff to determine based on the agenda. The
interpretation is to give every consideration not to put it off, but to keep things moving.

Ammon Allen asked if they need to make a definition of what a complete packet is.

Larry Gardner said they will make sure that all requirements of the checklist have gone through the full
review of city staff and it is ready to proceed prior to the Planning Commission meeting. This will put
more pressure on the developer to have all of the information provided.

Kent Shelton asked what kind of impact this will have on developers if the Planning Commission
wants something reworked before forwarding an application.
