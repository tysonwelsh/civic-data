PROCEDURE FOR PUBLIC COMMENT

1.   Any person or entity may appear in person or be represented by an authorized agent at any meeting of the
     Commission.

2.   Unless altered by the Chair, the order of the procedure on an application shall be:

     a. The supporting agency staff will introduce the application, including staff’s recommendations and a
        summary of pertinent written comments and reports concerning the application

     b. The applicant will be allowed up to 15 minutes to make their presentation.

     c. The Community Council representative can present their comments as applicable.

     d. Where applicable, persons in favor of, or not opposed to, the application will be invited to speak.

     e. Where applicable, persons opposing the application, in whole or in part will be invited to speak.

     f. Where applicable, the applicant will be allowed 5 minutes to provide concluding statements.

     g. Surrebutals may be allowed at the discretion of the Chair.

CONDUCT FOR APPLICANTS AND THE PUBLIC

1.   Speakers will be called to the podium by the Chair.

2.   Each speaker, before talking, shall give his or her name and address.

3.   All comments should be directed to the Commissioners, not to the staff or to members of the audience.

4.   For items where there are several people wishing to speak, the Chair may impose a time limit, usually 3
     minutes per person, or 5 minutes for a group spokesperson. If a time limit is imposed on any member or
     spokesperson of the public, then the same time limit is imposed on other members or spokespersons of the
     public, respectively.

5.   Unless otherwise allowed by the Chair, no questions shall be asked by the speaker or Commission Members.

6.   Only one speaker is permitted before the Commission at a time.

7.   The discussion must be confined to essential points stated in the application bearing on the desirability or
     undesirability of the application.

8.   The Chair may cease any presentation or information that has already been presented and acknowledge that it
     has been noted in the public record.

9.   No personal attacks shall be indulged in by either side, and such action shall be sufficient cause for stopping the
     speaker from proceeding.

10. No applause or public outbursts shall be permitted.

11. The Chair or supporting agency staff may request police support to remove offending individuals who refuse to
    abide by these rules.

12. After the public comment portion of a meeting or hearing has concluded, the discussion will be limited to the
    Planning Commission and Staff.

4.0 Introduction
Importance of Transportation
While the land use element lays out patterns of uses across Emigration Canyon Metro Township, the
transportation element examines how people access these uses. Transportation involves both mobility –
the ability to travel between places – and accessibility – the ability to reach valuable destinations within
a reasonable amount of time. For Emigration Canyon Metro Township, transportation planning is
important to uphold a safe, accessible, and enjoyable transportation system that enables residents to
meet their needs in the ways that work for them.


State Code
                               The State of Utah requires communities to plan for transportation. In
                               Utah State Code Section 10-9a-403, the State mandates that General
                               Plans include a “transportation and traffic circulation element” (UT
                               State Code). This element must contain the location of different road
                               types as well as public and active transportation infrastructure, and it
                               must connect to demographic projections and the land use element.
Communities without a major transit investment corridor – such as Emigration Canyon – must plan for
improved linkages among housing, employment, education, recreation, and commerce.


Transportation Planning Values
Values are beliefs about desirable states or ways of acting that guide behavior and prioritization. Well-
regarded values-based transportation frameworks include the Complete Streets framework, which seeks
to “ensure that the entire right-of-way is planned, designed, constructed, operated, and maintained to
provide safe access for all users” (Smart Growth America & National Complete Streets Coalition, 2015). In
this framework, successful transportation planning incorporates the values of safety, comfort, and
convenience in transportation systems for all users.

To be the most effective, values-based frameworks benefit from being tailored to reflect local values and
needs. In January 2020, 5 representatives from Emigration Canyon Metro Township participated in a
Transportation Planning Values and Principles workshop along with other representatives from MSD-
member communities. Emigration Canyon accounted for just under 16 percent of workshop attendees.
At this workshop, 32 members of MSD-community Planning Commissions, Councils, General Plan Steering
Committees, and several involved members of the public developed a list of transportation values broadly
applicable across the MSD. The top transportation values generated at this workshop included (see Figure
X for a complete list of the top values):

    •   Clean air
    •   Safe, user friendly streets for all types of users
    •   Aligning local and regional needs
    •   Ongoing engagement of stakeholders in local decision making
    •   Commitment to an outcomes-based approach to planning, design, and engineering and
    •   Good health (of individuals and communities, mental and physical).                                     Commented [BT1]: Wouldn’t it be easier to just make a

Utah Transit Authority
Utah Transit Authority (UTA) “provides integrated mobility solutions to service life’s connections, improve
public health and enhance quality of life”; they “provide an integrated system of innovative, accessible
and efficient public transportation services that increase access to opportunities and contribute to a
healthy environment for the people of the Wasatch region” (UTA). Currently, there are no public transit
routes in Emigration Canyon other than the Granite School District’s school bus route. However, any
future efforts to explore public transit options will necessitate coordination with UTA regarding public
transit options. Communicating feedback about possible route pathways, infrastructure conditions, and
frequency of stops cab be a useful way to engage UTA.

Workshops and Surveys
In order to elicit additional input on broad transportation planning needs across the MSD member
communities, a Transportation Planning Values and Principles Workshop was held on January 30, 2020.
This event was also open to and attended by several members of the public. At this event, five Emigration
Canyon representatives helped shape the broad transportation values of the MSD (discussed in section x
above, see Figure x for some of the key results of this workshop, or Appendix x – Report on the Workshop
for additional details).

A Transportation and Land Use Survey was also disseminated to Emigration Canyon residents to get a
better idea of the specific needs, concerns, and values associated with the area. The survey was sent to
the MTC, PC, and GPSC on April 13th, 2020, posted on the MSD website, shared on MSD social media with
targeted advertising, and posted on the Emigration Canyon Metro Township website. This survey utilized
Survey 1-2-3 to ask MSD-area residents about their transportation and land use concerns and values in 14
questions. The questions included spatial and mapping components to allow for site-specific concerns and
opportunities to be shared. 97 Emigration Canyon residents completed the survey (additional details can
be found in Appendix x – Report on the Transportation and Land Use Survey). Some key survey results
relevant to the transportation element are summarized in the subsection that follows.

Table X shows the employment-to-population ratio and employee-to-housing-unit ratio for Emigration
Canyon and Salt Lake County for the years 2010 and 2018. The employment-to-population ratio is
calculated by dividing the number of employed civilians residing in a geography by the working-age
population of that same geography. Between 2010 and 2018, this ratio fell for Emigration Canyon, while
it remained steady for Salt Lake County (Table X). The decrease in the ratio tells us that between 2010 and
2018, the percentage of the working-age population participating in the civilian labor force fell.

The employee-to-housing-unit ratio is calculated by dividing the number of employees working in an area
by the number of occupied housing units in that same area. In the best scenarios, this ratio would fall
somewhere between 0.75 and 1.5, indicating a relative match between employees and housing units
(EnviroAtlas, 2014). The ratio for Emigration Canyon was 0.2 in 2018, indicating that there are fewer jobs

in the Canyon than there are housing units (2018). The ratio indicates that there are not enough jobs
available in the Metro Township to support all the households that live there – Emigration Canyon
residents must commute elsewhere for work. Salt Lake County had an employee-to-housing-unit ratio of
1.6 in 2018, slightly outside of the desired range (Table X). This number reveals that there are more people
employed in Salt Lake County than there are occupied housing units. In other words, people are
commuting to jobs in Salt Lake County from their residences outside of the county. This ratio has
implications for air quality, commuting patterns, road infrastructure, and housing demand.


Public Transportation
Currently, there are no public transit routes in Emigration Canyon other than the Granite School
District’s public school bus route (for current information see
https://www.graniteschools.org/transportation/).

Active Transportation
Emigration Canyon Road is popular with active transportation users and has the potential to be a top tier
destination for them (see Figure X showing active transportation corridor). The allure of sweeping views,
mild canyon grades, and proximity to downtown Salt Lake City has not escaped the attention of a growing
number of cyclists and runners, but some substantial adjustments to the road are needed to improve
safety for these diverse users. Sidewalks are non-existent in the canyon, as are other standard urban
infrastructure such as streetlights, benches and restrooms. Infrastructure hazards including unstable
slopes, encroaching residential structures, tight shoulders, and canyon curves bring design challenges that
urban bike design guides like NACTO and AASHTO do not address.

A facilitated collaborative planning effort to address many of these challenges was undertaken in 2014 by
the Emigration Canyon Roadway Improvement Committee (ECRIC), a group formed under the previous
planning authority at Salt Lake County. Safety within a mixed-use corridor was the overarching
consideration. The committee developed a range of recommendations for immediate action, including 79
specific roadway improvements (Lochner 2016). An evaluation of the engineering deficiencies throughout
the canyon, proposed costs for solutions recommended by the ECRIC, and additional engineering analysis,
was conducted following the release of these recommendations and compiled into the Lochner report
(2016). A summary of the findings from this report are included in the Next Steps section below (Table X).

Trails
Emigration Canyon is a natural playground for outdoor enthusiasts, but the existing infrastructure to
support these activities is minimal at best. While some informal trails exist in the canyon, these are
mostly very rough and unmaintained. Services such as toilets and parking are also mostly non-existent in
the canyon. This means that excessive use by visitors from out of the canyon can lead to erosion,
property damage and pollution. A Trails Master Plan was adopted in 2007 to address many of these
concerns, but little has been implemented since the adoption of the plan (Ref Plan, PPT by BT). More
details on trails are included in the land use section of this general plan.

18.08.010 Procedure Generally
The planning commission shall be the land use authority for subdivisions. In order to assure that each subdivision
fully complies with the provisions of this title, the director or director's designee shall administer formal
application and review procedures for subdivisions. An application shall not be deemed complete until the full
application, fees and all required materials have been submitted. The payment of a partial fee and submission of
preliminary plans for a pre-submittal review does not constitute a complete application.
Each process shall include the following components:

    1. An application procedure, which shall include:
           1. Submission of an application form, as designed by the director or director's designee to clearly
               indicate the type of application, property address, applicant information, and other pertinent
               information.
           2. Submission of supplementary materials, including a legal description, property plat, the required
               number of plans/preliminary plats, and mailing labels (if required) for notifications.
           3. Payment of fees, as required under Title 3, Revenue and Finance.
    2. A review procedure, which shall include:
           1. An on-site review by the director or director's designee as provided by Utah Code § 17-27a-303.
           2. Review of the submitted site plan/preliminary plat for compliance with county land use
               ordinances.

18.18.050 Other Amendments to Subdivisions
An amendment to a recorded subdivision that involves the alteration or removal of an easement, private right-
of-way, condition, limitation, or special requirement shall follow the approval procedure outlined in Section
18.08.010 with the following variations:

    1. Only those persons or entities who have a direct interest in, or who will be directly affected by the
       proposed change (including the applicant) must be notified of any pending action; and
    2. No preliminary plat need be approved. The recommendations of the affected entities and the approval of

between lots #3 and #2. The initial understanding of these easements allowed a single point of
egress for both future structures to enter Emigration Canyon Road. The design of these
easements also allowed each separate home owner to back out of their garage and onto the
neighboring property to successfully turn their vehicle around to enter forwardly onto Emigration
Canyon Road. The intent of the easements was correctly designed and kept the egress to
Emigration Road to a single point of entry between 2 lots.

easements of access were completely ignored. The driveway was installed East of the
easement with a private electronic gate blocking access to only the lot #3 owner and
landscaping was installed over co-joined easements.

At a later unknow date lot #3 was sold to new owners who had had no know understanding of
the issue until I was able to bring it to their attention in May of 2020.

This past mistake directly effects the owner of lot #2 as he/she will have no ability to access
egress to their land without either forcing the owner of lot #3 to remove mature landscaping and
hardscaping at no cost to the owner of lot #2 (and the help of the County requirements) OR
illegally creating a new access from Emigration Canyon Road to lot #2.

Completing and approving this plat amendment of removing the joint access easements allows
the owner of lot #2 and lot #3 to access Emigration Canyon Road completely separately and off
their own land.
