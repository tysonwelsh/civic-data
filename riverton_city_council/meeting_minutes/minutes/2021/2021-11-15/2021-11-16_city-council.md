     Tyler Technologies, Citizen Self-Service Portal – Maddie McCambridge, Account
     Executive or Tyler Technologies and Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, explained that Strategic Priority #7 is to “create

desire to have a single sign on for all residents to conduct business with the City. The package

Maddie McCambridge explained that Lisa Gerard would be showing the software and after the

portal.

         Benefit Analysis – Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director and Trish Dixon,
         Human Resource Manager

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, explained that he sent the benefit analysis
presentation to the Councilmembers and Mayor last week. This presentation is included with
the packet. He said that his staff focused on parental leave as asked by Councilmember McCay
and then considered other areas in the policy that potentially needed to be updated.

Mr. Hicks said that the City has FMLA leave which is unpaid for up to 12 weeks. This can be
supplemented with leave time and the City also has short term disability that can be used for the
birth of a child. He said that his staff collected data from 8 different cities nearby to compare.
He reported that half of the cities that responded had a caregiver leave benefit and said that this
benefit would help with the COVID-19 issue. He explained that during 2020 the City was able to
use relief funds to pay employees for time off due to COVID-19. These relief funds are now
expired and he believes adopting a caregiver leave benefit could help with parental leave as
well as sickness related to COVID-19. He said it is difficult to identify a cost to this benefit,
however, in analyzing the last five years and how many employees had events that would
qualify, he estimates it would be about $7,000.

Mr. Hicks explained that his staff provided an analysis of the leave time. He said that the City
currently has a use or lose personal leave time and they recommend leaving personal leave as
personal leave but convert any unused personal time to sick time at the end of the year. He
said he wanted to receive guidance from the Councilmembers as to where they would like the
City to be in comparison to other cities on vacation, sick time and personal leave.

In response to Councilmember McCay’s job sharing questions from a previous council meeting,
Mr. Hicks explained that the City is very flexible with employees but this depends on the
department and the job. He said his staff identified a list of positions that could be job shared
if/when the job was vacated. Mr. Hicks said that Riverton City is one of the only cities that does
not recognize Veterans Day and his staff recommends that the City adopt Veterans Day as a
paid holiday. He also said that his staff recommends increasing the cash in lieu benefit for
employees not on the insurance plan from $200 to $400.

Councilmember McCay thanked staff for their work in preparing this presentation. She asked a
couple of clarifying questions. She said she is supportive of the caregiver leave benefits and
the Veterans Day holiday.

Mr. Hicks asked for clarification about the personal leave benefit so that it could be implemented
before the end of the year. Councilmember Stewart said that he agrees with the Veterans Day
holiday, supports the increase to $400 for the cash-in-lieu benefit, and supports the rollover of
personal leave to sick time at the end of the year.

Councilmember Buroker thanked Councilmember McCay for bringing this issue up, thanked
staff for their work, and said that she was supportive of all the recommendations from staff and
especially appreciates the caregiver leave benefit.

Councilmember McDougal also thanked staff and asked if there was a cap on sick leave. Mr.
Hicks responded that there is no cap on sick leave, personal leave is use or lose and vacation is
capped at 160 hours. He also clarified that at retirement sick leave can be cashed out at 10%.
Councilmember McDougal said he was supportive of the caregiver leave, $400 cash-in-lieu, and
the Veterans Day holiday.

Discussion/Action Item

         Ordinance No. 21-28-Amending Section 18.45, RM-6, amending the RM-6 zone to
         individual lot development only, amendments proposed by Riverton City

Mr. Lethbridge explained that Riverton City currently has in its code the RM-6 Zone, which
allows for multi-family development at a maximum density of six units per acre. That code
allows for both attached units within commonly held open space, as is more typical with multi-
family, or subdivision of property into individual lots. In its application of this Zone, Riverton City
has repeatedly only approved it with a Specific Development designation limiting development
to individual lots. As such, staff is proposing the zone itself be amended to only allow
development utilizing individual lots. The Council clarified the previous proposed draft to also
limit the zone to detached units. This is reflected in the proposed amendment.

Mr. Lethbridge said that the standards for individual dwelling lots are already contained in this
section, and so the amendment is primarily deletion of the sections for attached units and
common open space developments.

Councilmember Buroker asked for clarification on whether this ordinance would negatively
affect any current applications. Mr. Lethbridge said that no current applications will be
negatively affected.

Councilmember Wells MOVED that the Riverton City Council adopt Ordinance No. 21-28-
Amending Section 18.45, RM-6, amending the RM-6 zone to individual lot development
only. Councilmember Buroker SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on
the motion. There being none, he called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: Buroker-
yes, McCay-yes, Stewart-yes, McDougal-yes, and Wells-yes. The motion passed unanimously.
