Reports from other Municipal Providers

Unified Fire Authority (UFA)

                Chief Dustin Dern reviewed call for service for January. The organization is
moving through COVID well, 91.8 percent of employees have been vaccinated. There are 86
individuals that have tested positive for COVID but are getting better. The strategic plan was
approved and that will guide the department for the next 3 years. Recruit camp 54 started on
February 1 with 36 new recruits. There are a few bills that will impact the organization and those
will continue to be watched.

                                −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Unified Police Department (UPD)

                Officer Jeff Fenton stated he is working with UFA on a criminal investigation for
the fire that was caused by arson. Fraud is a big risk right now; UPD will never call to have an
individual pay for something with a gift card. He made a map of all the water wells for the new
officers. The Safecam program is available for residents to register and it can be done on UPD’s
website.

               Chief Randy Thomas stated UPD is about two-thirds vaccinated. The Board is
working on a proposal for addressing salaries for ranks in the market.

Council Member Reports

Unified Fire Authority (UFA) & Unified Fire Service Area (UFSA)

                 Council Member Perry stated the UFA Board voted to keep himself as the Chair
and Mayor Peterson as Vice Chair. The strategic plan was presented and approved for the next
three years. It will help the organization move forward. The Board approved unused vacation
hours that are over the yearly limit to be rolled over to the next year. UFSA Chair and Vice Chair
will remain the same. The UFSA Board is working on the revenue bond to rebuild stations and
one new station. The withdraws from UFSA for Riverton and Herriman Cities are completed, the
cities will continue to contract with UFA directly.

                                 −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Unified Police Department (UPD) & Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA)

               Mayor Flint stated Mayor Jeff Silvestrini was appointed as Chair and Deputy
Mayor Erin Litvack was appointed as Vice Chair for the UPD Board. The 2021 meeting schedule
was adopted and will continue to be the third Thursday of the month. Since Taylorsville is leaving,
the Board is discussing shared services. Duties will be shifted and added to existing staff and
some reduction in officers and some civilian positions. The Board is hoping those individuals will
be hired on by Taylorsville. SLVLESA approved the budget for the 2021 fiscal year. Salt Lake
County Council Member Dea Theodore was appointed to UPD and SLVLESA to replace Max
Burdick. A resolution was passed to replace Mayor Jenny Wilson with Assessor Chris Stavros.
The Board is working with the legislator to modify the tax levy cap. Council Member Jim Bradley
was appointed as the Chair and she was appointed as Vice Chair to the SLVLESA Board.

                                 −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
South Salt Lake Valley Mosquito Abatement District

                Council Member Price stated she was reelected to serve as Chair for 2021. The
Board is gearing up to put out notifications to start hiring. Hiring will take place in March and
employees will start in April. Part of the bylaws for the Board is to go through all the manuals for
the district every year. That process will start in February and will be taken one section at a time.
The AMCA national conference will be held virtually this year.

                                 −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (GSLMSD)

               Mayor Flint stated the managing director from United Way reported on the 211
program where individuals with mental health issues can get help. A consultant has been hired to
develop a strategic plan for the GSLMSD. The bond issue will be discussed starting in March.

Salt Lake County Animal Services

               Council Member Little stated the wildlife program wants to get more communities
involved and to pay into the program. There will be a drive-up licensing event held in White City.
The Board reviewed the bills that are currently going through the legislature. Salt Lake County is
working on the Animal Control ordinance and then it will come to each metro township to adopt.
He suggested that the Council waits until the ordinance is completed and adopted then review it
and adopt whatever parts make sense in White City.

UTA presented the transit report.
