WORKSHOP at 6:01 PM
	•	DISCUSS FY2021 PROPOSED BUDGET
Kane Loader discussed the Unified Police Department proposed budget increase. Chief Randy Thomas said with some department accounting adjustments that were needed, it left the City with a 4.6% increase. 

Kane Loader said staff has contacted Lewis Young Robertson and Cunningham to conduct a feasibility study for police services. This cost is a little over $15,000 to do the study.  His desire is to have the numbers by the last week in June but are working towards the second week of June.  He said the City Council asked staff to schedule a meeting with other city partners with UPD where a discussion will be held in regards to the concerns of the Council. He said the City can handle the day to day normal police services, but can they handle the big situations that require SWAT assistance. Two of those kinds of incidents happened last week in the City. UPD demonstrated the need for the size of department they are. If the City creates its own police department, there will be approximately 65 employees.  

Chief Thomas reviewed two incidents that took place last week. One in Millcreek and one in Midvale.  Millcreek required 49 UPD employees to be involved and Midvale’s incident required 71 UPD employees to be involved.

Kyle Maurer reviewed the outline of the budget book.  Matt Dahl discussed the reopening of city hall.  

Kane Loader said he met with the Arts Council. They would like to do some upgrades to the Performing Arts building. They are trying to coordinate the Hall of Honors ceremony and maybe moving it to the park on the stage. They are still in limbo with their summer concert series in the park. They may want to do something towards the end of summer if possible.

	C.	MODIFICATION OF EMERGENCY PROCLAMATION 
Lisa Garner read the modifications to the Emergency Proclamation as follows:

On March 6, 2020, Governor Gary R. Herbert issued an Executive Order declaring a state of emergency due to the novel coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19).

On March 11, 2020, the World Health Organization characterized the COVID-19 outbreak as a pandemic.

On March 13, 2020, as authorized by Utah Code § 53-2a-208, Mayor Robert M. Hale issued Emergency Proclamation No.1 of 2020 Declaring a Local Emergency due to the outbreak of COVID-19 and its threat to the health of Midvale’s residents and visitors.
On March 13, 2020, President Donald J. Trump declared a national state of emergency based on the continuing to spread of COVID-19.

On March 24, 2020, the Midvale City Council recognized the necessity of Mayor Hale’s Emergency Proclamation and authorized him to take any additional steps to preserve health, provide safety, and maintain peace and order for the benefit of Midvale and its residents and visitors.

On April 28, 2020, Governor Herbert announced under the Utah Leads Together 2.0 Plan that he was reducing Utah’s risk level from “High Risk” to “Moderate Risk” effective May 1, 2020.

I, Robert M. Hale, Mayor of Midvale City, as authorized by Utah Code § 53-2a-208(1)(a) and Midvale City Resolution No. 2020-R-12, hereby modifies Emergency Proclamation No. 1 of 2020 as follows:

1.	Section 3 is replaced with:
Utah Leads Together. Midvale City adopts the health guidelines provided in “Utah Leads Together” (coronavirus.utah.gov/utah-leads-together) as modified by Governor Herbert, the Utah Department of Health, or the Salt Lake County Health Department. Residents and businesses shall implement the health guidelines associated with the current risk level identified by Governor Herbert or, if authorized by the State, the Salt Lake County Health Department.

2.	Section 4 is replaced with:
Public Meetings. As permitted by Utah Executive Order No. 2020-5, all public bodies of Midvale City shall follow the public meeting requirements adopted in Midvale City Resolution No. 2020-R-11.

This Order will remain in effect until terminated by the Mayor or termination of the local emergency declared in Emergency Proclamation No. 1 of 2020.

D.	CONSIDER A PRELIMINARY SUBDIVISION PLAT FOR 6-LOT LENNON LANE SUBDIVISION LOCATED AT 455 EAST 7500 SOUTH
Jana Ward said the applicant, Gary Cannon, is requesting preliminary subdivision plat approval to reconfigure an existing parcel located at 455 East 7500 South into a six-lot subdivision with three new twin homes: one occupying the rear lots and two on the front lots.

The parcel is approximately 0.64 acres with 138 feet of frontage along 7500 South. The property is zoned Single Family Residential with Duplex Overlay (SF-1/DO). There is an existing single family home and detached garage on the existing lot. This proposal will require the demolition and removal of the existing home and detached garage. All new development will be required to comply with the standards of the SF-1/DO zone.

All major subdivisions require a review and recommendation from the Planning Commission and approval from the City Council of a preliminary and final subdivision plat. Public hearings are required to be held by each body on the preliminary subdivision plat. The Planning Commission
conducted a public hearing on this preliminary subdivision plat on April 8, 2020. Based on compliance with the City’s zoning and subdivision requirements, the Planning Commission forwarded a positive recommendation to the City Council to approve the preliminary subdivision plat for the Lennon Lane Subdivision with the following conditions:

1. 	The applicant shall prepare a final subdivision plat to be reviewed and approved by the City Engineer, Fire Marshal and City Council.

2.	A note shall be added to the subdivision plat indicating that a two-unit residential structure with a common wall is required to be constructed along the center line dividing Lots 1 & 2, 3&4, and 5&6 unless the subdivision plat is officially amended.  

3.	All new development on the flag lot shall comply with the flag lot requirements, i.e. setbacks, height (single story), parking, and 20-foot wide paved driveway. A note indicating these requirements shall be included on the final subdivision plat.

4.	The flag lot driveway shall remain clear of overhanging vegetation and be posted with “No Parking” signs every fifty feet (50’). A note to this effect shall be included on the final subdivision plat. 

5.	A minimum six-foot high screening fence shall be constructed and maintained around the exterior of the flag lot unless adjacent property owners agree in writing that a fence is not necessary. This fence shall comply with the fence requirements of the zone and shall be completed prior to a certificate of occupancy being issued for the structure on the flag lot.  The owner should work with the neighboring property owners in replacing or enhancing the existing fences as needed. A note indicating this requirement shall be included on the final subdivision plat.

6.	A minimum of two (2) street trees are required on both Lot 1 and Lot 6 along 7500 S. These trees shall be planted or guaranteed by a cash bond prior to certificates of occupancy being issued for the units on Lots 1 & 6. A note indicating this requirement shall be included on the final subdivision plat.

7.	The applicant shall obtain duty to serve letters for water and sewer prior to the approval of the final subdivision plat.

8.	The applicant shall provide evidence that courtesy notices have been sent to Dominion Energy, Rocky Mountain Power, Xfinity, Utopia, and Century Link regarding the utility easements on the subdivision plat prior to final approval.

In response to grading and drainage comments received during the public hearing on April 8th, Keith Ludwig the City Engineer, confirmed he will ask for grading information on the surrounding properties to be included with the final subdivision submittal. He would also recommend an easement for all retention ponds be added to the plat. These two recommendations have been added as conditions of approval.

Upon City Council approval of the preliminary plat, the applicant will be required to prepare a final plat accurately reflecting any conditions of approval imposed by the City Council. The subdivision shall not be considered approved until final action by the City Council on the final plat at a future date

Dear City Council Members: I am presenting for your consideration the Midvale City Corporation tentative budgets for Fiscal Year 2021. GENERAL FUND HIGHLIGHTS 
As required by law, the General Fund is balanced and as such, meets the basic service level needs of the community. As we go through each department’s budget, the staff will provide details on any proposed changes in funding or service levels. Departments were asked to curtail spending when possible. We are recommending a property tax increase for Fiscal Year 2021. The City’s General Obligation Bonds (which were used to construct the UPD building) ended in fiscal year 2020. Recapturing this property tax to help offset additional public safety costs and current debt service payments will allow the City to retain current service levels and will not impact the average Midvale City taxpayer. A.	DISCUSS DOCUMENTS WITH THE UTAH TRANSIT AUTHORITY WITH REGARDS TO PEDESTRIAN CROSSING LOCATED AT CENTER STRET AND THE TRAX CROSSING	
Keith Ludwig said the Utah Transit Authority (UTA) applied for and received $40,000,000 in TIGER Grant money. UTA then partnered with several communities along the Wasatch Front to participate in that grant. Midvale City was one of those communities.

The City’s project was the pedestrian crossing on Center Street located adjacent to the TRAX crossing. This came out of the Center Street Pedestrian corridor study that was completed by Parametrix.

Midvale City entered into an agreement with UTA in April of 2018. UTA estimated the cost of this project initially at $198,278. Of that, $158,622 would be covered by the TIGER Grant with the remaining $39,656 covered by the City. Midvale deposited the $39,656 with UTA so the City’s project would be one of the first ones to be built. UTA was to handle all the design and construction work. They partnered with Civil Science for design work and Granite Construction for the construction work. The work on the City’s project commenced late last summer and was completed earlier this year. 

As design work was completed and the contractor gave actual construction costs, the estimated cost for the project increased to $573,278. Some of that coming in the form of integration work required for the new signal with existing TRAX signals both up and downstream of this project. In order to keep the project going the City applied for and received up to $375,000 from Salt Lake County Transportation Funds. An agreement between Midvale City and Salt Lake County was signed in February of 2019. Now that the work has been completed there are several items that need to be dealt with to complete the paperwork. 

First. A bill of sale which transfers the ownership and maintenance responsibilities from UTA to Midvale City. 

Second. A supplemental agreement increasing the budget for this project and identifying the funding sources, i.e. TIGER Grant funds, City funds, and Salt Lake County funds. 

Third. A Wireline Crossing Agreement. This agreement is for the lifetime existence and ongoing maintenance of the fiber crossing which connects signals on both side of the TRAX crossing. Access hand holes are on either side of the crossing are outside of the UTA right-of-way so that access to the fiber would not require UTA involvement. UTA would only need to be contacted if the actual conduit encasing the fiber needs to be replaced.

UTA has confirmed that these documents will close out this project and no other agreements or forms will be needed. 

FISCAL IMPACT: 
None. Actual Midvale City funds have previously been deposited with UTA. Additional funding required from the City will be reimbursed by Salt Lake County through their Transportation Funds.

He said he would like to bring this item back at the next Council meeting for approval as a consent item.  The Council agreed.
