PC 23-047 Cache Valley Marketplace [Zone Change, Code Amendment, Subdivision, Design
Review] Mitch Hohlen/Cache Valley Realty LLC, authorized agent/owner are requesting a zone
change, code amendment, subdivision, and design review permit to redevelop the Cache Valley Mall
Site that will include the demolition of the existing mall, the construction of a new 205 million dollar
mixed-use project containing a 150,000 square foot retailer, 346 multi-family residential units, a 156
room hotel with 21,000 square feet of ground floor commercial, a new public right of way connecting
Main Street and 200 East, and a six lot subdivision on a total of 28.62 acres located at 1300 N Main
Street in the Commercial (COM) zone; TIN 05-0147-0078 (Adams Neighborhood).

STAFF: Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director reviewed the four elements of this
request that include a request to rezone the site with the Planned Development Overlay, a text
amendment to the Land Development Code to create the PD-1, Cache Valley Marketplace that will
define the future development on the site, a six-lot subdivision, and the Design Review Permit.

A Concept Plan was reviewed by both Planning Commission and City Council and came back with
recommendations for more or better consolidated open space near the residential, accessibility to
transit stops on Main Street and 200 East, pedestrian circulation throughout the site, and signage.

All proposed uses are permitted in the Commercial Zone. The Land Development Code allows a
density of 30 units per gross acre in the Commercial Zone. At a total of 25.5 acres and a proposed
346 residential units, the overall density is 13.5 units per acre.

The Commercial Zone requires all residential buildings to contain a commercial land use on the
ground floor and the proposed PD Overlay would waive this requirement.

The Land Development Code requires a 40-foot front setback for buildings taller than 41 feet. The
proposed multi-family buildings are approximately 58 feet tall; the hotel building is 52 feet tall, and
both are shown with front setbacks of less than 40 feet. The applicant proposes that the PD Overlay
modify these height and setback regulations to allow the project layout as designed.

The Land Development Code limits building heights to 55 feet in the Commercial zone with enhanced
setbacks. At mid-gable, the four-story apartment building is approximately 58 feet tall. The PD
Overlay is being proposed to modify these height regulations to allow for the project as

shown.

As proposed the project complies with building frontage, lot coverage, parking requirements, and
building elevations.

The 25.5-acre property (1,110,780 SF) is broken up into four areas, two commercial and two multi-
family. The commercial areas total 722,225 SF and would require 144,444 SF (20%) of outdoor
space and landscaping. The multifamily areas total 388,555 SF and would require 116,566 SF (30%)
of useable outdoor space and landscaping. Based on the two standards and two different
development-type areas, the project would be required a total of 261,010 SF of landscaping and
useable outdoor space. The PD Overlay includes the ability to place up to 5% of the required useable
outdoor space offsite to a future park located to the south behind PetSmart.

Based on street frontages and property width, the project would be allowed up to six (6) monument
signs at eight feet tall and 72 SF in size. As per the development pian request, the applicant is
proposing three signs at 16 feet tall and 15 feet wide (double the standard) with one on each frontage
(1400 N, Main, 1250 N). Staff is recommending that the City consider approving a slightly larger sign
size than what is permitted in the code for a reduced number of signs and views this as a reasonable
tradeoff and appropriate to the overall size and scale of the project. Staff is recommending that in
lieu of six monument signs, the project be entitled to three eleven-foot-tall signs with 108 SF, one for
each of the three frontages, as well as one standard-sized monument sign on 200 East.

The technical issues with the infrastructure and utilities that are currently being worked through with
the City include a Traffic Impact Study to identify impacts, deficiencies, and improvements. Water
modeling to determine availability, demand, and fire flow. Stormwater for onsite detention/retention.
Light and Power to evaluate existing systems, and Streets for points of access and right of way.

More Multi-Family housing is needed because Logan’s average annual growth rate is 1.2 %. In the
last 10 years, Logan has added 4,600 new people, and in the last 20 years, there have been 10,000
new residents who need to live somewhere. In the last ten years, there have been just under 3,000
new units permitted by Logan. Approximately 85% of Logan’s population growth is internally
generated. By 2030 there will be an additional 6,000 — 12,000 new residents. There has been a
housing supply shortage since 2008.

This is a good project in a good location. This is the fifth development team to put the Mall under
contract and get this far in the redevelopment process. This project is a big investment in the
community that will improve the tax base, will create new jobs, new housing, and is a significant
economic benefit to surrounding businesses. It locates affordable housing near services to reduce
car traffic and is walkable for new and existing residents. It implements the Logan General Plan and
the Adams Neighborhood Plan.

PC 23-045 Logan Entrada [Design Review] Ethan Poppleton/Logan Entrada LLC, authorized
agent/owner are requesting a design review permit to develop two 5-story buildings that will include
36 hotel rooms and commercial space on the main floors and 120 total apartment units on floors two
through five, located at 1720 South Hwy 89-91 in the Commercial (COM) zone; TIN 02-083-0013
(Woodruff Neighborhood).


STAFF: Tanya Rice, Planner reviewed the request for a Design Review Permit to develop 5.5 acres
off US Hwy 89-91 at 1720 South. The existing building on the property will be demolished and two
new 5-story buildings built that include 120 apartment units on floors two through five with 36 hotel
units and 9,600 SF of commercial lease space on the first floor. As proposed the project
meets the required setbacks, height, lot coverage, frontage, and design standards in the
Land Development Code.

T. Rice explained the minimum parking requirements and the proposed parking plan
submitted by the applicant. The applicant is proposing 250 parking stalls for the project. The
parking requirement for just the hotel and apartment units total 260 parking stalls.

If all of the commercial space is leased as restaurants there would be an additional 64 stalls
required for a total of 324 stalls required for the site, which is 69 more stalls than the
proposed 250 stalls, and which represents a deficiency of 27%.

If all of the commercial space is leased as office space there would be an additional 32 stalls
required for a total of 292 stalls required for the site, which is 37 more stalls than the
proposed 250 stalls, and which represents a deficiency of 15%.

If all of the commercial space is leased as a mixture of uses there would be an additional 48
stalls required for a total of 308 stalls required for the site, which is 53 more stalls than the
proposed 250 stalls, and which represents a deficiency of 21%.

The applicant is requesting a parking reduction of in the range of 15% to 27%, or 32-69 stalls and
has submitted an alternative parking plan showing that the site is near bus routes, sidewalks, has
access to 29 parallel street parking stalls and will utilize shared or flex stalls between apartment
tenants and lease space.

Commissioner Lucero asked if the project is proposing any excess green space above the
requirement. T. Rice they are meeting the setbacks and green space requirements.

PROPONENT: Ethan Poppleton said that with the property's proximity to major employers, gas
stations, grocery stores, bus stops, and trails, they do not see this project being a vehicular pressure
on the City. He spoke about removing some of the trees to create more parking stalls and how they
are not planning on having restaurants in the commercial spaces.

Jon Galbraith, Architect said that restaurant parking stalls are calculated off the dining space, which
would only be approximately 40% of the minimum 64 stalls mentioned for restaurants, and with that,
their proposed would be closer to 18% of the required parking.

PUBLIC: Adam Tripp said he is in favor of the proposed project. Mentioned that the commission
could consider allowing a 10-foot setback along 800 West to make room for the needed parking.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Heare said that they cannot count the parallel parking on 800 West
towards the required parking for this development.

Commissioner Lucero asked if the overlay setback could be reduced. M. DeSimone said that the
commission could approve a 10% variation, but that would not make much of a difference in the


parking area. Darren Farar, City Engineer talked about parking along 800 West or the possibility of
vacating the street.

Commissioner Lucero said there is not enough parking and the applicant needs to come back with
a better parking plan.
