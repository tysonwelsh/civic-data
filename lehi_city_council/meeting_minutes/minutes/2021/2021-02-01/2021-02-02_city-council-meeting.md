1. Welcome Breakfast
   Mayor Johnson welcomed everyone and noted that all Councilmembers were present. Paul
   Hancock offered the prayer.

2. Council Assignments
   Councilor Southwick led the discussion on Council assignments. The Mayor stated that he will
   now be attending the Mountainland Economic Recovery Committee and the UTA Advisory
   Board. Below are the new assignments that were made.

   TSSD- Councilor Condie
   Utah League of Cities and Towns- Councilor Koivisto
   Arts Council- Councilor Hancock
   Jordan Lake Commission- Councilor Albrecht

3. Mayor’s Wage Study
   Andy Gowans presented on the City Council and Mayor wage study. He noted that Lehi is 1%
   above the average for City Council wages only, in comparison to 24 other cities. Including
   benefits, it’s 44% above the average. The Mayoral wage is only 12% below in comparison to

   16 cites with part time mayors. With wages and Benefits, Lehi is 8% above average for Mayor
   compensation.

   There was discussion about having a part time Mayor versus a full time Mayor. It was suggested
   that the position be left as a part time mayor but improve the compensation. The Council agreed
   that they are on the City Council to serve and are not in it for the money. Councilor Hancock
   stated that he wants to fairly compensate the Mayor for his time. Councilor Koivisto expressed
   concern with increasing the Mayor’s salary and said there is no need for it. She would rather
   pay the professionals more money than add to the Mayor’s salary. Councilor Condie stated that
   it’s a demanding job and they should be compensated fairly. Councilor Albrecht agreed.

   Mayor Johnson stated that he worries about a full time Mayor position. He said you want
   someone that’s successful in life and has business experience.

   Jason Walker stated that they will bring a proposal to the Council for a vote at a future meeting.


4. Financial Update
   Dean Lundell, Finance Director, stated that they are in a good position for property tax revenue.
   The don’t know yet what impact Covid will have on property taxes. He stated that they could
   look at raising property taxes this year or next. Mr. Lundell stated that sales tax revenue
   increased significantly more than this time last year.


5. Capital Project
    Major capital improvements include parks and a civic center. Mr. Lundell stated that they are
   looking at $14 million for parks and $17.1 million for a city center phase one. Fire Station #84
   is projected to be $8.5 million, and the fiber network is for $65 million.

   There was discussion about other funding mechanisms such as a RAP tax which most cities
   have. Mayor Johnson stated that this is the best way to fund our regional parks. The other option
   is a GO Bond for Parks. Mr. Lundell also talked about an incremental property tax increase.

   Councilor Condie stated that he would like to add a RAP tax. Councilor Albrecht stated that the
   biggest question is how to allocate the RAP tax funds. That was previously a large concern for
   residents. Councilor Condie stated that he wants to do something this year to show they are
   committed to parks.

   Mr. Walker stated that they have been approached by an organization that’s interested in
   investing $35 million into baseball parks in Lehi. They also want to build a rec center. Mayor
   Johnson suggested that they privatize soccer and baseball in the community to save money.
   There was discussion about privatizing soccer and baseball. Mayor Johnson stated that this
   would save money which could then help develop the parks. He believes that the private entities
   could still offer registration discounts for financial needs.

6. Rate Studies (Sewer and power)

   Mr. Lundell stated that with the increased fees from TSSD, the sewer fund cash balance is
   decreasing and the sewer rates will need to be adjusted in the near future. Lehi has one of the
   lowest rates in TSSD. He stated that rates should increase over time just to cover the cost of
   inflation. He noted that the rates charged to residents need to pay for that utility.

   Joel Eves, Power Director, informed the Council of some concerns they have for inequitable
   rates for customers. He noted that commercial rates are subsidizing residential rates, which is
   okay, but that rate for commercial may need to be adjusted. He’s also concerned that the rates
   do not always match the costs. He also noted come inequity with solar costumers who may be
   paying less than they should. Mr. Eves also stated that the Primary Children’s Hospital is
   requesting to be an industrial customer, and the City does not have an established rate for that
   type of use, so they need to determine what that would be. Also, due to the increase in electric
   vehicles, they may need to establish a “time of use rate” for electric vehicles, so that electric
   vehicles aren’t charging during peak hours. Mr. Eves recommended that the Council authorize
   a rate study in next year’s budget to help address these issues.

7. Goals
   Beau Thomas, Assistant to the City Administrator, led a discussion on what the Council’s top
   priorities are. This included Arts Council building improvements, transportation, parks, public
   safety, infrastructure, Main Street improvements, library, additional staff, and invest money.

   Mr. Thomas inquired if the Council wanted to change its goal. The current goal is to “develop
   a cutting-edge, family centric community”. The Council agreed to retain this goal.

   Lunch

8. Jeff Thompson
   Jeff Thompson, BYU professor was the guest speaker. He noted that we tend to put limits on
   our abilities and discussed what it means to have a growth mindset versus a fixed mindset.

   Mr. Thompson also discussed ways to improve communication with others.


9. Survey Results (Y2 Analytics)
   Kyrene Gibb, with Y2 Analytics, stated that growth and development are still major concerns
   for residents especially regarding high density housing. Overall city residents report a high
   quality of life and are happy with the way the city is being run. Many downward projections
   from previous years have started to turn upward, which include the overall direction and quality
   of life, as well as approval of city leaders and likelihood of recommending Lehi to others.

   She noted that many prefer to receive communications from the city by the newsletter, but many
   also preferred email. Also, they are seeing a desire from residents for more green space and
   recreational development.

10. Policy Discussion
    Round-Up activities- Mel Hansen, Special Events Coordinator, informed the Council of the
    upcoming plans for summer events. She said they will most likely have events that include
    social distancing and mask requirements.

   Legislative Update- Shaye Ruitenbeek gave an update on the proposed bills at the State
   Legislature. The most concerning is the single-family housing modifications that would allow
   for ADUs anywhere in the city. She also noted a few of the bills that the City is supporting
   which include use of force reporting requirements and peace officer training qualifications.

   Water shortage- Dave Norman stated that they will not have the water that they’ve had in
   previous years and they will be looking at how they handle restrictions this year. He said that
   right now they are brainstorming ideas to help people see how much water they are using and
   how much water they really need. He said that parts of the city do have PI meters which could
   help educate those residents on what that could potentially cost them. He said they want to
   educate the residents early.

   ADUs (accessory dwelling units)- Katie Koivisto explained her proposal for allowing ADUs
   and how to capture that income for city infrastructure. She stated that the city should be
   collecting more fees because people are avoiding payment of impact fees or they are using their
   ADU as a short-term rental. She suggested that they need to entice people to pay for their impact
   fees by creating a monthly payment system, and by informing people that their home value will
   increase if they are permitted for an ADU. There was also discussion about establishing a short-
   term rental fee to help pay for its impact. Mayor Johnson stated that the bill being proposed by
   the State Legislature is concerning because of the impact it could have on Lehi’s infrastructure.

   Fiber Network- Mr. Walker stated that Magellan is creating a business plan study for the City.
   He also noted that the City will be creating an RFP for those that want to bid on the design. Mr.
   Lundell recommended that the city only borrow half of the 65 million right now. He explained
   the take rate and how that affects the payment of the bond. He suggested that they borrow the
   remainder at a future date when it’s needed. Councilor Condie noted that they haven’t received
   a lot of negative feedback for this bond.

   Human Resources Update- David Kitchen, Human Resources Manager, suggested a 1% market
   adjustment, or cost of living increase, for all employees based on inflation rates. He also noted
   that they will be conducting inclusivity training for employees.

   Administration Update- Mr. Walker informed that Council that Thanksgiving Point would like
   to have a joint venture with Lehi City for a seasonal ice-skating rink. Other neighboring cities
   may also participate, but it’s up to the Council to decide if they want to contribute and how
   much.
