Proposed Resolution 2022-8 scheduling a public hearing to allow public
input regarding proposed transfers from the City’s Utility Enterprise Funds
to the General Fund

A memo from Council staff came before the Council to consider a resolution scheduling a public hearing regarding a
proposed enterprise fund transfer. The memo stated as required by Utah State Code §10-6-135.5, a City that is proposing to
transfer funds from an enterprise fund to another fund for a purpose not directly related to the goods or services provided by the
enterprise fund must provide notice of the transfers to the users of the enterprise fund. The city must also hold a public hearing on
the transfers prior to adoption or the amendment of the City’s budget. In Ogden, a budgetary transfer from the city’s Water,
Sanitary Sewer, Storm Sewer, and Refuse Utility enterprise funds to the general fund has been done for many years; however, the
requirement to provide notice of the transfers and to hold a separate public hearing is a recent change to state law. These utility
funds are accounted for as enterprise funds in Ogden and are operated with user fees charged for goods and services. In the
proposed fiscal year 2023 budget, the City is proposing a transfer from these utility funds into the City’s general fund to act as the
collection of franchise and property tax. In order to comply with state law requirements, a public hearing on the proposed transfers
is scheduled for June 21, 2022.

Chair Nadolski introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2022-08, entitled:

“A resolution of the Ogden City Council scheduling a public hearing to allow public input regarding proposed
transfers from the City’s Utility Enterprise Funds to the General Fund.”

Proposed Ordinance 2022-23 approving the Capital Improvement Plan for
Ogden City for the Fiscal Years 2023 to 2027
A memo from Council staff regarding the proposed Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) for Fiscal Years (FY) 2023-2027
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the City’s process for reviewing the five-year CIP underwent
significant changes in 2021. Previously, the CIP was reviewed months before the budget process and rankings from both
Administration and Council were used to inform the budget development process. With the recent change, the CIP will now be
reviewed alongside the budget making it easier for the Council and Administration to align the plan for capital expenditures with
the actual budget. The CIP will be reviewed in conjunction with the budget for the Fiscal Year 2023 budget process. The CIP is
reviewed and adopted each year as a planning tool for major capital investments in the City’s physical plant over a rolling five-
year period. Each year’s plan is updated based on the changing needs of the city, funding opportunities, projects completed and
other similar factors. During this year’s budget process, proposed capital projects will be reviewed alongside each department’s
proposed budget and in context with the overall needs and resources available to the City through the budget process.
Chair Nadolski then introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2022-23, entitled:
“An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, approving the Capital Improvement Plan for Ogden City for the Fiscal
Years 2023-2027, and providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final
passage.”

Proposed Ordinance 2022-22 adopting the proposed tentative budget of
Ogden City for Fiscal Year 2022-2023

A memo from Council staff regarding the tentative Fiscal Year (FY) 2022-2023 budget came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated on May 3, 2022, Mayor Caldwell presented his proposed Fiscal Y ear 2022-2023 Budget totaling
$78,387,025. The Administration is requesting a few minor changes to the proposed FY2023 Budget prior to adopting the
tentative budget, via consideration of Schedule A, which increases the budget by $244,325.

Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss
the highlights of the FY 2022-2023 tentative budget; employees are the main budget priority for FY2023 and the budget includes
cost of living adjustments (COLAs) for fire (14 percent), police (8 percent), and general employees (13 percent). There are 25 new
employee positions included in the budget as well. Additionally, the budget includes allocation of American Rescue Plan Act
(ARPA) funding and a Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) project for the Marshall White Center (MWC). The Mayor has proposed a
tax increase that would generate an additional $2.8 million in revenue to fund some of these priorities. She provided a chart
illustrating the manner in which the City’s $28 million in ARPA funding will be allocated. One of the primary directives from the
Council’s Five-Year Strategic Plan is to “leverage our unique location, outdoor recreation, diverse population, and rich history to
offer residents, businesses, and guests a vibrant community that leads people to call Ogden home.” Council budget goals aimed at
meeting this directive include economic development, community safety, recreation, City image and reputation, and fiscal
sustainability and transparency. She briefly expounded on each of these budget goals and identified various budget items that are
aimed at achieving the goal, after which she touched on the adjustment to the process for reviewing the City’s Capital
Improvement Plan (CIP) in conjunction with the budget. Major projects included in the CIP funding for FY2023 include the
Marshall White Center project and public realm improvements, including the Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) line. She concluded by
discussing the proposed changes to the budget in the ‘Schedule A’ document provided by Administration. If Schedule A is
accepted, the General Fund will be reduced by $20,000 to reduce the payment for the 17" Street Development Agreement and
move funds within the Police Department budget to cover wages of three officers. The Sewer Fund would increase by $264,325 to
cover additional Central Weber Sewer District charges. The total change over the original proposed budget is $244,325. The
Council may direct additional changes in the Schedule A-1 to the ordinance for adoption of the FY23 final budget.
