5.     PUBLIC HEARING.CONSIDERATION.AND RECOMMENDATION on a Proposed
       Text Amendment to Bluffdale Citv Code 14.10.020 Amending the Number of Unrelated
       Handicapped or Disabled Persons who May Live Together in a Single Dwelling from
       Five (5) to Eight (8), Citv of Bluffdale. Applicant. (Application 2024-16) - Staff
       Presenter, Fred Donaldson.


Mr. Donaldson presented the amendment and reported that City Code currently restricts the number
ofunrelated persons who may live together in a single dwelling to four. In recognition ofthe need to
extend residential opportunities to individuals with handicaps imder the Fair Housing Act and
disabilities under the Americans with Disabilities Act("ADA"),the City previously enacted a statute
allowing up to five unrelated handicapped persons to live together in a single residential dwelling.
There is also a process to request reasonable accommodation to increase that number and there have
been several such requests in the past. Recently, expert testimony established that the number of
unrelated handicapped persons who should be allowed to live together in a single residential dwelling
is eight and the City should continue to allow requests for reasonable accommodations above that
number. The proposed amendment changes the number five to eight in Code Section 14.10.020.

Chair Cragun asked for additional details on the expert testimony, whether it was at the state or
national level, and the scope of the recommendation. Mr. Donaldson referred to two recent local
reasonable accommodation requests outlined in the Staff Report. One was to allow 16 unrelated
handicapped individuals to live together in a single dwelling. The hearing officer denied the request
in part but allowed a maximum of 12 individuals. Another request requested a maximum of 24
individuals, which was denied. In that case, the City's expert witness testified that eight unrelated
handicapped individuals living together was reasonably and therapeutically necessary.
Mr. Donaldson confirmed that the change would not impact any previously approved applications.

Commissioner Swanson asked about the Hearing Officer's decision to allow 12 unrelated individuals
in the first example. Mr. Donaldson responded that several different factors were weighed. The
official order was lengthy, and he would need to review it in order to provide specific information.
The decision was not appealed. It was his belief that the Hearing Officer wanted to make
accommodations for the applicant but the request for 24 beds was not reasonable. In response to a
question from Commissioner Swanson, Mr. Donaldson confirmed that no other part ofthe Code will

6.        PUBLIC HEARING.CONSIDERATION.AND RECOMMENDATION on a Proposed
          Text Amendment to Amend Various Sections ofthe Bluffdaie City Land Use Ordinances
       (Zoning and Subdivision Ordinances) Pertaining to Requirements for Developing and
       Accessing from Pre-Existing Rights-of-Wav and Newlv Created Private Rights-of-Wav.
       Citv of Bluffdaie. Applicant. (Application 2024-11)-Staff Presenter. Grant Crowell.

Mr. Crowell provided background on the proposed amendment and stated that in 2015, Bluffdaie
acknowledged that there were many preexisting private rights-of-way,some gravel, some paved,that
had been in the community for years. The tiered approach Staffin this proposed amendment was also
proposed in 2014,but the approved amendment was modified. The 2015 modification,for example,
allows subdivision access via a 20-foot existing private right-of-way, but the road in the subdivision
itself must be 52 feet wide.


The proposed amendment is intended for infill situations, mostly in the larger lot area of town. Per
the 2015 ordinance, the maximum number of lots that can access a pre-existing private right-of-way

is 30. That ties in closely with the Subdivision Ordinance. The issue is that the existing private rights-
of-way are not typically 50-foot-wide streets. The amendment would require that the right-of-way
meet the minimum Fire Code and be paved but the minimum width would depend on factors like
parking and hydrant location. Potential safety concerns were addressed in the amendment.

Proposed Standard ST-70 states that a preexisting private right-of-way can be used for new
subdivisions but must be paved to meet the minimum standard shown below:

   • A three-lot or smaller subdivision can propose a 25-foot private shared right-of-way with 20
        feet of all-weather surface.

   • A four to six-lot subdivision can propose a private right-of-way of26 feet.
   • A seven to 10-lot subdivision can propose 35 feet
   • Subdivisions with more than ten lots would need to meet the residential street standards.


Mr. Crowell also confirmed the following:

   • Easement requirements for water lines are retained in the Code.
   • The definition oflot size excludes rights-of-way, including the shared driveway right-of-way.
        The amendment would not change density, so that ordinance stays as-is.
   • Outdated terms and phrases used in the previous Code have been updated.

Mr. Crowell noted that Fire Chief, Matt Evans,and Fire Marshall, Lance Nielson, have reviewed and
approved of the proposed amendment. It allows fire officials to determine the necessary safe road
width but also gives flexibility for roads narrower than 52 feet.

In response to a question regarding whether neighboring property owner approval should be required
in addition to that ofthe Fire Department, Mr. Crowell noted that Bluffdale is unique in that some of
the areas are rural with large lots and long private rights-of-way. City Council Members often say
that property rights are paramount and if so, then those property owners should be allowed to
subdivide and build homes for their families ifthe Fire Department deems it safe to do so.

Chair Cragun asked for a summary of the most impactful change. Mr. Crowell indicated that the
specifications for subdivisions with four to six and seven to 10 lots is the most crucial change. The

Mr. Donaldson referred to Fire Code Appendix D,Section D103.3, Turning Radius, which states that

be able to do its job and respond to an emergency situation. The Fire Code Official is responsible for
determining that based on their specific equipment. It recognizes their expertise in determining what
is necessary for a particular City and subdivision.

Mr. Crowell stated that the City can require a subdivision to have public access to streets. For

7.     Planning Commission Training- Legislative Update - Fred Donaldson, Citv Attornev.

Mr. Donaldson reported that 591 bills were passed in the 2024 Legislative Session, as well as a $29
billion budget. He provided an overview ofthe bills most relevant to the Planning Commission.

With regard to HB-36, Open, and Public Meetings Act Amendments, there was a concem that
meetings of City Council Members or Planning Commissioners might be accidentally convened by
members. For example, there was a concem that it would be possible for an electronic meeting to be
convened through email or text messaging. This amendment clarifies that it does not qualify as an
electronic meeting. Meeting means "a gathering of a public body or specified body with a quorum
present and that is convened by an individual with authority to convene the public body or specified
body,and following the process provided by law for convening a meeting,and for the express purpose
ofacting as a public body." The intention is to eliminate the allegation that a public meeting was held
when there was no intent to do so.


In response to a question from Commissioner Swanson, Mr. Donaldson confirmed that members of
the City Council attending a seminar or ribbon cutting would not be a meeting. Emails or texts

between Commission or Council Members are govemed by the Government Records Access and
Management Act("GRAMA")and are not related to the Open and Public Meetings Act.

With regard to HB-256, Military Compatible Land Use Amendments,cities were previously required
to develop a Compatible Use Plan if the city was within a certain distance of a military installation.
That is still a requirement, and the City is now also required to consider the Compatible Use Plan in
processing Land Use applications within 5,000 feet of the military installation. This bill applies to
parts of Bluffdale because of its proximity to Camp Williams. Mr. Crowell reported that Bluffdale
was part of the process that created the last two plans at Camp Williams. He believes some action
will need to be taken to officially adopt components ofthose plans, so there is still work to be done.
Mr. Donaldson stated that that will come before the Planning Commission at some point this year.

HB-476, Land Use Planning Amendments was next addressed. Mr. Crowell stated that a number of
changes are covered in this bill. In the future, more Code changes will be brought before the Planning
Commission with regard to it. Outside Counsel, Jay Springer from Smith Hartvigsen, will consult
with Staff and assist them in drafting the necessary changes.

In response to a question from Commissioner Swanson, Mr. Crowell confirmed that there are not as
many changes as the previous year but some required changes are significant. For example, one
change regarding sidewalk phasing modifies the way subdivisions are constructed. Mr. Donaldson
stated that the presentation is available in the packet and the specific legislation is available on the
Utah State Legislature's website. The changes are not as extensive as those from the previous
Legislative Session but there are a number of changes with regard to land use, including changes to
phasing, development agreements, density bonuses, private partnerships, etc.

Modifications were made to what is allowed in Development Agreements. Municipalities can require
approvals related to height, parking exceptions, density bonuses, etc. They may not require a
Development Agreement as a condition for developing land within the municipality "if the
development otherwise complies with applicable statutory and municipal ordinances the development
is allowed on or a permitted use and the Land Use Regulations otherwise establish all standards for
the development." Some municipalities previously required a development agreement for all
development within their city. They are no longer allowed to do that.

In response to a question from Mr. Crowell, Mr. Donaldson confirmed that rezoning requests are not
covered by the change. The City can require a development agreement in that case. If the property
is already zoned for their purpose, it is administrative. Rezoning is a legislative decision, so the City
has the ability to ask for a development agreement and negotiate terms.

A municipality may require the seller of a newly constructed residence to inform the first buyer of
the newly constructed residence of a municipal ordinance requiring waterwise landscaping. Some
parts of Bluffdale have waterwise landscaping requirements but there is not a Code provision that
would require doing this at this time.


Changes were made to residential rear setback limitations. Certain porches and window wells do not
qualify as violating rear setbacks.


With regard to subdivision improvement plans, deadlines for when a municipality needs to complete
an initial review of a subdivision application were modified.

Municipalities are no longer allowed to withhold a building permit or certificate ofoccupancy based
on the lack ofcompletion ofa landscaping improvement that is not a public landscaping improvement.
Mr. Crowell commented that this amendment was brought forward in the previous session and
clarified in the recent session. Unless the landscaping improvement is covered in a development
agreement, the City's only remedy would be via Code Enforcement. Mr. Donaldson noted that the
change applies to private landscaping, not public landscaping improvements. Public improvements
will be owned by the City, such as public roads. Park strips in rights-of-way are potentially public
improvements, but that is a gray area.


The effective date of this bill is November 1, 2024, with the exception of certain provisions. If the
Planning Commission would like details or has questions on this or other legislation, Mr. Donaldson
will address those at a future meeting. Mr.Crowell indicated that the Wasatch Front Regional Council
and Utah League of Cities and Towns ("ULCT") have bill trackers on their websites. The Utah
Redevelopment Association also tracks RDA-specific bills.
