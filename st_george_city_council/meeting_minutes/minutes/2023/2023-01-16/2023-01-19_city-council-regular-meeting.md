PUBLIC HEARING/CONSIDER APPLICATION FOR CIB FUNDING:
     Public hearing to consider the City's application for funding from the Utah
     Permanent Community Impact Board (CIB) for construction of the new St.
     George City Hall and Parking Structure in the downtown area.

      BACKGROUND and RECOMMENDATION: This is a public hearing to consider the City's
      application for funding from the Utah Permanent Community Impact Fund Board
      (CIB) to partially fund the construction of the new St. George City Hall and Parking
      Structure in the downtown area. This public hearing is required for the City's
      application to the CIB in which we are requesting a loan for $20 million over a 30-
      year period and a low interest rate of 2.00% to 2.50%. If the City's request is
      accepted by the CIB, the terms of the loan will come back to the City Council for
      approval prior to receiving funding later this spring or summer. Staff recommends
      approval that the City submit an application for the CIB bond in the amount of $20
      million, with a 30-year term, and an interest rate of 2.00% to 2.50% (interest rate
      TBD by the CIB) to be issued through the City's Municipal Building Authority (MBA)
      as the official applicant.

      Assistant City Manager Deanna Brklacich presented the request to consider the City’s
      application for funding from the Utah Permanent Community Impact Board (CIB) for
      construction of the new City Hall building and parking structure in the downtown
      area. She provided the Council with a brief background for the item and commented
      staff is asking for approval to move forward with application for the loan. She then
      explained that the St. George City Hall was built 42 years ago in 1980. At the time, it
      housed nearly every city service provided to our community. Over the years as the
      city organization grew, every service or department that could be moved to a
      satellite location and still serve the needs of citizens have been relocated. It has
      been difficult to hire additional employees in development services, public works,
      human resources, utilities, and other general government support services in order
      to keep pace with the growth and demands of the City. Recently, the City hired
      Galloway & Company, an architect and engineering firm to perform a needs analysis
      for City Hall. The study concluded that additional space is needed at City Hall to
      accommodate needs for the next thirty (30) years. A location downtown was
      selected for the future site of city hall at 61 South Main Street. The property for the
      new downtown City Hall and parking structure was recently purchased by the City
      and design is underway. Staff met with the City Council during a work meeting on
      December 8, 2022 to review the preliminary plans and solicit your input from the
      Council. The new City Hall will be approximately 69,500 square feet with three (3)
      levels and will be occupied by departments which primarily serve the community:
      Utilities, Business Licensing, Development Services, Public Works
      Admin./Engineering, and Economic Development. It will also house departments
      which provide support to all City departments: Administration, Human Resources,
      Legal, City Manager, Finance, Technology Services, and others; and will also include
      the City Council Chambers/Community Room. The Parking Structure will consist of
      four (4) levels and includes three hundred and eight (308) parking spaces which,
      during regular business hours, will be used by City employees, Wells Fargo Bank
      employees, and citizens transacting business with the City. The Parking Structure will
      also be available to the community during after-hours and weekends as they visit the
      downtown area and attend area businesses and community events.
      The estimated cost of this project, not including land, is $43.14 million, and copies of
      the budget were provided to the City Council and are also available for the citizens
      tonight, and is included in the FY2023 Budget. Staff has evaluated funding options
      (Sales Tax Revenue Bonds, Franchise Tax Revenue Bonds, G.O. Bond, etc.) and
      decided to pursue a Community Impact Board (CIB) Loan through the State of Utah
      Dept. of Workforce Services. The CIB Board offers grants and low-interest loans
      specifically for these types of projects. Grants do not need to be repaid; however,
      loans require repayment. Although the CIB Board may offer a grant, due to the size
      of the request, a grant is not possible. The CIB board has certain dates that
      applications can be submitted and reviewed and therefore we have begun the
      process to secure a CIB loan for $20,000,000 at an interest rate of 2.00% to 2.50%
      over 30 years which will be facilitated through the City’s Municipal Building Authority
      (MBA). The CIB bond will be issued to the Municipal Building Authority as the

      OFFICIAL applicant, and this will be treated as a Lease Revenue Bond that the City
      (and thus its residents) will make an annual LEASE payment to the MBA until the

ORDINANCE ESTABLISHING A FREE SPEECH ZONE FOR EVENTS:
     Consider approval of an ordinance to establish a Free Speech Zone for
     Events.

      BACKGROUND and RECOMMENDATION: During the past several months there have
      been protests at special events. Establishing a Free Speech Zone will allow the event
      sponsor to not be interrupted by protesters during their event and it will allow
      protesters to get their message across in a designated area. During the Pride event
      there were protesters who marched through the event. During the Farmers Markets
      there were folks who wanted to communicate their ideas but did not get a special
      event permit of their own. The Free Speech Zone passes constitutional muster as
      establishing a free speech zone during events would serve a significant government
      interest including traffic flow, crowd control, or public safety at events on public
      property; it is narrowly tailored and provides ample alternatives for communication
      (intermediate scrutiny for content neutral time, place, and manner restrictions on
      private speech in a traditional or designated public forum).
