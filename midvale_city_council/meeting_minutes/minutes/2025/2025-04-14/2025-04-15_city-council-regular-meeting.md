       C.      CONSIDER AMENDING SECTION 17-4-1 OF THE MIDVALE MUNICIPAL
               CODE TO ALLOW THE CITY COUNCIL TO DETERMINE A PLANNING
               COMMISSION STIPEND
Wendelin Knobloch said In 2016 the planning commission stipend was changed from
$20 to $50 for every meeting attended. This change was not reflected in the city code,
which still shows the $20 figure. The proposed code amendment reflects the practice of
the last nine years and places the decision of the compensation amount into the
budgeting process where it should and has occurred for the last nine years as part of
the Planning Department budget.

Public notice was sent to affected entities as required in Section 17-3-9(B) of the
Municipal Code. No verbal or written comments have been received at the time of this
writing.

ZONING CODE AMENDMENT CRITERIA

Midvale City Code 17-3-1(F) outlines the criteria necessary for amendments to the
zoning code. A proposal may only be approved if it demonstrates one or more of the
following:

     1. The proposed amendment promotes the objectives of the general plan and
         purposes of this title;


                                           22
Proceedings of City Council Meeting
April 15, 2025




    2. The proposed amendment promotes the purposes outlined in Utah State Code
       10-9a-102;

    3. The proposed amendment more clearly explains the intent of the original
       language or has been amended to make interpretation more straightforward; or



     4. Existing zoning code was the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact.

Staff finds that this proposal meets the first, second, third, and fourth criterion listed
above.
Criterion 1 is fulfilled, because Title 17 intends to compensate planning commissioners
within budgetary constraints.

Criterion 2 is fulfilled, because planning commissions are a required part of the
governmental structure that enables land use regulation.

Criterion 3 is fulfilled, because the amendment clarifies who approves the amount of
planning commission stipends.

Criterion 4 is fulfilled, because the actual compensation amount of the planning
commission does not reflect the current language in the code.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommended he City Council to approve the code text amendment with the
following finding:

    1. The amendment complies with Midvale City Code 17-3-1(F) and fulfills all four
       amendment criteria.

PLANNING COMMISSION RECOMMENDATION

This item was heard during the 3/25/2025 Planning Commission meeting and the
Planning Commission recommended approval unanimously.

        D. REVIEW AND APPROVAL OF RESOLUTION NO. 2025-R-21
           AUTHORIZING THE SECOND PAYMENT TO THE COMMUNITY
           RENEWABLE ENERGY AGENCY FOR MIDVALE CITY
Erinn Summers said the Community Renewable Energy Agency (Agency), also known
as Utah Renewable Communities Program, was established under HB411, in 2019 (Utah
Code Ann. § 54-17-901). The Agency is an interlocal entity, made up of Utah
communities who have their electricity provided entirely, or in part, by Rocky Mountain
Power (RMP). The Agency, in collaboration with RMP, aims to provide net-100%
renewable electricity by 2030 to participating communities. When Utah Code Ann. § 54-
17-901 was established, it included a requirement that a municipality or county adopt a
resolution by December 31, 2019 to participate in the Agency. During the 2024 legislative
session, the Utah State Legislature passed SB 214 which removed the deadline
requirement from the code. The removal of the deadline provided Utah communities who
are not members of the Agency the opportunity to join. On June 3, 2024, the Community
Renewable Energy Board passed a resolution formalizing the pathway for prospective
parties to join the Agency. This pathway required cities to sign onto the interlocal
agreement, the utility agreement with RMP, pay two $11,000 fees (the first being due in
August 2024, and the second being due in August 2025), appoint a representative to the
Agency board, and pass an ordinance once the program is approved by the Public
Services Commission. New cities would sit on the Agency board after the initial payment
and signing the agreements, but would not be able to vote on Agency decisions until
after the new members adopted the ordinance.

On August 20th, 2024, Midvale City Council passed Resolution 2024-R-41 authorizing
the Mayor to join the Agency and make the first of two payments to the Agency. The
Midvale City Council specifically only approved the first payment, allowing the second
payment and the passage of the ordinance to serve as two additional opportunities for
the Council to determine if the program was the best fit for Midvale City residents.

On April 7, 2025, the Utah Renewable Energy Program Agency board discussed
allowing Midvale, as the only new member, to become a voting member early if Midvale
City is willing to make the second payment early. Having the opportunity to vote sooner
will allow Midvale City to weigh in on upcoming decisions in front of the Agency. One
major decision expected before the Agency board is the selection of the renewable
energy provider and the purchase power agreement (PPA). It is not expected that cities
                                            41

finalization of the PPA.


In January of 2025, the program design was submitted to the Utah Public Services
Commission for consideration and approval. The Program Design included:
    1. As required by Utah Code Ann. § 54-17-9, the costs for obtaining the energy and
         maintaining the program will be covered through the participating customer
         rates, and not by the Agency members or nonparticipating customers.
    2. Participating customers in member communities will see no more than $3 to

         energy production.
        • Current projects show the monthly cost will start at $3.21 for the average

        • Low-Income Lifeline Program enrollees will receive a credit on the monthly

            additional cost),
3. The amount of energy that the Agency purchases will ensure that it does not cost
   the average resident more than $3-4 per month.
        • The program will be scaled up slowly to keep the cost affordable.
4. RMP customers in member communities who are not interested in participating in
   the program will have the opportunity to opt-out of the program, within the first six
   billing cycles.
5. Each member of the Agency is responsible for paying a portion of the organization’s
   operational costs (legal and technical consultants, RMP program design and filing,
   etc.), which is calculated by both population and electricity usage.

       E. CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. 2025-R-19 ESTABLISHING THE
          MIDVALE 2025 FIREWORK RESTRICTION MAP
 Erinn Summers said Utah State Code (State Code) §15A-5-202.5 limits the authority
 of cities to prohibit fireworks, requiring adherence to specific conditions and
 timelines. State Code also mandates that cities reevaluate the restrictions annually
 to ensure the qualifying conditions still exist. Subsections 202.5(b)(1) and
 202.5(b)(2) of State Code §15A-5-202.5 require that the legislative body:
     1. Only restrict fireworks around defined hazardous areas, which include wild
        land urban interfaces, and mountainous, brush-covered, forest-covered, or dry
        grass-covered areas; and
     2. Designate the restricted area along readily identifiable features like major
        roadways, waterways, or geographic features; and
     3. Ensure that the boundary of the designated restricted area is as close as is
        practical to the defined hazardous area, provided that the closed area may
        include areas outside of the hazardous area to facilitate a readily identifiable
        line; and
     4. Identify the restricted area through a written description or map that is readily
        available to the public.

 Based on their assessment, Midvale City staff and the Unified Fire Authority (UFA)
 believe that State Code §15A-5-202.5 and §53-7-225 grant the Midvale City Council
 the authority to reauthorize the 2024 firework restrictions in Midvale due to the
 continued presence of hazardous conditions.
 Based on the qualifying conditions outlined in §15A-5-202.5, Midvale City continues to
 have elevated fire risk conditions along the Jordan River, within Salt Lake County Parks
 within Midvale, within Midvale City parks (Adams Street Park, Fire Station 126 Park,
 Fire Station 125 Park, Constitution Park, Fort Union Park, and Midvale City Park and
 Cemetery), and throughout most of the Jordan Bluffs neighborhood (Holden Street to

 the Jordan River, south of 7800 South). As the elevated fire risk conditions surrounding
 the Jordan River do not follow a readily identifiable feature, Midvale City Council
 previously adjusted the restricted area along the Jordan River to run along the
 roadways as close to the defined hazardous area as practical, as required in State
 Code §15A-5-202.5(2)(b). The restricted area extends from Center Street north to Euro
 Drive, east along Tuscany Road to Bingham Junction Blvd, then north to Junction View
 Drive, continuing along Fl Smidth Drive, crossing 7200 S to River Gate Drive, and
 ending west at River Reserve Court.
The Midvale City Council must make its own determination of the specific areas where
fireworks should be restricted and enact these restrictions through a resolution or an
ordinance in accordance with State Code. Any action taken must be based on a finding
of existing hazardous conditions and must meet the requirements outlined in State Code
§15A-5-202.5(2)(b).

Staff recommended that the City Council maintain the fire restriction areas approved by
City Council in 2024, as they continue to align with State Code requirements and
mitigate fire risks in historically hazardous locations.
