                                Water Assistance Program - FAQs

   1. What is the Water Assistance Program?

On December 27, 2020, the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2021 was signed into law. This
authorizing legislation included funding in emergency spending to assist low-income households
with water and wastewater bills. On March 11, 2021, additional funding was appropriated in the
American Rescue Plan Act (ARP) of 2021 in emergency spending to assist low-income
households with water and wastewater bills.
The Department of Workforce Services is the state pass-through agency for this funding and it
will be administered through the Housing and Community Development Division - State
Community Services Office.


   2. When will the program start?

The program is being aligned with the HEAT program seasons and will be open for applications
starting October 1st. The month of October is designated for outreach and target groups. This
means that in October we will only be processing applications for households with at least one
member that is 60+, disabled, or under 6 years old. This aligns with HEAT Program application
procedures.

   3. When will the program be open to the general public?

November 1, 2021

   4. What can the program pay for?

The program can pay for four types of water fees/bills -
    ● Drinking water
    ● Wastewater
    ● Storm water
    ● Ground water
The program will also pay for other fees or arrearages if it is required to restore water services
or prevent water shut-off for the household.

   5. Is this an on-going program?

No, this is a time-limited program that will end September 30, 2023. A household may only
receive benefit assistance from the program once in a program season (October-September).

   6. How can I apply for this program?

We are aligning processes with the HEAT Program. The current HEAT application will be
updated to include water. If a household is found eligible for HEAT, they may be categorically
eligible for Water Assistance as well. A customer can apply the same way they apply for HEAT -
a mailed in paper application, walk-in to a local HEAT office (if they are taking walk-ins), online
application through MyCase login, or over the telephone. Customers will be encouraged to
apply for both programs at the same time.

The HEAT agencies will also determine eligibility for the Water Assistance Program.
HEAT Agencies:
   ● Bear River Association of Governments
   ● Futures Through Training
   ● Utah Community Action
   ● Mountainland Association of Governments
   ● Southeastern Association of Local Governments
   ● Six County Association of Governments
   ● Uintah Basin Association of Governments
   ● Five County Association of Governments

   7. What are the eligibility requirements?

The income eligibility requirements are the same as the HEAT Program. These are some other
basic eligibility requirements:
   ● 150% FPL
   ● U.S. Citizen or LPR
   ● Utah resident
   ● Responsible for their own water bill/costs

   8. What is the maximum benefit a household can receive?

There are three priority groups with this program -
             1. Households whose water has been disconnected/shut off
             2. Households with a shut off notice and are in danger of water disconnection
             3. Households that do not meet the first two groups, but need assistance with a
                current bill
The first six months of the program will only be open to households that meet the first two
priority groups. Arrearages and fees will be paid for in the exact amount in order to get water
service restored or a shut-off threat removed. The goal of the program is to restore water
service and prevent water shut-offs.

Within the first six months, the program will re-evaluate the affordability of serving the third
priority group. If the third priority group is served, the maximum benefit a household can receive
is $360. This will be based on a benefit matrix that calculates the benefit based on income level,
water burden, and target group credit.

   8. How are benefits paid out?
Benefits can only be paid directly to a water company. Unlike in the HEAT Program, there are no
circumstances where a benefit can be paid to a customer. In order for a water company to get
paid the benefit on behalf of the customer, we must have a water provider (vendor) agreement
on file and they must be known to the state FINET system.

   9. What outreach efforts will be made to get the word out about the program?

We plan on conducting a social media campaign to advertise the program. We have held two
stakeholder meetings with water providers in the state. Fliers and rack cards will be sent to
water providers to include in their mailings. The program will be added to 211 as a resource.

7.60.030 Reserving City Park Facilities

     A. Prohibited Categories: It shall be unlawful for any person, group or organization to
        reserve any city park facility for commercial use without a special use permit (BCC
        7.70). "Commercial use" means any activity conducted for pecuniary or commercial
        gain including private lessons, classes, or group activities where the lesson, class, or
        activity has a fee or other monetary barrier to entry. “Commercial use” also includes
        any lesson, class, marketing, or commercial activities taking place on park system
        property that is associated with any trade, occupation, profession, business, or
        franchise. “Commercial use” does not include activities conducted on the City’s behalf
        or in conjunction with the City.
              1. "Commercial use" is defined as any group or organization that makes a profit
                 as its primary purpose.
              2. Persons, groups or organizations that charge fees associated with its use of city
                 park facilities shall be presumed to be commercial unless they are a not for
                 profit company or organization whose primary purpose is to build the
                 community, such as the Lion's Club, 4-H or similar organization.
     B. Reservation Procedure: City park facilities may be reserved for use Monday through
        Saturday, from six o'clock (6:00) A.M. to ten o'clock (10:00) P.M. year- round subject
        to the following conditions:
              1. The city reserves the right to withhold reservations for any given day during
                 the period of availability, or cancel an already scheduled reservation, in the
                 sole discretion of the city.
              2. City park facility reservations are made on a first -come, first- serve basis.
                 Reservations may be made up to six (6) months in advance without a Special
                 Event Permit and twelve (12) months in advance with a Special Event Permit
                 (in accordance with BCC 7.70).
              3. The city reserves the right to refuse any reservation if the associated group has
                 any history of violating these regulations, applicable law, or any other history
                 of behavior that the city determines constitutes an abuse of the city park
                 facilities.
              4. A person or organization may reserve city park facilities only up to three (3)
                 days in any given week unless expressly authorized by city officials.
              5. Depending on the facility sought to be used, the city park facility reservations




                                              Page 2
                 may require a fee as set out in the city consolidated fee schedule. The fees
                 may include usage fees, cleaning deposits, and other fees.
              6. Any deposits required pursuant to the city consolidated fee schedule shall be
                 one hundred percent (100%) refundable in the event of a cancellation at least
                 forty- eight (48) hours in advance. Any cancellation less than forty- eight (48)
                 hours in advance shall forfeit twenty -five percent (25%) of their deposit.

7.70.010 Purpose(Added)

The purpose of this chapter is to establish permit requirements for special events. The permit
requirements and other regulations in this chapter are designed to promote, protect, and assure
the safety and convenience of residents and visitors by mitigating potential issues resulting
from the special event. It is not the intent of this chapter to regulate in any manner the content
of speech or infringe upon the right to assemble, except for lawful time, place, and manner
regulations necessary to protect public health, safety, and welfare.

       SECTION 7:            ADOPTION “7.70.020 Scope” of the Bluffdale Municipal

7.70.020 Scope(Added)

This chapter imposes regulatory requirements on certain activities that are defined as "special
events". The requirements imposed by this chapter do not alter, supersede or nullify any
requirements contained in other Federal, State, or local statutes, ordinances, or regulations that
may also regulate special events. These requirements shall be applied in a content-neutral
manner and without discrimination as to race, sex, national origin, political affiliation, or other

       SECTION 8:            ADOPTION “7.70.030 Definitions” of the Bluffdale Municipal
Code is hereby added as follows:


                                          ADO P TIO N

7.70.030 Definitions(Added)

APPLICANT: Any person or organization that seeks a special event permit from the city to
conduct a special event governed by this chapter.

ATHLETIC EVENT: An organized competitive or recreational event in which a group of
people collectively engage in a sport or form of physical exercise, including, but not limited to,
running, jogging, walking, bicycling, or skating, on any city street or sidewalk or other city
property.

BLOCK PARTY: An outdoor party put on by residents of a neighborhood that requires
closing down a city street, sidewalk, or other city property.

CITY EVENT: A special event that is organized and operated by the city, whether directly or
by contract with a third-party operator.

ENTERTAINMENT EVENT: An organized event having its primary purpose the
entertainment or amusement of a group of people, including, but not limited to, parades,
festivals, carnivals, fairs, concerts, or similar activities on any city street or sidewalk or other
city property.

FILMING EVENT: Filming of a media broadcast (other than for news purposes), filming of a


                                                 Page 5
FILMING EVENT: Filming of a media broadcast (other than for news purposes), filming of a
motion picture or television broadcast, or still commercial photography, in any case involving
more than four (4) human participants.

LARGE SCALE EVENT: A special event, not sponsored by the city, with an estimated
combined attendance of five hundred (500) or more participants and spectators.

OUTDOOR SALES EVENT: An organized event in which a group of people or an
organization engages in the sale of products including, but not limited to, displays and
exhibitions, farmers' markets, craft fairs, outdoor sales, tent sales, and other similar activities.

PERSON: Includes an individual, corporation, partnership, association, or other entity whether
public or private.

POLITICAL EVENT: An organized event involving the preplanned participation of more
than fifty (50) people, not including an athletic or entertainment event, having as its primary
purpose the exercise of expressive activities of a political nature, including, but not limited to,
speechmaking, picketing, protesting, marching, demonstrating or debating public issues, on
any city street or sidewalk or other city property.

PUBLIC FORUM: City roads, sidewalks, rights-of-way, and city property that has by tradition
been used by the public for assembly and expression, or city property that has not traditionally
been open for public assembly and debate but that the city has opened for use by the public as
a place for assembly and expression.

SPECIAL EVENT: Any athletic event, city event, entertainment event, filming event, large
scale event, block party, political event, or outdoor sales event, whether held for-profit,
nonprofit or charitable purposes held on private or public property within the city.

SPONTANEOUS EVENT: A special event that is occasioned by news or affairs coming into
public knowledge fewer than fourteen (14) days before the special event.



      SECTION 9:            ADOPTION “7.70.040 Event Types And Threshold For
Review” of the Bluffdale Municipal Code is hereby added as follows:


                                          ADO P TIO N

7.70.040 Event Types And Threshold For Review(Added)

To the extent that events of a short-term nature have an impact on the provision of public
services and potentially affect public health and safety, the city hereby establishes the following
thresholds and review requirements for a variety of events that may occur within the city.

     A. Private Events on Private Property: Examples of this type of event include, but are not




                                                Page 6
       limited to, club meetings at private homes and weddings or receptions at churches or
       other nonpublic buildings. These events require a special event permit only when the
       expected attendance exceeds 250 people, a street is closed, or inadequate parking is
       available.
    B. Private Events on Public Property: Examples of this type of event include, but are not
       limited to, family reunions, weddings, receptions, and birthday parties in city or county
       parks, and club meetings in the courthouse. These events require permitting if
       attendance is expected to exceed a park's capacity, as listed on the city's website, and
       the event does not interfere with the public use of the property.
    C. Public Events on Private Property: Examples of this type of event include, but are not
       limited to, fundraisers at churches or private sites that are non-ticketed events,
       organization yard sales, and use of privately-owned parking lots for public events such
       as carnivals and festivals. All events in which the general public is openly invited and
       there is no ticketing or preregistration require a special event permit since attendance at
       a public event cannot be reasonably anticipated. The purpose of requiring a permit for
       this type of event is to ensure the events will not create unsafe conditions and will not
       negatively affect the provision of public services.
    D. Public Events on Public Property: Examples include, but are not limited to, rodeos,
       concerts, charity events, races, and competitions. All events in which the general
       public is openly invited require a special event permit since attendance at a public
       event cannot be reasonably anticipated. The purpose of requiring a permit for this type
       of event is to ensure that the events will not create unsafe conditions and will not
       negatively affect the provision of public services or unduly disrupt normal vehicular or
       pedestrian traffic.

7.70.050 Application And Standards(Added)

A person seeking a special event permit shall submit an application to the city on forms
provided by the city. The application shall be filed not less than forty-five (45) and not more
than three hundred sixty-five (365) days before the activity is proposed to commence. An

application and plans have been received.

    A. Application And Processing Fees: Application and processing fees shall be paid to the
       city by the applicant when the application is filed with the department to cover the
       administrative costs of processing the permit application.
    B. Late Notification Fee: Any applicant for a special event permit who fails to give at
       least forty five (45) days' notice to the city before the activity is proposed to commence




                                              Page 7
   shall remit to the city a late notification fee of one hundred dollars ($100.00) in
   addition to the application and processing fee.
C. Complete Copy Of SLCo Application and Permits: The application for any special
   event requiring a mass gathering permit, or any other permits, by Salt Lake Valley
   Health Department regulations must include a complete copy of their required
   application.
D. Access: Specific areas shall be designated for ingress and egress of emergency
   vehicles, including appropriate barriers to regulate vehicular and pedestrian traffic.
E. Plans: The following plans must be submitted with the application for a special event
   permit:
         1. Site Plan: The site plan shall depict the proposed layout of the entire property
            to be used for the special event. This site plan must include the exact address
            of the property, the name and address of the property owner, and the name and
            twenty-four (24) hour contact information of the contact person or coordinator.
            The site plan must clearly show all entrances, exits, roadways, walkways, and
            parking, all sanitation facilities, medical and first aid stations, waste containers,
            food stands, vendor areas, lighting, types of merchandise being sold, types of
            food being prepared/sold, alcohol sales, types of live entertainment, sound
            systems, temporary structures, temporary and permanent power sources,
            amusement rides, and all other activities associated with the event.
         2. Control Plan: A plan shall be submitted and approved establishing adequate
            provisions for traffic, crowd, and patron control and assurance of compliance
            with city, State, and Federal laws including, but not limited to, fire, health,
            security, and Americans With Disability Act regulations. Only city public
            safety personnel shall be used for traffic control on city streets, trails, or
            sidewalks for special events. No more than two (2) special events per calendar
            month shall be allowed when public safety personnel such as police, fire, or
            ambulance services are necessary.
         3. Sign Plan: Applicants shall provide a plan showing the location of all signs to
            be placed in association with the event. The sign plan must include the total
            number of signs with the dimensions and size of each sign. All signs must
            meet the requirements of BCC 11.220.
         4. Security Plan: A security plan shall be submitted to and approved by the city
            establishing the number and type of enforcement and security personnel
            needed to monitor and facilitate the event and provide spectator or participant
            control and direction.
         5. Parking Plan: A parking plan shall be submitted to and approved by the city
            identifying areas for parking in a capacity to accommodate the reasonably
            expected attendance at the event. Parking areas shall meet all requirements and
            specifications of the Fire Department. If parking spaces are not marked,
            parking personnel must be provided to ensure orderly and safe ingress and
            egress from the parking area.
F. Emergency Services: First aid supplies, equipment, and emergency medical services
   must be made available to meet public health and safety concerns and legal
   requirements.




                                           Page 8
G. Sanitation Facilities: Adequate sanitation facilities shall be provided based on the
    duration of the event and on the number of persons reasonably expected to participate.
H. Trash Removal: All trash shall be removed and the site restored to the condition the
    property was in before the event before the applicant or event sponsors leave the site.
    All trash must be disposed of properly and any equipment used returned to its
    designated location. It is the sole responsibility of the event organizers to provide
    appropriate means of trash disposal and site cleanup.
 I. Events On Public Property: With proper authorization from appropriate City
    departments, special events may be held in public places or on City-owned property.
    Events on public property must end by ten o'clock (10:00) P.M. on any given day
    unless an extension of time is granted by the Parks and Recreation Director. A written
    request to the Parks and Recreation Director must be submitted with detailed
    information as to the need to extend the end time of an event. This approval must be
    completed before a special event permit is issued.
 J. Events On Private Property: Special events are allowed on private property with the
    property owner's written approval. Notification of the event taking place shall be
    issued to all adjacent property owners before the event. Events on private property
    may not begin before 7:00 am or end later than 10:00 pm.
K. Insurance: Applicants for a special event permit must provide liability insurance to
    ensure the health and safety of the public. Mandatory Comprehensive General/Special
    Events liability insurance with a minimum of one million dollars ($1,000,000.00) per
    occurrence, with two million dollars ($2,000,000.00) general aggregate coverage will
    be required before the issuance of a special event permit, unless the City requires a
    higher or lower level based on level of risk. The liability insurance must also name the
    City as an additional insured by endorsement. Applicants must submit a Certificate of
    Insurance and an actual copy of the additional insured endorsement. The Certificate of
    Insurance and endorsement must be submitted at least seven business days before the
    proposed special event date.
          1. The following special events shall be exempt from the insurance requirement:
                   a. Political events, unless the event will include fireworks or other
                       similarly hazardous features.
                   b. School events located on, or directly adjacent to, school property;
                   c. Events sponsored in whole by the City; and
                   d. Block parties.
          2. In consideration for the issuing of a special events permit and use of City
             streets and sidewalks or City property, the applicant agrees to indemnify, save
             harmless and defend the City, its officers, and employees, against any claim
             for loss, damage, or expense sustained by any person on account of injury,
             death or property damage occurring because of or arising out of the special
             event.
L. Performance Bond: The City may require the applicant or sponsor of a special event,
    in addition to the aforementioned liability coverage, to protect the City's interests with
    a performance bond in a form and amount as determined by the City Parks and
    Recreation Director.
M. Inspections: Authorized Law Enforcement Officers, Code Compliance and Zoning



                                          Page 9
        Enforcement Officers, Fire Control Officers, and other government personnel shall be
        permitted free access to the event to make inspections to ensure compliance with all
        city, State, and Federal laws.
    N. Approval Of Other Public Agencies: The applicant has obtained the approval of any
       other public agency within whose jurisdiction the event or portion thereof will occur.
    O. Facility Reservations: If the application proposes the use of any City facility
       designated and designed to be available on a reservation basis and subject to a facility
       use agreement, the applicant shall also be responsible to comply with all rules and
       regulations applicable to the reservation and use of such facility, and pay all applicable
       fees and deposits before the issuance of the special event permit.
    P. Exclusive Use Of Public Property: The event will not require the exclusive use of the
       park or other public areas in a manner that will adversely impact upon the reasonable
       use or access to those areas by the general public unless such exclusive use has been
       approved by the Parks and Recreation Director under the department's policies.
    Q. Filming Events: For a filming event, the number of dates of filming and the number of
       locations to be used, and a report regarding discussions with property owners or
       occupants in the affected neighborhoods. The applicant shall provide a short written
       description of and schedule for the proposed filming to the owners and occupants in
       the affected neighborhoods (as defined by boundaries set by the Parks and Recreation
       Director). The applicant shall talk with owners and occupants of all such property and
       submit as part of the application a report noting any owner's or occupant's reaction
       along with the addresses and phone numbers of all such property owners and
       occupants.
    R. Large Scale Event Application: For a large-scale event, an applicant shall apply to the
       Parks and Recreation Department not less than ninety (90) days before the event is
       proposed to commence and shall be subject to the nonrefundable large scale event
       application fee. All conditions of a large-scale event permit will be outlined therein.
       Priority for large-scale event permits in Bluffdale City Park shall be based on past
       history of holding the same event in Bluffdale City Park, the value of the event to city
       residents, and order of submission.
    S. Prior Experience: Applicants must submit documentation of prior experience, if any, of
       holding a similar event, including where the event was held and when.
    T. Additional Information: Any additional information that the City finds reasonably
       necessary to a fair determination as to whether a special event permit should issue.



       SECTION 11:            ADOPTION “7.70.060 City Support Services” of the
Bluffdale Municipal Code is hereby added as follows:


                                        ADO P TIO N

7.70.060 City Support Services(Added)


City support services shall be provided for special events as determined by the city to protect


                                              Page 10
City support services shall be provided for special events as determined by the city to protect
the health, safety, and welfare of the public, at cost as stated in the city's consolidated fee
schedule, payable by the organizer.

    A. Payment Of Estimated Support Costs: The city shall not issue a special event permit
       until the organizer pays the estimated support costs as determined by the city based on
       the application and experience with similar events.
    B. Support Costs: City support costs include, but are not limited to, police, fire, and
       emergency medical protection, park maintenance, power, water, road closures, cleanup
       of city facilities before, during, or after the special event, and other costs to the city
       directly attributable to the special event.
    C. Services Limited: City support services shall be limited to the minimum necessary to

       property. Police protection needs shall be determined and assigned by the Chief of
       Police or designee after considering relevant criteria such as the size, location,
       duration, time, and date of the special event; the number of streets and intersections
       blocked; and the need to detour or preempt public travel and use of streets or
       sidewalks. Park maintenance as well as the closure of roads and streets shall be
       administered by the appropriate city departments or divisions.
    D. Payment Of Invoice: The city shall bill, and the organizer shall pay, the remaining
       support costs within thirty (30) days after the date of invoice. Any refund due to the
       organizer shall be paid by the city within thirty (30) days after the special event if
       actual costs are less than the estimate.
    E. Police, Fire, And Traffic Crowd Control: The organizer shall provide police and fire
       protection and traffic crowd control to the extent determined by the Police Chief, the
       Fire Chief, or their designees, and shall pay the fees therefor as stated in the city's
       consolidated fee schedule.



       SECTION 12:            ADOPTION “7.70.070 Conditions Of Issuance” of the

                                        ADO P TIO N

7.70.070 Conditions Of Issuance(Added)

      SECTION 13:            ADOPTION “7.70.080 Grounds For Denial” of the Bluffdale
Municipal Code is hereby added as follows:


                                       ADO P TIO N

7.70.080 Grounds For Denial(Added)

An application for a special events permit may be denied on any of the following grounds:

    A. The permit application is not fully completed or fails to comply with the requirements
       of this chapter.
    B. The permit application contains a material falsehood or misrepresentation.
    C. The applicant is legally incompetent to contract or to sue and be sued.
    D. The applicant or any person on whose behalf the permit application was made has on
       prior occasions:
             1. Damaged city property and has not paid in full for such damage, or has other
                outstanding and unpaid debts to the city;
             2. Made material misrepresentations regarding the nature or scope of an event or
                activity previously permitted;
             3. Has violated the terms of prior permits issued to or on behalf of the applicant;
                or
             4. Has held a special event in another jurisdiction that did not comply with a
                permit or the ordinances of that jurisdiction.
    E. The applicant has failed to comply with all conditions and regulations attendant to the
       issuance of the permit.
    F. The use or activity intended by the applicant is prohibited by the provisions of this
       Code, regulations of the city, or otherwise prohibited by law.
    G. The conduct of the special event will substantially interrupt the safe and orderly
       movement of other pedestrian or vehicular traffic contiguous to its route or location,
       unreasonably limit or obstruct access to private property, or unduly interfere with
       proper fire and police protection of, or ambulance service to, areas contiguous to such
       public assembly areas.




                                             Page 12
    H. The proposed event will unreasonably interfere with or obstruct the public's ability to
        use public property for activities unrelated to the event.
     I. The conduct of the special event will require the diversion of so great a number of city
        police officers to properly police the line of movement and the areas contiguous
        thereto as to prevent normal police protection of the city.
     J. The conduct of the special event is reasonably likely to cause injury to persons or
        property.
    K. Adequate sanitation and other required health facilities are not, or will not be, available
        in or adjacent to any special event areas.
    L. There are insufficient parking places near the site of the special event to accommodate
        the number of vehicles reasonably expected.
    M. A city event is scheduled for the same time and location.
    N. A special event permit application for the same time and location is already granted or
        has been received and will be granted.
    O. An event is scheduled elsewhere in the city where the police resources required for
        that event are so great that the deployment of police services for the proposed special
        event would have an immediate and adverse effect upon the welfare and safety of
        persons and property.
    P. The special event permit application was filed less than:
               1. Forty-five (45) days before the event for events with an estimated combined
                  attendance of fewer than five hundred (500) participants and spectators; or
               2. Ninety (90) days for a Large Scale Event.
    Q. The applicant failed to obtain a special event permit for a previous event that was
        required to have a special event permit.
    R. The Parks and Recreation Director has the discretion to deny a permit for any event
        that is detrimental to the health, safety, or welfare of the public.

     B. Notices of revocation shall be in writing and specifically set forth the reasons for the

        verbally of the revocation.

        offense and as a Class B misdemeanor for subsequent violations.

                                    ORDINANCE 2022-10

   AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF BLUFFDALE,
         UTAH, ENACTING REGULATIONS FOR FOOD TRUCKS.

       WHEREAS, the Bluffdale City Council has authority to enact, amend, or repeal
ordinances that it considers necessary and appropriate; and

       WHEREAS, the City Council desires to enact regulations for food trucks, as shown
below; and

       WHEREAS, the City Council held a public hearing and reviewed the subject text
amendments and finds that the subject text amendments will enhance public health, safety, and
welfare.

       NOW THEREFORE, be it ordained by the Council of the Bluffdale City, in the State
of Utah, as follows:


       SECTION 1:            ADOPTION “3.90 MOBILE FOOD VENDORS” of the
Bluffdale Municipal Code is hereby added as follows:


                                        ADO P TIO N

3.90 MOBILE FOOD VENDORS(Added)



       SECTION 2:            ADOPTION “3.90.010 Definitions” of the Bluffdale Municipal
Code is hereby added as follows:


                                        ADO P TIO N

3.90.010 Definitions(Added)

CITY OWNED PROPERTY: City property shall include all parks, open space, parcels
containing city operations, buildings, rights of way, and all other such properties. The city-
owned property includes all parking lots on these properties.

MOBILE FOOD VENDING: Sale or donation of food or beverages from a mobile food
vendor unit.

MOBILE FOOD VENDOR UNIT (UNIT): An enclosed truck, trailer, or similar vehicle-
mounted unit that is:




                                              Page 1
    A. A licensed motor vehicle or is capable of being moved by a licensed motor vehicle;
    B. Independent concerning water, wastewater, and power utilities; and
    C. Used for the preparation, sale, or donation of food products and beverages.

The definition of a mobile food vendor unit shall not include vending carts or ice cream trucks.



        SECTION 3:            ADOPTION “3.90.020 License Required, Health Department
Permit, Fees, Regulations” of the Bluffdale Municipal Code is hereby added as follows:


                                       ADO P TIO N

3.90.020 License Required, Health Department Permit, Fees, Regulations(Added)

    A. License Required: It is unlawful for any person to operate, conduct, carry on, or
       maintain mobile food vending without a business license. A mobile food vendor who
       has a valid business license from another political subdivision within the state is not
       required to obtain a Bluffdale City business license. All mobile food vendors must be
       able to produce a valid business license, health department food truck permit, and
       evidence of a successful fire safety inspection upon request by a city official.
    B. Separate Licenses Required: A separate business license shall be required for each
       mobile food vending unit.
    C. Health Department Permit And Fire Safety Inspection: Before issuance of a Bluffdale
       City license, vendors must obtain a Salt Lake Valley health department permit. Such
       permits must remain in force during the license period. A vendor must also pass a fire
       safety inspection performed by the city, or show that it has passed a fire safety
       inspection by another political subdivision within the current calendar year.
    D. Fees: The license fees for a mobile food vendor shall be set by resolution of the city
       council and published in the city fee schedule.
    E. Business Activity To Be Temporary: All business activity related to mobile food
       vending shall be temporary and subject to the following requirements:
             1. Permitted hours of operation are seven o'clock (7:00) A.M. to ten o'clock
                (10:00) P.M., except as otherwise authorized by the city.
             2. No overnight parking is allowed, except during a multiday event.
             3. The mobile food vending unit shall not conduct business within city limits for
                more than any part of sixty (60) days per calendar year.




      SECTION 4:            ADOPTION “3.90.030 Permitted Locations” of the Bluffdale
Municipal Code is hereby added as follows:


                                       ADO P TIO N




                                             Page 2
3.90.030 Permitted Locations(Added)

    A. Mobile Food Vending On Private Property: Vendors may only operate on private
       property with the express prior written permission of a person with authority to act on
       behalf of the property owner. The written permission shall include any restrictions
       such as specific location on the property, hours of operation, limitations on employee
       and/or public sales, etc. The vendor, while operating, shall produce the written
       permission upon request of an authorized city official.
    B. Zoning Restrictions: With the exceptions in Paragraph C, mobile food vending is only
       permitted in commercial, professional office, and industrial zones.
    C. Limited Permitted Residential Mobile Food Vending: Mobile food vending is
       permitted in residential and agricultural zones if the property is located on:
            1. Public or private school property,
            2. Church property,
            3. Government-owned (non-city) property, or
            4. Private property with the following conditions:
                      a. No public services such as police, fire, or ambulance, are anticipated
                         to be needed on-site for the event, whether for traffic control, on
                         standby, or for security at the site,
                     b. Adequate restroom facilities are readily available on the site for
                         attendees at the event or activity, and
                      c. Traffic, crowd, or parking control is not needed to accommodate
                         attendees.
    D. Mobile Food Vending On City Owned Property And In City Rights Of Way: Mobile
       food vending is prohibited on city-owned property and city rights of way (including,
       but not limited to, streets, on-street parking areas, and sidewalks) unless permitted in
       BCC 7.70 (Special Events).



      SECTION 5:            ADOPTION “3.90.040 Prohibited Acts” of the Bluffdale
Municipal Code is hereby added as follows:


                                       ADO P TIO N

3.90.040 Prohibited Acts(Added)

    A. Preparation Outside The Mobile Food Vending Unit: Vendors may prepare food and
       beverages outside of the unit (e.g., meat smoking, corn roasting) but such preparation
       shall not obstruct vehicle or pedestrian traffic nor create safety hazards to the public.
       Vendors, however, shall not serve food directly to customers from such outside food
       preparation areas.

    B. Items For Sale: Only food and beverage items, and merchandise branded with the
       mobile food vending logos such as apparel or beverage containers, may be sold from




                                             Page 3
       units. The sale or distribution of other merchandise, professional or personal services,
       or alcoholic beverages is prohibited.
    C. Obstruction Of Traffic: Placement of units or related accessories shall not obstruct or
       impede pedestrian traffic or vehicular traffic, access to and from driveways, or clear
       vision lines for vehicle drivers.
    D. Drive-Through Or Drive-In Service Prohibited: Units shall serve pedestrians only.
       Drive-through or drive-in service is prohibited.




       SECTION 6:            ADOPTION “3.90.050 Compliance With Law” of the
Bluffdale Municipal Code is hereby added as follows:


                                        ADO P TIO N

3.90.050 Compliance With Law(Added)

Unit placement, mobile operation, and any associated accessories must adhere to federal, state,
and local laws, regulations and policies. Local laws, regulations, and policies include, but are
not limited to, the city's zoning ordinances, noise ordinances, stormwater regulations, and fire
and building codes.
