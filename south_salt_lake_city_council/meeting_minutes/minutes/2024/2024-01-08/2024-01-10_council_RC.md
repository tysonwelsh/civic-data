       3. MAYOR COMMENTS.
          Mayor Wood said that she would share a brief statement regarding the Mayor’s
          Appointment item and explain the process of appointing a new police chief, as well
          as some City highlights from the last month.

           Per the South Salt Lake City Code, the Mayor is responsible for recommending a
           candidate and bringing them forward to the City Council for their advice and
           consent. The public process involves a Work and Regular Meeting with the needed
           discussion, if any. She also addressed some false statements that have been
           circulated and clarified that the current Deputy Chief of Police has not been
           demoted but will be reassigned. Per Police Department policy, each Police Chief
           may assign a Deputy Chief at their own discretion.

           Mayor Wood then talked about the City’s application for an HTRZ (Housing and
           Transit Reinvestment Zone) that was unanimously approved, which will bring an
           estimated $185 million dollars in tax increment dollars to the South Salt Lake
           Downtown area over the next 20 years. This will also bring an additional 12% of
           affordable housing units in the zone.

           Other City updates that Mayor Wood spoke on:

           Season of Sharing – 64 families were provided with support this holiday season.
           Mayor Wood thanked the community for their generous giving and Promise South
           Salt Lake for coordinating the drive.

           Arts Council Mural Fest – The open call for artists has already generated 692
           applications. The deadline is 5 p.m. on Friday, January 12th. Interest artists and
           residents may connect through themuralfest.com

           Council Member Bynum shared a message of appreciation for Chief Carruth’s
           service from resident, Michelle Ashburn. She also stated that her goal as Council
           Chair is to ensure that the Council stays focused on the business of the City and that
           it does so in a civil and professional manner. She also noted that as elected officials,
           anyone can file a GRAMA request on them at any time and reminded the Council
           that they should behave and speak in a way that such requests would be of no
           concern.

           Council Member Mitchell shared two very different experiences he has had with the
           South Salt Lake Police Department, one being great and the other being terrible. He
           said that he sees the need for improvement in the department and would like to
           know more about the potential appointee. As a new Council Member, he feels that
           he doesn’t have enough information to decide on at this time and will therefore be
           abstaining in the vote for the Mayor’s Appointment.

           Council Member Williams wished everyone a Happy New Year and talked about the
           City’s approved HTRZ application. As a sitting member on the State’s Association of
           Municipal Councils, she noted how a lot of other cities are trying to get this process
           started and how proud she is that, for being a smaller city, South Salt Lake has taken
           the lead in so many ways. She also shared her excitement about the upcoming
           Mural Fest and thanked Promise South Salt Lake Deputy Director, Edward Lopez, for
           the post they shared about the Season of Sharing’s success this holiday season.

           Council Member Huff shared her perspective on recent City events. Being in her
           now second term, she said that she has seen so much progress regarding general
           functioning, outwards results in terms of economic and community development,
           resident services, the quality, and dedication of the 300+ employees of the City, and
           the City’s financial strength. She noted how the City’s public safety teams continue
           to lead the State in supporting those who experience homelessness with strategies
           like the City’s unique Homeless Resource Officer team and how they work with
           individuals to connect them to housing, treatment, and resources. She talked about
           the $569,000 grant received from the Department of Justice that will be used to
           create a community court that will specifically handle the cases generated from the
           City’s homeless population. She said that 2023 also saw the first full year of the
           Civilian Review Board and that positive changes have been implemented with the
           Board’s feedback. She noted that a supportive City Council has played an important
           role in helping the City and its staff to continue to lead the way with its projects and
           grants funded programs.
