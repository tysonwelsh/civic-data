PC 25-001 DworQ Office Building -Continued from the January 9" meeting. [Design Review
Permit] James Johnson/DworQ LLC, authorized agent/owner is requesting a design review permit
for a new 44,178 square foot, three-story office building located at 1099 West 400 North in the
Commercial Service (CS) zone; TIN 05-093-0003, 05-062-0026 (Ellis Neighborhood).

Staff: Russ Holley reviewed the request for the DworQ building, which is planned as a three story,
44,000 square foot office building that will be used for a call center. The proposed project includes
two parcels totaling approximately 10 acres located at approximately 400 North 1099 West. The
southern parcel is approximately 1-acre and will be where the building and small parking lot will be
located. The northern parcel is approximately 9-acres and will be where the larger 300 stall parking
lot will be located. R. Holley reviewed the future 1200 West Street which will be located to the west
of the proposed project and will run to the north, along the west side of the northern parcel included
in this project, then connect to 600 North. The access to the proposed project is off 400 North,
however there could be a second access added once 1200 West is completed.

The Land Development Code requires any necessary wetland delineation to be completed prior to
the project going to the Planning Commission. This project has started the wetland delineation
review and permitting process with the Army Corp. of Engineers, but the process has not been
completed. The applicant wants to get the planning process complete, and their building plans


prepared and set for financing, but they are aware that they cannot proceed with any building until
the Corp. of Engineers approves their delineation. The Planning Commission will need to determine
if they are comfortable approving this project before the wetland permitting process through the
Army Corp of Engineers is complete, or if that process needs to be complete first.

There has been a lot of fill dirt added to the northern parcel over the years which the Corp. of
Engineers will also investigate to determine if there were any wetland areas illegally filled in.

The Land Development Code allows two options for an approved wetland delineation. Option A is
to not disturb the wetland and have buildings setback 50 feet from any wetland. Option B is to
mitigate the wetland, where the wetland can be covered but must be replaced at a higher ratio and
could be much more costly for the applicant. Option B requires Corp. of Engineers permits.

R. Rolley reviewed the proposed building which meets the required design standards in the Land
Development Code. The building height is proposed at 41 feet 8 inches tall which is 3.5 feet over
the maximum height requirement in the CS zone. The Commission can allow a height variance of
up to 10% and will need to determine if the additional height should be allowed.

R. Holley reviewed the public comments received on this project that brought up concerns with
traffic, parking for the existing businesses, wetlands, and obstructions of views.

Proponent: Cortney Wallace, Architect on the project explained that they have reduced the floor
heights as much as possible to get within that 10% height adjustment. Mr. Wallace also explained
that the 300 employees will have staggard work schedules between 6 am and 6 pm and will not all
be arriving or leaving at the same time.

Travis Johnson said the owner built the existing parking lot where the new building is being proposed
but he is not sure if there was a parking agreement with the other businesses in the existing
development to the west. R. Holley was also asked about a parking agreement and stated that he
was unaware of an existing, recorded parking agreement between the two properties.

Public: Craig Wallin asked where the cars currently using the existing parking lot will park.

Commission: Commissioner Doutre said she has no concerns with the height. Commissioner
Lewis said he is not in favor of allowing the height increase because there is no technical reason for
the extra height, Commissioner Lewis said that exceptions will only breed more exceptions.
Commissioner McNamara agreed with Commissioner Lewis.

Commissioner Heare said the Commission can allow a 10% height adjustment and doesn't feel that
it is an exception. Commissioner McNamara said the public concern with the height should weigh
in on the decision to allow the height. Commissioner Doutre said a three-foot height difference is
not going to appease the neighbors’ concerns and give them a better view.

Commissioner Doutre asked why the Commission should approve the proposed project without the
wetland delineation approved. Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director, said the
standard is that any potential wetland must be delineated by someone qualified to do so. The issue
is whether the Army Corp. will accept the delineation or not and whether they will permit the
proposed wetland impacts.

Commissioner Lewis said the Army Corp. should make their decision before the project comes to
the Planning Commission so the Commission has all the information to base their decision on.


The Commission determined that the project needs to be continued to the February 27" meeting to
allow the applicant additional time for the wetland delineation decision.

Motion: Commissioner Heare made a motion to continue a Design Review Permit for PC 25-001
DworQ Office Building to the February 27" meeting. Commissioner Doutre seconded the motion.

Moved: J. Lucero Seconded: S. McNamara Approved: 4-1

PC 25-004 The Bark Spot Logan Boarding [Conditional Use Permit] Scott Stettler/Blake Parker,
authorized agent/owner is requesting a Conditional Use Permit to operate a pet boarding, grooming,
and daycare located at 390 North 1000 West in the Commercial Services (CS) zone; TIN 05-085-
0009 (Ellis Neighborhood).

Staff: Aimee Egbert, Logan City Planner, reviewed the request for a pet boarding and grooming
business in the Commercial Services (CS) zone located at 390 North 1000 West. The dog daycare
can accommodate 10-30 dogs daily and boarding can accommodate 10-50 dogs overnight in private
sleeping areas. Their cat boarding can accommodate 8 cats in individual sleeping condos that are
separated from the dog boarding area. Their grooming and bathing services will be exclusively for
the pets enrolled in daycare and boarding. There are three fenced, outdoor play areas to the rear
and south of the building. The Land Development Code requires 12 parking stalls for this size of
kennel, the proposed project has 11 parking stalls available and is requesting approval of a 9%
parking reduction. The applicant has stated that customer parking for pick-up and drop-off is
typically five minutes, with customer arrival times varying throughout the day. The Planning
Commission will need to determine if a 9% parking reduction is acceptable.

Proponent: Teanna Flannery, Bark Spot Owner, said that she has owned a location in Layton for
20 years and the parking has not been an issue because of the quick drop-offs.

Public: No public comment was made.

Commission: Commissioner Heare said due to the nature of the business a reduction of one
parking stall is not going to be impactful.
