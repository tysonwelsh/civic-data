 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
       a. Continued discussion of Moderate-Income Housing Strategies
          The City Council reviewed moderate income housing strategy options provided by the
          State at a previous work session. City Planner/Zoning Administrator Larry Gardner
          asked that the Council provide final direction regarding which strategies to submit to
          the State with the Moderate-Income Housing Report due by October 1.

          After a brief review and discussion of each strategy, it was determined that the following
          strategies would be included.
                  (A) Rezone for densities necessary to facilitate the production of moderate-
                  income housing
                  (B) Demonstrate investment in the rehabilitation or expansion of infrastructure
                  that facilitates the construction of moderate-income housing
                  (F) Zone or rezone for higher density or moderate-income residential
                  development in commercial or mixed-use zones near major transit investment
                  corridors, commercial centers, or employment centers
                  (G) Amend land use regulations to allow for higher density or new moderate
                  income residential development in commercial or mixed-use zones near major
                  transit investment corridors
                  (J) Implement zoning incentives for moderate income units in new developments
                  (M) Demonstrate creation of, or participation in, a community land trust program
                  for moderate income housing. [Mr. Gardner voiced concern with this strategy
                  noting he had no experience.]

            a public hearing as soon as possible.

        b. Title 4 – Business License and Regulations Discussion
           Council Member Bloom left the room at 6:01 p.m. and returned at 6:03 p.m.

            Chair Whitelock expressed her desire for the Council to review City Code, and said her
            goal was for West Jordan City to be the most business-friendly city in the State. Code
            Enforcement Manager Brock Hudson updated the Council on implementation of the
            CityWorks software program. Mr. Hudson said he was working with the Legal
            Department to make a few modifications to definitions. He said he did not have
            additional changes to recommend, other than updating Code to meet current practices.

            Vice Chair Green said he had created a personal account on CityWorks. He asked about
            the five separate license categories listed on CityWorks for home occupations, asked if
            the Council had created all the categories, and commented that it seemed like a lot for
City Council Meeting Minutes – August 10, 2022                                                         Page 3


            staff to track. Barbara Larsen responded that some of the categories were needed to
            meet State requirements. The different license categories required different
            information and different fees. Vice Chair Green expressed a desire to simplify the
            business license application process. Mr. Hudson suggested staff begin by putting
            together recommended changes. Council Member McConnehey said he was interested
            in how much of the staff burden was administrative versus legislative. Mr. Hudson and
            Ms. Larsen answered questions from the Council. Chair Whitelock suggested the
            applications needed to be clearer and more simplified.

            Council Member Jacob said he liked that Riverton City zeroed out their business license
            fees. He said the FY 2023 Budget included $17,000 revenue from business license
            application fees and suggested the $17,000 could be removed from the budget that
            evening. Council Member Jacob suggested cutting business license fees 50% in a year,
            50% again the next year, and eliminating business license fees completely the following
            year, with a plan to replace business license fee revenue in the budget with sales tax
            revenue. Chair Whitelock suggested further discussion at another work session.

            Vice Chair Green asked if Section 4-1(c) could be rolled into Title 16. Mr. Hudson said
            that was the direction they were going.
