ZT-1-2022
West Valley City
A zoning ordinance text change to update the definition of hospital; prohibit hospitals in the
BRP, MXD, C-3, LI, and M zones; prohibit community uses in the Decker Lake Station
Overlay Zone; require windows and doors on homes within the RS zone to be Energy Star
rated; and update the list of uses allowed as home occupations.


City staff is proposing an ordinance amendment to:
    1. update the definition of hospital;
    2. prohibit hospitals in the BRP, MXD, C-3, LI, and M zones;
    3. prohibit community uses in the Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone;
    4. require windows and doors on homes within the RS zone to be Energy Star rated; and
    5. update the list of uses allowed as home occupations.

Each of these changes is addressed below.

Update the definition of hospital and prohibit hospitals in the BRP, MXD, C-3, LI, and M zones

Hospitals provide rehabilitation and detoxification services; however, these services aren’t
specifically listed in the definition of a hospital. Adding these services to the definition of the term
hospital will make it clear that providing these services make a business a hospital and not a
medical office or other use.

In staff’s view, hospitals are best positioned when located near the residential areas they serve and
near commercial services for hospitals and their employees. For example, the Jordan Valley
Medical Center West Valley Campus is located in the C-2 zone with residential uses to the north,
east, and west and commercial uses along 3500 South to the south. As proposed, the C-2 and CC
zones would be the zones where hospitals would be allowed.

These two changes together would also limit the number of locations for rehabilitation and
detoxifications services.

Prohibit community uses in the Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone

The Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone covers the entertainment area along Decker Lake Drive
largely between 3100 South and 3500 South. As the entertainment area for the City, several of the
businesses within the Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone serve alcohol. State law requires certain
separation requirements between establishments that serve alcohol and community uses like
schools and churches. In the past, an existing establishment that served alcohol would be allowed
to continue if a community use was later placed within the required separation distance even if the
establishment serving alcohol changed hands. However, recent State law changes would no longer
allow such a business to change ownership if a community use was placed within the required
separation distance. If this specific change is approved, it would be the only location where
community uses are prohibited in the City.

Require windows and doors on homes within the RS zone to be Energy Star rated

The City Council wants homes built within the RS zone to meet or exceed Energy Star standards.
With the exception of this proposed change, the standards in the RS zone already meet or exceed
Energy Star standards.
Update the list of uses allowed as home occupations

Currently, subsection 7 of Section 7-7-115 states that uses that are prohibited or conditional in the
C-1 zone are prohibited as home occupations. However, other home occupation regulations further
limit the types of uses allowed, creating some confusion on the part of applicants. For example, the
C-1 zone allows retail stores as a permitted use; however, the home occupation regulations prohibit
direct retail sales from display shelves. As another example, the C-1 zone allows restaurants;
however, the home occupation regulations cap the number of customers per hour to no more than
two and staff is concerned that a take-out type restaurant within a home would be out of character
for a residential neighborhood.

For these reasons, staff is proposing to tie allowed home occupation uses with uses allowed in the
RB (Residential Business) zone. The exceptions to this standard would be financial institutions and
veterinary hospitals would be prohibited and artist studios and non-retail neighborhood service
establishments like hair salons would be permitted.

For reference, a spreadsheet comparing the permitted and conditional uses for the RB and C-1
zones is included.

Ken Milne with Arcadia SP, Inc. has submitted a zone change application for a 1.93-acre parcel

Surrounding zones include R-1-8 to the north and east, R-1-10 to the west (Single Unit Dwelling
Residential, minimum lot size 10,000 sq. ft.), and A to the south. As a reminder, City ordinance
only allows zone changes to R-1-8 on properties less than 2 acres. Surrounding uses include single
unit dwellings on all sides. The subject property includes a home that was built in 1931.

lots with 3 of the 7 being flag lots. The existing home would remain on one of the lots. The average

Castlewood Development
2940 West 3650 South
Conditional use approval for a 329-unit apartment development
3.02 acres


This application was continued at the recommendation of staff on May 11th and again on May 25th
at the request of the applicant.

Castlewood Development is requesting conditional use approval to redevelop 3.02 acres at 2940
West 3650 South into a 329 unit, 3 to 7 story, Class A apartment development. The proposed unit
mix for the project is 36 studio units, 200 one-bedroom units, and 93 two-bedroom units. The
majority of the property (2.94 acres) is zoned CC (City Center) and the balance (0.08 acres) is
zoned R-2-8 (Two Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square feet). The entire
building is within the CC zone. The property is designated as very high density residential (over 20
units/acre) or office for most of the property and promenade/plaza for the remaining north portion
in the Fairbourne Station Vision, which is part of the City’s General Plan. The property is currently
vacant as the office development previously on the property was demolished in 2014.

During a study meeting there were questions raised about parking and possible future
improvements along 3650 South. While there are some locations on 3650 South where on-street
parking can occur now, the frontage of the subject property is not one of those locations.
According to the Public Works Department, 3650 South will be improved between 3200 West and
2700 West in 2024 or 2025. Planned improvements include consistent curb, gutter, and sidewalk
along both sides of the street as well as a left turn lane in the middle of the street. Once these
improvements are made, no on-street parking will be allowed.

For a project of this size, the ordinance requires a minimum of five amenities from the list in the

The ordinance requires a minimum of 10% of the site area as landscaping and a minimum of 40%
of the site area as landscaping and recreation space. The landscaping percentage for this project is

Architecture
Upon review of the latest plans against the City’s architectural requirements, the plans are deficient
in three ways. First, on exterior materials, the amount of brick shown is 16.2% of the exterior and
the required percentage is 20%. PVC or vinyl siding comprises 13.6% of the exterior and the
ordinance only allows this material as trim, soffit and/or accent material. The applicant has
indicated that the plans will be modified to address these issues.

Second, the south elevation is short on relief treatments. The applicant has stated that the plans will
be modified to incorporate additional recessions on the south elevation.

Third, the eastern portion of the north elevation is lacking in detail. The applicant is working on
some revisions to address this issue.

Staff is also concerned about the look of the decorative metal screening proposed for the parking
structure portion of the building. The applicant will bring a sample of this material for the
Commission to see.

C-14-2022
Wasatch Commercial Outside Storage
1820 W 2300 S
M Zone (3.01 acres)

Wasatch Commercial Builders is requesting conditional use approval for outside storage on their
property. The parcel is zoned M, Manufacturing, and the West Valley City General Plan
designates this area as light manufacturing. The surrounding zoning is also M. Adjacent land
uses include light industrial, office, and warehouse.

The applicant has an office at this location and would like to use the back portion of the property
as a storage yard. The applicant also leases out portions of the building to three other tenants
who will use the area for storage. In 2019 the applicant received a building permit for an
extensive interior remodel and some minor exterior improvements. At that time there was no
change in use on the property, so site and landscaping modifications were not required. The
building permit plans showed additional parking north of the building. The applicant’s intent for
that area has changed, and they are now seeking outside storage approval for that space. There
have been some enforcement issues on the property, mainly because there is currently no
conditional use approval for storage. The outside storage area covers approximately 1.45 acres
of the parcel. The applicant provided a letter outlining all the different items that are stored on
the property (see attached).

The applicant still needs to provide adequate parking on the property for the various uses and
businesses in the building, since existing parking areas on the north have been removed. There is
10,309 sq. ft. of office space in the building and 47,367 sq. ft. of warehouse space. Per the
parking requirements in the code, there needs to be a minimum of 65 parking stalls on the
property. There should be adequate existing parking on the property, but the applicant needs to
provide more information on the number of stalls on the west side of the storage yard and
building. These stalls should be properly striped and kept clear.

Outside storage is a change of use on the property to a more intense use. 7-6-303(6) of the City
Code requires that when there is a change to a more intense use that any areas that do not
conform to landscaping requirements on the property be brought into compliance. On the east
side of the property is a public street, 1800 West. 20’ of landscaping from the property line is
required next to a street. This will only apply to the outside storage area on the site where the
change of use is happening. The applicant has not submitted a landscape plan at this time, but
the landscaping needs to include a 20’ wide landscaped area, measured from the property line,
and there needs to be at least 50% live plant material provided within that area. If the
Commission is comfortable approving the use at this point, staff will work with the applicant to
make sure that a landscape plan is submitted and meets all code requirements.

Per 7-7-119, outside storage areas are to be screened from public view. There is existing chain
link fencing along the east and north property lines. The fence on the east side needs to be

moved so that it is located outside of the 20’ landscaped area. The code allows the screening to
be accomplished through fencing, landscaping, building placement, topography or berming. The
existing chain link fence has a mesh privacy screen on the east side. If that fence is moved to
meet the 20’ setback and the required landscaping is added, then that side should be adequately
screened. The north side is not directly adjacent to a public street, but the property is visible
from SR-201. The easiest way to screen that side of the property is by adding slats or a screen to
 the existing chain link fence.

 Surfacing for outside storage uses is also evaluated by the Planning Commission. There is a
 large area that is already hard surfaced with asphalt. The other areas are graveled or
 unimproved. The applicant is proposing to finish the non-asphalt areas with recycled and oiled
 asphalt. That surface is suitable for equipment storage and tracked equipment. Vehicles and
 other rubber-tired equipment should remain on asphalt. Any permanent cargo containers should
 also be placed on asphalt or concrete and meet the other requirements in 7-7-106(5).

Next Step Warehouse (Light Industrial/Warehouse
Complex) 3703 W. Parkway Blvd.
M zone, 3.95 acres

The applicant, Next Step Warehouse, is requesting conditional use approval for a multi-tenant
light industrial/warehouse complex at 3703 W. Parkway Blvd. The zoning for this property is
M, Manufacturing and the General Plan designation is Light Manufacturing. This property is
also located in the Bangerter Highway Overlay Zone and contains an easement for the
(JVWCD) Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District/Bureau of Reclamation Aqueduct. The
surrounding zone is M, Manufacturing to the north, east and west with C-3, Transitional
Commercial to the south and east. The surrounding uses include a print shop to the west,
marketing/sales and auto services to the east currently on the same parcel, and Lake Crest
Business Park to the southeast which has similar uses to what is being proposed.

The requested use for this property is a light industrial warehouse complex. It would provide
flex space for a variety of small business including contractors and service providers. The
development would include five (5) buildings ranging in size form 8,190 sqft – 11,790 sqft for a
total of 50,044 sqft. Based off the proposed uses the project would not be subject to the
Commercial Design Standards however it is also located within the Bangerter Highway overlay
zone which does have some architectural and screening standards.

       7-14-303.DEVELOPMENT STANDARDS.


       Development within the Bangerter Highway Overlay Zone shall comply with the following
       standards:


           (1) Architecture. All Building elevations shall be designed such that there is equal
               emphasis on all sides of the Building. No Building side shall appear neglected
               or downgraded from the other Building sides regardless of visibility.
           (4) Screening. Service and loading areas shall be screened from the adjacent
               Arterial Streets. Service areas and loading docks shall not be visible from any of
               the Bangerter Highway orientations and shall also be screened with walls or
               appropriate Landscaping when adjacent to interior circulation Streets.

The buildings will be constructed with a variety of colored and textured metal panels and block
with metal awning accents. There will be large overhead doors along the front of the buildings
to accommodate a variety of uses. Building C will also have overhead doors to the rear of the
building to accommodate outside storage yard space along the east side. This is the only outside
storage area provided on site and will be screened with a masonry wall on all sides. (Limited to
25% outside storage per 7-14-304).

The rear façade of building B is oriented towards Bangerter Highway and no overhead doors
are on that west façade. Staff recommends some architectural detail be added to the building
and landscaping be added there due to its visibility from Bangerter Highway and the
landscaping/screening restrictions in the JVWCD Aqueduct easement. This easement is 50’
wide, 25’ on each parcel, and prohibits trees and permanent structures like walls and fences.
Pavement and shallow rooted shrubs are allowed.

The west façade of Building E is also oriented towards Bangerter Highway so the overhead
doors there will be glass to soften that building elevation. The north and south ends of all of
the buildings will be a mix of split and smooth face block to provide a textured finish.

Adequate parking has been proposed to accommodate a mix of warehouse and light industrial
type uses. There are 84 stalls proposed which is 1 stall for every 600 sqft of building area. A
warehouse use requires 1/2000 sqft and Light Industrial is 1/800 sqft. Staff will continue to
monitor uses as businesses occupy the site to ensure the parking provided can accommodate
the tenant mix.

The landscaping along Parkway Blvd, which is a High Image Arterial, will be provided
as required on site. All areas not occupied by parking or buildings shall be landscaped.

There are several dumpsters provided throughout the complex. These will all be located in
masonry enclosures designed to match the buildings. Signage has not been proposed at this
time but they are permitted wall signs and a monument sign and all signage must meet the
standards set forth in Title 11.
