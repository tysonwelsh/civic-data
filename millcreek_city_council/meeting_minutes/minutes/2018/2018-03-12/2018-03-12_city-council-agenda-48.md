3. Discussion on Adding a Center Turn Lane to 300 West (potentially eliminate on
street parking)

Police Department (UPD) did a blitz on unregistered vehicles on the street and cars were
impounded as a result. There were cars parked on the street that were inoperable and they
have since been moved to inside the business complexes. UPD is recommending marking the
street as no parking. Miller said there were 2 fatalities on the street last year. Miller asked
what precedence the Council would like to set as a city moving forward. Council Member
Marchant said he is in favor of enforcing removal of inoperable vehicles from the street but
does not see a problem with allowing operable vehicles to park there during the day. Mayor
Silvestrini said businesses in the area were doing unlicensed activity. Miller said UPD is

Legislative Report; Dave & Ashley Spatafore

Dave Spatafore said the Utah Legislative session ended last week. He said he would go over
bills that passed that are good, bills that passed that are not good that affects Millcreek, and
bills that failed.

HB 259 Moderate Incoming Housing Amendments - Spatafore said it is good for all the
cities around the state. HB 269 Identity Theft Paraphernalia Provisions — Mayor Silvestrini
would like to talk to the Spatafores offline about the fiscal note. HB 430 Affordable Housing
Amendments — Spatafore said a proactive group will be looking at needed policies. SB 57
Police Service Animal Amendments — Spatafore helped get the necessary votes in the Senate.
SB 136 Transportation Governance Amendments — Spatafore explained that a quarter of a
cent goes to asphalt projects in counties of the first class. The bill provides transparency for
the distribution of those funds. The bill changes the UTA (Utah Transit Authority) name to
Transit District of Utah (TDU) and the governance of it. Spatafore said HB 462 Homeless
Services Amendments and SB 235 Homeless Shelter Funding Amendments were introduced
with different concepts on homelessness; HB 462 was about shelters being funded by $6
million in state contributions and SB 235 was about public safety services primarily for
South Salt Lake and Midvale. Spatafore said with SB 235, all local governments will be
affected the same on a per person basis. The money will be for providing police officers
around those shelters. South Salt Lake currently needs 12 new police officers and 12 new
paramedics, and Midvale needs 6 additional officers. The money is appropriated to them by
the statewide homeless coordinating committee through the social services appropriations
subcommittee. All cities must show the need, what they are getting, and receipts. Spatafore
explained the funding mechanism with a $200,000 cap for cities. There will be $2.5 million
available the first year and $5 million the following years for as long as there is a need. The
estimated impact on Millcreek will be about $120,000.

HB 124 Water Holdings Accountability and Transparency Amendments failed. Mayor
Silvestrini said it required cities that sell surplus water to provide a report on what their costs
are to their own residents versus the surplus customers. Spatafore said HB 175 Legislative

2. Planning Matters
2.1 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 18-17, Amendment to Chapter
19.80 of the Millcreek Code Regulating Off-Street Parking
Francis Lilly said the purpose of the ordinance is to simplify and clarify requirements,
to modernize standards for off-street parking, and to establish a procedure for parking
and traffic studies that are used to justify a reduction in standard parking
requirements. The ordinance makes sure that if a developer requests a traffic study,
that the City commissions the study then passes the cost on to the developer. That
way the study can account for what the City wants it to account for. He wants the
Planning Commission and staff to have consistency on how to evaluate studies over
time. He has drafted a RFQ for a pool of consultants for traffic studies. Mayor
Silvestrini asked about the predictability of fees. Lilly would like to request that the
engineers give an up front hourly fee and the developer will pass the money through
the City.

Lilly said other changes in the ordinance are: for compact parking stalls, pervious
pavement is required when parking stalls are in excess of 10 percent of what is
required, parking lot lighting, new parking lots must be designed so that vehicles can
exit onto a major street without having to back out onto it, and parking of recreational
and commercial vehicles. Council Member Jackson questioned the 10ft. behind the
property line parking requirement. Miller said the property line is typically a foot
behind the sidewalk. Lilly said this ordinance (19.80.035) is an education tool. The
setback applies unless the property does not have curb, gutter and sidewalk. Council
Member Marchant said it seems it would be easier to determine a distance if it is
measured from.the street and not the property line. Mayor Silvestrini said it would be
from the right-of-way, not the street. Council Member Marchant asked if it said 10ft.
from the street would resolve the situation. Miller said sight distance is what is being
discussed. Miller said it is based on speed and distance from the street, and in most
instances this standard will be fine except on 3300 S. Silvestrini suggested putting in
a discretionary term describing visibility. Lilly said that for residential streets,
recreational vehicles must be parked behind the property line and at least 10ft. back
from the street. Miller recommended sticking to 1 0ft. on all roads, but exceptions
could be granted. Mayor Silvestrini wants a defined measurement instead of basing it
on discretion. Robert May suggested 10ft. behind the street or public right of way,
whichever is greater.

Lilly went over other changes in the ordinance: parking by land use, community
parking credits, parking structures, and existing parking problems.

Lilly identified goals of the parking ordinance, which are:

e Resolve recent code compliance concerns raised by residents regarding
recreational vehicle parking.

e Observations of several parking and traffic studies recently submitted by
applicants seeking a reduction in required parking standards.

e An improved format for communicating parking requirements.

e Flexibility in stall widths and depths to accommodate compact vehicles, and to
meet specific site conditions, where appropriate.

e Incorporate changes to parking requirements in the RM zones into a city-wide
requirement for multifamily parking.

e Adopt best practices for parking ratios to account for bedrooms instead of
dwelling units for multifamily projects, and ratios that account for the size and

Lilly said the ordinance comes from input and recommendations from all community

2.2 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 18-18, Rezone Property located
at 911 E. Fairclough Dr. from R-1-8 to R-1-4 by applicant Itguapia LLC
Robert May said this a two-part application. The applicant is proposing splitting lot
2 into two lots, and to do so a rezone needs to take place. He said the housing type
is the same in the two zones. There are two existing foundations on lot 2.

Per Millcreek code 18.08.020, there are cases in which the City Council may grant
exceptions to the title, which includes setbacks. He said the rear setback would not
be met with the current plan unless an exception was granted. The community
council and Planning Commission have given favorable recommendations for
approval. May said some public comments expressed concern about on-street
parking capacity. May said the building permits would require a conditional use
permit, so they would be approved by the Planning Commission.

Mayor Silvestrini read the conditions recommended by staff, which were in the staff
report:

1. The applicant shall provide a minimum of 2 stalls per dwelling unit plus any
additional parking stalls requested by the Millcreek Planning Commission.

2. The design of the site and building shall comply with all applicable
development standards and any site development standards required by the
City Engineer and Fire Marshal.

3. Prior to receiving final plat approval, the applicant shall certify that existing
utility services have the capacity to accommodate additional demand induced
by the development, or that the services will be upgraded to meet the
additional demand.
