A.   ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING APPLICATION ZT-8-2020,
     FILED BY WEST VALLEY CITY, REQUESTING A ZONE TEXT
     CHANGE TO ADD SPECIFIC FENCE REGULATIONS TO PROPERTIES
     ALONG 4100 SOUTH
     Mayor Bigelow informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular
     Council Meeting scheduled January 26, 2021, in order for the City Council to hear
     and consider public comments regarding Application ZT-8-2020, filed by West
     Valley City, requesting a Zone Text Change to add specific fence regulations to
     properties along 4100 South.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

              The City’s Public Works Department is requesting a zone text change to
              Sections 7-2-114, which is the Section of the Zoning Ordinance that
              addresses fence regulations. The proposed change would add specific fence
              regulations to properties along 4100 South.

              The following justification for this change was provided by the Public
              Works Department:

              “The City has invested millions of dollars to the reconstruction of the 4100
              South roadway. Inside the project, it is planned to replace current fencing
              with a wrought iron (steel/aluminum) fence. As a main artery through the

INSTALLATION OF FENCING ALONG 4100 SOUTH
Mayor Bigelow discussed proposed Resolution 21-10 that would award a Contract to Evans
West, Inc. for the installation of fencing along 4100 South.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       As previously discussed with the City Council, the City seeks to enhance the 4100
       South corridor between Bangerter Highway and 5600 West by replacing existing
       chain link fences with ornamental iron fence. During the reconstruction project,
       chain link fences impacted by construction work were upgraded to ornamental
       fence with the project. This project provides an opportunity to upgrade the
       remaining chain link fences along the project corridor.

       The Public Works Department issued a Request for Proposals to identify the
       fencing contractor that would provide the best solution for the various fence and
       gate scenarios that would be encountered on the project. The selection criteria
       included price, solution flexibility, company experience, materials warranty, and
       installation warranty. Two contractors submitted proposals and Evans West, Inc.
       was chosen based on the specified selection criteria.

       The Public Works Department is preparing agreements for the individual property
       owners to sign giving the city consent to replace their chain link fences. The
       quantity of fence shown in the RFP was an estimated quantity, and when applied to
       the prices submitted by Evans West Inc, total $386,601. The actual quantity of
       fence placed will be based on the number of residents who consent to the fence
       upgrade. The public works department is requesting spending authorization up to
       $450,000 to cover potential fencing scope increases above the estimated scope.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Bigelow there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and he called for a motion.

       Councilmember Christensen moved to approve Resolution 21-10.

RESOLUTION 21-11: APPROVE A MODIFICATION TO A PROFESSIONAL
SERVICES AGREEMENT WITH AVENUE CONSULTANTS, INC. FOR A
STUDY OF TRAFFIC PATTERNS AND FUTURE IMPROVEMENTS ON
BANGERTER HIGHWAY
Mayor Bigelow discussed proposed Resolution 21-11 that would approve a modification to
a Professional Services Agreement with Avenue Consultants, Inc. for a study of traffic patterns
and future improvements on Bangerter Highway.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

   The City Council previously approved an agreement with Avenue Consultants for the
   above referenced work, on September 8, 2020. As the work has progressed, and with
   added input from the City Council, an expansion of the original scope of the project
   was needed to meet the City’s desires. The additional work is shown below:

          Task 1: Project Management & Meetings
              o Included time for 3 additional meetings with Project Team.
          Task 2: Traffic Analysis
              o Removed one option from the traffic evaluation (totaling three options
                  being evaluated)
              o Added three travel demand models – one for each option. These
                  TDM’s will be used to help to predict the changes in traffic
                  distribution and patterns for each of the three options. This helps
                  provide more accurate and reliable results. Measures of effectiveness
                  such as delay and queue lengths will be extracted from the models and
                  used to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed modifications.
          Task 3: Conceptual Design Layouts
              o Included time for one additional concept layout (totally three options
                  being laid out.)

Upon inquiry by Mayor Bigelow there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and he called for a motion.

       Councilmember Nordfelt moved to approve Resolution 21-11.

       Councilmember Fitisemanu seconded the motion.
