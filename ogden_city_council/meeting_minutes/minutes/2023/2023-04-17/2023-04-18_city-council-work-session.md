Ogden Nature Center Annual Report
The City Council has requested to receive information from local foundations and nonprofit organizations that have a
contractual agreement with Ogden City. These presentations help the Council to become more familiar with the objectives and
efforts of the organizations.
Jonathan Creel, Executive Director, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide an overview of the annual
report; this included a description of the Center’s organizational mission and goals, 2022 highlights, Informal Science Education
Enhancement (iSEE), community programs/events, major projects, conservation efforts, hydrology restoration, invasive species
management, pollinator habitat restoration, bird research, community collaboration, and goals for 2023.
There was brief discussion among the Council and Mr. Creel regarding volunteer recruitment efforts; the status of the
wetlands in and around the Nature Center; and progress towards converting facilities at the Nature Center to ‘net-zero’ facilities.
Vice Chair Richey commented on his experience as a board member at the Nature Center and all that he has learned about the
facility and its history; the Nature Center is truly a treasure in the community.

Mr. Cooper then stated he wanted to bring to the Council’s attention a project that the Department is working on between
17 Street and the Ogden River Bridge on Washington Boulevard. He presented a site plan for the project and noted the mixed-
use project will include commercial development, improvements to the bank of the Ogden River, and market rate apartment units.
The details of the project will be presented in the future as Administration will be seeking participation from the Redevelopment Oo
Agency (RDA). Council member Hyer stated he was at the Planning Commission meeting with the concept for this project was
discussed and he asked if the applicant has incorporated the feedback provided by the Planning Commission. Mr. Cooper
answered yes; the applicant will work closely with Planning staff and the Planning Commission to ensure they have an
understanding of the type of development allowed/preferred in downtown Ogden.
Chair Choberka then referenced an issue that has been reported to the Council regarding the reuse of the Jakes Over The
Top property and she asked for Mr. Cooper’s perspective on that matter. Mr. Cooper stated he has heard complaints from the
public about a rumor of a car wash to be constructed on the property; he reached out to the Planning Division about the issue and
is awaiting a full report. However, it is his understanding that the owner of Jakes Over The Top chose to close their business and
sell their property, which is their right. The potential buyer desires to construct a tunnel car wash on the property, which is a
conditional use according to the zoning of the property. Chair Choberka asked ifthe development application would be presented
to the Council. Mr. Cooper indicated conditional use applications do not come before the Council. There was a brief discussion
among the Council, Council staff, and Mr. Cooper regarding the types of conditions that can be placed upon a property for which
a conditional use permit is granted, which Chair Choberka noting she is very concerned about the impact the use could have on
surrounding properties in terms of noise and traffic. Mr. Cooper stated the area in which the Council has the best ability to make a
change is through the General Plan update project, which should be completed in the upcoming fiscal year (FY); the Council can
consider zoning throughout the City and permitted/conditional uses in each zone. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith noted
that will not have an impact on the Jakes Over The Top property, however.
Council member Hyer stated the City’s noise ordinance may not be sufficient to prevent noise disturbances that can not
be considered ‘disturbing the peace’; he wondered if the City needs to review and consider amendments to the noise ordinance.
Ms. Eller-Smith then noted that the Council has heard a repeated complaint recently regarding the length of time it takes
for aredevelopment project to move through the approval process. She asked Mr. Cooper to provide an overview of the timeline
for a recent project that has been acted upon by the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) Board. Mr. Cooper stated that every RDA
project is uniquely different, but he used the Capitol Square project as an example; this project required property assemblage, a
great deal of feasibility analysis and predevelopment work, negotiation of project specific agreements, and development of a
funding package. This led to discussion of environmental remediation efforts on the Capitol Square site and the status of other
properties that have been acquired for potential RDA projects.

Housing Access Opportunity Calculator: Council member White discussed the Wasatch Front Regional Council’s
, ) (WFRC) creation of the Housing Access Opportunity Calculator (HAOC); she shared information about the tool at a recent :
so conference and she shared the same presentation with the Council and explained how the calculator works to generate a score for fl
any given property to communicate the types of housing that are most appropriate and for which there are adequate services. The t
calculator can be found on the WFRC’s website, and anyone can use it to learn the housing access score for any property in the
City. Mr. Cooper built upon Council member White’s presentation by discussing the relationship between housing access and
zoning and whether the type of housing needed in an area or driven by the market is allowed under the zoning that has been
assigned to a given property. This led to discussion among the Council and Mr. Cooper about the factors that have influenced the
manner in which Ogden and other communities have built out; the reasons a developer may request a zone change for a property;
and priorities in Ogden and surrounding communities that have influenced and guided development.
