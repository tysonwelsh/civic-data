Background 
The Range Hill Subdivision was recorded on November 16, 2004, as part of the overall 
Promontory Specially Planned Area. It consists of two (2) total Lots, each containing 
approximately 2 acres. Lot 1 of the Range Hill Subdivision is a corner Lot but was assigned only 
one address (7867 N Promontory Ranch Rd). 
 
The Range Hill Subdivision includes a Platted Preservation Area (see image below) and an 
existing trail easement. The current Plat contains a Special Note stating,  
 
“Any property in a Preservation Area identified in this plat must comply with certain 
requirements of the development Agreement that are generally summarized as follows: 
no development of structures intended for occupancy is permitted within the 
preservation area. A driveway, road or other path is, however, permitted within the 
preservation area.”

vacation, alteration or amendment, the Land Use Authority, may vacate, alter or amend the 
final subdivision plat, or any portion of the final subdivision plat.

Background 
In 2020, the applicant applied for a sketch plan to get preliminary feedback for a proposed rezone from 
AG-40 to AG-10 and a 64-lot subdivision on the subject parcels. The Planning Commission held two (2) 
work sessions, and the applicant continued working on the applications. On December 30, 2021, the 
applicant submitted formal rezone/MPD and subdivision/MPD applications for The Farms at Blue Sky. 
 
Between 2022 and 2024, the applicant and staff had numerous discussions regarding the applicant’s 
intent with the Farms at Blue Sky. The Community Development Director determined that in addition to 
the rezone and subdivision, the request was also an expansion of the existing Blue Sky Ranch, located 
directly west of the subject property. Blue Sky Ranch was processed through a Conditional Use Permit 
under the use of a “Guest Lodge”. On August 26, 2024, the applicant submitted a formal CUP/MPD to 
expand the existing Blue Sky Ranch. The applicant also increased the number of lots from 64 to 76; 72 
lots were intended for single family dwellings and 4 lots contained amenities. The Planning Commission 
held a public hearing on November 7, 2024.  
 
On January 23, 2025, the applicant withdrew the rezone/MPD and CUP/MPD applications. They also 
revised the subdivision plat from 76 lots to 31 lots. The base density is 21 lots, but the applicant is 
utilizing the bonus density provisions of the Code. Section 11-4-12 of the Code requires a master 
planned development process for a subdivision with a request for bonus density. 
 
Bonus Density Calculation 
An applicant is eligible to apply for a bonus density subdivision if they provide a certain amount of deed 
restricted open space for public or private agricultural, scenic, or recreational purposes. Deed restricted 
open space does not include open areas in private individual residential lots, public roads, private roads, 
parking spaces and drive aisles in parking lots, land covered by structures not designated for active civic 
recreational use, and outdoor storage areas. 
 
The bonus density calculation is as follows: 
 
OS = (Z x L) - (0.7 x A) 
Open Space = (zone X number of dwelling units proposed) – (0.7 X area of parcel being subdivided) 
 
For property in the AG-40 zone district 
• Area of Parcel to be Divided = 554.59 acres 
• Base Density = 13.86 units 
• Density Requested = 21 lots 
• Bonus Density Calculation = (40 x 21) – (0.7 x 554.59) = 451.79 acres of open space  
 
For property in the AG-10 zone district 
• Area of Parcel to be Divided = 85 acres 
• Base Density = 8.5 units 
• Density Requested = 10 lots 
• Bonus Density Calculation = (10 x 10) – (0.7 x 85) = 40.5 acres of open space 
 
TOTAL OPEN SPACE REQUIRED (and provided) = 492.29 acres 
No more than 50% of 492.29 acres can consist of undevelopable land = 246.15 acres contain slopes

15. Section 11-4-12 of the Code requires a master planned development process for a subdivision 
with a request for bonus density. 
16. An applicant is eligible to apply for a bonus density subdivision if they provide a certain amount 
of deed restricted open space for public or private agricultural, scenic, or recreational purposes.  
17. Deed restricted open space does not include open areas in private individual residential lots, 
public roads, private roads, parking spaces and drive aisles in parking lots, land covered by 
structures not designated for active civic recreational use, and outdoor storage areas. 
18. The calculation is as follows: 
OS = (Z x L) - (0.7 x A) 
Open Space = (zone X number of dwelling units proposed) – (0.7 X area of parcel being 
subdivided) 
19. For property in the AG-40 zone district 
o Area of Parcel to be Divided = 554.59 acres 
o Base Density = 13.86 units 
o Requested Density = 21 lots 
o Bonus Density Calculation = (40 x 21) – (0.7 x 554.59) = 451.79 acres of open space  
20. For property in the AG-10 zone district 
o Area of Parcel to be Divided = 85 acres 
o Base Density = 8.5 units 
o Requested Density = 10 lots 
o Base Density Calculation = (10 x 10) – (0.7 x 85) = 40.5 acres of open space 
21. TOTAL OPEN SPACE REQUIRED (and provided) = 492.29 acres. 
22. No more than 50% of 492.29 acres can consist of undevelopable land = 246.15 acres contain 
slopes greater than 30%

chapter 2 of the Code.

Subject: Farms at Blue Sky Water Use Summary

Memo 
The following is a summary of the Water Use demands for the Farms at Blue Sky Subdivision and a summary of the 
measured water supply capacity of the newly drilled will for the project.  
The Blue-Sky Subdivision includes 72 residential lots, an amenities area, consisting of 6 buildings, a future Wastewater 
Treatment Plant and an existing residence (Stillman Residence). Residential water demands for these areas are 
included in the following summary.  
The projected water demand for each residential lot will be 800 gallons per day or one Equivalent Residential Unit 
(ERU).  The Amenities area will require 6,500 gallons per day (8 ERU’s), based on sewer treatment estimates and 
adding in the Stillman Residence and future Water Treatment Plant. The project estimates a total water use demand 
of 64,000 gpd demand (80 ERU’s). This equates to a yearly water demand of 36 acre-ft of water for residential use. 
As part of the project, a new 500,000-gallon storage tank, providing 89 ERU’s of storage, per state standards, along 
with a 3-hour flow at 1,100 gpm for fire flow. This tank will be located at the West end of Rockcress Road.  
The new well needs to provide 800 gpd per EDU for a peak flow of 71,200 gpd (peak flow) or 49.45 gpm.  The well has 
been tested and a steady flow rate of 80 gpm was observed.  The maximum safe yield is 2/3 of the tested yield, resulting 
in a projected flow rate of 53.33 gpm (96 ERU’s). This equates to 43.2 acre-ft of water provided yearly. This flow rate 
exceeds the 80 ERU’s required for the Farms Subdivision.  
The distribution system for the Farms Subdivision includes pipe to all lots and developed area. Due to the change in 
elevation across the site, the site has been divided into 6 pressure zones, with 10 pressure reducing valves.  The 
pressure in each zone will range from 55 to 100 psi during normal operation with the use of these pressure reducing 
valves. During Fire Flow situations (1,000 to 1,100 gpm) the distribution system expects a total flow of 1,250 gpm in 
the system. The anticipated pressures in this scenario are listed in the table below.

The UDWR definitions for the two habitat components area as follows: 
 
Crucial value - habitat on which the local population of a wildlife species depends for survival 
because there are no alternative ranges or habitats available.  Crucial value habitat is essential to 
the life history requirements of a wildlife species.  Degradation or unavailability of crucial habitat 
will lead to significant declines in carrying capacity and/or numbers of wildlife species in 
question. 
 
Substantial value - 
habitat used by a wildlife species but is not crucial for population survival. 
Degradation or unavailability of substantial value habitat will not lead to significant declines in 
carrying capacity and/or numbers of the wildlife species in question.

winter habitat for elk, substantial winter habitat for mule deer, but no crucial or

s

intersections: 
• Blue Sky Drive / Old Lincoln Highway 
• Barn Property Access / Old Lincoln Highway

C. Analysis Methodology 
Level of service (LOS) is a term that describes the operating performance of an intersection or 
r
oadway. LOS is measured quantitatively and reported on a scale from A to F, with A representing 
the best performance and F the worst. Table 1 provides a brief description of each LOS letter 
designation and an accompanying average delay per vehicle for both signalized and unsignalized 
intersections. 
The Highway Capacity Manual (HCM), 7 th Edition, 2022 methodology was used in this study to 
r
emain consistent with “state-of-the-practice” professional standards. This methodology has 
different quantitative evaluations for signalized and unsignalized intersections. For signalized, 
roundabout, and all-way stop-controlled (AWSC) intersections, the LOS is provided for the overall 
intersection (weighted average of all approach delays). For all other unsignalized intersections, 
LOS is reported based on the worst movement. 
Using Synchro/SimTraffic software, which follow the HCM methodology, the peak hour LOS was 
computed for each study intersection. Multiple runs of SimTraffic were used to provide a statistical 
evaluation of the interaction between the intersections. The detailed LOS reports are provided in 
Appendix C. Hales Engineering also calculated the 95 th percentile queue lengths for the study 
i
ntersections using SimTraffic. The detailed queue length reports are provided in Appendix D. 
Many of the figures in this report are printouts of the Synchro model. These figures are not meant 
to be a design exhibit for exact lane striping and design, due to the limitations of the Synchro 
software. Instead, the purpose of these figures is to show assumed peak hour turning movement 
volumes and the conceptual travel lane configuration of the study roadway network. 
D. Level of Service Standards 
For the purposes of this study, a minimum acceptable intersection performance for each of the 
s
tudy intersections was set at LOS D. If levels of service E or F conditions exist, an explanation 
and/or mitigation measures will be presented. A LOS D threshold is consistent with “state-of-the-
practice” traffic engineering principles for urbanized areas.

G. Auxiliary Lanes 
Auxiliary lanes are deceleration (ingress) or acceleration (egress) turn lanes that provide for safe 
t
urning movements that have less impact on through traffic. These lanes are sometimes needed 
at accesses or roadway intersections if right- or left-turn volumes are high enough.  
Deceleration (ingress) lanes are generally needed when there are at least 50 right-turn vehicles 
or 25 left-turn vehicles in an hour. These guidelines were used for the City roadways in the study 
area. Based on these guidelines and the anticipated project traffic, no auxiliary lanes are 
recommended.

Attachment: Final Site Plan Code Amendment Staff Report
Jennifer Leslie, Planner, presented a proposal from staff to remove the
final site plan requirements in the Eastern Summit County Development
Code Section 11-4-6. The proposal was the result of Conditional Use
Permit applicants questioning the need for the final site plan.
Commissioners asked questions of Planner Leslie regarding the
process for finding final site plans.
Planner Leslie responded to the Commissioners concerns about being
able to find final site plans with this change and described additional
ways these documents could be accessed. Discussion followed.

provided additional information for discussion.

legislation amending the CUP process to which Planner Leslie
responded.

integrating the final site plan requirements into the CUP requirements.
Rich Sonntag made a motion to ask staff to merge the standards of
the final site plan with the recording aspect of the final site plan
into what was proposed. Clinton Benson seconded, and all voted
