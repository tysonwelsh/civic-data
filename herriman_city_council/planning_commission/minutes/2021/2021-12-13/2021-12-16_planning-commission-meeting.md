Item 3.4 was discussed before item 3.3.

Goodboro Development representative Jason Dunham reviewed the background and boundaries for the
medium density residential project. Goodboro Development identified themselves as a small-scale developer
and builder. They focused on new urbanism planning principles, attainable housing, classical architectural and
traditional planning. They approach all products uniquely and English arts and crafts homes were their
inspiration for this project. Mixed housing types were placed throughout the plan. He reviewed the different
home types, many of which included porches, gardens, and courtyards. All sides of the buildings would be muted
earth tone colors and off-white stucco over wood frame. Commissioners were in favor of the traditional look
and thought the product flowed well but would like to see more brick and/or stone. They would prefer better
defined private and common areas. Commissioner Sickles requested stucco control joints. City Planner Maloy
reminded Commissioners this was a partial approval, and they may request the elevations come back for later
approval. Commissioners requested garages large enough to fit two cars plus specific garbage can storage spaces.
Applicant Dunham identified guest/overflow parking locations and stall totals. He agreed to send them parking
plans. The landscaping design matrix with specific plant types was provided. The underground drainage location
with a terraced seating area and pavilions provided multi-use open areas. Mail kiosks doubled as directional
signage. Multiple side yard space and easement options were being researched. Commissioners agreed it would
a desirable addition to Herriman, when completed as proposed.

This item was discussed after item 3.4. City Planner Maloy explained the applicant requested preliminary plat
approval of a residential subdivision with thirty-three new residential units broken down to three building types:
            • Five detached homes
             •Eight duplex buildings
             •Four triplex buildings
The subdivision was approved by the City Council in 2007 as part of the Miller Park & Wheatlands Planned
Unit Development (PUD). The space was originally intended for a church site. Access to the property would be
provided from easements on Springbok Drive and a fifty-foot-wide easement across the Herriman dental site.
The Commission had discretion to approve either the seven units per acre as per zoning conditions, resulting in
twenty-nine units, or approve eight units per acre which would result in thirty-three units as proposed by the
applicant. Staff recommended the Commission approve the eight units per acre as recorded in the approved
development plan. A second access would meet UFA requirements for emergency response, and setbacks were
to be established with completion of the PUD. The Building Department had concerns with the configuration
of the property lines for Lots 7-11 and 24-28. These lot lines were designed to give the owners of those lots a
designated open space. However, because of building code standards locating a property line directly along the
neighboring building it was not approvable as proposed. There were alternative solutions which the City had
discussed with the applicant, but no final decision has been made. Staff recommended the applicant continue
working with the City to establish acceptable setback requirements.

Applicant representative Sattar Tabriz informed the Commission the parking study required 95 spots and they
have provided more than required. Green space was utilized for detention, and all utility easements were
identified. A retaining wall was planned along the southern side with the dentist office. He clarified fencing and
private road locations.
