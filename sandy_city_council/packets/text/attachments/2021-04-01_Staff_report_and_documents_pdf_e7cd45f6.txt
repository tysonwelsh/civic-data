DESCRIPTION OF REQUEST
The applicant, Rose Bentley, for the Utah Animal Adoption Center, is requesting a Conditional
Use Permit to allow for animal boarding on the property located at 8465 S. 700 E. (See Exhibit
#1: Application Material).

The subject property is located on 0.39 acres (16,988 square feet) in the Boulevard Commercial
Zone (BC). There are two buildings on the lot a 1,717 primary building and a 500 square foot
accessory structure. The applicant is proposing to utilize only the 1,717 square foot building and
leave the accessory structure unused until such time that the site be redeveloped. The proposed
use is as a veterinary clinic and boarding facility. The boarding facility will accommodate
approximately 20 cats with dogs being boarded in residential homes as foster animals.

The applicant has approximately four employees and estimates that with employees, volunteers,
and outside visitors there will be no more than 10 people in the building at a given time. At their
current location they are operating by appointment only and are proposing to continue to operate
with their current hours which are 12PM-4PM Tuesday through Friday and 12PM to 4:30PM on
Saturdays.

The property is part of the Mountain Breeze subdivision and is located on the east side of 700 E
between Vics Road and 8425 S. Properties to the north are single family homes and an insurance
company zoned Boulevard Commercial (BC). Properties to the east and south are single family
homes under county jurisdiction. Properties to the west are condominiums zoned PUD(9).

NOTICE
A neighborhood meeting was held on March 22, 2021. Notices were mailed to property owners
within 500 feet of the subject property. Five neighbors and four representatives from the Utah
Animal Adoption Center attended the meeting. The meeting lasted approximately 40 minutes
and the following concerns were raised:

   1. Noise from the dogs barking.
   2. The smell of the feces from both the dogs and cats.
   3. Increased traffic.

In addition, to the neighborhood meeting staff received two emails regarding the project that
echoed the above concerns (Exhibit #2 – Neighborhood Concerns). Staff also received two
letters of support for the project (Exhibit #3- Support Letters).

ANALYSIS
Under Section 21-08-02(B) of the Sandy City Land Development Code the proposed cat
boarding is classified as a “Animal Kennel, Commercial” and requires a conditional use in the
BC zone. The other part of the business is an animal hospital, veterinary office which is a
permitted use within the BC zone.

Staff response in italics.

   15. Such other conditions determined reasonable and necessary by the City to allow
   the operation of the proposed conditional use, at the proposed location in compliance

   That the applicant complies with all Building & Safety, and Fire & Life Codes.

CONCERNS
Staff is concerned about the potential smell created from the animal feces. Due to this
concern staff has required that the applicant submit a waste mitigation plan as a condition
of approval.

The other concern with this property is the use of the accessory structure. Historical
aerial photos of this property show that there were two homes on this property and the
accessory building was used as either a garage or storge shed for a home that has since
CUP-03-21-5999 Utah Animal Adoption Center                                                  Page 3


been removed. It appears that the previous owner has been using this shed as part of their
business. Staff is not aware of any site plan or approved conversion of this building to be
used as a commercial structure. Staff has informed the current property that the shed may
be used for storage only and not be used for any formal business operations.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommends that the Planning Commission approve the Conditional Use Permit for
Rose Bentley on behalf of the Utah Animal Adoption Center for the property located at
8465 S. 700 E. to allow an “Animal Kennel, Commercial” as described in the staff report
based upon the following findings and subject to the following conditions:


Findings

   1. The proposed use meets the intent of the BC Zone.

   2.The proposed use should have minimal to no impact on the surrounding uses.

Conditions

   1. That there be no boarding of dogs on site.

   2. That the accessory structure not be used for any business operations (except for
   storage) until such time that the project goes through a full site plan review.

   3. That the applicant provide an acceptable waste mitigation plan for the animal feces
   to be reviewed and approved by staff.

   4. That the applicant complies with all Building & Safety, and Fire & Life Codes.

   5. That the applicant be responsible for meeting all provisions of the Sandy City
   Development Code (or as approved by the Planning Commission), and all conditions
   of approval imposed by the Planning Commission.

   6. That this Conditional Use Permit be reviewed upon legitimate complaint.

                                   Our Mission and the Nature of Our Business

Utah Animal Adoption Center, a 501(c)(3) non-profit entity, has been dedicated to the health, safety, and well-being
of cats and dogs in Utah since 1983. For over 37 years, we have devoted our efforts towards the cause of animal
welfare, protection, and safety of abandoned cats and dogs in Utah. These animals often have no other hope since
most are on immediate euthanasia lists. We make it our mission to change that for each animal while reducing the
pet population throughout Utah and working towards making Utah a No-Kill state.

Our mission is to eliminate euthanasia of healthy, adoptable cats and dogs through rescue, spaying/neutering,
education, and adoption.

Our business's nature is to rescue, care for, protect and rehabilitate those abandoned cats and dogs in Utah that have
nowhere else to turn. We are their last chance, the last hope they have for a loving future.

When we rescue these animals from euthanasia at overpopulated shelters, we dedicate our every effort to ensuring
their health and well-being for today and every tomorrow they have.

From the moment we welcome them into our facility, we treat them with love and care. We attend to every medical
need they may have upon arrival. Once they are ready for adoption our volunteers devote their energy to ensuring
the right adoption for each animal in their “Forever Loving Home."

We work with municipal shelters to rescue these animals, opening up another space for another abandoned animal to
take its place and have a chance at a better future.

Our day-to-day business involves a non-profit veterinary clinic (a permitted use in the BC zone), together with the
housing of cats awaiting adoption. We do not operate a commercial kennel (a conditional use in the BC zone), but
we have followed staff’s direction to process this application for conditional use permit because of the overnight
housing component to our rescue operation. Although we rescue both dogs and cats, our dogs are placed in foster
homes pending adoption and are not housed on-site. At any given time, we anticipate that no more than 20 cats
would be housed on-site.

                                                Hours of Operation

Access to the Utah Animal Adoption Center is by appointment only.

                                           These appointment hours are:

                                          Tuesday – Friday 12 pm – 4 pm

                                             Saturday 12pm – 4:30pm

There are never more than ten people total on-site at any given moment. This includes staff, volunteers, and outside
visitors who have appointments.

We believe that by keeping a limited occupancy and limited appointment hours, we can effectively create a home-
like and peaceful environment for our animals. Limited appointment hours and occupancy also allow us to be more
efficient with outside visitors when they are on-site, and to limit the impact of our operation on surrounding
properties

                                              Impact on Traffic Flow
We anticipate the operation of our non-profit to have no noticeable impact on the traffic flow that moves in/out and
around the surrounding area due to our limited appointment only hours and limited occupancy. This site is located
on 700 East, an arterial with plenty of capacity in a commercial area, and a few more trips per day for our operation
will be imperceptible.

                                             Impact on the Community

Our belief is that the proposed use of this property will not be contrary to the public interest due to our strict
adherence to protecting the welfare and health of our animals and the surrounding community members. Our
dedication to the people of the Utah communities we serve is just as strong as the animals in these communities.

We have a strong presence in the various communities throughout Utah through partnerships with organizations
including Petco, PetSmart, and Utah’s Hogle Zoo, to name a few. These partnerships are a small part of our
dedication to serve our communities, including the people and businesses in them.

When this is related to the Conditional Use of this property and its impact on the public, we will operate our facility
peacefully and harmoniously in the existing area. All animals will be kept indoors and will only be provided access
to the outdoors with a staff member or volunteer. This outdoor area will be fenced in and kept clean, neat, and tidy
by our staff members and volunteers at all times. We do not anticipate additional noise, odors, perceptible traffic, or
other detrimental effects from our business operations.

Utah Animal Adoption Center will continue the character of the already existing zoning in this area, with little to no
changes to the existing property other than fencing.

  To whom it may concern and to my colleagues in Sandy City.

  I have been informed that the Utah Animal Adoption Center is seeking to relocate its clinic and professional
  facility to our community. I am writing to express my support for the UAAC and their migration to Sandy City.

  UAAC has assisted Sandy City Animal Services for many years by providing rescue and placement opportunities
  for animals that have come into our care and required additional networking and resources to find good homes.
  It has been my experience that UAAC is a professional, conscientious, and well-established rescue organization
  with an emphasis on the health and wellbeing of the animals they bring into thier care.

  What sets UAAC apart from similar organizations is thier vet clinic and medical staff.

  I believe that UAAC will be an asset to our community and a resource that will directly benefit several programs
  operated by Sandy City.

  In 2015, Sandy City Council updated our city’s animal ordinances and directed Sandy City Animal Services to
  establish community cat and TNR programs. One of the challenges with the implementation of these programs
  has been the ability to schedule veterinary services in a timely manner. Affordable and reliable veterinary services
  can be hard to come by during the best of times and are especially challenging during a pandemic.

  The addition of UAAC to Sandy City will place a local veterinary care provider focused on rescue and adoptable
  animals. I have had several discussions with Jode Littlepage, Director of the Utah Animal Adoption Center,
  about veterinary care and support opportunities that will directly benefit our city’s TNR and community cat
  programs.

  Consistent with its reputation, UAAC has worked diligently with local community leaders, rescues, and animal
  control agencies to elevate the quality of animal welfare in our state. They have proven to be good neighbors
  with the community in their current location and believe they will bring that same care and consideration to
  our city.

  I encourage consideration of a plan to facilitate UAAC’s relocation to Sandy City.

  Sincerely,




  Ian S. Williams
