Agenda Approval: Commissioner Doutre asked Mike Desimone, Community Development Director
to explain the role of the Planning Commission regarding short-term rentals. M. DeSimone explained
that the Planning Commission's role is to approve Conditional Use permits based on the
requirements of the Land Development Code being met. The City Council has already determined
that short-term rentals are compatible and allowed in residential zones under certain criteria.
Through the conditions placed on a Conditional Use Permit, the Planning Commission ensures that
the use is compatible with the neighborhood. Concerns with the policy allowing short-term rentals
need to be brought to the City Council.

The agenda was approved as written.

PC 24-003 Robinson Ranch Subdivision [Subdivision Permit] Adam Mackelprang/MV Properties
LLC is requesting a subdivision permit to subdivide a 3.19-acre parcel into 15 single-family building
lots located at 715 Park Avenue in the Traditional Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) zone; TIN 02-
071-0021 (Woodruff Neighborhood).

STAFF: Tanya Rice, Planner reviewed the proposal for a 15-lot subdivision on 3.19 acres located at
715 Park Avenue in the Traditional Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) zone. The NR-6 zone allows
for up to 6 units per acre and the proposed subdivision has a density of 4.70 units per acre. The
proposal also meets the required lot sizes, ranging from 6,000 square feet to 8,634 square feet, and
all lots are larger than 70 feet wide.

The staff has determined that there shall be a 20-foot setback from the North Branch of the Logan
River that runs along the north side of the subdivision, affecting lots one, two, and three. Per the
Land Development Code, this section of the river does not drain more than a square mile of drainage
area and would thus be considered a canal, ditch, or stream and is subjected to a smaller setback
of 20 feet. The 20-foot setback area also requires that no more than 20% of the land area can be
disturbed. As new homes are proposed on individual lots, setbacks will be reviewed and approved
for compliance.

The Land Development Code requires one street connection for subdivisions with 15 lots and
requires stubbing of new internal streets to allow for future street extensions through adjoining
developable parcels. The proposal shows two street connections onto Park Avenue without a stub
to access adjoining developable parcels. The Commission will need to determine if a stub is needed
for future development and connectivity.

Condition number eight can be stricken as Engineering's condition number eight addresses the
easement requirements.

PROPONENT: Jared Nielson with MV Properties said that looked at several different options for this
property and found that the other options would put more driveways onto Park Avenue. Mr. Nielson
spoke about the surrounding subdivisions having no connectivity and there not being any future
roads planned in this area that a stub would connect to. Mr. Nielson also spoke about the property
to the west having access to Southwest Street and the property to the south having access to Park
Avenue.

PUBLIC: Max Stedman who owns the property to the north of the proposed subdivision spoke about
the large trees along the riverbank that are dying and breaking and how they are a safety hazard to
any home adjacent to them. Mr. Stedman submitted a packet of pictures to the commission showing
the trees and said that if the trees cannot be removed there should be a larger setback to protect the
homes when the trees fall.

Owen Anderson who owns the property to the west of the proposed subdivision said he is in favor
of a stub on the west side of the subdivision so his property does not become landlocked and stated
that the existing access to his property is from a shared easement from Southwest Street.

Josh Molitor said he agrees that there should be a larger buffer zone from the Logan River to
preserve the natural wildlife.

Commissioner Doutre asked how the setback from the river was determined. Mike DeSimone,
Community Development Director said that this portion of the river is fed from a man-made ditch that

17.30.170 Connectivity Standards
A. All streets must connect to other streets, forming a grid street pattern that extends Logan's historic
street grid.
B. Stubbing of streets to allow for future street extensions through adjoining developable parcels is
required for all new developments. The street system shall be designed to connect with existing,
proposed, and planned streets outside of the development.
D. The hearing body may grant adjustments to the standards if there are no practical design
alternatives for complying.

STAFF: Tanya Rice, Planner reviewed the request for a new 800 square foot restaurant with a drive­
through lane, walk-up window, and no public seating. The .18-acre project site is located in the
southeast corner of the 2.16-acre property located at 623 North Main Street. The proposed canopy
over the drive-through is 4 feet from the south property line. A condition needs to be added to require
a 5-foot setback from the south property line.

The Land Development Code requires 50% of the site to contain building mass to meet the building
frontage requirement. The proposed building width of 20 feet, the drive-through canopy of 1 O feet,
and the 3-foot canopy over the pick-up window create a frontage of 50.7%. The Commission will
need to determine if both canopies count towards the building mass and can be included in the total
frontage.

PROPONENT: Dan Rigby said they are excited about the project and asked about the fenestration
requirement and how it is measured. T. Rice said that the fenestration has to be at 30% on all street
facing facades and is measured on the exterior of the building.

PUBLIC: Jeannie Simmonds asked how the site is accessed. T. Rice said there will be a new
driveway just to the north of the project site.

Commissioner Peterson said he would like a more solid canopy over the walk-up window to count
toward the building mass. The walk-up canopy should match the drive-through canopy in terms of
scale and mass, especially if it is being considered towards meeting the minimum building frontage
requirement.
