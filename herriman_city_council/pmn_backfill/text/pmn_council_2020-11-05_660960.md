2. Reports and Presentations
    2.1. Discussion of the City Manager Recruitment Options – Travis Dunn, Human Resources
         Manager
Human Resources Manager Travis Dunn presented the Council with a few national recruiting firms to help fill
the vacant position of City Manager. He estimated the timeframe to get the position filled was 4-6 months
and would cost the City between $35,000-65,000 to utilize a national recruiting firm. He reviewed the potential
firms including Robert Half, Prince Perelson, and the Mercer Group.

Human Resources Manager Dunn recommended the City contract with Prince Pereson and Associates group
as they were a more local firm who understood the specific needs of the community. Mayor Pro Tempore
Smith relayed his support to use Prince Perelson. Councilmember Ohrn relayed her desire to attract national
attention to the recruitment of a City Manager and asked if Prince Perelson could accomplish the goal. Manager
Dunn answered in the affirmative. Councilmember Shields relayed his position to utilize the nationally ranked
firm of Robert Half. He also requested if Robert Half would be willing to offer a six month contract similar
to that offered by Prince Perelson, and also what the trial period would entail. Attorney Andrizzi explained the
Council could also enter into a six month probationary contract if the Council desired. Councilmember Ohrn
and Councilmember Shields leaned more to soliciting help from the national firm, and requested this item
come back during the November 18, 2020 meeting to allow Councilmember Henderson and Mayor Watts an
opportunity to provide feedback. Mayor Pro Tempore acknowledged the good return either corporation could
provide, and supported the option to have a six month trial period for the position. He relayed his support to
use Robert Half.

3. Public Hearing – 6:00 PM
    3.1. Public Hearing regarding the proposed creation of the Herriman City Fire Service Area to
         fund Fire and EMS Services within Herriman City – Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi explained the Herriman City Council recently adopted a resolution signaling its
intent to create a local district to fund fire and EMS services within Herriman City boundaries. Currently, the
Unified Fire Service Area collects property taxes from Herriman City Residents to pay to Unified Fire Authority
(UFA) for Fire and EMS Services within Herriman City boundaries. The Resolution adopted by Herriman City
Council would create a new district that would take the place of UFSA is Herriman City by collecting resident
property taxes to pay UFA for Fire and EMS Services. The proposed district would maintain the same tax rate
levied by UFSA. Herriman residents would not see any tax increase but will receive the same level of Fire and
EMS Services. The tax rate for Fire Service was set at .001746 which would equate to $523.80 per $300,000 of
assessed property value. Councilmember Ohrn explained there would be a 100-percent increase for the
Herriman City Fire Service Area and a 100-percent decrease for the UFSA. Mayor Pro Tempore Clint Smith
explained this is the same mechanism used when the Herriman City Safety Enforcement Service Area was
created.

Attorney Andrizzi explained Utah State Code required that a public hearing be held within 45 days of the
adoption of that resolution and that the public hearing be held within the proposed service area. The purpose
of the public hearing is to allow public input on the following items:

   1. Whether Fire and EMS Services are needed in Herriman City boundaries.
   2. Whether Fire and EMS Services should be provided by Herriman City directly or provided and funded
      through the proposed local district.
   3. All other matters relating to the request or the proposed local district.

Attorney Andrizzi added that during the public hearing, the public will be allowed to ask questions of and
obtain further information from the Herriman City Council regarding the issues contained in or raised by the
Resolution.

Mayor Pro Tempore Smith stated on or before January 4, 2021, the public may file adequate protests with the
Herriman City Recorder. Under Utah Code Ann. § 17B-1-213, “adequate protests” means protests that are
signed by:

     1. The owners of private real property that are located within Herriman City boundaries, that cover at
        least 25% of the total private land area within Herriman City, and who are equal in value to at least
        15% of the value of all private real property within Herriman City.
OR

     2.   Registered voters residing within Herriman City equal in number to at least 25% of the number of
          votes cast in the applicable area for the office of president of the United States at the most recent
          election prior to the adoption of the resolution.

An owner who signs or submits a protest may withdraw such protest at any time before January 4, 2021. If
adequate protests are filed, the City may not take any further action to create the proposed local district. If
adequate protests are not filed by January 4, 2021, the City may approve the creation of the local district.

Mayor Pro Tempore Clint Smith opened the public hearing at 6:03 p.m.

Steve Slessenger appreciated clarification of tax rate and referenced the misinformation streaming on social
media about the taxes for Fire and EMS Services doubling. He asked for clarification on how EMS services
would be charged.

Andy Rasmussen asked the Council what the impetus of leaving the Unified Fire Service Area. He wondered
if there had been allegations of improper use of funds that should have otherwise been used for the benefit of
Herriman City. He questioned if the Unified Fire Authority not been able to provide required or necessary
services to Herriman City and its residents. He opined if the answer to his questions was no, he questioned
why the Herriman City Council thought there was a need to take on this responsibility of managing and
allocating the funds for the Unified Fire Authority when an entity existed that was already doing that and there
has been no indication they are misusing or depriving Herriman City of necessary services. He asked why the
City Council thought the City could manage those funds better than UFSA could.

Mayor Pro Tempore Smith explained the processing element of utilizing EMS services noting there would be
additional service charges when they transport to medical facilities and those costs would be dependent on if
the facility was within the service area or not. UFA Battalion Chief Widdison concurred. Councilmember
Shields asked if changing the taxing entity would result in a fee change for this service. Chief Widdison
responded in the negative. Mayor Pro Tempore Smith added the costs are outlined by state statute.

Steve Slessinger asked if an ambulance came to his home would a cost be incurred. Chief Widdison
responded in the negative and iterated the additional cost would only be associated with transportation from
the home to a medical facility.

Mayor Pro Tempore Smith referenced the comment offered by Mr. Rasmussen and explained this creation
would not change the service provider. Councilmember Shields added there was no indication of
mismanagement of funds within the Unified Fire Service Area. Mayor Pro Tempore Smith continued
acknowledging the impetus was to keep the funds collected within the jurisdictional boundaries of Herriman
City instead of subsidizing other parts of the UFSA service area.

Councilmember Shields referenced a presentation previously offered by Finance Director Alan Rae outlining
the benefits received compared to the amount being charged for services. He noted the City Council wanted
to ensure the City was paying for services being rendered, and have control over the resources provided within
the jurisdictional boundaries. He also highlighted the support offered from the Unified Fire Authority, and
reiterated this was not related to the services being provided. Councilmember Ohrn touched on the liability of
bonding for assets in the community was not in the City’s best interest at this time and wanted to be able to
manage the debts as the Council determined.

Councilmember Ohrn moved to close the public hearing. Councilmember Shields seconded the motion, and all voted aye.
