I move the City Council ADOPT Ordinance 20-17, amending section 18.150.010 and

Background

Signs or signage in Riverton are governed by Chapter 18.150 “Sign Regulation Ordinance”. The
chapter was adopted in 2008 with several revisions and clarifications since. The ordinance sets
out standards for each type of sign, and designates which type of sign is allowed in each zone.

This proposed amendment is for correcting contradictions and deficiencies in the ordinance but
does not alter the existing character or design requirements in the code. The most significant
modification is to the “Table of Permitted Signs” (see 18.150.110). The proposed changes adds
the RM-6 (Residential Multi-Family 6du/acre) and PCC (Planned Commercial Center) Zones to
the table. The RM-6 zone signage is assigned the same permissions as the RM-8 Zone. The
PCC Zone signage is assigned the same permissions as the CR (Commercial Regional) Zone.
Also in the Table permissions for “Bus Bench” are removed to be consistent with the text in
18.150.120 (2). The amendment also removes a restriction that limited certain monument signs
to only a business name and address, but did not refer to specific zone.

The Planning Commission recommended approval of the proposed changes, and staff concurs
with that recommendation.

(8) Monument Signs.


          (a) Height. The height of a monument sign may vary depending on the grading of landscaping
          upon which the sign is located. However, the combined height of the sign plus any landscape
          berming shall not exceed eight feet above the elevation of the nearest sidewalk. In order for this
          result to occur, the entire frontage of the site which the sign serves, not just the sign location,
          must have berming incorporated into the landscape design.


          (b) Construction. A monument sign shall be constructed of brick or masonry materials which
          match the buildings identified by the monument sign, and shall be designed to be harmonious
          with the building architecture. Copy of individual monument signs in these zones shall consist
          only of the name and address of the occupant.

3.    GENERAL PLAN, RIVERTON CITY IS PROPOSING AMENDMENTS TO THE
      LAND USE ELEMENT OF THE GENERAL PLAN.

Mr. Lethbridge presented the staff report and stated that the above item was discussed
during a City Council Work Session. The City Council discussed areas within their districts
and gave specific direction for Land Use designations within the General Plan. This issue
was noticed as a public hearing, however, given the impact and intent of the General Plan,
approval from the Commission was not required. Mr. Lethbridge desired to provide an
opportunity for the Commission to give feedback and for the public to comment.

Mr. Lethbridge identified the property located north of 12600 South and east of 1300 West.
The current General Plan shows a preservation designation for this property that has current
A-5 zoning. The City Council and the Council Member responsible for the area expressed
the intent to keep the designated zone. For this property specifically, the Council suggested
creating a new designation in the General Plan for agricultural preservation. The three
parcels are designated as low-density residential with a one-third acre minimum lot size,
consistent with the adjacent subdivisions. They have limited and private lane access.

Commissioner James asked if the new designation allows structures to be built on the
parcels. Mr. Lethbridge confirmed that they will and reviewed the A-5 zoning requirements.
Previously, the A-5 zone revision removed agricultural industrial uses. Animals are allowed
but are limited in the area; however, any animal commercial use is prohibited.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that several amendments listed in the new designation are intended
to bring the General Plan in line with the underlying zoning. The properties were displayed
on a map showing potential locations.
