1.   Discussion, BDO Development

     Mr. Montgomery stated at the time the DDO was closed in 1995 the City had developed some
     goals relative to its future development. Ogden became the land use authority for the
     closure, and has partnered with the Boyer Company to manage and attract business. The
     main concern at its closure was its potential for economic development and the creation of
     new jobs. Because the entire DDO property had been annexed into Ogden City, Ogden
     became the sole land use authority.

     Mr. Aaron Austad presented a short slide show showing how development has occurred. He
     reviewed the job history of the area, stating the facility was opened by the US Government in
     1940 and in 1941 there were 800 employees. Because of the war at that time, employment
     increased in 1942 to 6,000, including both Italian and German POW’s. He stated it was
     determined it would be closed in 1995 and remained vacant from 1995-1997. In 1999 Ogden
     City and Boyer entered into a 70 year lease allowing Boyer to actively pursue employers to
     the area. Because Utah has a strong industrial real estate climate, businesses started
     considering this location. During the first seven years, all income was required to be
     reinvested into the facility, and $300 million was invested in new buildings and $21 million in
     infrastructure.

     He stated the City had adopted zoning regulations and identified two new industrial zones for
     the site, including the MRD (Manufacturing, Research and Development) and the DDR
     (Defense Depot Reuse). Uses in each of these zones were identified and what has developed
     is warehousing in the DDR zone and manufacturing uses in the MRD zone. It had been
     assumed separate office uses might be desired, which would be located in the MRD zone
     which is the outer portion of the site next to residential neighborhoods, but these uses have

been incorporated within the manufacturing uses. It has been found the manufacturing uses
create more noise, particularly during evening and night-time hours, and Boyer is suggesting
warehousing is a better neighbor to residential uses. Of particular concern is the MRD zone
which is adjacent to 1200 West, as the area to the west is mainly residential, and are
suggesting a distribution center would be a better neighbor than would be manufacturing
uses.

He reviewed existing vacant property within the BDO site and stated some is still needing
remediation and some are still owned by the Federal government. He stated at the time DDO
was closed there were 3,000 employees and the goal for economic growth has been met as
there are now over 6,000 employees. He stated at the time Boyer began work, their goal was
for the development of seven acres per year. The construction is approximately 30 years
ahead of schedule, with total build-out anticipated to be completed within 7-10 years.

Mr. Cameron Cook stated during 2020 there was an increased interest in industrial
development and 2 million square feet has been created during the past year. New buildings
are 90% occupied and five new buildings are now under construction, with the hopes of
seven additional buildings being started in March 2021. Changes are trending towards more
distribution centers and there is a greater need for warehouse storage space than for
manufacturing uses, stating manufacturing is typically less than 25% of a building’s need
with most space needed for storage. He reviewed the operation procedure for several
tenants within the BDO, stating on the outside the use of manufacturing and warehousing
appear to be the same. He expressed concern that the MRD zone limits the use of
warehousing to under 50,000 square feet and suggested a warehousing use would be better
next to the residential neighborhood on 1200 West than would a manufacturing use.

Mr. Montgomery acknowledged the change in nature for industrial uses may be reason for
the Commission to consider either code or zone changes. He stated it was anticipated there
may be more retail or restaurant uses, particularly along the 12th Street frontage. He stated
food producers are allowed in the MRD zone.

Discussion continued relating to uses within the project, with the Commission wondering if
there might be more interest if buildings were able to be purchased rather than leased.
Commissioner Safsten felt there should be some flexibility, particularly toward the interior of
the facility. Chair Graf asked if the Ogden Nature Center has been apprised of potential
changes. Commission asked about both transportation within the facility as well as the
potential of a FrontRunner station within the BDO property, suggesting ease of transportation
may attract employees. It was suggested a food court might be a good use as most
employees are allotted only a 30 minute lunch break.

Mr. Austad again indicated the 1200 West corridor is not a good place for manufacturing
businesses due to noise of compressors and other equipment. He stated existing
distribution buildings in the area have their docks facing north and south so the impact of
trucks to the residential neighborhood is reduced.
