2. Presentations and Reports
2.1 Discussion of Pressurized Irrigation Contamination
Mayor Johnson asked for an update on the recent E. coli contamination. Greg Allred, Water
Manager, provided a timeline of events as the city became aware of the contamination along with
what information was made available to the city. Mayor Johnson expressed frustration at the delay
and lack of clarity in communicating about the contamination. He raised concerns about the lack
of information made available to the city which presented challenges to identify the specific ponds
for treatment. The Mayor emphasized the need for effective collaboration and communication
between the city and the health authorities.

Cindy Burnett and Brianna Osbourne, both with the Utah Department of Health, along with Jason
Garrett and Damon Swenson, both with Utah County Environmental Health, were available to
answer questions. They discussed the challenges in sharing information while adhering to patient
privacy laws (HIPAA) and the need to balance public health concerns with patient privacy.

The E. coli contamination in the PI water supply, particularly in reservoirs and ponds has affected
many individuals, including children who have been hospitalized. State Heath representatives
expressed their concerns about the severity of E. coli 0157, its potential health effects, and the need
for continuous treatment of the water to ensure safety.

The Mayor acknowledged difficulties in communicating with the public and the need for improved
education and awareness about the risks associated with using irrigation (PI) water for recreational
purposes. Additionally, the Council discussed the challenges in determining the contamination
source and the necessity of potential regulatory action to mitigate future outbreaks. The
conversation emphasized the importance of finding a rational approach to safeguard public health
while considering the community benefit of a pressurized irrigation system.

The Council discussion expressed worries about recommendations from the CDC, water usage,
potential sources of contamination, and the need for concrete recommendations to address the
situation. They also talked about the challenges of finding the contamination source and the
ongoing efforts to treat the ponds to ensure water safety. The officials requested information on
water reuse regulations and discussed ways to effectively communicate risks and preventive
measures to the public.

14. Consideration of Ordinance #47-2023 an amendment to the Lehi City Development Code
    Chapter 4, Amendments, changing when General Plan amendments are allowed to be
    processed.
    Mr. Struthers stated that this will amend the code to be consistent with the Council’s decision
    to only allow applications for a General Plan amendment for 2 months out of the year.

    Mayor Johnson asked for public comments.

    Michelle Stallings stated that she opposes the proposed change related to rezoning requests,
    expressing concerns about potential hardships for those purchasing property, particularly in
    business deals where time is critical. She stated that she believes that concentrating such
    changes twice a year could burden staff and may not be necessary, suggesting that the council
    should exercise self-discipline and refrain from unnecessary amendments that General Plan.

    Councilor Hancock stated that he supports the proposed change, emphasizing that it's not about
    council self-discipline but rather about enabling a holistic view of upcoming developments.
    He highlighted the importance of having a comprehensive understanding of the impacts on the
    city's infrastructure, which is facilitated by the proposed approach to general plan amendments.

    Councilor Koivisto expressed disagreement with the proposed change, emphasizing the
    importance of following through with the existing general plan.

    Councilor Albrecht mentioned that, in 2022, there were only eight proposed general plan
    amendments. She emphasized the importance of considering these changes holistically and
    stated that their perspective isn't about easing staff burdens but about comprehensively
    reviewing proposed amendments.

    Councilor Southwick agreed that allowing applications for 2 months out the year is
    appropriate, especially compared to the original proposal of just 1 month per year.

23. Consideration of Resolution #2023-58 directing the City's Election Officer not to notify
    a candidate of the failure to file a campaign financial statement by the deadline or
    accept from a candidate a campaign financial statement filed after the deadline set
    forth in Utah code section 10-3-208.
    Mr. Wood explained that the state law was revised, granting the City Recorder discretion to
    notify candidates electronically if they miss the filing deadline. However, the ordinance's
    wording poses confusion regarding the extent of this discretion. The Lieutenant Governor's
    office clarified that candidates now have a 24-hour grace period after the deadline to submit
    their campaign financial statements, making the initial deadline somewhat irrelevant. The
    ordinance needed adjustments to align with this interpretation, ensuring that the City
    Recorder must accept the statements within the 24-hour window after the original deadline,
    but the City Recorder should not notify the candidate if they miss the original deadline.

    Mayor Johnson asked for public comments.

    Jason Hill stated that he agreed with the content but felt that the timing is bad.

    Michelle Stallings believes that there should be a grace period for candidates if they miss the
    deadline.

    Motion:     Councilor Condie moved to approve Resolution #2023-58 directing the City's
                Election Officer not to notify a candidate of the failure to file a campaign financial
                statement by the deadline, but strike out the next section and clarify that the
                Recorder must accept from a candidate a campaign financial statement filed 24
                hours after the deadline as set forth in Utah code section 10-3-208. Councilor
                Southwick seconded the motion.
