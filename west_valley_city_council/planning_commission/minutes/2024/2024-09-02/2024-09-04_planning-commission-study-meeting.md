 GENERAL PLAN/ZONE CHANGE APPLICATION

 GPZ-5-2024      Petition by Grow Development, LLC requesting a General Plan change from Large Lot
                 Residential (2 to 3 units/acre) and Low Density Residential (3 to 4 units/acre) to Small
                 Lot Residential (4 to 7 units/acre) and a zone change from A (Agriculture, minimum
                 lot size ½ acre) to R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000
                 square feet) with a planned unit development (PUD). The property is located at 3814
                 and 3876 South 6000 West on 6.68 acres. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545 or
                 steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov)

 Discussion: Steve Pastorik presented the applicants’ PUD concept plan of 41 lots, 45-feet wide and
 50-feet wide. The plan includes two-story homes starting at 2250 square feet, four elevation options,
 and two open-spaces with amenities. Commissioner Winters asked if the existing homes are going to
 be raised and start from scratch. Steve replied that they would be. Commissioner Winters asked if the
 lots meet the 8,000 square feet criteria. Steve replied that they do not meet the requirement in that
 these lots would be quite a bit smaller than 8,000 square feet, which is allowed under a PUD.
 Commissioner Winters pointed out that three of the elevations are a similar style with traditional
 architecture and gabled roofs, and one elevation is modern looking. He asked if the Code requires

                                                                                             Page 1 of 4
PUD architecture to be similar, rather than having one elevation that looks odd compared to the others.
Steve replied that applicant did not want a carbon copy, cookie-cutter PUD.
Commissioner Drozdek asked if there is room for three-car garages. Steve replied that the 50 feet-wide
lots will be wide enough space to have a 10-foot setback concrete pad off to the side of the garage.
Seventy-five percent of the lots would be wide enough to accomplish that. Commissioner Drozdek
asked if they have any intention of installing solar panels. Steve replied that the Developer would not
be installing solar panels. Commissioner Drozdek asked if the existing lot landscaping was going to be
removed. Steve replied that the Developer is considering water-wise landscaping. The Chinese Elms
on the lot are problematic and with all the construction the lot will most likely be cleared and re-
landscaped.
Commissioner Lovato asked if the open spaces at the ends of the PUD is the best placement for them,
given how close together the homes will be. Steve replied that the lots are smaller at 90-feet deep, and
they must be rectangular to get the number of units where it makes sense to redevelop the site.
Commissioner Lovato also asked if there is curb and sidewalk on both sides of the street in the Plan.
Steve responded that there is and will be installed. Referring to the plan, Commissioner Lovato asked
what the significance of the little green strip is? Steve replied that he’s not sure and it could possibly
be removed, unless there is a utility reason it needs to remain.

CONDITIONAL USE APPLICATIONS

C-15-2024      Petition by AWA Engineering requesting a conditional use approval for a Maverik Gas
               Station and Convenience Store. The property is located at 2384 South 5600 West on 3.99
               acres and is zoned LI (Light Industrial) and located within the 5600 West Overlay Zone.
               (Staff – Jody Knapp at 801-963-3497 or jody.knapp@wvc-ut.gov)

Discussion: Jody presented. The applicant is wanting to assemble one large property from a couple of

  CA-5-2024 Petition by Rasoul Gassmepour requesting a conditional use amendment to add automobile
            service to the Ark Plaza development. The property is located at 1927 West 3600 South on
            1.03 acres and is zoned C-2 (General Commercial). (Staff – Jody Knapp at 801-963-3497 or
            jody.knapp@wvc-ut.gov)

 Discussion: Jody presented. The Applicant’s original application for Ark Plaza was approved in 2022
 for 51% retail and 49% warehouse as it met the 50/50 split for the C-2 zone. The lot was submitted
 and approved to have 31 parking stalls. Now that he is finally leasing to tenants, he wants to expand
 the types of businesses that are allowed in the building. At this time, nine of the eleven tenant spaces
 have open business license applications, and none have been approved yet. These new business types
 are already operating and has created a lack of parking, issues of open overhead doors during business
 hours, displayed/storage of vehicles and jet skis in the parking lot, and complaints from adjacent
 property owners of these new businesses taking up parking on the private street. With this amendment
 to add automobile services to the property, Jody estimated there would need to be 36 parking stalls.
 She feels the application could still be reviewed as a conditional use amendment, but she is not close
 to being able to approve business licenses. She hopes to have more concrete numbers at the Public
 Hearing on the building’s square footage and the tenants’ business hours to accurately calculate the
 number of parking stalls needed.
 Commissioner Porter commented that the Applicant’s application is premature since the business
 licenses haven’t been approved. Jody agreed, commenting the Applicant thought automotive service
 was included in the C-2 zone, but based on his originally approved application the parking calculation,
 impact fees, floor plan, and occupancy will not apply to this amendment. Jody said she is working on
 these, but she doesn’t think there is going to be enough parking for the mixed-use tenants. Stop Work
 orders have been issued and she is working with Building Inspections and Fire to get the situation
 fixed.
 Steve Pastorik added that Applicant’s initial proposal to him was disingenuous because he held that
 half of his building was going to be warehouse. It appeared he made a proposal that would get the
 parking approved for a lower amount, then came back with new uses that clearly are not allowed.
 Steve feels this is a future enforcement problem and thinks Jody is doing a great job of trying to get
 the Applicant to come into compliance. He feels these issues could have been avoided had the
 Applicant been straightforward about what their uses were going to be.
 Jody closed with informing the Commissioners that they can continue the application to see if the
 Applicant can manage his tenants and clean up the parking lot and get everything off the road. But if
 the amendment were approved, she still won’t issue business licenses, until he meets the conditions of
 the approval to begin with.


There being no further business the meeting was adjourned at 4:35 pm.
