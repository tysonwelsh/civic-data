Discussion Items

         Budget Discussion

Mayor Staggs called out a couple of items in the budget; the contribution of money to another
chamber of commerce and the water fees for residents who own over an acre of land. He said
he expected public comment about the water fees and wanted to have a discussion with
Councilmembers. He asked Mr. Hicks how many residents this rate increase would affect.

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, said that about 70 residents would be affected by
the increase in the fee structure. Mayor Staggs explained that he doesn’t think this fee should
be increased given the fact that the City will be installing secondary water meters within the next
two years.

Mayor Staggs said that he would like to have policies that encourage water conservation. He
said at least 5 residents have said they will water continually if the rate increases. He asked if
staff could identify how much acreage was actually being watered.

Councilmember Buroker asked if the City could determine which district each of the 70 residents
live. She said a lot of time was spent at the last council meeting and she received no negative
feedback from residents in her district. She thought they came up with a compromise and
would like to see the fee stay.

Councilmember McCay said she felt good about the compromise from last council meeting.
However, since it is such a small dollar amount, she would like to wait until meters are installed
so that residents can be charged by their usage.

Mayor Staggs said that he believes the state will require that residents are charged by usage
once meters are installed.

Councilmember McDougal said he would prefer to keep the fee in the schedule because he
would like to have equity across the board. He said it is not about money. He said that this is
all based on the assumption of how much water each resident uses, however, he does not want
to waste staff’s time or money to figure out how much water is being used.

Mr. Hicks said that there are two fees; residents that are not connected pay $2.50 and residents
who are connected but still pay the half acre fee because they have canal shares.

Councilmember Stewart said he would like to wait on the fee increase until secondary water
meters are installed.

Councilmember Wells said he would like the fee to be removed. Mr. Hicks asked the Council
not to remove the line but change it to be the same rate as the line for 0.75 to 1 acre which is
$40.87.

Councilmember Buroker said she is concerned because most residents assume that the City is
installing secondary meters but will not be charging by usage. Now, the message is changing in
that the City plans to charge residents based on usage once the meters are installed. Mayor
Staggs responded that once the meters are installed, the Council will have the information
needed to determine the fee schedule.

Councilmember Stewart said that his comments about charging for usage are only if the state
were to mandate it.

Mayor Staggs said that Tyler Technologies will have the ability to show residents their water
usage within 4-6 hours. Mr. Hicks said this would only be available once the City has
converted all systems to the new software and this will take a couple of years.

Councilmember McCay asked if the Southwest Valley Chamber would be included going
forward. Mayor Staggs said it would stay in the budget unless they voted to change it tonight.
Mayor Staggs said that having two chambers is very confusing. Currently, a letter is sent to all
new businesses in Riverton that includes the South Valley Chamber logo and the Riverton City
logo and encourages them to join the South Valley Chamber. The City encourages these
businesses to use the money saved, from not paying a business licensing fee to Riverton City,
for a membership to the South Valley Chamber or to take courses.

Councilmember McCay said that South Valley Chamber is doing a great job. She is concerned
because Riverton businesses are choosing to go with Southwest Valley Chamber because of
the membership price. She feels that the City is not supporting these businesses that are part of
the Southwest Valley Chamber. According to the director of the Southwest Valley Chamber,
there are 80 Riverton businesses which are members. She appreciates all of the programs that
the Southwest Valley Chamber puts on and highly recommends that the City pay $4,000 per
year to support the Southwest Valley Chamber.

Councilmember McDougal said he did some additional research and would prefer that
Southwest Valley Chamber membership be removed from the budget. The role of the chamber
is entirely different than the past. He personally thinks that recognizing teachers is a function of
the PTA, not the chamber. He would like to have one entity that creates a unified front.

Councilmember Wells said he is torn on this issue. He noticed that the ribbon cuttings do not
mean that they were a member of either chamber. The Communications department will be
affected by having two chambers and he does not want to create more work for them. He would
like to see this taken out of the budget.

Councilmember Buroker wants the Southwest Valley Chamber to remain in the budget and
agrees with everything that Councilmember McCay expressed.

Councilmember Stewart said he would like the Southwest Valley Chamber to be removed from
the budget.

Public Hearings

       Ordinance No. 22-18 – Adopting Amendments to the Landscaping and Watering
       Requirements for Residential and Commercial landscaping
Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained the Riverton City Council has been
Page | 6 Minutes – City Council Meeting
June 21, 2022




reviewing recommended waterwise landscaping standards for development within Riverton City.
The standards are based on similar standards put forward by the Jordan Valley Water
Conservancy District and others. The standards recommend methods of reducing water use
within landscaped areas, both residential and commercial. This can be accomplished both by
reducing or eliminating more water intensive landscape materials, and also by reducing the
overall landscaped area.

Mr. Lethbridge said Riverton City is currently proposing adoption of the standards as
“recommended and encouraged” for both the development community within Riverton City, and
residents in general. Riverton City will also look to implement more waterwise and water-
efficient landscaping within municipal projects. The Planning Commission has forwarded this to
the City Council with a recommendation for approval.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments;

Mike Dusharm commented that trees diminish the winds and help to save homes.

Mayor Staggs called for additional public comments; there being none, he closed the Public
Hearing.

Councilmember Buroker said that she was disappointed that the standards were too soft,
however, she appreciated Mr. Lethbridge’s explanation and she will support this ordinance.

Councilmember McCay supports this ordinance. Councilmember Stewart asked for clarification
on the trees. Mr. Lethridge said that these standards do not address trees. Councilmember
Stewart does not want to remove the requirement for trees help decrease the amount of water
that is used in landscaping.

Councilmember McCay MOVED that the Riverton City Council adopt Ordinance No. 22-18 –
Adopting Amendments to the Landscaping and Watering Requirements for Residential
and Commercial landscaping. Councilmember Wells SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs
called for discussion on the motion. There being none, he called for a roll-call vote. The vote
was as follows: Buroker-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes, Stewart-yes and Wells-yes. The
motion passed unanimously.
