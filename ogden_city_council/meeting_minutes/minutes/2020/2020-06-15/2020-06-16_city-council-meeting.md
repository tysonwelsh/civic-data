301 West 2nd Street Local Historic Designation
         Petitioners Anna and Michael Keogh are proposing the home and property located at 301 West 2 nd Street, known as the
Stone Farm, be listed as an historic resource on the Ogden City Register of Historic Places. The designation would require any
significant changes proposed for the building, including demolition, to be approved by the Landmarks Commission through the
approval of a Certificate of Historic Appropriateness. The designation would help ensure the preservation of the property and
would help to ensure that any changes proposed would maintain the character and historicity of the home and property. The City's
Landmarks Commission has reviewed the proposed designation and has forwarded a recommendation of approval finding that the
building meets both the historical and architectural criteria listed in the City's code for local designation.
         Deputy Planning Manager Spencer used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the nomination to include
the subject property on the register of historic places. He used the aid of an aerial photograph to identify the location of the
property, which is part of the historic Stone Farm. The home on the property was constructed in 1925 on a unique fort remains
foundation. There are four accessory structures, including a garage, chicken coop, three-sided tractor shed, and workshop. The
homeowner has approved the nomination. When considering a nomination, there is a focus on historic significance of the property
and below are the findings of historic significance:
              •   The five Stone Fann buildings were built in the 20's and 30's and tell a story of the property being used as a
                  diversified farm.
              •   The property was a first-rate dairy farm, transitioning to diversified farming of sugar beets, hay, and wheat.
              •   Further transitions included from horses to tractors.
              •   The Stone farmhouse was a modem, new bungalow.

                     The architectural significance of the primary dwelling unit was also considered; the Landmarks Commission found it is a
           one-story bungalow constructed with a bungalow roof; it has two gables on the north fa9ade and gabled ends include arts and
           crafts elements with half-timbered design of wood and plaster. There are original doors and windows on the main level and an
           original enclosed frame porch altered in the 1980s with new windows and siding. The Landmarks Cmmnission voted unanimously
           to recommend approval of the property at 301 West 2 nd Street to be in the local historic register based on the property's historic
           and architectural significance.
                     Council member Stephens inquired as to the property's future plans for continued historic preservation and how
           development of other properties in the area could impact the historical significance of the property. Mr. Spencer stated that if the
           nomination is approved, the property owner will acquiesce some control of their property to the Landmarks Commission; when
           exterior modifications are made, they must be done in a historically significant context to preserve historic integrity. He stated it is
           his understanding that the applicant does not plan to make modifications to their property at this time and they were simply
           seeking an additional layer of protection for the property by way of including it on the local historic register. In tenns of how this
           action will impact future development in the area, it will not impact other properties as the designation only applies to this
           property. Council member Stephens asked if historic markers will be placed on the property. Mr. Spencer stated the applicant
           could place markers on the property, but that is not something the City would facilitate.

           Utah Risk Management Agency lnterlocal Agreement (URMA)
                    Chief Deputy City Attorney Brown indicated City Administration is requesting the Council approve the First Amended
           and Restated Interlocal Agreement (Agreement) with the Utah Risk Management Agency. She provided an overview of the
           changes that have been proposed in the agreement, many of which are clerical and grammatical; there are very few substantive
           changes, the most significant one being that the URMA Board of Directors can make minor changes to the agreement without
           needing to approach each member municipality for approval. The agreement term is 50 years, but the City can tenninate at any
           time during that tenn. The City's Chief Administrative Officer Johnson is the City's representative to the URMA Board of
           Directors and he is also a member of the Executive Board.
                    Council member Hyer inquired as to the number of cities in the State that are members ofURMA, to which Mr. Johnson
           answered approximately 20. Council member Hyer asked if other cities can join. Mr. Johnson stated cities can apply to be a
           member ofURMA and the Board of Directors will evaluate their risk level before detennining if they should be accepted.
                    Council Executive Director Eller-Smith inquired as to City Attorney Williams' involvement in drafting the agreement.
           Ms. Brown stated that URMA has a fairly new Executive Director and that individual asked for support from City Attorneys from
           member cities. Mr. Johnson noted that Mr. Williams was instrumental in pursing some amendments to the agreement to the
           benefit of Ogden and other member cities.

           FY 2020-2021 Budget discussion
                    Budget Message: Council Communications and Public Engagement Coordinator Garside provided an overview of the
           budget message that has been drafted by the Council office; there is a heavy focus on the relationship between City Council
           Strategic Directives and corresponding projects/expenses in the budget document.
                     Goals and Guidelines: Council Policy Analyst Mabey used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the City
           Council's budget guidelines for the FY21 budget; budget guidelines are intended to further define budget goals and provide action
           items, as well as inform budget development, review, approval, and implementation. The budget guidelines that have been
           identified include: annual budget development; annual and quarterly reports; competitive salaries and benefits; Capital
           Improvement Plan (CIP); Consolidated Plan and Annual Action Plan; Council notifications; enterprise and special revenue funds;
           fee changes; utility rates; non-profits; grant reporting and monitoring; policy development; annual certified tax rate evaluation,
           focus on Recreation, Arts, Museum, Parks (RAMP) tax projects; tourism and marketing, and monitoring of transient room tax
           revenues. The current budget goals were adopted January 21, 2020 and they define budgetary intents and priorities of the Council;
           they also align the budget with strategic plan initiatives. Areas of focus within the goals include economic development,
           community safety, recreation, City image and reputation, and fiscal sustainability and transparency.
                     Council Executive Director Eller-Smith asked that the Council review the budget message and provide any feedback or
           recommended changes back to the Council office by the end of this week. She reminded the Council of the decision to not
           consider adjustment of the tax rate this year due to the impact that COVID-19 has had on residents and the local economy;
           however, Council staff needs feedback from the Council regarding whether to consider a tax adjushnent next year as the budget
           process for FY22 will commence early this fall. Council member White stated the State of Utah taxation laws prevent the City
           from capturing any increased revenues associated with inflation; that is why she feels strongly that Administration and Council

           Council member White has discussed several times; it is important to get accustomed to reviewing the tax rate on a regular basis

         Schedule A-1: City Comptroller Stout explained Schedule A-1 amends the adopted tentative budget; Administration is
