Planner Strader said a conditional use permit (CUP) is required for an accessory building 
larger than 2,000 square feet.  The applicant wishes to build a garage of 2,600 square feet and 
an accessory dwelling unit of 988 square feet.  She explained that an accessory dwelling unit 
can be no more than 1,000 square feet.   
 
Planner Strader said the property is located on the Old Lincoln Highway.  The property has a 
combination of zoning including the highway corridor (HC) and AP-40 zones.  The site plan 
was displayed.  The existing home was pointed out.  The proposed structure has a dwelling 
unit, a garage portion, and a carport in the back.  A new septic system will be installed.  
Water will provided by the existing well.  Planner Strader said the setback and height 
requirements are being met. 
 
The floor plan and elevations of the building were shown and the main elements were 
pointed out.  There is an attic and crawl space.  Planner Strader said that Staff recommends 
that following a public hearing, the Commission vote to approve the CUP based on the 
findings of facts, conclusions of law, and conditions of approval as found in the Staff Report.   
 
Chair Ure asked about the memorandum of understanding listed as condition #1.  Planner 
Strader said the memorandum of understanding states that the accessory dwelling unit is 
accessory to the primary use.  It can’t be sold separately or condominium-ized.  She said this 
is a standard requirement for this type of structure.   
 
Commissioner Henrie said since the structure will be located in the AP-40 is there a need for 
a CUP.  Planner Strader said the CUP is needed because the structure exceeds 2,000 square 
feet.  On its own, the accessory dwelling unit would require a low impact permit, but because 
it is connected to the garage, Staff decided to process the entire structure under the CUP.

1. Eastern Summit County Transportation Master Plan Overview – Derrick Radke, 
Public Works Director  
 
Chair Ure welcomed Public Works Director, Derrick Radke who was in attendance to give 
an overview of the Transportation Master Plan and to answer questions.  Director Radke said 
he was asked by Director Putt to have a discussion with the Planning Commission about the 
Transportation Master Plan and the impact that might occur if zoning increases.  He said 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
August 6, 2015 
Page 7 of 12 
 
Kent Wilkerson went parcel by parcel and built a transportation model that was based on 
projected growth with the current zones. 
 
Director Radke said a principal that was used in developing the Capital Improvement Plan 
from the Transportation Master Plan was to achieve the maximum mobility for the minimal 
cost, by using the existing resources as efficiently as possible before investing in new 
infrastructure.  A second principal was to use trip reduction strategies on area roads before 
widening or making major improvements.  A third principal was to work to achieve the 
highest cooperation among all the entities, including counties, cities, and the state.     
 
Commissioner Willoughby asked Director Radke to describe what a trip reduction measure 
would be.  She was told that it would be using other modes than vehicle.  Having a 
neighborhood commercial center would reduce trips said Commissioner Wharton.  People 
wouldn’t have to drive all the way to town.   
 
Director Radke said most of the improvements are minimal in nature.  There are no large 
projects planned on the east side.  Most projects are intersections, the widening of roads for 
multiple uses, and the widening of roads for capacity on SR 32.  He said the plan included a 
lot of trails, one new road, and a proposal for a cross connection.   
 
The estimated cost of the plan by 2040 was 60 million dollars.  That does not include any 
projects that the cities threw into the Master Plan.  Commissioner Clyde asked if there was 
any widening planned for roads other than state roads.  Director Radke said there is a 
proposal to add shoulders and bike lanes to most of the major roads.  
 
Commissioner Vernon asked if the bike lanes will help to reduce the traffic or just add a play 
area.  He thinks the bike lanes will add to the traffic problems.  Commissioner Clyde said 
they need to focus on other things at this meeting.  They should continue with the outline of 
the plan and not discuss its merits at this point.   
 
Director Radke said every road in the North and South Summit area, with the exception of 
SR 32, has a level of service (LOS) A.  This means there is no congestion and the road is free 
moving most of the time.  He said SR 32 has a LOS of C.  On a bad day, you can see traffic 
backing up.  Most roads are good and there is very little need for major dollars to be spent.   
 
Director Radke said the 2010 census and the Governor’s office projects that by 2040, Eastern 
Summit County will go from 7,200 residents to 24,500 residents.  This is an increase of 
240%.  Even with the installed improvements, the majority of the roads will remain LOS A, 
but there are some roads that will become LOS B, C, D and F.  He listed those roads as:  
• SR 32 between Oakley and Kamas will fail (LOS F) 
• SR 32 between Kamas and Francis will become a LOS D 
• Weber Canyon Road and others will go to a LOS D 
 
The percentage of failure is relatively small, but there are roads that will be affected.  Chair 
Ure said Mr. Wilkerson figured that every zone and piece of land would be at maximum
