       4.4     South Valley Sewer District Report of Operations and Finance(Presenters Joel
               Thompson-General Manaeer: Cody Keddin2ton-Finance: and Perk Timothy-
               Chairmati),


Former Mayor, Derk Timothy,reported that he has represented the City ofBluffdale on the Sewer
District's Board of Trustees for the last 12 years. This will be his last official act in this capacity
and it has been a privilege to represent the City. He truly believes the Sewer District has the City's
best interests at heart. General Manager, Joel Thompson was introduced. He does a good job and
steers everyone in the right direction. The Board and staff members have a very good relationship
thanks in large part to the efforts ofMr.Thompson. Cody Keddington serves as the ChiefFinancial
Officer("CFO") and keeps the finances in line. He is always well-prepared and able to answer
questions that arise. Mayor Timothy commented that he has enjoyed working with both men.

Mayor Timothy commented on his experience with the Citizens Academy and stated that he
completed the course when he was Mayor along with his wife, Tami. They learned a lot and
enjoyed participating. He recommended it highly.

Mr. Thompson reported that he has served as the General Manager since April 2023 when he took
over for Craig White. He was with the district for 10 years working in the Collections Department
and most recently in the Jordan Basin Water Reclamation Facility before moving into the office.
A brief overview was provided of what the District does. It was founded in 1961 under Salt Lake
County at which time it was called the Salt Lake Sewerage Improvement District #1. It was passed
by resolution of the Salt Lake County Commission and the original district comprised 54 square
miles in Draper, Sandy, Riverton, South Jordan, and Bluffdale. Today, the South Valley Sewer
District incorporates six different cities with roughly 1,100 miles ofpipe in the ground. There are
over 94,000 connections, 27,000 manholes, flows that are sent to three separate treatment plants,
and roughly 70 employees on staff.

The Board of Trustees is the governing body for the district and includes five members. Four are
appointed and one is elected. Bluffdale has one of the appointed seats. The Board is very
supportive ofstaff and Mayor Timothy was thanked for his leadership.

Mr. Keddington reported that he has been the Chief Financial Officer("CFO")for six years and
previously served as the Controller at Central Valley Water. The finances of the district are in
very good shape. They are a Single Enterprise Fund withjust over $40 million in operating revenue
per year. They have $30 million in service charges in addition to a property tax component. Impact
fees are collected firom new developments as well. $5 million is currently collected but is expected
to increase with future other large developments. They are required to separate them by type. Mr.
Keddington reported that he also serves as the Administrative Services Director and does all ofthe
billing and working with customers.


Mr. Thompson described some ofthe services the district provides. The collection system, which
includes the mainline pipes in the ground, is inspected and maintained regularly. They have in-
house Blue Stakes utility markings, perform lateral inspections for new construction, have an
Engineering Department that reviews design and construction services, and provide wastewater
treatment.



They provide main line cleaning and maintenance and conduct inspections. They have a rotation
to get through the entire system to ensure that it is maintained and operating properly. During
inspections,they find broken pipes, grease, and other obstructions that can be responded to prevent
issues fi-om reaching homeowners. A major component of the district is treatment. They treat
roughly 19.2 million gallons of wastewater per day. The three different treatment plants that flow
are sent to include the following:


   • The Jordan Basin Water Reclamation Facility, which is located in Riverton,and owned and
       operated by the District.
   • The South Valley Water Reclamation Facility. The District is one of the five-member
       entities with about 6 million gallons sent there daily.
   • The Timpanogos Special Service District ("TSSD"). Flows firom the top of South
       Mountain are taken by gravity south into Utah County.

Mr.Thompson reported that as a District, they are approximately 50% built out. The densities and
housing issues the State is facing will create challenges that they will accommodate. The Jordan
Basin Facility expects to expand from 15 to 30 million gallons in the next five to seven years. The
Prison and Kennecott properties will also be developed in the coming years.

Mayor Hall asked which facility would be the best to tour. Mr. Thompson recommended the
Jordan Basin Facility. Council Member Aston was asked to arrange a tour.

Mayor Hall commented that to have someone serve on the Board for 11 years is unique. Mayor
Timothy was presented with a plaque in gratitude for his service on the Sewer Board and his
dedication. Mayor Timothy commented that the Sewer District thinks well into the future in terms
of growth and continually analyzes the plans for various cities. The Jordan Basin Treatment
Facility is only 50% built out but there is a footprint for the remaining 50%, which it is designed.
Eventually, more capacity will be needed. The District approached Sandy City and asked them to
buy their unused capacity at the South Valley Reclamation Center.

Mayor Timothy reported that Council Member Aston attended a recent Board Meeting where she
was introduced to staff and the Board. She has a good head start and her appointment is a very
good choice.
