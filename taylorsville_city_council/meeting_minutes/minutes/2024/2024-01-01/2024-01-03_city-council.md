        1.2      Mayor’s Report

Mayor Kristie Overson congratulated Councilmembers Barbieri, Burgess, and Cochran on their
reelection. She looked forward to an exciting 2024 with the City Council.

There had recently been a ribbon cutting for Jade’s Corner Deli was now open on 4700 South. It
was a fun event with many food choices offered.

Saturday with Santa was a well-attended event with many games and treats available. The Mayor
expressed appreciation to the Historic Preservation Committee, the Parks and Recreation
Committee, Youth Council, and Unified Fire for their help during the event which was a long-
standing Taylorsville tradition.

For the second consecutive year, Wreaths Across America took place on December 16th with a
ceremony held at city hall followed by the laying of wreaths on veterans’ graves at the Taylorsville
Cemetery. Mayor Overson said this was a very moving, inspiring event which she looked forward
to having the city participate in for many years to come. She thanked the Youth Council, Honor
365, Taylorsville Jr. ROTC, Taylorsville Police, cemetery sexton Suzie Smith, and many
volunteers who helped make it a great event.

Speaking of Honor 365, the non-profit organization donated a huge ham and large basket of
groceries and asked that the city give both to a family in need. TVPD knew just such a family, and
the mayor sincerely appreciated the generosity from Honor 365.

As was the case each December, there were many holiday activities to attend. She expressed her
appreciation to the councilmembers for their participation and for representing Taylorsville so
well.

The Youth Council was very busy over the holidays, helping at city hall as well as at the Heritage
Museum. The kids were a delight and were now working on job shadowing of various city
employees. Each would write a report on their experience, and she looked forward to reading about
their impressions. It was a great learning experience for staff as well as the youth. Earlier today
they had discussed various topics regarding elections, and the importance of serving one’s
community.

City representatives recently met with the Food Truck League to review how things had gone for
Starry Nights on the Plaza last summer. The League was pleased with their sales and looked
forward to participating again during Summer 2024.

                3.1      Unified Fire Authority – Captain Richard Rich

       Unified Fire Captain Richard Rich reported on UFA activities during the 3rd quarter of 2023
(July through September 2023.) This included a total of 1,365 calls for service, 45% of which (621
calls) were classified as emergent. He presented a bar graph depicting the total number of calls
during this quarter in each of the last four years. During Q3, UFA responded to the following fire
calls in Taylorsville:
     12 structure fires
     10 fires in natural vegetation
     5 fires involving outdoor rubbish
     3 vehicle fires
     1 fire inside a vehicle

The most prevalent medical calls during the quarter were:
    Sick Person (141)
    Motor Vehicle Crash (100)
    Short Falls <6 feet (84)
    Unconscious Person (64)
    Breathing Problem (64)

Other call types during Q3 were:
    Public Service Assistance (45)
    Unintentional Detector (35)
    False Alarm/False Call (19)
    Detector Malfunction (18)
    Flammable Spills & Leaks (10)

Captain Rich provided information regarding the number of calls that each station in or near
Taylorsville, with station no. 117 remaining the busiest, with 56.93% of engine/truck responses
and 44.76% of the medical calls.

Regarding emergency calls, 50% of them involved a response time of 04:52 or better and 90% of
the calls showing an arrival time within 07:11.

                 Code to Implement Utah Senate Bill 174, Related to the Subdivision Review
                 Processes – Jim Spung

      Senior Planner Jim Spung explained that during the 2023 General Legislative Session, the
Utah legislature amended the process by which cities and counties review and approve subdivision
applications. The intent was to establish a more uniform, streamlined, and predictable subdivision
review and approval procedure throughout Utah. Local jurisdictions were given until February 1,
2024 to adopt updates to their subdivision ordinances. He also pointed out that the amendments to
Chapter 13.35 were no longer applicable.

Mr. Spung explained the following requirements regarding review of preliminary subdivision
plats:
     Maximum of two review cycles
     Staff to provide initial review comments within 15 business days of completed application
       submission;
     Applicant required to provide written response to each staff comment;
     If applicant responds within 20 business days of staff’s initial review, staff will then have
       15 business days to review and provide comments for the 2nd submittal; however, if
       applicant fails to respond within 20 business days, staff will have 35 business days to
       review and provide comments for 2nd submittal.
     A review cycle is not considered complete until the applicant has adequately addressed
       each of the redlines identified by the city.
     Upon receipt of 2nd submittal, the city will notice and schedule a public hearing—of which
       only one is allowed.
     The Planning Commission is the preliminary subdivision plat land-use authority;
     No new redlines/comments may be made by the city after the first review cycle in
       responses to changes made by the applicant except to protect public health or safety or to
       enforce state or federal law;
     If the application complies with adopted local regulations, it shall be approved and proceed
       to the final review.

Regarding the review of final subdivision plats:
    Maximum of two review cycles
    Staff to provide initial review comments within 20 business days of application submittal;
    Applicant required to provide written response to each staff comment;
    If the final subdivision application complies with adopted standards, the city shall approve
      the final subdivision application.
    The Department Director is proposed to be the final subdivision plat land use authority,
      although the Mayor will still need to sign the mylar plat prior to recordation at the Salt
      Lake County Recorder’s Office.

8.      NEW ITEMS FOR SUBSEQUENT CONSIDERATION (No Action)

        8.1      Councilmember Burgess – expressed interest in changing city code to allow for a
                 campaign donation over $1,500 for those running to serve on the City Council. He
                 recommended the limit be increased to $2,000 or $2,500 and pointed out that the
                 maximum for a mayoral candidate’s campaign was $7,500.

        Councilmember Harker asked what the reason was behind having such a limit. Mr. Cowdell
        responded that a previous Council had chosen to impose the limit years ago. He explained
        that while some people believed large donations resulted in greater influence over an
        elected official who received the donation, others felt it was a free speech issue and that
        one should be able to donate as much as one wished. The current limits had been in place
        for many years.

        Mayor Overson stated that the topic could certainly be placed on a future agenda.
