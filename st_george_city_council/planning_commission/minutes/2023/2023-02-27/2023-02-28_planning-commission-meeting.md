   1. ZONE CHANGE (PD-A) (Public Hearing) Legislative

       A. Consider a to change the zone from A-20 (Agriculture 20-acre minimum lot size) to R-1-20
          (Residential, Single Family, 20,000 sq ft minimum lot size) on approximately 20.5 acres located on
          the north-east corner of 2450 South and 2580 East. The project will be known as Old Farm. The
          applicant is proposing single family lots on the property and a potential site for a church. The
          applicant is Red Sands F-1, LC and the representative is Bob Hermandson. Case No. 2023-ZC-002.
          (Staff – Dan Boles)

          Dan Boles presented the following:

          Dan Boles – The whole property is about 70 acres. We are only looking at rezoning about 20 acres.
          You will see the preliminary plat at the next meeting but I put this in here so you could see how it
          would layout. All four lots will meet the zoning minimum. Staff is recommending approval. I did
          get one letter which is before you.

          Chair Kemp opened the public hearing.

         informed as to how the project began, what’s been going on behind the scenes and the future of it.

      B. Consider a to change the zone from R-1-8 (Single Family Residential minimum 8,000 sq ft lot size),
         R-1-10 (Single Family Residential minimum 10,000 sq ft lot size) to PD-R (Planned Development
         Residential). The applicant is seeking approval to change the zone on 14.99 acres to build a
         townhome development consisting of 134 units. The property is generally located at 1100 W Curley
         Hollow Dr. The applicant is Tonaquint Inc, and the representative is Tim Stewart. The project will
         be known as Rosewood Townhomes. Case No. 2023-ZC-001. (Staff – Mike Hadley)

         Mike Hadley presented the following:

         Mike Hadley – Mike described the location, and showed elevations of the townhomes.

         Commissioner Anderson – I know the applicant; they do a fantastic job. The concern here is the
         clay. With that the engineer is asking that there not be any vegetation 10 ft from the buildings. If the
         report is followed and the buildings are 20 ft apart. That means there will be no vegetation between
         these buildings.

         Jared Bates – What Tim is anticipating doing is over excavation and getting everything out.

         Commissioner Anderson – I know, but they are still saying 10 ft from the buildings. You might
         have to lose a few buildings, but you will need to move them a bit apart. The other question I had is
         what kind of amenity will you put in there?

         Jared Bates – A pool and changing room and some playground equipment.

         Commissioner Chapman – So it doesn’t meet the landscape requirement?

         Commissioner Anderson – I don’t know, I didn’t calculate it.

         Commissioner Chapman – I don’t think you can do it with that density.

         Commissioner Fisher – What they are saying is if we don’t address the landscaping here then the
         ordinance will require a certain percentage of landscaping.

         Commissioner Chapman – I think the density is too dense regardless.

         Commissioner Anderson – Right now it’s R-1-8 but what is the General Plan Mike?

         Mike Hadley – It is MDR which is 5-9 units per acre.

         Chair Kemp – How many units?

         Mike Hadley – 134, so it is 8.9 units per acre.

         Chair Kemp opened the public hearing.

         Chair Kemp closed the public hearing.

         Commissioner Chapman – I think it’s too many, that area is too busy as it is. I think 134 units is
         going to be an awfully lot of families. There is not a lot of space there in the unit. I think adding
         another 200 and some cars out of there is crazy. If you have R-1-8 it is still a single-family home, it
         will add some traffic, but not as much.

         Discussion on densities.

         Commissioner Anderson – I’m not as concerned about the multifamily. The MDR gives this
         multifamily, with R-1-8 you can’t be multifamily. With this zone they can be connected, which
         makes a lot of sense with this type of soil. The main concern I have is I know this is a tough site
         with the hill, with the clay, which I’m sure the developer is fully aware. We just want to make sure
         that we don’t have 20 ft wide gravel beds and maybe there is some way to massage in a unit here or
         a unit there, a few tweaking to be sure we have a good landscape plan. There are a lot of retaining
         walls on it., and that limitation on the 10 ft is going to be to the retaining walls as well so we want to
         identify that on the landscape plan. Make sure we have a nice neighborhood there.

         Commissioner Fisher – I think if that’s done, it may accomplish a little bit of what Commissioner
         Chapman is concerned about as far as density goes because it will probably affect the number of
         buildings. As I look at this report as well, on page 11 of the report are the drainage recommendations
         after the over excavation is done. I have done enough work defending of these folks, these
         Geotech’s, to know that of course they are putting things in there to be protective as well, to reduce
         their liability. But this one goes further than most reports I’ve seen. It does mention what
         Commissioner Anderson said as far as the 10 ft, but they have added an impermeable membrane as
         well. From the foundations of not only the homes, but also the walls. So, obviously, there is some
         greater concern given the soil here. So perhaps in addressing Commissioner Anderson’s concern, it
         may just solve Commissioner Chapman’s concern as well if there is a little bit of adjustment as far as
         location of units to allow for some amount of trees, bushes without sacrificing too many units but at
         the same time perhaps maybe approaching a number that Commissioner Chapman might be more in
         line with.
