STAFF PRESENTATION
The Staff Report to the Planning Commission provides details of the facts of the case and the Staff's analysis, conclusions,
and recommendations.
   • Melia Dayley gave an overview of the proposed changes and answered previous questions regarding current ADU
       allowances, potential ADU numbers, Provo rental rates, enforcement rates, and comparisons to other cities.
   • Scott Johnson (Zoning Administrator) defined the process for ADUs and answered questions regarding
       enforcement procedures.
   • Marcus Draper (Provo Attorney) detailed family definitions and parking regulations.
   • David Day (Provo Engineering) responded to questions regarding increased infrastructure needs for detached
       ADUs, but had no issue with the current proposals.

CITY DEPARTMENTAL ISSUES
• The Coordinator Review Committee (CRC) has reviewed the application and given their approval.

• City-wide application; all Neighborhood Chairs received notification.

        “move-up” housing.

APPLICANT RESPONSE
Key points addressed in the applicant's presentation to the Planning Commission included the following:
   • Melia Dayley responded to questions regarding occupancy of the ADUs, locations of ADUs, and reasons for

• This item was City-wide or affected multiple neighborhoods.

CONCERNS RAISED BY PUBLIC
Any comments received prior to completion of the Staff Report are addressed in the Staff Report to the Planning
Commission. Key issues raised in written comments received subsequent to the Staff Report or public comment during
the public hearing included the following:
    • Becky Bogdin stated that neighbors support the current standards and are against the proposed amendments.
    • Dan Bahr stated that he is not in support of the requested changes to the General Plan.
    • Erin May stated she is against the plan and that areas for higher density are already addressed in the area plan.
    • Lynn Dalton was hopeful that his property could develop at higher density and supportive of the changes.
    • Lee Sands stated he supported the changes for reasons of property rights.

APPLICANT RESPONSE
Key points addressed in the applicant's presentation to the Planning Commission included the following:
   • Jon Jensen shared his experience of trying to bring this proposal forward for several years and believes things
       have changed since 2020 that should open up the area for higher density.
   • Jay Henrie gave an overview of the concept that would be brought forward for the property on 1804 N Geneva if
       the proposed General Plan amendments were approved. He detailed the proposed sewer connections, use, and
       application of “grey-water” systems to help mitigate the concerns on infrastructure limits. He reiterated his belief
       that the proposal would not put a strain on wastewater or water capacity. He believes he needs the higher density
       to make the project attractive and financially sound.

PLANNING COMMISSION DISCUSSION
Key points discussed by the Planning Commission included the following:
   • Raleen Whalin inquired about the needs and reasons for the higher density request and asked the applicant about
       unit prices in his concept plans.
   • Lisa Jensen noted that while the system mitigation efforts were helpful for applicants’ specific project, the entire
       westside of the City would be allowed to increase density with the proposal which could have infrastructure and
       financial implications for the City.
   • Jeff Whitlock stated that there was not a lot of information and data to back up the claims of the applicant, and
       that the property could also be sold to someone who doesn’t apply mitigating measures.
   • The Commission agreed that the main concerns stated in the staff report have not been addressed and that the City
       should not vary from the adopted plans for the westside that have taken a lot of time and study to establish. The
       proposed changes would undermine previous planning efforts.

Item #4       Bob Jones requests a variance through the Planning Commission to impact slopes exceeding 30 percent
              (Section 15.07.010) for a proposed residential subdivision, located at approximately 379 E 4900 N in the
              R1.10 zone. North Timpview Neighborhood. Brandon Larsen (801) 852-6408 jblarsen@provo.org
              PLPSUB20210136


The following action was taken by the Planning Commission on the above described item at its regular meeting of
September 28, 2022:

                                                 CONTINUED
On a vote of 6:0, the Planning Commission voted to continue the above application.
Reasons for Continuance: 1. Applicant must submit to Public Works data that has been requested including a plan for
impacted slope restoration, 2. Geotechnical report for slope stability, 3. Confirmation from the State regarding dam safety,
4. To allow staff time to consider boring for the water line as an alternative, and 5. A statement from the property to the
north as to the feasibility of a water line.

Motion By: Jeff Whitlock
Second By: Andrew South
Votes in Favor of Motion: Jeff Whitlock, Andrew South, Melissa Kendall, Robert Knudsen, Raleen Wahlin, Lisa Jensen
Lisa Jensen was present as Chair.


STAFF PRESENTATION
The Staff Report to the Planning Commission provides details of the facts of the case and the Staff's analysis, conclusions,
and recommendations.

CITY DEPARTMENTAL ISSUES
• The Public Works Department had concerns with the requested action. The proposed action would cut into an existing
   30% slope. No information had been provided to show how the slope will be restored to a natural condition. No
   Geotechnical Report showing slope stability had been submitted. Requests for State approval for dam safety had not
   been satisfied.

NEIGHBORHOOD MEETING DATE
• A neighborhood meeting was not required nor held on the request for a variance.

NEIGHBORHOOD AND PUBLIC COMMENT
• Nine members of the public spoke to the Planning Commission. Concerns raised included the following:
    Destruction of the detention basin that was built for Phase I of the subdivision (The Preserve on Canyon Road)
    Problems with grading already performed by the applicant without city permits
    The need for a detention basin due to the amount of water that comes off the hillside during heavy rain
    That there are other options that would not impact 30% slopes
    That the hardship is partially created by the land and partially created by the applicant
                                                        Page 1 of 2
     Approval of this variance would create an inequity with other developers who follow the rules
     The applicant cannot be trusted because he has repeatedly graded the hillsides without permits
     This approval would violate the Critical Hillside Overlay Zone
•   In addition to these comments, the city received many letters from citizens that were forwarded to the Planning
    Commission. Those letters are attached to this report of action.

APPLICANT RESPONSE
The applicant was represented by legal counsel who generally agreed with the analysis and recommendation of the staff
report. Counsel pointed out that the hardship with the property was with its elevation and inability to obtain water service
from existing infrastructure, like Phase I. Counsel, however, took exception with a statement on page three of the staff
report which suggested that the widest point of the limits of disturbance was all 30% slope, when in actuality only a small
portion of this area was 30% slopes or greater. Counsel also took exception with the statement that other alternatives for
a water line were possible and he took exception with the hardship being anything other than the land, itself.

PLANNING COMMISSION DISCUSSION
Key points discussed by the Planning Commission included the following:
   • Questions were asked regarding when the ordinance prohibiting impact to 30% slopes was adopted, when the
       property was purchased and when the Critical Hillside Overlay Zone was adopted.
   • A question was asked regarding the boundary between the city and the county for property to the north.
   • In response to a statement made by a Planning Commission member, the city attorney clarified that all of the
       criteria for a variance must be met in order for a variance to be approved.
   • The Planning Commission asked about the feasibility of a water line to the north that would not impact 30%
       slopes.
   • Staff suggested a continuance of this item before the Planning Commission asked any questions or had any
       discussion specific to the variance criteria.

Subject: Item 4 - 30% slope disturbance variance request

Planning Commission Members,
     Thank you for your service.
    I'm writing in regards to the requested 30% slope disturbance variance request. I have looked at the
topography maps included in the staff report. It appears to me that this is as simple as moving where the
applicant would like to put the water line so it does not encroach on a possibly sensitive hillside area. By
moving one or 2 proposed homes or lots slightly east the line could be run and paved over (as the public
works department prefers for access) also allowing the possibility of future foot traffic or road access for
future developments in the area. It is possible that moving those lines may require changing the size of
some of the proposed homes. This is not a hardship that the city of Provo is required to make up the
difference for a developers speculation in an area whose topography he knew would in some way affect
how building would proceed.
    Staff presents that this variance should be granted to 'create a more favorable environment for the
citizens of Provo' but that is actually why so many people worked so hard to create the Critical Hillside
Overlay Zone just two short years ago - to create protection for the already magnificent environment that
Provo now enjoys. The CHOZ was created to protect exactly this sensitive hillside area.
    The applicant has known for years that this project was not feasible without more water. It was
explained years ago that proceeding with any development here would require creating a new water
storage tank in the area. I don't see in the report where/what this water line will actually tie into. Has the
new water tank been created for this line to even go to yet? Public works has suggested that it would be
best for the line to go somewhere else. (These are the people who have to be able to access these lines
to work on them in the future). It says the variance will be for an eight inch water line but that the area of
disturbance at it's widest will be 300 feet! - is that a typo? Even if 300 feet is a reference to the length of
the trench, we are still talking about an almost football field size hole in a critical hillside area. Why create
a hole of that size to disturb a sensitive area for a water line that may or may not have a destination yet. A
football size width hole in a critical hillside sensitive area to lay an eight inch pipe does not sound like a
reasonable request.
    You all work so hard for the benefit of the future of Provo as a whole. I ask you at this time not to grant
this request for a variance to the CHOZ standard which was created to protect not only the beauty of the
area but the safety of the future residents in that area. Let's take time to decide where exactly would be
best in the long term for the needed infrastructure for safety, future accessibility for public works and
protection of this sensitive area as outlined in the CHOZ code.
    Thank you again for sharing your time with me,
Sharon Memmott
Provo
