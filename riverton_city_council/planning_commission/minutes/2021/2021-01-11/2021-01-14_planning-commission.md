       A.     HOME OCCUPATION, SARAH, AND JOSH CALDWELL ARE REQUESTING
              A CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT FOR A SHORT-TERM RENTAL AT 1923
              WEST CARRIAGE AVENUE.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich, reported that this is a short-term rental located at 1923 West
Carriage Avenue, near the south border of the City. The location of the property was
described. It is in a subdivision that was built in the late 1970s and is an all-brick rambler.
The house has approximately a 1,600 square-foot footprint plus a basement underneath and
is on 0.32 acres. All the zoning around this parcel is R-4, which are a one-quarter acre or is
10,000 square-foot lots. It is larger than some of the neighboring lots. There is a two-car
garage plus an extra driveway and the yard is fenced.

Mr. Prestwich reported that short-term rentals are home occupations but have their own
unique rules. Some of the criteria that must abide by are as follows:

       1.     The applicant must own the home and live there.

       2.     Rentals are limited to 150 nights per year.

       3.     There shall be only one Rental Agreement at a time with no more than eight
              people per Rental Agreement.

       4.     Rental Agreements cannot exceed 29 days to keep it within the definition of a
              short-term rental. If a renter stays longer, it moves it into a different section of
              the State Code which deals with apartments or a housing contract.

       5.     The owner must provide a contact number to neighbors within 300 feet.

       6.     The backyard must be fenced.

       7.     The address should be clearly visible on the front of the house or the mailbox.

       8.     Off-street parking must be provided.

       9.     The applicant must obtain a Riverton Business License.

Mr. Prestwich explained that with the business license, they will register with the Utah State
Tax Commission and they have to collect a room tax. The City Council passed that

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the 11800 South is sometimes called
Meyer’s Lane. There is a subdivision immediately to the east, known as the Craig View
Subdivision. It is also a private lane and is currently under construction. The neighborhood
is mostly built out but there are four narrow and deep lots.

With regard to zoning, three parcels to the west, including the subject parcel, are zoned RR-
22, which is one-half acre with animal rights. The Craig View got a rezone to match the
General Plan to R-3. The General Plan for the RR-22 parcels is also R-3. Mr. Prestwich
reported that the applicants are not asking for a rezone but simply wants to do a private lane
development on the 1.67 acres with the existing RR-22 zoning in place. The request tonight
is for approval of the Concept Plan only.

Mr. Prestwich reported that a private lane in Riverton City’s Code is allowed but is not the
preferred method of development. The City’s Code encourages development to go with
public streets and to meet City standards. The Code also recognizes that there are certain
circumstances where City streets do not make sense or that development cannot occur with
a City street. It, therefore, gives three criteria for evaluating an application on whether a
private lane can be used. Rule #3 specifies that the development does not impede the
necessary access for adjoining properties as required by the Master Transportation Plan.

Mr. Prestwich reported that with the existing pattern, there is no master-planned street in
this area. The Master Transportation Plan does not identify any and there are no stubs. The
Commission can approve this if they find that there is no other way to develop the property.
Given that the lot is so narrow, if a public street were done solely on the lot, it would take up
so much of the land that it would make development impossible. Staff’s opinion was that it
meets the criteria for a private lane.

Mr. Prestwich reported that comments were received on the application. One of the
neighbors raised concerns about the barn, which would be handled with the site plan. The
barn was discussed with the Craig View Subdivision. It is about 5,600 square feet in size
and was constructed between 1997 and 2002. It is 2 ½ feet from the property line and the
walls on the east property line are 12 ½ feet high. The applicant is showing that it is staying
on the plans but that does not mean it would stay. It would have to meet the ordinance with
the site plan approval and the subdivision approval.

Staff recommended approval with the conditions set forth in the staff report. Mr. Prestwich
reported that the applicant is only asking for three lots. With the zone in place and with the
1.67 acres, that’s all they could get. Staff limited it to three instead of four. The applicant is
required to meet all other ordinances as well. Mr. Prestwich reported that the Conditional
Use will expire by July 14 this year if the applicant does not submit a subdivision application
by then. He further stated that this was noticed as a public hearing and the neighbors were
invited to attend.

       E.     ZONE TEXT CHANGE, RIVERTON CITY IS PROPOSING CHANGES TO
              THE SETBACKS IN THE PLANNED COMMERCIAL CENTER ZONE.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the matter was brought forth by staff.
It was noticed as a public hearing and published in the newspaper and on the City’s
webpage. Mr. Prestwich stated that the Commission will make a recommendation. If the
Planning Commission forwards a recommendation to the City Council, the Council would
make a legislative decision to change the laws. Mr. Prestwich presented the current City
zoning map and noted that the discussion pertained to the PCC Zone, which is south of
12600 at 4150 West. It is tucked in between Bangerter Highway and the Parkside
development, which is RM-14. On the east side of Eagle Flight Rise Road is the Riverton
Business Park. On the west, PCC comes up against residential. Mr. Prestwich stated that
there is a bit of conflict there, an ambiguity in the ordinance.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the PCC Zone was adopted around 2014. The City recognized
there was a huge field and there was no master plan. A zoning ordinance was prepared
that anticipated a large development there. Mr. Prestwich reported that master plans of the
area have been seen several times as part of the SLR development. The RM-14 area was
removed from the PCC and zoned multi-family residential. Holiday Oil has also obtained a
rezone to CR to avoid ambiguities of the PCC. Mr. Prestwich reported that there is an
ordinance with two different standards. One specifies that rear yard setbacks or side yard
setbacks from residential shall be 40 feet to the property line. The other says that whenever
the development in the PCC goes up against a non-compatible use like residential, there
must be 10 feet of landscaping plus 100 feet to the building. Staff was asking for clarification
of the 40 feet and 100 feet.

Mr. Prestwich reported that an applicant has met with staff about developing. He displayed
a rendering showing how narrow the piece becomes for the 100-foot setback to be met. No
comments were received on the item. Mr. Prestwich stated that no direct mailers were sent
because this is a zone text change. He pointed out that the units in Parkside are fairly close
to the property line. When it was approved, the applicant indicated that the property to the
east was to be developed as residential. The setbacks are now 10 or 15 feet. Mr. Prestwich
further stated that this only impacts the PCC’s west boundary unless SLR develops housing
there. Mr. Prestwich stated that it could be changed to 40 feet with 100 feet removed from
the other section. Other minor changes were identified. He noted that on a large parcel the
setbacks from building to building will be 20 feet.

4.     DISCUSSION ITEMS

       A.     Elderly Housing Overlay Zone.

Commissioner Rushton reported that the matter came about because of the Elderly Housing
Overlay that went on to City Council a few meetings prior. The City Council tabled it because
the Planning Commission or City Council acted on it when the master plan was reviewed.
The Planning Commission was asked to look at it.

Commissioner Rushton’s thoughts on the DEJ application was that it is a process that allows
an applicant to pursue higher density housing using the Elderly Housing Overlay with no
designation of an R Zone. He questioned that avenue to get to high-density housing. There
is also no defined standard for removing an Elderly Housing Overlay once it is approved.
He asked if that would be problematic in the future once a zone is applied. Mr. Bott stated
that there are several ways to get around it. He further stated that if the overlay provides a
useful benefit to the City in managing R issues, it should be kept. If it does not, it is just a
value that someone came up with that has run its course.

Commissioner Rushton looked at South Jordan’s ordinances and found that Riverton is
broad in terms of what it allows. South Jordan City separates institutional housing such as
rehabilitation centers from independent living. Riverton City allows all of those to exist.
Commissioner Rushton reported that the City Council discussed senior housing recently.
Those cited as good examples do not include the Elderly Housing Overlay. They exist either
because of an HOA or how they were developed. He added that the Elderly Housing Overlay
is viewed as a mechanism to enable quality elderly housing but it is not needed for that. He
suggested that it be refined and refocused.

Commissioner Lefgren concurred with Commissioner Rushton and expressed concerns that
elderly housing is becoming a mechanism for increasing density. He stated that the density
