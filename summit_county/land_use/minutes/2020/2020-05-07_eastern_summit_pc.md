1. Discussion regarding the amendments to the Eastern Summit County Development 
Code Chapter 3 specific to Front Setbacks, Front setback if property lines extend to 
the center of a public road, and Front setback if property lines extend to the center of 
a private access road. Additional discussion regarding amendment to Appendix A 
definition of Setback, Front. – Tiffanie Northrup-Robinson, Senior Planner 
 
Planner Northrup-Robinson shared the background for this discussion. There have been 
some issues arise with projects in regard to setbacks. The current Code set in 2018 defines 
Front Setbacks as twenty feet (20’) from the front property line, or fifty-five feet (55’) from 
the center line of a public road or private access road. The current Code does not address if 
the property line goes beyond the road. There was an applicant who had a road running 
through their property with a structure less than twenty-five feet (25’) from the road, 
which did not meet current setback requirements. The property line extended beyond the 
road. The proposed amendments would simplify setback requirements to make it clear 
what Front Setback requirements are. Currently, if there is a road running through a 
property, all front setback requirements need to be met, with no exceptions for corner lots. 
The proposed amendments will allow for exceptions if the lot meets certain standards. 
The proposed amendments were shared and discussed. One thing that is not in the 
proposed changes that should be added is to specify if there is a corner lot, that the setback

2. Discussion regarding the affordable housing component of a proposed rezone 
(from AG-80 to Commercial) and Master Planned Development (MPD) for the 
Whisper Ridge Development, a 200-unit multi-family project, Highway 248, Parcel 
CD-417-418-D, PC Development LLC, applicant. – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
 
Planner Milliner shared the background for this application. There was a preliminary 
discussion of this project on March 5, 2020. This project is for 200 units with a mix of 
affordable, attainable, and workforce housing and market units. One of the takeaways from 
the Planning Commission at that last meeting was they would like to see a breakdown of 
what is affordable. There needs to be a compelling reason for going from one unit of 
density to 200 units. Attainable housing is such a need in our community. 
Henry Sigg stated that a request was made at the last meeting for an analysis of costs to 
build these types of units. Planning Staff was provided with a national cost of construction 
for affordable housing. The Housing Affordability Study was referenced. The project was 
considered as a standard project with AMI (Area Median Income) criteria. The minimum 
requirement for any application in Eastern Summit County would be twenty percent of the 
units being AMI. The rest of the units are allocated at market rate. Many of the market units 
could potentially fall within an AMI category. The unit breakdown was shared, including
