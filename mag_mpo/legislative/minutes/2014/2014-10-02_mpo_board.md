Bylaws amendment and Resolution Discussion - Shawn Seager After a year of listening to many different people, consulting the Chair and Vice Chair, and the MPO Finance Committee it was determined to amend the wording in Article II, Section 3, paragraph 3 of the MPO Bylaws. Currently, the Bylaws state that the 'leadership of both legislative chambers shall appoint someone to attend Regional Planning.' The amendment states the MPO Regional Planning Committee may appoint someone to attend. Commissioner Gary Anderson moved to amend the MAG MPO Bylaws Article II, Section 3, paragraph 3, to "One member of each of the State House of Representatives and the State Senate may be appointed by the MAG MPO Regional Planning Committee. These members must reside within the MPO boundaries." Mayor Randy Farnworth seconded the motion. The motion passes unanimously. In accordance with the Utah Open Meeting Act: the Regional Planning Committee has the authority to take action on motions and resolutions. The Regional Planning Committee can establish its own bylaws. Demographic Projection Process - Shawn Eliot Developing the long range transportation plan staff uses the best available data on projected growth, municipality general plans, employment projections, and proposed developments. All this data is input into the regional growth projection model which forecasts where population, households, and employment will be through 2040. Demographic projections are done on a small areas known as a Traffic Analysis Zone (TAZ) which are about 6 urban city blocks in size. Staff met with all the municipalities to review the data projections. Modal runs were done to compare jobs and households to the previous 2008 data model forecasts. There was very little change from the 2008 data forecasts. So the conclusion was a little less growth south of Spanish Fork, a little more growth in the northwest county area, and more density along the I-15 corridor between Orem and Provo. Draft MAG Regional Transportation Plan 2015-2040-Roads, Transit, and Bike/Pedestrian Projects Branded as "TransPlan40" - Shawn Eliot, Chad Eccles, and Jim Price The regional transportation plan is updated every four years to reflect anticipated changes in population, employment, and land use. We are branding this plan as 'TransPlan40' and it will be completed and adopted by June 2015. Staff has developed a list of regional highway, transit and bikes/ped projects that are listed on as needs basis. The projects are phased by 2015-2024, 2025-2034, 2035-2040, and vision projects. Federal regulations require the project lists meet air quality conformity and the plan is fiscally constrained to reasonable funding assumptions. This process will be done later this year or early next year. Highways: regional roads include minor arterials and larger facilities currently on or proposed for the State Functional Classified Highway System. Transit: the travel modal shows Draper to Spanish Fork a light rail in phase 2; Provo to Payson commuter rail in phase 2. Bus routes are determined by UTA. Bike/ped: projects are based from the cities' bike/ped studies and by the Utah Valley Trail Committee. Projects are on-road and independent bikeways. Bike/ped projects are not modeled nor are they phased. TAC Committee recommends Regional Planning approve the proposed draft projects. Mayor J.H. Hadfield moved the MPO Regional Planning Committee, based off the MPO Technical Advisory Committee recommendation, approve the draft listing of Highways, Transit, and Bike/Ped. This approval allows MPO staff to present the needs based listing of projects to the public knowing that it is a draft and further refinement will be needed. Commissioner Gary Anderson seconded the motion. The motion carried unanimously.
