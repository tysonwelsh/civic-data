4.     Planning Commissioners HB 409 Training on Subdivision Regulations and
       Improvement Guarantees, Impact Fees, Ordinance and Code Drafting in Compliance
       with State Statutes - Staff Presenters, Grant Crowell, Zoning Administrator, and Dan
       Tracer, Assistant City Engineer.

Assistant City Engineer, Dan Tracer, addressed impact fees, which are a way to help the City pay
for services and keep up with growth. He explained why they are charged and how they are used.
He displayed a graph depicting the number of Building Permits that have been issued over the past
20 years. He expects Bluffdale to be completely built out by about 2035 and 2045 .

Utah State Law allows a municipality to charge a fee to any new development that will pay for its
impact on the City ' s existing system. The impact fee can be applied exclusively to new growth.
The fees cannot be used to fix any existing problems. A dete1mination should be made regarding
which one is derived from a massive written Master Plan for all construction and development that
takes place in Bluffdale. The document identifies existing and future needs based on new growth.
An Impact Fees Facilities Plan identifies issues resulting from new growth and its cost.
Determinations need to be made to distinguish between new growth that can be paid from Impact
Fees and current conditions that need to be paid from other funding sources.

The Impact Fee Project Plan looks at a 10-year window of development. Some of the costs
represent new growth and represent existing deficienci es. The existing deficiencies are paid from
taxes and the General Fund. It was noted that road maintenance is not part of the Impact Fee
although new lanes could come from Impact Fees. Inflation and market conditions were included in
the projected cost estimates. Estimates are based on the best assumptions possible, which can be
challenging due to market fluctuations.

Mr. Tracer explained how the Impact Fee will be calculated per residential unit for the storm drain
proj ects that are under consideration. Residential and commercial Impact Fees are calculated
differently. Impact Fees are periodically adjusted as the development picture comes into clearer
focus. Mr. Tracer showed a slide of w hat the Impact Fee would be for a typical single-family home
in Bluffdale:
            • $6,500 for Parks and Recreation ;
            • $2,200 for Public Safety, Police, and Fire;
            • $600 for Storm Drains;
            • $4,200 for Transpo11ation; and
            • $4,500 for Drinking Water.
