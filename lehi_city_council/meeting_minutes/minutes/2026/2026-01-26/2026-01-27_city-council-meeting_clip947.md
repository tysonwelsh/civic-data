2.2) Presentation of Citizen Survey Results- Y2 Analytics
      The presenter explained that 78% of residents would recommend Lehi as a great place to
      live, with an average quality of life rating of 75 out of 100.
       Growth and traffic were identified as top resident concerns, with rush hour traffic
       becoming an increasing problem while road conditions were less concerning. Most city
       services scored above 50, with nine of 22 services scoring above 70. Power services,
       newly added to the survey, scored 79 out of 100.

3) Work Session Items

3.1) Police Department Staffing Discussion- Chief Darren Paul
      Chief Paul explained that the department is updating its staffing study, which uses a
      “saturation index” and shows how many officers are needed by hour of day; he said
      they’re still vetting the new numbers and will bring back hard data, including a
      breakdown by shift and more detail on high-priority/dangerous calls and backup needs.
      He emphasized several key considerations: rapid growth and traffic are driving a rising
      call load, many time-consuming calls are non-criminal service calls, and staffing must be
      high enough that officers aren’t routinely handling dangerous calls alone or waiting long
      for backup. He also stressed that the model’s ideal officer count is more than can be
      funded in a single budget cycle, so he wants to use the updated study to build a phased 4–
      5 year staffing plan, while continuing interim efficiency measures such as aligning shifts
      to peak hours, expanding online reporting, using officers to cover training gaps, and
      leveraging the state SIAC analysis center to speed investigations. Finally, he noted that
      grant-funded positions help with onboarding but are not a sustainable growth strategy,
      since the city must absorb full ongoing costs after the grant period, so any long-term
      staffing increases need stable, recurring city funding.

3.3) 1900 South Right-of-Way Presentation- Engineering
       Brad Kennison presented updates on the Pony Express Parkway extension project. The
       project would extend 1900 South from Center Street in Lehi to approximately 700 West
       in American Fork.
       The project was granted $12.69 million in funding from MAG in 2022, with Lehi's share
       being approximately $430,000 (6.77% of total cost). The design would purchase right-of-
       way for five lanes but initially construct only two lanes with widening at intersections.

       Brad reported that Saratoga Springs had received approval to widen their section to five
       lanes, which would be completed by the end of next year. He estimated an additional
       $15-20 million would be needed to upgrade Lehi's section to five lanes immediately, split
       between Lehi and American Fork.

       Challenges included American Fork's concerns about increased traffic and the question of
       where five lanes of traffic would terminate. UDOT had indicated the vineyard connector
       was now a priority for funding, with construction possible by 2034-2035.

       Several council members expressed concern about traffic funneling into neighborhoods
       and discussed potential solutions including front-loading construction costs with
       reimbursement when future funding became available.

9) Consideration of Concept Plan approval for the Chiu Property, a 219-unit development
     located at approximately 600 South 2900 West.

       Tom Romney from Focus Consulting presented a concept plan for developing the Chiu
       property, a portion of which is currently in an ESA zone.

       The proposal would preserve open space, connecting to existing city open space and
       Jordan Willows development. Romney argued this approach would be less
       environmentally detrimental while providing better transitions to planned regional
       commercial development in adjacent Saratoga Springs. Romney also noted that the intent
       was to improve road connections.

       The council felt that the requested density increase was far too large and was inconsistent
       with the general plan, especially given that much of the site is in the 100-year
       floodplain/ESA. They were concerned about repeating a pattern where Lehi takes the
       housing and costs while Saratoga Springs holds the commercial. There were concerns
       that a large townhome project would not match the established single-family character of
       surrounding neighborhoods. Members also disliked trading higher density for “leftover”
       open space that the city would have to maintain.

       Braden Cooper with Century Communities emphasized landowner rights and the need to
       find viable development options. The applicant noted utility service challenges and
       expressed that the current zoning wasn't economically viable for the property owner.

       Overall, the council saw this specific 219-unit proposal as a poor fit for the area, though
       some indicated limited openness to modest, single-family density that better aligns with
       adjacent lot sizes.


Motion:               Councilor Harrison moved to deny the concept plan, with findings that
                      include the need for the proper infrastructure, consideration of the land to the
                      south in Saratoga and having a plan for that abutting up to the property under
                      question, and it does not meet the General Plan intentions. Councilor
                      Lockhart seconded the motion.

Roll Call Vote:       YES: Councilor Freeman, Councilor Harrison, Councilor Lockhart,
                      Councilor Newall, and Councilor Stallings. The motion to deny passed
                      unanimously.

     Chapter 20, Home Occupations, adding Adult Daycare.



Lehi City Council Meeting                         7                                  January 27, 2026
       Kim Struthers presented a development code amendment to Chapter 20 regarding home
       occupations, specifically adding adult daycare as a permitted use. The city currently
       allowed child daycare as a home occupation but had received recent requests for adult
       daycare services for elderly individuals or those with disabilities who needed daytime
       care but not 24-hour residential care. Kim clarified that group homes are treated
       separately in the code; this item is only about daytime care in someone’s own home.

       The main concern was licensing and safety requirements. Councilor Stallings pointed out
       that the draft language only referenced childcare rules and recommended adding parallel
       requirements for adult day care. Kim confirmed that Lehi would require a city business
       license and agreed state licensing requirements should be explicitly added. Councilor
       Lockhart voiced support, noting adult day programs are a valuable service for
       families caring for elderly or special-needs relatives.

Motion:               Councilor Stallings moved to approve Ordinance #02-2026, approving a
                      development code amendment to chapter 20, home occupations, adding adult
                      daycare, but also include that in order to obtain and maintain the validity of
                      a Lehi City Business License for any adult daycare, the applicant shall obtain
                      and keep a valid, Utah Department of Health adult daycare license and an
                      annual fire safety inspection. Any adult daycare home occupation shall also
                      strictly comply with all other requirements set forth in Utah Administrative
                      Code R501-1; and include the DRC comments. Councilor Freeman seconded
                      the motion.

Roll Call Vote:       YES: Councilor Freeman, Councilor Harrison, Councilor Lockhart,
                      Councilor Newall, and Councilor Stallings. The motion passed unanimously.


11) Consideration of Resolution #2026-05 of the Lehi City Council, urging preservation of
     local land use authority and opposing new statewide high-density or "by-right"
     housing mandates.

       Councilor Harrison presented a resolution urging preservation of local land use authority
       and opposing new statewide high-density or "by right" housing mandates. He supported
       keeping land use decisions closest to the people and maintaining each city's unique
       identity and development approach. The resolution was edited to remove specific bill
       references based on feedback from the Utah League of Cities and Towns (ULCT). The
       principle focused on cities maintaining zoning and land use authority rather than having
       state-mandated housing requirements.
       Councilor Newall appreciated the collaborative approach and tone of the resolution,
       emphasizing the importance of local control while maintaining good relationships with
       state officials.

       Councilor Stallings acknowledged that infrastructure costs get shifted to local taxpayers.
       She supported the resolution as a tool for future discussions with state legislators.
