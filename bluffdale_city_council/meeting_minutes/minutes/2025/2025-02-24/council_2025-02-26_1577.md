 1   Mr. Springer stated that Craig Smith of Smith Hartvigsen has been helping Bluffdale for a long time,
 2   and they would be discussing the end of life of some of the projects he worked with them on. The
 3   rules have changed significantly in the last 20 years.
 4
 5   An RDA creates project areas and provides incentives, and there are ongoing reporting requirements
 6   for project areas. The preferred term is now Community Reinvestment Agency, but in more
 7   developed areas they are still called redevelopment agencies or redevelopment communities. The
 8   City can do a lot of things, and the RDA can do even more as it both supplements and is separate from
 9   the City. Even though the City Council is the RDA board, it is a separate body with different meetings
10   and obligations. It does not have tax authority and does not need to have staff. The agency engages
11   in agreements with other parties, including developers and other taxing entities.
12
13   Mr. Springer noted that there has been a big push in recent years for the but-for argument for
14   developments, which gets carried over into all conversations. Mr. Crowell added that the but-for
15   analysis for an RDA is, “But for this project/agency/entity, nothing will be built,” but the County’s
16   position is that they also have to report to taxpayers.
17
18   Mr. Springer gave an overview of the ongoing responsibilities, which include annual budgeting.
19   Reporting information is publicly available via a new online portal. The biggest burden on Staff is
20   monitoring agreements and incentives in addition to internal controls, budget reporting, audits, and
21   documentation.
22
23   The project area initially includes preparing a project plan and budget. There are then a lot of different
24   steps including revisions, amendments, and new participation agreements for the same project with
25   different incentives or details. Mr. Crowell indicated that Bluffdale currently has three project areas.
26   The Bluffdale Gateway RDA is an old retail project area on Redwood Road, where Smith’s is located,
27   that took a long time to come to fruition. The Eastern Bluffdale RDA runs along 14600 South and
28   has developed a lot of resources. The Jordan Narrows RDA has hit its cap.
29
30   Mr. Springer gave an overview of resources for Tax Increment Financing (“TIF”). When a project
31   area is created, they capture the difference between future property tax revenue and the actual tax

 1   value and use that to incentivize development. He presented an example showing growth over the
 2   base amount since a project area started. Assistant City Manager, Bruce Kartchner, stated that
 3   ultimately the RDA’s portion comes back in tax revenue. City Manager, Mark Reid, added that since
 4   they are using everyone else’s TIF to finance, the City gets approximately 1% of that money. They
 5   use the School District or County TIF to do projects. When it is turned back over to the taxing entities,
 6   it is fully developed, whereas without it the area would have stayed undeveloped. Mr. Springer added
 7   that at the end of a project, the increase in value does not go away. Increments are taken to incentivize
 8   new development for 20 years. After that, the increased value is the new baseline for all taxing
 9   entities.
10
11   Mr. Kartchner stated that currently, the different RDA project areas have different management fees.
12   One is 5%, one 8%, and one 10%. At the time they close out, revenues should increase overall. They
13   have been looking at timing that with the bond payment for the Public Works Building so the cash
14   flow is available. Mr. Reid stated that the three project areas have a 25-year window, and they have
15   15 years within that window to take increment. He then asked Mr. Springer to discuss the effect of
16   COVID on the timeline.
17
18   Mr. Springer stated that the State Legislature allowed RDAs to determine if a project area was affected
19   by COVID, and if so, they could unilaterally extend the time to collect taxes on it. Bluffdale took
20   advantage of that, and some entities like the County did not like it. TIF is used as an incentive, and
21   the only people who dislike working with RDA are the taxing entities. Mr. Crowell reported that the
22   primary taxing entities are the City, the County, and the School District, as well as the Mosquito
23   Abatement District and the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District, and all parties agree to split
24   some of the money coming in.
25
26   There are opportunities to be creative. The RDA can support the project area through direct
27   contributions, reimbursements, waivers, grants, loans, rebates, etc. They have done a lot with parks,
28   open space, and roads. Sometimes the developer wants to spend all their money on their buildings
29   and asks the RDA to chip in to build the roads. They could also have pioneering agreements where

 2   must go to affordable housing projects. The $20 million limit has been met on the Jordan Narrows
 3   project, so they must designate where the 20% will go as they complete that project.

 5   Referencing Mr. Springer’s comment about flexibility, Mr. Crowell stated that they can see if they
 6   can make any idea fit. They discussed 14600 South. When the current project is completed and
 7   UDOT’s project starts at 800 West, there may still be a gap that the Eastern Bluffdale EDA can step
 8   in to complete. They could also acquire property near the mink farm. Mr. Reid stated that the mink
 9   farm used to be a primary discussion point because people would complain about insects when it was
10   operational, so the RDA Board committed $3.00 per square foot to anyone who would purchase and
11   convert it to another use. The property is currently being used for storage.
12
13   Mr. Springer stated that when Mr. Crowell brings an idea to him to evaluate, he determines if it will
14   fit into an approved category. Mr. Kartchner stated that they are normally talking about projects
15   within the specific project area, but some projects may extend past the immediate geographic area.
16   For example, if they had to run a 1,000-foot pipe to provide water to the property, that could be an
17   appropriate use. Mr. Springer stated that that is a common occurrence. They could also use funds to
18   improve a park across the street from moderate-income housing because it would benefit the
19   moderate-incoming housing.
20
21   Mr. Springer reported that retail is permitted as an incidental use only, not the primary objective.
22   Bluffdale agreed to that at the outset with the Eastern Bluffdale project area and defined it so it could
23   be managed properly going forward. He noted that retail is not a favored use for these types of
24   developments, but mixed-use is.
25
26   In response to a question from Council Member Lord, it was clarified that the RDA ultimately
27   approves projects. Staff discusses the idea and develops the concept, then presents it to Mr. Springer
28   to determine if it fits the legal requirements and if any adjustments need to be made. Mr. Crowell
29   stated that the County monitors how RDA funds are used. Other taxing entities want to ensure that
30   they are being good stewards and following their plans. Mr. Reid stated that there can be some
31   creativity. When Stotz Equipment came in, they needed three acres, but the entity they were buying
