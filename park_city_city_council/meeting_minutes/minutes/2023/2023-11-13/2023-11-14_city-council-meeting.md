1. Continuation of the Policy Discussions Regarding the Potential to Create a
Regional Housing Authority:
Council Member Toly, Housing Authority Committee Chair, and Council Member Harte,
Vice Chair, thanked the committee and stated they had 18 meetings in the last few
months. Council Member Toly displayed questions for the Councils to consider and
Council Member Harte indicated the Councils should give direction today to move
forward in forming a regional housing authority or not.

Jason Glidden, Park City Housing Manager, reviewed the timeline in considering this
proposal. He displayed an evaluation matrix based on feedback from other communities
and housing authorities. He noted some benefits of a housing authority included greater
efficiency in the development of affordable housing, a centralized clearing house for
housing-related issues, neutralized political pressures and competing interests that
could be found in local government, access to funding sources that might not be
available to other governmental agencies and non-profit organizations, and the state of
Utah mandates increasing regional collaboration among government agencies.

Glidden covered the current annual costs for housing between the City and County was
estimated at $1.2 million. The potential housing authority annual budget was estimated
at $1.5 million. He stated a housing authority would centralize regional housing
services. Efficiency would be achieved in the development of affordable housing. He
indicated all entities would agree to contribute equitable resources to fund the housing
authority projects as determined by scope and form of governance and commit to
support the authority until it became financially self-sustainable. Each entity would need
to transfer full-time employees, land, cash, in-kind services, and space for the authority
to succeed. He hoped the Councils would discuss interlocal agreements and resolutions
to get the housing authority running. Council Member Hanson asked how long the
commitment would be, to which Glidden stated it would take more than five years.

Council Member Rubell noted the matrix scoring was close. The financial obligation was
$1.5 million to get it up and running. Each entity agreed to $500,000 and so there would
be a $500,000 funding gap. Council Member Harte stated the entities were currently
spending $1.2 million so the additional funding needed was $300,000.

Council Member Stevens noted there would be extra costs for legal, human resources
and IT services. Council Member Toly stated getting the housing authority running was a
five-year process, so things would be moved every year and not abruptly. The benefit
would be that there weren’t three different teams working on affordable housing.

Council Member Rubell indicated he supported this if there was not a net increase in
spending. He suggested utilizing a taskforce plus to achieve access to funding. The
Councils thought additional funding was available for a housing authority, but if not,
maybe a housing authority was not a good idea. He thought a regional taskforce was a
good idea until a housing authority became feasible. He wasn’t against a housing
authority but didn’t think this was the right time. Glidden stated a taskforce was

option. Chair Armstrong used High Valley Transit as an example and stated three

Council Member Dickey agreed with Council Member Rubell and felt a housing authority
would be expensive. He favored putting resources in a shared service such as a
taskforce and stated that would produce cost savings. Housing was complex, but it
could be a shared service because it had repeatable functions as well as stand alone
functions. He expressed concern for all the housing projects in the pipeline in Park City
and stated turning them over to a housing authority was a risk. Over time he favored
merging housing into one place. Council Member Toly hoped that if a housing authority
was created, the staff would move with it so the momentum of projects would continue.

Council Member Rubell was excused at 9:40 a.m.

Council Member Doilney stated sometimes they had to take a step back in order to
move forward. He asserted the City and County should be working together on
affordable housing. The more government was limited, the quicker projects could move
forward and he favored moving forward with a housing authority. The City had a goal for
the last eight years to build affordable housing, and things were moving too slowly.
Council Member Dickey stated he supported moving forward, but he wanted to ensure it
was a shared service.

Council Member Gerber stated it would take a while to get the housing authority running
and the natural first step would be a taskforce. She favored starting with a taskforce and
beginning the long-range collaboration, then transitioning to a housing authority. If the
group decided to move forward with the housing authority, both entities needed to be all
in. She noted it would take a while to build a solid financial structure.

Council Member Stevens agreed with Council Members Doilney and Dickey and
thought having an affordable housing clearing house was a great benefit to the
community. Council Member Hanson was concerned about the budget, but she thought
other municipalities could get involved and contribute to the housing authority as well.
She liked the efficiencies and collaboration. She favored moving forward with the
housing authority and thought starting with a taskforce would get it up and running more
quickly.

Council Member Robinson stated the problem to be solved was to build affordable
housing. He wanted to hear more about the City’s projects such as Clark Ranch. He
thought the top priority should be housing development. He indicated the County hadn’t
built any projects and he wanted a structure that would result in construction. He would
like development on the Gilmor property, but he didn’t see the housing authority doing

that. He liked the idea of having joint ventures, but not a housing authority over that

Chair Armstrong agreed the County had not considered areas for affordable housing,
but their goal was to make affordable housing a priority in the future. He thought it would
be difficult to give that decision-making authority to an independent entity. He would not
object to a housing authority and a taskforce was needed no matter what the decision

Mayor Worel asserted there wasn’t one place people could go to find out about
affordable housing. She favored a housing authority and wanted to move forward and
act quickly. Putting the logistics in place would be a heavy lift, but it would be exciting to
see the results. She thought it was time to bring in additional resources. Chair
Armstrong indicated the governor was stressing affordable housing and he hoped there
would be additional funding allocated for that.

Council Member Harte stated the City was spending the majority of the $1.2 million cost
and if the County ramped up its efforts, the cost would go up to $2 million or more. He
also noted Mountainlands Community Housing Trust would be a resource to a new
housing authority. The risks were real. Time would be needed to make organizations
succeed. He favored moving forward with a housing authority and noted the direction
today was not a final vote, but direction for staff to continue working out the details.
Council Member Toly noted the Councils recommended reconvening in January to
discuss next steps to get to a housing authority and the costs needed. It would be
important to understand real costs and benefits and look at a five to10-year plan. At that
point, a taskforce could bring on more Council members and stakeholders in the
community.

Chair Armstrong stated there would still be a need for nonprofits, interlocal agreements
and collaboration with other entities. He proposed that the taskforce would plan the
housing authority. He wanted the Councils to look at their budgets to understand their
financial resources. He thought it would be a giant financial obligation on the front side
of implementing the housing authority. He wanted to figure out a revenue stream to
support this.

Council Member Stevens stated the County had deed restricted properties but the
restrictions would expire. She thought the utility of a housing authority would be having
a team tracking the data and keeping the affordable stock out of the nightly rental
market. Council Member Robinson noted the last column of the matrix was state
compliance and asked for clarification. Jeff Jones, Summit County Economic
Development and Housing Director, indicated having a contract with Mountainlands and
funding a housing authority were both allowed. Council Member Dickey stated he
wanted to work together to get housing going in the County and he thought a housing
authority would jumpstart that.

Council Member Robinson asked what it would take to issue an RFP for a public/private
partnership on County land. Council Member Gerber noted it took years of planning
before the City could jumpstart more affordable housing. The land management code
needed to be revised and she didn’t know if the County had been doing that planning as
well. She advised that the County get started with that process in order to make future
housing projects more feasible. Council Member Stevens stated if both entities were
focused on projects, they could be moved into the housing authority. Council Member
Harte added a housing authority would not prohibit cities from doing their own projects
as well. Council Member Doilney stated the taskforce would help figure out everyone’s
role in housing. He was glad the Councils agreed to move forward and the next step
would be to discuss the “how.” Council Member Harte indicated the committee would
work on the cost and bring it back in January. Chair Armstrong asked that part of the
conversation focus on what percentage of housing should be affordable. Council
Member Gerber asked to discuss models that got a return on investment and felt that
information would be helpful.

Council Member Harte summarized the majority of the group wanted to move forward
with a housing authority with an intermediary step of using a taskforce group. Council
Member Robinson requested that the next meeting should be focused on the housing
authority and the taskforce.

SCHEDULE NEXT JOINT MEETING

It was indicated the next joint Council meeting would be held Friday, January 12th at the
County building.

ADJOURNMENT

With no further business, the meeting was adjourned.
