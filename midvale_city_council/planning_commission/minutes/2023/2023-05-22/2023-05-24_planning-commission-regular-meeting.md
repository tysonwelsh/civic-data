GENERAL SESSION

Chair Liedtke called the Planning & Zoning Commission meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. He
explained how the meeting would proceed. First, the Planning Department would brief the
Commission; then the applicant would speak to the Commission; the item will be opened to the
public for their comments on public hearing agenda items; the Midvale Deputy City Attorney
will then brief the Commission on their role on the given item; and then the Commission would
deliberate and decide.

PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION MAY 24, 2023.
         First, the differences between a permitted use, conditional use, and non-permitted use.
         Depending on the scenario, there are types of permits that will and can only be
         approved based on special requirements.
         Within the Midvale City Code, there is a Use-Table for each zone listed for clarity.
         The table only lists the uses for allowed uses, for the conditions, see the code.
         Detailed conditions and qualifications are outlined for very specific businesses,
         animal regulations, building rules, and more.
         Legislative versus administrative decisions regarding CUPs.
         Generally, staff will bring legislative changes to meetings whereas administrative
         decisions are generally brought by residents and developers.
         Legislative decisions are Planning Commission makes recommendations to City
         Council. Administrative decisions are more of a checklist that must be met to be
         approved. More discretion is needed regarding legislative decisions, whereas little to
         no discretion is required for administrative applications.
         Subjective versus objective standards are important to consider when approving or
         denying applications. Subjective standards cannot be upheld in the law and therefore
         are not to be considered when deciding on the matter; however, they can help to guide
         development in a certain direction.
         “For the record…”
         The staff report serves as an important part of the official record. The votes and all
         audios are recorded to be able to refer in case of someone challenging a decision. All
         public comments, discussions, written comments, and minutes all become a part of
         the record. Any comments discussed or presented outside of the meeting are supposed
         to be disregarded. Per the bylaws, all ex-parte communication should cause a
         commissioner to recuse themselves on the matter to avoid any bias.
         CUP Guidelines
         Permits can expire and or be revoked based on code compliance. Permits are
         transferrable so long as the new owners apply and maintain the pre-existing use that
         was approved at that location. So long as the use is continuous and no lapse over a
         year occurs, the approved conditional use stands.
