     I. Location and topography of the property including history of existing structure

    I.      Location and topography of the property including history of existing structure

The location and topography of my property in Pinecrest Canyon poses significant challenges. The
property is located within the Salt Lake City watershed and is adjacent to the stream. The topography of
the property exhibits slopes with grades greater than 40 degrees which are within the 50 foot to 75 foot
setback area from the stream corridor. These slopes exist along the entire length of the parcel. The
property is also within the boundaries defined as the wildland-urban interface area.

The existing structure on the property was originally built around 1913 and was rebuilt in the early
1940’s after a fire. The existing structure is almost entirely within the 50 foot and 25 foot stream
setback area and has an aging septic system which is within 75 feet of the stream. In addition, the
existing structure does not have a foundation (it sits on concrete pillars) and is made almost entirely of
wood. My family has owned the property since 2007.

    II.     New proposed structure

The decision to work towards an approval of rebuilding a noncomplying structure was made considering
the age and condition of the existing structure, the limitations in code Section 19.88.030 - Maintenance
Permitted and Section 19.88.040 - Repairs and Alterations Permitted relating to additions, modification
and improvements to existing structures, balanced with safety considerations and the needs of my
family. All these factors have been discussed at numerous formal and informal meetings (including
several site visits) with multiple agencies including Salt Lake County Planning and Development Services,
Metro Township Municipal Service District (MSD), Emigration Canyon Metro Township, Emigration
Township Planning Commission, Emigration Improvement District, Salt Lake County Health Department -
Environmental Health division, Salt Lake County Board of Health and Salt Lake City Public Utilities. The
outcome of the earlier meetings was the decision to move forward with rebuilding a noncomplying
structure. A summary and timeline of the efforts related to this project over the last 4 years are
summarized in Appendix B (separate Excel file).
The new proposed structure as shown on the site plan provides the greatest separation between the
structure and the stream corridor. As illustrated on the site plan, there is no physical space to build any
structure entirely beyond the 50-foot setback line from the stream. In addition, Section 19.72.060 D4 -
Slope Protection doesn’t allow any construction on slopes greater than 40 degrees (See Appendix A for
copy of code section). Slope averaging was key in positioning the new proposed structure as far from
the stream as possible. The stream setback waiver approves a new structure which is 57% beyond the
50-foot setback line which is the best compromise between the proximity of the stream and the steep
slopes. All living space within the 25-foot setback area has been eliminated.

The living space of the new structure is approximately 2000 sq. ft. and is modest in size compared to
other new residences approved in Emigration Canyon. The size of the new structure is also in line with
the size of other existing structures in the canyon, some of which are much larger in size and some of
which are smaller in size.

The living space of the new structure is designed as a year-around residence for three adults, one of
which requires ADA adaptations in the home including a separate ADA designed bathroom and wider
hallways and doors to accommodate wheelchair passage. In addition, the stairs leading from the living
space to the indoor garage space are designed to include a lift to allow this adult to safely move from
the living space to the two-car garage (ground) level. The garage level will also house all utilities
(including water pipes, gas heating system, water heater, Tesla power walls and pumps related to the
new wastewater disposal system).

The building materials to be used in construction of the new structure decrease the fire hazard severity
associated with a home in the wildland-urban interface area. Construction materials include metal
siding over an insulated concrete form structure, metal garage doors and entry doors, steel exterior
stairs with open steel treads and a class A single ply membrane roof offering the highest resistance to
fire. The roof also contains a drain system which directs stormwater to a dry well.

The approved wastewater system is an alternative septic system which uses a packed bed media filter to
treat effluent and an absorption system with deep wall trenches that releases the safe, treated effluent
back into the ground. Please refer to section IV for more information related to the wastewater system.

    III.    Approvals in the form of Variances and Waivers received to date

Using a critical path assessment, approvals for the stream setback and the new alternative septic system
were received first. Below is a summary of those approvals.

    1) Stream setback waiver – After a multiagency analysis was performed on the proposed site plan,
       a stream setback waiver was approved by both Salt Lake City Public Utilities and the Salt Lake
       County Health Department on August 3, 2020 with a clarifying letter supporting the approval
       dated August 21, 2020. Note that in December of 2020 (after the August approval), MSD
       requested an amendment to the approved site plan in relation to the location of the exterior
       stairs. As of Monday, March 8, 2021, MSD and Salt Lake City Public Utilities (Watershed
       personnel) were still in communication regarding the updated site plans showing the relocation
       of the stairs and no definitive response has been provided yet by Salt Lake City Public Utilities.
    2) Wastewater Disposal variance – A Wastewater Disposal variance to install an alternative septic
       system was approved by the Salt Lake County Health Department - Environmental Health
       Division and the Salt Lake County Board of Health on September 3, 2020.


    IV.     Cooperative agreement with Emigration Improvement District

Emigration Improvement District (EID) is particularly interested in the wastewater disposal system
solution I am proposing. Historical EID meeting minutes show that addressing the wastewater issue in
the canyon has been discussed as far back as 2015. Meetings have been held between EID, Salt Lake
County Health Department and the State of Utah relating to septic system issues, however, no
agreement on a solution to the septic issues has been reached. Lack of a solution is in part due to
hydrology constraints in the canyon that need to be addressed when considering wastewater system
improvement alternatives.

Since an alternative septic system returns treated effluent to the soil, EID is supportive of my proposed
system. EID has provided me with a signed letter indicating their support of the proposed alternative
septic system. In addition, their letter states that EID will monitor the operation of my system on a
monthly basis for one year to gather data related to the system. That information could then be used to
consider and encourage use of similar wastewater systems by other homeowners with aging septic
systems throughout the canyon. Given the number of aging septic systems, especially along the stream
corridor throughout the canyon, this cooperative effort is an important step towards both a cleaner
environment and cleaner water in the canyon.


    V.      Emigration Canyon Metro Township Municipal code

MSD provided me with a list of Municipal code sections that my proposed site plan violates noting that
these violations can only be addressed with approvals from the Land Use Hearing Officer as spelled out
in Section 19.88.070 Additions, Enlargements, Moving and Reconstruction of a Structure and 19.02.020
Purposes of Provisions (see Appendix A at the end of this document for a copy of Section 19.88.070 and
the conclusion section for a copy of 19.20.020).


    VI.     Conclusion

I believe my presentation today has provided sufficient background of the property, the existing structure
and the challenges I have faced and overcome in the last 4 years to move closer to the reality of rebuilding
a home in Pinecrest.

The municipal code requires the Land Use Officer to consider whether a proposal to reconstruct a
structure is in harmony with one or more of the purposes stated in Section 19.02.020 Purposes of
Provisions which is shown below.

19.02.020 Purpose of Provisions
This title is designed and enacted for the purpose of promoting the health, safety, morals, conveniences,
order, prosperity and welfare of the present and future inhabitants of Emigration Canyon Metro Township,
including, among other things, the lessening of congestion in the streets or roads, securing safety from fire
and other dangers, providing adequate light and air, classification of land uses and distribution of land
development and utilization, protection of the tax base, securing economy in governmental expenditures,

19.88.070 Additions, Enlargements, Moving and Reconstruction of a Structure

    1. A noncomplying structure or building occupied by a nonconforming use shall not be added to or
       enlarged in any manner or moved to another location on the lot or reconstructed at another
       location on the lot except as provided by subsection B of this section unless such additions and
       enlargements comply with the regulations and intent of this title.
    2. A building occupied by a nonconforming use or a noncomplying structure may be added to or
       enlarged or moved to a new location on the lot or reconstructed at a new location on the lot upon
       a permit authorized by the land use hearing officer, provided that the land use hearing officer
       shall find:
           1. The addition to, enlargement of, moving of, or reconstruction of the structure at a new
                location on the lot is in harmony with one or more of the purposes of this title as stated
                in Section 19.02.020 of this title, and is in keeping with the intent of this title;
           2. That the proposed change does not impose any unreasonable burden upon the lands
                located in the vicinity of the nonconforming use or structure.
