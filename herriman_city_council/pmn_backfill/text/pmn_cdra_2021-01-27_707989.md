    2. Discussion and Action Items
            2.1. Discussion and consideration of a resolution approving a Participation Agreement with
                 Herriman 73 Partners, LLC for the designation of the Automall as a project area and the
                 sales tax incentive for the project– Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi offered a background of the proposed participation agreement. The Participation
Agreement would be triggered whenever the Community Development and Renewal Agency of Herriman City
(CDRA) decided to request the funds from Herriman City. This generally would occur when there would be some
level of sales tax being generated in the area. In other project areas, the CDRA has worked with the developer and
participant to organize this date.

Attorney Andrizzi explained an incentive cap of $18 million dollars was proposed so the participant would directly
receive 40-percent ($7.2 million) of the amount for development related activities as spelled out in the project area
budget and as permitted by the Utah State Code. The participant would have the ability to commit the remaining
portion ($10.8 million). This commitment would be formalized by the CDRA in the form of another Participation
Agreement between only the CDRA and the end-user. The CDRA would pay the portion of the committed
incentive directly to the end-user and not the participant. While this amount exceeded the amount suggested by
Chair Watts, Attorney Andrizzi believed it would be more congruent with other similar incentives and would be in
line with what would be necessary to stimulate the development and to entice dealers to enter. Attorney Andrizzi
used Draper City as an example outlining their City pledged $3.1 million to one dealership, waived impact fees while
also contributing $200,000 of City funds to a road. In total, the incentive to the one dealer was closer to $3.5
million. He continued with a proposed 5-8 dealers in the Herriman Automall, an incentive cap of $18 million would
be less per dealership than what was provided in Draper. However, most of the development costs for the
Herriman Automall would be borne by the participant and, as a result, the incentive to end-users would be
justifiably less than what had been provided in Draper.

Attorney Andrizzi explained the participant would be required to submit invoices and receipts to the CDRA prior
to receiving their portion of the Incentive. This added measure ensured transparency and provided greater control
over the incentive. This funding could be used for any legitimate purpose proposed in the project area budget as
well as anything anticipated in State Code. The incentive may not be used for the acquisition of land. He iterated the
importance to point out that a permitted use is to “pay-down” already purchased land and noted this was common
practice in CRA projects and had been included in this participation agreement. The incentive would be distributed
in percentages equal to what has been approved. He continued with when the incentive cap had been reached, the
CDRA would have no additional obligation to provide additional incentive which could ultimately allow the
agreement to terminate early which could be mutually beneficial.

Director Henderson relayed his opposition of pledging sales tax money to incentivize a developer to try and entice a
dealership to come into the City. He would be in favor of moving forward as presented if the developer and the
end-user were the same entity. Director Henderson indicated the sales tax funds should be pledged to the dealership
to bring them into the area, and suggested if money needed to be available for infrastructure costs, the project
should be treated like a typical CDRA area and get all affected entities involved to help offset those costs to the
developer. Attorney Andrizzi understood this perception, and explained he only had looked at sales tax incentives
for the project area due to the information provided in the approved project area plan and budget.

Director Shields relayed he had the same concerns as previously depicted. He acknowledged there was an argument
to be made, this infrastructure was necessary to enable a dealership to move forward within this project area. He
observed the proposed six-percent infrastructure reimbursement; however, the agreement conceptually offers up to
40-percent incentive to the developer to be reimbursed for infrastructure, pay down land purchases or event pledge
their own incentive to a dealership. Director Henderson interjected his opposition for the developer to have the
ability to pay down the price of the land. Attorney Andrizzi explained certain property within the project area had a
substantially higher purchase price per square foot than other parcels, and added this project would only be
successful if the entire area had been developed as an automall. The buy down of property could allow the
developer to sell the property at a reduced rate to the end user. Director Henderson responded this was the reason
the behind the City’s willingness to be a partner in the original purchase of the automall property, and could not
support the incentive to be used in this manner.

Director Henderson relayed there was mechanisms which could be utilized to help offset to the cost of
infrastructure. He recommended these tools be pursued to allow the sales tax incentive to be completely available
for the dealerships. Director Henderson stated he did not want to repeat past mistakes for CDRA project areas.
Director Smith indicated this agreement would be similar to the CDRA agreement approved for the Anthem
Commercial area which contemplates both property and sales tax. Finance Director Alan Rae outlined the
incentives offered in the Anthem Commercial agreements. Director Smith clarified the sales tax funding was used to
help offset to the cost of the land in the Anthem Commercial agreement, which was essentially the same request
being presented here. Director Henderson disagreed. Director Shields reiterated the conceptual recommendation
offered by Director Henderson that there was a different mechanism to pay for the installation of infrastructure,
which should be considered. Director Henderson did not want to hinder the ability for the City to incentivise a
dealership by pledging the sales tax funding to infrastructure.

Director Smith wanted to understand the change of direction from the Board from the previous meeting which had
been held two weeks prior He acknowledged Director Henderson’s absence, and should have the opportunity to
interject his desires. Director Smith relayed his thought of the previous meeting had been productive and had
offered direction in a collaborative effort. Director Shields responded he reserved the right to change his mind
based on a better understanding of the facts, and felt he received clarification the two CDRA project areas were
very different. He believed there was a better mechanism for infrastructure which would not remove the ability to
offer business incentives. The infrastructure funding could be found with participating entities who would be willing
to consider donating their share of property tax for the project area. Councilmember Smith did not believe the City
could get support from affected entities to pledge their portion of property tax to incentivise the development of an
automall. Directors Henderson, Ohrn, and Shields were not in favor of moving forward with the agreement
allowing incentives to be utilized for infrastructure.

Director Smith indicated this project is trying to get to a point where it would be attractive for a business to come
into the project area. Director Henderson disagreed and observed the lack of interest from end users. Director
Shields indicated this discussion was hypothetical on how to divvy up anticipated sales tax revenue, and indicated he
was not 100-percent opposed for sales tax money to be available for infrastructure. He was adamant he could not
favor 40-percent of the funding to be allocated for this purpose; however, did not have a percentage he could show

support. Director Henderson said his comfort level of pledging sales tax funding to infrastructure without an end
user being committed was none. He reiterated if the partner wanted to utilize the mechanisms available through
CDRA project areas to offset infrastructure costs, he would be willing to discuss those options. He indicated it did
not make sense to offer sales tax to the developer as incentives should be offered to the actual dealerships. Director
Henderson also relayed his concern of the current parcels within the automall project area being utilized for
commercial development rather than being preserved for dealerships and observed some of the users in the area
were not sales tax producing vendors.

Director Henderson moved to continue the participation agreement with Herriman 73 Partners, LLC for the designation of the
Automall as a project area and the sales tax incentive for the project. Director Ohrn seconded the motion, and all voted aye.
