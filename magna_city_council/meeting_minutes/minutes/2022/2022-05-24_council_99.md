Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD) – Code of Ordinances

                Brian Tucker, Interim Planning Supervisor, MSD, updated the Council on the
MSD’s code revision, which is being done to streamline and ensure ordinances are in compliance
with state legislation and that they conform to goals and policies of general plans. The MSD will
be creating a model code, and it will use the pieces of the model code that apply to the
municipalities. So, Magna Metro Township’s code will not be what fits for the Kearns Metro
Township’s code, nor will it be the old Salt Lake County code. The goal is to have this complete
by the end of the year, and in front of the Planning Commissions in December.

There are a couple of phases – the MSD is currently working on a definitions chapter on the
districts, i.e., residential, commercial, agricultural, manufacturing, and forestry. The plan is to
have that to the planning commissions and the councils no later than July 21, 2022, so the metro
townships can make those their own before December. It will provide the policy and procedures
chapters, and the parking, landscaping, and sign regulations by September 16, 2022. Then, it
will deliver Title 18, the subdivision ordinances and all the miscellaneous chapters in the zoning
ordinance, by October 26, 2022.

Magna Water District

                 Clint Dilley, General Manager, Magna Water District, stated the Magna Water
District is moving forward with the replacement of waterlines on Edith Drive and Florence Drive,
between Katherine Drive and Helen Drive. It is also investing in infrastructure in that same area
to improve fire flow and service, and to cut down on the number of repairs it has been making.
That will start the first part of June. Newman Construction was awarded the project. Information
on waterline projects will be attached to electronic bills and sent out with mailed bills. People can
also sign up on Magna Water District’s website to receive emails and news alerts, and texts in
the case of an emergency.

              Dallas Henline, Wastewater Operations Manager, Magna Water District,
announced the Reuse Open House on June 22, 2022, at the Magna Water District’s main office,
where Magna Water District will unveil the preliminary designs on the reuse project – turning
wastewater discharge into secondary irrigation, and go over questions and answers. It can also
answer questions about concerns. Mr. Dilley passed out a flyer on that here. The flyer will also
go out in a mailer. He also showed pictures of two 42-foot long by 14-foot wide trucks used by
the Magna Water District, and explained what they were used for and how difficult it was to
navigate them on crowded streets.

               Mayor Peay asked when the reuse project would go into effect.

             Mr. Henline stated Magna Water District hopes it will be operational by the
summer of 2024, and fully operational January 1, 2025.

               Council Member Prokopis asked if that would be districtwide.
                                                 7
DATE            TUESDAY                                   MAY                              24, 2022


               Mr. Henline stated no.

              Mayor Peay asked who was responsible for repairing a road when a homeowner
had to have their sewer line replaced from the house to the road.

                Mr. Dilley stated if the road was cut laterally by the contractor working for the
homeowner, it would be their responsibility. Magna Water District would be involved in the portion
of the road at the main waterline connection.

              Council Member Barney asked who was over quality control when a road had to
be repaired because it was torn up by a water district, and if there were standards and
requirements.

                Mr. Dilley stated different authorities enforce the roads. If it is a Utah Department
of Transportation (UDOT) road, the Magna Water District’s contractor who did the work has to
follow their specifications and requirements. Then, Magna Water District will hire an inspector or
provide an in-house inspector to make sure that is done. UDOT and other entities have their own
inspector too who watches that. However, there are a lot of areas in the Magna Metro Township
where the subgrade is so bad, that even after the repair is done to specifications, settling starts
to occur.

             Mark Elieson asked what Magna Water District’s watering restrictions were for the
Magna Metro Township.

                Mr. Dilley stated the Magna Metro Township is in better shape than it was last
year from a drought status, but it is projected to be a hot summer and a lot of the reservoirs have
not built back up. So, the Magna Water District will continue with the mandatory level it restricted
the users to last year. The goal is to raise awareness, rather than being punitive. The community
has been responding to that; overall, it conserved 15-20 percent of water.

               Council Member Hull asked if the Magna Water District had a program for
replacing a parking strip with xeriscaping and if it had grant money to do that with.

              Mr. Dilley stated Magna Water District does not have that program. It gets a small
amount of wholesale water from the Jordan Valley Conservancy District through the Utah Water
Savers, which partners with the Utah Division of Water Resources and other big districts. The
Magna Metro Township is eligible to use that water.
