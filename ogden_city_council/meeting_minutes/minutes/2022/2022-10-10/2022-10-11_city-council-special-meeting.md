Caldwell declaring Ogden City’s support for Utah’s bid to host the Winter
Olympics

A memo from Council staff came before the Council to consider a joint resolution supporting Utah’s bid to host the
Winter Olympics. The memo stated Salt Lake County successfully hosted the 2002 Olympic Winter Games and Paralympic
Winter Games and the communities of Salt Lake City and Ogden were critical to the success. City Council and Mayor desire to

Proposed Resolution 2022-20 authorizing disposition of unclaimed firearms
in the possession of the Ogden City Police Department to public use

A memo from the Police Department came before the Council to consider a resolution authorizing the use of custodial
property. The memo stated the Ogden Police Department is requesting authorization to sell surrendered/seized firearms currently
in the custody of the Department to 1) reduce inventory and create storage space for incoming items; and 2) dispose of
surrendered/seized firearms in accordance with Utah State Code 24-3-103.5. The request requires the Council to authorize the
appropriation of the property - the firearms - for public use. The firearms will be sold by Hy & Mike’s Pawn and the proceeds
donated to the Weber-Morgan Narcotic Strike Force.

Chief Young summarized the memo and stated the surrendered firearms have not been reported stolen, nor has anyone
attempted to claim them. The seized firearms are from adjudicated cases and cannot legally be returned to the owner. The
Department has contacted prosecutors with the Ogden Justice Court and the 2™ District Court and has authorization to move
forward with the disposition of the property.

Chair Nadolski inquired as to the number of firearms that will be sold versus destroyed. Chief Young stated that more
firearms will be sold than destroyed, but he is unsure of the actual ratio. The types of guns that will be destroyed are those that are
not safe for ownership and use. He would be happy to provide a follow-up report on the firearms that are sold versus destroyed
once the action is carried out.

Chair Nadolski introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2022-20, entitled:

“A resolution of the Ogden City Council authorizing disposition of unclaimed firearms in the possession of the
Ogden Police Department to public use.”

COUNCIL MEMBER RICHEY MOVED THE RESOLUTION BE ADOPTED, AND THE CHAIR BE

Proposed Ordinance 2022-45 amending the budget for the fiscal year July
1, 2022 to June 30, 2023 by increasing the anticipated revenues and
appropriations for a gross increase of $194,900 transferring CIP funds to
the Redevelopment Agency to purchase and improve the Union Stockyard
Exchange Building and recognize new grant awards

A memo from Comptroller Stout regarding a proposed amendment in the Fiscal Year (FY) 2023 budget came before the
Council for consideration. The memo stated the budget amendment would increase the FY2023 City budget by $194,900; it
involves transferring funds from the Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) budget to the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) for purchase
of the Exchange Building and for developer incentives ($845,000). The proposed budget amendment also recognizes revenues
from United Way for police vehicles to support homeless outreach efforts ($88,200), recognizes grant funds from the Utah
Commission on Criminal and Juvenile Justice (UCCJJ) for police equipment ($14,500), and recognizes RAMP EZ Grant funds for
purchase of equipment for the Golden Hours Center ($4,000).

Proposed Ordinance 2022-42 amending Ogden Municipal Code to allow
metal siding for accessory structures over 400 square feet and on less than
one-half acre

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed Zone Text Amendment to
regulations for metal accessory buildings came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Mike Miller has submitted
a petition to allow non-architectural metal sided accessory buildings over 400 square feet on lots smaller than one-half acre. The
current ordinance allows the case-by-case review and approval of accessory buildings larger than 400 square feet if they are sided
with architectural metal or non-architectural metals on lots larger than one-half acre. The ordinance does not allow accessory
buildings over 400 square feet made with non-architectural metals on lots smaller than one-half acre. Mr. Miller applied for a
permit to replace a wooden sided one-car garage with a larger two-car, metal sided garage and was denied based on the current
ordinance. Mr. Miller has filed a petition to amend the ordinance to allow the type of garage he is requesting.

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and stated at the September 20, 2022 Council meeting, Council
members tabled the proposal to the October 11, 2022 meeting and directed a change to the ordinance to allow Planning staff to
review and approve applications rather than the Planning Commission. In addition, minor text changes were directed to provide
clearer standards for approval.

Chair Nadolski introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2022-42, entitled:

“An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Section 15-13-33.C to
allow metal siding for accessory structures when over 400 square feet and on less than one-half acre; and by
providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final passage.”

A copy of the proposed ordinance was deposited with the Deputy City Recorder and ordered that the City Recorder have
at least one copy available for public inspection in her office during all business hours.

Chair Nadolski then called for public input regarding the proposed ordinance.

Mike Miller stated that building materials have changed over recent years and metal sided garages can be aesthetically
pleasing; he thanked Planning staff and the City Council for considering his petition as he believes it will accomplish his goal of
using the materials he would like to on his garage while being aesthetically pleasing. He asked that the Council consider an
adjustment that deals with orientation of specific building materials; his contractor has indicated that some materials must be
installed horizontally rather than vertically to ensure a longer useful life of the building.

Angel Castillo stated she is pleased that the Council is coming up with standards that make it easier for an application to
navigate a Planning approval process. She agrees with the petitioner that the action taken against his initial application was an
unintended consequence of the Code and she asked that the Council add an exception in regard to the orientation of specific
building materials.

Heath Satow stated that he also supports approval of Mr. Miller’s petition.

There were no additional persons appearing to be heard.

Council member Hyer agreed that the orientation of building materials should not be a concern of the City.

Public Comments

Angel Castillo stated there are lot of great things happening in Ogden; LUPEC is holding its Hispanic Festival this
weekend at the Union Station, and she encouraged all to attend.

Heath Satow stated that it is his understanding that earlier this evening, the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) purchased
land from a developer or property broker, gave land to him across the street, and the person that was given the land did not
perform as promised, so the City must purchase the land again. This is the use of taxpayer money for purchase of the same
property two times, resulting in millions of dollars wasted. The same developer has also received an acre of land in Ogden’s
downtown free of charge and many other incentives. The City’s Community and Economic Development Director hid this from
the RDA Board; he suggested that the RDA ask some tough questions as it seems as if the RDA is more concerned about hurting
the feelings of these developers than being responsible for taxpayer money. He has heard the Mayor say that the City has been
working towards having the land developed for many years, but he asked if the City has advertised that they would give any
developer $1 million to take on the project. There is no transparency related to these types of projects and he is very
uncomfortable with the same developer participating in consecutive redevelopment projects, though that fact was hidden from the
Board. The Board cannot do their job appropriately if they do not have all the information. He concluded by thanking the Police
Department for moving traffic at the intersection of 26" Street and Harrison Boulevard when the traffic signal was
malfunctioning; they did a great job.
