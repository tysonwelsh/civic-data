problem. Mr. Jurges clarified that it is only an accounting problem if there is a reasonable number
of ongoing capital projects going on. Capital projects tend to be spread across budget years.
Typically, funding is received, then an engineering study is conducted to determine the scope of
work, and the money is not actually spent until the next budget year. In that case, the City can
borrow from itself and use that money to support other areas. However, utilizing those funds
would decrease the interest allocation to the General Fund. There was approximately $1.5 in
flexibility available. The City could legally go below 15%, but he was uncomfortable with that
option and would recommend delaying some expenditures until December or January to ensure
that mandatory costs are covered.

Budget.

Subject: Preliminary Site Evaluation and Project Considerations - Indoor Training
Range

This memorandum provides a preliminary overview of key considerations related to the
proposed indoor training range project. The purpose of this document is to present initial
findings, identify viable site options, and solicit input prior to development of a formal
recommendation for City Council consideration.

Site Consideration- City Hall Site (North Side)



Advantages:

   •    Cost Efficiency: Use of a footing system
        (in lieu of a full concrete pad) significantly
        reduces construction costs and
        minimizes drainage impacts.

   •    Utility Access: Existing gas and electrical
        infrastructure reduces installation
        complexity and cost.

   •    Design Compatibility: Proposed structure can incorporate architectural elements
        consistent with City Hall.

   •    Operational Benefits:

           o   Enhanced security due to proximity to City operations

           o   Immediate access to classrooms and restrooms

           o   Improved oversight, maintenance, and day-to-day management

           o   Enhanced safety with 8-foot concrete wall
Site Visits and Safety Evaluation

A recent site visit to Las Vegas included a factory tour and technical briefings addressing
key safety considerations, including lead exposure mitigation, noise control, and projectile
containment. These concerns were thoroughly evaluated and addressed.

Additional visits to operational facilities-including the Las Vegas Metropolitan Police
Department CSI range and Bass Pro Shops indoor range-provided practical insight into
comparable systems. While these facilities did not incorporate the level of sound
mitigation proposed for this project, they demonstrated effective operational models.



Cost and Engineering Analysis

Project costs remain under evaluation pending further engineering analysi s.

   •   Milo System Cost: $2,160,000 (complete system delivered on site)

   •   CHPD Oversight: Absorbed with in existing staffing and range master resources

   •   Utility Connections: To be determi ned

   •   Footings: Included in MILO contract

   •   Stormwater Improvements: $50,000 existing line item

   •   Lead Management: No cost anticipated; Action Target will collect and purchase
       lead, resulting in a revenue-neutral impact

   •   Annual Maintenance: Estimated at $17,500 (filter replacement cycles)

   •   End-of-Life (EOL): Milo has historically repurchased and salvaged systems

Key Takeaways

   •   Inside the range:
       Sound levels are high (100+ dB), consistent with expected firearm discharge.
       Hearing protection is required.

   •   Outside the building:
       Sound drops significantly and is comparable to common urban or equipment noise.

   •   At distance (75-100 feet):
       Noise levels are similar to typical city background noise and not distinguishable as
       sharp impulse sounds.

   •   Sound reduction:
       The data shows a drop of approximately 30 dB from inside to 100 feet, representing
       a ~1,000x reduction in sound intensity.



Bottom Line: While firearm discharge inside the range produces sound levels similar to a
chainsaw, the building significantly reduces noise, and at approximately 100 feet the sound
is comparable to normal urban background levels such as traffic or household equipment.

   •   Premium pricing for advanced training capabilities

   •   Expanded availability (weekends and after-hours use)

                                   RESOLUTION NO. 2026-33


   A RESOLUTION EMPHASIZING SEVERE DROUGHT CONDITIONS AND PROVIDING
  ENHANCED WATER CONSERVATION MEASURES FOR RESIDENTS AND BUSINESSES

        WHEREAS, the State of Utah is currently experiencing extreme drought conditions,
requiring immediate and proactive measures to protect the long-term sustainability of the regional
water supply and the health of the Great Salt Lake; and

       WHEREAS, the Salt Lake City Department of Public Utilities ("SLCPU') and the Jordan
Valley Water Conservancy District ("JVWCD"), who provide culinary water services to the
majority of the city of Cottonwood Heights (" City"), have issued advisories for significant water
use reduction; and

       WHEREAS, City's city council (the "Council") met in regular session on 2 June 2026 to
consider, among other things, fom1ally emphasizing the upcoming severe drought conditions and
providing enhanced water conservation measures for City's residents and businesses ("residents")
during the pendency of the drought; and

       WHEREAS, the Council recognizes that while most residents utilize culinary water
systems, others utilize private irrigation systems which operate on a "water turn" basis and have
their own distinct management requirements; and

        WHEREAS, the Council also recognizes that while turf irrigation must be reduced, the
preservation of our urban forest is a critical priority, as healthy trees promote clean air and provide
vital shade to mitigate the effects of earth warming;

     NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE COTTONWOOD HEIGHTS
CITY COUNCIL:

       1.     Standard Watering Schedule. Residents served by SLCPU or JVWCD are strongly
encouraged to limit outdoor lawn irrigation to a maximum of twice per week.

         2.     Private Irrigation Users. Residents on private irrigation systems are encouraged to
strictly follow the specific guidance provided by their system's managers, recognizing that these
systems operate on a traditional "water tum" basis.

       3.      Smart Technology & Weather Awareness. City urges the use of "smart irrigation
controllers" that adjust based on local weather data. Regardless of the schedule, no irrigation
should occur during high rain events to prevent significant water waste.

        4.       Deep Watering and Trees. Residents should practice "deep watering" to encourage
resilient root growth. Preservation of established trees is a priority; trees should be watered directly
to ensure they continue to provide shade and air filtration for the community.

        5.      Xeriscape and Plantings. City promotes transitioning to xeriscaping and drought-
tolerant native plantings. Residents are encouraged to utilize local landscape principles to design
functional , water-wise landscapes.

         6.     Best Practices.

                (a)    No outdoor watering should occur between the hours of 10:00 a.m. and 8:00
p.m.

                (b)    Residents should regularly inspect irrigation systems for leaks or broken
heads.

         This resolution, assigned no. 2026-33, shall take effect immediately upon passage.

         PASSED AND APPROVED effective 2 June 2026.
