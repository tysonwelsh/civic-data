BRIEFING SESSION ~ 6:00 P.M.
1. City Updates | |
Chair David Young started the briefing session at 6:03 pm, giving the floor to Dina Blaes.
Ms. Blaes shared some statistics on what she shared with the mayor’s team once a
month, regarding planning inquiries. She thought it would be interesting to know how
many calls were handled. She stated October had been busy and she expected that trend
to continue. "
Ms. Blaes presented information on the Utah Zoning Atlas and explained that Utah was
one of 15 states to have its zoning maps fully digitized and available to the public. The
atlas revealed that currently only 5% of land in the Salt Lake County metro area allowed
for multi-family housing by right. Twenty-five percent of single-family land in the county
required lots of 1 acre or more. Taylorsville was not far off the mark for the first finding,
but the second one was not typical of Taylorsville. She felt the Atlas would be an
interesting tool for planners and policy makers to use over the next several years.
She then reported that a vacant Tesoro gas station at 3621 West 5400 South would now
be a Mountain America Credit Union. It would be the 5" MACU branch in Taylorsville and
was quite close to an existing branch. It was still unclear what would happen with another
nearby location which was also home to the Utah Hispanic Chamber.
1


Ms. Blaes shared some examples of what future commercial development could include.
Specifically, she discussed unmanned “gas” station/convenience stores designed solely
for electric vehicles. Taylorsville’s code did not contemplate such a use, so this was one
example of the type of policy question they might be asked to consider in the future.
The city had received an inquiry from Amazon Now, which was a specialized sub-model
of Amazon’s broader operations. The business model focused on local, immediate
delivery of grocery essentials which would come from a mini-fulfillment hub and originate
from an online order. .
Ms. Blaes noted that this type of business did not promote a sense of community and was
in contrast to the direction traditional convenience stores were now moving. However,
she reported that there had been several conversations with Amazon Now and that they
were looking for a site that was 3,000 — 5,000 sq. feet.
Finally, Ms. Blaes provided an update on the city's Redevelopment Agency project area
at 5400 South Bangerter Highway. The urban renewal area, originally formed in 2008 with
an expected expiration in 2028, had not seen the anticipated level of development activity.
She reported that the taxing entity committee had approved an amended budget
extending the tax increment capture period by ten years to 2038. This extension could
potentially increase the collected increment from $4.5 million to approximately $21 million,
which would help fund significant infrastructure improvements, particularly sewer
upgrades needed to support higher-density development in the area.
There were no further topics discussed and the briefing session adjourned at 6:27 pm.

3. Request for Extension of Approval for Reasonable Accommodation
at 3547 South Biathlon Circle; File No. 1SI-RA-000537-2025; Hawa Mallay,
Applicant
Planner Terryne Bergeson explained that the Planning Commission had granted
conditional approval for a reasonable accommodation request on July 22, 2025. The
request was to increase the number of unrelated adults allowed to occupy a single-family
2


dwelling from four to eight to enable operation of a residential facility for people with
disabilities.
Ms. Bergeson noted that during the July meeting, the applicant, Ms. Hawa Mallay, had
mentioned a basement that was not shown in the plans previously provided. A condition
of approval required the applicant to provide full floor plans including the basement. Upon
review of these plans, staff discovered an unpermitted kitchen in the basement. As
residential facilities must comply with building, safety, and health regulations, the
applicant was instructed to obtain the necessary building permit for the kitchen.

The approval was set to expire on January 23, 2026, but Ms. Mallay had not yet submitted

the building permit. Ms. Bergeson explained that the applicant had requested a three-

month extension before the expiration date to allow time to submit the building permit
application, complete the review process, and schedule inspections.

Commissioner Willardson inquired about the purpose of the kitchen, and Ms. Bergeson

responded that it was installed when the home was the applicant's primary residence.

Commissioner Wilkey asked how many people currently occupied the home. Mr. Tevi

Lawson (who was present electronically) identified himself as both a structural engineer

and a relative of the applicant. He explained that the home was now unoccupied and that

Ms. Mallay had installed the kitchen when her daughters were living there. She had later

contacted him to assist her with the permitting process.

Chair Young asked if there was a staff recommendation on this matter and Ms. Bergeson

indicated there was none.

MOTION: Commissioner Wright moved to approve a 90-day extension for the
applicant’s reasonable accommodation approval. The motion was
seconded by Commissioner McElreath.
