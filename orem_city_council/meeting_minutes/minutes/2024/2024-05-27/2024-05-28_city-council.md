1600 North 400 East Intersection Study Presenter: John Dorny, Transportation Engineer and
JUB Engineers

Mr. Dorny introduced Craig Friant and Hollee Wood from JUB Engineers who have been
working on the 1600 N 400 East intersection. Mr. Friant will be reporting on the findings for the

City Council Minutes — May 28, 2024, (pg.1) 4 complete video of the meeting can be found at www.orem.org/meeting

intersection. The Murdock Trail goes through this intersection. The trail was enclosed in 2011
and 2012, it was completed in May 2013, and a trail was built over the, now enclosed Murdock
Canal, This trail is a 17 mile continuous route from Orem to Lehi and the annual rider count
exceeds 170,000 riders in Orem.

Mr. Friant explained that the purpose of this project is to identify solutions to improve
compliance and safety for vehicles and trail users. The project started by brainstorming ideas
and from this brainstorming JUB came up with 12 development alternatives. The evaluation
criteria they used was vehicular road safety, trail user safety, cost, vehicle level of service, trail
level of service and compliance. They used this criteria to score each of the alternatives. All 12
alternatives were presented to the Transportation Advisory Commission (TAC). After the
meeting with the TAC, the alternatives were narrowed to 5 options to present to the community:
1) at-grade improvements at intersection, 2) at-grade improvements with relocated crossing, 3)
grade separation crossing, 4) roundabout, and 5) pedestrian scramble.

Mr. Friant continued to give details about the different alternatives. Alternative A gives
directional crosswalks with no right turn lanes. Alternative B relocates the crossing to 350 E and
the addition of a hawk signal. Alternative C proposes a grade separated crossing at 400 East and
1600 North. Alternative D is a roundabout with rectangular rapid flashing beacons at all
crosswalks. Alternative E is a pedestrian scramble that stops all traffic in the intersection at once.
During the process, JUB was asked to look at different grade separated alternatives. First option
is an underpass north of the Murdock Canal Aqueduct. The estimated cost would be $10 million.
Second option is a pedestrian bridge over the intersection. Estimated cost is over $14.5 million.

Ms. Wood spoke about the public outreach part of the study. They asked residents to
complete a survey, they held a public open house, they posted lawn signs near the
intersection/trail, they sent postcards invitations to all residents within /% mile of intersections
and they used social media posts. The public open house was held at Timpanogos High School.
There were 111 attendees and 29 written comments from the open house. The survey was open
from October 27 to November 13. It was 16 questions and 518 responses were received. The
survey ranked the alternatives. Alternate C was ranked most safe followed by Alternate A, B, E,
no change and then D. The survey asked if residents were supportive of increased safety for
pedestrians and cyclists, knowing that it would likely increase delay time for drivers. Sixty
percent were supportive. Some additional public feedback is a drinking fountain, bench or bike
repair station as potential amenities at the south west corner. Another alternative is to do E now
and the plan for C in the future.

Mr. Friant spoke about short and long term recommendations. The short term would be to
add directional crossing with no right hand turns allowed, alternative A. There is also an option
to do alternative A and keep right hand turns. The other short term recommendation is Pedestrian
Scramble, alternative E. The long term recommendation is the grade separated crossing,
alternatives C. The key takeaways are focus on project purpose and encourage creativity in
brainstorming, include the TAC to help, open and transparent public involvement to gain

Waste Water, Pretreatment, and Local Limits Code Updates Presenter: Reed Price, Assistant
Public Works Director

Mr. Price is presenting on the Water Reclamation Code, Pretreatment and Local Limits.
In previous works sessions the discussion was to have the Pretreatment Ordinance be in a new
and stand alone section. There were also discussions about moving portions from chapter 20 to
chapter 11 and updates to Water Reclamation Code, chapter 12. Today will be a review of the
code before adoption in the general session in June.

Mr. Price began with the pretreatment section. He explained that it's a federally required
program that regulates pollutants from certain industrial users to reduce pollution at the source,
protecting the environment and extending the life of Orem’s infrastructure. Orem’s program was
developed nearly 30 years ago and it was patterned after the state’s model ordinance. The new
update will simplify the reporting process and should not adversely affect businesses.

Local limits are part of pretreatment and are required by the EPA. The code defined the
amount of certain pollutants allowed in wastewater discharge by certain industrial and
commercial users. The changes will protect our workers, protect Orem’s infrastructure, and
publicly owned treatment works (POTWs) must elevate the local limits. These proposed changes
should not adversely affect businesses. Developing local limits is a process and includes
determining pollutants of concern, sampling and lab analysis, calculating how much the
treatment facility can handle, and dividing amongst the industrial users. Mr. Price reviewed the
EPA’s 15 national pollutants of concern and the Orem Water Reclamation Facility also regulates
the following pollutants of concern: ph, phosphorus, aluminum and phenol. The new local limits

were displayed. The effects on the industry is that new users must comply upon approval,
existing users will have 60 days to comply. This should not be a concern for existing industrial
users. The final steps for local limits was to reach out to all industrial users informing them of
this change and a public comment period was opened and has now closed. No public comments
were received. The DEQ has reviewed and approved the proposed changes to both the ordinance
and local limits. Currently, they are awaiting the final approval from the Director of the Division
of Water Quality (DWQ). Once that is received then we can adopt the ordinance. This is

Mr. Price spoke about the changes to the water reclamation code. These include updated
definitions, standardized correct terminology, removed references to pretreatment regulations
and defined sewer requirements for ADU’s. The changes to the code include the discharging
flushable wipes is specifically prohibited, it provides requirements for acceptance of a private
sewer main to public ownership, and regulates restaurants and auto shops discharge of fats, oils
and grease. The next step is to approve this in the general meeting on June 18, 2024.

Municipal Wastewater Planning Program (MWPP) Presenter: Reed Price, Assistant Public
Works Director

Mr. Price is speaking about the annual update of the municipal wastewater planning
program (MWPP). The MWPP uses an annual survey to assist owners of municipal wastewater
collection systems and treatment facilities to evaluate and summarize the technical, operational,

and financial conditions. It helps communities better understand business operations and can
identify and resolve potential problem areas before they become serious and costly. There are
three areas of evaluation, 1) financial evaluation, 2) collections system, and 3) treatment facility.
Mr. Price reviewed the questions from the survey.

in-kind for a total of 57 sponsors.

University Place and will include the entrepreneur market and the Jersey Streets concert on
Wednesday, June 5th. Sportsfest was added this year at the new Hillcrest Park, with a pickleball
tournament running June 5-7 and Comhole tournament on June 6th. The Orem Express will be at
