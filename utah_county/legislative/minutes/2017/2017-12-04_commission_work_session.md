Commissioner Lee welcomed all present and explained Commissioner Ivie was joining the meeting by telephone from the
Utah State Capital and Commissioner Graves was out of town. The meeting began at 9:24 A.M. He also explained the
meeting was a not a meeting to ask questions but was one where the market study would be presented. He added there
would be further meetings where questions could be asked later today.

Victoria McGrath, CEO of McGrath Human Resources Group, presented the market study her company worked on. Shortly
into the meeting, she was interrupted by Raphael Millet from the back of the room asking for the methodology she used.
Commissioner Lee said Raphael was out of order and explained there would be another meeting later in the day where he
could ask questions. When he didn’t quiet down, Commissioner Lee said he would have the Sheriff remove him so that
order could be had. At that point, Ms. McGrath continued her presentation explaining the methodology that was used in
the survey. She described how compensation data was collected and what the market analysis showed when current
positions were evaluated. She explained how they looked at the total compensation package in comparison to Davis County,
Salt Lake County, Washington County and Weber County. She said the County salaries were a little less than average, but
health insurance premiums brought the County up to average. Ms. McGrath identified the concerns they found in the study.
She described that a classification system is a schedule of job titles based on skills, qualifications, and education needed to
perform the position and not based on individual behavior or performance. She explained they recommend an 18-step

system where Step “F” is the average market rate. She commented this system would eliminate the 1, 2 and 3 titles. She
explained the benefits of this proposed compensation system. She described the six factors that make up the point system
they recommend such as education, experience, and what equipment you can operate.

Ms. McGrath explained how difficult it is to implement a new compensation system. She presented 4 different options and
the costs associated with each option. These options cost between $2.6 million and $5.8 million. She explained these are
approximate because the figures fluctuate daily. She commented how she usually recommends organizations adopt Option
2. Ms. McGrath recommended moving the annual employee evaluations, so they occur annually in July in the future.

Ms. McGrath covered the in-depth review that was done about the County’s Health Insurance, HSA contributions, and the
waiver programs, vacation and sick leave, and what benefit recommendations were given to the County.

In summary, she covered how they recommended a structured plan that is tied to the market be adopted.

After Ms. McGrath completed her presentation, Commissioner Lee explained how the meeting was set up so that the
Commissioners would ask questions after the presentation. Then, employees would have the ability to ask questions at
future meetings. He explained how employees had previously emailed questions to him. At this point, Raphael Millet
identified himself and interrupted Commissioner Lee by announcing from the back of the room that he wanted to make
public comment in this meeting. He yelled, “Comment, Buckshot! Comment, Buckshot!” Commissioner Lee warned
Raphael he was out of order and was told this would be the last time. He was told he would be asked to leave if he continued.
Raphael shouted: “Public Comment!” again. At this point Commissioner Lee asked him to leave. Raphael answered he
would but then continued yelling: “I will get you on the record, Bill Lee, I will, I will. Public policy…you shut me down.
You are not a true Republican if you shut anyone down in an institution meeting.” Raphael continued saying “Now, I have
to go to another meeting. Do I need to take off time for it? I will.” He then turned his attention to Commissioner Ivie and
asked: “Commissioner Ivie, am I not going to get any public comment here?” Commissioner Lee explained: “I did not say
there couldn’t be public comment here.” Raphael continued yelling from the last row in the room, “Can we have one? No!
You said they were only coming from you.” Commissioner Lee answered: “I said the questions are coming from the
commission.” Raphael countered that he had questions and could debate him on methodology and policy. Finally,
Commissioner Lee stated: “I am done with this, Sheriff.” Raphael was then asked to leave the meeting. To which, Raphael
proclaimed: “I will find you, Bill. We will make public policy comment. We will get on your case.”

Commissioner Lee and Ms. McGrath discussed the methodology that was used or not used when the comparison was done
with other cities or counties. He asked her to clarify what he had told her where he wanted Utah County to be in the market.
She answered that two of the commissioners stated they wanted the county to be at the average market rate. She explained
this was common among governments and didn’t think they needed to go higher. Commissioner Lee commented he said
he wanted the employees to have a fair wage.

Commissioner Lee commented on how several employees were concerned that the market study showed newer employees
would be paid more or slightly less than longer-term employees. Ms. McGrath explained HR would need to look at that
and make adjustments to the schedule so that doesn’t happen. She said it was suggested in the second year of the plan,
employees with more than 6 years tenure would be given a 2 step increase to move them past Step “F.” She also said HR
needed to make sure no one was hired at a higher step range than anyone already in the system or had less years of service
was at a higher step range.

Commissioner Lee asked why titles were eliminated. Ms. McGrath explained this was a good time to reset the system to a
more minimalistic way. She continued explaining how if an employee was doing the same type of work, they should have
the same title. They discussed the changes that were suggested to titles so there would be more consistency.

Commissioner Lee asked why the hire date is proposed to not be their anniversary date. Ms. McGrath answered that the
salary schedule was recommended to be changed in January and the anniversary date be changed to July 1st. She explained
it was easier to have all the anniversary dates at one time. She suggested evaluations be simplified.

Commissioner Lee thanked Ms. McGrath for coming and opened the meeting to public comment. He explained this portion
of the meeting would not be a question and answer period nor a debate with Ms. McGrath.

Hyrum Cox, Sheriff’s Office, spoke about the inequity of pay in positions based on time served. He asked for these to be
looked at on an individual basis. Commissioner Lee commented he had received many emails about this problem.

Tyler Plewe, Health Department, commented that he wondered why Washington County was used as a comparable because
no previous employees had been lost to Washington County.

Pam Bair, Sheriff’s Office, explained how in the past Utah County considered itself at market if it was at 80% and now it
is considered market at 40%. She explained how long-term employees will receive nothing from this proposal. She said it
wasn’t fair for the “red-lined” employees.

Craig Bostock, Environmental Health, commented how when benefits were looked at Social Security was not included. He
said the other 4 counties used as comparables looked at pay into Social Security.

There was no further public comment.

        Commissioner Ivie made the motion to close the work session. Commissioner Lee suspended the Roberts
        Rules of Order. The motion was seconded by Commissioner Lee and carried with the following vote:
                                                                       Aye:    Commissioner Lee
                                                                               Commissioner Ivie
                                                                       Nay:    None

The meeting adjourned at 10:28 A.M.
