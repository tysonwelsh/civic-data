staff, and cemetery staff all went above and beyond in order to prepare for and host this event.

Centennial Plaza. A lovely dinner had been held to celebrate the art as well as the artists and she

to step forward.

Roseanne Matavao explained that she lived just off 4700 South and was concerned about the
coming “expressway.” She indicated that many years ago a UDOT representative had promised a
20’ high sound barrier between the expressway and nearby homes. She had more recently been
told the wall would be only 8’ tall. She mentioned that both children and adults could scale 8’
walls quite easily.

Chris Anderson explained that 4700 South was near her backyard. Their primary concern was that
they had no idea that the project was happening and wanted to be kept in the loop. They would
appreciate being informed via email about issues that directly affected them. She had been told the
barrier would only be 6’ tall—not even 8’. She felt that was insufficient.

Blake Anderson did not understand why he was going to lose 10’ of his yard to the project which

Judy Garrigus explained that she lived next door to the Andersons. She said that she and other

Claudia Wilson lived right across the street from Fore Lakes Golf Course on 4700 West. She

protection that a 6’ fence would provide. She explained that she had several people jump her fence

the expressway would be an improvement. However, if the wall was not taller than currently
planned, she would move out of Taylorsville, and she really did not want to do that.

Councilmember Harker pointed out that the project was still in the design phase, so nothing was
written in stone. The citizen comments would be taken under consideration and there would be
future meetings to communicate with nearby residents. She encouraged residents to keep an eye
on the city website and Facebook to stay in the know. Chair Barbieri mentioned that several articles

A citizen in the audience asked how they would know when BRT would be discussed if not that

Mr. White stepped to the podium and pointed out that the BRT was not a city project. Instead, it
was a Utah Transit Authority (UTA) project. Therefore, it was UTA that would be responsible for
keeping residents in the area informed of project plans and activities. Still, it was a federally funded
project which meant that certain procedures needed to be followed, whether it was UTA or UDOT
(Utah Department of Transportation). UDOT had involved a public engagement professional who
would be tasked with keeping the nearby residents informed so it was actually UTA rather than
the city who would keep the residents informed on that specific topic.

Several audience members began to address Mr. White with complaints regarding the lack of
communication they had experienced thus far. City Attorney Tracy Cowdell interjected that UTA’s
Bus Rapid Transit project was not on the council’s agenda for consideration and the council needed
to move forward with the city business that was on the agenda. He recommended the residents
take these particular concerns to UTA.

Mr. White told the audience members that UDOT’s public engagement professional, Amalia
Andrews was present and currently moving out into the foyer. If people had questions for her, they
were welcome to speak with her outside the council chambers.

7.      OTHER MATTERS

        7.1      Ordinance No. 22-27 An Ordinance of the City of Taylorsville amending
                 Taylorsville Municipal Code Section 2.12.033: Electronic Meetings – Tracy
                 Cowdell

              City Attorney Tracy Cowdell explained that in the last legislative session, a law had
        been passed which clarified how quorums were to be calculated, particularly when a
        member of the body was attending the meeting electronically. Therefore, he was
        recommending a change to the language currently in city code so that at least three
        members of the council were required to be physically present in the “anchor location” (the
        council chambers) but up to two members could attend electronically. There was a different
        state law regarding electronic meetings in emergency circumstances such as the Covid
        pandemic. This proposed change only addressed typical, non-emergency circumstances.

        The second part of the proposed ordinance made it clear that any time a councilmember
        attended a council meeting electronically, and a member of the body wished to vote “no”
        on a matter, there now must be a roll call vote. A simple voice vote was no longer sufficient
        for any motion when a member was attending electronically.

MOTION:          Councilmember Harker moved to adopt Ordinance No. 22-27, amending the

        7.2      Discussion Regarding 4700 South Improvements – Ben White

              Mr. White apologized that his report was not on the topic that some people had
              expected. He explained he was there to address the roadway on 4700 South between
              2700 West and I-215. There were still many moving parts to the project, but the intent
              was to build an additional westbound lane on the north side of the road which would
              involve the relocation of overhead power lines. The burial of those lines would begin
              on the east side of I-215 and would be buried by the substation near 2200 West to
              2700 West. On the south end, a roundabout was proposed at Sable Drive. The intent
              was to eliminate the traffic signal at Chentelle Drive so that through traffic on 4700

               vehicles to move through that section of roadway than was currently the case.
