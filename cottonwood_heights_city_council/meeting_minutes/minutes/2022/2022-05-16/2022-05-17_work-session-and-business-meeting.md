1.      WELCOME – Mayor Weichers.

Mayor Mike Weichers called the meeting to order at 4:00 p.m. and welcomed those present.

2.      REVIEW OF BUSINESS MEETING AGENDA – Mayor Mike Weichers.

The Business Meeting agenda was reviewed and discussed.

The first agenda item was consideration of the issuance of the City’s Sales Tax Revenue Bonds,
Series 2022 (the “Series 2022 Bonds”). Administrative and Fiscal Services Director, Scott Jurges,
reported that a public hearing is required any time debt is issued, specifically related to the $7.5
million sales tax revenue bonds for the City’s Public Works Building. Bonds were authorized by
the City Council and include the requirement that a public hearing be held. He reported that the
bond rate on the preferred bid is 3.7%.

The second action item was consideration of Resolution 2022-28 approving a fireworks display
contract with Lantis Productions for 2022 Butlerville Days. Records Culture and Human
Resources Director, Paula Melgar stated that the agreement with Lantis Productions was for the
firework set up at a cost of $12,000 and was included in the 2022-2023 budget.

The third action item was consideration of Resolution 2022-29 amending the designated areas
closed to the discharge of fireworks for 2022. Assistant Fire Chief, Riley Pilgrim stated that they
have included a small area above Crescent Park in the restricted areas.

was a recent name change that was not largely publicized.

Lumen/CenturyLink Consumer Fiber Group representative, Jim Parks, stated that he is involved
in acquiring State broadband grants and partnerships that allow fiber build-out to the home.
CenturyLink operates in 37 states as an incumbent local exchange carrier or common telephone
company with assets in Central and South America. Quantum fiber brand is currently being
offered with the intent of being at a build level of 1.5 million to 2 million customer residential
locations with the Lumen brand being marketed to enterprise, large businesses, and government
education. Pricing was described. Mr. Parks reported that speeds vary and are dependent on the
location. A rendering representing the speed census was displayed. He explained that if there are
areas with top speeds reported at less than 100 megabits down and 120 megabits up, there is a
possibility that those areas would be subject to a grant. It was anticipated that the Federal
Communications Commission (“FCC”) will provide a more detailed map by the third quarter of
2023. A “477 Map” depicting topographical details of speeds available within Cottonwood
Heights provided largely through Comcast was reviewed.

Mayor Weichers stated that it is the objective of the City to provide fiber cable to the homes of as
many residents as possible. Mr. Parks confirmed that CenturyLink does not burrow or perform
micro trenching but utilizes rights-of-way through existing utilities. He confirmed that they will
not be moving in the direction of micro trenching and was unsure of how much of Cottonwood
Heights will build out.

The Federal Grant Program was next discussed. Mr. Parks stated that there may be underserved
areas within the City where fiber cables may be run up to a complex where the implantation of
each unit would be too costly. Installation is largely dependent on the developer. He explained
that there is available technology that provides speeds of up to 945 gigabytes and they have begun
to deploy XGS or 10-gigabit technology. They are currently in the process of developing projects
that offer multi-gig levels. He confirmed that CenturyLink is an active participant in low-income
programs. Links to access the programs were provided.

Council Member Peterson asked if fiber service is shared when brought in by a different provider.
Mr. Parks explained they do not support the UTOPIA model and are focused on maintaining
complete control over their network to create flexibility with standardized pricing. In a
competitive environment, there is minimal market share. Quantum fiber is currently advertising
at $65 per month and includes an optical network, the terminal, unlimited data, and a gateway with
an optional Wi-Fi router.

Council Member Birrell expressed concern with an increase in pricing and residents being required
to maintain an expensive service. Mr. Parks stated that there are low-income programs available
and they strive to maintain competitive rates and focus on customer retention.

        a.      Council Budget Discussion – City Manager, Tim Tingey and Administrative and
                Fiscal Services Director, Scott Jurges.

City Manager, Tim Tingey, presented the Council Budget discussion and stated that a Tentative
Budget has been adopted. A public hearing was to be conducted during the Business Meeting.

The Budget Discussion will continue at the June 7, 2022, Work Session with the likely
continuation of the public hearing to allow for additional comment. A final budget decision will
be required prior to the June 22, 2022, deadline.

Modifications to the budget for final consideration included the following:

        1.      The Valley Emergency Communications Center (“VECC”) reported an error in the
                calculation of the formula of funds provided by Cottonwood Heights. There will
                be a reduction in the current tentative budget of $14,550.

        2.      The Public Works Building will include principle interest for the current year and
                will be incorporated in the amount of approximately $500,000.

        3.      The Unified Fire Authority (“UFA”) budget proposal will reflect a percentage
                reduction and be forthcoming.

        4.      The financial impact of the Central Wasatch Commission (“CWC”) Big
                Cottonwood Canyon Area Traffic Study on the participating entities was $15,000.

        5.      The Benefits and Compensation Committee’s evaluation of compensation increases
                regarding sustainability and recommendation of a market study were to be
                considered during the upcoming budget year. The levy rate was expected to be
                finalized and ready for the June 21, 2022, Business Meeting.

Mayor Weichers was not in favor of a Cost-of-Living Adjustment (“COLA”) of less than 6% or
that of the Unified Fire Authority. He believed that as a Council, they have a fiduciary
responsibility to make sound, financial decisions. He stated that the absolute minimum for any
employee would be 7%. The step process was discussed.

Council Member Birrell suggested more frequent evaluations to ensure that the City is up to date
with what is trending and to protect the retention of valued City employees.

Council Member Peterson expressed concern with potential inflation and believed every project
will receive cutbacks or require further review.

Council Member Bracken recommended adjustments be made in October. It was confirmed that
the budget can be adjusted as needed.

Mr. Tingey reported that the Merit Schedule is built for retention. He believed that if adjusted,
issues may arise. COLAs are meant to be reviewed annually and he was more comfortable
addressing them as needed. Turnover has been low with the exception of a few individuals from
the Police Department who are entering retirement. He confirmed that additional discussion will
take place at the Work Session on June 7.

3.0     CITIZEN COMMENTS

Will McCarvill gave his address as 3607 Golden Hills Avenue and stated that the position of the
Economic Development Director must be split from the Community Development or Planning
Director. He felt that the positions are inheritably in opposition and lead citizens to suspect that
development will take priority. He stated that the Appeals Hearing Officer must not have any prior
roles in the City since decisions made by a person in this position who previously served on the
Planning Commission are viewed as suspicious by citizens. He recommended that descriptions be
considered from other municipalities.

Mavis Glad was present representing the Daughters of Utah Pioneers who are responsible for the
upkeep of the Union Pioneer Cemetery. She serves as President and extended an invitation to
Council Members and citizens to join the Memorial Day Program to be held at the cemetery at
8:30 a.m. Refreshments will be served.

Vaughn Horton gave his address as 6835 Nye Drive and stated that Rolling Knowles Way has a
number of stop signs. He stated that the close proximity of the stop signs encourages drivers to
run them. He recommended the City investigate the stop signs and review the Engineering
Manual.

There were no further citizen comments.
