Evergreen Solutions — Compensation Study for Elected Officials and
Chief Administrative Officer

The Council contracted with Evergreen Solutions to perform a benchmarking study on salaries for Ogden’s elected
officials and chief administrative officer (CAO). The study compares salaries and benefits to those of similar sized cities with the
same form of government. Evergreen Solutions will also provide a recommendation for establishing salaries for elected officials
and the CAO.

Jeff Ling and Connor Holcombe of Evergreen Solutions, LLC, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the
compensation study for elected officials and the CAO; the City’s peer municipalities for the study included Logan, Murray, Provo,
Salt Lake City, Sandy, St. George, and West Jordan. The findings for City Council were that salaries in Ogden City are higher
than peer averages, but stipends are 71.6 percent lower than for peers. The findings for the Mayor were that his salary is 9.0
percent higher than the peer average with St. George’s data removed as an outlier. The Mayor’s stipends are 14.4 percent higher
than the peer average. For the CAO, Ogden’s salary is 13.8 percent lower than the peer average, but the stipend for that position is
26 percent higher than peer averages. Evergreen recommends adjusting the salary for all Council members to $22,950; this
includes the chair and vice chair positions, which have historically higher compensation amounts. However, the vice chair and
chair could receive a supplemented stipend. Evergreen recommends the Mayor salary be $136,440.90 with a $6,097.56 stipend.
And, for the CAO, Evergreen recommends creating a salary range with a minimum salary of $138,068.97 and a maximum salary
of $201,914.78. There are different methodologies for determining the CAO’s placement within the range and Evergreen
recommends the ‘hybrid parity’ methodology; this would result in the CAO’s salary being increased to $168,667.07.

Council discussion with Council staff and representatives of Evergreen Solutions included topics such as expenses
covered by current stipends offered to elected officials and the CAO; the data relied upon to determine the proposed wage range
for the CAO; and the demographics and income levels of peer cities.

Council Executive Director Eller-Smith noted that the recommendations made by Evergreen can be forwarded to City
Administration for consideration as they begin developing the Mayor’s proposed budget for Fiscal Year (FY) 2022-2023.

Vice Chair Lopez stated he fully supports increasing salaries for general employees to respond to the market, but he is
very uncomfortable supporting salary increases for elected officials; he asked that the salaries for those positions be listed separate
from other salary increases in the budget so that separate votes can be taken by the Council. He cited a saying that ‘you cannot
have a rich government in a poor community’ and that is true for Ogden City.

Council discussion centered on Vice Chair Lopez’s recommendation and the Council supported listing salary adjustments
for elected officials separate from general employees. Several Council members expressed their support for the chair and vice
vhair receiving higher compensation — whether in salary or in the form of a stipend — than other Council members.

Marshall White Center Update

Representatives from the Recreation Division, the Marshall White Advisory Committee and VCBO Architecture
presented their recommendations for future development and use of the Marshall White Center (MWC) Facility. The
recommendations included:

e Providing a safe place for individuals and families to gather;

e Honoring the legacy of Marshall White;

e Increasing the size of the building to provide additional recreational and community event programming, one or
more aquatic features, fitness studios, and all-abilities access.

The Council reviewed the conceptual design with Recreation staff and VCBO Architecture representatives and provided
feedback regarding their desires for adjustments to the design to meet the needs of the community; reuse of original building
materials in the project; waterwise landscape elements; capacity of the facility and the ability to host large community events;
ongoing operational costs for the facility; funding sources for the project — which has an estimated price of $23 to $25 million; and
planning for improvement of the area surrounding the MWC facility.

Proposed Land Transfer and Development Agreement — Capitol Square
(2459 Quincy Avenue)

Community Development Manager Ward Ogden provided an overview of the proposed Land Transfer and Development
Agreement (LTDA) for property located at 2459 Quincy Avenue. The agreement is with JF Impact, LLC for the conveyance of
the two acres of property for the development of a 176-unit podium parked multi-family residential apartment building. The
proposed agreement would convey the property to JF Impact, LLC for sum of $757,760. The purchase price is to cover the
outstanding debt the Agency has incurred against the property. The proposed project is part of the larger Capitol Square project on
the former Rite-Aid site. Predevelopment activities, such as property acquisition, land consolidation, demolition, and
environmental remediation, have been taking place on the site since 2015. The Council approved a rezone with a project master
plan concept on December 14, 2021, and the Planning Commission reviewed and approved the site plan for this specific site at its
December 15, 2021 meeting. The developer has pursued housing tax credits through the Utah Housing Corporation. As such, the
proposed development will include units that may be offered to renters earning 60 percent of the average median gross income.

The Council communicated they are comfortable taking action on the LTDA at an upcoming meeting.

Proposed Text Amendment — Aligning Expiration Timelines for
Development Approvals

Planning Manager Montgomery reported staff is suggesting several changes to City code to help simplify expiration
dates for development applications. At the moment there are different expiration timelines for conditional use permit applications,
plat applications, multi-phase developments, site plan reviews, and several other land use applications. The different expiration
timelines are confusing for both staff and applicants. Planning Staff recommends an 18-month expiration period for each
application and a possible 6-month extension if related ordinances have not changed. Doing so would provide uniformity
throughout each of the applications.

The Council expressed support for the code amendments and Council staff indicated they will calendar required actions
for a future meeting.
