2. CITIZEN PUBLIC INPUT
Mr. Mark Lewon  stated after he came here three weeks ago about the recreation center 
swimming pools being closed because of a lack of lifeguards, he got a call from the Parks 
& Recreation Division letting him know the County had a contract with StarGuards 
through the end of the year, and StarGuards requires four lifeguards at a pool at any given 
time.  That meant there were not enough lifeguards to open pools at times.  Four 
lifeguards are unnecessary, particularly in the early morning hours when most of the 
people who use the pools are lap swimmers and master swimmers.  That is not a good 
use of taxpayer property.  He asked the Council to get involved and not contract with 
Starguards again when the current contract ends.  
The following residents of Mt. Aire Canyon spoke expressing their gratitude to the 
Council for passing an ordinance banning mining and mineral extraction in the County ’s 
foothills and canyons, and explained their heritage as descendants of original settlers who 
homesteaded there.  If mining and mineral extraction are allowed, the canyon will become 
nothing but an open gravel pit, and that would be a catastrophe to those residing in Mt . 
Aire and to the history of the Wasatch Front.
Sam Dunham
Shelley Thayne Rich

Council Member Stringham  shared her history with the Daughters of Utah 
pioneers, where she learned about pioneers: Green Flake, Hark Wales, Oscar 
Smith, and Jane Elizabeth Manning.  These pioneers are being honored with a 
monument, that will be dedicated Friday, July 22, 2022, at This Is The Place 
Heritage Park.  She read the following proclamation recognizing these 
pioneers:
A PROCLAMATION OF THE SALT LAKE COUNTY COUNCIL
DECLARING JULY 22, 2022 AS GREEN FLAKE, HARK WALES, 
OSCAR SMITH, AND JANE ELIZABETH MANNING DAY IN SALT 
LAKE COUNTY, UTAH
WHEREAS, July 24th, 1847, is an important day in Utah history and the 
history of the United States, marking the day that Brigham Young and a 
determined company of Mormon Pioneers entered the Great Salt Lake 
Valley; and 
WHEREAS, this first group of pioneers was the vanguard of one of the 
greatest treks in American History, pulling handcarts or driving wagons with 
oxen or horses; and
WHEREAS, these thousands of pioneers carried a firm commitment to 
America’s belief in freedom of religion as they slowly trudged across the 
plains to a vast desert landscape that became known as the Utah Territory; 
and
WHEREAS, these pioneers came from many nations to create a new life and 
exemplified courage, foresight, and faith that continues to inspire modern -day 
pioneers; and
WHEREAS, there are many monuments commemorating the pioneer leaders 
and courageous individuals who made their trek across the plains to a new 
home; and 
WHEREAS, there is now to be a new memorial monument commemorating 
the sacrifice, hardships, and contributions to the early Utah territory of both 
free and enslaved African American pioneers who came with those early 
groups; and 
Page 3 of 12
County Council Meeting Minutes July 19, 2022
WHEREAS, this new monument and the stories it represents will serve as a 
place of learning, reflection, and healing in its prominent location at the This 
is the Place State Park; and 
WHEREAS, this new monument will more specifically honor Green Flake, 
Hark Wales, and Oscar Smith, the first of the vanguard company to come 
into the valley as enslaved pioneers and Jane Elizabeth Manning James; and 
WHEREAS, Jane Elizabeth Manning James did not arrive into the valley with 
the vanguard company, she was the first documented free African American 
woman to come to the Utah Territory, arriving with her family as a Mormon 
pioneer; 
WHEREAS, these free and enslaved pioneers lived in poverty and often did 
not have the barest essentials for survival, yet they persevered in the face of 
great trials and obstacles; and 
WHEREAS, there are many journal entries documenting the grace and 
charity that this faithful, strong, and courageous group of pioneers endured, 
and demonstrating how they gave of themselves, their food storage, and their 
service to their fellow pioneers and to their God; and 
WHEREAS, these stories deserve to be told and retold in recognition of the 
incredible contributions given by these pioneers to the many communities 
that they helped to build; and
NOW THEREFORE, BE IT PROCLAIMED by the Salt Lake County 
Council that July 22, 2022, shall be known and honored as Green Flake, Hark 
Wales, Oscar Smith, and Jane Elizabeth Manning Day in Salt Lake County, 
Utah. The Council further proclaims that July 22, 2022, shall be a day of 
remembrance for the African American pioneers who entered the Salt Lake 
Valley in 1847, as well as day of honor for their families and their 
descendants who have given so much to our many communities in Utah . 
May their legacy and stories for ever be revered in our histories and taught to 
our children and their families for generations to come. 
SIGNED AND PROCLAIMED this 19th day of July, 2022.
By /s/ LAURIE STRINGHAM

Mr. Mauli Bonner , Executive Producer and Director, Green Flake, a 
movie, thanked the Council for this proclamation stating it is important to 
hear these stories and add to the great history Utahans already celebrate .  
This shows a bigger picture of history, one that the future generations will not 
be blindsided by and can celebrate alongside their peers.  This meant so 
much to citizens, the community, and especially descendants of these 
pioneers and black folks.   
Ms. Tamu Smith stated she is a descendent of Green Flake and was thrilled 
about being included and reclaimed by the pioneer community.  She thanked 
the Council for the resolution and for sharing the history.  It meant a lot to 
her, to her children, and to her grandchildren to hear these stories.  These 
pioneers were just a few of the pioneers who entered the valley, so she hoped 
more stories would be told.      
Ms. Cleo Balfour , Utah Arts Alliance, and a founder of Salt Lake 
Juneteenth, stated it was important that all members of the community know 
the inclusive history of those who have contributed to building this state, city, 
and county.  That could not be done without steps like this to pay tribute to 
those who were part of that.

Mr. Chris Harding , County Auditor, delivered a PowerPoint presentation 
on the Audit of Salt Lake County Golf Courses, which included the 
objectives; significant risks; Finding 1 - incomplete monthly reconciliations; 
Finding 2 - no review and sign -off on daily deposit documentation; Finding 3
