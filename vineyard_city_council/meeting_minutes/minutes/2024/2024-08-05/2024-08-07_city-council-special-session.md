 2.  BUSINESS ITEMS
     2.1. PUBLIC HEARING - Proposal to Consider a Change in Government
          This is the second of two (2) public hearings to receive public comment on a proposal
          to change the form of government from a five- to a six-member council. No action will
          be taken on this item.
Mayor Fullmer turned the time over to City Attorney Jayme Blakesley for an update.

     Mr. Blakesley gave a summary of the process for the proposed change in government.

Mayor Fullmer called for a motion to open the public hearing.


   Motion: COUNCILMEMBER RASMUSSEN MOVED TO OPEN THE PUBLIC HEARING
AT 4:49 PM COUNCILMEMBER SIFUENTES SECONDED THE MOTION. MAYOR
FULLMER, COUNCILMEMBERS, HOLDAWAY, RASMUSSEN, AND SIFUENTES VOTED
YES. COUNCILMEMBER CAMERON WAS EXCUSED. THE MOTION CARRIED
UNANIMOUSLY WITH ONE ABSENT.

Mayor Fullmer called for public comment.

    Resident Daria Evans, living in the Villas, requested that the council reconsider their decision
on changing the form of government.

Councilmember Carmeron joined the meeting at 4:51 PM.

    Resident Karen Cornileous, living in the Villas, agreed with Ms. Evans and felt that this
process needed more time, research, and more input from the community.

     Resident David Lauret, living on Holdaway Road, agreed with the previous speakers. He felt
that this was not the time to make this change and that the benefits would not be the ones they
thought they would gain.

    Resident David Pearce, living in The Cascade subdivision, felt that the choice to change the
form of government should be given to the citizens. He believed in the proposed form of
government. He also felt that the current form was not bad, but the people needed to be given the
choice.

    Resident Tim Blackburn, living in the Sleepy Ridge subdivision, agreed with everything that
had been said. He said that he did not believe that the six-council form was the best and felt that
they should put the decision on hold.

Mayor Fullmer called for a motion to close the public hearing.

    Motion: Councilmember Rasmussen moved to close the public hearing at 4:59 PM.
Councilmember Cameron seconded the motion. Mayor Fullmer, Councilmembers Cameron,
Holdaway, Rasmussen, and Sifuentes voted yes. The motion carried unanimously.

    Mayor Fullmer called for final comments from the council.

    Councilmember Holdaway stated that he was against moving ahead with the change in
government at this time due to the proposed tax increase and budget constraints.

    Councilmember Rasmussen thanked the public for sharing their comments.

    Councilmember Cameron noted that Vineyard was still new in their organized government.
She expressed concern with only being able to meet with three councilmembers to collaborate. She
understood that the mayor-council form could bring more contention.

    Councilmember Sifuentes agreed that they were handicapped with only being able to meet
with only one councilmember and the mayor at a time. She felt that it would be beneficial to have
the mayor, and two councilmembers meet to collaborate. She belied that having a better separation
between the legislative executive body sounded appropriate. She proposed putting this on the ballot
and that changing the form would improve their government.

     Councilmember Holdaway noted that the six-member form would increase their tax burden
but would not take effect until 2026. Mr. Blakesly clarified that the mayor would vote on the
appointment of city manager, to break a tie in council meetings, votes on any ordinance change
that affects the mayor’s powers and functions. The mayor would not vote on all other council
decisions such as: budget questions, routine resolutions, contract approvals, ordinances that affect
anything other than the mayor’s and responsibilities. The mayor would have executive, ceremonial,
and chair of the council who coordinates among the council to come to a consensus.

     Mayor Fullmer asked Councilmember Holdaway for clarification on his comment about the
council all being over legislative and executive authority. Councilmember Holdaway stated that in
the five-member council they all had executive power. Mr. Blakesley clarified the authority of the
council under all three forms of government. He explained that under all three types the mayor is
the executive officer.

    Mayor Fullmer asked about the tax increase for the six-member and noted that the mayor-
council would be an even hire increase. She asked if he meant to stay with the five-member
council. Councilmember Holdaway replied that he wanted to keep it a five-member form of
government for now and concentrate on more important things in the city. He thought that the six-
member council would be a better form of government but felt that they were three to five years
out before they should look at changing the form of government.
