    2. PRESENTATION OF THE SERVICES THAT THE WEBER/MORGAN HEALTH DEPARTMENT PROVIDES
       AND PROMOTES FOR THE COMMUNITY LIVING IN WEBER COUNTY

         Brian Bennion, Weber/Morgan Health Department Director, noted that there are a lot of community
         partners. He gave an overview of the many services that the Health Department provides, such as
         inspecting public water systems, inspecting restaurants, immunizations, the Women Infants and
         Children’s program, motor vehicle safety, issuing birth and death records, emergency preparedness.
         The commissioners expressed gratitude to Mr. Bennion.
    3. PRESENTATION OF THE STATE OF THE COUNTY
         Chair Gibson reported that the state or our county is extremely strong and the future is brighter than
         ever. The county has become one of the premier locations in the nation, to live and to do business.
         He referred to the diverse landscape and the unique balance of culture venues and arts, independently
         owned restaurants, farmlands, over 450 miles of trails, exciting nightlife, a backyard access to year-
         round high adventure and countless recreation opportunities. Our capital city has been changed for
         the better since the reopening of the Latter Day Saint Temple (with nearly 1 million visitors, more
         than to any single event in the county’s history, even the Olympics), and he expressed thanks to the
         leaders of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints for their tireless commitment to help us
         rebuild economically and spiritually. As a result of many citizen’s hard work, we have seen
         economic prosperity that is the envy of the nation and phrases such as “best place to live and work,”
         “best place to do business,” and the “best place for recreation and family” have been used to describe
         our community. He said that the County Commission is united in purpose and works hard to be
         fiscally prudent and have managed smarter without asking for more from taxpayers, who received an
         average decrease of $10/$100,000 residence as part of the Sheriff’s realignment of cost of service to
         cities. The county has healthy fund balances that exceed the industry’s standards and recently
         received the AAA bond rating for the second consecutive year and is one of only 7 counties west of
         the Mississippi with this type of rating. The county strives to increase transparency and in January
         became the first county in Utah to live-stream Commission meetings and more financial details are
         being presented in these meetings. He expressed thanks to the great public servants of the county,
         elected officials and citizens, including Lt. Nathan Hutchinson who was shot four times as he
         courageously rushed toward an active killer in an attempt to rescue his fallen comrades in 2012.
         Commissioner Bell expressed thanks for Commissioner Gibson’s leadership stating that he is
         president of UAC and puts in a lot of time into his many boards. Commissioner Bell said that newly
         elected Commissioner Ebert is a joy to work with and works very hard. Commissioner Ebert said
         that the Commission has a very strong vision; they are laying out a plan to meet present and future
         needs and they will continue to work diligently.

    1. AMENDMENT TO AN ASSESSMENT ORDINANCE RELATING TO THE SUMMIT MOUNTAIN ASSESSMENT
       AREA – ORDINANCE 2015-4
       John Bond, County Treasurer, stated that the county partnered with Summit Mountain Holding
       Group to help facilitate a bond that the owners in that area will pay back over 20 years. An
       Assessment Area was created and now constitutes several properties and the bondholders will receive
       an annual payment starting in July. He said that Zions Bank, county financial advisor, will help
       manage the billings and payments. Alan Westenskow, of Zions First National Bank Public Finance,
       said that when bonds were issued in August/September 2013 there were 1,700 acres with one owner
       and no development. The bonds were to finance infrastructure (roads/water/sewer), which would be
       ultimately owned by public government, to facilitate development of that area. Each of those
       properties has an assessment and is required to make payment on the bond; the county is not
       responsible to make the payment. As subdivisions are approved, the Assessment Ordinance will be
       amended and recorded so that prospective owners can know that there is an assessment on the
       property. There have been 115 properties platted thus far and will be recorded.
       Commissioner Bell moved to adopt Ordinance 2015-4 amending the Assessment Ordinance relating
       to the Summit Mountain Assessment Area; Commissioner Ebert seconded.
       Commissioner Bell – aye; Commissioner Ebert – aye; Chair Gibson – aye
