4. Public hearing regarding potential amendments to Section 11-6 of the Eastern 
Summit County Development Code for Minor and Major Solar Arrays. – Ray Milliner, 
Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner shared the background for this application. The Code amendments 
would add major and minor solar arrays to the Code for solar panels on homes or a major 
commercial solar facility. The proposed language would have Minor Solar Arrays as an 
Allowed Use and Major Solar Arrays as a Low Impact Use requiring a permit. 
Commissioner Sonntag stated there should be something in the Code for solar arrays, but 
the Code would not supersede stricter rules such as restrictions in CC&Rs and 
Development Agreements.  Planner Milliner will clarify that in the Code language. 
Commissioner Clyde stated the idea of having a solar panel on a pole six feet away from a 
property is unacceptable and a free-standing panel needs to be farther away. The wording 
of “generally” cannot be enforced and should be changed. The mitigation language is too 
general. Having solar panels twelve feet above the roof line is not acceptable, and the 
panels should be flat to the roof. There is not an adequate definition for “non-maintenance.” 
Rocky Mountain Power’s name should be taken out of the Code in case of a name change. 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
September 17, 2020 
Page 9 of 11 
 
Size matters. A major solar array should not be allowed in the Rural Residential 2.5 zone at 
all. There should be size limitations to solar arrays. The allowed size should be different for 
different zones. There is a worry about the zoning for windmills too. 
Commissioner Wilde stated he supports clean energy. There are a few solar arrays in 
Hoytsville, including panels on a shipping container. Solar panels belong on the roof of a 
residential home and should be allowed as what can practically fit on the roof of a home. 
Commissioner Wheaton stated her concern that a neighbor could decide to have forty 
acres of solar panels. The impact to neighbors needs to be considered. 
Commissioner Rydalch stated a Minor Array should not be allowed to be a stand-alone or 
freestanding structure. Major Arrays should not be allowed in the AG5 or smaller zones. 
Chair Sargent stated that solar panels can be ugly and should not protrude above the roof 
of a home. 
Commissioner Clyde stated that reflectivity should be taken into account in the 
Conditional Use process. There could be a template from other communities that could be 
used for the wording in the Code for solar arrays. 
 
The Public hearing was opened. No comment was made and the Public hearing was closed. 
 
Planner Milliner thanked the Planning Commission for their suggestions and summarized 
the changes that he would make. Major solar arrays will be eliminated from the 2.5 and 
AG5 zones. Minor solar arrays will only be allowed to follow the roofline, but could be 
allowed to be freestanding in the AG20 and larger zones. Freestanding arrays could be 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
September 17, 2020 
Page 10 of 11 
 
allowed through a Conditional Use Permit in smaller zones. Solar panels should be flush 
with a pitched roof and may be two to three feet above a flat roof with screening. The 
setback for a freestanding solar array needs to be increased. Other cities’ Codes will be 
looked at as a template. There will be another Public Hearing before the Major and Minor 
Solar Arrays are added to the Code.
