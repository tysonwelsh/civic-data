PLANNING ITEMS
   a. ZONE CHANGE
      The City has received a rezone application for a set of properties located on 600 E, and from 100
      S to almost Center Street along the Salt Creek. The owner currently owns two parcels in the area
      and is looking to split/reconfigure the parcels into three parcels.
      The applicant is ALLOWED by right to split the lots into either 2 or 3 parcels (if they meet the
      frontage, and minimum lot size requirements.
      Staff do not support this rezone for the following reasons:
      1. The applicant can still substantially have what they wish for in the current zone they are in.
      2. Staff is not in favor of “spot-zoning” only one property without an overall area cohesive plan.
      3. Staff feel the survey could be re-configured to make lots more desirable without the rezone.
      4. A hallmark of Nephi is their eclectic, creative lots. At times, the most creative lots have been
          the most desirable in the city.
      5. The applicant could approach bordering neighbors and offer to acquire additional land that
          would allow a more favorable configuration (with a boundary adjustment).
      Property owner, Addie Prost, addressed the council briefly concerning the zone change. The
      council decided to table the decision until the next meeting. Councilor Travis Worwood made a
      motion to table the decision. Councilor Jeramie Callaway seconded the motion. The motion
      passed on unanimous vote.

       A. STAFF PRESENTATION
               a. Seth Atkinson, City Administrator, explained the proposed sales tax bond to fund the
                   improvements at the golf course for the 9-hole expansion. Sales tax bonds have
                   been issued for past city projects including the splash pad and The Hive recreation
                   facility. These bonds are issued against the existing sales tax collections that are
                   already being received by the city.
               b. Mr. Atkinson then cleared up misconceptions concerning the bond.
                          i. A sales tax bond will not raise taxes for our residents. It is anticipated that
                             golf revenues would be the source to pay back the debt service with sales
                             taxes acting as a backstop. The city receives a better interest rate by using
                             the sales tax bond.
                             Councilor Tate Douglas explained the sales tax bond is like a co-signer on a
                             loan and only the co-signer pays if the loan applicant cannot. Here, only
                             sales tax will be used if the golf course cannot make the payment.
                         ii. Residents were concerned about the amount the city was subsidizing the
                             golf course each year. Mr. Atkinson displayed a graph and explained the golf
                             course has collected enough revenues over the last several years to be self-
                             sufficient. They pay for their utilities. City contributions of approximately
                             $200,000/ year have been used for capital improvements.
                        iii. Concerned residents wondered why the bonds could not be used to
                             construct an indoor pool. Mr. Atkinson reviewed past research on the costs
                             of constructing and operating an indoor pool. Despite revenues received for
                             their pools in other communities, pools are subsidized between $350,000 to
                             $500,000 per year, not counting funds for capital improvements or for debt
                             service costs to fund construction. The city’s outdoor pool is subsidized by
                             $100,000 per year for the 3 months it is open. Based on these costs, it is
                             very infeasible for the city to invest in a pool at this time. In addition, the
                             debt service for the sales tax bonds to be issued will be paid back with golf
                             revenues. It would not make financial sense to use golf revenues to pay for
                             an indoor pool.
                        iv. Troubled citizens were concerned over the $7.5 million dollar bond pricing.
                             Mr. Atkinson clarified this is simply an upper limit.
                                  1. The golf course project is estimated at $4.9 million.
                                  2. This bond is also to provide revenues to purchase Orgill Fields from
                                      the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints. This is a field that has
                                      been used for decades, under agreement with the Church, for
                           recreational purposes. The property has unexpectedly been offered
                           for purchase.
                       3. The city has also applied for a grant from the Land and Water
                           Conservation Fund for the golf course project. If any funds are
                           awarded to the city through this grant, the bond amount would be
                           reduced.
      c. The city has owned property for years with the specific purpose of expanding the
          golf course to eighteen holes. In the past, anemic revenues were a reason that
          project could not be pursued. Now that the golf course is performing at an elevated
          level and has the appropriate staffing to continue this trend, the Council has
          determined that this project is ready for implementation.

Work Session 9:07 pm
   1. Electric Rates
           a. Mr. Atkinson explained the need for a temporary rate adjustment for electric utility rates
               while waiting for the consultant. This bridge rate increase would be based on the current
               five-year plan that the city has been following. He would like to include the rate
               adjustment in the next fee resolution.
           b. The council discussed the importance of keeping fees relevant to the services provided
               to avoid subsidizing taxpayer dollars. Councilor Worwood mentioned that the city’s rates
               are about 30% lower than the rest of the intermountain west.
   2. Cemetery Overtime Fees
           a. City Recorder, Leslee Anderson discussed the need to adjust the cemetery overtime fees
               to better reflect costs. The current fee was a flat rate of $200. She proposed the rate be
               $200/hour.
           b. The council discussed the importance of maintaining reasonable fees for cemetery
               services but also expressed a desire to revisit the non-resident rate for burials and burial
               rights.
   3. Broadcasting Meetings
           a. The council discussed the pros and cons of broadcasting city meetings including
               potential for viral videos and the need for a digital meeting ordinance.
           b. Mr. Atkinson mentioned the importance of setting rules around public comment and
               participation.
           c. It was suggested to start with a cost-effective approach and expand as needed.
           d. The council agreed to explore options for broadcasting meetings while ensuring proper
               governance and community engagement.
