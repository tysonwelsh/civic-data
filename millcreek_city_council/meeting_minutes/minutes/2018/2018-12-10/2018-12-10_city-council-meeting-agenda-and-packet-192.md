(Single-Family Residential)
Blaine Gehring said Leslie Blackham is the owner of the property at 4284 South 900
East. A few years ago, she was able to purchase additional property behind her home and
has been renting the property for extra income. She would like to move her primary

residence to this address and keep horses on the property. This would require a rezone to
R-1-21 which requires a half acre of property and her property is .83 acres in size. The
keeping of up to four horses for private use only (not rentals) is a permitted use in the R-

2.2 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 18-74, Rezoning Certain Property
Located at 1136 E. 3300 S. from the C-2 (Commercial) and R-1-8 (Single-Family
Residential) to the R-M Zone (Multi-family Residential)

Blaine Gehring said the Millcreek Hollow Condominiums are located at 1136 East 3300
South with a parking lot and access off 1100 East at about 3335 South. The condos were
built in 2003 in Salt Lake County in a C-2 Zone with the parking lot and access off 1100
East in an R-1-8 Zone. The condos are considered legal non-conforming as neither use
conforms to Millcreek’s current zoning ordinance. The subject property consists of 3.36
acres with 96 condominium units. The condos and parking lot do not conform to the
current zone requirements of the C-2 and R-1-8 zones. The condos are multi-family
residential. Currently, the C-2 zone requires commercial mixed use if residential is
proposed for the zone. There is no commercial use in the condos. He said the parking and
access on 1100 East is in a single-family zone, and parking and access for a multifamily
use is not allowed in a single-family zone. Millcreek City is seeking a rezone of the
property and condos to align their use with the zone. The Millcreek Community Council
and Planning Commission sent favorable recommendations to approve the rezone. He
said two residents of the condos spoke in favor of the rezone at the community council
meeting. Mayor Silvestrini said this item is a correction zoning. Council Member Jackson
asked how many property owners there are. Gehring said there are 96 units and the Home
Owners Association (HOA). Lilly said there has been no formal report from the HOA on
the matter, but they were notified of the issue before the meetings started.

Mayor Silvestrini asked for public comment.

Cory Minson, 3417 Millbert Dr., said he participated in Envision Committee meetings
and they discussed a community of homes. He said the committee did not talk about high

cannot keep adding high density. He said his neighboring lot used to be a single-family
lot and it now has 6 housing units on it. He said it does not fit in the area. He asked the
Council to consider the density. Mayor Silvestrini said the Council is not contemplating
approving a new development, but an existing condominium project that is already built.
The application is to change the zone to conform to the City’s land use ordinances.
Council Member Jackson said it has been around for 15 years.

Council Member Marchant moved to approve Ordinance 18-74, so it will conform to
existing use as it has been done for many years changing it to the R-M Zone; item 2.2.
Council Member Jackson seconded.

Mayor Silvestrini thanked staff for pointing this out. He said the rezone would help the property
owners with possible refinancing and such than if their homes had remained nonconforming to
the zone. The City Recorder called for the vote. The motion passed unanimously.

2.3 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 18-75, Amending Code Chapters
19.04, 19.44, and 19.60 of with Respect to Regulating Licensed Massage Practices in
Millcreek

Francis Lilly said when Millcreek was incorporated, there was a restrictive policy on
massage therapy practices in the City; each massage therapist had to have their own
business license, each business licensee had to be separated from another business
licensee by 500 feet, there was an intersection perimeter setback of 300 feet from a
number of listed intersections in the City, and “massage” was not defined in code. The
Planning Commission made a recommendation for staff to come up with a definition for
a licensed massage therapy practice, and to do away with the 300-foot intersection
separation and replace it with 200-foot separation if the licensed massage therapy
practices are not in a multi-tenant office building. He said there were a couple of
commissioners that did not think the ordinance needed to change, some felt that no
restriction was necessary, and some felt some sort of intersection was required. He said
there was no formal reason from the Planning Commission as to why this ought to be.
Lilly said the same intersection separation requirement exists for businesses like title
lenders, pawn shops, smoke shops, and commercial storage units. He said the Council
considered a comprehensive update to commercial zones a year and a half ago and that is
when this restriction came in. Lilly said he learned a lot from working on this ordinance
and felt confident that staff could handle any problems that come up without having a
separation requirement. Lilly presented two ordinance options; option one considers a
200-foot separation requirement reflected in RM and Commercial zones, and option two
that does not contemplate a separation requirement. He said from an administrative
standpoint, staff can handle either option.

Lilly went through the proposed ordinance changes. Council Member Jackson asked
about hours of operations. Lilly said there are no restrictions on hours of operation and
they can now operate in the C-1 zone. Council Member Catten asked about other cities
having proximity restrictions in intersections. He said he could not find anything, but
South Salt Lake City caps the number of licenses issued. Council Member Catten asked if
any massage therapy businesses were currently in the restricted intersections. Lilly said
there are some. Mayor Silvestrini said two people, Suzanne Dunbar and Bijan Hosseini,
advocated for this ordinance to not have the proximity restrictions in the November 26,
2018 Council work meeting. He said Suzanne Dunbar had issues with the reported
number of licenses in Millcreek. Mayor Silvestrini had asked a planner to confirm the
number of license and to produce a map that showed the location of massage business in
Millcreek to determine if they were on commercial corners. He said that map needed to
be part of the record and it reflected a number of licenses closer to what Dunbar had
mentioned than what staff had provided before; closer to 40 than 60. Mayor Silvestrini
said the location of those businesses generally align with the commercial corridors.
Mayor Silvestrini mentioned that he and Council Member Jackson drove around
separately throughout the City and did not observe a great number of those business
congregated on the listed corners at this time. Council Member Jackson would prefer to
let the market dictate what businesses go where.

Mayor Silvestrini said he is inclined to vote for the ordinance without the proximity
restriction. He said he checked with the police force and with the Utah Division of
Occupational and Professional Licensing and there are hardly any instances of licensed

massage therapists engaging in unlawful conduct. The City does not restrict other
personal service businesses. Council Member Marchant said the Council received a letter
(dated 11/20/18) from Richard Merrill, a licensed massage therapist, and then read from
it, “Make no mistake we are as committed as you are to the elimination of the illicit
activity and use of massage as a cover for it. We should be partners in this not
adversaries.” Council Member Marchant said Merrill made four suggestions and he
would like staff to respond to. They are: to require every person doing massage therapy to
have a Millcreek City business license; control the signage, no suggestive text or partially
nude signs; review existing laws and develop prosecutorial means to enforce those laws,
and encourage the development of legal massage practices through appropriate zoning.
Lilly said the City is mostly in compliance with those suggestions. He said those who
practice massage therapy in Millcreek are required to have a business license. The
appropriate zoning issue is up to the Council to decide today. He said there are provisions
that give the City sufficient leverage to suspend or revoke a business license as
prosecutorial means to enforce illegitimate practices. He said obscenity is obscenity with
signage, but regulating signs based on content is tricky. Lilly said the professional
credibility of massage therapy is on the line.

Mayor Silvestrini asked the Council if they had a problem removing the proximity
restriction. Council Member Uipi said she would like more information from the
Planning Commission about their recommendation and mentioned that other cities do not
require a proximity restriction. Council Member Marchant said he saw the point of
keeping revenue building businesses in commercial corners, but the City does not
discriminate against other similar types of businesses. Council Member Catten said she
wished she understood the Planning Commission’s rationale on 200 feet instead of 300
feet. Council Member Jackson it was fair to let the market should dictate where
businesses go.

Mayor Silvestrini asked for a show of hands from the audience about their support of the
ordinance. The public supported the ordinance with no proximity restriction without any
opposition.

Joan Haven, 4219 S. Mars Way, said she did not see a difference in the two ordinance
proposals, they both show a proximity restriction. Mayor Silvestrini said as of today there
was a version that fixed some oversights. Council Member Jackson said the proximity
restrictions Haven saw in the ordinance was in reference to other businesses. Haven said
massage therapists are a healthcare profession and they should not be treated different
than any other. Council Member Uipi thanked her for her suggestion in changing the
word massage therapy establishment to massage therapy practice.

Ron Findly, representing the Utah Massage Professionals, said he applauded the Council.
He said Commissioner Carlson from the Planning Commission referred to his experience
as an attorney in South Salt Lake twenty years ago when this topic came before the
Planning Commission. He said Commissioner Carlson said Millcreek does not have a
problem with the profession, but the idea was to zone them out. The licensed massage
therapists thought his remarks were discriminatory. Findly reiterated that massage
therapists are recognized by the State as health care practitioners.
