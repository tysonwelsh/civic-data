 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
       a. Discussion of the Fiscal Year 2026 Tentative Budget
          Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck listed the following responsibilities of the
          Community Preservation Department: Business Licensing, Good Landlord Program (if
          approved by the Council), Neighborhood Improvement Program, Code Enforcement, and
          Administrative Law Hearings. Community Development Director Scott Langford added
          that short-term rentals would also fall under Community Preservation. Ms. Steck
          presented the FY2026 Tentative Budget for Community Preservation, which included
          funding for a Good Landlord Program Administrator.

          Ms. Steck listed responsibilities of Public Works Administration: Engineering, GIS, Solid
          Waste, Fleet, Streets, Class C Roads, and Road Capital Projects, and presented FY2026
          Tentative Budgets. She explained that 60% of Public Works Administration time was
          spent on activities outside the General Fund.

          Council Member Bloom joined at 4:06 pm.

          The Council and staff discussed staffing and equipment for the Streets Department. Ms.
          Steck agreed that setting aside reserves for an additional snowplow was a good idea. She
          explained that the Class C Roads Budget included a one-time transfer of $3,586,618 from
          Roads Capital Projects to accomplish desired projects. Responding to a question from Vice

          Chair Whitelock, Public Works Director Brian Clegg explained that staff were conducting
          a quiet-zone study for railroad work around 4000 West in-house, and did not need
          additional funds allocated for the study at that time. Ms. Steck listed project studies that
          would be supported by the Roads Capital Projects Fund in FY2026.

          Council Member Shelton requested the City add another cement crew to save money on
          contractors. Mr. Clegg said the department was currently two years out with cement work
          orders. Council Member Jacob requested to see cost and level of service numbers for
          utilizing contractors versus adding a crew in-house.

          Council Member Shelton believed residents would support a tax increase to fund such
          basic services. Vice Chair Whitelock said she would not be able to support a tax increase
          as long as the FY2026 Budget included “wants” as well as “needs”. Council Member
          Shelton felt Vice Chair Whitelock made a good point, and believed some of the “wants”
          may need to be cut to fund needed sidewalk work. Council Member Bedore commented
          that increased service often came with increased cost. The Council discussed funding for
          needed sidewalk work versus funding for more events already included in the Tentative
          Budget. Vice Chair Whitelock agreed with the request for a cost comparison of contractors
          versus an additional in-house cement crew.

          Chair Lamb said he heard more complaints from residents about streets than about
          sidewalks. Council Member Bedore said the number of complaints he heard about streets
          and sidewalks had been fairly equal.

          Council Member Green joined at 4:45 pm.

          The Council and staff reviewed the Public Utilities Administration Tentative Budget. Staff
          answered questions about overtime and department vehicles.

          Ms. Steck presented FY2026 Tentative Budgets for Public Services Administration, and
          the Cemetery. Staff said they estimated the cemetery would be full in five to six years. City
          Administrator Korban Lee said an internal study done two years ago showed the cost to
          expand the cemetery would far exceed potential revenues if plot sales stayed on the
          current trajectory. He emphasized the cemetery was currently subsidized by tax dollars.
          Ms. Steck said it would have been ideal if the City had adopted a perpetual care fund when
          the cemetery was first developed. Council Member Green asked if a perpetual care fund
          could be started and built up over three to five years. Mr. Lee gave an example of a $7
          million endowment for a perpetual care fund with an annual 5% return, and said
          perpetual care could be funded by the interest. Ms. Steck commented that the $7 million
          would be permanently set aside and untouchable.

          Council Member Green said some of the fees on the Consolidated Fee Schedule had not
          been adjusted in quite some time. He expressed the opinion that fees should be reviewed
          and adjusted every three to five years to cover rising costs. Ms. Steck said Mr. Lee asked
          all department heads to evaluate fees with the budget process every year. Vice Chair
          Whitelock said she would be in favor of drastically increasing cemetery fees for non-
          residents.

          Ms. Steck presented a Tentative Budget for Events. Council Member Green asked if staff
          were comfortable with the way in which Western Stampede parking revenue was
          accounted. Ms. Steck said she was not. Council Member Green expressed concern with a
          contractor collecting money for parking during Western Stampede, and suggested
          developing better accountability measures. Council Member Green suggested the small
          amount of revenue from event parking was not worth the time and trouble for staff and
          contractors. Council Member Jacob suggested raising the Western Stampede ticket price
          and making parking free. Council Member Bloom suggested finding a sponsor for the
          parking. Public Services Director Cory Fralick said he would like to see big community
          volunteer committee involvement. Mr. Lee suggested Mr. Fralick run a cost-benefit
          analysis. Council Member Shelton said he liked the idea of paid VIP parking and free
          general parking. A majority of the Council appeared to support an increased ticket price
          with the cost of parking incorporated. Mr. Fralick suggested completing a cost-benefit
          analysis this year, and continuing discussion after the analysis with preparations for the
          2026 Western Stampede.

          The Council and staff reviewed the FY2026 Tentative Budget for Facilities. Council
          Member Bloom asked the percentage of preventative versus reactive maintenance
          included in the budget. Mr. Fralick said the balance had been tipping toward reactive, and
          said he believed the planned transition to in-house custodial would help. Council Member
          Bloom said she was interested in reviewing a sheet showing the balance. Mr. Fralick said
          he believed the quality of service would also be improved with the transition to in-house
          custodial service. Council Member Green said he would rather spend $20,000 on new
          technology such as robotic lawnmowers and robotic snowblowers than on holiday lights.

          Council Member Bloom asked if the City had applied for state or federal grants relating to
          energy efficiency. Mr. Fralick spoke of rebates received for energy efficient measures.
          Council Member Bloom asked about the City’s back-up energy plans. Mr. Fralick said staff
          had been working on back-up energy sources.

          Ms. Steck presented FY2026 Tentative Budgets for Parks and Parks Capital. Council
          Member Bloom asked if there were areas of the City that were underserved with parks.
          Mr. Fralick said staff intended to update the Parks Master Plan next year. He said new
          park amenities were included in the FY2026 Tentative Budget that would revitalize some
          of the parks. The Council discussed existing City parks and planned projects.

          Ms. Steck presented the Police Tentative Budget. Council Member Shelton asked if the
          budget for overtime needed to be increased, and Deputy Police Chief Jeremy Robertson
          responded he was comfortable with the budgeted amount for FY2026. Staff answered
          questions about the arrangement between West Jordan and Utah Highway Patrol
          regarding Bangerter Highway. Council Member Green said he would like the City to have
          the ability to submit a bill to recover City costs incurred for highway incidents. Mr. Lee
          said staff would explore and expand the City’s ability to recover costs as broadly as
          possible. Council Member Green asked if hiring a couple additional officers would
          eliminate the need for overtime. Deputy Chief Robertson responded that overtime was
          primarily used to cover the SWAT Team or detectives when they were called out.
          Responding to a question from Council Member Bloom, Deputy Chief Robertson said

          funding was adequate for evidence handling, digital forensics, and crime lab needs.
          Deputy Chief Robertson said the Police Department’s biggest challenge was keeping up
          with the growth of the City in a responsible way.

          Deputy Chief Robertson answered questions about license plate readers and Axon
          DraftOne AI report-writer, which were requested by the Police Department, but not
          included in the FY2026 Tentative Budget. Deputy Chief Robertson emphasized the cost of
          the AI report-writer would increase in July if the City was not already signed up. He said
          the AI report-writer would potentially save the City money in the long run by improving
          time management and reducing the need to hire more staff. Deputy Chief Robertson
          emphasized that, while the AI program would help save the officers time in writing
          reports, the department would still need to employ enough officers to manage multiple
          incidents and scenes.

          Council Member Green spoke of the need to balance the growth of City departments with
          the growth of population and property tax revenue. Council Member Jacob emphasized
          that new growth meant increased demand for existing services. Council Member Green
          stated new growth should cover the cost of expansion of City services, unless the City was
          already behind. Council Member Bloom asked if reserves could be put toward the
          unfunded license plate readers and AI program, and Ms. Steck responded that both items
          were ongoing expenses, not one-time expenses.

          Chair Lamb recessed the meeting at 6:38 pm and noted the intent to reconvene
          after the agency meetings.

          The Committee of the Whole reconvened at 8:31 pm with all members of the
          Committee present.

          Ms. Steck presented the FY2026 Tentative Budgets for Animal Services, Crossing Guards,
          and SWAT, and answered questions from the Council.

          Ms. Steck and Fire Chief Derek Maxfield presented the FY2026 Tentative Budget for the
          Fire Department. Ms. Steck said she recently learned an additional $600,000 was needed
          in the budget to fully fund the purchase of a tiller truck at time of order, or the City could
          defer payment until delivery for an increased cost. Council Member Green said it made
          sense to him to use reserves to pay at time of order. Mr. Lee expressed concern with
          making the decision quickly, and commented that the City’s current tiller had been a
          challenge to maintain and had spent quite a bit of time in the shop. He suggested a cost-
          benefit analysis on a tiller versus a traditional ladder truck or regular engine. Chief
          Maxfield explained differences between the vehicles, and emphasized a tiller provided
          the ability to make tighter turns to get into places a traditional truck could not. He said
          his department had a fleet replacement plan that included a reserve ladder truck. Council
          Member Shelton spoke in favor of considering the less-expensive purchase of a used
          vehicle. The Council and staff discussed the potential high cost and inconvenience of
          maintenance and repairs on a used vehicle.

            Chief Maxfield spoke of extensive cancer screening now required by the State for
            firefighters, and answered questions about overtime. Council Member Bloom asked if a
            non-ambulance vehicle could be used for some of the medical calls to save wear and tear
            on the ambulance. Chief Maxfield explained planned changes to dispatch coding to
            improve acuity and response. Chief Maxfield explained plans for Emergency Management
            in FY2026.

            Ms. Steck summarized one-time revenue ($1,610,194) and one-time expenditures
            ($1,451,441) included in the FY2026 Tentative Budget. Responding to a question from
            Council Member Bloom, Ms. Steck said a 1% property tax increase would equal
            approximately $200,000 in revenue. Adding a new concrete crew and equipment, two
            police officers, the Axon DraftOne AI report-writer, and license place readers to the
            FY2026 budget could be funded with a 4% property tax increase. Council Member Shelton
            commented that West Jordan provided residents with excellent value for money
            compared to other taxing entities.

            Ms. Steck said the next budget discussion would begin with review of the Fleet FY2026
            Tentative Budget.
