1. Discussion regarding Chapter 3 of the Eastern Summit County Development Code – 
Ray Milliner, Principal Planner  
 
Chair Brown welcomed all present.  He said the Planning Commission instructed Staff to 
review the zoning of the North Summit and the South Summit areas independently.  Tonight 
Staff is reporting back with some additional ideas they had.  He turned the time over to 
Community Development Director Patrick Putt.

Director Putt introduced himself and Planning and Zoning Administrator Peter Barnes to the 
public.  He said the maps they will be using are a result of Administrator Barnes’ work.  The 
public was invited to come to the table in order to view the maps.  He asked Administrator 
Barnes to give an overview of the exhibits.   
 
Administrator Barnes said almost the entire eastern part of the County is included on the 
maps he has created.  He pointed out the main geographical features of the maps, such as 
Echo Reservoir and Coalville City.  He hasn’t included Woodland or Henefer in the mapping 
exercise.  He said one map is from Coalville to Wanship and the other is the Kamas Valley.       
 
Administrator Barnes said two of the maps show the existing zoning.  Another map 
demonstrates the geographical features such as steep hillsides, rivers, and roads.  The current 
highway corridor (HC) zone line (at 250 feet) is outlined in yellow.  An outer dashed line 
shows what the HC zone would look like at 500 feet on each side of the highway.  At the last 
meeting, the Commission recommended Staff begin with the old HC zone.   
 
Administrator Barnes distributed a copy of the maps to the public.  He said the wetlands, 
rivers, steep slopes, and flat lands have been added.  This will allow them to study the 
impacts of any zoning changes.  They need to communicate with the cities what their plans 
are.  Some of the cities will be impacted.     
 
The North Summit area was discussed.  Administrator Barnes said he left the 250 foot 
corridor on the west side and 500 foot on the east side.  From the current HC zone, they leap 
to the AG-40 zone.  Many of these lots range in size from 5 to 20 acres.  Most are less than 
40 acres.  It seemed to make sense to put a buffer zone between the HC and the 40-acre zone.  
They are going to call the buffer zone, the AG-6 zone.  He showed where this zone is laid out 
on the map.  Behind the 40-acre zone, would be the AG-80 zone.     
 
Administrator Barnes said certain things leapt out when they started to study the details.  One 
of these is that Wanship is a historic town.  It was laid out and platted as a town.  It has 
freeway access.  It has road intersections.  If they are going to give opportunities to increase 
and develop, it would make sense to do this around an existing town.  He added that 
Coalville has ambitions of expanding.     
 
Chair Brown reminded everyone they are keeping agriculture as the underlying zoning base.  
The AG -6 zone would allow for some farming and a home.  The landowner can retain the 
greenbelt status.  Administrator Barnes said he, Director Putt, and Planner Ray Milliner have 
been debating where to draw the buffer zone lines.  There are places where high density 
makes sense.  There are places it does not.  He would like to see the towns concentrated, but 
this is something the Commission will debate in these meetings. 
 
Administrator Barnes said currently Eastern Summit County doesn’t have a commercial 
zone; however, from the Hoytsville church to Wanship there is an area of home businesses.  
This may be a good area for a commercial use zone.

Director Putt said he grew up in a rural area that was similar to Coalville and Hoytsville.  
There was a development pattern that was typical for many rural areas.  Along with a small 
group of homes at the crossroads, there were local businesses, offices, and shops that were 
intended to serve the immediate neighborhood.  The businesses were clustered and were 
close enough he could walk to them.  They didn’t have to drive to town for all of their needs.  
He added this is something he would like to explore.  He would like to receive input from the 
public and the Commission on this idea.  This area could be designated as light neighborhood 
commercial or even office uses.   
 
A member of the public, Bill Wilde, asked if that would restrict residential development in 
the area.  Director Putt said it wouldn’t have to.  It would depend on how the uses were 
defined.  It would be his recommendation that residential uses would still be allowed, but 
there would be a broader pallet of uses that could be incorporated.

throughout Eastern Summit County in great detail.  Staff is wary of leaking density in the 
middle of the valley and establishing a kind of “no-where-ville.”  He noted there are two 
anchor points on each end of the North Summit area.  These are Wanship and Coalville.  
Where the existing towns are makes sense in terms of geography.

The South Summit area was next discussed along with where the AG-1 and AG-6 zones 
would be located.  Administrator Barnes said as he was drawing these lines in, he tried to 
recognize existing ownerships and lot sizes.  He asked himself how they could encourage 
new zoning where it makes sense, taking into account the sensitive lands.  If there are non-
conforming lots, how many could they bring into conformity?   He is trying to create a base 
zoning map that will give them a sense of where they are today with a clue of where they 
want to be decades from now.   
 
Director Putt said that upon hearing both points of view, they will bring back two maps.  One 
will be a map of what the HC zone looked like previously.  The second will be the exercise 
that Administrator Barnes was talking about, which is to try to come up with zoning in the 
right places while attempting to solve the non-conforming lot size.  They will try to be 
consistent with the annexation declaration areas (ADA).  They will also recognize that 
growth needs to occur along the available infrastructure.  They will take into account the 
physical characteristics of the wetlands and steep hills.   
 
Director Putt said it is important that everyone understand this is a new starting point.  There 
will be other opportunities to build beyond the starting point.  He explained they would like 
to increase density, but put it in the right locations.  The base zoning will be agriculture.   
 
Director Putt said the Commission has recognized they will be growing residentially, but 
there are no residential zones.  To move to one of these zones, the landowner would be 
required to go through the rezoning process.  At some point, the Commission is going to ask 
the County Council to adopt a new zoning ordinance and a new zoning map.  They hope this 
will solve problems without creating more.   
 
Director Putt said one thing they haven’t talked about is what could happen in the AG-6 
zone.  As Administrator Barnes has stated, this is a transition zone.  He has asked himself if 
there is a way to create incentive and flexibility.  He wondered if this is something they could 
look at on a case-by-case basis.   
 
Director Putt said another item Staff and the subcommittee have discussed is the idea of a 
residential overlay zone.  This would be an opportunity to design a development in the right 
spot.  What they have come up with is a residential master plan overlay.  The way it would 
work is if someone had land in the AG-1 and AG-6 zones, they could apply for a master plan 
rezoning.  It would place the development where there would be sufficient access, utilities, 
water, waste water, and power.  This would be done in a comprehensive manner.     
 
The landowner would come with a unified approach to develop the property.  This would 
take into account the characteristics of the land.  Is it wet?  Is it steep?   The landowner could 
possibly achieve a three-to-one density.  Currently, the landowner is tied to strict standards 
such as parcel size, lot frontage, and setbacks.  Through the master plan process, there might

available.  He said they may want density, but they don’t want those kinds of issues.  
Director Putt said he will research to see if that could be a result.     
 
Commissioner Wharton said perhaps they should simultaneously look at creating a graduated 
residential overlay zone.  Chair Brown said one of the goals of reconfiguring the zoning 
districts was to create a higher level of conformity in all zones.  He has thought about this 
concept over the last couple of weeks.  He believes there is some validity in breaking this 
into two 250 segments because there are a lot of existing homes that are currently in a non-
conforming status.  Between Wanship and Coalville there are over 250 houses that are non-
conforming.  Most of the non-conforming lots are in the HC and the AG-40 zones.  When the 
HC zone was altered from ½ acre to 1 acre, a lot of non-conforming lots were created.     
 
Commissioner Wharton said Staff should prepare a map with a 250-foot-deep residential 
zoning map.  Chair Brown said they have to be careful because there are some agricultural 
areas that would find themselves nonconforming in both use and with structures.  
Commissioner Hanson asked if they could establish the zone by date.  Director Putt said this 
could set them up with the same problem they are trying to fix.   
 
Director Putt said he would like to add two things to the conversation.  The first is to zone it 
what you mean and expect to see it.  He told Commissioner Vernon he hears his concerns,

Director Putt said one of the purposes of zoning is to describe what goes where and how 
much.  Zoning is a great tool for establishing predictable value.  It is a way of designating 
what is going to happen in the future.  At the fundamental level, it is a tool in implementing
