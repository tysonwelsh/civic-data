Proposed Resolution 2020-14 prohibiting the use of ignition sources
including fireworks, lighters and matches in certain areas of the City through
September 20, 2020
          A memo from Council staff came before the Council to consider a resolution enacting firework regulations. The memo
stated the Council office received a request from the Administration to consider a resolution prohibiting the use of fireworks,
lighters, matches, and other ignition sources in various areas of the City. The Administration also requested expedited
consideration given the high fire danger that currently exists.
          Fire Chief Mathieu summarized the memo and summarized the request that the Council designate areas within the City
where fireworks, matches, or other ignition somces are prohibited. He used the aid of a map of the City to identify the areas
recommended for restrictions as follows:
               • All areas east of Harrison Boulevard from the north city boundary to the south city boundary;
               • All wooded areas along the Ogden and Weber River Parkway beginning at Harrison Blvd and continuing along
                   the pathways to the west city boundary and the south city boundary and all associated parks along the River
                   corridor;
               • All of Fort Buenaventura, the city baseball park and dog park area located off A Avenue;
               •   The old landfill property located at approximately 2550 Avenue near Fort Buenaventura;
               • All open fields, vacant lots, wooded areas, and brush covered hillsides throughout the city.
          Chief Mathieu concluded that if the proposed resolution is adopted, exceptions to the restrictions may be authorized by
permit issued by the City Fire Marshal. The restrictions are recommended to last until September 20, 2020 or until conditions
improve.
          Chair Choberka introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2020-14, entitled:
                  "A resolution of the Ogden City Council prohibiting the use of ignition sources including frreworks, lighters
                   and matches in certain areas of the City through September 20, 2020."

Mayor Comments
         Mayor Caldwell thanked the Police Department for providing an enviromnent where residents can share their viewpoint
and protest any matter they are opposed to. He added that he has been so impressed by the manner in which the Police Department
responded and carried themselves in the wake of Officer Ly day's death; he also appreciated the Council's support during that time
and their attendance at events organized to honor him and cmmnemorate his passing. He stated there are many differing opinions
and the best way to express them is to get involved and respectfully communicate their position. He stated he is grateful for the
support of the community at this time and he is proud to serve as a representative of this great City.

Council member Comments
          Council member Lopez reported the Council received communication from a resident, Ralph Price, drawing their
attention to an organized protest occurring on the steps of the Municipal Building at this time.
          Council member White stated that like Mayor Caldwell, she also could not be more proud to be a resident of Ogden City.
However, in light of what is occurring locally and across the nation, she has recognized the damage created by inaction; she has
realized she has the ability to use her white privilege to help address racial injustices. It is necessary to move from Newton's frrst
law to Newton's second law; she is cormnitted to becoming educated and doing more. She thanked the Black Lives Matter
movement for forcing her to sit in her discomfort and move in the right direction at this very important time in history.
          Council member Stephens stated he is also very proud of the community and thankful for their respect as the City is
morning the loss of Officer Lyday. He referenced comments made by Officer's Lyday's mother during his funeral and stated he
hopes that everyone can band together to combat injustices and endeavor to unite. He stated that the Council and Administration
must work together to dete1mine the best way to proceed; some changes will be needed and this can start with trust,
understanding, and compromise. He has also tried to familiarize himself with the Black Lives Matter movement; they are working
towards a meaningful goal that he supports. He added that all life matters and he would encourage all people to come together to
ensure that the loss of valuable life is not in vain.
          Council member Hyer stated he does not want to make comments based on current emotion; however, he has read the
comments that have been emailed to the entire Council and wishes to formulate a well thought out response.
          Chair Choberka reflected on Officer Lyday's death; it is a very difficult time for the City and she agrees that it is not
possible to understand the burden carried by a police officer without having ever served in that position. She also believes that it is
appropriate to engage in discussions about changes that could be made to address the request for community prograrmning aimed
at addressing violence or achieving reform. She is cmmnitted to participating in those conversations.
