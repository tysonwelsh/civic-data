26          Project CUP-22-011 – A Public Hearing and Possible Action on a Request by
27          Katherine Pasker, on Behalf of Robert Bryne, for a Wall Height Extension to
28          Construct an Eight-Foot Wall Along the North Property Line at 7275 South Caladora
29          Cove.
30
31   Community and Development Director, Michael Johnson presented the Staff Report and stated
32   that the above item is a request for a wall height extension to construct an eight-foot wall along
33   the north property line at 7275 South Caladora Cove. Wall height extension involves a process in
34   the Ordinance that allows residential properties to build fences or walls or combinations of both at
35   heights greater than the normal six feet in the side or rear yard. A property rendering was
36   displayed. Mr. Johnson stated that the summary reflects approval to construct portions of a side
37   yard fence up to eight feet in height with portions that are six feet in height. Staff measured and
38   verified that the fence does not exceed eight feet at the highest point.
39
40   Mr. Johnson stated that a fully detailed project background is included in the Staff Report. The
41   most recent Appeals Hearing Officer determined that the fence that was constructed violates the
42   Ordinance. The applicant did not obtain a Building Permit or a Conditional Use Permit for the
43   eight-foot portion. Nevertheless, the City is required to accept and process the application.
44   Relevant Ordinance references were identified. Mr. Johnson reported that there are two required
45   criteria for wall height extensions. The first is the existence of a unique or special circumstance
46   related to the property that would be substantially minimized or eliminated by the increased height.

11          Project ZTA-22-003 - A Public Hearing and Possible Action on a City-Initiated
12          Ordinance Amendment Related to the Commercial Use Requirements in the Mixed
13          Use (“MU”) Zone.
14
15   Mr. Johnson presented the Staff Report and stated that the above matter is a City-initiated
16   amendment to the Mixed-Use Ordinance. The City Council directed Staff to provide an
17   implementation of the vertical mixed-use required in the Mixed-Use Zone. Specifically, ground-
18   floor retail in mixed-use projects should be stricter than in the current City Code. The General
19   Plan that is in progress includes a complete overhaul of the Mixed-Use Ordinance to a more form-
20   based concept. He explained that what is proposed will serve as an interim amendment until the
21   General Plan process is complete. The current zoning was reviewed. Mr. Johnson reported that
22   what is being recommended does not necessarily dissuade future applications but will impact
23   residential products.
24
25   The proposed language will state that commercial uses are required for all development and
26   redevelopment projects that are approved under this section. Commercial or retail uses shall
27   occupy a minimum of seventy percent (70%) of the width of the ground floor of buildings along
28   the project’s street frontage with a minimum depth of forty feet (40’). For projects that include
29   multiple parcels, calculations shall be based on the total project frontage.
30
31   Live/work units may be used to meet the requirement for commercial uses under this Section,
32   subject to the following requirements:
33
34      •   The live/work unit must provide direct access to the primary street frontage and/or public
35          parking areas;
36      •   Excluding private garage space, the entire ground floor of the live/work unit must be
37          designed, constructed, and used as commercial space;
38      •   Commercial spaces within the live/work unit that are used to satisfy the minimum
39          commercial frontage requirements of this chapter may not be used for residential parking
40          or storage;
41      •   The live/work unit’s first story must be a minimum of twelve feet (12’) in height, measured
42          from the finished floor elevation; and
43      •   Parking provided for the live/work unit must meet both the residential and commercial
44          requirements of this chapter.

36          3.1       Project CUP-22-011 – A Public Hearing and Possible Action on a Request by
37                    Katherine Pasker, on Behalf of Robert Bryne, for a Wall Height Extension to
38                    Construct an Eight-Foot Wall Along the North Property Line at 7275 South
39                    Caladora Cove.
40
41   Community and Economic Development Director, Michael Johnson presented the Staff Report
42   and stated that the above matter involves a request for a wall height extension to construct an eight-
43   foot wall along the north property line at 7275 South Caladora Cove. Due to previous enforcement
44   and appeal measures, the City is required to accept the application retroactively and process it as
45   they would a new application. Staff measured the entire constructed fence and determined that it
46   does not exceed eight feet at any point. A portion of the fence is fully compliant with City Code



     Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 10/05/2022                                         4
 1   but where the property slopes and the fence exceeds six feet, Conditional Use approval is required.
 2   Renderings of the site were presented. He reported that the request requires a Conditional Use
 3   Permit and a Building Permit but no neighbor consent.
 4
 5   Mr. Johnson reviewed the measurements of the fence and stated that there are two required criteria
 6   for wall height extensions. The first is the existence of unique or special circumstances of an
 7   adverse nature relating to the property that will be substantially minimized or eliminated by the
 8   increased height of the requested fence. The second is that the erection of such a fence is the most
 9   reasonable solution under the circumstances. He reported that there have been 15 applications for
10   wall height extensions since 2019, all of which have been approved. Privacy was used as a valid
11   rationale. Staff recommended public comment be allowed tonight with deliberation and a decision
12   to be made at the next Planning Commission Meeting. He recommended approval with conditions
13   set forth in the Staff Report.
14
15   Commissioner Chappell asked if there is a precedent or penalty for building without a permit.
16   Mr. Johnson stated that it is generally a Code Enforcement action where a Notice of Violation is
17   issued and the citation process initiated. If the situation persists, the matter could end up in court.
18   One of the appeals was that there was an open violation for a fence exceeding six feet in height
19   having been constructed without approval. Initially, Staff refused to accept the Conditional Use
20   application. The decision was appealed and the City’s Appeals Hearing Officer determined that
21   the application must be accepted. In the end, the City did not have the authority to refuse to hear
22   the request.
23
24   Senior City Planner, Samantha DeSeelhorst, explained that on a case-by-case basis, the Code
25   Enforcement Department will sometimes use financial citations but depends on the situation. She
26   considered the citation process to be a helpful tool to discourage construction prior to submitting
27   an application.
28
29   Commissioner Steinman shared Commissioner Chappell’s concern with moving ahead and asking
30   for forgiveness later. He recognized that it is not a penal situation and the Commission is tasked
31   with applying rules fairly without being arbitrary and capricious. In this case, the applicant knows
32   the process, rescinded the application, and proceeded anyway. Mr. Johnson reminded the
33   Commission that their purview is as the Land Use Authority.
34
35   Katherine Pasker identified herself as the legal counsel for the applicants, Robert and Ashley
36   Burns. Their comments have been submitted and provide additional context on the situation. They
37   appreciated the Commission’s frustration but believed there was a basis in the Code and confusion
38   regarding how to measure the fence height. Structures are based on an average height, which was
39   what Mr. Burns understood would be acceptable. After speaking with her clients and City Council,
40   the applicants voluntarily initiated the filing of a new application. She noted that the point of City
41   Enforcement is to encourage compliance. In this case, the applicant tried to come into compliance
42   on their own.
43
44   Commissioner Ebbeler stated that he read the neighbors’ comments and empathized greatly with
45   their concerns. The frustration pertained to an issue with a barking dog. He considered this to be
46   a Code issue and not necessarily open for Commission consideration.

 4   Kyle Fielding identified himself as an attorney representing the neighbors, Mr. and Mrs. Yaganti

38          3.2     Project ZTA-22-003 - A Public Hearing and Possible Action on a City-Initiated
39                  Ordinance Amendment Related to the Commercial Use Requirements in the
40                  Mixed Use (“MU”) Zone.
41
42   Mr. Johnson presented the Staff Report and stated that the above matter involves a public hearing
43   and possible action on a City-initiated Ordinance Amendment related to the Mixed-Use Code
44   requiring ground-level commercial. The desire is to avoid mixed-use projects that are
45   predominantly residential at ground level. The update to the General Plan will include a new




     Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 10/05/2022                                        6
 1   Mixed-Use Development Ordinance. The text amendment will function as a temporary measure
 2   until that time.
 3
 4   The Mixed-Use Ordinance defines a mixed-use building as a single building that contains more
 5   than one type of land use or development with more than one building. Mr. Johnson reported that
 6   what is being proposed are requirements that would mandate commercial uses be developed in any
 7   mixed-use project and occupy at least 70% of the width of the ground floor of buildings along
 8   project street frontage along with a depth of 40 feet. Additional language clarified what live/work
 9   units must include. He noted that what is proposed is an effort to mandate ground floor commercial
10   without mandating vertical mixed-use in every case and ensuring that the ground level of mixed-
11   use projects remains commercial.
