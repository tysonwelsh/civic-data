          1.2    GENERAL PLAN HISTORY – Community and Economic Development
                 Director, Michael Johnson.

Community and Economic Development Director, Michael Johnson presented an overview of the
statutory requirement for a General Plan as well as Cottonwood Heights’ existing General Plan.
He stated that a General Plan is an overarching vision document that any city with land use
authority uses to guide decisions and policy regarding a variety of functions including:

      •   Land use;
      •   Growth and development;
      •   Housing;
      •   Transportation;
      •   Economic development;
      •   Parks, trails, and open space;
      •   Water usage; and
      •   Sustainability.

Mr. Johnson explained that a General Plan is also a high-level document that provides a 20 to 30-
year view into the future of a municipality. The State of Utah requires the following elements to
be included in all new General Plans:

The original General Plan was adopted in 2005 after the City’s incorporation and encapsulates the
other plans within the City, which were adopted and created through the public process.
Recommendations from those plans were utilized as the basis for the current draft. Mr. Johnson
reviewed the General Plan timeline. He reported that the project began in 2021 with the review of
existing plans to create a baseline for recommendations in the various areas located within the City
and included in the General Plan. Staff met with various City committees to receive feedback
followed by the publication of a project website sharing preliminary General Plan goals via City
social media profiles. A General Plan Steering Committee was created and was comprised of
representatives from the City Council, Planning Commission, Open Space Committee, the
Business Association, and Ben Hill from the Parks and Recreation Service Area.

The first public Open House was held in March 2022 where conceptual ideas and scenarios were
presented for feedback and comments. Mr. Johnson received multiple comments from residents
who emphasized the importance of involving the City Council and Planning Commission prior to
the implementation of the formal public input process. He considered public input to be critical to
the success of any General Plan and felt they had listened closely and implemented feedback.

After the current meeting, Staff will prepare early draft materials to present a complete draft of the
General Plan at a September Public Open House. The Planning Commission process will follow
at which time public input will be welcomed and forwarded to the City Council who will also
receive public input eventually leading to a decision. The City’s website was described. Public
comments, community ideas, and project boards were provided. Mr. Johnson reported that the
Planning Commission and City Council are the City’s leadership and land use authority. He
emphasized the importance of their ownership and input in the final General Plan.

Mr. Johnson reported that the City is tasked by the State with accommodating growth and housing
types but has not yet received specific direction. He asked for input regarding the handling of the
rapid growth rate throughout the State and region. Various options were proposed. It was
anticipated that the population will double by the year 2040. He emphasized the importance of
planning accordingly. An important question the plan seeks to address is how Cottonwood Heights
should respond to the rapid growth rate being experienced throughout the State and region.
Cottonwood Heights is a mature community with limited growth and development opportunities.
Less than 7% of the City is undeveloped and most is concentrated in the 200-acre gravel pit that
is situated at the base of Big Cottonwood Canyon.

Concept scenarios were next presented. Mr. Johnson stated that four scenarios were created with
one being eliminated prior to public input as it was not viable. The remaining three were presented
for public input at the March 2022 Open House. One scenario was not recommended or supported
by the public and had since been eliminated. The two options eliminated focused growth and
intensity on very few sites as it would create a disproportionate impact on certain areas of the City
with traffic and quality of life issues.

The first of the two remaining scenarios included a focus on redevelopment sites, the Fort Union
corridor, and appropriate redevelopment to create a Town Center. The gravel pit site may be
constructed with higher density in a manner that does not directly impact City residents. A longer-
range focus on other Fort Union commercial areas was considered. The second scenario included
a focus on spreading growth out to create less of an impact on any one place within the City. This
allows for the maintenance of character and viability of the single-family neighborhoods while
still focusing growth and development opportunities on the target areas. This will also focus on
smaller neighborhood pocket areas. Mr. Johnson recommended that when continuing discussion,
the perspective remain long-range and general.

        1.3      Cottonwood Heights Tomorrow- Guided Discussion of Cottonwood Heights
                 Tomorrow Draft Materials, by Council District.

Council Member Birrell reported that she has studied the Cottonwood Heights Master Plans
extensively and was hopeful that the focus would remain on citizen input and their desires for the
City. She commented that the first and second scenarios reflect aggressive growth for the City.
She also reviewed the results of the Vision Compensation Survey. While the gravel pit may seem
like a natural location for high density, she believed that Scenarios 1 or 2 will be contrary to what
the public has been demonstrating. She recommended the discussion include a process that
identifies what the existing residents want for the City. It was her understanding the two scenarios
are based on the addition of 11,000 households over the next 28 years. She reiterated the need for
further discussion to determine reasonable growth. She commented that office space should also
be considered and raised the question of the City wanting more office space and commercial.

Mr. Johnson reported that the scenarios came from the City Council and the public. The ideas
were intended to encourage discussion. The figures presented are based on growth within the City
and State and are obligated to plan for a variety of housing plans to accommodate that growth.

Council Member Bracken stated that the question to the public should be to clarify the growth they
wish to see and tie it to a result. If a zero-growth plan is considered, the City will need to consider
the resulting consequences while still considering revenue to pay Staff, Police, Fire, and Public
Works needs. He believed that the dissemination of information should include defining terms to
provide clarity prior to being presented to the public.

Mayor Weichers stated that the purpose of the General Plan should be clarified as well as the State
requirements. He noted that if the City is not compliant, the land use may be revoked. Mr. Johnson
explained that the purpose of the General Plan is as a City-wide, long-range Master Plan that

focuses on the future of land use, transportation, water, and housing. The plan makes long-range
recommendations for how a city is going to address land use challenges and opportunities,
transportation issues, the demand for water usage, and to address the housing affordability issue.
The State has yet to place a number on planning but has focused primarily on the housing
affordability issue and requiring municipalities to plan for a variety of housing types to meet the
needs of people of various income levels. A five-year plan must be in place with an
implementation strategy. Housing components were reviewed.

Growth was next discussed. Planning Commissioner, Sean Steinman, stated that with the two
scenarios and the potential for a zero-growth proposal if there are case studies of cities that have
seen zero growth.

Council Member Petersen described a recent conversation regarding growth when former City
Treasurer and Financial Reporting Manager, Dave Muir, emphasized that the City will suffer if
there is no more growth. He believed that responsible growth in a primarily built-out city will
require certain situations be revisited and sustainable growth must be considered.

Council Member Birrell was not advocating zero growth and emphasized the need for attainable
housing units. She believed that lower-income individuals and the elderly should be the primary
focus.

Commissioner Mills suggested that care be taken when comparing Cottonwood Heights to
adjacent municipalities. Proximity to the canyons and views allows the City to attain unique access
to the public from a revenue-generating standpoint. He was recently introduced to a concept
addressing those who fall within the moderate-income level and supported discussing the
conceptual view of what is taking place currently. He expressed concern with a homogenized
group within a community and suggested a pathway for those who wish to live in Cottonwood
Heights. The use of a main street versus a downtown concept was preferable. He understood that
funds will come from property taxes or through a certain amount of economic growth. He
suggested caution be used when considering locations and that there be a balance between
economic and population needs.

Commissioner Chappell stated that affordability for those entering the home market is important
and she supported making the City accessible for the young and old. She liked the idea of making
Cottonwood Heights a thriving City with sustainable growth while considering smart, intentional
growth that preserves a vibrant community for future generations.

Chair Allen stated that when they only have a perspective of what is in their backyard, they miss
the opportunity to plan for an entire community. Without a Master Plan, there may never be an
opportunity for smart growth to take place. He considered it short-sighted and unfortunate for
those wishing to reside in the City and emphasized the need to plan for smart growth in the right
places without negatively impacting the character of neighborhoods. He supported allowing for
enough growth to support new business and housing types while determining the best locations.

Commissioner Ebbeler stated that it is important when evaluating their responsibilities and duties
to understand the requirements the State has charged the City with when creating a long-range

General Plan and growth and development for all. He emphasized the need to plan for growth
while considering current conditions and maintaining realistic development. The Utah Code menu
items suggest a change in density to allow affordability for those with moderate income levels. He
acknowledged the various housing types and cost benefits in terms of revenue generated from each
type of property versus the cost to the City of providing services. It was noted that the greatest cost

Council Member Petersen stated that when considering affordability, the 1700 East Fort Union
Boulevard Project included 23 homes with rents of $2,700 to $2,800. He did not believe that
density necessarily represents affordability and found it difficult to understand how 80% can live.
He believed that viability and growth is complex and he was in favor of commercial remaining in
the City with the tax base being of utmost importance.

Mayor Weichers stated that those present are elected officials. He understood that the consensus
seemed to focus on maintaining the current community while supporting strategic growth. The
importance of maintaining Cottonwood Heights as a suburb with growth that does not impact
neighborhoods was also stressed.

Council Member Birrell preferred the term “smart growth” and suggested that they utilize the
consulting firms the City has retained relative to the General Plan to provide data and examples of
what has taken place in other municipalities. Mandates were discussed. Council Member Birrell
stressed the need to make the City a place where citizens can visit and have a natural experience.

Commissioner Chappell stated that in addition to serving on the Planning Commission, she is a
Professional Structural Engineer and serves on the State Seismic Safety Commission. She reported
that in the State there is an issue with injury in a seismic event due to contraction types. She
suggested looking at the City and General Plan from the perspective of disaster resilience.

Council Member Bracken stated that prior to asking residents for their input, clarity regarding
density should be provided. He asked if the desire is to preserve a cultural feel or if they are
seeking more physical aspects. With the shared interest in transit, he understood that it works best
when there are more dense locations with greater demand. A need for balance was emphasized.

Mayor Weichers commented that to involve the community more effectively in the process, they
should involve and encourage the participation of a Citizen Committee. Mr. Tingey concurred with
the importance of engaging and deliberate involvement of citizens in the process. He has been
involved in multiple General Plans and emphasized the importance of allowing opportunity within
all geographic areas of the City.

Commissioner Ebbeler recommended negotiating density restrictions among other tools available
at the City’s disposal. He reported that large cities are moving in the direction of hiring a Chief
Resilience Officer to deal with many of the issues discussed. Guidelines and strategic methods for
smart growth were described.

Commissioner Steinman stressed the importance when looking at City growth to understand the
desires of the citizens relative to their experiences in the County and State. He emphasized the

need to remain thoughtful when understanding those pain points and educating residents when
looking at the 30-year plan. When comparing Cottonwood Heights to surrounding cities, they
should look at transit, walkability, safety, and focus on how they can be different. He suggested
they remain vigilant and strategically share opposition to high-density buildings and instead create

diminished due to poor ridership and UTA has focused on TRAX and FrontRunner. She
commented that the idea of transit-orient development and mixed-use will develop the City’s
thought pattern in a positive direction. She appreciated the discussion focusing on attainable
housing and development and the central part of Fort Union redevelopment.

Mayor Weichers stated the focus of the General Plan update is land use, housing, and
transportation. He stated that the consensus of those present was to focus on smart or strategic
growth and maintain Cottonwood Heights as a community.

Mr. Johnson recognized that there is a strong interest in maintaining the value of what makes
Cottonwood Heights unique with an emphasis on smart growth and related principles. He
suggested the City hold district-specific public engagement events where smart growth-type
recommendations within those Districts provide feedback to establish a land use and housing
vision that is prioritized around smart growth, attainable/affordable housing types,
interconnectivity, active transportation, and resiliency. Mr. Tingey suggested augmenting events
with specific scientific surveys and taking the two input points to develop the components of the
input to continue with even larger public engagement. Staff explained that what is recommended
in the District-by-District exercise will inform other elements of the General Plan. Public
engagement will continue as will discussion with the Planning Commission and City Council to
determine the prevailing desire of each area.

It was the consensus of those present that there was a need to continue to educate and receive
public input. Providing examples of density levels and clarity of terms was suggested to provide
a more positive educational experience and the creation of an action plan was recommended. The
process of holding a General Plan-specific Town Hall Meeting was reviewed. Mayor Weichers
stated the purpose must be clear and include the State requirements and expectations of the City
while developing a General Plan. He thanked City Staff and the Planning Commission Members
for their participation.
