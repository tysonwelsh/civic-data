Mr. Spung made a single presentation for all three related agenda items. He explained
that the required public hearing had been held on November 12th and therefore, the city
was not required to hold a second one that evening. However, the Planning Commission
could do so if they wished. Commissioner Wright felt it was important to hear from the
public again.


The applicant had made adjustments based on previous feedback from the public, staff,
and planning commissioners. He now proposed 41 townhomes and 9 single-family homes
for a total of 50 residential units, and the open space had been increased as suggested
by staff. Additional changes were: the middle building now had four units instead of three;
a townhouse had been eliminated in order to provide additional buffering on the street,
additional sidewalk was provided, connecting 3975 West internal to the project; park strips
had been added along the single- family dwellings on both the east and the south sides;

      50 new residential units in the City
      A minimum of 6 townhomes that would participate in the First -Time Home Buyers
           Assistance     Program

           It would enhance the visual aesthetic and gateway of the city and neighborhood,
           mitigating on -going concerns related to dust, rodents and odors associated with
           the current land use.


Unique features of the proposed development were the size of the lots ( between 4, 653
and 5, 408 square feet), alley -loaded townhouses, and modified street cross -sections.

asked Hales Engineering to update their traffic study and include the intersection of Hazy

 you won' t see that much in any new development in Salt Lake County." He explained

She explained that the subject property was a landlocked parcel behind Auto Zone and
Family Dollar. The applicant proposed a 6, 297 mixed -use structure with 3 commercial
ground floor units and five residential units, all to be individually owned which required a
subdivision condominium plat. There was a 25' access easement going from Redwood
Road east through the Auto Zone parking lot before turning north and going           in front

of the proposed structure and exiting to the north past Family Dollar on 4200 South.

The property was designated as community -commercial on the General Plan, and the
parcel was zoned the same. According to the land use table, a mixed -use building
required a non -administrative conditional use permit along with a subdivision plat.
Stormwater was to be maintained on site underground and there were 30' rear and side
setbacks     to work with which      met the requirement.         There was also a 10' side set -back to

the north between commercial properties. A 10' landscape buffer was in place around
the residential units, and it also provided 6' wide sidewalks in front and along the south.

There would be three commercial spaces on the ground floor with five two -bedroom units
on the second. Two -car garages would be located on the ground level with stairway
access to the homes upstairs. Architectural plans, renderings and landscaping plans were
all in line with the land development code requirements.


The proposed parking exceeded what was required for both commercial and residential
development.


As for water service,         Taylorsville    Bennion   Improvement       District    had   indicated   that the

meters would need to be moved to the southside of the building so that there would be
sufficient room for the easement.


Staff recommended approval of the shared parking plan within the site plan, with the
condition that the Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions ( CC& Rs) include language that
more strongly addressed shared parking between the two uses outside of commercial
hours to accommodate guests.
