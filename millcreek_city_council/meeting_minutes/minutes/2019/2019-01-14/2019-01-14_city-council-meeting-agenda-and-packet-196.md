WORK MEETING — 6:00 p.m.
TIME COMMENCED: 6:02 p.m.

Mayor Pro Tempore Uipi called the meeting to order. She excused Mayor Silvestrini from
the meeting and thanked the City Manager for putting together the new city hall remodel.

1. Discussion on 2018-19 Fiscal Year Budget Amendments
Laurie Johnson said this was the second amendment to the 2018-19 Fiscal Year budget. She
said a budget is a projection and changes happen throughout the year. She then went over the
Revenue Budget, which had line items updated on the premise of increased information
collected. She said Property Tax increased by $342,955, Sales and Use Tax increased by
$150,000, Transient Room Tax increased by $2,000, and Licenses and Permits increased by
$320,000. She said intergovernmental grants are recognized when they are received; the local
grant was for the Promise Program from Rocky Mountain Power, and the State grants are
from the State of Utah for new sidewalks and the EMPG grant. The Interest Earnings have
increased by $105,000 based on projections and year-to-date collection. The total increase to
Revenue is $1,955,475. She said one thing that was different from the last amendment, was

that she used to recognize the note proceeds from borrowing for the Unified Police
Department contract and debt payments. She removed those from the budget based on the
auditor’s recommendation. Johnson said what is recognized now is the actual property tax
collections and anything Millcreek needs to pay to service that debt.

Johnson said she included a note on each expense budget and then went through each budget.
The Mayor and City Council Budget increased by $12,561 due to an increase in benefits to
capture all benefit costs and in professional services to pay for the Jordan River Commission
membership. The City Manager Budget increased in training and miscellaneous line items by
$3,500. The City Recorder Budget increased by $7,183 for benefits and public notice line
items. The Business License Administration Budget included costs associated with the
addition of the Business License Assistant hired on January 2, 2019. The City Services
Budget decreased in salaries, increased in maintenance and supplies, training, and the
professional services line item to cover building inspection contractor costs. The
Communications and Programs Budget increased in operating costs to cover the increased
newsletter costs and the events line item to cover Utah Venture Out and Youth City Council
costs. The Community Development Budget increased in the benefit line item by $29,588 to
capture all benefit costs. There was no change in the Promise Program Budget. The
Economic Development Budget included increased costs associated with the addition of an
Economic Development Assistant. The Emergency Management change included an
increased benefit cost of $1,821 and the removal of “Risk” from the title.

The Engineering Services Budget was consolidated with the Public Works budget. The
Facilities Budget included an increased lease cost and supplies cost. The Finance Department
Budget included increased costs associated with the addition of an HR-Finance Assistant.
The Fleet Budget includes an increase of $75,500 with the addition of a vehicle. The Front
Desk Administration includes increased personnel costs and postage costs. Council Member
Jackson asked if the increase reflected the rise in postage stamp costs. Johnson said it did not,
it increased due to the frequency of mailings. The Human Resources Budget includes
increased costs associated with the addition of a new employee, the Administrative Outreach
Specialist and Risk Manager. The IT Budget had an increase expense of $20,000 for
telephones. The Justice Court Budget reflects a minor increase to cover contract costs. The
Legal Services Budget included increased benefit and operating costs. The Non-
Departmental Budget contributed to the Capital Improvement Projects (CIP) fund and
increased the bank charges and debt interest line items. Johnson went back to the Revenue
Budget and said the Reappropriation of Fund Balance line item increased by $946,906 which
is going to Fund Balance. She then moved back to expenses. The Public Safety Budget
included a $39,305 increase associated with the transfer of the alcohol grant funds to Unified
Police Department. The Public Works Budget reflects a reduction for road maintenance
which is now classified under Class B & C Road funds, and in professional services because
a staff engineer was hired in December. The Public Works Budget also allows for the hiring
of a construction inspector, bringing the total amendment to $1,702,421.

Johnson said the CIP fund is a separate fund and it will increase by the same amount
transferred from the General Fund. She said every time a project is added, it will be added to
this fund. Johnson then went over the Consolidated General Fund Expenses which showed
percentages of the total budget. She also went over the Fund Balance sheet which projected a
12.98%. The City must have at least 5% but not more than 25% in Fund Balance.

REGULAR MEETING - 7:00 p.m.

1. Welcome, Introduction and Preliminary Matters
1.1 Pledge of Allegiance
Mayor Pro Tempore Uipi called the meeting to order and excused Mayor Silvestrini. She
thanked the City Manager for the new Council chambers and expanded City Hall. Girl
scouts from troop 1747 led the pledge of allegiance.

1.2 Public Comment
Mayor Pro Tempore Uipi extended an invitation to the public to address the Council.

Collyn Mosquito, 4036 S. Golden Circle, said he was a new resident. He said he hoped to
be involved in the community and with the Council.

Diane Wenzl, 3240 S. 1000 E., said she submitted a letter to the Council and the
Community Development Department. She said the commercial properties along the
north and south side of 3300 S. from 945 E. to 1100 E. are adjacent to single family
residential neighborhoods. The proposed future land use map indicates a change in Mixed
Use | to Mixed Use 2. She said the change would encourage development up to a 4-story
building with medium to high density. She said the neighborhoods were worried about
the effect the high-density housing will have on them, their property values, and quality
of life. It could create a loss of privacy, could create light pollution, could block sunlight
and views, could increase traffic and subsequently parking demands, and it could also
create inversion in the winter. She added that what is already there should not be seen as
setting a precedent for the area because it used to be in Salt Lake County’s jurisdiction.
She asked the Council to consider keeping Mixed Use 1 along 3300 S. from 900 E. to
1100 E.

Council Member Marchant asked staff about background information on the future land
use map of the General Plan that was brought up. He said the east end of 3300 S. was
designated as Mixed Use 2, but 3300 S. going west to 900 E. was designated as Mixed
Use 1. The new map shows the west end as Mixed Use 2 and the east end as Mixed Use
1. Mayor Pro Tempore Uipi said Francis Lilly, Community Development Director, was
not present at the meeting, nor was the item up for discussion on the agenda tonight.

Diane Wenz! asked if she could speak again before the Council on January 28", Mayor
Pro Tempore Uipi said she could. Council Member Jackson said the map does not reflect

2. Financial Matters
2.1 Public Hearing on Opening and Amending the 2018-19 Fiscal Year Budget
Laurie Johnson said she respectfully requested adoption of Ordinance 19-03, which
increases the Fiscal Year 2018-19 General Fund revenue and expenses in the amount of
$1,955,475 as well as the 2018-19 Capital Improvement Project Fund revenues and
expenses in the amount of $2,654,500, as discussed in the work session.

Mayor Pro Tempore Uipi opened the public hearing. There was no comment.

Council Member Marchant thanked Johnson for preparing the budget and presenting it
well during the work session.

Council Member Marchant moved to close the public hearing. Council Member Jackson
seconded. Mayor Pro Tempore Uipi called for the vote. The motion passed unanimously.

2.2 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 19-03, Amending the 2018-19 Fiscal
Year Budget

Council Member Marchant moved to approve Ordinance 19-03, which amends the 2018-19
Fiscal Year Budget. Council Member Jackson seconded. The City Recorder called for the
vote. All Council Members voted yes. The motion passed unanimously.

3. Business Matters
3.1 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 19-01, Adopting Millcreek’s 2019
Regular Meetings Schedule
Mayor Pro Tempore Uipi said the Council meets the second and fourth Monday of the
month but will meet on two Tuesdays during the year due to Memorial Day and
Veteran’s Day. Council Member Jackson brought up the December 23" meeting and how
the Council may cancel it if there was no pressing business.

Council Member Catten moved to accept Ordinance 19-01, Adopting Millcreek’s 2019
Regular Meetings Schedule. Council Member Jackson seconded. The City Recorder called
for the vote. All Council Members voted yes. The motion passed unanimously.

3.2 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 19-02, Amending Code Chapter 5.21
Relating to Business Licensing of Tobacco Specialty Businesses

John Brems said this was an update to code to comply with state law. In order to get a
business license for a tobacco specialty business, an applicant must meet the normal
requirements plus get a permit from the Department of Health and show a valid sales tax
number.

Council Member Jackson moved to adopt Ordinance 19-02, Amending Code Relating to
Business Licensing of Tobacco Specialty Businesses. Council Member Catten seconded.
The City Recorder called for the vote. All Council Members voted yes. The motion passed
unanimously.
