(Track A, 08:40)

best practices for risk management. Benefits include a cash award and discounts to insurance premiums. Mr. Folsom

partnership with Utah County.

       POWERHOUSE SUBDIVISION, PLAT ‘A’: (1) TO EXTEND THE ONE-YEAR APPROVAL
       VIABILITY PERIOD FOR RAMSAY POWERHOUSE SUBDIVISION, PLAT ‘A,’ TO AUGUST 13,
       2018, AN EXTENSION PERIOD OF SIXTY-ONE (61) DAYS, DURING WHICH SIXTY-ONE (61)
       DAY PERIOD THE PLAT MAY BE RECORDED AND DURING WHICH PERIOD THE PLAT
       APPROVAL REMAINS VIABLE; AND (2) TO EXTEND THE TIME FOR CONSTRUCTION OF
       REQUIRED IMPROVEMENTS FOR RAMSAY POWERHOUSE SUBDIVISION, PLAT ‘A,’ TO
       AUGUST 13, 2018, AN EXTENSION PERIOD OF SIXTY-ONE (61) DAYS, DURING WHICH SIXTY-
       ONE (61) DAY PERIOD CONSTRUCTION OF REQUIRED IMPROVEMENTS FOR RAMSAY
       POWERHOUSE SUBDIVISION, PLAT ‘A,’ MAY BE MADE
(Track A, 16:10)
Brandon Larsen of the Utah County Community Development Office summarized the applicants of the Ramsay
Powerhouse Subdivision have been working on the project since approval was granted one year ago. All that remains
is to complete installation of a pressurized irrigation system, an upgrade from what was originally submitted for
approval. The deadline is approaching next week and the applicant has requested an additional two months for this
change. They anticipate completing before that 60-day period ends. Mr. Larsen noted the unusual 61-day request is
to allow the end date to fall during regular business office hours, on a Monday rather than Sunday.

                                             WORK SESSION
1.     GIVE DIRECTION TO STAFF AND THE ATTORNEY’S OFFICE ON PREPARATION OF AN
       ORDINANCE FOR THE DIESEL EMISSIONS PROGRAM (Continued from the May 29, 2018
       Commission meeting)
(Track A, 21:10)
Utah County Health Department Executive Director Ralph Clegg came forward with Bureau Director Steve Alder,
and Scott Burton, of Environmental Health – Air Quality Division. Mr. Clegg explained recent legislation will require
the county to install specific computer software to test diesel emissions. The new laws will be implemented January
1, 2019, and it will take time to collaborate vehicle registration with the Utah State Tax Commission and train more
than 600 mechanics before that time. Commissioner Ivie commented on the legislation expiring in three years and
not applying to new vehicles because of federal regulations. Mr. Alder talked about the increase in test fees and
answered Commissioner Lee’s questions about registering outside of Utah County. When Mr. Clegg mentioned a
potential temporary waive for tampered vehicles, Commissioner Ivie expressed the small percentage of the population
with after-market changes are the reason these laws are being created. They discussed solutions to thwart violators.

                                         PUBLIC COMMENTS
(Track A, 31:15)
Lehi resident and retired District Attorney Scott Scoville brought up Regular Agenda Item No. 6 on today’s calendar,
regarding the Dane Dixon Mink Ranch; he lives less than a quarter mile from the ranch. Mr. Scoville explained
citizens are seeking guidance on how to address their concerns now that the property has been removed from an
Agriculture Protection Area. He talked about congestions and traffic hazards, submitting photographs of sidewalks
near the high school and elementary school (copies of which can be found attached to these minutes).

(Track A, 41:00)
Lehi resident Chris Bowerbank expounded further on Mr. Scoville’s comments above, noting he does not oppose the
removal of agricultural protection status for the Dane Dixon Mink Ranch. The focus is compliance with best
agricultural practices for intense operations. Mr. Bowerbank outlined a pattern of defiance by the Dixon Mink Ranch
owners (a copy of which can be found attached to these minutes), and requested the commissioners work to establish
those best practices and hold the mink ranch to those standards.
