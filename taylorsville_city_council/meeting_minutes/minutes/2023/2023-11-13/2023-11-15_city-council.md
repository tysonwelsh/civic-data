14     Agl Mayor Kristie Overson reported on ribbon-cuttings at both Dirty Dough and Revity Medical.

23   position papers in preparation for the next legislative session.

25   The life of Taylorsville High' s Principal Lidell had recently been celebrated at a fun and touching

27   giving her the courage to recite a proclamation made in Ms. Lidell' s honor.

29   There was a mural now on display on the second floor of city hall. She had been working for a

33   enjoyed sitting with and listening to veterans and learning from their experiences. Councilmember
34   Harker' s third-grade students had written some very cute letters to the vets. She enjoyed getting to
35   thank them for their service.
36
37   Mayor Overson reminded everyone that there was still an opportunity to support those veterans
38   interred at the Taylorsville Cemetery. The Wreaths Across America program hoped to collect
39   enough funds to lay a wreath at the grave of each veteran.

38            3. 1      Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District—Shane Swensen
39

40            11E1 Shane Swensen, Chief Engineer at the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District
41   presented       information regarding the Regional Water Treatment Plant Drought, Wildfire,     and
     Taylorsville City Council Minutes
     November 15, 2023
     Page 5




 1   Earthquake Resiliency Project. He stated that JVWCD needed the city' s assistance with the
 2   project. Mr. Swensen explained that JVWCD was a wholesale water provider that provided water
 3   to approximately 800,000 residents of Salt Lake County. They provided it to Taylorsville-Bennion
 4   Improvement District who in turn provided it to city residents. The Jordan Valley Water Treatment
 5   plant was the largest municipal water treatment plant in Utah. He provided a brief explanation of
 6   how water got from the plant to people' s homes. The plant had been around a long time and now
 7   needed to be upgraded. JVWCD was applying for $46 million in FEMA grant funds to help pay
 8   for this $70 million project.
 9
10   Mr. Swensen explained some of the problems that could arise as a result of various effects from
11   Mother Nature and stressed the importance of mitigating the potential for service disruption to
12   hospitals and other recovery centers, continuing fire suppression capabilities and ensuring
13   continued residential sanitation needs.

14
15   Mr. Swensen sought input from Taylorsville as well as the other communities they served. He
16   believed community support would increase the likelihood that FEMA would fund the project.
17
18   Chair Knudsen asked what would happen if the funding was not awarded. Mr. Swensen responded
19   that JVWCD would then need to bond for the project as it was not a " want" but was a " need."
20   Councilmember Cochran asked how that would impact the average residential customer. Mr.
21   Swensen did not believe it would be a huge impact but agreed that there would be an impact.
22
23   Councilmember Burgess asked what the chances were that the grant application would be
24   successful. Mr. Swensen reported that this was the third time they had applied for the grant and he
25   had been told they had come in second both previous times. He was very hopeful that their
26   application would be successful this time.
27
28            3. 2    Salt Lake County Public Works— Crystal Hulbert
29

30            1E1 Director of Salt Lake County Public Works Operations Crystal Hulbert explained that
31   trucks were currently being converted for the winter months. A flyer had been prepared to help
32   educate the public regarding what her crews could and could not do when it came to snow removal.
33   She explained that the first priorities were high traffic roads, school zones, steep hills, and arterial
34   and collector roads before most residential areas would be plowed. Things residents could do to
35   assist snow removal personnel:

36             Refrain from parking on the street
37             Be patient and understand that crews must prioritize
38             If possible, refrain from driving during snow events
39             Refrain from moving snow from driveways onto the roadways. ( It could refreeze and cause
40             ice build-up.)
41             Keep a safe distance when driving near plows

11            3. 3      Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District—Pam Roberts

12             ti General Manager/ CEO Pam Roberts reported that the WFWRD Board of Directors
13   had recently adopted the 2024 budget. Commercial pickup services would increase 10% across
14   the board and there would be an 18% increase for recycling.
15
16   Ms. Roberts presented the results of a district-wide recycling survey that gleaned responses from
17   approximately 5, 000 residents. Overall, customers seemed quite supportive of and satisfied with
18   current recycling services. She explained that landfill voucher use had gone down in Taylorsville,
19   although it actually increased district-wide.
20
21   Residents had diverted approximately 18% of reusable waste from the landfill which had increased
22   3%   since      June.   Unfortunately, the recycling sticker campaign had resulted in very little
23   improvement.

25            3. 4      Annual Comprehensive Financial Audit—Scott Harrington/Rob Wood
26
27               Assistant City Administrator/CFO Scott Harrington explained that the budget
28   committee had met the previous evening and provided some comments about the audit which had
29   since been incorporated into the final draft. He then introduced Rob Wood of accounting firm
30   HBME, LLC.
31
32   Mr. Wood presented information regarding the recent ACFA. He pointed out that it was a" clean"
33   audit with no exceptions. He commended the city for having such a large and knowledgeable
34   citizen budget committee. He also expressed his appreciation to Mr. Harrington and the rest of the
35   financial staff for their continual hard work.
36
37   Mr. Wood explained that the storm drain fund had taken what was similar to a temporary loan
38   from the general fund of approximately $800,000. That was still on the books but would be going
39   down. He had explained to the budget committee as well as Mr. Harrington that if it ended up
40   being more of a long-term loan( longer than twelve months), the city should consider making it an
41   interfund note with an interest rate at the PTIF ( Public Treasurer' s Investment Fund) rate.
