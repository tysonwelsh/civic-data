PUBLIC HEARING - MAY 12, 2015-5:30 P.M.-PROPOSED CHANGES TO COUNTY
EMISSIONS PROGRAM - Vice Chair Merrill asked Josh Greer, Bear River Health Department,
to explain the changes.

Greer said the major changes are involving how waivers are issued. New wording allows for
repairs to be done before the official test and still count towards the waiver. Greer believes this
will better serve the public and still meet the needs and requirements of the emissions program.
Vice Chair Merrill asked what percentage of vehicles pass the first time. Greer said 85% pass
the first time and the majority of those returning a second time pass. Tail pipe testing has about
a 78% pass rate the first time.

Vice Chair Merrill opened the Public Hearing and invited public comment.

3

Martin Booth asked for an explanation of the waivers and what is meant by people being proactive. The
waiver process was explained and proactive refers to citizens who believe their vehicle will not pass
emissions testing and take the vehicle in for repairs before the test. Those repairs will now count toward the
waiver requirements.

Booth also asked why the county chose to impose the cost of emission testing equipment on individual
shops. Why not have emission testing locations in the north and south ends of the valley run by the county?
Council member White replied their choice was to leave it up to free enterprise. Shop owners could choose
whether to purchase the equipment and become an emissions testing site. Council member Potter added
the county did not want to create a bureaucracy with county or state operated inspection sites. Potter also
reminded Booth that the county was forced to institute vehicle emissions testing by the State DAQ and the
EPA. Failure to do so would have prompted penalties of approximately $50,000.00 a day to the county and
cutting of all federal transportation funding to the state.

There was no other public comment.

ACTION: Motion by Council member Yeates to close the Public Hearing-May 12, 2015-
5:30 p.m.-Proposed Changes to County Emissions Program. White seconded the motion.
The vote was unanimous, 7-0.

PUBLIC HEARING — MAY 12, 2015-5:45 P.M.-DISPOSITION OF REAL PROPERTY — Executive
Buttars stated that an individual wants to purchase 1.6 acres of county-owned property adjacent to
his property. It is also adjacent to three other property owners and a road bisects the property.

Buttars asked Attorney Edwards to explain the process for disposal of real property by the county.

Attorney Edwards explained that in this case the county must first declare the property as surplus
property and authorize the County Executive to dispose of the property and the disposition must be
approved by the Council by resolution with reasonable notice given at least 14 days before the
disposition. Disposition of the property will not vacate any county right-of-way (road).

Director Runhaar pointed out that the property is located in Clarkston. Runhaar also stated a series
of boundary adjustments may be the best solution.

Vice Chair Merrill opened the Public Hearing and invited public comment.

Dave Nielsen asked if the value of the parcel has been determined. Executive Buttars said it has not;
however, fair market value is required.

There was no other public comment.

PROCLAMATION

WHEREAS, the Congress and President of the United States have designated May 15, 2015 as Peace Officers’
Memorial Day, and the week of May 10 — 16, 2015 as National Law Enforcement Week; and

WHEREAS, the members of the Cache County Sheriff's Office play an essential role in safeguarding the rights and
freedoms of Cache County; and

WHEREAS, it is important that all citizens know and understand the duties, responsibilities, hazards, and sacrifices of
their law enforcement agency, and that members of our law enforcement agency recognize their duty to
serve the people by safeguarding life and property by protecting them against violence and disorder, and by
protecting the innocent against deception and the weak against oppression; and

WHEREAS, the men and women of the Cache County Sheriff's Office unceasingly provide a vital public service;
NOW THEREFORE, in recognition of all law enforcement officers and in particular the men and women in the Cache County

Sheriff's Office, be it resolved that the Cache County Council and the Cache County Executive, County of
Cache, State of Utah, hereby proclaim the week of May 10 through May 16, 2015 as

LAW ENFORCEMENT WEEK

and call upon all citizens of Cache County and all patriotic, civic and educational organizations to observe this week with appropriate
ceremonies and observances in which all of our people may join in commemorating law enforcement officers, past and present, who,
by their faithful and loyal devotion to their responsibilities, have rendered a dedicated service to their communities and, in so doing,
have established for themselves an enviable and enduring reputation for preserving the rights and security of all citizens,

and Friday, May 15, 2015 as

PEACE OFFICERS’ MEMORIAL DAY

and further call upon all citizens of Cache County to honor those law enforcement officers who, through their courageous deeds, have
made the ultimate sacrifice in service to their community or have become disabled in the performance of duty, and let us recognize
and pay respect to the survivors of our fallen heroes.

In witness thereof, as Chair of the Count; cil and County Executive, we have hereunto set our hand this

FINDINGS AND DETERMINATION FOR TAX YEAR 2015

This matter came before the Board of Equalization of Cache County on March 30, 2015 on the verified
application for property tax exemption submitted by IHC HEALTH SERVICES, INC., (IHC) for the
LOGAN REGIONAL HOSPITAL (LRH).

FINDINGS
Based upon the verified application, supplemental documentation, and matters presented to the Board of
Equalization at the hearing on March 30, 2015, the Board of Equalization finds that:

1. IHC is the owner of record of the LRH which is located on the real property described in the attachment
“Exhibit A" Real Property Schedule. Supplementary information submitted by IHC:

"2015 Fleet Inventory" listing vehicles owned by the Logan Regional Hospital

“2015 Charity Plan” USTC Nonprofit Hospital Standards and IHC application of same

"List of Tenants”

“List of Personal Property”

“List of Current Personal Property Accounts”

2. On March 30, 2015, Board of Equalization member, Val K. Potter met with IHC representatives,
Kyle Hansen, CEO/Administrator, Chris Dallin, Director of Communications/Government Affairs, and
Alan Robinson, Finance Director at LRH. Also attending were County Assessor, Kathleen Howell and
Deputy Auditor Dianna Schaeffer. The hospital representatives gave a short presentation, after which
additional documentation was requested by Howell and Schaeffer regarding:

> Revise real property to reflect New Budge Building at 75% taxable

> Provide a list of all tenants

>» Tie Schedule B personal property to a physical location and resubmit

» Submit accounting data for LRH substantiating local “gift” of over $1.5 million

3. The relevant laws, rules, and standards for determining exemption status of non-profits include:
Utah Code Annotated 59-2-1101 and 59-2-1102

Utah Administrative Code R884-24P-35 Annual Statement for Certain Exempt Uses of Property
Utah State Tax Commission (USTC) Standard 2.18 Filing Annual Signed Statement

USTC Appendix 2D Nonprofit Hospital & Nursing Home Charitable Property Tax Exemption Standards

VVVV

These regulations are not mutually exclusive. According to USTC Standard 2.18 and Administrative Code
R884-24P-35 the applicant must annually submit “any other information the county may require.” In
addition, the last paragraph of Appendix 2D Nonprofit Standard V states:

“Institutions seeking exemption are required to show: (a) accounting data establishing the amount and value
of unreimbursed care to medically indigent persons, and subsidized patients; (b) accounting data establishing
unreimbursed value of community education and service programs, including research and professional

education programs; (c) accounting data establishing the amount and uses of volunteer time and donated
funds, and (d) descriptions of intangible or unquantifiable community gifts.”

It is recommended that the requested exemption be granted to the applicant for the tax year 2015 on the
basis that IHC/LRH property qualifies under USTC Appendix 2D Nonprofit Hospital and Nursing Home
Charitable Property Tax Exemption Standards, and they have provided some accounting data to
substantiate the local gift to the community that Howell estimates is well over $1.5 million.

4. The total market value is disclosed on the parent parcel (historical parcel number) marked 100% tax

exempt. But we recognize that there are for-profit activities performed on the hospital campus and these
taxable market values have been determined based on the annual statement for continued tax exemption
provided from IHC and LRH. These for-profit activities have been reported on a shadow parcel number.

5. Personal property details submitted to the County Assessor on April 27, 2015 will also be exempted.

6. The Board of Equalization, in a public meeting held May 12, 2015, upon lawful notice, met and
reviewed the requests for tax exemption as contained in these findings and supporting documentation.

DETERMINATION

The Board of Equalization of Cache County, Utah, determines that IHC/ LRH is eligible for an exemption
from property taxes for portions of the real property described in the attachments, the personal property
filed separately, and vehicles listed herein for the tax year 2015.

The foregoing findings were discussed and voted on by the following members of the Board of
Equalization on May 12, 2015.
