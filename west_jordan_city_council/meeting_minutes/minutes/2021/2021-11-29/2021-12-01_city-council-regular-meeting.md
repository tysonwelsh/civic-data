 C.    Alternate Council Meeting Location
Council Office Director Alan R. Anderson said staff had updated information to present regarding
an alternate Council meeting location during City Hall renovations. Council Member Worthen said
she believed Mr. Anderson had the information and knowledge to make the best decision regarding
where the Council would meet, and said she was willing to trust the decision to him. Mr. Anderson
said he wanted the Council to make the decision after staff presented the data.

Policy Analyst & Public Liaison Cassidy Hansen explained the Justice Center Community Room was
already outfitted with all technology that would be needed to stream Council work sessions and
meetings. The Justice Center Community Room had an outside entrance, and was located near City
Hall. The Public Works Training Room had a central location in the City, and existing tech could be
modified to accommodate Council needs. She explained concerns related to public access of other
areas of the Public Works Building. Ms. Hansen said the Public Works Training Room had sufficient
parking. The Fire Station #53 Training Room had a central location, limited parking with no
crosswalks, and currently had no tech capability in terms of broadcasting meetings.

Ms. Hansen shared results of an informal survey completed by four of eight potential respondents
(seven Council Members and one Council Member-elect), with the weighted top choice being the
Justice Center Community Room. Mr. Anderson emphasized that staff would accommodate any
decision made by the Council. He explained how he would suggest configuring the Public Works
Training Room for Council meetings.

Chair Jacob confirmed with the Council that the Fire Station could be eliminated from consideration.
Council Member Whitelock said she felt the Justice Center may be unnerving for some members of
the public. Council Member Worthen said she believed the Justice Center made the most sense. Vice
Chair Green said he suspected people would go to City Hall expecting to attend a Council meeting,
and pointed out that the Justice Center was near City Hall. He said it would take much longer for
citizens to get to the Public Works Building from City Hall than the Justice Center.

 A.     Drought Management Plan Update
Utilities Manager Greg Davenport requested Council feedback on a proposed Drought Management
Plan. He reported voluntary reduction in water use of over 10% in West Jordan in 2021. Mr.
Davenport presented the proposed Drought Management Plan, and explained enforcement
mechanisms. Vice Chair Green said he was uncomfortable with the threat of termination of service,
and commented that standards for restoration of service needed to be clear and objective.

Mr. Davenport explained that the new AMI billing system would be able to alert the City to those
who violated water use restrictions when citywide 4G meters were installed. The AMI system would
also help identify leaks in a water system. Responding to comments from the Council, Mr. Davenport
said a mechanism may be needed for citizens to be able to dispute charges. He emphasized that with
a first violation, citizens would receive a warning along with the Drought Management Plan and
Response Guide. Council Member Worthen commented that a resident would be knowingly and
obstinately using too much water if they got to the point of termination of service.

Council Member McConnehey said as a resident he would want a specific number as a goal. Mr.
Davenport said the goal was to reduce total water usage by 30% City-wide. Council Member
McConnehey suggested reaching out to the larger water-users to communicate goals. He said he
would want to see month-by-month stacked bar charts showing baseline and seasonal usage for
both commercial and residential. Mr. Davenport said he could also provide the information for
industrial.

Mr. Davenport commented that residents would have 14 days to comply following a first-violation
warning. Council Member McConnehey asked if the City could offer an incentive for significantly
reduced usage. He said he would rather see incentives than penalties. The Council discussed
proposed drought stages and responses:

       •   Stage 1: Moderate Conditions – Voluntary 5% Reduction
       •   Stage 2: Severe Conditions – Encouraged 10% Reduction
       •   Stage 3: Extreme Conditions – Mixture of Encouraged/Mandatory 20% Reduction
       •   Stage 4: Critical Conditions – Mandatory 30% Reduction

Council Member McConnehey suggested vehicle washing restrictions in the plan should be clarified.
Vice Chair Green said he was not comfortable with the unelected JVWCD Board telling the City
Council what the West Jordan City ordinance should be. Council Member McConnehey said he

 5.    PUBLIC HEARINGS
       a. Wood Ranch Development
Community Development Director Scott Langford described the Wood Ranch Development Project as
a complicated, fine-grained mixed-use project, and spoke of unique challenges and creative solutions.
He said staff fully recommended the proposed MDA. He recommended the Council listen to the rest of
the presentation, open the public hearing, and continue the issue to the December 15, 2021 meeting.

Senior Planner Ray McCandless said the Planned Community Hillside (PCH) Zone, on the agenda for
consideration at the December 15, 2021 meeting, would allow five units per acre. The proposal for
the Wood Ranch Development was four units per acre. The project would be developed in ten phases,
starting in the center and moving north, with the final phase on the southeast corner of the property
on U-111. Multi-family residential would be limited to no more than 50% of the housing units. Mr.
McCandless showed images of conceptual residential architecture and spoke of trails and other
planned amenities. Under consideration was the Master Development Agreement (MDA), a Wood
Ranch General Land Use Map amendment, and a rezone from Agriculture 5-acre lots (A-5) to PCH
Zone (including application of the PCH Zone to the annexation area).

Council Chair Jacob opened a public hearing at 7:42 pm

Citizen Comments:
Ty McCutcheon with Third Cadence, Wood Ranch Master Developer, said he was proud of the
proposal in front of the Council. He said Wood Ranch would be a great place to live, and a great
addition to the City. Mr. McCutcheon commented on the open space included in the plan and said
the mixed-use would give a broad opportunity for a lot of different housing choices with a unique
architectural theme. Mr. McCutcheon said the unique City-option parcel would give the City some
control with commercial down the road.

Council Chair Jacob closed the public hearing at 7:47 pm

Vice Chair Green referred to 15 acres of property to be donated for non-secular use and asked what
would happen if the donated property was not used for a non-secular purpose. Jody Hoffman,
outside counsel, said development of the donated property would not need to follow the Master
Development Guidelines unless the property were used for any purpose other than institutional.
Vice Chair Green pointed out there was a difference between institutional use and secular/non-
secular use. Ms. Hoffman said a well-known religious entity was prepared to take title of the 15
acres. If used for a church, development of the property would not need to go through the Master
Development process. If used for any other purpose, development of the property would need to be
within Master Development parameters.

Responding to a question from Vice Chair Green, Ms. Hoffman stated the City would not be liable for
maintaining any of the trails within the project while the conservation easement was in place prior
to platting. Vice Chair Green asked about the possibility of the conservation easement being
altered/terminated. Ms. Hoffman responded that all agreements could be terminated. The
conservation easement would be terminated through merger-by-deed. Vice Chair Green stated he

        b. Drinking Water Source Protection Overlay Zones Amendments
City Planner/Zoning Administrator Larry Gardner explained the following proposed amendments
to Section F of the Zoning Ordinance to protect drinking water sources.
        •    Require a public process when updating map.
        •    Update the DWSPO map from professional study.
        •    Require Water Department to review all CUP applications in a DWSPO.
        •    Add “Business Licensing” and “Business Activities” for clarification.
        •    Require additional fuel and water impermeable liner for fuel tanks rather than concrete
             vault.

Mr. Gardner showed the current map and the proposed map and answered questions from the
Council. Vice Chair Green asked if language in the document could be changed to require City Water
Division “approval” rather than “review” prior to submission to the Planning Commission. City
Attorney Rob Wall responded the administrative authority could be delegated and suggested the
document would need to include enough parameters to ensure authority was not exercised
arbitrarily. Assistant City Attorney Duncan Murray commented that approval was usually only given
by one land use authority and recommended the Water Division give a positive recommendation to
the Planning Commission, who would make the final approval. Vice Chair Green expressed
agreement.
