3. Public hearing and possible action regarding phase 2 of a proposed Conditional 
Use Permit to allow for increased pole heights to increase power transmission 
capacity; Rocky Mountain Power, applicant – Sean Lewis, County Planner 
 
Planner Lewis said this meeting is essentially a continuance of a public hearing.  This is for 
phase 2 of a Rocky Mountain Power upgrade.  The phase 2 boundaries begin just south of the 
Coalville City limits and ends at Brown’s Canyon.

Planner Lewis said this application was discussed with this body in February.  At that time, 
there was a lot of discussion about the location and alignment of the power poles along West 
Hoytsville Road.  The discussion included whether to relocate the poles of five different 
properties to the hillside.  The Planning Commission gave clear instruction to Rocky 
Mountain Power and the property owners to work things out through private negotiations.   
 
Planner Lewis said it has come to Staff’s attention that the negotiations have stalled.  Rocky 
Mountain Power has requested to come back to the Planning Commission.  They ask the 
Commission vote to approve the original pole alignment.   
 
Planner Lewis said as he understands it, there has been consensus on how to get up and 
across the mountain, but coming back down the mountain is where the negotiations have 
failed.  Rocky Mountain Power has approached several landowners in the area and has not 
been able to reach an agreement.  They have reached the point where they need to move 
forward, or it will delay the project and will cost them time and money.   
 
Planner Lewis said that Staff is recommending approval of the transmission line upgrade.  
Most of the conditions and findings are the same as they were in February, with a couple of 
edits.  A condition was added at the end, that Rocky Mountain Power work with the 
landowners to move poles within the original alignment to help them with their farming 
activities.  Rocky Mountain Power is asking that Staff review the pole heights if the locations 
are modified; otherwise, they will have to come back before the Planning Commission.  The 
rest of the application is very much the same.  Staff recommends approval with the 
easements that were in place since 1916.

Director Putt said he would like to let the Commission know why he is requesting they go 
through this process.  It is because of how things have happened in the past.  A number of 
years ago, the Planning Commission made the decision to change some very important 
aspects of the zoning process, both for the map and the Code itself.  Neither he nor 
Administrator Barnes was there, but they have read about it.  As they have read about it, 
there are some common themes that emerged.      
 
The first was that people were surprised.  They didn’t know about the changes.  They didn’t 
have the ability to be involved in the process.  Over a short period of time, everything 
changed.  The second theme was that many people felt that the decisions were impulsively 
made and subjective in nature.   
 
The third part is that there was a lack of explanation of why they chose to make those 
changes.  What were they trying to fix?  He believes this is probably not a good game plan to 
follow.  They shouldn’t surprise people, they don’t want the changes subjective (or arbitrary 
and capricious decisions), and they should have a good reason for any change made.   
 
Director Putt said State and County law requires if the zoning map and laws are changed, 
these changes have to be mostly consistent with the General Plan.  The law requires an 
explanation why.  If not, their decision making process is potentially suspect to the same 
process that was done by their predecessors, but in reverse.  That is why they have held 
multiple meetings.  They have invited the public to the workshops, and they have added 
meetings to the monthly calendar.   
 
Director Putt said the Commission asked Staff to bring to them a basis to begin that 
discussion, which Staff has provided.  The last thing that needs to be done before this is taken 
to the County Council is to provide their reasoning.  This doesn’t have to be done before they 
present this to the public, but based on prior experience the public and the Council want this.  
They need to know the reasons why.  Ultimately they have to reach a point where a decision 
gets made even though they know that not everybody will be happy.  They don’t expect to 
have a unanimous vote.
