14          1.3     Project ZTA-19-001 – Discussion and Possible Action on a Proposed
15                  Ordinance Update Relating to the Sensitive Lands Evaluation and
16                  Development Standards (“SLEDS”) Ordinance. (Section 19.72 of the Zoning
17                  Ordinance).
18
19   Mr. Hulka shared a PowerPoint presentation related to SLEDS Ordinance amendments. He noted
20   that there was a lot of information included in the packet but he wanted to review the recent
21   changes and answer outstanding Commissioner questions. Mr. Johnson informed the Commission
22   that the SLEDS Ordinance relates to regulations that are triggered in redeveloped areas with
23   potential natural hazards. That includes the Riparian Protection Zone that was previously
24   referenced in the Ordinance but does not exist anywhere. It also includes areas that are subject to
25   high groundwater, liquefaction potential, landslide, rock fall, fault hazards, steep slopes, and other
26   types of natural hazards. The Sensitive Lands Overlay Map covers one-third of the City. The
27   SLEDS Ordinance has been in place for several years and has been successful. It was time for the
28   City to update it to include best practices for each of the hazards. Staff had been working with the
29   Commission on the amendments since January 2022.
30
31   Mr. Johnson reported that the current presentation relates to the riparian section of the SLEDS
32   Ordinance. The updates were made at the request of the Commission at the last Planning
33   Commission Meeting. He reported that the riparian-related pages comprised five to 10 percent of
34   the SLEDS Ordinance. Mr. Hulka added that comments related to other sections could be shared
35   with Staff.
36
37   At the July 6, 2022, Planning Commission Meeting, there were several comments from the public
38   related to the buffer areas. The Planning Commission directed Staff to research the feasibility of
39   extending the Riparian Protection Area beyond 125 feet. Previously, all zones were under the
40   same buffer, which was up to the maximum area of 125 feet. In response to the last meeting, a
41   zone-by-zone analysis was conducted on the potential impacts of an extension. Staff found that
42   zones with smaller lot size requirements were not feasible and zones with larger lot sizes were.
43   An expanded buffer zone was now proposed for the zones with larger lot sizes:
44




     Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 08/03/2022                                         4
 1      •   Within the F-20, F-1-43, F-1-21, RR-1-43, RR-1-29, RR-1-21 zones, and CR zones, the
 2          Riparian Protection Area is defined as all areas within 150 feet of the tops of bank of
 3          streams, creeks, and other above-ground watercourses.
 4      •   Within all other zones, the Riparian Protection Area is defined as all areas within 125 feet
 5          of the tops of bank of streams, creeks, and other above-ground watercourses.
 6
 7   Mr. Hulka overviewed images of the buffer zones. Area A is where no structures are allowed.
 8   Area B allows accessory buildings or small sheds. Area C is where new single-family homes
 9   would be permitted. Home sizes were limited to 2,000 square feet within Area C. If someone
10   wants to build a larger home with a larger footprint, any area of the footprint beyond that would
11   need to be in Area D. The maximum building area for Area B was an accessory structure less than
12   200 square feet in size. Accessory Dwelling Units (“ADU”) would not be allowed in Area B and
13   would need to be included in Area C instead. Mr. Johnson reported that the purpose of these
14   regulations was to protect the waterways and the natural ecosystem.
15
16   Commissioner Shelton asked for additional information about the decision to expand the buffer
17   areas. Mr. Johnson explained that at the last Planning Commission Meeting, the Commission
18   asked Staff to look into whether it was feasible to increase the buffer areas. However, some of the
19   smaller lot sizes could not accommodate an increased buffer zone because it would make normal
20   uses difficult. As a result, the recommendation was to expand the buffer zones on larger lots that
21   have more size and dimension. There were discussions regarding Conditional Use Permits in
22   Area A. For instance, an Impact Study could be conducted if someone wanted to build something
23   that is permitted in Areas B or C. Mr. Johnson stated that there was a section related to
24   grandfathered uses, also referred to as non-conforming uses, and how to modify those.
25
26   Commissioner Shelton wondered if there would be a significant difference between 125 and
27   150 feet. Commissioner Mills explained that the Commission discussed the item previously and
28   felt there would be a significant difference. Undeveloped pieces of land next to the creek were
29   locations where large structures could be built in the future. Wastewater could go into the creek
30   as a result. The Commission felt it was necessary to protect the areas that were not yet developed.
31   It seemed reasonable to state that development cannot take place at the edge of the water. The
32   buffer would protect water, wildlife, and the structures themselves. Mr. Johnson stated that the
33   strictest regulation was Area C, which limits the footprint of a new primary structure. It would not
34   impact existing homes but would impact newly developed structures.
35
36   Mr. Hulka overviewed some of the other proposed amendments to the SLEDS Ordinance. One
37   recommendation from the Commission was to add “Licensed Surveyor” to the list of people who
38   can establish where the top of the bank is when reviewing permits. Previously, it only listed a
39   Hydrologic Engineer, Hydrologist, Wetland Scientist, and Fluvial Geomorphologist. The
40   amendments would also establish that the Development Review Committee (“DRC”) could
41   request that verifications take place. As for non-conforming uses, there was a section that allowed
42   non-conforming uses to remain. If an existing home was in one of the buffer zones and was closer
43   than what would be allowed for a new build, it was considered non-conforming. Expanding a non-
44   conforming use could be done but the language states that the expansion could not be closer to the
45   water than the closest point of what already exists on the property.
