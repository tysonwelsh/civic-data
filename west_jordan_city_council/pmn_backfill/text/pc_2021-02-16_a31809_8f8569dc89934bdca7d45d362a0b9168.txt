2.      Jones Ranch Sub-Area Plan 1; 8600 South 6400 West; Sub-Area Plan (404 lots on 85
        acres); P-C Zone; Ivory Development/Bryon Prince (applicant) [#22943; parcel 26-03-
        200-004]

Bryon Prince, Ivory Development, said that the overall Jones Ranch plan consists of 592 acres and was
presented to the planning commission one year ago. Subarea Plan 1 is 86 acres in size. Village 1 and
Village 4 of the overall plan are included in Subarea Plan 1. The land use designations in Jones Ranch
are low density, medium density, high density, commercial, and open space. This sub-area will have a
mix of the residential densities, passive open space along Barney’s Creek corridor, and a 13-acre
groomed park (Club Ivory). He reviewed the road layouts and residential areas that range from 1/3-
acre lots to townhomes. There are 408 residential units in this sub-area. He explained that the master
development plan requires that the pool amenity be constructed and functioning by the time they get to
200 certificates of occupancy and the clubhouse needs to be built by the 400th occupancy, which is why
it is included with sub-area plan 1.

Pamela Bloom asked if a traffic study had been done and what it says about the intersection at 9000
South and 6400 West, which is already heavily congested during commute hours.

Bryon Prince said the master development plan and the city ordinance require a traffic impact study at
the preliminary plat stage. He said that 9000 South will be 67 feet wide and a ¾ road that will be built
in the first subarea plan. They have communicated with Jordan School District about the frontage
piece. They envision this construction to begin later this year to provide vehicular connection from
6400 West to 9000 South as soon as possible.

Matt Quinney asked if there will be more than one access on each of the main roads and if they will
have full turning access.

Bryon Prince pointed out the two points on 9000 South that will be full-functioning intersections, and
there will likely be an additional connection farther west. The property south of the future 9000 South
is currently vacant.

Ammon Allen referred to page 10 of the plan that shows a yellow line connecting into the school
property. He asked if there had been conversations with Jordan School District about connections.

Bryon Prince said they talked to Jordan School District last year regarding the general site plan and
locations for vehicular access on 6400 West and 9000 South. The district did not anticipate any vehicle
connections interior to the development, but they were open to a pedestrian connection. They verbally
agreed to this with the district in the event the plan is approved. They will coordinate on the final
location and design.

Ammon Allen asked why the passive open space along the wash north of Club Ivory isn’t shown on
the plan.

Bryon Prince said that area is part of a future Village. The yellow line on this plan is a 12-foot wide
asphalt trail that runs the whole length of Barney’s Wash corridor. They anticipate that portion of the
trail will run through the Club Ivory amenity instead of along the wash.

Larry Gardner said the land use map shows ‘fluctuating density’ so that a variety of lot sizes and
housing types can be in the same area. This allows people an opportunity to change housing types
without the need to move away from an area, and it is better planning. Staff had a parking concern
about the road that fronts the townhome product. The applicant explained to him that every home in
sub-area 1 would have full driveways at least 22 feet long and at least a two-car garage. A large portion
of the concerns would be alleviated if this can be guaranteed. He pointed out the small lot, single-
family product that has sold out quickly in other areas of the city because it is more affordable and
there is a market for it. The signature feature of Jones Ranch will be constructed in the first part of the
development. He showed product examples of the house plans. The plans may change with the market,
but they will still have the architectural features that make it a higher scale product than what is
commonly built. Ivory Development has agreed to enhanced facades on corner lots and facades that are
street-facing. All public streets will be built to city engineering standards. The private lanes have been
reviewed by the engineering department and accepted, and they will be privately maintained. He liked
that in 20 years the wash/trail system will become a crowning achievement for the City of West
Jordan. Eventually you will be able to go from the Oquirrh’s to the Jordan River Parkway, and this
section of the trail will be second to none. It includes multiple types of trails that have been designed
and approved with the master development plan. All of the trails will go under the roads so there won’t
be a conflict with automobiles. This trail system will set apart Jones Ranch from other developments.
The P-C zone requires additional amenities depending on the number of units. A list of amenities to
choose from is included and will give latitude to the developer to provide those that will be best for
their community. He clarified that although driveways aren’t shown on the single-family lots, every
unit will have full length and full width driveways.

Staff recommended approval of the Jones Ranch Sub-Area Plan 1; 8600 South 6400 West; Sub-Area
Plan (404 lots on 85 acres) in the P-C Zone with the following conditions:

1.     All current redlines from city staff are corrected.
2.     Applicant is to work with City Staff to ensure that increased parking is available within the
       high-density areas.
3.     Applicant is to coordinate with the Jordan School District and City Staff to come to a mutually
       agreeable solution regarding the anticipated needs for school property access from the Jones
       Ranch Subarea 1.

Jay Thomas asked for an explanation regarding availability of water.

Larry Gardner said there will probably only be enough water for the larger lot portion of this sub-area
plan until the zone 5 tank is built. That tank is in the city’s master plan but hasn’t been designed or
built. Ivory Development was made aware of that fact between 1 to 1 ½ years ago. Funding for water is
usually bonded for and then paid with impact fees. The city is still paying off other bonds at this time.

Bryon Prince said they are aware of the culinary water capacity issue and are working with city staff
and the city consultant on the design for the zone 5 tank. That tank is projected in the city’s capital
facilities plan to be fully funded by 2028. Ivory is working with staff on plans to move forward with
construction of the tank with a development agreement that has language providing full reimbursement
to Ivory by 2025. Ivory will likely build the tank in the next 18-24 months and will receive
reimbursement at a later date.

Trish Hatch disclosed that she is an employee of Ivory Employee Leasing and works with the private
foundation for the Ivory family.

Trish Hatch opened the public hearing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

Todd Johnson explained that the city’s water master plan update shows capacity for 100 additional
units in the zone 5/zone 6 area. That capacity is used by each development in the order they are
approved on a first come first served basis. There have been some units already approved since
adoption of that plan, so the full 100 units are not available for this development. That capacity can be
used for small subdivisions in the area as they are approved. Once that capacity is used, the reservoir
will have to be constructed in order to build future subdivisions. Regarding traffic, 9000 South has
two accesses that are appropriately spaced at one-quarter mile and meet engineering standards for full
access.

Bill Baranowski said a traffic signal is planned for 9000 South and 6400 West in the future. The plan
shows 9000 South connecting all way to Mountain View Corridor within the next five years. The
traffic study for this development will determine if a signal is warranted. But based on the number of
units in this sub-area plan, it will probably happen within the next five years. He pointed out that any
trail crossing on 6400 West will have rapid rectangular flashing beacons, and the island for that
crossing is already built. The city is excited for the trail connection to the west.

Larry Gardner asked if anything is being done currently or planned in the future to improve traffic flow
going east on 9000 South.

Bill Baranowski said the city is applying for funding from the Wasatch Front Regional Council to pay
for the connection to Mountain View Corridor. 9000 South is an important corridor that is expected to
have 25,000 to 30,000 vehicles per day. They envision a 40-mph arterial street, so 6400 West would be
a great place for a traffic signal.

Trish Hatch asked Mr. Baranowski about his discussions with the school district regarding access to
the adjoining property.

Bill Baranowski said the previous developer showed a roadway on the west frontage of the future
middle school. Jordan School District was in favor of that, but if there wasn’t one then the district
would build their own internal to the site. A pedestrian access from this development onto the school
property would be great. He usually deals with issues associated with student drop off after the
development is constructed. So, there may be some negative aspects to having a walkway on the back
