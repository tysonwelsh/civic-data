Resolution #1044:  Approval of the Housing Choice Voucher Section 8 Payment Standards  – Housing Connect Chief Financial Officer 
Andre Bartlome explained that this is an annual exercise which is triggered by HUD’s new Fair Market Rent (FMR), and then the  Board 
approves setting payment standards for each bedroom size for the Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) program. 
 
He showed the current household payment standards.  The majority of the households participating in the program are 1 -bedroom units, 
and the majority of the rest are 2- an 3-bedroom units.  These make up 90% of the HCV program. 
 
He explained HUD’s rule that payment standards can be set between 90% and 110% of FMR.  He showed current FMR and new FRM and 
explained that the increase, based on bedroom size, was between 9% and 11.5%.  He then presented and reviewed various scenarios. 
 
Housing Connect recommends raising 1 - and 2-bedroom units to 110% and raising the studios and 3 -bedroom and higher units to 97%.  
This recommendation is being made because it would give families the ability to find units more easily.  It would also reduce  the number 
of families being served by 39 families.  The trade -off is more buying power for families that are participating, but less families are served 
versus more families being served but less buying power and with a lower payment standard.  Housing Co nnect CEO Janice Kimball added 
that it is common for housing authorities not to have the budget authority to serve all of their vouchers. 
 
Chair Moffat said that it looked like Housing Connect was able to put a dent in the number of people who turned in vouchers unused (i.e., 
unable to find housing). 
 
CFO Bartlome showed the rent plot chart and how the new payment standards would impact voucher holders. 
 
CEO Janice Kimball thanked Andre Batlome, Mike Kienast, and Ilez Brady for their work on the payment standards. 
 
Commissioner Bernal motioned to approve the recommended payment standards, and Commissioner Nguyen seconded the motion, with 
all Board members present (Chair Moffat and Commissioners Bernal, Leonelli, Litvack, Nguyen, and White) voting to support the motion, 
and the new payment standards were approved. 
 
 
 
 
 
Refugee Emergency Resettlement  – Housing Connect CEO Janice Kimball summarized the emergency resettlement situation for 
Afghanistan refugees who will be arriving in Utah.  These households will not have the status of refugees, but instead will h ave a 
humanitarian parole status, which m eans that they will have less resources available to them.   Housing Connect is looking at how it can 
support these families (estimated at 500 – 600 individuals or 200 – 250 families). 
 
Housing Connect would like to get approval from the Board to write down  the rent for up to 10 units for refugee families, targeted at 
properties where the agency does not have federal funding and where there is adequate cash flow o as to not put the property at risk in 
terms of maintenance, management, and care.  Housing Conn ect anticipates that this would cost anywhere from $600 to $1,100 per 
month per household (or a maximum of $132,000 per year).  The cost would be considered as less income towards the property (t he 
agency would self -subsidize the tenants), and Housing Conn ect would regularly touch base with the families and make adjustments 
where appropriate and would also work with resettlement agencies and the families to get on waiting lists now so that when th ey obtain 
refugee status they could qualify for affordable housing. 
 
Spencer Moffat summarized that this is an emergency measure to get the refugees housed and that the long -term objective would be to 
get them on some other program where housing could be provided.  CEO Janice Kimball said that the agency would work with the 
Refugee Advisory Board and resettlement agency partners at Bud Bailey Apartments to support these households.  The refugees w ould 
be given a preference they first arrive, but they would be put in the normal queue after that, as each housing progra m has its own 
waiting lists and preferences. 
 
Ten units would be the high end of units which Housing Connect could provide, given the properties which the agency thinks ar e going to 
be available.  CEO Janice Kimball said that this would give the State an example to use with other landlords. 
 
Commissioner Litvack motioned to approve the agency resettlement plan, and Commissioner Nguyen seconded with all Board member s 
present (Chair Moffat and Commissioners Bernal, Leonelli, Litvack, Nguyen, and White) voting in favor of the motion. 
 
6. DIVERSITY, EQUITY, AND INCLUSION (DEI) PRESENTATION 
 
Housing Connect CEO Janice Kimball introduced Tracy Carmen -Jones from ChoicePoints Learning Solutions.  Housing Connect contracted 
with ChoicePoints Learning for an assessment a nd real in -depth training for staff and some help with forming goals and area to move 
forward in regards to diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI). 
 
Tracy Carmen-Jones gave a brief introduction, and then gave a presentation to the Board. 
 
Among her slides,  she explained that t he Inclusion Edge Approach was designed to provide connection, discovery, and belonging to 
Housing Connect – from Connect (Phase I) to Discovery (Phase II) to Belonging (Phase III) to Traction (Phase IV) to Desired Outcomes.  
This has culminated in the creation of a 3 -year Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion strategy.  ChoicePoints Learning conducted several 
interviews with staff.  They held eight “New Normal” training sessions with all of Housing Connect staff.  They also did IDI (DEI assessment 
tool), as well as WOKE training with management.  The strategic planning resulted in the strategy. 
 
Workforce, Culture, Economic Impact, Community, and Workplace were the key diversity and inclusion focus areas.   The three areas that 
Housing Connect will work on are 1) Workforce; 2) Culture; and 3) Workplace. 
 
Housing Connect Developed a 3-year DEI strategy, b ased on input provided by management, people managers, and employees.  The 
strategic thrust areas and outcomes centered around Workforce Diver sity, Recognition Culture, and Engaged Workplace.  Housing 
Connect CEO Janice Kimball added that she had included this as part of her goals to the Board and will be reporting back to t he Board on 
progress towards these goals.   Tracy Carmen -Jones also mentioned that CEO Janice Kimball also wanted to develop around client 
outcomes. 
 
Commissioner Nguyen praised the presentation on a subject that was difficult to land on an actual plan and turned it into some thing that 
is understandable and possible.  Chai r Moffat remarked that he appreciated that this is actionable and that there are metrics and things 
the agency can use.  As a Board, Tracy Carmen -Jones has given some good tools to track (along with CEO Kimball’s goals) and that having 
these tools at the Board’s disposal will help the Board. 
 
Tracy Carmen-Jones said she would get the presentation to the Housing Connect leadership, and she excused herself from the meeting. 
 
CEO Janice Kimball said that the presentation would be shared with the Board and that  it was shared with the supervisor and 
management teams and that it would be rolled out at an All-Staff meeting.  The first work will be started around some training and to get 
a DEI council established.  Commissioner Litvack expressed that focusing on the  internal organization and the improvements to it (setting 
the stage in-house and something that is embraced entirely through the culture of the organization) is critical to the success of translating 
to this community impact.

7. THE HUB WARRANTY ISSUES 
 
Housing Connect CEO Janice Kimball explained that the agency is in the warranty period in regards to The Hub of Opportunity, and there 
is a significant issue:  There is a water leak coming from the common area on the second floor, and water is leaking into the garage and 
into some of the commercial space.  It was pointed out to R&O Construction during the walk-thru in June.  It’s under concrete and will be 
pretty substantial to fix. 
 
Housing Connect wants to look at making sure it has the right legal counsel, should it be needed, and look at considering hiring an 
engineering firm to review and make sure that the repairs being proposed under the warranty are the ones that Housing Connect feels 
are in the best interest of the property and the agencies (Housing Connect and Columbus). 
 
Housing Connect Real Estate Development Consultant Troy Hart said that M&H Engineering (the contractor’s forensic engineer) suggests 
removing the topping within the courtyard (the landscaping and the concrete – about 3 to 4 feed in depth) and then do the local integrity 
test and see what that looks like before moving forward with the architect, and, if needed, to engage an engineer to look at the submittal 
and what is being recommended. 
 
CEO Janice Kimball reminded the Board about the partnership arrangement for The Hub:  While Housing Connect provided the 
guarantees, the Columbus Foundation is the majority owner.  Housing Connect, however, will work with Columbus to navigate the best 
course. 
 
COO Mike Kienast went over some photos of the damage.
