Proposed Resolution 2025–8 adopting the Five-Year Consolidated Plan
(July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2030); The Neighborhood Revitalization Strategy
Area Plan; and the Annual Action Plan for the period from July 1, 2025
through June 30, 2026
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2026-
2030 Five-Year Consolidated Plan, the FY 2025-2026 Annual Action Plan (AAP), and FY 2025-2026 Neighborhood
Revitalization Strategy Area (NRSA) Plan came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Administration is
proposing the Council adopt a resolution that approves the five-year Consolidated Plan, an updated Neighborhood Revitalization
Strategy Area plan, and the first year Annual Action Plan.
         Program Compliance Administrator Fuentes summarized the memo and stated when receiving federal funds for
economic development and housing programs, the City is required to submit a Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) to the Department of
Housing and Urban Development (HUD) every five years. The last plan was adopted in 2020 for fiscal years 2021 through 2025.
The Consolidated Plan is part of the City’s application to HUD for Community Development Block Grants (CDBG) and HOME
money. Along with the Consolidated Plan, the City must prepare and submit an Annual Action Plan each year as part of the
annual application for funds. The Annual Action Plan indicates how funding will be used during the coming year and how the
grant funds will further the goals in the Consolidated Plan. It establishes annual goals and outcomes for programs and projects
funded by the CDBG and other federal grant funds. Included with the ConPlan and the Annual Action Plan is a proposed renewal
of the Neighborhood Revitalization Strategy Area (NRSA) designation. The proposed NRSA contains the same seven census
tracts as the previous NRSA plan (from the previous 21-25 ConPlan). When a neighborhood is designated as an NRSA, the city is
offered flexibility in the use of HUD funds, in order to address the specific needs of distressed neighborhoods. Ms. Fuentes also
used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the ConPlan planning process, public involvement, community priorities
for ConPlan activities, the draft ConPlan, draft AAP for FY26, draft NRSA plan, and proposed Own in Ogden. For a copy of the
presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
           Council member Choberka asked Ms. Fuentes if she or her team have any concerns about potential impacts to the
ConPlan or AAP based upon adjustments to federal grant programs in recent months. Ms. Fuentes stated that there is a great deal
of uncertainty, but most recently Congress passed a continuing resolution on March 15, 2025, which indicates that the Community
Development Block Grant (CDBG) and HOME investment partnerships grant will be funded the same as FY24. Grant funding is
based upon a formula determined by the Federal Government and City staff is unsure how that formula will be adjusted and the
amount of HUD funding the City will receive. Council member Choberka asked if City staff has developed a contingency plan to
respond to drastic changes to HUD funding, to which Ms. Fuentes answered staff is currently working on that.
           Council member Myers asked if the tenant and homeless rental assistance plan is funded with HUD funds or City funds.
Ms. Fuentes stated they are funded with HUD funds that were granted to the City via the American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA). The
City has employed a request for proposal (RFP) process and selected the Ogden Housing Authority to administer that program on
behalf of the City. The goal was to assist 22 persons, but over 70 people have been housed using the funding. Council member
Myers asked if those individuals can also apply for other subsidies from the Federal Government, to which Ms. Fuentes answered
yes.

in the City, but the ConPlan does not include funding for any apartments. The ConPlan includes housing programs that are
specifically geared towards preserving the naturally occurring affordable housing, which is defined as the house originally built
for a low to moderate income household and is located in a census tract for low to moderate income. There is a possibility for
funding to be used to assist a community housing development organization with apartment renovations for subsidized housing
apartments and that is the only time HUD funds would be used for anything related to apartments, but there is nothing included in
the current scheduled use of HUD funding for that purpose. Ms. Fuentes then addressed the question about the budget for
homelessness funding; the City received a one-time ARPA grant for the purpose, but the $846,000 mentioned by Mr. Baum was
not a one-year budget. Rather, it will be spread across four years and each household can get assistance for two years. The money
is also available to veterans and the City would be happy to receive applications from veterans for the funding.

Proposed Resolution 2025-9 adopting changes to the Own in Ogden Down
Payment Assistance Program
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed Own in Ogden Program
guidelines amendments came before the Council for consideration. The current guidelines allow potential homebuyers to have no
more than $20,000 of cash savings to qualify for the program. The proposed changes include increasing the homebuyer cash
savings amount to $40,000 and to clarify that all equity proceeds from the sale of a prior residence must be used toward the
purchase of a home in Ogden.
         Program Compliance Administrator Fuentes summarized the memo and stated the proposed amendments reflect the
current home purchase price maximums determined by the Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). Equity
proceeds received from the recent documented sale of a prior residence by the homebuyer will be considered liquid assets but may
exceed the $40,000 maximum cash limit. All equity from the prior home sale must be used toward the purchase of a new home in
Ogden. No equity cash may be withheld from the transaction.

                 “A resolution of the Ogden City Council adopting changes to the Own in Ogden Down Payment Assistance

Proposed Joint Resolution 2025-2 of the City Council and Mayor approving
the naming of the Marshall N. White Community Center
         A memo from the Public Services Department regarding the proposed name for the new recreation center – “The
Marshall N. White Community Center” – came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the original recreation
center was named in honor of Marshall White, president of the Ogden chapter of the NAACP, and an Ogden police officer who
was killed in the line of duty in 1963. Ogden Municipal Code § 7-18, et seq., states that if a named building is demolished and
replaced, it must be renamed in accordance with City Code.
         Public Services Executive Director Anderson summarized the memo and stated the proposed building name honors
Detective Sergeant Marshall N. (Doc) White, whose legacy of service, leadership, and dedication to the Ogden community
continues to inspire us. He provided background information about Marshall N. White, and concluded that following extensive
community outreach, it was determined that a new community center should be built at the same historic location, ensuring that it
continues to serve as a central gathering place for the community while honoring Marshall N. White’s legacy. Groundbreaking
ceremonies for the new state-of-the-art facility took place in July 2023. Renaming the new center the "Marshall N. White
Community Center" serves multiple purposes:
               • Historical Significance: It acknowledges the profound impact of Detective Sergeant Marshall N. (Doc) White's
                   contributions to our community and ensures that his legacy is preserved for future generations.
               • Cultural Representation: The Marshall N. White Community Center was the first public building in Utah named
                   after a Black individual.
               • Community Identity: The name reinforces the center's role as a gathering place for all residents, fostering unity,
                   pride, and a shared sense of purpose.
         Naming the center after Marshall N. White not only honors a local hero but also strengthens community bonds. It
provides a tangible connection to our history, inspiring current and future residents to engage in the center's programs and uphold
the values of service, leadership, and community spirit that Marshall N. White exemplified. It is with respect that the Ogden City
Council is urged to approve the naming of the new community center as the "Marshall N. White Community Center." This gesture
will honor a distinguished member of our community and reaffirm our dedication to fostering an inclusive, united, and vibrant
Ogden.
         Chair White introduced in writing proposed Joint Resolution 2025-2, entitled:
                   “A joint resolution of the Ogden City Council and Mayor approving the naming of Marshall N. White
                   Community Center.”

Proposed Resolution 2025-10 amending the interlocal agreement between
Ogden City and the Redevelopment Agency authorizing the sharing of tax
increment in the Adams Community Reinvestment Project Area
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed amendment to the
Interlocal Agreement with the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) regarding the Adams Community Reinvestment Area (CRA) came
before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposed amendment includes extending the tax increment period with
a reduction in participation rates after 2027. If the amended interlocal agreement is approved, the taxing entity participation
includes a maximum tax increment financing (TIF) of $6,855,243 with a participation rate of 100 percent for 10 years and 90
percent for the remaining 17 years.
          Economic Development Director Meess summarized the memo and stated the 2018 budget for the Adams CRA is set at
$10,811,794 of which $7,985,689 was available for development and infrastructure. This budget was based on the project pipeline
and financial conditions that existed at the time. Much of the $7.99 million development budget has been allocated to projects that
are complete or underway, including TIF participation for the Monarch, the Peery Apartments, and the Forest Service Building.
Additionally, TIF has been allocated to the design and maintenance of the Dumke Arts Plaza and Ogden Avenue, and it is
expected to be used for the Dumke Plaza reserve fund in future years. A budget amendment is necessary to support any additional
projects in the Adams CRA. The budget has been updated to reflect anticipated projects that will generate new value in the Adams
CRA, some of which will require TIF support to be financially feasible. The proposed amendment would increase the budget to
$28,520,773, of which $21,348,418 would be available for development and infrastructure. The overall CRA term would remain
unchanged, expiring in 2044. The taxing entities (Ogden City, Weber County, and Ogden School District) are currently
participating at 100 percent, with the city and county participating through the end of the CRA term in 2044 and the school district
participating through 2037. Community and Economic Development is proposing that the taxing entities maintain their current
participation levels of 100 percent through 2027. After 2027, the county and school district’s participation would decrease to 75
percent and the City’s would decrease to 90 percent. Community and Economic Development is also proposing that the school
district extend its participation to the end of the CRA term in consideration of the new 25 percent pass-through starting after 2027.
The proposed budget amendment will require amended interlocal agreements with the city, county, and school district. The Ogden
School Board has approved an amended interlocal agreement to support the proposed budget amendment. Community and
Economic Development staff anticipate that the Weber County Commission and Ogden City Council will review and take action
on amended interlocal agreements by May 13, 2025.
          Council member Richey asked if it is safe to say that the tax increment growth in the CRA has exceeded the schedule that
was included in the original project area plan. Ms. Meess stated the maximum tax increment has not been reached to date, but it is
growing more rapidly than anticipated. Through 2027, the RDA will continue to receive 100 percent of the increment that is
generated in the project area, and at year 10, the taxing entities will begin to receive some of the benefits of the tax increment
growth.

Public Comments
          Chair White stated that members of the Public Services Department and the Water Utility Division of the City have been
invited to provide a report regarding recent instances of discolored water in locations throughout the City.
          Water Utility Director Herd provided an update on the discolored water issue, attributing it to the Canyon Pipeline
Replacement project, and other factors which have increased flow and velocity in the system. The City is actively testing and
flushing the system to clear the discolored water, and residents are advised to run cold water and avoid using hot water until the
issue is resolved. The Council and Mayor discussed communication between City officials and residents regarding the safety of
the water; the appearance of the water; areas throughout the City where discoloration issues have been more predominant; daily
and routine testing parameters; ongoing and future water infrastructure improvement projects; and the City’s commitment to
ensuring the safety and quality of drinking water for Ogden residents.
          Public Services Executive Director Anderson then explained the historical context of Ogden City's water system, noting
that City leaders in the early 1960s did not raise rates or replace pipes, ultimately leading to a $1.5 billion replacement cost. A
significant water outage in the north end of the City in 2008 led to the identification of systemic issues and subsequent projects to
replace pipes; the City has been actively replacing pipes, with new lines being at least eight inches in diameter, compared to the
old four to six-inch lines. He acknowledged the inconvenience caused by the current system issues and encouraged residents to
report any unauthorized use of hydrants.
          Joseph Bauman questioned the construction of a 42-inch pipeline during the winter, suggesting problems with connecting
to the well or draining sediment from the lines could have been prevented with better planning and construction practices. He
stated he sits on different water boards associated with Pineview Water Systems and he has appreciated Mr. Herd’s partnership on
larger water projects in the Ogden Valley; making it possible to complete related water projects at the same time has saved
Pineview Water Systems time and money.
          David Lewis referenced public input provided during the last City Council meeting regarding the City’s closure of a trail
crossing at the Mt. Ogden Golf Course and the concerns about safety of trail users on the golf course; there is a lot of pedestrian
traffic on the golf course and children and family playing at different areas on the golf course. Golfers have many issues to
contend with, and they are reminded of horror stories dealing with accidents that have led government agencies to make
improvements, such as the installation of a pedestrian overpass or traffic signal. He hopes that it does not take a tragedy occurring
at the golf course to cause an appropriate reaction to the safety concerns at the golf course.
          Laura Lewis criticized the City's handling of water issues, citing contamination and lack of notification to residents. She
raised concerns about the impact of sediment on sewer lines and the overall quality of the water, urging the city to address these
issues. The City should not be blaming the current situation on something that happened 20 years ago; it is time to correct the
issue and prevent it from occurring again in the future.
          Caden Sparks highlighted the danger of pedestrians crossing fairways at Mt. Ogden Golf Course, comparing it to a tragic
incident at Sleepy Ridge Golf Course that resulted in the death of a six-year-old girl.
          Steve Holland encouraged the Mayor and Council to maintain the closure of the trail crossing on the Mt. Ogden Golf
Course in the vein of public safety. Allowing the trail crossing is very dangerous and problematic and he hopes the Council will
not be held hostage by unsubstantiated suggestions and unhelpful minutia communicated by those that desire the crossing to
remain opened. He suggested the Mayor made the correct decision to address a dangerous situation that should have been
addressed years ago.
          Anna Croft expressed concerns regarding the Adams Community Reinvestment Area (CRA); as the neighborhood is
improved, existing residents may experienced increased property taxes and it may be too much for them to afford. Her own
property taxes have increased $1,500 in the last four years and this is a significant increase that many may not be able to afford.
She appreciates development and historic preservation efforts in the Plan area, but noted the developers that are working on those
projects are not local and may only be interested in generating a profit.
          Linda Sanchez discussed the potential health risks of E. coli in City water. She also stressed the need for safe golf course
access for all users in the community.
          Travis Pate called for better research and planning for development projects in the community, emphasizing the need to
preserve historical elements and to ensure proper setbacks.
          Wesley Cowdren addressed comments made by golfers who are supporting the closure of the trail crossing through the
golf course; trail users do recognize that the current trail crossing through the golf course is unsafe and they are only encouraging
the City to identify an alternative route through the golf course that is safe. There are ways to cross the golf course without
entering a fairway.
