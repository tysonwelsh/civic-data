   The following are the minutes of the City Council Meeting of the Herriman City Council. The meeting was
   held on Wednesday, September 15, 2021 at 5:30 p.m. in the Herriman City Fort Herriman Conference
   Room, 5355 West Herriman Main Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by
   law, was posted in the City Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members of the Council, media, and
   interested citizens.

   Presiding: Mayor David Watts

   Councilmembers Present: Jared Henderson, Sherrie Ohrn, Steve Shields, and Clint Smith.

   Staff Present: City Manager Nathan Cherpeski, Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas, Assistant City Manager
   Tami Moody, City Attorney Chase Andrizzi, Communications Manager Jonathan Lafollette, City Recorder
   Jackie Nostrom, Finance Director Alan Rae, City Planner Michael Maloy, Public Works Director Justun
   Edwards, Fire Chief Anthony Widdison, Community Development Director Blake Thomas, and Police Chief
   Troy Carr. Jonathan bowers,

5:30 PM:
   1. Call to Order
Mayor Watts called the meeting to order at 5:31 p.m.

    2. Administrative Reports
          2.1. Discussion relating to the proposed Olympia Master Development Agreement - Chase
               Andrizzi, City Attorney
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi this meeting was to discuss the terms and conditions of the Olympia Development
Agreement as requested by City Council. Developer Ryan Button presented a list of consolidated items offered by
Councilmember Steve Shields.

Councilmember Shields indicated there were several variables in the process and it was impossible to solve all the
issues initially. Currently, the units count is at 6,330 units throughout the development. He recalled a previous
September 15, 2021 – Special Herriman City Council Minutes                             Page 2 of 10
discussion where the density in other agreements were calculated depending on certain criteria and acknowledged he
was not aware of the appropriate method in determining the outcome. He also questioned the composition of
different housing types and how it would impact the density.

Developer Button confirmed the current entitlement of the development is 6,330 units and opined he would like to
move forward with this privilege in place. He observed a minimum of 30-percent of the development would be
detached single-family dwellings, 33-percent would be individually platted town home units, and no more than 37-
percent shall be apartment dwelling units. Councilmember Smith questioned if the 6,330 units would, in reality,
could be constructed. Developer Button responded the initial entitlement was over 9,000 units and now has been
substantially reduced and opined all those units could be constructed. If commercial development increased or Utah
State University was brought back into the development, those units would still be allowed as there was no limiting
factors above the range maximum. Theoretically, 3,000 units could be constructed in the town center areas;
however, Mr. Button understood the theory for flexibility and did not want to be unreasonable. Councilmember
Shields relayed the concern of having an unlimited number of units to be constructed and suggested limiting the
number of stories in the areas to no more than five-stories for residential and commercial buildings. Developer
Button noted the design guidelines, partner guidelines, setbacks, and market forces would set parameters on the size
of the structures. Legal Counsel Mr. Bruce Baird interjected the standard building template was five stories over two
level podium as driven by economics and fire code specifications which would total seven stories. Councilmember
Henderson explained the flexibility caused uncertainty for the City. Councilmember Smith indicated the City
building height would offer some limitations. Developer Button suggested having a five-story residential building
maximum and if a mixed use is located within the building it would fall back to the standard five stories over the
two-level podium.

Councilmember Shields looked to limit the number of units in any one location of the development. The Council
discussion ensued how placing most of the entitled units at the very most west portion of the development would
adversely affect the community. Developer Button proposed the three pods adjacent to U-111 to have a density cap
at 25 units per acre.

                                    Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn arrived at 6:11 p.m.

City Attorney Andrizzi offered an example of development that would be like this proposed development along U-
111. Councilmember Henderson relayed the traffic congestion cause by the level of density in the development was
offensive. Councilmember Smith proposed a density cap of 20 units per acre. Developer Button agreed.
Councilmember Shields suggested there needed to be a certain number of units needed to be at the main town
center development. Councilmember Smith was hesitant to define the number of units and wanted to allow for the
flexibility. Councilmember Shields agreed.

Councilmember Henderson observed the east/west traffic corridors and the necessity to preserve corridors to
accommodate the connectivity. Developer Button indicated Salt Lake County could appropriate funding for
corridor preservation. The Council questioned the possibility of being granted the funding as there were other
pressing projects. Developer Button observed the roads which would be constructed as per the City’s adopted
Master Plans. He agreed he would be willing to upgrade the roadways if the City would like to try and capture
September 15, 2021 – Special Herriman City Council Minutes                               Page 3 of 10
corridor preservation funding. The Council discussed the possibility of having the right-of-way preserved for future
growth. Councilmember Shields asked the development team if corridor preservation funds were secured, would
they agree to sell the right-of-way needed for the future expansions. Developer Button agreed to the proposal;
however, wanted to have a timeline placed on the request and suggested having a three-year window for the
purchase option. Councilmember Henderson stated the Council needed to start somewhere to help alleviate traffic.
Councilmember Smith agreed on a more proactive approach on corridor preservation and appropriately placed the
burden to get the proposed connection on regionally planned maps on the City. Councilmember Shields stated he
would like to have the language authorizing the purchase of the right-of-way based off the regional transportation
study. The Council agreed. Legal Counsel Baird indicated he wanted to have a section in the development
agreement for the special provisions which had been negotiated. Developer Button explained the team already had
secured corridor preservation funds allocated by Salt Lake County for 120-feet of right-of-way along 12600 South.

Developer Button reviewed the infrastructure obligations including building all roads depicted in purple, blue and
orange on the presented map which would be eligible for reimbursement through the Public Infrastructure District
contract fee. He acknowledged the agreement that the developer would pay for their proportionate share of off-site
system improvements. Councilmember Ohrn asked about the cost estimates provided and questioned the
distinction between the local roadways and roads. Developer Button explained the difference between those
identified distinctions would be whether the system improvement would be eligible for reimbursement.
Councilmember Ohrn asked for additional clarification on the cost estimates. Developer Button indicated the team
would not seek to bond for the total eligible amount and noted it would be further discussed later.

Developer Button indicated Councilmember Shields highlighted three issues relating to commercial development
within the project: requirements for service, commercial square footage, and commercial benchmarks. The square
footage amounts currently outlined in the agreement had not been fully vetted. He noted he had talked with two
different brokerage firms in order to gain an unbiased opinion relating to the realistic amount of square footage
within the development. He offered a comparative analysis of the 144,000 square-feet in the Anthem development
related to the Olympia development. Mountain West Brokerage suggested 200,000 square feet with the
incorporated of the 12600 South and U-111 intersection being located within the development; however, suggested
if the intersection ended up being outside of the city boundaries, the anticipated commercial square footage would
anticipate being between 20,000 and 30,000. DRH Brokerage suggested 155,000 square feet with the location of the
intersection within development boundaries. Developer Button presented the three U-111 proposed roadway
connections for Council consumption. Each roadway proposal would have a dramatic effect on the commercial
development within the development and should be considered with those elements in mind. He suggested those
scenarios be laid out within the development agreement.

Developer Button indicated currently there was no obligation within the Salt Lake County Master Development
Agreement to construct any commercial development within the project. Councilmember Smith indicated there was
a lot of public clammer suggesting what was required in the development under the county jurisdiction. Developer
Button clarified there was a number pulled out from a traffic study which had been conducted prior to 2019
outlining an assumption a large amount of commercial development could be regionally supported in the area. He
stated in the current development agreement with the County, there were no requirements to provide any
September 15, 2021 – Special Herriman City Council Minutes                        Page 4 of 10
commercial. There were requirements outlined if a phase being constructed had a commercial component, they
would like to have an economic analysis of the component.

Councilmember Ohrn indicated there would be 6,300 units in the proposed development and opined the residents
within this development would need access to commercial components including a grocery store, bank, and gas
stations which would exceed the 155,000 square footage recommendation. Councilmember Shields touched on the
balanced economy study had been conducted which offered an analysis on the amount of retail and office space of
other similarly situated cities such as Cottonwood Heights and the Highland/Alpine area. He offered an excerpt
from the study and opined this commercial anticipation was low based on the population. The key design element
of the town and village center in the Olympia development was curtailed around employment, service, retail, jobs,
and transportation being included in those respective developments. Developer Button stated he was just reiterating
what the current obligation was with Salt Lake County, not what he had proposed to construct.

Councilmember Shields quoted the Salt Lake County Olympia development website which indicated this would be a
place where Utahns can live, work, learn, and play. Olympia Hills is the kind of liveable, walkable, 21st century
community that will come to define the future of Utah development projects. With a sustainably planned mix of
office space, residential neighborhoods, and hundreds of acres of open space, Olympia Hills residents would never
be more than a quarter mile away from a park, trailhead, or other natural area. Cutting-edge office space would
attract the high-tech employers that continue to flock to Utah, creating a strong local economy built around a West
Bench version of the thriving Silicon Slopes area, and since employees could live near where they work, they won’t
have to commute across the valley. The world-class design and development team behind Olympia Hills had
planned decades into the future to minimize traffic and construction impact on surrounding communities, and
account for the infrastructure, schools, resources, and open spaces that would make Olympia Hills such a great
place to live, work, and raise a family. Councilmember Shields asked what had changed in the initial proposed
development to suggest a reduction to 100,000 square feet of commercial space. Developer Button responded
Covid-19 pandemic was a huge factor warranting the reduction of commercial space. In the Salt Lake County
agreement, Olympia was prohibited from developing more than 75-acres in commercial space. Developer Button
indicated he did not anticipate Herriman wanting to have a cap on commercial development. He opined he was
trying to base it off what the current market recommended and acknowledged, in the future, could change.
Developer Button indicated he would entertain a discussion regarding the amount of commercial development.
Councilmember Henderson suggested today’s projections on viable commercial development in the area could have
an adverse effect of the development in the future which has been a reoccurring issue with developments in
Herriman City. Developer Legal Counsel Bruce Baird offered an overview of the commercial space in Highland
City which had sat vacant for 20 years and opined it was not in the best interest of the City or developer to leave
massive areas of land abutting 12600 South undeveloped for an extremely long period of time. Councilmember
Shields appreciated the perspective; however, iterated those commercial parcels were vacant until there were the
appropriate number of rooftops constructed around the area to support the businesses. Legal Counsel Baird offered
a philosophical approach to determining the appropriate balance while factoring in what the development could
guarantee versus overall desire. He indicated Developer Button was offering a reasonable proposal to fit each
“what-if” scenario.

Councilmember Henderson relayed the point of view from the residents notating the land use needed balance for
economics. He noted the commercial areas should not only include retail space as job centers are needed to bring in
a daytime population and would alleviate some traffic congestion in the area. Developer Button presented his
minimal proposal relating to the three U-111 road alignment scenarios. Scenario 1: if the intersection would be
located onsite, he proposed to incorporate 150,000 square feet of commercial development within a quarter mile of
the respective intersection with an additional 10,000 square feet of gross commercial leasable space on the west
bench. Scenario 2: if the intersection ultimately ends up outside of the City’s boundaries, he proposed to reserve
30,000 square feet in the town center pod along with the 10,000 square feet of commercial development along the
west bench. Scenario 3: considered if U-111 turned into a bypass road, he proposed 10,000 square feet of
commercial in the town center, 8,000 square feet of commercial space on the foothills and 10,000 square feet along
the west bench would be reserved for commercial development.

Councilmember Smith indicated the City has been working with retail recruiters to try to encourage commercial
development and noted there were metrics to help determine the appropriate balances for development and asked
for an update to what those figures entailed. Assistant City Manager Tami Moody responded she had reached out to
a consultant to look at this property with the corresponding proposed intersections could be placed and they
offered rough estimates based on if the intersection being located within the development which included 200,000
square feet of commercial space if there was a grocery anchor which would then lead to additional commercial
space. She indicated the consultant did not foresee a large commercial development such as the Mountain View
Village in this area; however, could change based on the destination anchor.

Councilmember Shields indicated the current circumstances brought numbers lower than what had been
determined prior to the pandemic would have displayed and understood the need to be cautious. He recommended
planning for commercial space somewhere between the optimistic view and the pessimistic view and proposed a
realistic range of having 350,000-400,000 square feet of commercial development if intersection scenario 1 became a
reality. Councilmember Ohrn added if the vacant land does not have a vision and preserve the space, the
commercial aspect of the development would not happen. She relayed her desire to not be short sighted.

The Council temporarily recessed the work meeting at 7:30 p.m.

The Council reconvened the work meeting at 7:44 p.m.

Councilmember Ohrn relayed her desire not to have the pod where the trailhead was situated to be high density and
relayed her concern of entitling the area to a higher level of density when it was not the intended purpose. She
requested the area be considered as either open space or high density residential on all maps for the development.
Developer Button explained why it was depicted as open space on one map because it was not completely finalized
by Salt Lake County and needed to have a contingency plan in case it was not accepted. He questioned if the
topography makeup was conducive to a park and wanted to preserve the ability for commercial in the area.
Councilmember Ohrn reiterated her desire not to overinflate the property at a higher value based on entitlements.
Developer Button relayed the intent to dedicate the 20-acre parcel to Salt Lake County for the purpose of having a
trailhead placed there; however, they could force the County to accept the property. If it happened to be determined
the County did not want to place the trailhead in the area, the developer would come back to the City to determine
September 15, 2021 – Special Herriman City Council Minutes                              Page 6 of 10
what type of open space would be acceptable and ensuring the 20-percent open space requirement had been
achieved. Councilmember Ohrn asked about the size needed for a programmable park. Assistant City Manager
Wendy Thomas explained the largest space needed would be for a rugby field at 1 ¾ acres without all the ancillary
amenities. She offered an example of the Butterfield Park to help everyone get a vision of the programmable space
within the park. Councilmember Ohrn indicated the amount of density in the proposed annexation would bring the
need to have programmable park space for the community. Developer Button reiterated the development would
meet the 20-percent open space threshold as outlined by City ordinance. Councilmember Ohrn suggested going
through the master development process was to offer exceptions to the ordinance and noted there also was a
topography caps dictating the percentage of acreage could be counted toward the open space requirement.
Councilmember Henderson added this development was denser than any other in the community and felt the open
space requirement should increase to help offset the additional burden being proposed. Developer Button noted the
current open space yielded 3.7 acres per 1,000 residents in Herriman City. Olympia would have 7.6 acres of open
space per 1,000 residents. He believed the trails system was a huge benefit to the community as they connect
regionally; however, recognized the importance behind programmable spaces. Councilmember Henderson stated
unusable open space would not be counted towards the requirement at the full rate. Mayor Watts asked for
clarification on the open space requirements based on topography.

Councilmember Shields indicated the City needed more programmable space, not just open space. Councilmember
Smith disagreed an opined the City did need to have trail connectivity. Community Development Director Blake
Thomas offered a synopsis of the open space requirements for planned unit developments and noted the 20-
percent open space would be required and if the area was considered unbuildable, it would be counted at 25-percent
rate. He defined unbuildable area as property which was incumbered due to slope, wetlands, flood drainage, or
contamination. Director Thomas further clarified unbuildable slope was anything greater than 30-percent grade.
Councilmember Henderson stated the rules were outlined in City code for this specific purpose and he
recommended following through with the requirements set forth. Legal Counsel Baird observed the unique
situation of the property throughout the development which would lead to an exception as outlined in the Large
Master Planned Community Overlay Zone. Councilmember Ohrn indicated the topography on the land varies
throughout developments and developers should try to mitigate those anticipated hurdles. Councilmember
Henderson observed different apartment complex subdivisions throughout the community where there was lacking
on programmable space for those residents.

Councilmember Shields observed the requirement within the Salt Lake County plan to have one 50-acre park and
four 10-acre parks. He asked the development team to articulate their thought process on how that requirement
would be achieved. Developer Button indicated the trail and parks outlined achieved the goal. Legal Counsel Baird
interjected the County did not require a 50-acre programmable area. Councilmember Henderson asked how this
development would be better for Herriman residents if it is incorporated into the City as opposed to being built in
the County. Developer Button responded there was a huge financial benefit to the City which did not include only
one-time monies. Councilmember Henderson indicated he had reviewed the numbers and opined the viewpoint
was debatable; however, wanted to just solely focus on the development plan benefits. Legal Counsel Baird
observed the 150,000 square feet of commercial development and dedicated park space. Councilmember Shields
observed the substantial amount of money the development would bring to the community. Councilmember
Henderson noted he did not want to debate the financial benefit of the development as he was solely looking at the
September 15, 2021 – Special Herriman City Council Minutes                              Page 7 of 10
development plan as opposed to what was approved through the county. Councilmember Ohrn indicated those
one-time monies are not there to benefit the City, they are collected to upgrade infrastructure systems to support
the added impact to the community. Legal Counsel Baird acknowledged some of those one-time monies ended up
in the General Fund; however, the City would also receive reoccurring point of sales tax funds. Councilmember
Ohrn observed the operation and maintenance costs associated with development. Councilmember Shields
interjected the financial analysis offered by the finance team took into consideration all aspects of anticipated
revenues and their respective expenditures based on the agreement approved through Salt Lake County and
concluded there would be excess revenues for the development at buildout. Councilmember Henderson indicated
the impact fees associated with the development would be utilized for the intended purpose. Councilmember
Shields reminded the Council the development would be responsible for all the system improvements within the
development. Councilmember Henderson explained the impact fees that would be collected would now just be
covered by a contract fee and observed the reduction in the revenues anticipated in the financial analysis. The
Council ensued a discussion comparing the benefits of collecting the contract fee over the impact fees for
development.

After a robust discussion, Councilmember Henderson and Councilmember Ohrn looked to have the open space
requirements align with the current ordinance. Councilmember Smith relayed his belief that was the starting point
of the negotiations and was amenable to the plan as presented. Councilmember Shields agreed.

The discussion continued with secondary water requirements. Developer Button understood water zones five and
six did not have a system in place to get secondary water to those areas; however, systems are in place for zones
three and four, but are not active. He proposed not putting secondary water infrastructure in the project at all. City
ordinance requires infrastructure to be placed in zones three and four for large buildings such as apartment
buildings and churches. Developer Button opined it was not logical to pass the added cost to the end user. Mayor
Watts relayed his desire to have the secondary water infrastructure installed. Councilmember Ohrn indicated a small
portion of Herriman residents utilize secondary water currently; however, there is a large portion of the community
that will have the ability to use it when available. She relayed her position not to remove the requirement.
Councilmember Henderson agreed.

In response to a question posed by Councilmember Smith, Public Works Director Justun Edwards discussed the
projected development capacity and infrastructure cost estimate for secondary water from a reliable source was $25
Million. To bring additional supply, it was anticipated to cost $12 Million. Director Edwards reported the
assumptions offered by Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District estimated a reduction in total outdoor usage by
60-percent because of the water efficiency standards being implemented. Director Edwards opined the general cost
of utilizing culinary water over secondary water would have a $371,000 annual increase. Developer Button agreed
the secondary water system would be incorporated into zones three and four.

The conversation turned to 6400 West and Developer Button observed a portion of 6400 West and noted the City
had a previously existing obligation to construct 6400 West southward from 12600 South through the Creek Ridge
development. He requested the City entertain a reimbursement for the developer to install the City obligated
infrastructure which was estimated to cost approximately $2 Million. Councilmember Ohrn asked for clarification as
to why the improvement was not outlined in a previous agreement. Developer Button indicated he was unable to
September 15, 2021 – Special Herriman City Council Minutes                              Page 8 of 10
provide the clarification requested. He offered a suggestion of instead of entering into a standard reimbursement
agreement for the project, the City’s portion could be retained to pay for offsite improvements incurred by the
development as outlined in the proposed development agreement. The Council relayed their desire to agree to the
proposal. City Attorney Andrizzi preferred to have a reimbursement agreement in place for this portion of the
agreement. The Council concurred.

The Council temporarily recessed the work meeting at 9:04 p.m.

The Council reconvened the work meeting at 9:11 p.m.

Mayor Watts indicated the next item for discussion was regarding phasing in the Olympia Development.
Councilmember Shields indicated this item was essentially to not have all the commercial development be
constructed at the end of the project and wanted commitments placed in the development agreement outlining how
and when the development would occur.

The Council discussed for a written commitment to work with the development team to lobby for state funding for
major roadways along with the east-west corridor preservation along 12600 South with the anticipation this could
allow for an expressway to Bangerter Highway.

Councilmember Ohrn relayed her concern regarding the financing for improvements and suggested the cost
estimates were extremely inflated. Developer Button explained the realistic view of improving an acre of property
and the realization of not installing all the needed infrastructure immediately. With the delayed installation, it
required the development group to have a greater contingency budget for inflation. Developer Button confirmed
the development team would not look to bond for the entire eligible amount, only what was necessary. Legal
Counsel Baird explained the 30-percent contingency budget is due to the fact the infrastructure plans had not yet
been engineered. Councilmember Henderson asked for documentation detailing the developer would install the
infrastructure and not left for the City to complete in the future. City Attorney Andrizzi offered a brief synopsis of
the provision in the development agreement notating the developer would have to install all aspects of
infrastructure for a development phase prior to an application for building to be submitted. If the developer
determines to abandon the project after infrastructure approvals have been granted, the developer would be
required to finish the installation. Legal Counsel Baird explained how the bonding mechanism would function for a
developer which would ensure the installation of the infrastructure. The infrastructure would be installed prior to
the recordation of any development plat. Councilmember Henderson stated the Council had been given
reassurances by previous legal counsel which ultimately was proven to be false. Councilmember Shields iterated the
developer would be required to install the improvements and outlined the process if the developer happened to
default on the bond.

Developer Button suggested the group was close on negotiations for the units per acre in the town center area
abutting U-111. Mayor Watts relayed the desire of the Council to not have all the units end up on the west end of
the City which was the reasoning behind requesting a density cap for those pods. Developer Button observed the
height restriction concern. Councilmember Shields suggested the height be limited to five stories above grade
allowing for underground pedestals and a density cap of 20 units per acre for residential development west of 8200
September 15, 2021 – Special Herriman City Council Minutes                      Page 9 of 10
West. The Council concurred. Developer Button asked about mixed-use height restrictions. Council consensus
determined mixed use height would be five stories over the two-story pedestal.

Developer Button observed the commercial development concept and proposed three differed scenarios.
Discussion ensued regarding the potential alignment of the U-111 and 12600 South and the amount of commercial
development was desired in the area. The Council further discussed the type of commercial, services, and civic
could be counted at a higher level of credit.

The Council temporarily recessed the work meeting at 10:08 p.m.

The Council reconvened the work meeting at 10:19 p.m.

Attorney Bruce Baird suggested a hybrid model of commercial development. The development would include 20
acres of commercial property located at the intersection of U-111 and 12600 South be reserved for 15 years;
however, if the area was not fully developed after 10 years, the reservation would be reduced to 15 acres. The
proposed requirement would require 200,000 square feet of general leasable commercial area across the project
based on the recommendations from the third-party commercial brokers and consultants. He further proposed to
have a minimum of 350,000 square feet of general leasable commercial area for 15 years. After 15 years, the
minimum would decrease to 200,000 square feet and the far west area, also known as west bench, would have a
minimum of 10,000 square-feet of general leasable commercial area. The foothills would have 8,000 square feet of
general leasable commercial area. A double credit would be received if the commercial development was achieved
for civic, services, or an employment center uses.

The discussion turned to open space as it relates to credit offered for unbuildable areas and programmable space.
Developer Button suggested to grade and highlight the natural features of the area and apply the same credit as
other open space. Assistant City Manager Thomas indicated trails were highly requested by the public and this
proposed open space was a regional trail system in coordination with other communities. She relayed her desire not
to diminish the open space requirement for the trail system. Assistant City Manager also highlighted the need for
programmable parks and observed the 15-acre proposed programmable park space and questioned what the
Council was willing to contribute to the upsize of the park and acknowledged the ongoing maintenance associated
with the programmable space.

    3. Adjournment
Councilmember Smith moved to adjourn the Herriman City Council work meeting at 11:00 p.m. Councilmember Shields seconded the
motion, and all present voted aye.
