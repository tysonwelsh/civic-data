1.1 Public Hearing on Consideration of the Draft Project Area Plan and Budget for
Millcreek Center Community Reinvestment Area
Mike Winder said community reinvestment areas (CRAs) are a tool created by state
law to help in the reinvestment of an area. They capture tax increment to help fund
the improvement of an area, and if there is an impediment to development (blight)
component it can lead to eminent domain for the purpose of private development. He
said the blight component was ruled out of being used in the Millcreek Center
Community Reinvestment Area Plan. He said CRAs are designed to assist existing
businesses, new development, infrastructure improvements, affordable housing, and
crime reduction. Winder went through the four steps of the creation process of a
CRA. He said step 3 was happening tonight with a public hearing regarding the
project area plan and budget and adoption of the plan by the Community
Reinvestment Agency and City Council. He said the boundaries have shrunk from the
original plan due to public input. The first tax increment receipt would come in 2020.

The base year is 2017 with $125 million taxable value. There will be $182 million of
incremental value added over the next twenty years. The total assessed value in
twenty years will be $308,684,872. He said the result is not due to a tax increase, the
property taxes are frozen the first year of the project (base year). A portion of
property taxes in excess of the base year are redirected to the Community
Reinvestment Agency for use within a defined geographic area in the community.
Winder showed a table of assumptions that listed the taxable value and pass-through
increment by taxing entity. He said the total funds over twenty years will be
$33,097,481 with a net present value of $20,867,501 if the plans play out well. By
state law, ten percent must be used for affordable housing. The taxing entities,
specifically Salt Lake County, asked if that could be increased to fifteen percent
because of the housing crisis in Utah. So, Winder said, fifteen percent of the
increment generated will be used towards affordable housing. Chair Silvestrini
clarified that money did not need to be spent on affordable housing in this area, but
city-wide. Winder confirmed. He then said that the bulk of the increment will be used
for redevelopment activities and five percent would be used for project area
administration and operations.

Winder said the next steps are that the Community Reinvestment Agency will adopt
the various taxing entity interlocal agreements and publish a summary of them in the
newspaper. Chair Silvestrini said the process went from the Taxing Entity Committee
to an interlocal model and every taxing entity has approved. He then opened the
public hearing.

Conrad Evans, 1020 Fairclough Drive, asked if the boundary was fixed or if it could
be expanded. John Brems said it could not be expanded without a new hearing and
public process. Mike Winder said a mini community reinvestment area could be
proposed around the city center if property was acquired from Salt Lake City. Board
Member Marchant clarified that that would be Miller Avenue. Winder affirmed it
would be between Miller Avenue and Woodland Avenue.

Bruce Parker, 3070 E. Cruise Way, thanked the Agency and suggested a link be made
between the Millcreek Center Community Reinvestment Area Plan and the City
Center Master Plan. He said there should be clarity of direction for the area. This plan
identifies an iconic 15 story building, but the General Plan says the maximum height
in the area will be 6+ stories. He suggested 15 was a lot more than 6. He said there
was no timeframe for the benefit of the increment coming back to the City.

Chair Silvestrini said the City Center Master Plan was not ready for adoption because
it needed a lot of community engagement. He said the plans will be linked. He said
the City needs to capture the base year 2017 before the buildings that are permitted to
be built get built or the City will miss out on the $100 million taxable value that they
provide. He said Millcreek residents want more sidewalks and improvements and the
way to get there is by improving the tax base.

Winder said the consultant’s, Lewis Young Robertson & Burningham,
recommendation is to have a broader CRA Plan, but it does say it needs to match the

Jeff Plowgian, 1357 E. 3300 S. - Plowgian Auto Repairing Inc., said his child asked

1.2 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 19-02, Adopting an Official Plan for
the Millcreek Center Community Reinvestment Project Area

Board Member Jackson moved to adopt Resolution 19-02, Adopting an Official Plan for
the Millcreek Center Community Reinvestment Project Area. Board Member Catten
seconded. Chair Silvestrini thanked the public for their engagement. He said he received a lot of
comments since the small area city center draft plan was released and most of the comments
were encouraging. He said this is our city and we want everyone to be involved and to make the
plan as good as possible for our kids and grandkids. The Agency Recorder called for the vote.
All Board Members voted yes. The motion passed unanimously.

1.3 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 19-03, Adopting the Project Area
Budget for the Millcreek Center Community Reinvestment Project Area

Board Member Uipi moved to approve Resolution 19-03, Adopting the Project Area
Budget for the Millcreek Center Community Reinvestment Project Area. Board Member
Marchant seconded. Board Member Jackson asked if the approval needed to be contingent on
acquiring the signatures from the other taxing entities. John Brems said no, this was just the
budget. The Agency Recorder called for the vote. All Board Members voted yes. The
motion passed unanimously.
