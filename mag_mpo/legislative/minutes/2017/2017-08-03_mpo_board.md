UDOT Funds Transfer (Redwood Road) – Bob Allen, MAG staff The Transportation Commission separated one large Saratoga Springs project into three projects in May 2017. The Region had excess funds in the first two projects to ensure that the projects could be awarded and constructed this year. The two projects have been awarded, and the Region would like to take the excess funds totaling $6,542,749 and move them to the last project in preparation for advertisement. The amount to move from PIN 10689 is $3,933,381. The amount to move from PIN 15274 is $2,609,368. The total of these funds needs to be added to PIN 15275. UDOT would like to modify the TIP to reflect these funding changes. MPO staff recommends a modification of the TIP to reflect the funding transfer to the remaining Redwood Road project in the TIP. Mayor Wilson moved that the TIP be modified to reflect the transfer of $6,542,749 to the remaining Redwood Road project. Mayor Brunst seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. C. The Region would like to move some of the funds off this project to add funding to five existing projects within Region 3, two of which are within the MPO and are listed below. To Grandview in Saratoga Springs) 4. $1.1M to PIN 15573 (SR-191; Stauffer Chemical to Red Loop Rd Guardrail) 5. $80K to PIN 15572 (US-40 VMS S of Heber & E of Duchesne) Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee August 3, 2017 Page 4 Mayor Hadfield raised a concern that two of the five projects are outside of the Utah County jurisdiction and questioned why the MPO Regional Planning Committee is making recommendations on them. Wasatch Front Central Corridor Study Findings – Jeff Harris, UDOT Planning Director The Wasatch Front Central Corridor Study is a collaborative effort between WFRC, UDOT, UTA and MAG to find solutions that will meet transit demands as Utah’s population continues to grow. The study looked at a variety of creative options to address future transit demands from South Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee August 3, 2017 Page 5 Davis County to Northern Utah County and the width in Salt Lake County between the area of 700 East to Redwood Road. Federal Highway Administration applied for the TIGER planning grant which was used to fund approximately half of the study. 3rd Quarter Cent Sales Tax Discussion – Andrew Jackson, MAG staff A brief history of Transportation Planning at Mountainland AOG was given dating back forty years. An executive order was established in 1970 identifying seven planning areas within the state. While the Governor created the boundaries, the interlocal agreement between cities and counties created the Association of Governments. The executive order that created the boundaries ordered regions to do the following: • Coordinate state plans and programs • Coordinate federal sponsored programs at sub-state levels • Strengthen the role in the execution of state and federal programs at the local level • Provide consistent framework for planning and admin • Eliminate overlap and effectively use the State’s resources A brief history of the local option sales taxes for transportation was given. 1st Quarter Cent Tax (Mass Transit): Adopted 1984-2011, 100% UTA, $18m annually 2nd Quarter Cent Tax (Rails/HWYS/BRT): Adopted 2006,.3 cent tax, 87% Rail/5% BRT-Bus/8% Regional Roads, $20m annually 3rd Quarter Cent Tax (HWYS/Transit/Trails): Adopted 2008, 100% MPO/County, $18m annually The 3rd quarter cent local option sales tax for transportation was discussed and how the state law determines it should be allocated. The dialog was prompted by the July 5, 2017 County Commission meeting agenda item regarding approval of funding for the projects previously approved by the MPO. Transportation goals include: • Improve Safety • Increase person throughput • Improve travel time reliability • Increase accessibility to jobs and education • Improve air quality • Improve economic outcomes • Reduce household transportation costs • Improve mode balance Solutions include: • I-15: Double-Decked • Transit: No-fare transit system wide • Active Transportation: Cycle superhighway • Incentive Programs: Pay-per-use transportation applications • Surface Streets: Improve street connections Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee August 3, 2017 Page 6 Mayor Gygi, Andrew Jackson and Commissioner Bill Lee discussed various points of view and whether or not the County has the right to supersede the MPO. All parties agreed that this issue needs to be explored further. Mayor Clyde proposed that MAG staff check with other MPO’s in the state to see if they have faced similar challenges. Andrew Jackson suggested also contacting the Office of Legislative Research and General Counsel for clarification on S.B. 245. Mayor Gygi made the recommendation that MAG staff and County Commissioners contact their attorneys to clarify state statute. The recommendation was approved by the committee.
