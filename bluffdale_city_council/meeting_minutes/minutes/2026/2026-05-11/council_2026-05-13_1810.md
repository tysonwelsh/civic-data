2.2 Water Use Conservation & Pricing Discussion. (Staff Presenter, Bruce

Kartchner)
Mr. Kartchner reported that over the past three weeks, he had spoken with multiple organizations
regarding water conservation and the current drought. He attended a Jordan Valley Water
Conservancy District (“JVWCD”) meeting where they indicated that they have 30,000 acre feet of
water available to allocate to new development across agencies. They may be able to develop
more water by acquiring water rights from irrigation companies, but that was the total amount
currently available for development. Additionally, they reported that water usage was increasing
even though development was not. Bluffdale utilized 20% more water than contracted in 2025,
which was concerning, as the JVWCD was considering mandating a 10% decrease from the
contract. The City is unique in that it has a number of unregulated, unmetered homes using
irrigation water.

Mr. Kartchner asked the City Council to consider whether additional landscape restrictions should
be implemented. For example, allowing a maximum of 10% turf. Other cities were offering
conversion grants like Flip Your Strip, and South Jordan’s program had been so successful that
they were seeing a change in water usage due to the efforts. Equipment grants can be offered for
devices that track moisture content and create an irrigation schedule based on need. At the JUWCD
meeting, they also discussed potentially prohibiting turf on commercial properties.
Bluffdale currently has a tiered culinary water rate schedule. Normal and moderate drought
conditions did not affect rates, but severe drought increased the rate, with additional increases for
extreme or critical drought conditions. Some cities were also increasing their Tier 4 base rate to
over $7. That may feel punitive, but it was an effort to encourage conservation. The Council could
consider adopting a resolution authorizing rate changes to be invoked in severe, extreme, or critical
drought states, but he neither advocated for nor discouraged that action.
Mayor Pro Tem Wilding asked if the fee table calculations were monthly. Mr. Kartchner
confirmed that the water rate is based on monthly usage. Mayor Pro Tem Wilding asked about the
impact on the City of using 20% more water than the contracted amount. Mr. Kartchner reported
that a penalty rate applies. Mayor Pro Tem Wilding asked if the additional cost was covered by

the City invoked severe and higher drought condition water rates.

o 410 animals were rescued.

e 238 total animals, primarily cats and dogs.
o 169 were officer impounds.
o 40 were brought directly to the shelter.

inquiries.
