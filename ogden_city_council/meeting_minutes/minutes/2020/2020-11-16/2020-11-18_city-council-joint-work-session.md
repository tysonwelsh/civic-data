Land Use Training related to Engineering Standards
          As part of the City's commitment to continued training for planning and land use issues and as required by the City's
contract with Utah Risk Management Agency (URMA), the City Council and the Planning Commission are holding a joint work
session to receive training on a topic related to land use regulations in the City. The topic has been chosen by the City's Legal
Counsel, Planning Department, and Council Staff. The topic was chosen based on what City staff felt was a current and relevant
issue from which the Council and Commission could benefit. This year's training will focus on recent state law changes related to
the requirement for the legislative body to approve engineering standards as part of the City's land use regulations. Per State Code
Section 10-9a-5, only a legislative body may enact a land use regulation. Prior to changes in this section in 2019, the City's
engineering standards were compiled and approved for reference by the City Engineer. With the recent change to state law, the
formal engineering standards document is now adopted by the Council as it is considered a land use regulation. The joint work
session will provide background on the state law, the engineering standards document, what standards are included in the
document and how it is used.
          Assistant City Attorney Stratford used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss why engineering standards are
important in the process and why the City Council is involved in this process. Only a legislative body, as the authorized body to
weigh policy considerations, may enact a land use regulation. This provision was updated in the 2017 Utah Legislative Session via
adoption of House Bill 232. Land use regulations must be enacted by an ordinance; it is a law, code, map, specification, fee, or
rule that governs the use or development ofland. In 2018 there were additional changes to emphasize that a land use regulation is
a legislative decision enacted by ordinance and includes the adoption or amendment of a zoning map or the text of the zoning
code. However, a land use regulation does not include a temporary revision to an engineering specification that does not
materially increase the cost of development or impact the use of land. Ogden City has engineering standards for public
improvements and the adoption of these standards predates the 2017 changes to Utah Code; however, updates to the engineering
standards that have been adopted recently have been brought to the Council in accordance with Utah Code. Applicants must install

important for the City to adopt and regularly update engineering standards in order to require necessaiy infrastructure

          Public Services Deputy Director Anderson briefly noted that he and Assistant City Engineer Nielsen carefully craft
engineering standards from the viewpoint of protecting the public when they are using any form of public infrastructure, such as
roadways and sidewalks. The engineering standards are also intended to ensure effective use of taxpayer monies for public
improvements and to limit the City's liability. Mr. Nielsen then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the City's
engineering standards; there are several components of the engineering standards, including standards for public improvements
amendments to the APWA, and standard drawings. Engineering standards have been in existence since 2008 and multiple editions
have been updated, including the 2013 and2017 versions. The City also utilizes additional standards such asAPWA and AWWA.
The overall goal and importance of engineering standards is to ensure that as infrastructure is built throughout the City, it is built
to a standard that protects City assets. Design standards address general instructions, general improvement requirements,
roadways, sanitaiy sewer, stonn drainage, water infrastructure, APWA amendments, and standard drawings. He then presented
several examples of the types of drawings included in the standards document to illustrate technical requirements for different
types of infrastructure projects, such as project designs, construction materials to be used, and construction methods.
          There was discussion among Mr. Nielsen and the group regarding the types of maintenance projects that can be
performed to extend the life of infrastructure in the City; and opportunities for partnering with the Utah Department of
Transportation (UDOT) to improve City infrastructure in conjunction with a UDOT project on a State road within the City.
          Mr. Nielsen then noted that several of the different Master Plans that have been adopted in the City- such as the Bicycle
Master Plan and Transportation Master Plan - have informed the engineering standards; various types of studies are relied upon to
determine the appropriate type of infrastructure development or improvements in given ai·eas of the City and the engineering
standards ensure that any contractor working on a infrastructure project is operating from the same position. He then concluded
that there is a great deal of information in the engineering standards document and within his presentation and he would be willing
to expound on any subject in the future if so desired by the Council.
          Discussion ainong the group centered upon the involvement of different deparhnents or divisions in the City relative to
updating standards and ensuring that all projects in the City conform to the standards. Mr. Anderson concluded by encouraging
Planning Commissioners and the City Council to reach out to any member of the Public Services Deparhnent if they believe that
an infrastructure project in the City may not conform to the engineering standards. Several members commented on the thoughtful
development of and updates to the engineering standards over the year and thanked Mr. Anderson and Mr. Nielsen for the manner
in which they represent the City.
