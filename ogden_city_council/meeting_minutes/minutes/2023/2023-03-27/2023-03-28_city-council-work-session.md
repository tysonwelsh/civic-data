The purpose of the work session was to review the proposed amendments to the MAKE Ogden Downtown Zoning
Ordinance and Zoning Map; discuss Council business; and hear Council comments.
; MAKE Ogden Downtown Zoning Ordinance Amendments and Zoning
Map Amendments
Planning Manager Brierley explained the proposed amendments and rezones are the next steps in the implementation of
both the MAKE Ogden Downtown Master Plan and the Nine Rails Creative District Master Plan. In 2020, the City Council
adopted the MAKE Ogden Downtown Master Plan as a major step toward revitalizing and improving the downtown core of the
City. The plan was meant to act as a guide for public and private investment in downtown Ogden and to create an economic
engine that would serve the entire community. Since that time, significant development projects have been proposed and are
currently in the carly planning and development stages. This includes both the WonderBlock mixed use project and the
redevelopment of the Union Station campus and UTA property north of Union Station. In addition, the Nine Rails Creative
District Master Plan, adopted in 2018, establishes a plan for the development of an active and vibrant arts district located between ,
the downtown core and the City’s East Central neighborhood.
Mr. Brierley introduced Jessica Garrow of Design Workshop, the City’s partner in creation of the zoning ordinance, who
used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed ordinance that will be used to implement the MAKE Ogden
Plan. Ms. Garrow first reviewed the process that was followed to get to this point, which was guided by goals that were identified
for the project as follows:
e The new code should be simple, easy to understand and apply
e People will want to visit downtown and say great things about the area
e The code should clearly explain the process so most applicants can successfully navigate it and understand what
is expected of their application
e The code should strive to protect Ogden’s character
e The code should result in design and form that does not reflect a cookie cutter design process
e The code should encourage development patterns in downtown that create small, “15 minute” neighborhoods
e Incorporate code language to reflect the recommendations of the MAKE Ogden Plan
Ms. Garrow presented a map of the study area, which also highlighted existing zoning within the area; the MAKE Ogden ;
Plan proposes zoning adjustments for several areas within the district and she offered a comparison of the current zoning map with
, the proposed zoning map if the adjustments are implemented. She then discussed community engagement; Design Workshop
; J facilitated small group meetings, a roundtable luncheon, provided notices to downtown businesses and owners, and checked in
with staff and various boards. The goal of these meetings was to get input on the following questions:
e What aspects of the Downtown character need to be preserved?
e What are current frustrations and challenges of the code?

Key takeaways are related to building height limits, streetscape improvements, land uses, and the need to clarify the land
use code. This feedback was used to inform the draft documents that were first presented to the Planning Commission in work
sessions and public hearings; the Planning Commission made refinements to land uses, provided design criteria for small cell
developments, addressed the interaction between the Code and other adopted plans, provided color and mural standards for the
Nine Rails District, and reviewed view and shade studies.

Ms. Garrow then expounded on the input received throughout the public engagement process and discussed the manner
in which this input influenced recommendations for revisions; key changes deal with height limits, development standards,
parking regulations, land use updates, streetscape improvements, definition revisions, and a streamlined review process. Ms.
Garrow then provided a detailed explanation and offered examples of each of the revision topics listed above and engaged in
discussion with the Council regarding the potential implications of some of these revisions. There was also discussion among the
Council and Mr. Brierley regarding the manner in which property owners in the MAKE Ogden Plan study area were notified of
public meetings and the number of opportunities they had to be involved in the planning process.

Mr. Brierley then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to highlight specific properties within the MAKE Ogden Plan ,
area and illustrate the manner in which their zoning will change ifthe proposed amendments are approved; he discussed how this
will impact setbacks, landscaping requirements, and non-conforming uses. He engaged in discussion with the Council regarding
the manner in which the changes will impact the existing uses of various properties in Ogden’s downtown area, including some
properties on 25" Street, and the manner in which the proposed zoning change of these properties will be communicated to the
property owners. He expounded on the City’s ability to prohibit formula-based or chain businesses within the MAKE Ogden Plan
area as well as the relationship between Wonder Block and the Union Station; the Union Station plan has heavily influenced the
proposed amendments, specifically relating to building heights for development in the surrounding area.

Council Deputy Director Symes then noted that Council staffhas scheduled a follow-up discussion on this topic for next
Tuesday’s work session meeting and he asked if the Council needs that discussion or if they have outstanding questions. Chair
Choberka stated she would still like to have the follow-up discussion; she believes that some constituents will review the materials
from this meeting and reach out to the Council over the next week with questions they have. Council member Hyer added that he
would like the hear Council staffs thoughts about the proposals as well.

Concluding discussion centered on the timeline for proceeding with consideration of the proposed ordinance
amendments; Mr. Brierley noted the notice of pending ordinance will expire mid-April and Planning staffrequests an action from to]

scheduled for April 11 and the Council can decide whether to act upon the proposed ordinance after receiving public input.
Council Business

Ogden Wildlife Rehabilitation Center: Council member White stated she has been contacted by some residents who are
concermed about the City’s action to evict them from City owned property. She asked why that is being done now. Management
Services Director Brown noted that the existing location was intended to be temporary in nature and the Center was made aware
when they first occupied it that they would need to look for a more permanent home. The rental agreement included a clause that
gave the City the ability to provide proper notification of the need to vacate the premises. Council member White asked if the City
owns the building the Center is located in, to which Ms. Brown answered yes. The space is needed for the expansion of the
Dinosaur Park. Chair Choberka noted that the Dinosaur Park will be providing its annual report to the Council in the coming
weeks and the Council could ask more questions about this matter during that discussion. Council member Nadolski stated that six
months is not a great deal of time, and he asked if Administration would be willing to give the Center more time to find a new
location. Ms. Brown stated she has not discussed that matter with Administration, but the terms of the agreement provide for a six-
month eviction period. Council member Lopez stated he would like to make an official request for Administration to consider a
longer period of time. He also asked for a copy of the lease agreement and noted it feels as if the City is not working well with the
Center. Ms. Brown stated that she will discuss that request with Administration and the agreement is part of the public record.
Council member Lopez asked when the Center was notified of the termination of the agreement, to which Council Executive
Director Eller-Smith answered approximately one month ago.

Flood Preparedness: Council member Blair referenced a recent Standard-Examiner article regarding the availability of
sandbags to aid in preventing flooding; he asked if the City has sandbags that residents can pick up for use on their own property.
Ms. Eller-Smith noted Administration has a strategic plan for responding to flooding risks; they do not want to make sandbags
available for anyone to pick up because that may cause people who do not really need them to take them. The Public Services
Department has a plan for distributing sandbags to areas of the community where they are most needed. Ms. Brown added that any
concerned resident can contact Public Services and they will visit an address to see if sandbags are needed. Chair Choberka added
that a webpage with helpful information about how to prevent flooding has been created and she encouraged the Counciltoreview |
and share it.
