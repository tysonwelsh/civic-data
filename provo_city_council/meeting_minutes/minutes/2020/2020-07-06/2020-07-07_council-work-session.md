1.       A discussion regarding proposed amendments from the Sign Committee to Provo
         City Code regarding electronic sign hold times and permitted locations. (20-101)
         (0:24:18)

comparisons with other municipalities’ sign regulations. During the ensuing discussion, the
Councilors who served on the Sign Committee shared insight and clarifications on the elements
of the proposal. Councilors shared comments, concerns, and feedback on the proposal:
     Councilor Shannon Ellsworth shared concerns about the commercial designation along
        south State Street, as this area was set for a lot of residential growth. There were many
        factors negatively impacting quality of life in this area of the city, and she was reticent to
        contribute further to diminish the quality of life for residents of this area. She thought the
        proposed long hold-time area should be restricted to south of 1860 South.

2.       A discussion regarding Provo City’s permitting process for demonstrations and
         protest. (20-103) (0:52:57)

Wayne Parker, CAO, presented. Mr. Parker shared details from Chapter 6.20, Assemblies and
Special Events, in the Provo City Code. He reviewed the City’s process and responded to
questions from Councilors, noting the challenges inherent with unplanned events taking place.

Councilor George Handley expressed concern about the recent counter-protests with armed
citizens forming a spontaneous militia of sorts. Camille Williams, Assistant City Attorney, noted
that Utah is an open-carry state and shared insight on the nature of the recent spontaneous
gatherings. Brian Jones, City Attorney, noted that armed protesters had the same Constitutional
rights to gather; if armed protestors took action, then traditional criminal and legal principles
would apply—other laws regarding defense for the protection of life or property could come into
play. Mr. Handley reiterated his concerns about the perceived vigilantism demonstrated in the
community; Mr. Jones clarified that unless these armed citizens actually took action, all they
have done is engage in exercising free speech. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked whether there
was recourse for bad actors built into the permitting process. Mr. Parker noted that it was
problematic when bad actors were not necessarily associated with a specific group.

Mr. Parker highlighted other considerations with public protests and similar events; for
protestors, often the process and experience is important and visible locations are desirable. Utah
County has frequently allowed use of their grounds for events. Ms. Williams noted some
procedural methods in Salt Lake City with the establishment of protest zones. After some earlier
conflicts, this concept was introduced to help reduce friction as well as mitigate opportunities for
assault or other criminal attacks. Police Chief Rich Ferguson noted that in recent weeks, the
Police Department’s Community-Oriented Police officers worked closely with business owners
downtown to provide resources. Councilor Bill Fillmore shared his concerns with enforcement
(or lack thereof) of the permitting process for events. Mr. Parker indicated that future events
would follow the specific process in place.

3.       A discussion regarding rezoning all R2.5, R3, R4 properties to Low Density Res.,
         Medium Density Res., or High Density Res. and the removal of the R2.5, R3, R4,
         R5, and Campus High Density zones from City Code. (PLRZ20190427 and
         PLOTA20190428) (1:42:07)

Javin Weaver, Planner, presented. The city code currently contains 71 zones. Mr. Weaver noted
that many zones share similar elements; staff has reviewed some of the existing zone
designations and have recommended zone changes to parcels in the R2.5, R3, and R4 zones to
reassign them to currently existing zones that were more broadly applied throughout the city. Mr.
Javin responded to a question from Councilor David Harding about what changes would take
place with these zone changes; Mr. Weaver indicated that there would likely be a few non-
conforming elements, but that new projects would conform to the new zone designations, which
were very similar to the predecessors. This was a code cleanup item that would streamline the
city code by removing the redundant and disused zones. PRO zones were a separate topic and
would be addressed separately. Presentation only. This item was already scheduled for the
Council Meeting on July 7, 2020.
