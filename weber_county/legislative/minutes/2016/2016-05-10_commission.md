G. PUBLIC COMMENTS:

       Debbie Perry, of unincorporated Weber County, addressed the construction of Skyline Drive (Skyline), an
       important road to the citizens who live in its corridor and use it for traveling, recreation and active
       transportation. She said that the inclusion of a bike route on Skyline shows it is an important part of the
       community. At the 5/2/2016 evening community meeting at the Combe residence there were 75 citizens
       from Weber County, South Ogden and Ogden City neighborhoods that are affected by Skyline. Also in
       attendance were Chair Gibson and Holin Wilbanks from Weber County (who toured the area before the
       meeting) and Sallee Orr of the South Ogden City council. Three items have emerged that the citizens of
       the Skyline corridor would like to see resolved and she addressed two of them:
       1) Safety of those who live on and cross Skyline, particularly the elementary school students—a steady
       stream of those students walk and ride bikes/scooters down Eastwood Blvd. and cross Skyline Dr. during
       the peak morning traffic and the common afternoon commute hours. She said that the community would
       like the current 2-way stop at that intersection to become a 4-way stop, that a crossing guard be hired as
       the vehicle count increases, and that the speed limit on Skyline and in that area remain at 25 mph. A
       March 2015 Standard Examiner article stated that the county’s original plan was to put wide sidewalks
       and bike lanes on both sides of the new construction, and it is the community’s feeling that having
       continuous sidewalks on both sides of the road is the safest approach to the heavily used area, and they
       would like those things remain in the project—the residents have been told that on one side of the road
       there may not be a sidewalk.

       2) Completion of landscape medians that currently exist on the Ogden City section of Skyline. Ms. Perry
       said that originally a conceptual plan drawn by the City to help the county receive the funds showed the
       landscape medians as part of the plan. She said that as property was being purchased from Jonathan Ford,
       he was informed that medians would be included, but since then they have been removed from the project
       and citizens are upset with this plan change. She understands that the county generally does not add or
       maintain medians and this may not be a typical situation for the county, and she asked the commissioners
       to consider the completion of the county’s section of Skyline as part of a bigger project ultimately shared
       with Ogden City, which has set the precedent on this particular road. It would be fair for the county to
       cite a partnership approach with the City as the reason for this exception. The county’s portion of Skyline
       is less than ¼ mile long. The City is in the planning phase of adding medians on their new and existing
       sections. The small county section would probably only require two medians to keep it continuous and
       tie-in to the planned landscaped roundabout. She said it has been expressed that the medians would not
       only provide continuity but beautification, increased land values, traffic calming, and safety in winter
       conditions. She said that two City engineers expressed that adding the medians would not be difficult or
       change the engineering of the road. She suggested subcontracting with the City, which will already be
       maintaining 1½-2 miles of its medians. She stated that Mr. Ford, land owner of that portion of Skyline,
       has expressed a desire to work together, discuss how to bring the medians to fruition and provide for their
       maintenance, and to possibly assist in the cost of the landscape installation. The citizens surrounding
       Skyline’s corridor would like the county to find timely workable solutions for these two issues of safety
       and medians.

    Commissioner Bell said that a couple of years ago he held conversations with Mr. Ford on a regular basis
    in front of many people and Mr. Ford knew there was no island planned there. Commissioner Bell had
    told him that if he wanted one he would have to pay for it and Mr. Ford had then said he did not think he
    wanted the island. The island would be through Mr. Ford’s property. He stated that many people
    attended the public hearings but no one mentioned the medians. He noted that he is not against making
    some changes. He had discussions today about the 4-way stop sign with County Engineering and a 2-
    hour meeting with Rocky Mountain Power. He noted that a resident contacted him about a 4-way stop on
    Jared and Eastwood. He said that the 25 mph makes sense. The commissioners stated that these items
    will be considered. Chair Gibson also noted that they had a related positive discussion with the City.

    Nancy Waterfall, of unincorporated Weber County, stated the county is constructing the continuation of
    Skyline in southeast Weber County. She joins in the concerns raised by Ms. Perry and asked the county
    to take advantage of this opportunity to build a roadway that considers the surrounding natural landscape
    to include calming traffic features such as landscape medians and bike lanes, which would be a
    continuation of the Bonneville Trail in their neighborhood. Her main concern is the plan by Rocky
    Mountain to install high voltage power lines and poles in this residential neighborhood as part of the
    Skyline project. In a meeting with them they indicated that it is not necessary to upgrade their system but
    they simply just want to. She outlined the following: underground utilities are the standard in this
    residential neighborhood and future utilities should also be underground for several reasons: 1) Safety.
    Combe Road is a winding, narrow country road and there have been traffic accidents involving cars and
    power poles. Those poles constitute a danger to vehicle traffic and downed power lines create a
    significant hazard to people. 2) Health. Studies indicate that overhead power lines contribute to adverse
    health conditions, including cancer. 3) Appearance. Overhead power lines are distracting, an eyesore,
    collect windblown objects and are a hazard to birds, eagles and other fowl, and are inconsistent with the
    surrounding neighborhood. 4) Power Continuation. As recent as Friday, the power was out for 6 hours in
    her neighborhood due to a power pole that caught fire. With the recent windstorms many county
    neighborhoods experienced extensive power outages—some lasted for days from downed power lines. 5)
    Cost Effective. Long term cost savings are inherent with underground utilities. Any additional cost to
    install underground utilities is offset by the long term benefits. 6) Consistency and Fairness. Rocky
    Mountain Power requires private property landowners to bury their power lines in new subdivisions, new
    homes or even to get power to an outbuilding, chicken coop, etc. Rocky Mountain Power should be held
    to this same standard and be required to bury their planned expansion along Skyline. She respectfully
    requested that the county require Rocky Mountain Power through its agreement to bury the power lines.

    On 5/2/2016 her community met with Chair Gibson and Ms. Wilbanks to discuss Skyline development
    issues and she would like to hear a follow-up from each commissioner and the status moving forward.
    She stated that private property owners have no leverage with Rocky Mountain Power, and this was
    evident in the meeting with them, but the commissioners have power. In March 2016 Rocky Mountain
    presented a plan that was not a part of the agreement the people had agreed to and signed a letter of
    memorandum to those points. She stated that this completely changes it. She is disappointed after having
    worked on this for many months to have this happen now. She hopes this can be resolved in a
    compromise.

    Chair Gibson apologized that the other commissioners had not been present at the 5/2/2016 meeting. He
    hoped that the two speakers would take it back to the group that they were not present because they had
    not known about it and he had not really understood what it was. He stated that the other commissioners
    are very interested in the issue. The commissioners have been told that, according to statute, Rocky
    Mountain Power is not required to follow the same standards that they hold the citizens to. The
    commissioners will work with them. Commissioner Bell reiterated that they held a 2-hour meeting with
    Rocky Mountain Power this morning and presented some concerns.
