38      a. Fraud Assessment – Utah State Auditor Alert 20-21
39
40   Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, explained that the Office of the State Auditor
41   developed a Fraud Risk Assessment to help local government board members understand best
42   practices in fraud detection and prevention. He said the Assessment assigned an overall risk
43   level of undetected fraud based upon points earned for the implementation of the identified best
44   practices. He said the best practices identified in the Assessment were not required, but were
45   highly recommended, all of the best practices identified in the Assessment may be implemented
46   at a low or no cost, and the Assessment was not a replacement or duplication of the internal
47   control assessment performed by an independent auditor as part of a financial statement audit.
48   While they were both risk assessments, their methodologies and purposes are distinct. Mr.
49   Hicks said that requirements beginning in 2020 were that all local governments would complete
50   the Assessment internally, the Assessment must be completed and presented to the governing
51   board before the end of the 2020 fiscal year, the Chief Administrative Officer and Chief Financial

 1   Officer must certify the results of the Assessment, and the Assessment must be submitted to

40a.   Resolution No. 20-48 - To Confirm Riverton City Council Shall Not Provide the Services
41     Proposed to be provided by the Riverton Fire Service Area
42
43     Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained the following information:
44
45              “The Riverton City Council has expressed the desire to create a new service area
46              that would replace Unified Fire Service Area ("UFSA"). To this end, On April 21,
47              2020 the City Council adopted resolution No. 20-31 which formally commenced the
48              process to begin the creation of the Riverton Fire Service Area, (“RFSA”).
49               The next step in the process, scheduled for the June 16, 2020 meeting of the
50              Riverton City Council, is to conduct a public hearing which allows the public to ask
51              questions and obtain further information regarding the issues contained in the
     Page | 9 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     June 16, 2020



 1            resolution No. 20-31. [See: Utah Code Ann. §17B-1-210(2)(d)(ii)]. At beginning and
 2            the conclusion of the public hearing, the City Council is required to announce the
 3            deadline for filing protests and generally explain the procedure and requirements to
 4            create RFSA, or protest the same. [See: Utah Code Ann. §17B-1-210]
 5
 6            Protests may be filed within 60 days of the last public hearing required under Utah
 7            Code Ann. §17B-1-210. Because we are scheduled to convene only one public
 8            hearing, the hearing conducted on June 16, 2020 constitutes the last hearing
 9            required before the time begins to run for filing protests. The 60-day deadline to file
10            protests lands on August 15, 2020; however, this date happens to land on a
11            Saturday. This means the proper deadline will carry forward to the following
12            Monday, August 17, 2020. To be valid, a protest must be filed with the Riverton City
13            Recorder’s office, and signed by the owners of private real property that is located
14            within the proposed local district (in the present case, RFSA), or by registered voters
15            residing within the proposed boundaries of the district.
16
17            In the event that the land held by persons filing a protest: (a) is located within the
18            proposed local district; (b) covers at least 25% of the total private land area within the
19            applicable area; and (c) is equal in value to at least 15% of the value of all private
20            real property within the applicable area; then adequate protests will have been filed
21            to bar further action by the Riverton City Council to create RFSA. Alternatively, if
22            protests are filed by registered voters residing within the proposed boundaries of
23            RFSA which equal in number to at least 25% of the number of votes cast in the
24            applicable area for the office of president of the United States at the most recent
25            election prior to the adoption of the resolution, then further action to create RFSA is
26            also barred. [See: Utah Code Ann. §17B-1-213].
27
28            A final point is that after opening and closing the public hearing described herein, the
29            next step in the process is for the Riverton City Council to adopt a resolution
30            indicating whether Riverton City “will provide to the area of the proposed local district
31            within its boundaries the service proposed to be provided by the proposed local
32            district.” Utah Code Ann. §17B-1-212. Utah law further requires said resolution to be
33            adopted within 60 days after the last hearing to consider adoption of RFSA pursuant
34            to Utah Code §17B-1-210. Thus, after the June 16 hearing is closed, the Council
35            may adopt the enclosed Resolution No. 20-48 but is not required to. Adopting said
36            resolution will not affect future steps in the process to create a district, so long as it is
37            adopted within 60 days of the June 16, 2020 hearing.”

23
24   Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, explained that enhancements to Tier 2 Public
25   Safety & Firefighter retirement benefits would take effect beginning July 1, 2020. He said they
26   apply to new and current employees in the Tier 2 Public Safety & Firefighter Retirement System
27   for benefits earned on or after July 1, 2020. Due to the cost of the plan enhancements,
28   participants in the Tier 2 Public Safety & Firefighter Hybrid plan would be required to make
29   employee contributions in addition the employer contributions. According to IRS law, an
30   employer may "pick-up" the employee's portion of the retirement contribution and treat it as an
31   employer contribution; however, in order to do so, formal action must be taken.
