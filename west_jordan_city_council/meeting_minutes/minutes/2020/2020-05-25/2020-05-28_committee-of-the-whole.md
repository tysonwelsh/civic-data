    • Dumpster Program
Chair McConnehey said he knew from experience that the dumpster program helped keep
neighborhoods clean. He acknowledged that change was necessary to reduce City expense, ideally
keeping the first dumpster free for neighborhoods. Committee Member Green said that, given the
tight budget, he would prefer some amount of fee, even for a first dumpster.

Ms. Steck explained the City could fund a maximum of 1,500 free dumpsters within the FY 2021
Budget. Committee Member Worthen said she liked the fee structure proposed by Ms. Steck, with
a first dumpster free for neighborhoods.

Staff explained that Ace Disposal was not interested in participating in a front-load dumpster
program because of liability. The group discussed dumpster programs in other cities. Committee
Member Whitelock said she would like to provide some method of disposing of refuse free-of-
charge, but said she would not want the City to raise the solid waste free to support the few who
use multiple dumpsters each year. She said she would agree with charging a nominal fee for a first
dumpster.

Chair McConnehey said he would support limiting the number of free dumpsters to 1,500 per year
on a first-come-first-serve basis, and charging for a second dumpster with clear understanding that
even with the charge for a second dumpster, the dumpster would be open and available for anyone
in the community to use. Committee Member Green suggested finding a way to limit the number
of free dumpsters available within a neighborhood or area. Responding to a question from
Committee Member Worthen, Chair McConnehey said he was not in favor of the “neighborhood
cleanup” idea.

Ms. Steck clarified that after the first 1,500 free dumpsters were used, the cost for any dumpster,
first or subsequent, would be $200, not $50. A majority of Committee Members indicated support
for charging $50 for every first dumpster rental, and $200 for every subsequent dumpster rental,
with a City-managed neighborhood cleanup plan. Chair McConnehey stated he did not agree with
the majority.

    • Fees for Service
Committee Member Green commented that City fees had not been adjusted for inflation for a long
time. He suggested an across-the-board inflationary increase, and an ongoing plan for inflationary
fee increases. Chair McConnehey said he would want each fee to be reviewed. He requested Ms.
Steck bring back an adjusted Fee Schedule for the Committee to consider.

Committee Member Whitelock suggested a nominal filing fee to run for public office to cover
Staff time. Four Committee Members indicated a desire to include a filing fee on the revised Fee
Schedule. Committee Member Jacob requested a comparison of filing fees in other cities.

   •   No Decrease to Public Safety

       Committee Member Whitelock expressed concern with public safety personnel cuts made by the
       City, specifically mentioning the position of Neighborhood Watch Coordinator. She said fully-
       funded public safety was a priority for her. Committee Member Green said he believed the
       departments had done a good job of refining processes and reducing expenses without reducing
       first responder personnel. Committee Member Whitelock expressed concern with funding cuts for
       public safety training. Committee Member Pack said he believed public safety to be the backbone
       of a community, and said he would be reticent to cut back on training. Committee Member Pack
       said he would not want to cut back on needed funds for overtime. Committee Member Green said
       he believed the proposed budget for overtime was too high compared to the amount used in the
       last year. Committee Member Pack emphasized that he would not want to reduce level of service.
       Mr. Lee commented that the Neighborhood Watch Coordinator position would be filled with an
       experienced volunteer. Chair McConnehey suggested continuing the discussion later in the budget
       process, with the Police Chief and Fire Chief.

       ADJOURN

       At 8:37 p.m., Committee Member Whitelock moved to adjourn the meeting. Committee
       Member Worthen seconded the motion, which passed by unanimous vote (7-0).
