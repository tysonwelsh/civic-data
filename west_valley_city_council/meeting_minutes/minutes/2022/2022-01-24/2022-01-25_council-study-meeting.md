ORDINANCE 22-03: AMEND SECTION 1-2-103 OF THE WEST VALLEY CITY
MUNICIPAL CODE TO UPDATE TEXT CHANGE APPLICATION FEES
Steve Pastorik, CED Director, presented proposed Ordinance 22-03 that would amend
Section 1-2-103 of the West Valley City Municipal Code to Update Text Change Application
Fees.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The current application fee for an ordinance text change is $250. This fee was
       adopted in 1998. The proposed fee of $1,300 reflects the City’s actual cost to
       process a typical ordinance text change application and comply with requirements
       in State code.

Wayne indicated that Steve outlined each step of a zone text change process
meticulously, by staff wage, and calculated all other costs associated. He indicated that
this proposed change is backed by detailed factual numbers. Councilmember Nordfelt
asked how a zone text change process works. Steve detailed the process from an
applicant applying, going through the Planning Commission, and coming through
Council. He indicated that this change would apply to anything in the zoning ordinance
only. Councilmember Nordfelt stated that if a citizen wanted to petition a zone text
change, they could always bring it up to a Councilmember who could bring it to the
Council as a whole and the Council could direct that change at no cost. Councilmember
    MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JANUARY 25, 2022

Huynh asked what staff will do with the extra money. Steve replied all application fees
go into the general fund. Councilmember Nordfelt noted that the fee increase will help
offset the amount of staff time and associated costs that currently exist. Wayne asked for
a recent example of a zone text change that was denied. Steve stated that a developer with
a specific purpose wanted to modify an overlay zone to accommodate drive-through’s.
He noted that staff told the developer that this was not the direction the Council wanted to
go but the developer decided to move forward with the process anyway because the fee
was only $250. Wayne indicated that this increase is not to generate revenue but to make
sure that interested persons are serious and committed to their request. Councilmember
Whetstone asked how this proposed fee compares to other Cities. Steve replied that this is
likely on the higher end but noted that Cities do not often look at increasing or adjusting
fees for applications.

The City Council will consider Ordinance 22-03 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
February 1, 2022 at 6:30 P.M.

RESOLUTION 22-23: AUTHORIZE THE CITY TO ENTER INTO A
DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT WITH S.E.A., LLC FOR APPROXIMATELY
0.62 ACRES OF PROPERTY LOCATED AT APPROXIMATELY 2781 WEST 4100
SOUTH
Steve Pastorik, CED Director, presented proposed Resolution 22-23 that would authorize
the City to Enter into a Development Agreement with S.E.A., LLC for Approximately 0.62
Acres of Property Located at Approximately 2781 West 4100 South.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The property located at 2781 West 4100 South is owned by S.E.A., LLC and is
       zoned R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square
       feet). With an area of 27,170 square feet, the property is large enough in area for
       three lots. However, the 190’ width of the property is not wide enough for three lots
       since the minimum lot width in the R-1-8 zone is 80’. Staff suggested a
       development agreement to the owner as a way to grant an exception to the lot width
       and develop three lots in exchange for the new homes meeting the City’s prior
       housing standards. The owner agreed to the proposal with the understanding that
       the City Council can deny the agreement. If the Council does deny the agreement,
       the owner will be able to develop two homes on the property that meet our current
       housing standards, which, due to State law changes, are significantly reduced when
       compared with our prior standards.

Councilmember Nordfelt asked if the lots would all be narrow. Steve replied yes and noted
    MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JANUARY 25, 2022
                           -4-


that they would each be about 65 feet wide. Councilmember Whetstone stated that the
Board of Adjustment often grants variances for this type of situation and asked why this is
being proposed differently. Steve replied that variances are granted for properties that have
a unique shape or situation that would not allow for a normal development that other
properties are able to obtain. He indicated that this suggestion is just a trade-off. He
indicated that the State Legislature took away rights from municipalities to govern specific
architectural features and home sizes on newly constructed homes. He noted that this
Development Agreement would allow the applicant to construct an additional home but
would require the homes to be built at the City’s previous standard which is higher than
what State code currently describes. Councilmember Nordfelt asked what the difference in
standard is. Steve replied one of the major differences is the size of a home. He indicated
that State code requires a minimum of 1,000 square feet where the City’s previous code
required homes to be built at 2,000 square feet or higher. He indicated many of the
architectural standards are in conflict as well. Councilmember Harmon stated that 4100
South is a busy street and he would be concerned about too many driveways backing out
onto an already difficult road. Councilmember Lang asked if garages are addressed in State
Code. Steve replied State Code did not address garages so a 3 car garage would still be
required.

The City Council will consider Resolution 22-23 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
February 1, 2022 at 6:30 P.M.

CONSIDER APPLICATION PUD-2-2021, FILED BY KEN MILNE, REQUESTING
FINAL PLAT APPROVAL FOR PROPERTY LOCATED AT 6087 WEST
PARKWAY BOULEVARD
Steve Lehman, CED, presented proposed Application PUD-2-2021, Filed by Ken Milne,
Requesting Final Plat Approval for Property Located at 6087 West Parkway Boulevard

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Mr. Ken Milne, is requesting final approval for the Heather Villas PUD. The
       subject property was rezoned in April 2021 from the A zone to the RM zone. The
       rezone was accompanied by a development agreement reviewed and approved by
       the City Council. The subject property is bordered on the north by Parkway Blvd,
       the east by vacant land owned by UDOT and the south and west by existing single
       family residential housing.

       Heather Villas will be a residential community for senior living having a total of 84
       units on just under 14 acres. This equates to an overall density of 6.1 units per acre.

       A club house is also proposed for this site and will be located near the entrance of
       Parkway Boulevard.

       Residential units will be one story in height. All units will have a loft option that
       would add approximately 900 square feet to each dwelling if selected by future
       buyers. The developer is proposing units that are 1587, 1717, 1918 and 2024 square
       feet in size. All units will have a large two-car garage of approximately 680 square
       feet where a standard size is 400 square feet.

       Building architecture is the same as reviewed during the rezone application.
       Materials will be stone, fiber cement siding and stucco. All units will have a private
       patio that is accessible from the dining and master bedroom areas. The developer
       is proposing a minimum of 2 and potentially 3 different color schemes to be used
       throughout the project to add variety.

       As mentioned previously, all townhome units will have a large two-car garage for
       parking. All units will have a driveway that will be sufficient in depth to allow
       parking. In addition to the garage and driveway spaces, the developer is providing
       guest parking in and through the project as well. The parking ratio for this project
       is 4.5 spaces per unit.

       The developer is proposing 3 amenities for this project. The proposal includes a
       club house of approximately 3,400 square feet to include a gathering room with
       kitchen, theater room, fitness room and mail room. A walking path around the
       perimeter of the development, and a pickle ball court is planned on the east side of
       the clubhouse. Although not an amenity listed in the development agreement, each
       unit will have a private courtyard or patio space with access from the kitchen and
       master bedroom.

Councilmember Fitisemanu asked what the parking ratio would be. Steve replied all units
will have 4 spaces and there are an additional 20 visitor spaces for a total of 4.5 spaces per
unit. He indicated that staff is comfortable with what is being proposed. Councilmember
Harmon asked if on-street parking will be allowed on the main loop. Steve replied no and
indicated that there will be an HOA that governs this as well as various maintenance issues,
snow removal, etc. Councilmember Lang asked if the proposal meets fire code
requirements. Steve replied yes and noted that 100 multifamily units can be accessed on a
single point. He noted, however, that the developer is working with Public Works and
UDOT on a potential secondary access to 5900 West. Councilmember Harmon asked
where this second access would be located. Steve replied it would be in the southwest
corner of the project and will be determined at a future phase. Councilmember Huynh asked
if this is a wetland area. Steve replied no. Councilmember Harmon asked if basements are

COMMUNICATIONS
A.  ANNUAL TRAINING REGARDING ETHICS, OPEN MEETINGS, AND
    SEXUAL HARRASSMENT
    Eric Bunderson, City Attorney, provided a quiz to the Mayor and Council and staff.
    He stated that all elected officials are required to receive this training under the
    Open Meetings Act. Eric stated that West Valley City is a Council-Manager form
    of government. He noted that this form of government no longer exists in State
    Code so the City is one of many that are grandfathered in. He stated that Wayne
    Pyle acts as the executive of the City while the Mayor and City Council act as the
    legislative body. He noted that the specific duties of each are defined under State
    Statute. Eric stated that the Mayor and Council can vote on items that they have a
    conflict of interest in as long as they declare that on the record prior to voting. He
    stated that all Council’s in the State of Utah are part-time and cannot make a living
    as a Councilmember alone. Eric stated that bribery is still criminal. He noted that
    the Council can, however, accept non-monetary gifts under $50. He indicated that
    a good example of this would be an occasional lunch or dinner. Eric stated that
    Ethic Laws state that a Councilmember cannot take information that they obtain
    and use it for their own personal purpose. He stated that an example of this would
    be that a Councilmember cannot learn specific information and then use that
    information to receive monetary value. He added that the Council cannot use their
    position to get special privileges. Eric detailed the City’s sexual harassment policies
    and stated that the City has a great process in place for preventing and investigating
    these types of allegations. Eric indicated that the majority of the Council body
    cannot meet in any circumstance unless that meeting is properly noticed under the
    Open Meeting requirements in State Code. He detailed the rare circumstances in
    which the Council can meet in a closed session. Councilmember Whetstone asked
    if there is an insurance bond for Councilmembers. Eric replied yes and noted that
    there are bonds and insurance in place against any kind of liability. He noted that
    there are also protections in place that allow governmental immunity if the Council
    listens to the City Attorney’s advice on any issue.

       The Mayor and Council had no further questions or concerns.

A.   POTENTIAL FUTURE AGENDA ITEMS

    Energy Star Programs
    Councilmember Harmon stated that Energy Star Homes are something that he is
    very familiar with in his current career. He stated that contractors are also familiar
    with this program and noted that it is a national standard. He asked why the City
    chose to go with the standards that they did in the RS zone and not this national
    program that is recognized by many. Wayne indicated that this was a policy
    decision by the Council. He noted that the Council discussed their priorities and
    requested specific items. Wayne stated that the City is flexible and happy to modify
    the ordinance as the Council directs. Councilmember Harmon stated that he
    believes the homes in the new RS subdivision that was approved by Council will
    be great but from a marketing perspective, he believes the Energy Star Program is
    a standard that is recognized and understood and can be more efficiently marketed.
    He noted that a third party gives the developments a rating. Councilmember
    Christensen stated that the idea of the RS zone was to give a higher standard than
    the Energy Star Program. Councilmember Harmon agreed but noted that some
    standards are lesser. Councilmember Huynh stated that the Council was still
    learning when the zone was put in place and knew that it would and likely should
    be modified in the future. Councilmember Fitisemanu agreed that the initial
    discussion was appropriate but stated that he is not opposed to changing standards
    to meet the Energy Star Program. He added that the City could also still include
    additional ratings or ratings at a higher standard. Mayor Lang stated that it may be
    wise to revisit the ordinance and discuss potential changes. Councilmember
    Nordfelt agreed and stated that it may be good to speak with the developer of the
    newly approved RS development to see if they have suggestions to reach the City’s
    goals. Councilmember Fitisemanu asked if this would conflict with the State
    Legislature removing municipality rights at defining housing standards. Steve
    replied that the City cannot require higher energy standards than what the State
    provides but can require higher standards when a developer requests a zone change
    to the RS Zone with a Development Agreement.
