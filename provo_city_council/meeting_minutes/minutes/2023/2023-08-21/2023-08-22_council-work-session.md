3. An update to the Municipal Council regarding improvements on Center Street in
   downtown Provo and associated construction. (23-060) (00:22:36)

    Gordon Haight, City Engineer presented. He talked about some upcoming changes
    they’re going to make along Center St. to make it more walkable and pedestrian
    friendly. This project was scheduled to start at the end of August and completed before
    Labor Day. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth wondered when these new improvements
    could be expanded throughout the city. Mr. Haight answered that after the city adopted
    the 31 ideas that came from TMAC and the Planning Commission, and that a
    comprehensive plan would include some of the infrastructure components underneath
    the streets which would put the total bill between $8 million – $11 million dollars and
    the involvement of several other city groups. Vice-Chair Rachel Whipple inquired about
    I-15 signage and was told that they would need to coordinate some with UDOT but that
    some preliminary ideas were being drawn up before being presented to UDOT as that
    group typically likes to see a basis of an idea so they can collaborate from there.
    Presentation Only

4. A presentation regarding the Annual Fraud Risk Assessment. (23-049) (00:40:49)

   Presenting John Borget, Administrative Services Director. He went over the background
   of the assessment (directive from the State Auditor’s Office) and the different parts that
   are included within it. He also talked about how the city needs to select an internal
   auditor. He tried to grade the city fairly and explained his reasoning behind each score.
   Vice-Chair Rachel Whipple asked when Provo had lost their internal auditor and how
   long the process has gone on. Mr. Borget answered that the auditor left a little over a year
   ago. He went on to explain that one of the challenges that we have is that those that
   respond to the Request for Proposals (RFP) were more expensive and some were not
   located within Utah which could potentially require travel costs but could possibly work.
   Justin Harrison, Executive Director of the City Council, added that there were seven
   responses to the RFP and the next action is for the audit committee to make its review of
   those RFPs and select an internal auditor, if there’s a candidate that they like. He did say
   they could also start another RFP process if needed.
   Presentation Only

6. A presentation addressing the dog problem. (23-059) (01:13:43)

   Wayne Parker, CAO, presenting. This presentation is in response to residents
   complaining about dogs running at large and on parks and trails, contrary to leash laws
   and the waste that they leave behind. Mr. Parker presented some different options the
   council could take and there was also testimony by the animal enforcement part of Provo
   P.D. and the Police Captain over that group as well. Chair Katrice MacKay asked if there
   was any kind of penalty fees associated with these problems and Mr. Parker stated there
   is and it’s in the form of a citation. Overall, Councilor Handley supported strong
   educational efforts on this as did Councilor Bill Fillmore. Chair MacKay and Vice Chair
   Rachel Whipple were in supportive of reviewing current code and seeing if any updates
   were needed. Discussion was had regarding the restricting animals at city events such as
   the Farmer’s Market and perhaps Sports Parks.

   A motion was made for the administration to return with a plan for education,
   enforcement and associated costs.

   Passed Unanimously 7-0

   A motion was made for staff to come back with recommendations on policy including
   definitions of service and emotional support animals, places and events where dogs
   should be restricted, and what code changes may need to be made regarding
   enforcement and penalties.

   Passed Unanimously 7-0

8. A discussion regarding the Provo City property tax rate for general operations. (23-
   058) (02:32:55)

   Justin Harrison, Council Executive Director, presenting. This is a follow-up request from
   the August 8th Truth in Taxation hearing, where staff was directed to prepare two possible
   rates for the general operations portion of the Provo City property tax. Council staff will
   present tax rates of 0.000587, which would bring additional revenue into the general fund
   of $254, 316, and a rate of 0.000567, which would bring in additional revenue to the
   General Fund of $151,943. Councilor Dave Shipley wanted to know if the amount for the
   library is included in these two rates (as originally advertised) or if it was separate.
   Director Harrison responded that the library would be added to it, for the final resolution.
   The Councilors than took turns sharing their views on the two amounts and which they
   supported and why, Councilor Hoban preferred to find another way to obtain the funds
   for what the taxes would cover.
   Brian Jones, Provo City Attorney did make the clarifying statement of wanting to be sure
   the Councilors understood that the Council did not have to make/pass a motion in this
   meeting, but if one was not chosen then at the night meeting the budgeted ordinance
   would include a rate of 0.000560.

   A motion was made to adopt the property tax rate of 0.000576.

   The motion failed 3-4 with Councilors Hoban, Shipley, Fillmore and Whipple
   against.

   A second motion was made to adopt the general operations tax rate of 0.000587.

   The motion failed 3-4 with Councilors MacKay, Ellsworth, Hoban and Shipley
   against.

   A third motion was made and seconded to pass the original motioned amount of
   0.000576.

   Passed 6-1 with Councilor Hoban against.

   9. A discussion regarding expanding the tax increment finance agreement with the
      Noorda College of Osteopathic Medicine. (23-045) (03:27:44)

      Presenting Keith Morey, Assistant Director of Development Services, presenting. His
      group would like the RDA Board to do two things: one – reset the Tax Increment Finance
      (TIF) clock and two – include in the completed medical college building that has already
      been constructed. Due to unforeseen events like COVID and the supply chain hardships it
      caused, the Noorda group would like the city to remain partners with a group that is
      trying hard to revitalize an area of Provo that has been neglected for years. Board
      Member Dave Shipley wanted to make sure he understood the numbers correctly and
      received some clarification. Board Member Travis Hoban wondered how much the city
      would be giving up to make this work and he’d rather use some of those funds to help
      other projects. Some of the board members wanted to make sure that the tools they have
      were being used properly and in the wisest manner possible. Board Members
      contemplated if it was abnormal to ask to extend TIF to a building that was already built.
      Other board members supported the project but wanted to be sure there would be exact
      start/stop dates so that they could plan for other projects and most of the board wanted
      there to be some kind of accountability in place, within the resolution that should the
      builder not meet certain benchmarks, some of the TIF money would be returned to Provo
      City.

      A motion was made to expand the project area, including the existing building, and
      extend the redevelopment period from 12 to 20 years.

      An amended motion was made to instruct staff to bring back an agreement that defined
      the 20-year period, including proposed project milestones at 5 and 10 years, including
      associated penalties for failure to meet milestones.

      Passed Unanimously 7-0
