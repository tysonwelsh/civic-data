         The purpose of the work session was to discuss proposed rezones at 981 12th Street/1245 Monroe Boulevard and 605
North Jackson Avenue; discuss the proposed Ogden City Fiscal Year 2024-2025 budget for the Police Department, Management
Services Department, Public Services, and proposed Schedule A Changes; discuss Council business; and hear Council comments.

Proposed rezone – 981 12th Street/1245 Monroe Boulevard
         Deputy Planning Manager Simpson explained Jeremy Krause has submitted a petition to amend the zoning boundary
between two parcels so that the zoning boundary follows the property line. The properties involved are the Dylan’s restaurant
property on the corner of Monroe Boulevard and 12th Street and the property directly south of Dylan’s which has R-3 zoning on
the entire property. The Planning Commission held a public hearing on this proposal on March 6, 2024, and recommended
approval of the proposal subject to the following conditions:
              1. The petitioner completing the small subdivision plat process to have the property line between 981 12th Street
                  and 1245 Monroe Boulevard follow the new zoning boundary lines.
              2. The existing row of parking stalls be shifted out of the required 10’ wide landscaped setback along west
                  property line and the new property line/zoning line, and also the required 20' wide landscaped setback along
                  Monroe Boulevard., which will need to be completed under a site plan review process.
              3. The dumpster for Dylan’s Restaurant be placed in screened enclosure out of the setbacks along the streets and
                  be approved through a site plan review process.
         Mr. Simpson used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the history of the property as well as the
implications of the zone change; for a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
Discussion among the Council and Mr. Simpson centered on parking and setback requirements for the Dylan’s Restaurant and
preliminary site plan documents that have been submitted for the proposed improvements.

Proposed rezone – 605 North Jackson Avenue
         Planning Manager Brierley explained Ogden City Community Development has submitted a petition to rezone 6.9 acres
from R-1-6 to R-1-5. The City purchased 6.5 acres from the school district as surplus property and seeks to establish zoning that
would allow slightly smaller single-family lots than the current zoning allows. A portion of the 6.9 acres is in the process of being
purchased from the school district. The Planning Commission held a public hearing for the proposal on May 1, 2024, and
recommended approval of the petition. Mr. Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the history of the subject
property and the uses of the surrounding properties. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for
the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and staff centered on public improvements that will be completed on the streets
surrounding the property – specifically along Jackson Avenue – that will be completed as part of the proposed subdivision of the
property; potential density of the residential development; and the price points of the homes to be built in the project.
          Council member Hyer expressed concern about changes to the road layout around the property; when discussing the real
estate transaction for this property with the Ogden School District Board, the City had made some commitments relating to the
existing cul-de-sac and other transportation improvements and he feels those commitments are not being honored, which could be
cause for concern for the residents that live in the area. There was brief discussion regarding this matter and the Council
concluded that concerned residents can voice their position to City staff or the Planning Commission as the development
application moves through the process.

Proposed Ogden City Fiscal (FY) Year 2024-2025 Budget
          Police Department: Police Chief Young used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed FY2025
budget for his Department which also includes investigations, support services, uniform police and the Weber Morgan Narcotics
Strike Force. The Police Department general budget will increase by 8.5 percent when compared to FY2024. Significant budget
line-item changes for the Police Department budget include wage and benefit increases, fleet replacement adjustments,
professional and technical expense increases, and contract maintenance increases. The Police Department consists of 192 total
employees, the majority of which are police officers and administrative support. The department budget accounts for 177
employees, with 12 police officers, 1 homeless advocate, and two victim services program coordinators charged out and budgeted
under miscellaneous grants. The Department is proposing one additional police officer position and a title change for the Police
Division Commander to Police Division Captain. Relative to the Weber Narcotics Strike Force, Ogden City provides six
officer/agent positions, one sergeant, one lieutenant, and one office assistant. The bulk of the Strike Force funds come from state
and federal grants, and funds are managed in a Trust and Agency Fund. The Strike Force budget is increasing by 20 percent when
compared to the FY2024 budget.
          For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          There was discussion among the Council and Chief Young regarding general operations of his Department; partnerships
with other entities in the community, or even new technology, that can assist with crime deterrence and some enforcement
measures; crime statistics in the community; staffing for special events; animal licensing fees; compensation trends for public
safety agencies across the State of Utah; recent rumors regarding citation quotas in Ogden City and other cities in the State;
recruitment and retention of employees in the Department; grant funding in the Department; and expanded training opportunities
for Department employees.
          Management Services Department: Management Services Director Stout used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
summarize the proposed FY2025 budget for her Department which also includes internal service funds for Fleet and Facilities,
Risk Management, and Information Technology. The Management Services general budget will increase by 8.03 percent, when
compared to the FY2024 budget. Fleet and Facilities budget will increase by 2.57 percent, Risk Management by 1.71 percent, and
Information Technology will decrease by 0.21 percent. Significant changes to the Management Services Department relate to
administration and marketing staffing, creation of a paid parking fund – including staffing for the new function, and wage and
benefit adjustments in all other divisions of the Department.
          For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          There was discussion among the Council and Ms. Stout regarding general operations of her Department; cost saving
measures in the Department over the past year; change management; immediate and ongoing challenges of the various divisions
of the Department; benefit adjustments for employees City-wide; and the timeline of the implementation of the paid parking
system and rates for paid parking.
          Management Services Department Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) Projects: Ms. Stout indicated her proposed
FY2025 budget includes three CIP projects:
               • FL007 – Maintain and care for existing facilities;
               • FL039 – Safety improvements at the Public Safety Building and Community and Parks Buildings;
               • FL001 – Developing a plan for improvements needed as part of the Amphitheater Restroom remodel project.
          Proposed Schedule A Changes: Council Deputy Director Symes reported on May 7, 2024, Mayor Benjamin K. Nadolski
presented his proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget. There are two changes proposed as Schedule A changes so far. The first is
an adjustment for the addition of the new Service Writer/Stores Clerk position. This was not included in the proposed budget. As
proposed, an additional $60,400 would be used from Fund Balance as well as a reduction in Part Time Wages of $23,250 to total
the $83,650 needed for the position. The second change is specific to the final Entitlement Grant numbers received from U.S.
Housing and Urban Development (HUD) for the Annual Action Plan. The Council adopted an updated Annual Action Plan on
May 7. The corresponding changes are included in this proposed Schedule A change. The adjustments include a total reduction of
$41,275 in the Major Grants section of the budget.
          Public Services Department: Public Services Director Anderson and Deputy Director Nielsen used the aid of a
PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed FY2025 budget for their Department, which includes the Water, Sanitary
Sewer, Storm Sewer, and Refuse Utilities, as well as Recreation, Engineering, Parks and Cemetery, Operations, and Golf. With
respect to the utilities funds – which are enterprise funds – significant changes when compared to the FY2024 budget include the
prescribed annual Consumer Price Index (CPI) increase to the charges for services. This increase is based on the Blended
Inflationary Adjustment (BIA) adopted by the City Council in 2021. In addition, the administration is proposing additional rate
increases for the Water Utility to fund debt service associated with the estimated $77 Million bonds for the 36” Canyon Water
Line. The proposed increases for the Utilities are as follows:
              • Water Utility: 7.18%
              • Sanitary Sewer: 3.18%
              • Storm Sewer: 3.18%
              • Refuse: 3.30%
         The presentation also summarized accomplishments from 2024; biggest immediate and ongoing challenges in the
Department; proposed adjustments to staffing levels in various divisions of the Department; new funding requests; a proposal to
shift away from the seasonal staffing model; special events at City facilities; significant construction projects that will be
underway in 2025; the 24th Street/Interstate 15 interchange project study; major equipment purchases; sidewalk repairs and the
50/50 sidewalk program; expansion of the Green Bike program; and proposed fee changes for the golf courses and recycling
program.
         For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Public Services Department Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) Projects: Mr. Nielsen identified CIP projects in the
Public Services Department that he expects will be completed in FY2025, including fencing and lighting for the Marshall N.
White Community Center, pickleball courts at Lester Park and Golden Hours, general park improvements and enhancements at
Lester Park, Mt. Ogden irrigation Phase One, pool maintenance, and the 2nd Street water main installation. He also touched on
significant CIP projects that will commence in FY2025, with a heavy focus on the Canyon Water Line project.

Council Business
         Council staff reviewed the Council calendar for the coming summer months.
         Chief Administrative Officer Brown provided details regarding an upcoming military training event at a City park. The
Council stressed the need to publicly advertise the event to help avoid a situation where the public is surprised by the event and
does not understand that it is a training event.
