     DISCUSSION – 2020-2024 Consolidated Plan for HUD-funded Programs

Ms. Mathews said a consolidated plan was required every five years, and they were working in
collaboration with Provo and Utah County to complete the plan. Dan Gonzalez with Provo and
Jessica DeLora with Utah County were present to answer questions. Ms. Mathews provided an
update on the collaboration with other entitlements, which included using the same application
process, timelines, and deadlines. She reviewed the anticipated Community Development Block
Grant (CDBG) funding based on the previous year’s amounts, but added that the current Trump
administration cut CDBG funding each year only to reinstate it at a later time. They were working
with the assumption that the same would happen this year. Discussion ensued regarding the
number of applicants seeking funding, CDBG program administration, a survey conducted by
Civicus Consulting Group to understand community priorities, and the futsal courts and
playground improvements at Geneva Park.

In response to questions from the City Council, Ms. Mathews said that there would be larger
projects that would be county-wide but also smaller projects for each entitlement. She reviewed
the joint funding process, and shared details about Orem-specific projects. To illustrate the
challenge the CDBG Advisory Commission faced in determining funding awards for applicants,
Ms. Mathews provided each council member with $800 in play money and set out large cards with
different categories for funding projects. She asked them to distribute their play money to see
which projects were deemed a priority.

        DISCUSSION – Neighborhood Partnership Initiative (NPI) Advisory Board Proposal

Mr. Downs reviewed the history of the City’s neighborhood programs. The Neighborhood
Partnership Initiative (NPI) was introduced in 2019 and had seen great results. Communication to
neighborhood residents was done through targeted emails, mailings, social media, utility bills,
newsletters, and more. He said there were approximately 19,500 Orem residents on the email list.
Neighborhood plans had been completed for eight of the nine neighborhood groups, and the final
plan was underway.

The Council had questions about the larger neighborhood areas, and whether that may cause
“splinter groups” to form within the areas. Mr. Downs said the point was well taken, but it was
more administratively manageable to have nine areas rather than 22, and they had decent
representation from the areas to create the neighborhood plans. He added that some from existing
“splinter groups” had been heavily involved and provided constructive feedback during the
planning process. Discussion continued regarding public outreach and opportunities for residents
to participate. Mayor Brunst asked that the Council be kept abreast of the program as it moved
forward.

Mr. Downs said the proposal was to set up a committee that represented each of the nine
neighborhood groups throughout the City. The makeup of the NPI Advisory Council would be one
representative from each neighborhood, and officer from the Neighborhood Preservation Unit
(NPU) in the Orem Police Department, and two council members that would serve as liaisons. The
suggestion was to have members selected through an application and interview process, and then
be approved to serve by the City Council to serve a three-year term. Mr. Downs reviewed the
criteria they would consider through the application and interview process, and the proposed
schedule of meetings.

The consensus of the Council was to move forward with the NPI Advisory Council. Mr. Downs
said he would return with the required language to create and codify the new advisory body at a

     City Council Reports (Boards and Commissions, New Business, Etc.)

Mr. Sumner gave a report on his assignments as a City Council liaison to the Public Works
Advisory Commission (PWAC), Library Advisory Commission, the UCCU Board at UVU, and
the Historical Preservation Advisory Commission. He said that a recent PWAC meeting had been
eye-opening when it came to the dedicated work of Public Works employees, particularly when it
came to snow removal and keeping the roads safe in the winter. They were the first to get up in
the early morning hours on a cold and snowy day to go out to clear the roads, and often missed
holiday time with their families, to ensure that others would be able to travel safely. He said the
Library and UCCU Board had not yet had meetings for the year so he had nothing to report. Finally,
he said there had been discussion of creating a time capsule in collaboration with the Historical
Preservation Commission.

Mayor Brunst provided an update of items currently in discussion in the 2020 State Legislative
Session.

Mr. Downs gave an update regarding the Geneva Steel property restoration, and said information
had gone out to residents regarding the project. He said the smell had been an issue, but they were
working on solutions to mitigate the odor and the State Department was monitoring the cleanup to
ensure safety for the public.

Mr. Davidson said they had reached the point in the Orem Fitness Center build project to send out
the fourth bid package, which was the largest and most expensive bid package. He said the Fitness
Center was trending a lower cost per square foot than they had projected. Weather had been
problematic in terms of construction, but they anticipated the work to ramp up as the weather
improved.

       RECOGNITION – “Medal of Heroism”

Fire Chief Gurney invited Amanda Tuttle-Reynolds and the fire crew that was in attendance to
come forward. He asked Deputy Chief Earl to present the “Medal of Heroism” to Ms. Tuttle-
Reynolds, explaining that it was the Orem Fire Department’s highest honor given to civilians.

Deputy Chief Earl said on October 12, 2019, a fire started at the Countryside Condominiums in
the apartment above Ms. Tuttle-Reynolds well after midnight. Barefoot and in her pajamas, Ms.
Tuttle-Reynolds ran door to door, knocking and alerting the other residents to the fire. A recently
moved-in single mother and her daughter from China were still in the building on the top floor,
struggling to get out. Ms. Tuttle-Reynolds rushed up the stairs amidst the thick, black smoke and
assisted them in moving to safety away from the building.

Deputy Chief Earl then presented Ms. Tuttle-Reynolds the “Medal of Heroism” and she, along
with the fire crew from that October night, received a standing ovation from the audience.

neighborhood to landscape the stretch of land on the south side of 800 North. He recalled being
told that 800 North was not a city road but a state road, and it could not be landscaped by the City.
However, the area from 900 West to 1200 West had been recently landscaped with rockwork so
he was confused. Mr. Johnson said the neighborhood was willing to provide the labor if the City
would provide the supplies and the design plan. The neighbors hoped to replace the weed patches
with more attractive landscaping, and he asked that they consider the request.

CITY MANAGER INFORMATION ITEMS

Mr. Davidson provided an update on issues currently before the State Legislature. He said staff
was following a number of bills, but a specific bill he felt the Council should be aware of was
House Bill 273. HB 273 was sponsored by Orem Representative Val Peterson and related to
amendments for the Property Rights Ombudsman. Mr. Davidson said the ombudsman typically
intervened in developer/government issues with property and offered an opinion. Those decisions
had been non-binding and had been taken as sound, credible legal advice relevant to development
laws. However, there was a current case being considered that would alter governmental immunity
in terms of compensatory damages that may be paid. Mr. Davidson said he wanted to bring this to
the City Council’s attention so they may track the bill’s progress, and he would forward any
additional information he received to them.

Mr. Davidson touched briefly on some development projects, including the Mike Dunn transfer
station on Geneva Road and the Orem LDS Temple site. He said the Dunn property appeared to
be for sale, so the municipal waste taken there would now be taken elsewhere. Also, there had not
yet been a site plan submitted by the LDS Church because it was still in the internal approval stage,
but he expected that to be forthcoming.

Finally, Mr. Davidson spoke about the fire chief recruitment process and expressed appreciation
to Chief Gurney for his commitment. He said they were casting a wide net in the state and
nationally seeking out the best candidate, and said it would be an inclusive process. This
recruitment process would take time, but he did not want to “hit the pause button” on what the
department already had in the works. Mr. Davidson said this would not be a democratic process
where there would be a vote, but they intended to include many voices in the assessment and
vetting of the candidates, and that input would be part of the data set used to select the new chief.
Deputy Chief Jason Earl would serve as the Interim Chief through the recruitment process.
