         The purpose of the work session was to review the agenda for the City Council business nieeting scheduled to begin at
6:00 p.m.; review the Fiscal Year 2019 Audit and Fiscal Year 2019 Consolidated Annual Financial Report; discuss Council
business; and hear Council comments.

Agenda Review
          Chair Choberka and members of Council staff briefly reviewed the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to
begin at 6:00 p.m. There was a focus on the agenda item dealing with the proposed Lynn Community Plan amendment and rezone
at 140-152 West 2 nd Street. Planning Manager Montgomery discussed some of the feedback he has received from other property
owners on 2 nd Street and the petitioner. Council Deputy Director Symes indicated the petitioner has communicated his intent to
adjust the design of the proposed development he would complete on the subject property; if the Council is willing to consider
those adjustments, they have the option of tabling action on the Plan amendment and rezones. He summarized the other options
available to the Council this evening. Mr. Montgomery discussed the type of development the property owner could pursue if the
current zoning were upheld. Mr. Montgomery and Mr. Symes facilitated discussion among the Council regarding the petitioner's
plans for the property; this led to discussion regarding the type of development that is appropriate for the neighborhood. No
consensus was reached and Chair Choberka concluded to continue the discussion in the meeting; given that not all Council
members were present, there was a discussion regarding the appropriateness of tabling the action on the Plan amendment and
rezone for one week. Council Deputy Director Symes suggested the Council receive the presentation from Planning staff and hear
any public input before taking an action to table the items.

Fiscal Year (FY) 2019 Audit and Fiscal Year 2019 Consolidated Annual
Financial Report
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reported Paul Skeen and Alana Echols of Eide Bailly will review the Audit
Report and answer any questions Council members have regarding the audit findings. City administration will then review the
Consolidated Annual Financial Report (CAFR) and will also answer questions.
          Mr. Skeen smmnarized the audit responsibilities of his firm; the audit approach used is called a risk-based approach. The
auditor does not examine every expenditure of the City and, instead, determines areas of focus based upon past experience or
complexity of transactions and then develops an audit plan to address those risks. The City's finance team has experienced
tremendous challenges over the last few years and relative to general accounting and related management practices, this year's
audit appears to be the most efficient in comparison to prior years. The City's overall financial health is defined in the CAFR and
the City seems to be doing well. However, there are definitely areas of the budget that are much tighter than others. This may be
by design, but there are funds in the City that are being subsidized by others. Recmmnendations for improvement are minimal, but
he would suggest that the City's financial team maintain their continuing education efforts that will aid in improving operations.
He then discussed the difference between a material weakness and significant deficiency; material weakness is the most severe

           Ms. Echols then discussed any upcoming adjustments to Government Accounting Standards Board (GASB) standards
that could impact the City's accounting procedures. First is GASB 87 regarding leases and will be effective July 1, 2020. The City
will need to recognize certain lease assets and liabilities for leases that previously were classified as operating leases and
recognized as inflows ofresources or outflows ofresources based on the payment provisions of the contract. This change will
apply to long-tenn leases, which are leases of 12 months or longer. She concluded there are other GASB changes on the horizon,
but none that will impact the City in the next FY.
           Council member Stephens asked ifthere were any orders from the State Auditor that impacted this year's audit. Mr.
Skeen stated this was the first year in several that there were no significant changes to audit requirements; however, there may be
some changes at the conclusion of the current State Legislative Session.
           Council Executive Director Eller-Smith thanked Mr. Skeen and Ms. Echols for the information provided. She noted this
is the last year of the current contract with Eide Bailly and the City is in the process of working through the procurement process
to determine who will have the audit contract going forward.
           Comptroller Stout approached and introduced her staff members present and who participate in the annual audit process
as well as the creation of the annual CAFR. She summarized the role the Finance Division of the City plays in the audit process.
She then facilitated a review of the CAFR; she focused briefly on the City's support of the Airport Fund and Golf Fund and
indicated that administration will be asking the Council to consider continuing supp01t in the coming fiscal year. She reviewed
interfund loans that, when totaled, have an outstanding balance of$6.9 million; most of the loans are related to Redevelopment
Agency (RDA) activities and the funds responsible for the debt are not able to support payment amounts. She then moved to a
review of the General Fund balance, which she deemed to be healthy; this is due to a combination of several things that have
occurred in the City in recent years. The greatest positive impact on the fund balance is related to the actions taken by
Administration and the City Council to support maintaining or increasing the City's property tax rate. Ms. Stout indicated
management agrees with each of the three findings in the audit and she summarized the actions taken by her and her staff to
ensure that the same situations do not occur in the future that led to the finding.
           Ms. Stout then referred to page 74 of the CAFR document, which contains note seven relating to long-term liabilities.
This is a schedule of the balances of all long-term debt in the City. To date, the Fleet and Facilities Fund has paid off all capital
leases. This is significant given that in 2009 the City owed 4.2 million for leased equipment; she is pleased with the direction in
which that fund is moving and feels that debt reduction speaks to the financial health of the City.
