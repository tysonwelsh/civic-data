                           MEMORANDUM
To:           Honorable Mayor and City Council
From:         Planning Department
Date:         March 3, 2026
Subject:      Ordinance 26-04, “Priority Builders Rezone,” PLZ-25-4012, General Plan
              Amendment and Rezone of approximately 0.71-acres located at 1758 and
              1742 West 11800 South. Changing the General Plan Designation to High
              Density Residential and the zoning to RM-8-SD, a multi-family residential
              zone up to 8 dwelling units per acre. Applicant- Scott Yermish of Priority
              Builders


On February 12, 2026, the Planning Commission held a public hearing to review this
application. Minutes from that meeting are included below. The Planning Commission voted
to recommend approval of this rezone application, with the following recommended motion:

I move the City Council approve Ordinance 26-04, the “Priority Builders Rezone,” amending
the General Plan Designation to High Density Residential and rezoning the subject properties
to RM-8-SD for the approximately 0.71-acres located at 1758 and 1742 West 11800 South.
With the following Specific Development (SD) Designation(s):

     1-The development shall be limited to ‘townhomes’ only.

Background

This is a public hearing for a rezone application brought by the applicant, Scott Yermish
representing Priority Builders. This request covers two parcels known as the Baird properties,
which are located on 11800 South. These parcels are adjacent to one another, and they border
on other property, which was rezoned on June 3, 2025 to the RM-8-SD. If this rezone request
is approved, the properties in this Baird rezone request would be joined with those rezoned in
June 2025 to make one project.

The subject properties of this rezone have existing single-family homes at 1742 West and 1758
West on 11800 South. The homes were built in 1956 and 1970 and are occupied by renters.
The two (2) parcels included in the application with the following parcel numbers:
   • 27-22-379-011
   • 27-22-379-012

The northwest corner of 11800 South and Redwood Road was a mixture of zones prior to the
June 2025 rezone. Now it consists of only RR-22 and RM-8 Zones. The subject Baird properties,
have the existing RR-22 zone as do two parcels to the east, and one to the west of the subject
properties. These other properties are not a part of this rezone request and would remain RR-
22. To the north is RM-8-SD zoning.
The General Plan in the area includes High-Density Residential, Medium-High Density
Residential, and Estate Density Residential. With the Estate Density applied to the older
properties in the area, including the Baird Properties. This Rezone request would also change
the General Plan Designation on the Baird Properties to High Density Residential.

The requested RM-8 Zone allows for a maximum density of eight dwelling units per acre.
Permitted uses in the RM-8 Zone include Condos, Townhomes (attached or detached),
Residential planned developments, parks, open space, and household pets.           The SD
designation would limit the housing type to “Townhomes Only” consistent with the June 2025
Rezone.

The applicant describes his proposed use on the property as “Townhome Community that mimics
plans from the adjoining townhome subdivisions to the northwest and south.”

If the zoning were approved there would be additional applications, studies, reports and public
hearings for the site plan design.



Public Notices and Response
Notices were sent to all property owners within 1,000 feet of this site and posted on the City’s
webpage.

The Planning Commission held a public hearing on this application at which they voted to
recommend approval. Staff concurs with that recommendation.

Comment – Baird Rezone – Sterling


I received the notice for the application to rezone this property. I am a neighbor to this
development.



I would like to submit the following comments:



I am against the rezoning proposal. These parcels sit around 4 other parcels that are not
zoned as high density and while we need more housing , this is not the place or the property
to allow it. The entire neighborhoods surrounding the property are a current small buffer to
the existing high density that is to the north and south of the space. It is clearly a best
practice amongst all city planning to break up the zoning so there are breaks and dividers
that are natural and normal. Trying to make all of redwood road properties high density is
not desirable for anyone and not for the city. Not allowing this zoning will set the precedent
that there is a natural and needed break in the flow of usages along this area of the city and
too much is too much…
