A. REQUEST TO VACATE CITY-OWNED RIGHT-OF-WAY LOCATED AT

APPROXIMATELY 320 WEST PARK STREET

Alex Murphy said Midvale City owns a 30’ wide right-of-way at approximately 320 West Park
Street and has received a request to vacate the right-of-way by Frank LeBaron and the Canyons
School District (“CSD”). Mr. LeBaron and CSD each own property adjacent to the right-of-way
and Mr. LeBaron is interested in pursuing a single-family residential development on his property.
The right-of-way was dedicated to the City as Jordan Avenue with the Eastvale Addition
Subdivision in 1917. At the time, the right-of-way was dedicated at a half width with the
intent to dedicate the remaining half width on the next plat to the west. Prior to the next
subdivision occurring, the adjacent property was developed into a school and no
additional property was dedicated to the City to finish the right-of-way.
The Jordan Avenue right-of-way is the only frontage available to several lots created by
the Eastvale Addition plat (Lots 6-17 of Block 3). However, public road standards require
a minimum of 50’ of right-of-way width, which is not available for these properties. As a
result, the properties are landlocked and have sat undeveloped for decades.
Should the right-of-way vacation be approved, the ROW property would be returned to
the adjacent property owners. Mr. LeBaron, who owns the northern 3 parcels (Lots 11-17
of Block 3) along Jordan Avenue, would then be able to install a private road to access
his properties. CSD owns the southern 2 parcels (Lots 6-10 of Block 3) and has leased
those parcels to the City with the rest of the adjacent park. Neither the City nor CSD plan
to extend the right-of-way or develop park property, so the park configuration would
remain as-is.
The City’s Municipal Code dictates the process vacation requests must go through. Per
§12.16.050, this request was forwarded to other affected City departments to determine
potential impacts to the City. The affected City departments concluded vacation of the
right-of-way was appropriate and raised the following concerns to be addressed during
the process:

1. A sewer line runs through the right-of-way before cutting west across the adjacent
park. A water line and storm drain line may also run through the area requested to
be vacated, depending on the exact boundary.

2. The parking lot servicing the community center will need to be reconfigured to
provide the private street access.

3. There are multiple large trees that may be impacted by the construction of a private
road. If removal of the trees is necessary, the developer will be expected to
complete their removal at his own expense.

4. The adjacent park’s irrigation system may run through the area to be vacated. If it
does, the developer will be expected to reroute and reconnect the system at his
own expense.

5. The right-of-way and adjacent properties are in the SF-2 zone, which allows single
family detached development. Any future development on this parcel will be ]
required to comply with the development standards in effect at the time of
application.

Staff has reviewed the request and concerns raised by other City departments and agrees
with the conclusion that the City should vacate the right-of-way with conditions to address
the identified concerns.

All vacation requests require a review and recommendation from the Planning
Commission and approval from the City Council with public hearings held before each
body. The Planning Commission conducted a public hearing regarding this request on
May 13, 2020 and forwarded a recommendation to the City Council to approve the request
with the following conditions of approval:

1. A public utility easement shall be provided to ensure future access to existing utility
lines.

2. The developer shall be responsible for any costs incurred with removing existing
trees, rerouting and reconnecting the existing park irrigation system, and
reconfiguring the community center parking lot to provide access. Each of these
activities shall be required to comply with the applicable City standards and
ordinances relating to design, installation, and acceptance of such activity.

The requested vacation is also a prerequisite for a subdivision plat also under

consideration by the Council. Should the Council decide to proceed with the vacation,

Staff will prepare an ordinance approving the request and vacating the right-of-way for

final approval in a later meeting so that the ordinance and final subdivision plat can be
reviewed and approved at the same time.

FISCAL IMPACT:

Because the street vacation returns property to the original owner or successor in interest,

no compensation will be required or accepted for the property. Vacating the right-of-way

will shed responsibility for ongoing maintenance and costs associated with any

improvements will be the responsibility of the developer.

Should the right-of-way remain under City control, the City will continue to be responsible

for ongoing maintenance costs and may be obligated to provide access to the adjacent

properties on a substandard public road.

Alex Murphy said Frank LeBaron, representing JLF Investments LC, is requesting approval to
subdivide property located at approximately 320 West Park Street into four lots to create new
developable lots. The subject property is approximately 0.77 acres in size and is bounded by
Midvale City Park on the south and west, Park Street on the north, and existing single-family
detached residences on the east. The proposed subdivision would create 4 lots for new single-

L family detached residences accessed by a private street. This proposal is dependent on the

Jordan Avenue vacation being approved and this Report assumes approval by the Council of the
vacation request.

The proposed subdivision is subject to Midvale City Municipal Code Title 16
(Subdivisions) and Chapter 17-7-2 (Single Family Residential [SF-2] Zone).

Because the proposal includes 4 total lots, the large subdivision process laid out in
Section 16.04.040 applies. With the exceptions identified in the numbered list below, Staff
has found the proposed subdivision complies with the requirements of Title 16 and
Chapter 17-7-2. The proposed lots comply with the lot standards of the SF-2 zone and
the proposed private street meets the right-of-way width and layout requirements of Title
16. Maintenance of the private street will be the responsibility of the property owners and
a private maintenance agreement has been prepared for the private street accessing the
proposed lots. Future development on the properties will be required to comply with the
SF-2 zone requirements.

1. The boundary of the subdivision plat and requested vacation do not match along
Park Street. The plat will need to be adjusted to retain the 50’ right-of-way required
for Park Street.

INTERNAL SERVICE FUNDS

The City has a Fleet Fund, where operations and replacement costs are assessed to all :

City budgets which use vehicles. As such, operations costs are recognized in the

appropriate departments. Funds required to replace a vehicle at the end of its life are

available in the Fleet Fund, eliminating the need to lease or borrow, and smoothing the

annual operating fund budgets. As a budget saving measure in fiscal year 2021, the

General Fund departments’ contributions and replacements are being delayed one year.

The City also has an Information Technology Internal Service Fund. This fund operates

in the same manner as the Fleet Fund. Departments contribute annually for maintenance

of computers and for replacement of hardware at the end of its useful life. We are

recommending funding to this fund due to the critical nature of Information Technology

infrastructure, and the negative consequences that will occur if end-of life equipment and

IX. DISCUSSION ITEMS
A. DISCUSS MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING WITH THE UTAH
PROSECUTION COUNCIL TO USE A PROGRAM CALL
EPROSECUTOR
Lisa Garner said currently the prosecution uses a software system called Pims. It is
managed through the Utah Prosecution Council. Pims will not be compatible with our new
server as far as being secure. It poses a threat to our system. The Utah Prosecution
Council went out to bid for a new software program. Eprosecutor was chosen for the
software program they will be going with. Staff would like to use this for our case
management system. In order to use this software would need to enter into a
Memorandum of Understanding and also with the technology owner of Eprosecutor. The
annual cost for using this software program is $3,600 per year. She requested this item
be brought back at the next council meeting on the consent agenda. The Council agreed.
