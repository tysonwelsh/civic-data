PUBLIC COMMENT:

  Jim Welburn addressed the council and publicly apologized for calling City Administrator out in a
previous meeting as a poor communicator. He thanked the council and the Nephi City employees that
worked on correcting the stormwater drainage problems in front of his home in Chapel View Estates
Subdivision. He stated that the water from the latest storm did not pool and flowed properly.
  Jennifer Rowley addressed the council about the issue of homes in Nephi sinking because of
collapsible soil. She proposed changes in the building code to require thicker foundations and more
rebar and concrete. She showed a poster of the process of home buyers and how the end owner of a
home may not have any idea that the home could sink. She showed the three homes that she had
owned in Nephi. She said that it is simple math to realize that it would be better to spend less on the
front end of building a home on a firmer foundation than spending much more money later if the home
eventually sinks.
  Cory Thomson addressed the council about the current vicious dog ordinance. His dog had recently
been killed by a vicious dog. He stated that if a dog runs at large, it should be impounded. He expressed
that it shouldn’t matter what a dog attacks whether it is a human being or another animal, the dog
should be impounded. He was disappointed that only one officer is on duty during the day, and he
thinks that needs to change. He stated that a simple change and more clarity in the code could help
with the dog problems.

ANNEXATION AREA CHANGE REQUEST APPROVED:

    City Recorder/Finance Director Lisa E. Brough reported to the council that the City had received a
request from Patrick Painter to change several parcels on the annexation area map to a Level 2. The
properties are in the vicinity of Sunset Rail which is located at 1075 North 500 West. Currently some of
the requested parcels are designated as a Level 2 and some do not have a designation which would
make them a Level 3. Ms. Brough stated that Mr. Painter was aware that any underlaps/overlaps or
property disputes would need to be resolved prior to annexation. Mr. Painter was in the room, and he
verified that all boundary disputes had been cleared up and that he is willing to bring water rights to the
table for this possible annexation. Changing the annexation area level from a Level 3 to a Level 2, would
trigger an analysis to determine whether the properties would be recommended to Level 1 and ready
for an annexation petition.
   Councilor Memmott expressed some concern with county roads in the area and Ms. Brough said that
all those type of questions can be addressed in the analysis. Councilor Ostler moved to change the
Parcels in yellow on the map owned by the Jarrett’s to a Level 2 on the annexation area map. Councilor
Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

RECOGNITION PLAQUES PRESENTED TO DANIEL AND HEIDI ALSUP AND EDGE AUTO:

  Chief of Police Michael Morgan and K-9 Officer Taylor Hurst and K-9 dog Grizz presented Dr. Daniel
Alsup, Heidi Alsup and McKenley Park, representing Edge Auto, with plaques in appreciation for their
generous contributions to the K-9 police program. The Alsups contributed a bullet-proof vest for Grizz
and Edge Auto contributed $5,000 to the program for food, supplies and other needs.
 Chief Morgan thanked the donors and said that already the K-9 program had confiscated many pounds
of drugs, including meth, cocaine, heroin, and marijuana.

GARRETTS RV PARK AGREEMENT EXTENSION APPROVED:

   City Attorney Kasey Wright explained to the Council that an agreement with Keti and Kyle Garrett to
build some necessary additions to their RV park to make the park compliant with a new city ordinance
needed to be extended. Keti Garrett said that they had hired a contractor, and they are making
progress, but it is taking longer than anticipated. The Garretts are building bathrooms and a laundry
facility. Amending the agreement would give them until November 6th to get the work completed.
  Councilor Memmott moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the amendment to the agreement to
extend the time allowed to make improvements to the Garrett’s RV park. Councilor Ostler seconded the
motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

AMENDMENT #2 TO DIVISION OF WILDLIFE RESOURCES AGREEMENT APPROVED:

  Ms. Brough updated the council that for the past couple of years, Nephi City has been engaged in a
land exchange process with the Utah State Department of Natural Resources, Division of Wildlife
Resources (DNR/DWR). The land exchange is meant to provide additional land to the east of the golf
course for the purposes of course expansion. To date, the city has negotiated with the U.S. Fish and
Wildlife Service to determine how the land exchange could take place, had a yellow book appraisal done
for both properties, had both properties surveyed, and had an environmental assessment
completed. All information for the exchange has been sent on to the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service for
their review. They are short-staffed and the review could take a while.
    The City anticipates an acceptance of the exchange, the deadlines specified in the original agreement
and amendment #1 have both expired and it is necessary to adopt another amendment to extend the
time frame. The amendment extends the deadline to June 30, 2024, for the first land exchange (City
land to DWR) and August 31, 2024, for the second land exchange (DWR land to City).
    Councilor Worwood moved to authorize the Mayor to sign amendment #2 to the original agreement
with the Utah State Division of Wildlife Resources. Councilor Callaway seconded the motion. The
motion carried a unanimous vote.

BENNETT PAVING AWARDED BID FOR AIRPORT HANGAR SPACES PAVING PROJECT:

  Councilor Callaway moved to approve the low bidder, Bennett Paving, for the hangar space paving
project at the airport. The bid came in at $282,108.00. Councilor Worwood seconded the motion.
Councilor Memmott abstained from voting due to a family relationship with Mr. Bennett. The motion
passed unanimously.

ANZALONE DRILLING AND PUMP AWARDED BID FOR THE WORWOOD WELL REPLACEMENT PROJECT:

   The City received three bids for the Worwood Well Replacement Project. The lowest bidder was
Anzalone Drilling and Pumps with a bid of $759,150.00. Councilor Worwood moved to accept the low
bid from Anzalone. Councilor Callaway seconded the motion. The motion carried on a unanimous vote.
The two other bidders were Lang Equipment, LLC ($1,222,980.00) and Hydro Resources-Rocky Mountain
Inc. ($1,272,780.00).

PUBLIC COMMENT-SAM EVANS:

  Mr. Sam Evans, Mortgage Loan Officer with Team USA Mortgage, asked the Mayor if he could have
some time because he had arrived late and missed the public comment portion of the meeting. Mayor
Seely allowed the comment. Mr. Evans informed the council about several grant opportunities available
to rural communities from T-Mobile and that UVU is extending their campus to Payson, and this could
bring opportunities for economic growth to the Nephi area.

CITY COUNCIL AND CITY ADMINISTRATIVE UPDATES:

       Councilor Worwood turned the time over to Library Director Ruth Bonzo who reported that the
        Library had recently received the “Quality Library Award”. The award is given to libraries that
        go above and beyond the requirements for certification with the Utah State Library. Ms. Bonzo
        shared with the council the six criteria that were met in order to receive the award. The council
        thanked Ms. Bonzo for all the hard work she is doing to make the library successful.

Work Session 8:40 pm

   1.  The council discussed a draft of the Nephi City Police Department Vehicle Use Policy. Councilor
      Memmott expressed that he thought it was fine for the police to use the police car for
      commuting and to drive to community events but was against using the city vehicle for personal
      run-around vehicles.
   2. The council discussed changing the vicious dog section of the Nephi City Code to be clearer and
      to have enhanced fines and charges for repeat violations.
   3. Public Works Director Kyle Marchant asked the council if they would be in favor of giving more
      pay to water/sewer employees that get their backflow administration and testing certificates.
      The council agreed to incentive pay for certificates earned.
   4. Councilor Memmott moved to adjourn the meeting. Councilor Worwood seconded the motion.
      The motion passed unanimously.
