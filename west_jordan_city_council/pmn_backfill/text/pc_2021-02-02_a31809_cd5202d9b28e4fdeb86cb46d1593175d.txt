2.    Park Valley Townhomes; 7652 South Highlands Loop Road; Preliminary Site Plan,
      Preliminary Subdivision Plat, Preliminary Development Plan; HFR Zone; Peterson
      Development/Barrett Peterson (applicant) [#21329, 21330, 21282; parcel 20-26-476-003;
      20-26-456-005; 20-26-400-028, 025, 016, 018, 020]

Kaylen Nichols, Peterson Development, said Park Valley is part of the Highlands community that was
approved in 2012. The project has arterial street access to 5600 West and 7800 South and is within
close proximity to the Mountain View Corridor. She reviewed the surrounding uses. She read from the
code the HFR zoning district’s purpose. The general plan and zoning designation call out the perfect
elements of this proposal next to the commercial zones and major roadways. They hope in the future
that UTA will bring in a rapid transit bus line to the area. There are trails as designed in the city trails
master plan. The HFR zone is performance based with required improvements that meet good planning
principals. This project incorporates installation and connection to the regional trail system, pedestrian
scale and consistent architectural lighting, traffic calming measures, good connective street design,
pedestrian friendly and walkable neighborhood design, and attractive theme-based architecture on all
four sides of the structures. Optional amenities allow for added densities. Before even looking at the
buy-ups, they created a program of amenities that would make a great project for this community as
well as the surrounding community. The proposal is for 217 units on 17.06 net acres at 12.7 units per
acre. The HFR zoning district allows for a density range of 9.01 – 18 du/ac. The property would
qualify for a minimum of 153 units and a maximum of 307, depending on optional amenities. With

their proposal, the optional amenities would allow a 51% buy-up, but they are only asking for 41%.
The design provides for the least amount of impact to the adjacent to Sommerglen Heights residents.
The buildings are situated so that only 14 end units are adjacent to 11 homes. If the buildings were
configured in the other direction, then there would be about 28 adjacent units. There is a 30-foot
landscape buffer on the north end, and the closest building would be at least 65 feet away. Eighty
percent of roads are alley loaded and all internal roads are private streets. She reviewed the streets that
will be dedicated to the city as part of the Nielsen’s Custard development. Each unit has a side-by-side
two-car garage. End units are 3-bedroom with 1888 square feet and interior units are 2-bedroom with
1673 square feet. Building styles range from 3-plex to 8-plex with the majority being 4-plex units,
which adds variety. Maximum height is 29’5”, which is below the 30-foot maximum. Materials will
include brick, stucco, and metal siding. The Design Review Committee asked for more architecture on
the garage elevation, so they are adding a metal awning or eyebrow to give interest. The total parking
requirement is 532 stalls. They are providing 109 parking stalls in addition to the garages, for a total of
533 spaces not including ten spaces provided at the park. Garbage collection is private, and there is
space inside of the garage for two bins. Two dumpsters are also provided. Overall open space is 8.05
acres at 47% of the net acres. The WSPA requires a minimum of 20%. 2.73 acres are in the utility
corridor and will not be landscaped. 5.16 acres will be landscaped and irrigated within the townhome
area and .16 acres adjacent to the detention basin will be dedicated to the city. The overall irrigated
open space will be 31%, so the minimum percentage is met even excluding the corridor property. Two,
12-foot wide regional trails are within the project. The dry wash will be piped, regraded to meet the
100-year flood design standards, and it will be landscaped and irrigated. The clubhouse is 4530 square
feet and includes a leasing office, fitness center, recreation room with kitchen, restrooms for the pool
area, and a pool maintenance room.

Ms. Nichols reviewed the optional amenities. A swimming pool and two hot tubs are near the
clubhouse. Two playgrounds, one will be inside of the regional detention basin park. Two benches and
trash receptacles along the trail. Two entrance monuments and an overall Highlands master plan sign at
the main gateway. 80% of the units are alley-loaded. The clubhouse has a BBQ area with pergola and
seating. In addition to dedicating open space for the detention basin park, they are installing the
parking lot. The dog park is on the west side of the project within the utility corridor. For the short
term, the residents will benefit from it and in the future, it may become more of a public dog park since
it is next to the trail.

Kent Shelton asked if the utility corridor open space will have any landscape treatment.

Kaylen Nichols said that a portion will be grass, but most of it will be left native. As the city’s plans
for the utility corridor get refined there might be a chance for more improvement. The turf grass area
will be a portion of the dog park.

Larry Gardner showed visual displays regarding the subdivision, site plan, and master development
plan. The Highlands development was adopted in 2012. Most of the residential areas are developed
with this and one other project to the north yet to be built. He reviewed the building type and road
access and potential traffic patterns. The buy-up chart and amenities were reviewed. These features
have been common within the Highlands development. Each unit will have a two-car garage, front
doors will face each other. Lighting will match the common theme throughout the Highlands and is
pedestrian friendly. Architecture is modern looking and gives the project its own identity with a unified
theme. Floor plans were reviewed. Mr. Gardner stated that a resident of Sommerglen Heights called

with concerns of drainage and potential flooding. Drainage from all hard surfaces will be channeled
toward streets, city-owned storm systems, regional detention pond, or the wash. All water will sheet
from north to south. He has been assured by the engineering department that whatever steps are
necessary will be taken to keep the water and drainage from the area of concern. Installation of
landscaping will help immensely along with grading and/or installation of a swale, if needed. A traffic
study was conducted for a large area that included this development and Copper Rim. It showed 50%
of traffic going to Mountain View Corridor, 10% going south on 5600 West, 20% going east on 7800
South, and 20% going north on 5600 West. There is a connection to the stub street in Sommerglen
Heights. Traffic from some of the residents living right next to that connection may use it to go north,
but it would be easier for most to use arterial streets. However, it is a public road and is designed to
meet the capacity of whatever comes through. A preliminary landscaping plan was submitted and will
be approved at the final review. He recommended that the Commission consider a requirement for a
landscape screen such as high growing bushes or evergreen trees in the 30-foot buffer on the north to
add some privacy. The dog park will be fenced with a black powder-coated chain link, and a wrought
iron fence is shown around the pool and clubhouse. There is an existing 6-foot wall on the border of
Sommerglen Heights. The stepped slope has created some sections that are lower than six feet and the
neighbors are concerned about privacy. The Commission could consider a recommendation that the
developer work with staff to address the issue with grading or some type of swale or another way so
that there is a full six feet on the Park Valley side of the wall.

Matt Quinney felt that is was unfair to make this developer replace the fence when they weren’t the
ones who installed it.

Larry Gardner said he is not asking for Peterson Development to replace the fence, but to find some
way to mitigate it. For example, an 18” swale could mitigate the issue. However, he asked that the
recommendation allow staff to work with the developer since the final grade is unknown at this time.
Mr. Gardner reiterated that the total proposed units is 217 at 12.7 units per acre density. He felt that
this will be an attractive development and it meets all ordinance requirements.

Staff recommended approval of the Preliminary Site Plan, Preliminary Subdivision Plat, Preliminary
Development Plan for Park Valley Townhomes; 7652 South Highlands Loop Road with the conditions
of approval listed below.

Preliminary Site Plan
1.     All areas not maintained under the terms of the Highlands Assessment Area must be owned and
       maintained in perpetuity by the property owner.
2.     Before the final plat, site plan and development plan are stamped for construction purposes by
       the West Jordan Engineering Department, all redline comments shall be completely addressed.
3.     Preliminary site plan approval shall be valid for one year per City Code, Section 13-7B-5.

Preliminary Subdivision Plat
1.     All areas not maintained under the terms of the Highlands Assessment Area must be owned and
       maintained in perpetuity by the property owner.
2.     Landscape and irrigation plans shall be submitted detailing installation and maintenance
       responsibilities of all landscaping and irrigation systems.
3.     The Final Subdivision Plat shall address all engineering requirements.
4.     Preliminary subdivision plat approval shall be valid for one year per City Code, Section 14-3-8.

Kent Shelton said since Peterson Development didn’t have to pay to install the wall, they are
benefiting from it. His feeling is that this developer should make sure that the residents have a full six
feet. He supported the concept of a landscaping screen. He asked if the units have basements.

Larry Gardner said they are slab on grade units. He agreed that a landscape screen will help with
privacy issues and make the development look nice.

Kent Shelton said although this property had been zoned as high density for a number of years, he was
concerned for the residents living on that property line. He was pleased with the recommended
mitigations.

Trish Hatch opened the public hearing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

Clarification was made on certain points. There are about 30 parking stalls at the clubhouse. It will not
be a gated community. The current plan is a for-rent product, Airbnb rentals hadn’t been discussed.
Topography of the site was discussed as it pertained to wheelchair accessibility. Some units will have
main door access to the second floor with the garage on the downhill side, but they are not necessarily
ADA accessible.

Ammon Allen asked if it were staff’s intention that the Commission turn the issues of landscape
screening and fencing over to staff to resolve.

Larry Gardner said that is what he would ask since staff knows the intent of the Commission, they
would be able to work that out with the applicant in the final approval. However, the Commission
could also ask that it come back to them to determine that it meets the intent. The Commission
typically will not review it for final approval. Preliminary approvals last one year, but he didn’t think
it will take that long before review of the final plans. Ms. Nichols and Peterson Development are aware
of the concerns in that area.

Ammon Allen expressed his trust in staff, but he wanted to make sure the Commission talks about
what they want the landscape screen to look like. He said there is a public utility easement on the plat
and asked if they anticipate utilities in that location.

Todd Johnson said there is a proposed 10-foot utility easement around the perimeter and there are no
utilities planned for that area at this time. The easement location is flexible and could be adjusted to
allow for landscaping, so there shouldn’t be a conflict.

Matt Quinney was 100% in favor of a landscaping screen and not in favor of asking Peterson to
remedy the fence since they didn’t install it. Also, the homes sit so far above six feet it won’t make a
difference.

Trish Hatch noted that a hand indicator had been raised by someone from the public, so she asked if
there was a motion to reopen the public comment.

MOTION:        Ammon Allen moved to allow for public comment. The motion was seconded and
               passed.

Corbin England agreed with Commissioner Quinney’s comments to have staff work with the applicant
on providing only the landscape screening.

Ammon Allen asked which of the three applications the landscape screen would be referenced in.

Larry Gardner said they could state that with the development plan so that City Council will see that
the Commission had addressed it. Then it can be carried out in the site plan and subdivision approvals.
In the Highlands, all conditions in one area of approvals have to be reflected in all areas.

Dustin Wamsley, West Jordan resident in Sommerglen Heights, said he appreciated the discussion and
concerns about the wall and privacy screen. He explained that the wall is already problematic. Water
runs down the wall and uncovers the footings. It isn’t providing 6 feet of height, and most of the wall
on his property is waist height, so it does not provide the privacy or security that the residents would
like.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

Kent Shelton said that Mr. Wamsley made a good point that the wall is not just for screening, but to
provide a level of security. He asked the Commission how the conversation would differ if the wall
was not there. The wall isn’t of value in its current state.

Ammon Allen appreciated the thought. He assumed that the wall does not encroach onto the Peterson
property, and they can’t be forced to do something on private property. The Commission can either ask
for landscape screening or a second wall or fence, but that would create a no-man’s land, as was
discussed in a similar situation near the airport.

Larry Gardner felt that something could be accomplished with grading, making sure to protect the
footings, then the wall would be higher at least on the Park Valley side. He hasn’t addressed this issue
with the applicant, so he doesn’t know what they are willing to do.
