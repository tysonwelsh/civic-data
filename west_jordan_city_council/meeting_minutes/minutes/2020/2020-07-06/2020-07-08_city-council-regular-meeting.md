WORK SESSION
A.     Wood Ranch Development
Chief Administrative Officer Korban Lee thanked the Council for taking time recently to visit the Wood
Ranch Development site in person. He summarized that the proposed development concept was to
protect and preserve the west hills on the property and develop in the valleys and the tops of two hills
with cuts to moderate slopes.

Mr. Lee asked if the Council would be happy with the project as shown and described. Council Member
Whitelock said she thought it would be a nice development for the community. Council Member
Worthen said her concerns had been addressed and agreed with Council Member Whitelock. Council
Member Pack said his concerns were mitigated by the site visit. He said he would want to make sure
City Council Work Session APPROVED Minutes – July 8, 2020                                            Page 2


foundations and flooding would not be an issue and said he would want to rely on the geotechnical
opinions of more than one professional. Council Member Jacob said the plan looked good but
emphasized that he would continue to check details throughout the process.

Council Member Green said he did not know if he would be happy with the project in 20 years because
it was unknown what would happen with the proposed geotechnical work and soils. He commented that
no Council could commit or bind a future Council. Council Member Green said he would be happy
with the proposed geotechnical work with some guarantees. He said he liked the overall masterplan for
the hills.

Council Member Lamb said he would be okay with the project as shown. Chair McConnehey said he
was more comfortable with the project after the site visit but said he would pay close attention to details.

Mr. Lee reviewed previous assumptions and decisions:
   • Previous Council gave informal (work session) approval for proceeding with the project being
      in the Kearns Improvement District for water and sewer.
   • Direction from Council during work session on March 5, 2020:
           o Annexation – okay to proceed, understand will need to annex existing neighborhood and
              the costs associated with such.
           o Assignability – Daybreak agreed to have City approve language.
           o Density and Dispersion – MDA to require the granularity, City to work with Daybreak
              on a cap on number of apartments, okay to have residential density float within the area,
              lock in locations of amenities, commercial, streets, trails.
           o PIDS – okay, but limited to added amenities, value of added amenities above and beyond
              City requirements. Debt term – no greater than the life of the asset, up to 30 years, debt
              up to $25 million and a Mil Levy cap of 4 mils.
           o Lanes – okay to be City owned, paid for with PID, but HOA maintained (snow removal,
              lane repair and replacement), Class C road funds passed through to HOA if City receives
              money for lanes.

The Council responded to a series of questions posed by Mr. Lee:
   • A majority of Council Members indicated they were okay with the general development plan but
      had concerns about foundations settling or sinking over time.

        Mr. Lee explained that Daybreak would be required to perform a thorough geotech study and
        would pay for an independent geotech study hired by the City as a second opinion. He stated
        fills would be done with structural fill material; an “in the hole” geotech inspection would be
        required at the time of construction of each home; and a community disclosure would be
        required at the time of sale. Council Member Green stated he would want an agreement between
        the geotech professionals, the builder, and the homebuyer that gave the homebuyer recourse if
        the geotech work went wrong.

    • A majority of Council Members indicated the mitigation strategies listed by Mr. Lee would not
      adequately address their concerns. It was suggested that further liability recourse would be
      needed for homeowners. Mr. Lee suggested privity of contract liability or some form of financial
      bond or insurance as options.
City Council Work Session APPROVED Minutes – July 8, 2020                                         Page 3


        A representative of Daybreak commented that every homeowner would have a contract with the
        homebuilder and could seek redress from the builder. He expressed confidence that the science
        concerns could be addressed and stated that Daybreak would not want to see any failures. City
        Attorney Rob Wall clarified that there would be a statutory one-year warranty from the
        homebuilder to the homebuyer and said he would need to research the extent to which the
        warranty would cover settling and subsidence.

     • The Council unanimously indicated support for Daybreak’s plan to fill the holes in the valley to
       create the desired drainage and lessen the slopes in the valleys.

     • The Council unanimously indicated support for the cuts from the tops of the two hills identified
       in the site visit. Council Member Green qualified his response, stating that he would support the
       cuts with guarantees that the rest of the slopes would not be cut.

     • A majority of the Council indicated they were comfortable with the tradeoff of eliminating
       development in the west hills in exchange for development on the two interior hills. Council
       Member Green repeated that he would want a permanent guarantee that the rest of the foothills
       would not be touched. Council Member Jacob said he did not view the request to develop 80%
       of the land as a tradeoff.

     • The Council unanimously indicated they were comfortable with Daybreak’s plan for
       development on the portion of the project that was currently in Unincorporated Salt Lake County.

     • The Council unanimously indicated support for the proposed mass grading as opposed to
       individual site grading.

     • A majority of the Council indicated support for Daybreak’s proposal to build near ridgelines of
       the interior hills (at least 30’ back, behind the trail, plus lot setback).

     • The Council unanimously indicated support for the concept of the front doors of some of the
       homes opening on to the 30’ ribbon of green space, trail, and hillsides.

       b.     Micro Wireless Facility and Small Wireless Facility
Senior Planner Ray McCandless explained that the Small Wireless Deployment Act, enacted by the State
in 2018, required Cities to allow small wireless telecommunication facilities on public rights of way.
Currently, City Code only allowed placement of the facilities on private property. Mr. McCandless said
the City had interest expressed by wireless telecommunication providers to place facilities on public
rights of way. He said the Planning Commission considered the issue and forwarded a positive
recommendation to the Council by unanimous vote.

Mr. McCandless explained proposed amendments to Chapter 16 regarding “Micro Wireless Facility”
and “Small Wireless Facility”, and Low Power Radio Service Antennas and Chapter 13-2-3 Definitions
of the 2009 City Code. Responding to a question from Council Member Whitelock, Mr. McCandless
stated the City was required by the State to allow the facilities on any public right of way at least 60 feet
wide, with some flexibility regarding the aesthetic design of the poles.

Chair McConnehey opened a public hearing at 8:09 p.m.

Comments:
None.

Chair McConnehey closed the public hearing at 8:10 p.m.

Responding to a question from Council Member Jacob, Mr. McCandless stated that an RF engineer
would have to certify that any wireless telecommunication facility complied with all FCC standards. Mr.
Wall commented that the City Council could not base their decision on possible health effects.

MOTION:       Chair Christopher McConnehey moved to approve Ordinance No. 20-17 amending
              the 2009 City Code Title 13 to include “Micro Wireless Facility and Small Wireless
              Facility” in Chapter 16, Low Power Radio Service Antennas.

        d.     Consolidated Plan and Annual Action Plan for the 2020 CDBG funds
CDBG Coordinator Chuck Tarver explained that the City of West Jordan received an annual allocation
from the Department of Housing & Urban Development Community Development Block Grant (CDBG)
program. The amount allocated to the City for Program Year 2020 was $574,748. The funds may be
used to provide services to low- and moderate-income residents of West Jordan. Services must provide
for public services, housing, public facilities, and program administration. Mr. Tarver stated that the
usual cap of 15% of the allocation for public services was removed for 2020. He said it was anticipated
that the need for rental assistance agencies and food pantries would increase as the eviction moratorium
in place due to COVID-19 was raised. He said the need for legal aid for domestic violence victims had
also increased since the start of the pandemic.

       e.     Action Plan for the CARES Act Program
Mr. Tarver explained that the City received an allocation from the U.S. Department of Housing & Urban
Development CARES Act program in the amount of $338,105. The funds must provide for public
services, housing, public facilities and program administration for those affected by the COVID-19
Virus. Under the CARES Act guidance provided by HUD, the City could approve service types to be
funded with CARES Act funds prior to moving forward to awarding funding to providers. Resolution
No. 20-48 before the Council proposed funding to the following types of activities:

   •   Emergency Rent Assistance and/or Counseling Services;
   •   Emergency Food Services;
   •   Legal Assistance required as a result of COVID-19;
   •   Emergency Housing & Shelter Activities;
   •   Any Eligible CDBG Public Service Activity affected by the COVID-19 Virus;
   •   Housing Improvements resulting from the COVID-19 Virus.
