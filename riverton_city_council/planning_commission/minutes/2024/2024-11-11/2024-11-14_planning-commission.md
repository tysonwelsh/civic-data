      B.     “PELAEZ REZONE,” PLZ-24-4006, AN APPLICATION FOR A GENERAL
             PLAN AMENDMENT AND A REZONE OF APPROXIMATELY .96-ACRES
             LOCATED AT 3516 WEST 12600 SOUTH FROM R-1 (RESIDENTIAL
             ONE-ACRE LOTS) TO C-G (GATEWAY COMMERCIAL) AND THE
             GENERAL PLAN DESIGNATION CHANGED FROM ESTATE DENSITY
             RESIDENTIAL TO COMMUNITY COMMERCIAL. APPLICANT – DANIEL
             PELAEZ.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich presented the Staff Report and displayed an aerial view of
the property. South of the subject property is the future America First Credit Union site.
All properties to the north are zoned R-1 and have a General Plan designation of Estate
Density Residential, 0.5-acre lots or greater with animal rights. The zoning designation
specifies what can be done with a property, and the General Plan gives notice that the
City may consider a different use for it. In the past, Riverton has opted to change the
General Plan designation on a property and then let property owners make the change
when they are ready. Mr. Prestwich referenced the Roberts rezoning and indicated that
the General Plan designation for that area was changed in the early 2000s, and then
property owners requested rezoning of their parcels as needed. In this case, the General
Plan still designates the area as Estate Density Residential. However, the subject
property is on a major roadway and in the vicinity of commercial property.

Notices were sent to all property owners within 1,000 feet of the property and posted on
the City’s website. Comments were received from 67 residents, all in opposition to the
request.

The General Plan and Zoning Map were reviewed. If the zoning were changed, the
applicant would be required to submit a Site Plan for consideration at a public Planning
Commission hearing. A six or eight-foot masonry fence would be required on either side
of the property. The applicant has indicated that they would like to open a heating and
air conditioning business, but Mr. Prestwich reminded the Commission that anything
allowed in the zone could be built on the parcel. The Planning Commission’s options
were discussed, including recommending approval, denial, or a different option such as
recommending that the City Council consider re-designating the entire area.

In response to a question from Commissioner Park, Mr. Prestwich stated there are
commercial properties in the area, but all adjacent parcels are residential.

Commission Rushton asked for clarification on why rezoning was being requested if the
applicant intended to have a home-based business. Mr. Prestwich reported that the
applicant’s intended business would exceed what is allowed for a home occupation. In
response to a follow-up question from Commissioner Cannon, Mr. Prestwich added that
home-based business limitations include requiring that the applicant reside in the home.
The applicant wishes to use the home as an office and showroom and live elsewhere,
which disqualifies it from home occupation. Outside storage, the number of employees,
and other factors are also limited for home businesses.

Chair Matheson stated that if the property were rezoned, it would require a six- or eight-
foot masonry fence on the north, east, and west sides. The Planning Commission also
had the option to recommend that the City Council change the entire block to Gateway
Commercial. If so, anyone with a house in that zone could either continue to live there or
convert it to a commercial property. Mr. Prestwich stated that the City’s General Plan will
be updated in 2026, and at that time the Council could consider making that change.

Commissioner Cannon asked if, in the event that the General Plan was amended and the
houses on 12600 South had a commercial overlay in place, property taxes would increase
for residents who choose not to convert their homes to a commercial use. City Attorney
Ryan Carter stated that it would not affect their property taxes. Tax Assessors determine
property value based on the actual use of the property, not the zoning designation.

Property owner, Rob Freeman stated that the business currently operates in a residential
area of Bluffdale. His company works with high-end custom homes and also provides
service. He needs office space and a place to meet with customers. Their goal is to turn
the existing house into an office and showroom and build a storage area for equipment in
the rear.

Commissioner Rushton indicated that a number of flex space units have been built in
Riverton and asked if Mr. Freeman had considered moving directly into a similar space in
a commercial zone. Mr. Freeman stated that the subject property is central to their
service area. He is not looking for warehouse space, but a place to meet with customers.
In response to a follow-up question, he clarified that he does need flex space, so a retail
location would not be appropriate for his needs. His company currently has employees
working from home in different areas, and he needs to centralize his operations.

Commissioner Park referred to the garage currently on the property and asked if
additional space would be required for storage and parking. Mr. Freeman clarified that
employees take their vehicles home, and other vehicles will be parked inside a building
to be constructed behind the existing house. If the company continues to grow, it will
open a second location. He is aware that he would need to construct a masonry wall
around the property. The business is currently based in his home, which has a six-foot
masonry wall.

              SELL ONLINE. APPLICANT – BRIAN CLARK.

Ms. Halversen presented the Staff Report and noted that the Clarks intend to run a family
business. Mrs. Clark is the inspiration and creative director and the rest of the family
assists with the production of candles, lotions, and body sprays that don’t contain artificial
fragrances. The subject property is 0.50 acres and is located in the southwest corner of
Riverton in Deer Mountain Estates. It is zoned RR-22. The home is two stories with a
basement, three-car garage, and extra-wide driveway. The applicant intends to use part
of the garage for packaging the product, a main floor bedroom to create the products, and
a small portion of the kitchen. Two employees will park in the section of the driveway
behind the gate.

City Code indicates that any Home Occupation that uses space in an attached or
detached garage requires a Conditional Use permit, which will allow the applicant to use
up to 50% of garage space. They are also only allowed one nonresident full-time
employee, but the Planning Commission can allow for additional employees if sufficient
off-street parking is available. Notices were sent to nearby property owners and posted
on the City’s website, and no responses were received.

Ms. Halversen reviewed the recommended conditions of approval outlined in the Staff
Report and invited commissioners to ask the applicant more about his proposed business.

The applicant, Brian Clark, reported that his wife has suffered for years from headaches
due to fragrances in lotions, perfumes, candles, etc., and they have discovered that many
people have the same issue. After making candles with their grandchildren resulted in
another headache, she learned that paraffins, phthalates, and other crude oil products
were the cause. She created more candles without those chemicals and did not get a
headache. She then created lotions, body sprays, and perfumes, and realized the need
was great enough to have an online business. Their goal is to grow the business and
eventually move into the detached garage. If it continues to grow, they will then move to
a commercial site.

In response to a question from Commissioner Keele about the business name, Mr. Clark
stated that Mrs. Clark lived in Australia prior to their marriage. There was a business
there called Purple Duck, and she decided that if she ever had her own business, she
would call it Purple Duck.

Mrs. Clark has created colognes and beard oil with the same fragrances, as well as a
Purple Ducklings line of children’s products. She plans to make Purple Pooches products
for pets in the future. In response to a question from Chair Matheson, Mr. Clark stated
that he does not know all the ingredients, but the products she uses are chemical-free.

      D.     “UTAH COLLECTIVE”, PLZ-24-8015, AN APPLICATION FOR A
             PRIVATE SCHOOL AND EDUCATIONAL COMMUNITY CENTER TO BE
             LOCATED ON ABOUT 1.28 ACRES AT 2673 WEST 11800 SOUTH. THE
             REQUESTED APPROVALS INCLUDE AN AMENDED SUBDIVISION
             PLAT, A CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT FOR AN EDUCATIONAL
             FACILITY, AND A FINAL SITE PLAN. APPLICANTS – BENJAMIN AND
             SARAH MILAR.

Mr. Prestwich presented the Staff Report and indicated that the application was for a
commercial or quasi-public use in a residential zone. A conceptual rendering of the school
building was displayed which indicated that the building would have a residential feel but
be designed to commercial standards. The neighboring properties on 11800 South are
part of an older subdivision, and the subject property is approximately 1.75 acres that has
remained vacant. Previous property owners have requested rezoning of the parcel for
commercial use, and it has also been the subject of General Plan discussions, but each
time the City Council decided that the residential zoning should remain intact. The Gospel
Hope Church to the south of the property is also zoned RR-22 and operates under the
same section of code that would allow a school in the zone. Public and quasi-public uses
are allowed as Conditional Uses, and a private school is considered a quasi-public use.

Notice was sent to residents within 300 feet of the subject property, and no comments
were received.

The application included a Conditional Use for the school, a final commercial Site Plan,
and a subdivision. The neighbor to the east and property owner agreed to adjust their
common line to give the neighbor in the existing residence more property, so a subdivision
was required to meet State code requirements.

Mr. Prestwich reported that both 11800 South and 2700 West are collector roads with
heavy traffic. The applicant and his engineer moved the access points to the farthest
extremes to create the least possible chance of interference with the intersection. Access
for drop-offs and pick-ups will be one way. Cars will enter the property through 2700
West, parking is angled to encourage the one-way flow, and the exit is onto 11800 South.

The proposal included three different fencing types. Along the south property line is an
existing fence that was installed by the church. On the north and west sides, the applicant
proposed a six-foot-tall, five-rail metal fence. Typically, fences are not allowed in the front
yard of residential zones. The Planning Commission could decide to allow it for the
Conditional Use or require the fence to meet residential setback requirements of 20 feet
from the property line on 11800 South. Mr. Prestwich noted that it may be a challenge to
add fencing on the east side because the site sits approximately two-and-a-half feet
higher than the neighboring property, but the City Council has been concerned about
grade differences in commercial properties that abut residences. In this case, Staff
requested a condition of approval to indicate that the fence must sit six feet above the
parking surface to mitigate the increased impact on the residential property.

The applicant provided a traffic study indicating approximately 96 peak-hour morning
trips, and Staff was satisfied that their proposed traffic flow could accommodate that. If
the number of trips were exceeded, other options would need to be considered.

Project renderings were reviewed. Mr. Prestwich indicated that they have done a good
job of adding a residential feel to the commercial use, but at 34.5 feet it is much taller than
traditional residences. Landscaping will include sod around the building and indoor
gymnasium, and waterwise landscaping around the border of the property. The
gymnasium will have garage doors that open to a concrete play area between the
buildings.

Commissioner Cannon expressed his appreciation for the applicant and Staff’s efforts on
the project since it was first brought before the Commission. His questions for the

In response to a question from Commissioner Rushton, it was clarified that there are no
plans to widen either 2700 South or 11800 West.

Commissioner Park was concerned about the type of fencing. Mr. Prestwich clarified that
the requested height would be an exception, but it was within the Planning Commission’s
authority to dictate the fencing type.

different traffic patterns, headlights may shine into homes, and residents’ privacy is
affected. The Council is working to give the Planning Commission and Staff the tools to
ensure those concerns are addressed in the future. Because the current application was
for a Conditional Use, the fencing and grade could be addressed in the motion. A Text
Amendment would then be forthcoming to permanently address the issue.
