26   Mr. Lethbridge presented information regarding a property located near 3100 West and 13400
27   South, consisting of two parcels totaling approximately 2.3 acres. The current zoning is R-22,

39            Jordan Valley Aqueduct Presentation
40
41   Jason Luettinger, Bowen Collins representing Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District,
42   addressed the Council regarding the Southwest Aqueduct project, a major infrastructure
43   initiative that will impact the City. The project involves installing a 66-inch diameter welded
44   steel pipeline approximately two miles long along 3200 West, connecting sections of the
45   existing Southwest aqueduct to increase water conveyance capacity. This pipeline is part of a
46   larger effort to expand water treatment capacity at the Herriman plant from 180 to 255 million
47   gallons per day. The $30 million project, awarded to Conde Construction, is planned to take
48   two years, with construction anticipated to begin in September. The District has prioritized
49   public communication, employing a full-time public information manager to coordinate with
50   residents and City staff. The pipeline will be buried about five feet underground and is large
51   enough to walk through, serving as a critical water corridor similar in importance to I-15 for

 4            Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District Presentation
 5
 6   Shazelle Terry, Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District, provided an overview of the
 7   proposed property tax increase, explaining it supports critical infrastructure projects to meet
 8   the growing population’s water demands. She highlighted the upcoming Central Utah Project
 9   water delivery starting in 2026, which will flow through the expanded treatment plant and new
10   pipeline, offering system redundancy and maintenance flexibility. The District’s $111 million
11   budget relies on property taxes for about 30%, and the proposed 11% increase—roughly $3
12   million—would add about $11.78 annually for the median home. This funding will help cover a
13   $30 million pipeline project and a $180 million, multi-phase expansion of the treatment plant,
14   which has not been upgraded since 1985. Additional projects include new reservoirs and
15   groundwater development to ensure future water pressure and supply. This presentation is
16   included in the packet.
17
18   Mayor Staggs raised concerns about the proposed property tax increase, suggesting the
19   Board consider selling the Conservation Park—a valuable asset that could offset the need for
20   higher taxes since it currently costs the District millions annually despite some private funding.
21   He also referenced a previous study indicating that the District should ideally fund about $20
22   million of its budget through property taxes, but the current proposal would push that amount
23   to $33 million, exceeding the recommended level by $13 million. He questioned the rationale
24   behind this increase, especially since a tax raise was approved just two years ago, and
25   wondered if rate adjustments or other revenue options might be explored instead. He intends
26   to convey these points to the Board at the upcoming August 11 public hearing.
27
28   In response, Ms. Terry explained that the current Conservation Garden Park covers seven
29   acres, with plans to expand by three more. She emphasized the garden’s role as a critical
30   educational tool since its creation in 2000, designed to teach residents not just how to
31   conserve water, but increasingly why conservation is necessary. While acknowledging it's
32   difficult to quantify exactly how much water the garden has saved, she pointed to a drop in per
33   capita water use from 255 to 206 gallons per day as a positive trend since the garden’s
34   inception. She noted that the garden hosts 35,000–40,000 annual visitors, offers dozens of in-
35   person and online conservation classes, and trains landscapers—making it more than just a
36   park, but a statewide resource.
37
38   Councilmember Pierucci commented that the District adopted a new policy within the last year
39   and a half requiring new developments to either provide water shares or pay for excess water,
40   marking a significant shift in approach and the first of its kind in the state. This change ensures
41   development is tied to actual water availability. He also acknowledged the complex dynamic
42   between water delivery and conservation, especially in light of concerns related to the Great
43   Salt Lake. He argued that the current incentive structure is imbalanced, as low water rates—
44   kept low by property tax subsidies—discourage conservation. Because of this, he reiterated
45   his opposition to the proposed property tax increase and advocated instead for rate
46   adjustments that would create a stronger financial incentive for individual water users to
47   conserve.
48
49   Councilmember McDougal emphasized the importance of being forward-thinking and
50   expressed concern that developers have historically profited at the expense of taxpayers by
51   connecting to the water system without adequately contributing to the cost of expanded

 2   shares or pay for excess use but felt this shift came too late. Reflecting on the drought years

 8            Fraud Assessment Discussion
 9
10   Nick Geer, Administrative Services Director, provided an executive summary of the annual
11   fraud risk assessment, noting that the risk level had improved from “low” to “very low” this
12   year—an increase of 20 points on the scale. This improvement was attributed to the addition
13   of a Certified Expert to the management team. Of the 395 total possible points, the City
14   missed only 20 points, which were lost due to the absence of a formal internal audit function.
15   While the City conducts its own internal audits, the State Auditor’s definition requires an
16   independent party with no operational involvement to carry out those audits. He noted that
17   implementing such a function is something the City could explore, but overall, the current
18   rating is strong and within the desired range.
