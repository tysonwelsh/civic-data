CAMPAIGN FINANCIAL DISCLOSURES

A campaign finance report (or disclosure) is a document that shows the financial activities of a political campaign, including who donates money and how funds are spent. These reports typically list contributions, expenditures, loans, debts, and in-kind donations (non-monetary support like goods or services). The goal is to promote transparency, allowing the public to see who is funding campaigns and how funds are being used. Rules and filing deadlines vary by state and local laws.

CONFLICT OF INTEREST DISCLOSURES

A conflict of interest form for elected officials is a document where they disclose any personal, financial, or professional interests that could affect their ability to make unbiased decisions in their official role. This includes relationships, investments, or other ties that could create a real or perceived conflict between their private interests and public duties.

Elected officials are required to complete and sign a new conflict of interest disclosure annually. This ensures ongoing transparency, as circumstances may change over time. The purpose is to promote accountability and allow the public to understand any factors that might influence an official's decision-making, fostering trust that officials act in the best interest of the community rather than for personal gain.
