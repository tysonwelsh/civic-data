        A. RECEIVE PUBLIC COMMENT ON PROPOSED MIDVALLEY SEWER
            DISTRICT BOUNDARY LINE ADJUSTMENT.
Branden Anderson said Midvalley Sewer District has taken steps to adjust the boundary
of their district to better reflect the area in which they provide services. This adjustment
affects 16 parcels located in Midvale (see Exhibit C) and would result in the properties
being removed from the Cottonwood Improvement District and added to the Midvalley
Sewer District. Midvalley Sewer District is seeking Midvale City’s approval for the
boundary line adjustment as required by State law. Midvalley Sewer District originally
brought this issue to staff’s attention in 2024. At that time staff did not seek City Council
approval, because the change did not affect Midvale’s service area. Subsequently, the
Lieutenant Governor’s Office contacted Midvalley to inform them that they would need a
resolution from Midvale approving the boundary line adjustment.

The effected properties will have their fees and taxes adjusted to reflect those charged
by Midvalley Sewer District. There will be no change in service, because of the affected
properties are already receiving services from Midvalley Sewer District.

Midvale will be the last City to approve the requisite resolution. Midvalley has expressed
interest in having the resolution approved as soon as possible, and therefore staff is
requesting to suspend the rules and have the City Council consider the approval
Resolution 2025-R-60 during the October 7, 2025, meeting.

FISCAL IMPACT

This will not affect Midvale City financially.

       B. RECEIVE PUBLIC COMMENT REGARDING AMENDING VARIOUS
           SECTIONS OF THE SSC, RC, AND CI ZONES OF THE MIDVALE CITY
           MUNICIPAL CODE RELATING TO VEHICLE RELATED USES, DELETE
           THE DEFINITION FOR “VEHICLE-RELATED USE” FROM SECTION 17-2-
           22, AND ESTABLISH SECTION 17-6-5 REGULATING VEHICLE RELATED
           USES
Wendelin Knobloch said this amendment proposes that the definition for “vehicle-related
use” be removed from the Definitions section as each of the following uses have their
own existing definition: vehicle rental, vehicle repair, vehicle filling station, vehicle sales
(minor), vehicles sales and service (major). The current standards/regulations for the
aforementioned vehicle related uses in the SSC, RC, and CI zones are all the same. To
condense the municipal code and more easily find governing standards, it is proposed
that the standards are relocated to a new section in Supplementary Regulations. Vehicle
sales (minor) standards have been consolidated to remove redundancy. Otherwise, no
standards have changed. Additionally, the amendment changes each of the vehicle
related uses to allowed uses in the SSC, RC, and CI zones as the code can mitigate any
negative impacts and the review for the uses can be done with their business license
application.

Public notice has been sent to affected entities as required in 17-3-9.B of the Municipal
Code. No comments have been received as of the writing of this report.

-ZONING CODE AMENDMENT CRITERIA-

Midvale City Code 17-3-1(F) outlines the criteria necessary for amendments to the zoning
code. A proposal may only be approved if it demonstrates one or more of the following:
   1. The proposed amendment promotes the objectives of the general plan and
      purposes of this title;
   2. The proposed amendment promotes the purposes outlined in Utah State Code
      10-9a-102;
   3. The proposed amendment more clearly explains the intent of the original
      language or has been amended to make interpretation more straightforward; or
   4. Existing zoning code was the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact.

Staff finds that this proposal meets the third criterion listed above as it improves the code
structure and makes interpretation clearer and more straightforward.

        C. RECEIVE PUBLIC COMMENT REGARDING AMENDING VARIOUS
            SECTIONS OF THE SF-1, SF-2, RM-12, AND RM-25 ZONES OF THE
            MIDVALE CITY MUNICIPAL CODE RELATING TO MASTER PLANNED
            DEVELOPMENTS.
Wendelin Knobloch said the existing master planned development standards have been
consistently difficult to implement and are not providing the City’s desired outcome. It is
proposed that master planned developments are now only required for projects that
include common areas. Staff find that development projects with common area require
coordination and a variety of housing types where projects without common area do not.
The amendment removes incentives to reduce variation between projects. Development
requirements have been reduced to include only those that the city prioritizes and have
straightforward interpretation. A new section has been added to allow building footprint
lots. Additionally, parking requirements have been changed to require 2 off-street spaces
and 0.5 guest parking spaces per dwelling unit, and that driveways cannot be utilized for
guest parking.

        A. DISCUSSION ON STORM WATER MASTER PLAN
Jerimie Thorne said the Stormwater Master Plan for the City of Midvale, prepared by
Hansen, Allen & Luce under an agreement dated December 12, 2023, provides a
framework for managing stormwater runoff, reducing flooding, and protecting the
environment across Midvale and its tributary areas, spanning approximately 3,872 acres.
The plan evaluates the City’s existing stormwater system, identifying deficiencies such as
aging infrastructure and capacity limitations. A Capital Improvement Plan (CIP),
developed in conjunction with hydrologic models and input from City staff, proposes
infrastructure upgrades, maintenance enhancements, and proactive measures, such as
pipe inspections, to mitigate the risk of flooding. Conceptual cost estimates are included
in the plan to guide implementation. Challenges include funding constraints and
maintaining aging infrastructure.

        D. CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 2025-R-59 AUTHORIZING THE MAYOR TO
           COMPLETE PARTICIPATION FORMS ON BEHALF OF MIDVALE CITY AND
           JOIN THE SECONDARY MANUFACTURERS SETTLEMENTS AND
           FUTURE OPIOID SETTLEMENTS UNDER CERTAIN CONDITIONS
Garrett Wilcox said Midvale City has been approached by the Utah Attorney General’s
Office about participating in another national opioid settlement. This proposed settlement
would resolve opioid litigation between the State of Utah and many of Utah’s counties
with secondary manufacturers including Alvogen, Amneal, Apotex, Hikma, Indivior,
Mylan, Sun, and Zydus. The State and the counties will receive funds from the settlement
because they provide health services related to the opioid epidemic. Although
municipalities will not be receiving any of the settlement funds, they stand to benefit from
the additional services and resources provided by the counties and the State from the
settlement funds. The final settlement amount will be determined based on the amount of
participation by Utah’s political subdivisions. If all counties and cities participate, Utah is
anticipated to receive $8.8 million from the defendants to address the epidemic.

Proceedings of City Council Meeting
October 7, 2025



In order to maximize the potential settlement amount, Midvale must participate in the
settlement. Midvale may participate in the settlement by executing and submitting a
provided participation form no later than tomorrow, October 8, 2025. The City was not
notified of this settlement until a few weeks ago due to the Utah Attorney General’s Office
having outdated City contact information.

By participating in the settlement, the City will waive all of its rights to individually sue any
of the involved companies for opioid epidemic-related issues. Additional information about
the settlement can be found at: https://nationalopioidsettlement.com/.

Midvale has previously participated in other opioid national settlements in coordination
with the Utah Office of the Attorney General. On December 14, 2021, the City Council
approved Resolution No. 2021-R-40 authorizing the City to register in the national opioid
settlement with McKesson, Cardinal Health, AmerisourceBergen, Janssen
Pharmaceuticals, and Johnson & Johnson and the Purdue settlement. On April 4, 2023,
the City Council approved Resolution No. 2023-R-14 authorizing the City to register in the
national opioid settlement with Allergan, Teva, CVS, Walgreen’s, and Walmart. On
February 20, 2024, the City Council authorized staff to submit a ballot on behalf of Midvale
City for the Endo International PLC proposed Chapter 11 Plan of Reorganization in
coordination with the Utah Office of the Attorney General. On July 16, 2024, the City
Council authorized the City to register in the new national opioids settlement with Kroger.
In each of these instances, the settlement amount available to the State and counties was
directly dependent on the number of municipalities who joined the settlement.

All of the opioid settlements have followed largely the same process and have been
negotiated by the Utah Attorney’s General Office on behalf of the state and its political
subdivisions. City staff is not aware of how many additional settlements may still be
negotiated, but there have been a number of the settlements in which the City has
received fairly late notice of the settlement and the deadline to join.

In order to provide the City the flexibility to join these settlements under tight deadlines
without requesting a special City Council meeting, City staff is requesting that the City
Council provide authorization to the Mayor and City staff to complete any required forms
to join future opioid settlements under certain conditions without additional City Council
authorization. City staff propose the following three conditions:

   A. The settlement has been negotiated on behalf of Utah government entities by
      a Utah government entity, such as the Utah Attorney General’s Office, or a
      firm specifically hired to represent Utah government entities;
   B. The City has no intent to file suit against one of the named settling parties for
      The same or similar claims; and
   C. City staff have reviewed the settlement documents and are satisfied that
      joining the settlement is in the best interest of the City. Staff may consider,
      much like the Secondary Manufacturers Settlements, the indirect benefits that
      the City may receive by Salt Lake County and the State of Utah receiving

All of the settlements that the City have joined have met these criteria. Staff hopes that
these three criteria will provide adequate guardrails to prevent the Mayor or Staff from
joining a settlement against the City’s interests while allowing the Mayor and Staff
flexibility to meet tight deadlines.

       E. CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 2025-R-61 AUTHORIZING THE MAYOR TO
          ENTER INTO AN AGREEMENT BETWEEN MIDVALE CITY AND UDOT FOR
          THE 8000 SOUTH BRIDGE REPLACEMENT PROJECT.
Branden Anderson said the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) has selected the
bridge located at 100 E 8000 S for replacement. This bridge serves as a canal bridge that
conveys water for Salt Lake City Utilities.

UDOT conducts regular inspections of bridges statewide. As part of the federally funded
Bridge Formula Program (BFP)—which targets approximately 90 bridges within Utah over
a 5-year period—this bridge was identified for evaluation and selected for repair or
replacement. The project is fully funded by UDOT, with no cost to the City.

Although the City will not incur direct costs, City staff will support the project through
design coordination and public engagement with residents.

This project also presents an opportunity to complete unfinished roadway paving near the
TRAX line. Previously, the City was unable to pave this section due to permit restrictions.
Since UDOT will be working near the TRAX line and obtaining the necessary permits, the
City will partner with UDOT to include this paving work. The bridge and adjacent roadway
will be paved simultaneously, improving efficiency and reducing disruption.

The anticipated completion is projected for Winter 2025–26 or 2026–27, depending on
