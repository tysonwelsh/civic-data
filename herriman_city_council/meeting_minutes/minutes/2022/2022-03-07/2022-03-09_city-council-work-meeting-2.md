  *2.4. Legislative Update and Discussion on HB 151 and implications - Nathan Cherpeski, City
          Manager and Chris Bleak, Lobbyist
Lobbyist Chris Bleak explained the legislative session was now over, and said the session discussed
many high level items, one of them being significant infrastructure changes. He said the State
legislature had appropriated $1 Billion dollars in cash for infrastructure, mainly accelerating road
projects. Of those appropriations, $382 Million dollars were designated to go to Point of the Mountain,
and $75 Million dollars were set aside for double tracking the Front Runner Station.

Lobbyist Bleak said Representative Christopherson had passed a bill which would move state funded
UTA projects to UDOT, which was significant since the State had never funded transit projects. He
said while there were still some things to be worked out, he was glad the change had been made, since
he felt the Utah Transit Authority’s (UTA) ability to complete capital projects were maxed out for the
next several years. He thought this would accelerate transit projects in the near future, and felt it was
an important step. He commented the Point of the Mountain transit line began at the prison property,

    2.5. High Speed Internet Task Force update - Blake Thomas, Community Development Director
Community Development Director Blake Thomas stated the City needed to look at better options for
broadband. He reported the taskforce had worked with industry professionals to find out what all the
options for broadband installation were, and also to assist them with technical details. He said across
all cities, broadband was underrepresented, and so Google Fiber was coming into those cities to
provide high speed internet. He noted while that was better than nothing, Google Fiber was not the
same as broadband, and thought there would be a benefit to having a broadband system in order to get
infrastructure. He thought it would also be a good incentive for the City to at least partially own the
system, and it would also be an advantage to make the City a smart city. Currently, Herriman City had
the private service provider model, and relied entirely on them for high-speed internet. Director
Thomas said another option would be to work up to a full utility model, which would have an opt-in or
opt-out option, like UTOPIA. He elaborated the City would compile a list of providers to choose from,
that would provide support for email and internet. He summarized the recommendation from the task
force was to utilize the opt-out model to meet the needs of the residents and the City.

Councilmember Shields said the original task of the Council had been to get high speed internet for
residents, since the private model did not satisfy needs, and people had complained about not getting
decent service to their location. He recalled how their focus had shifted once they realized the solution
for the internet was a different conversation than the one about the potential of broadband. He pointed
out broadband would allow for high speed internet for residents, and should be looked at as an essential
March 9, 2022 – City Council Minutes                            Page 5 of 13
utility, like water, sewer and electricity. He added it would enhance services in the City, and lay the
groundwork for their development in the years to come. He also agreed the option would allow for
the most flexibility is the opt-out model. He said the committee had been unified in its
recommendation, and all agreed it was the best option for the City. He then stated there were some
reasons to consider expediting actions, and move forward aggressively with the installation of
broadband. He noted cities who chose to partner with a private entity would end up being faced with
the same decision to reinstall broadband infrastructure.

Director Thomas said the next step would be finding funding, and engage in a financial plan to outline
a timeline and funding structure. Councilmember Shields added regardless of the direction, it would
be some time before the plans were announced to the public, and so there were numerous possibilities
for the Council to change direction, and right now there was nothing being committed besides time.
Councilmember Ohrn agreed the opt-out system would be the most fair for the City, since not all
residents wanted to have broadband. She wanted to see what fees would be involved, as well as the
benefit and cost reduction, if broadband was implemented throughout the city. Councilmember
Henderson noted he was interested to see the overall vision, and also expressed interest in seeing the
cost model.

Councilmember Shields explained realistically, a public and private partnership would create the most
opportunity for competition. He stated the network partner would support ten gig speeds, and while
most providers were forced to throttle their network speeds during peak times, which would not be
the case with the new network, so speeds would not slow during times of the day more people were
using it. He said the utility itself would become a part of the economic development of Herriman, and
it would make other projects possible for the City. He said it would give residents unparalleled high
speed internet access, with more consistent, better speeds at the cost which was currently being paid.
The Council briefly noted there were many dead zones in the City, where cell signal dropped, and
acknowledged the addition of broadband, those zones would be eliminated.

City Manager Cherpeski reported on research he had done, cities who had adopted the single-user
model had had issues, and in one case they had actually switched over to Google Fiber. He noted
American Fork and Provo were currently going through changes with their internet as well. He said
he did not know of another city around their size in the area which had adopted the opt-in model.
Councilmember Shields said in the single user system, there was a risk of the system not paying for
itself, and the risk was not there with opt-out. City Manager Cherpeski affirmed the opt-out model
would be the best option for Herriman, and estimated it would take about two years for these plans to
come to fruition.

Councilmember Shields asked if any grants were available, and staff discussed there was not funding
available for broadband. Councilmember Ohrn noted to get a government grant, they would need to
prove financial hardship in the City, or have a large number of residents below the poverty line.
Councilmember Shields reiterated with this model, the City would not be the actual service provider,
although it would be the most cost-effective option for residents to go with the provider offered by
the City.
