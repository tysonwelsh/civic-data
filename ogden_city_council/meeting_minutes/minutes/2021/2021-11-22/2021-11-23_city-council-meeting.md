Capitol Square Mixed-Use Project (located between 24th Street and 25th
Street and Monroe Boulevard and Quincy Avenue)- Proposed Mixed Use
Zone Text Amendments; and Proposed Rezone from NC-2 to MU and
Project Master Plan for Capitol Square
          Planning Manager Montgomery explained the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) is proposing to rezone the block where the
old Rite Aid building used to be located. If approved, this rezone would place the whole block under the Mixed Use (MU) zone
and would allow for multiple types ofland uses within the same zone (i.e., residential and commercial). This zone text amendment
is specific to the Capitol Square site and will not apply to other MU zones in the city. The ordinance outlines specific land uses
and design standards that will apply to the site. The purpose of the zone text amendment is to provide a mixed-use development
that is designed to be complimentary to the historic community in which it is located. Additionally, the Redevelopment Agency is
proposing to rezone the block between 24 1h and 25th Streets, and Monroe Boulevard and Quincy A venue from Expanded Scale
Neighborhood Commercial (NC-2) to Mixed Use (MU). This property is owned by the RDA and was once the location of the Rite
Aid building. The purpose of the rezone is to allow for a variety of uses within the same zone, including townhomes, apartments,
single-family residences, commercial, entertainment, and office uses. Mixed Use zones also allow for multiple use-types to be
incorporated within the same building (e.g., retail on ground level and residential on each level above). In addition to considering
the rezone request, the zoning code requires the City Council to simultaneously review and approve a project master plan.
Reviewing the master plan ensures that the developer follows design and layout standards established in zoning Code 15-39-4.
Two concept plans have been submitted to the Council to review: Concept One includes a grocery store, office development,
residential development, open space, and pedestrian ways. Concept Two is similar, but the grocery store would be replaced with
mansion townhomes on 24th Street. Both would provide a similar number of dwelling units, 358 versus 361, respectively. The
Planning Commission made it clear that Concept One, with the grocery store, should be a higher priority than Concept Two. He
presented the aerial plan for both of the concepts, taking time to identify the various uses included in each concept. He concluded
the Planning Commission has recommended approval of the requested zone change from NC-2 to MU for the area encompassed
within the Capitol Square project area; approve the concept development plans as modified (Concept One with grocery store is
preferred); adopt mixed-use development standards for Capitol square; and pursue future development agreements with
architecture requirements and LEED or sustainability standards.

Council Business
          Holiday Gift Exchange: The Council discussed the idea of collecting donations among the group to be donated to
charitable organizations in the community; this activity would be in-lieu of a holiday gift exchange among Council members.
          Unsheltered Individuals: Council member Choberka provided an update on efforts to assist unsheltered individuals in
the community; many individuals have been congregating a park near an elementary school and a local community agency has
aided in relocating them, sheltering them, and providing much needed services.
          Marriott-Slaterville Residents: Council member Hyer referenced the recent input from Marriott-Slaterville residents
regarding a constriction project on the west side of Business Depot Ogden (BDO); he asked if City Administration and The Boyer
Company would consider erecting a solid fence on the western BDO property line to help screen the project. Council member
Lopez added that the residents would like to establish an open line of communication with City Administration, and he asked if
City Administration is willing to entertain that. The entire Council engaged in discussion of their own conversations with the
Marriott-Slaterville residents, with a focus on the infonnation that the residents have received from The Boyer Company
regarding the project and potential mitigation of negative impacts on the adjacent neighborhoods.
          Council Initiatives: Council member Lopez stated that he has requested a bill/initiative tracking mechanism and he
asked for an update on that request. Ms. Eller-Smith indicated she sent an email to the City Council today including a sample of
the type of tracking mechanism that Council staff could administer; it also included a status of each of the ongoing initiatives.
          Council Calendar: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith briefly reviewed the Council calendar for the remainder of
the year.
          Marshall Wflite Center: The Council's discussion of the Marshall White Center (MWC) centered on the position of City
Administration and the City Council regarding whether the City will renovate or rebuild the MWC in its current location or
replace it with a community center at another location in the City. Chair Blair questioned whether the City needs to choose one or
the other or if there may be a way to pursue both projects; he agrees that the project needs to be completed and he does not see a
need to eliminate the MWC at its current location. What the City needs to determine is which recreation elements will be included
in the MWC when it is updated. Council member Nadolski stated the MWC Advisory Committee indicated they want the MWC
to remain in its current location and for it to be converted to a recreation center. Chair Blair asked what they mean by 'recreation
center'. Council member Lopez stated the community wants the existing building to be tom down and replaced by a new
recreation center facility. Council member Nadolski agreed and stated the Cmmnittee was adamant that the MWC is the recreation
center for the City and should remain where it is. Council member Choberka spoke to the direction given to the MWC Advisory
Committee as well as the results of the survey that they administered. She is frustrated that the Council is still asking what the
cmmnunity wants before first allowing the Committee to fmish their cmmnunity engagement plans. She feels a broad conversation
about all recreation projects and plans for the community should be undertaken.
          The Council then engaged in discussion of recreation center options, ultimately reaching consensus that the MWC should
remain in its place as an improved recreation center for the community. Council member Lopez stated that the decision to perform
an additional survey regarding the issue has created fear in the community regarding the permanence of the MWC. Council
member Hyer stated that if building a premier recreation center at the MWC site will make it impossible to build an additional
center that will serve the entire City, that will be difficult for him to support. He stated that the area of the City that he represents
also feels underserved in tenns of recreation; they have not had access to a recreation center in their neighborhood for the past 50
years and if the City chooses to continue to serve the area directly surrounding the MWC at the expense of serving the rest of the
City, that will create problems. Council member Lopez stated that a premier recreation center at the MWC site will serve the
entire community and even residents from other cmmnunities. Council member Choberka stated she does not disagree with either
point, but it is important to have conversations with the community about these matters before making assumptions or decisions
about the MWC. Council member Nadolski stated that he feels that the location of a premier recreation center is detennined; it
should be where it is already located at the present MWC site. The project should include a pool and all the amenities the
cmmnunity desires based upon the results of the survey. He added that he feels there should be conversations about creating other
recreational opportunities in other areas of the community as well. Council member Hyer agreed and noted that it makes no sense
to him that the City and the Ogden School District cannot fmd a way to work collaboratively to create recreation facilities that
serve the entire community.
          Vice Chair White stated she has taken the standpoint of trying to figure out how to fmance a premier recreation center;
there have been discussions about including a bond question on a ballot, but ifthe cmmnunity does not support the bond, the City
will need to pursue other fmancing options. She would also like to consider partnering with another agency to manage the
recreation center. She also wondered if a market study should be completed to detennine growth rates throughout the cmmnunity
