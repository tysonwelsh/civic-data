COMMENCE 
 
The Housing Connect October 2023 Board of Commissioners Meeting took place on Wednesday, October 18, 2023, and commenced at 1 1:42 a.m.  
Vice Chair Phil Bernal facilitated and led the meeting. 
 
1. GUESTS/PUBLIC COMMENT 
 
Karl Westbrook attended the meeting as a member of the public.  He is a former member of both the Housing Authority of the County of 
Salt Lake and the Resident Advisory boards.  He indicated that he was attending as part of a re-introduction to involvement in housing. 
 
2. CONSENT AGENDA 
 
The Consent Agenda contained only the staff update.  It was pointed out that there are not very many open positions.  Chief Operating 
Officer Mike Kienast noted that incentives are working. 
 
Commissioner Nguyen motioned to approve the Consent Agenda, while Commissioner White seconded the motion.  All Board members 
present (Vice Chair Bernal and Commissioners Johnson, Nguyen, and White) supported the motion, and the Consent Agenda was approved. 
 
3. ACTION ITEMS 
 
The Board voted on two items, as indicated below: 
 
- September 2023 Housing Connect Board Meeting minutes:  The Board did not have any questions or concerns, and Commissioner Johnson 
motioned to approve the minutes.  Commissioner Nguyen then seconded the motion.  All Board members present (Vice Chair Bernal  and 
Commissioners Johnson, Nguyen, and White) voted in favor, and the September 2023 Board Meeting minutes were approved. 
 
- Resolution #1093 – Approval of owner’s agent for New City Plaza (NCP):  Given the significant challenges with the construction contractor 

 
 
 
 
(on both NCP and East 72), Housing Connect went out for bids for an owner’s agent for New City Plaza.  Eight firms were solicited, and two 
firms submitted proposals.  Housing Connect management has recommended using OAC Consulting, who had the higher average score and 
has more direct experience in working with multi -family housing than the other firm.  OAC Consulting also happens to be working with 
Housing Connect at East 72.  Vice Chair Bernal provided his support for the proposed selection as the best choice (rather than selecting by 
the lowest price).  Real Estate Development Director Kirk Moorhead further explained that one of the reasons for this procurement request 
was because Housing Connect wanted someone on -site on a regular basis .  The sources and uses were then discussed  to present to the 
Board how the agency is planning to pay for these services. 
 
Commissioner Johnson motioned to approve Resolution #1093, with Commissioner White seconding the motion.  All Board members 
present (Vice Chair Bernal and Commissioners Johnson, Litvack, Nguyen, and White) voted in favor, and Resolution #1093 (Appro val of 
owner’s agent for New City Plaza) was approved. 
 
4. REAL ESTATE DEVELOPMENT UPDATE 
 
- New City Plaza (NCP):  Construction is moving forward.  Building A (the second half) is essentially complete, and the construction for 
Building B is proceeding.  On Thursday, October 12, there was a meeting with the City’s fire marshal and others.  While the fire safety plan 
has already been approved, the fire marshal’s office wants Housing Connect to improve some things and bring the building further up to 
code (related to fire alarms, smoke detectors, etc.).  In order to move tenants over from Building B to Building A, t he fire marshal wants a 
life safety plan, so that he is comfortable that the residents are safe during the construction process.  The life safety plan should show that 
there is adequate separation between construction and the living space.   It would also include having appropriate exit signs throughout 
the renovation, fire extinguishers, and two means of egress, as well as a fire-rated wall between spaces.  In order to move the tenants, the 
fire marshal wants Housing Connect to be able to s how that the existing sy stem works as designed.  A moving consultant is also involved 
with the moving of tenants. 
 
- East 72:  Construction is proceeding, although the schedule is uncertain.  The project is about 82% complete, and there is about $200,000 
in contingency remaining. There is an indication from the contractor that the project will not be completed by the deadline.  This is new 
information and we will keep the board apprised.   
 
- Granger:  The Section 18 disposition application was approved by HUD.  Housing Connect has received the notice to proceed from the 
equity investors (Wells Fargo) for construction to start.  The tentative closing date has been set for Thursday, October 26.  On Wednesday, 
November 1, at 2:00 p.m., Housing Connect will hold a celebratory kickoff. 
 
5. CEO’s REPORT 
 
CEO Janice Kimball reported to the Board the following items: 
 
- recent fire at the Medina Apartments:  The Medina Apartments are 40 units, co-owned with First Step House .  No one was injured in a 
recent fire, and the sprinklers did a really good job.  Five units were damaged, and eight households were relocated, and four of them 
should be able to move back soon. 
 
- pending federal government shutdown:  CEO Kimball noted that Housing Connect’s programs will continue, as Housing Connect is deemed 
essential. 
 
- outreach opportunity #1:  CEO Kimball participated in a Pacific Islander community outreach event on Friday, September 22, at Salt Lake 
Community College. 
 
- outreach opportunity #2:  Jeff Olivet, the Executive Director of the U.S. Inter-agency Council on Homelessness, recently toured the Central 
City property.  This was an opportunity to highlight Housing Connect’s partnership with First Step House and that particular housing model. 
 
6. CLOSED MEETING 
 
At 12:15 p.m., Commissioner Nguyen motioned for the Board to enter Closed Meeting to discuss real estate and possible litigation issues.  
Commissioner Litvack seconded the motion, and all Board members present (Vice Chair Bernal and Commissioners Johnson , Litvack,

the Board returned to regular session. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
7. 2024 BUDGET TIMELINE 
 
Chief Financial Officer (CFO) Andre Bartlome provided a timeline for the 2024 budget and said he and Controller Tom Biesinger had been 
working on it since the beginning of September, meeting with department directors.  He will have a preliminary budget by November 1 and 
will send it to the owners and investors of Low-Income Housing Tax Credit entities that day.  Revised budgets will be completed before the 
Finance Committee meeting (the proposed date for that is Thursday, November 9).  The 2024 budget will be discussed at the November 15 
Board meeting, and he asked that Board members provide their input at eithe r the Finance Committee meeting or at the following Board 
meeting (on November 15), in order for the budget to be wrapped up and approved at the December Board meeting (currently scheduled 
for December 20).  The Housing Opportunities, Inc. (HOI) and the Affordable Housing Associates, Inc. (AHA) boards will also meet then to 
approve their respective 2024 budgets, while the Housing Connect Fund (HCF) Board will approve its budget at the December 4 HCF Board 
meeting. 
 
8. FINANCIAL REPORT 
 
CFO Bartlome provided the Board with a financial  summary of programs for the 14 months ending August 2023, including asset 
management projects, public housing, ROSS FSS, central office, central management, development, owned units, supportive housing, and 
Housing Choice Voucher (HCV), as well as other e ntities, such as AHA, HOI, and HCF.  He also reviewed LIHTC properties for the 8 months 
ending August 2023, and he provided a summary of reserves (restricted, unrestricted). Overall, the agency is doing well.
