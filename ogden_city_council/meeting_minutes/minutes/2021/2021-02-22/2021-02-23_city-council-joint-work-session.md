    resources and community guides that can be helpful to business owners and visitors alike. Finally, the ODA created the Applied
    Creative Arts Academy, which is a student based free program where students can create content that will be embedded in ODA 's
    messaging via the Shop Small social media campaign, the Urban State music series, and the Ogden Culture miniseries "The

Fiscal Year 2021 Budget and Salary Schedule Amendments - Building
Services
          Management Services Director Brown reported Administration proposes to amend the budget and salary schedule for the
Building Services Division to increase the salary ranges for building inspectors and plan reviewers. The amendment includes a
transfer of funding from the salary contingency fund to cover the additional Fiscal Year (FY)21 payroll costs. She noted that this
adjustment has become a priority because of the great deal of competition in the building services field; many of the City's
inspectors and plan reviewers are being aggressively recruited by other municipalities as well as private building inspection
companies. The $8,050 increase would be implemented in the current FY and would also be an ongoing expense in future FYs.
Ms. Brown then noted the budget amendment also recognizes revenue from the sale of fleet assets ($286,900) and appropriates the
funds in the Capital Improvement Plan for use in the fleet replacement program. Vehicles were surplussed from five Departments
across the City and the revenue generated by this action will be used to reinvest in the fleet replacement program.
          Council member Nadolski inquired as to the balance in the payroll continency fund. Ms. Brown stated she is unsure of
the current balance but can report back to the Council at a later date. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith added she also does
not know the current balance, but noted Administration typically includes $200,000 to $300,000 in that account each FY; this
money is also used to payout vacation leave balances when employees leave the City. Deputy Comptroller Sorensen noted that the
annual budget amount is typically $200,000, but in FY21 the line item was reduced to $100,000 as part ofthe budget adjustments
responsive to the COVID-19 pandemic.
          Vice Chair White stated that she would like to understand what happens to money that is unspent as a result of a position
vacancy. Mr. Sorensen stated that any unspent wage allocations are rolled into the general fund balance at the end of each FY.
          Ms. Eller-Smith stated that if there are no objections, there will be an item listed on the March 16 meeting agenda to set a
public hearing regarding these proposed adjustments. No objections were voiced.

Fiscal Year 2021 Budget Amendment- Airport; and Fiscal Year 2021 -
2025 Capital Improvement Plan Amendment- Airport
          Mr. Sorensen reported the proposed budget amendment recognizes and appropriates $6 million from the State of Utah.
Funds will be used for infrastructure improvements at the Airport, including utilities, roadways, access, and direct flight support.
This action would result in a net adjustment of $6 million to the Airport Fund in the FY2021 Ogden City Budget. He noted the
project has four distinct components:
              1. Tie into, improve, and extend 4000 South onto Airport property, turning north until just past the two concrete
                   water tanks where the road will terminate in a cul-de-sac with a sufficient diameter to allow semi-trucks with
                   heavy earth-moving equipment to access the development site.
              2. The design and construction of a partial parallel taxiway west of the approach to Runway 17. This taxiway will
                   fully meet all FAA requirements and will allow aircraft using either OGD Airport runway, to exit the runways
                   and gain access to the new west side development.
              3. The clearing, grading and drainage of the new OGD Airport west side development area. This includes the
                  removal of the concrete and construction debris from the dumping that has occurred at the OGD Airport over
                   the past 3 0-40 years, including debris from the old Ogden Mall. This will require all basic stormwater elements,
                   the pulverization of old taxiway and runway surfaces, and preparing the grade for proper drainage and
                   maintenance.
              4. The strategic stubbing out and placement of the utilities so that the developer( s) can access these utilities at a
                   reasonable and known expense.
          Council member Stephens asked if the Federal Aviation Administration is contributing to the project. Mr. Sorenson noted
the City receives annual funding from the FAA, but for this particular project, there is no additional FAA funding. Community
and Economic Development Director Christopulos noted the City only receives FAA funding for tarmac improvements. This
project is new construction and is not eligible for FAA funding.
          Mr. Sorensen noted that Administration recommends the Council schedule a public hearing for March 16 to take action
on the proposed amendments.

2nd Quarter Financial Report
         Management Services Director Brown used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the 2nd Quarter Financial
Report. Tax revenues are expected to exceed budgeted amounts by $3.3 million due to higher-than-expected sales tax revenue.
The City's conservative approach in budgeting due to unknown impacts ofCOVID-19 is the primary cause ofthe difference.
Charges for services are projected to be below budget by approximately $400,000. She provided a chart offering a comparison of
budgeted expenses/revenues with actual expenses/revenues and noted all other City funds are tracking with the FY21 budget.

Council Business
          CO VID-19 Meeting Procedure Adjustments: Council member Stephens stated that he noticed the Weber/Morgan Health
Department changed the areas status due to the decreases in COVID-19 cases; he asked if the City Council may be able to resume
in-person meetings in the near future. Chair Blair stated Council staff and Council leadership will continue to monitor the situation
to determine when it is possible to resume in-person meetings. Council member Choberka added that the change in status only
impacts the number of people that can gather in a group setting. There are still requirements for social distancing and mask
wearing and it can be difficult to maintain a six-foot distance in an in-person meeting setting.
          Continued Review of Council Meeting Agenda: Council Deputy Director Symes referenced Council member
Choberka's earlier comments regarding the utility billing adjustments, and he facilitated discussion among the Council regarding
whether there is support from other Council members for delaying that item until they have the opportunity to discuss the proposal
specific to late fees further. Council member Choberka stated that she is concerned about imposing late fees where there is not a
standard due date set for each utility account; she is also concerned about the difficulty in enrolling an electronic payment
program and the impact that unreasonable late fees will have on those on a fixed income. There was discussion about the bill
payment timelines; Mr. Symes stated there is a 14-day grace period and late fees will be imposed three days after the expiration of
the grace period. Council member Choberka stated she would like further clarification on the grace period and the timing of the
imposition of late fees prior to holding the public hearing. Ms. Eller-Smith recommended pulling the item from the consent
agenda for tonight's meeting to avoid noticing a document that could change between now and the time that the public hearing is
held.
