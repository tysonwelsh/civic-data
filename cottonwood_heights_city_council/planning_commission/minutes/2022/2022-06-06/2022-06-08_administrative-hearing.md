29   Assistant City Planner, Maverick Yeh, presented the staff report for the two requests above. The
30   property is located in the R-2-8 zone where short-term rentals may be approved as a conditional
31   use. The property is part of a PUD that contains 34 units. The property fronts private lands. The
32   application must be complete, and a Business License obtained. A public hearing notice was sent
33   out and one comment was received.
34
35   The applicant, Jeffrey Lunt, stated that they take the necessary steps to ensure that they are good
36   neighbors. He stated that short-term rentals are a viable solution to meet lodging needs within the
37   City. In response to a question raised, Mr. Lunt stated that each unit is allotted two stalls with a
38   maximum occupancy of six. They also closely monitor the properties with a decibel meter inside
39   that alerts them of rowdy behavior. In the event of noise, they address it immediately. Each unit
40   has two bedrooms and two bathrooms with each bedroom sleeping up to three people. Four is the
41   typical occupancy.
42
43   Community and Economic Development Director/Hearing Officer, Michael Johnson, opened the
44   public hearing.
45
46   Mark Lambert identified himself as an owner of Park Ridge Estates. He reported that his wife
47   serves as Secretary of the Homeowners Association (“HOA”) and Jeffrey Lunt serves as President.

 1   Messrs. Lunt and Romero have been operating the short-term rental unlicensed for approximately
 2   six months. Mr. Lambert found out about it three weeks earlier when his tenant called him with
 3   concerns about parking and the potential for her special needs daughter to gain unauthorized access

 7   Chairman of the Architectural Committee, and he expressed his objection to the proposal. There
 8   have also been instances involving Methamphetamine use in the complex where two units were
 9   condemned. Two additional units tested positive.
10
11   Bijan Fakhreih reported that he has owned a unit in the complex for over 40 years. He has also
12   served previously as President of the HOA. He stated that the requests of Messrs. Lunt and
13   Romero should have received approval from the HOA before coming to the City since the Bylaws
14   rule. He was concerned that the applicants did not present their request to the HOA before making
15   an application with the City.
16
17   Mr. Johnson stated that short-term rental applications are common and often operate in areas where
18   there is an HOA, which involves private contracts that are entered into between an ownership
19   organization and the owners. They are private agreements that are fully enforceable but not by the
20   City. They are not factored into any City decision. In each case, there is assurance that City
21   ordinances are followed.
22
23   Mr. Lunt stated that the hot tub is on his private property but is not fenced. He expressed a
24   willingness to fence it. Mr. Johnson felt that was reasonable and stated that because short-term
25   rentals are conditional uses, they can look at potential negative impacts and impose conditions to
26   mitigate them.
27
28   With regard to the concern about parking, Mr. Johnson stated that City ordinance requires that
29   parking in any short-term rental use only the parking that is entitled to the unit. There are
30   conditions in the City Ordinance addressing noise violations or nuisances. Specific instances can
31   be reported and investigated. Ongoing issues may result in license revocation. Mr. Johnson felt
32   that the units meet all of the ordinance criteria for short-term rentals. Staff provided a
33   comprehensive list of recommended conditions of approval. He saw nothing in the ordinance or
34   the application that would prohibit the City from granting the request.
