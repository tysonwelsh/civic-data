      B.     "SCHOOL ORDINANCE CODE TEXT AMENDMENT," PLZ-24-5002,
             AMENDING RIVERTON CITY CODE SECTION 18.195.060(3)(I),
             ADDRESSING MINIMUM PARCEL SIZE FOR A PRIVATE OR QUASI-
             PUBLIC SCHOOL.

Mr. Lethbridge presented the Staff Report and indicated that the item was heard at a
previous Planning Commission meeting, where discussions were held regarding the
proposed amendment and its potential ripple effects. This section of City Code regulates
schools that are outside the public school system. The current Code specifies a minimum
parcel size of three acres, and there has been interest in the community to open this type of
school on a smaller parcel. Discussions were held between Staff and the City Council
regarding why the three-acre minimum was imposed. When it was adopted, broad impacts
were considered. The initial presentation to the Planning Commission proposed decreasing
the minimum from three acres to one acre. Other standards allow the Commission to require
more property to accommodate parking, circulation, and other site-specific needs. Potential
impacts were reviewed, including the number of pickups and drop-offs and their traffic
impacts, and additional changes were made in an attempt to address those concerns and
simplify the Code.

When the ordinance was adopted, there were only a couple of private schools. A proposed
school was very large with a potential student population in the hundreds. That school did
not come to Riverton City but if it had, the impacts could have been significant. That market
is now focusing on smaller applications. For example, the Montessori School on 13200
South is smaller in scale and has a smaller student body. The proposed amendment would
decrease the minimum required parcel size from three acres to one acre, but the language
would be retained in the ordinance to indicate that more acreage may be required for larger
facilities as determined by the City. That would allow the Planning Commission to make
decisions in context.

The amendment specifies that these types of schools will be allowed only on property
adjacent to a collector or arterial roadway. These are larger roads, for example, Redwood
Road, that are designed for higher capacity use. Minor collector streets are excluded.
These quasi-public schools will not be permitted in residential zones. This helps the City
regulate impacts inside of neighborhoods, on cul-de-sacs, or smaller streets that are not
designed to accommodate that type of traffic. Potential impacts including traffic noise,
parking, emergency response, and stormwater would be considered when the Conditional
Use Permit is reviewed. Existing language that will be retained in the Code specifies that
further restrictions can be imposed based on proximity to other schools and the
neighborhood's context. The language is not overly specific to avoid unintended
consequences and leaves discretion to the Commission to set requirements and limitations
based on the specific application but also make clear what must be addressed.

Commissioner Cannon thanked Mr. Lethbridge for clarifying the wording in the amendment.

In response to a question from Commissioner Park, Mr. Carter stated that there should not
be any compliance issues with state law with regard to the size of the property.

Commissioner Rushton stated that he hoped to see the number of students outlined in the
amendment. Mr. Lethbridge noted that daycares and home schools are addressed in
Section 2, and sports in Section 3. He gave an example of six-year-olds going to a home
school in someone's basement versus 16-year-olds attending that school. Section
18.195.060(3)(b) in the existing Code states, "The number of students allowed to attend or
be boarded at a private or quasi-public school shall be determined based in part on the size
of the parcel or property on which the school is to be located." Part of the Conditional Use
Permit process is reviewing the number of students and determining whether the property
is sufficient for that number of students. The authority is already there for the Planning
Commission to say, for example, that the number of students or demographic of students
being proposed would create impacts that could not be mitigated.

In response to a follow-up question from Commissioner Rushton, Mr. Lethbridge stated that
if someone applies to open a home-based quasi-public school, the Home Occupation
Ordinance would take precedence. They would have to meet the same requirements as
home-based daycares, preschools, dance studios, etc. This ordinance would not entitle
someone to open a school in their home. These types of schools are a conditional use in
residential property. If someone desires to convert a home into a school, the ordinance
would apply but they could not circumvent the Home Occupation Ordinance by saying they
are a private school. If they want to open this type of school in an empty commercial space,
it would still be a conditional use. If the property has poor air circulation or insufficient
parking, the Planning Commission would have the authority to deny the application. The
underlying zoning in that case would be governed by the Commercial Code. This
amendment only applies to residential zoning.

       A.     ARCHITECTURAL DISCUSSION RELATED TO BUILDING CONCEPT 1830
              WEST 12600 SOUTH.

Mr. Prestwich presented a pre-application for a restaurant concept plan for discussion. The
opportunity to review the plan and provide advance feedback on the building architecture
and appearance can help streamline the process and prevent applications that are not in
compliance with the City Code or the theme of an area of the City. He noted that there will
be no motion on the item.

The pre-application is for a restaurant at the intersection of 12800 South and 1300 West in
downtown Riverton. The applicants plan to renovate the old Carl's Jr. building, which was
built approximately 20 years ago. The interested party and their architect were present at
the meeting to respond to the question. The initial concept is a significant change from the
current architecture, which is why it was brought to the Planning Commission for discussion
prior to application. If it proceeds to an application, a full hearing and review will be required.

Architect Brian Russell, a previous member of the Riverton Planning Commission, spoke on
behalf of the project. The proposed renovation incorporates turn-of-the-century architecture.
The rooflines will be broken up, a tower element will be added at the entry, and heavy brick,
tall wainscotting, and deep fascia lines will be added around the playland portion of the
building. It is a completely different look both for Burger King and the current Carl's Jr.
building.

Commissioner Cluff clarified that the building must include turn-of-the-century
characteristics because it is in downtown Riverton. She asked if additional standards were
required on the neighboring building. Mr. Prestwich noted that the buildings in this area
were approved with requirements for consistency. The Intermountain Farmers Association
("IFA") Center covenants require that the IFA sign off on any architectural changes to the
surrounding buildings, so an application for this project will require their approval. A
component of the discussion on the adjacent building was about preserving the consistent

building in the area that had stone, which did not match anything else in the city center.
