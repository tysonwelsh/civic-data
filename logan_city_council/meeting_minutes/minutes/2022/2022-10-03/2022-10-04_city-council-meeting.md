OPENING CEREMONY:
Ed Stafford, USU Professor provided the opening ceremony and led the audience in the
pledge of allegiance.
Mr. Stafford co-led the Utah Clean Air Marketing Contest (initiated in 2015 at Logan
High School). He thanked the Council and staff for all that they do include punch passes
from the aquatic center to be used as prizes. This past year, 2020, there were a thousand
students from across the state participating from Southern Idaho to Montezuma Creek.
The contest integrates art, environmental science, and marketing to create public service
announcements to encourage fellow teens to not idle their cars, etc. There were 60
finalists across the state with 23 state winners winning from $100 to $250 cash. There
will be another contest hosted this year and it is all thanks to the support from the City of
Logan, businesses in Cache Valley, and across the State.
Chair Simmonds requested further clarification on the process of the contest.
Mr. Stafford responded that in June, he sends out donation letters requesting awards or
cash from various businesses across the state. There are High School winners where the
High School selects their finalists to go to the State level. There are judges who will serve
on a panel to determine the winners. This past year there were 30 judges to evaluate the
60 semi-finalists. The award ceremony is at the North Eccles Harrison Museum of Art on
the second Saturday of every February for the presentation of awards. Thousands of teens
have participated in the contest since 2015. Two former winners are at present marketing
majors at USU, where he will have the privilege to teach.

Domestic Violence Awareness Month – Alyna Ohling, CAPSA

Alyna Ohling from CAPSA addressed the Council thanking them for the opportunity to
present. October is domestic violence awareness month. Throughout the year, they have
the opportunity to help survivors who have experienced domestic violence (which is
present in our community). We are happy to provide individual support tailored to the
needs of the community.

CAPSA started in 1976 as the Cache Valley Rape Crisis team. Law Enforcement began
to immediately use this as a resource. Our community has benefited from cooperative
efforts from across the community. If CAPSA did not exist that is 2,000 individuals who
would have social supported services (for domestic violence, sexual assault, or rape for
the year 2021). There would be 8,000 calls that go unanswered (on average in a fiscal
year). 300 individuals would have no place to safely escape to (meaning that they and
their children would not have a safe place to reside in). On average, 350 individuals are
sheltered. Thousands of individuals would not have had advocacy or therapy to help them
heal from their abuse. 400 individuals would be left without a place to learn and process
(via support groups). And 89 families would be left without a long-term home following
an escape from abuse. There is a transitional housing program in Logan, (CAPSA owns

Certification of Zollinger Global Property Annexation Petition – Consideration of
an annexation petition filed by Lance Zollinger and Guy Marker. Property located
on the East side of North 1000 West at approximately 2000 North, consisting of
18.93 acres and is identified as parcels (04-076-0010 and 04-076-0011) – Mike
DeSimone, Community Development Director

Community Development Director Mike DeSimone addressed the Council regarding the
annexation petition. He stated that Lance Zollinger and Guy marker, on behalf of Global
Property 2000 LLC, have submitted a formal annexation petition for property located on
the East side of 1000 West at approximately 2000 North, consisting of approximately
18.93 acres identified as TIN: 04-076-0010 and 04-076-0011.

Mr. DeSimone explained that once an annexation petition has been submitted to the City
according to UCA 10-2-405, the Municipal Council needs to either accept the petition for
further consideration or reject the petition due to specific deficiencies. If the annexation
petition is accepted by the Council, the City Recorder will certify the petition and then it
will b referred to Community Development for distribution to city agencies, affected
entities, and the Planning Commission for review and recommendations.

The proposed annexation request has tentatively been scheduled on the Planning
Commission’s October 27, 2022, public hearing agenda, the Council’s November 15,
2022, workshop agenda, and the Council’s December 6, 2022, public hearing agenda.

If the Council rejects the petition, staff will then follow the process outlined in UCA 10-
2-405(3)(a) and (b) and direct the applicant to address any deficiencies with their
annexation petition and then have them resubmit once corrected.

Chair Simmonds inquired if the annexation is within the airport overlay.

Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director responded the airport has been
notified of the annexation petition. The location will stay within boundaries, the plan is to
build cold storage units with potential tenants already lined up.

(Continued from the September 20, 2022, Council meeting) CODE AMENDMENT –
Consideration of a proposed amendment to Land Development Code Chapter
17.10.100 Mixed-Use Development Standards and Chapter 17.34 Residential
Density and Height Bonuses. These changes modify specific density, design &
development standards for projects in the Mixed-Use zones as well as establish a
density bonus program for Mixed-Use projects – Ordinance 22-19 – Mike
DeSimone, Community Development Director

Community Development Director Mike DeSimone addressed the Council regarding the
proposed code amendment. In the Council packet, he provided the Council with updated
Land Development Code Section 17.10.100 and 17.34. Based on the input from the last
Council hearing on September 20, 2022, he eliminated the Commercial proximity
provision and included language describing the types of land uses that are indicate of a
Mixed-Use development. The purpose of this language is to recognize that while certain
uses are permitted or conditionally permitted in the Mixed-Use Zone, some of the Mixed-
Use development standards would not necessarily apply. The specific requirements or
standards that generally would not apply would be the minimum Residential
requirements, the minimum Commercial requirements, and the requirement to put
Commercial on the ground floor of buildings next to an arterial or collector street. All
other design and development standards, e.g., setbacks, heights, parking, design, etc.,
contained in the Land Development Code would still apply.

The Council expressed they were content with the changes made after their concerns
were addressed.

Chair Simmonds inquired regarding the use of sidewalks in front of small shops as open
spaces.
Mr. DeSimone said the definition of public gathering in open areas (if the sidewalks in
front of a shop are a public gathering area). A typical standard sidewalk does not
automatically qualify for a public gathering. The criteria require elements such as width,
room for seating tables, landscaping, etc. And ONLY then can this area be reviewed and
considered a public gathering area.

Chair Simmonds asked if a public gathering area that did not include a 15 ft. sidewalk
commercial area still qualifies.

Mr. DeSimone replied that would depend on if the criteria are met or not. The language
intent is for the Planning Commission to be able to be discretionary. As well as to make it
more inviting and more attractive for the community. The distinction is how, they plan,
and develop the space.

Vice Chair López requested clarification on page 05 of the document, (numbered pg 10-7
in the packet). "All buildings, or portions thereof, fronting onto an existing or planned
arterial or collector street, as designated on the Logan City Transportation Master Plan,
shall have at least 50% of the ground floor designed and built for commercial uses." The
concern is that this paragraph leaves room for misinterpretation of design and building
rather than say use.

Mr. DeSimone explained that is not the intent, what the language is saying is that the
front half of a building on an arterial or collector street must be designed/built for
commercial activity.

Vice Chair López inquired if a building on an arterial or collector is built and designed to
the commercial standards, but can the owner rent the intended commercial area as
residential.

Mr. DeSimone said that the front ground floor cannot be residential. The owner could do
the side or back half of the building as residential, but not the front ground floor. The
majority of the building on the front end of the street, 50% must be commercial.

Vice Chair López highlighted the technicality of the language. The main concern is the
previously mentioned example and if it could be misconstrued.

Mr. DeSimone clarified that would be a problem as design/built for commercial is
different from design/built for residential. There are different parameters and needs for
construction including the use itself of the space.

Vice Chair López asked what the consequence would be if said situation came about.

Mr. DeSimone answered firstly a building permit would need to be acquired. However,
staff would issue said permit per the municipal code.
