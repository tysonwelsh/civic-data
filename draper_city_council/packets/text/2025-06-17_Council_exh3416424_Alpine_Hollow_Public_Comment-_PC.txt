Dear Draper City Planning Commission,

We are writing in opposition to the proposed Alpine Hollow development of 50 single-family homes, which includes only
a single road for ingress and egress—located directly behind our neighborhood (Mercer Retreat). While we recognize
that the developer owns the land and may have a right to build within applicable zoning guidelines, we respectfully urge
the city to enforce its current subdivision and fire safety standards, which limit developments with a single access point
to no more than 30 homes (DCMC §17-5-030(G), (H)).

This proposal exceeds that limit by 20 homes and would only be possible if the city grants an optional exemption from
its two-egress requirement—an exemption the developer is not entitled to by right. Given that a second access point
through Alpine has already been denied in past proposals and that many surrounding residents—including us—strongly
oppose this level of density, we urge Draper City not to make exceptions that prioritize developer interests over the
safety, standards, and quality of life of existing neighborhoods.

Please uphold Draper’s subdivision and fire code standards and limit the project to 30 homes unless a fully compliant
second access point is provided.
