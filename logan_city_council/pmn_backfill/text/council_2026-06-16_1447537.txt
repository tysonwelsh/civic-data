69      •    Force Reductions: The department's baseline force dropped from 60 officers to 56
70           personnel last July.
71      •    Wage Pressures: Cache County implemented an arbitrary 10% wage increase for its
72           personnel to stem retention losses. To avoid losing additional staff (having lost 12
73           officers to neighboring high-paying agencies in two years), Logan PD vacated 4

 74           departing positions and reallocated those funds to match the 10% raise for remaining
 75           experienced staff.
 76      •    Operational Strain: Operating at 56 officers creates "zero margin scheduling".
 77           Illnesses, injuries, and mandatory training deplete active road shifts. The traffic shift
 78           had to be disbanded to absorb baseline demands. Increased dependency on overtime
 79           is causing severe officer burnout and a resulting inability to fill special event shifts.
 80      •    Population Metrics: Logan's core population sits at roughly 54,000 residents
 81           (inclusive of university students). With 56 officers, Logan's officer ratio is 1.03 per

 84           force.

 86           seeking two backfilled positions, this budget cycle, one funded directly by property tax
 87           and one funded internally through the department.

101   Fund 520: Wastewater Treatment

102      •    Revenue Status: Year-to-date revenue sits close to $4.6 million. Impact fees
103           collected year-to-date total $734,000 for Logan City, with participating entities adding
104           an extra $1.4 million.
105      •    Debt Service: The largest single budget item is annual debt service matching $5.2
106           million. The project broke ground via a 30-year bond program. The outstanding debt
107           was $77 million at the start of fiscal year 2026 and will drop to roughly $72 million by
108           the end of this fiscal year at low interest rates.
109      •    Fund Restructuring: Reorganizations were finalized to pull the pre-treatment division
110           from Fund 570 (Solid Waste) directly into Fund 520, as its functionality scales directly
111           with wastewater operations.
112      •    Personnel Requests: Requesting a new Superintendent position to coordinate
113           vendors and operations. Current structures drag down physical labor shifts when
114           operators focus on administrative duties.

116           is covered by two distinct EPA grants matching $5 million and $1.75 million on an

124   Fund 570: Solid Waste Collection & Disposal

125      •    Operations Data: The system services 21,000 residential clients, 3,700 curbside
126           green waste accounts, and 3,200 commercial operations. It processed 113,000 landfill
127           entries last year.
128      •    Tonnage Throughput: Logan Landfill accepted 89,000 tons of CND (Construction &
129           Demolition) waste, while the North Valley site took in 86,000 tons of MSW (Municipal
130           Solid Waste). North Valley tracks 300 to 350 tons daily, five days a week.
131      •    Logan Landfill Lifespan: The physical capacity of the Logan location will be
132           exhausted in approximately 2 years. Post-closure, CND transport operations must
133           shift entirely to North Valley. Because standard transfer structures cannot drop dense
134           CND waste cleanly from standard MSW trailers, a separate open-air CND transfer
135           yard is slated for buildout.
136      •    Reserves & Rates: Total solid waste reserves hold $33 million, though $5.2 million is
137           locked strictly for mandatory legacy landfill closure phases. Capital projects carry
138           forward $6.3 million for the new CND transfer layout. A predefined tipping rate
139           increase moves costs from $38 to $40 a ton this July.
140      •    Fuel Deficit Concerns: The baseline fuel plan targeted a $3.00 to $3.50 per gallon
141           metric for diesel, but subsequent 30% to 40% real-world price spikes run the risk of
142           squeezing the system's $1 million fuel budget lines.

232      •    Context: Mr. Anderson stated that sometime prior, Cache County passed Resolution
233           2026-23, which effectively amplified their dedicated fire district tax base. Because
234           Logan City manages its own fire department and remains independent of the county
235           district, Logan taxpayers are entirely exempt from that specific increase.
236      •    Tax Levy Reduction: To balance numbers, the county is subsequently decreasing its
237           general county levy baseline applied to all area residents, resulting in an estimated
238           property tax reduction of $1.360 million for Logan property owners.
239      •    Logan's Balancing Increase: Finance proposed that Logan City implement an ad
240           valorem property tax rate hike to recapture that exact $1.360 million structural window
241           being vacated by the county.
242      •    The Zero Net Impact Reality: Because Logan's proposed rate hike directly copies
243           the financial drop implemented by the county, the actual net impact on an average
244           resident or commercial business owner's total combined tax statement matches 0%
245           ($0.00 net increase). Legally, this must be aggressively advertised to the community
246           as a 21% to 22.88% surge in internal city property tax revenues, despite yielding a
247           net-neutral change on real-world tax statements. The shift keeps local tax dollars
248           directly localized inside city safety programs.

249   Allocation of the $1.360 Million Shifted Tax Revenue:

250      1. Fire Department Operational Fund ($1,174,300): Allocated directly to cover the
251         baseline operational staffing costs of the upcoming, long-anticipated Station 73.
252      2. Police Department Operational Fund ($131,086): Dedicated specifically to rescue
253         and retain one additional Police Officer 1 position that would otherwise face
254         baseline elimination under legacy budget realities.
255      3. Non-Departmental Fund ($54,614): Routed cleanly into internal street lighting utility
256         line accounts to fulfill mandatory power bill clearings with the Light and Power
257         Department.

269           purchases. Operational funds will temporarily stack inside capital reserve structures
270           alongside interest yields and external contract cash loops over the next 2 to 5 years
