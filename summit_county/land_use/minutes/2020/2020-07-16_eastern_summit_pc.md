he can participate fairly. 
Planner Milliner shared the background for this application. When lots 2 and 3 were built, 
the contractor built the road around the hill. When the lots were platted, the road ran 
through the middle of the hill. The proposed plat amendment would change the plat to 
follow the existing road. Staff recommends holding a public hearing and approving this

Commissioner Clyde stated there are a million of these kinds of situations and wishes 
they could be done administratively to save the cost to the applicant. 
 
Findings of Fact:  
1. The applicants, Fox Run Homeowners Association are requesting a plat amendment for Lots 2 
and 3 of the Fox Run South Subdivision.  
2. The property is in the Agriculture-10 (Ag-10) zone.  
3. Built Fox Run Road does not match platted Fox Run Road.  
4. Fox Run Road is a private road.  
5. There is a hill that protrudes into the platted right-of-way at the approximate lot line between 
lots 2 and 3.  
6. When the road was constructed, rather than excavate the hill, whomever built the road decided 
to circumvent it, bringing the road outside of the right-of-way.  
7. Comments from service providers, including County Engineering, County Health, County 
Recorder, North Summit Fire, and various State and Federal Agencies found no significant 
issues with the amendment.  
 
Conclusions of Law:  
1. There is good cause for the proposed Plat Amendment.  
2. No one will be materially harmed by this Plat Amendment.  
3. The Plat Amendment is consistent with the Eastern Summit County General Plan, as amended.

4. Public hearing and possible action regarding a Conditional Use Permit for a Rocky 
Mountain Power transmission line upgrade in the U.S.R. 40 corridor area between 
Snyderville Basin and Eastern Summit County Planning Districts and along I-80 
corridor up to Kimball Junction, Don Watts representing Rocky Mountain Power, 
Applicant. – Amir Caus, AICP, County Planner 
 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
July 16, 2020 
Page 5 of 15 
 
Planner Caus shared the background for this application. The upgrade for the power lines 
is part of the wild fire protection initiative as well as an update of aging equipment. The 
lines are in a wild fire area and are older than forty to fifty years. Snyderville Basin 
Planning Commission voted to approve their portion of the Conditional Use Permit (CUP). 
There will be a revegetation/reclamation plan if any vegetation is damaged. There is a 
small cut and fill area that will be recontoured to its original state. The EPA (Environmental 
Protection Agency) will have to sign off on the project. Staff recommends approval of the 
CUP according to the Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law, and Conditions of Approval in 
the Staff Report. 
Lisa Romney, the Regional Business Manager stated that it is not typical to tear down and 
rebuild an entire line. There is a heightened risk of wildfires in the area. This project will 
replace like for like and pole for pole. There will not be an increase in the number of poles 
or lines. If the poles are currently wood, they will remain wood. There will be some changes 
in the Snyderville Basin Planning area. Planner Caus stated the poles will be the same style 
but an average of seven feet taller.  
Commissioner Sonntag stated there will be an adverse view impact in Promontory by 
raising the poles higher. Homeowners built carefully to avoid the power lines. Brad 
Richardson, the Project Manager stated that the project will meet current codes. The 
standards and clearance to the electrical fields requires higher poles. Andrew Thum stated 
that new structures are being built to meet current standards and current Code. The new 
lines are avian safe. The very top wire protects from lightning fire hazards. The poles are 
being replaced with newer, stronger poles. Commissioner Sonntag requested that the 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
July 16, 2020 
Page 6 of 15 
 
current poles be left in place for 329 through 333. Andrew Thum stated the entire line 
shows older poles that will need to be replaced. Planner Caus stated the lines and poles 
were replaced in 1972. 
Commissioner Benson asked if there was any way to bury the lines near residential areas 
to mitigate the view and give more of a buffer for the fire area. Lisa Romney answered that 
there is no way for Rocky Mountain Power to fund burying lines. The lines could be buried 
but the cost would have to be funded by a government agency. The cost of replacement 
would be fifteen to twenty times more expensive as the conductors are very large and 
expensive. The lines are regulated as an overhead utility, and if the request is different then 
the project must be funded by the requester. The goal of this project is to bring the lines up 
to current standards, including fire protection. The Promontory homeowners have not 
made any comment about the project. Planner Caus stated that notices were sent out to 
every property within 1000 feet of the property line of the power line. 
The Public hearing was opened. No comment was made and the Public hearing was closed. 
Commissioner Wheaton stated that she suspects the homeowners are not aware of the 
height of the new poles and the impact to their views. 
Commissioner Clyde stated his surprise at the shorter life cycle of the poles. 
Chair Sargent stated that power line upgrades are never fun. There is a visual issue, but 
the cost to bury any lines is exorbitant.  
Findings of Fact:  
1. The applicant is requesting to upgrade ~7.5 miles of an existing Rocky Mountain Power 
transmission line (~100 poles), located in the U.S.R. 40 corridor area between the Snyderville 
Basin and Eastern Summit County Planning Districts and along I-80 corridor up to Kimball 
Junction (~7.5 miles).  
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
July 16, 2020 
Page 7 of 15 
 
2. The upgrades take place in both the Eastern Summit County Planning District and Snyderville 
Basin Planning District.  
3. The purpose of the project is to upgrade the lines as part of an ongoing effort for wildfire 
mitigation.  
4. According to the applicant, the lines will be rebuilt to the up-to-date national electric safety 
codes and avian safe standards.  
5. The upgrade will accommodate existing and future electric power needs for Summit County.  
6. Per the Eastern Summit County Development Code, “Utility towers and associated 
transmission and distribution lines greater than 45 feet in height” are reviewed under a 
Conditional Use Permit.  
7. The average pole height of the existing transmission line is ~63 feet.  
8. The average pole height of the upgraded transmission line is ~70 feet.  
9. Heights of poles are determined through this Conditional Use Permit.  
10. All poles in the Eastern Summit County Planning District are wood poles with dark, earth 
tone colors which blend into the background to help mitigate visual impact.  
11. Portions of the transmission line are located in the Silver Creek Overlay Zone and the United 
States Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) will need to forwarded their approval prior to 
this Conditional Use Permit taking effect.  
 
Conclusions of Law:  
1. The proposed project, as conditioned, meets the requisite standards set forth in the Section 11-
4-7(B) of the Eastern Summit County Development Code.  
 
Conditions of Approval:  
1. The location and height shall be as per the Exhibit A drawings as found in this Staff Report.  
2. No outdoor storage is permitted.  
3. No modification to the approved Conditional Use Permit is permitted without the prior 
approval of the Summit County Community Development Department.  
4. Final grading shall be reviewed and approved by the Engineering and Planning Departments at 
the grading permit time.  
5. All disturbed area shall be revegetated with a native, drought tolerant seed mix.  
6. A bond shall be in place to insure site restoration/revegetation of the disturbed areas.  
7. All necessary permits must be obtained and fees shall be paid prior to the commencement of 
any construction activity, including but not limited to the Summit County Engineering and the 
Summit County Building Departments (when applicable).  
8. All other service provider requirements shall be met.

5. Public hearing to review potential amendments to Section 11-6 of the Eastern 
Summit County Development Code. The purpose of the amendments is to provide 
permitting regulations for both commercial and private Campgrounds in Eastern 
Summit County. – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner shared the background for these potential amendments. The County 
Council has been fielding questions regarding private and commercial campgrounds, 
mainly in Samak and Manor Lands. The Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
directed Staff to draft regulations of campgrounds, and staff is now presenting the initial 
draft. There are individuals currently living in recreational vehicles on platted lots. There 
are potential waste issues and the regulations would help to mitigate the impacts from 
these recreational vehicles. 
Seasonal use of recreational vehicles, with time limits and limited number of recreational 
vehicles on one lot would be in place to protect the groundwater. Campgrounds are not 
allowed within platted subdivisions. Attorney Strachan stated that people with multiple 
recreational vehicles (RVs) on one property would not be grandfathered in because the RVs 
are moveable and not an allowed use. Planner Milliner stated RVs are not a permanent 
structure or permanent use. Attorney Strachan stated that she will look into whether 
having a septic system and water connection would change the use. Nate Brooks, the 
Summit County Environmental Health Director stated that tiny houses come standard with 
incinerators, which is not allowed, but they are still used. Typically, anything with wheels is 
not a regulated use in the County. Planner Milliner stated that square footage of a home is 
not regulated, but anything mounted on a trailer does not meet the requirements of the 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
July 16, 2020

prohibited to live in a travel trailer on a lot. This original draft is more restrictive knowing
