      A.    1150 Park Avenue - Plat Amendment - The Applicant Proposes to Remove 
            the  Internal  Lot  Line  Common  to  Lots  12  and  13  and  the  Block  Line 
            Common to the Additional Parcel to Create a Single Lot of Record. PL-22-
            05472. 
 
City Planner, Virgil Lund reported that 1150 Park Avenue is a Landmark Historic Structure on 
the  Historic  Sites  Inventory.  It  was  built  around  1895  and  in  1947 the Historic  Structure  was 
moved from 663 Woodside to its current location. The Landmark Historic Structure sits on the 
Lot line common to Lots 12 and 13, and the Accessory Structure in the rear sits on the block 
line.   The  structure  is  located  in  the  Historic  Residential – Medium  (“HRM”)  Zoning  District.  
Planner Lund stated that the proposed Plat Amendment complies with the HRM Zoning District 
requirements.   Condition  of  Approval  7  states  that  the  applicant  must  enter  into  an 
Encroachment Agreement  with  the  City  to  correct  the  portion  of  the Accessory  Building  that 
encroaches .2 feet into City-owned property.  The Encroachment Agreement must be recorded 
with Summit County prior to recordation of the Plat.  He noted that Conditions of Approval 5 and 
6  provide that  any  development  on  the  Lot,  including  additions  to  existing  structures,  shall 
comply  with  the  Land  Management  Code  (“LMC”)  in  effect  at  the  time  an  application  is 
submitted.  Additionally,  any  development  could  not  increase  the  non-conformity  of  the 
Accessory Building. 
 
Staff  recommended  Conditions  of  Approval  to  comply with Accessory  Apartments,  Nightly 
Rentals, and  parking  requirements.   He  referenced  Condition  of  Approval  9 which prohibits 
Nightly Rentals in the Accessory Apartment and would only be allowed in the Landmark Historic 
Structure.   In  addition,  a  deed  restriction  shall  be  recorded  for  the  Accessory  Apartment  that 
requires the long-term rental of at least 90 days.  03.08.2023 
 
Planner Lund explained that Condition of Approval 10 states that the property owner shall retain 
one Code-compliant parking space per Accessory Apartment bedroom on the site for as long as 
the Accessory Apartment is retained on the property.  He reported that Staff found good cause 
for  removing  the  Lot  line  common  to  Lots  12  and  13,  and  the  block  line  because  the  HRM 
zoning character would be retained.  He noted that this proposal was consistent with the Zoning 
District because it would create one Lot, and would allow the property owner to preserve the 
Landmark  Historic  Structure  while  allowing  for  opportunities  for  an  addition  or  Accessory 
Building compliant with Code.  In addition, no public street or Right-of-Way would be vacated or 
amended, and no easement would be vacated or amended.  
 
Planner  Lund  advised  that  the  Development  Review  Committee  met  on  January  3,  2023, 
reviewed the proposal, and did not identify any issues.  The Planning Department, Engineering 
Department, and  City  Attorney’s  office  also  reviewed  this  application.  On  January  3,  2023, 
public  notice  was  posted  on  the  City’s  website,  the  Utah  Public  Notice  website, and the 
property.   Additionally,  courtesy  notice was  given to  neighboring  properties,  and notice  was 
publishedApproved in The Park Record on that date.  No  public input had been received at the time. 
 
Staff  recommended  the  Planning  Commission  review  the  proposed  1150  Park  Avenue  Plat 
Amendment, hold a public hearing, and consider forwarding a positive recommendation for City 
Council’s consideration on March 9, 2023, based on the Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law, 
and Conditions of Approval in the Draft Ordinance.

      B.    Land  Management  Code   Amendment    - Amendment  to  the  Land 
            Management Code Section 15-2.13.2 to Prohibit Fractional Use and Private 
            Residence Clubs in Bellevue Phase 1 1st Amendment and Bellevue Phase 2 
            (Bellevue Court HOA). PL-23-0550903.08.2023 
 
City Planner, Spencer Cawley reported that Bellevue Court Homeowners’ Association (“HOA”) 
representatives, Bob  Sertner  and  Ben  Shapiro, were  present  online  to  answer  questions.  
Planner Cawley stated that  the  LMC  amendments  were  to  Section  15-2.13-2,  which governs 
uses in the Residential Development (“RD”) Zoning District.  He explained that this application 
was specific to the Bellevue at Deer Valley HOA, which requested a prohibition on Fractional 
Use  and  Private  Residence  Clubs  in  the  Bellevue  Subdivision.  The  Bellevue  Subdivision 
consists of  two  subdivisions,  Phase  1 First Amendment, and  Phase  2,  as  identified  on  the 
Summit  County  Assessor’s  Map.   The  subdivision  is  located  in  the  Upper  Deer  Valley 
neighborhood and consists of 14 properties.  He reported that all owners support the proposed 
amendment, which is a change from what was reported in the Staff Report.   
 
Planner Cawley stated that the Upper Deer Valley neighborhood encompasses both the Estate 
and Recreation Open Space (“ROS”) Zones, including the RD Zone.  This neighborhood does 
not  abut  any  other  zone.  Both  of  the  proposed  prohibitions are currently  conditional  uses.  
BecauseApproved the pending Ordinance issued by the City Council asked Staff to review transient uses, 
both  Fractional  Use  and  Private  Residence  Clubs  were  prohibited  in  the  RD  Zone.  The 
proposed LMC amendments for Fractional Use and Private Residence Clubs comply with the 
General Plan and the Park City Land Management Code.  The sense of community, which is a 
core  value  in  the  General  Plan,  helps  preserve  areas  for  primary  residents.   Additionally,  he 
mentioned the mitigation of adverse impacts related to these transient uses and other uses not 
specific to primary residential uses.

Planner  Cawley presented  the Water  Wise  Landscaping  updates made to  the  Land 
Management Code.  He reported that in 2019, the City Council identified opportunities for the 
City to address Water Wise Landscaping as a means to mitigate changes within the climate, as 
well as ways to conserve water.  As part of that, the Water Department worked with the City 
Council to develop incentive programs that would allow residents of Park City to remove turf in 
exchange  for  cash.   He  reported  that  Staff  worked  with  the  Water  and  Sustainability 
Departments to look at how they could holistically include the requirements so the requirements 
could be met for the incentives. 
 
Planner Cawley reported that House Bill (“H.B.”)03.08.2023 282, enacted in the last Legislative Session, 
addressed specific changes to definitions for Water Wise Landscaping, which Staff included in 
previous versions of this Ordinance.  They also met with industry experts, landscape architects, 
architects, and applicants who would use this Ordinance, and they expressed excitement over 
changes to the LMC that they could actually use.  They used information obtained from a public 
survey to help direct how they could move forward with the amendments.  He stressed that this 
is not the endpoint of amendments to the LMC for landscaping but is a step in the right direction.  
 
Planner  Cawley  stated that the  objective is to  identify  opportunities  to  improve  water 
conservation in Park City.  He reiterated that the Water Department is working on incentives for 
turf removal, so they need to include State regulations that will allow them to qualify for State 
funds  that  ultimately  would  be  passed  down  to  the  residents.  He  presented  the  text  of  the 
current  Landscaping  Code  and  the Water  Wise  Landscaping  regulations but stated that the 
requirements set forth in the current language were difficult to understand.  
 
Since  the  January  2023 meeting,  Staff  reviewed  recommendations  from  the  Planning 
Commission and included in the Staff Report the originally proposed amendments in red, and 
updatesApproved  as  suggested  by  the  Planning  Commission   in  blue.  He  called  out  Municipal  Code 
Section 14-1-5 which includes a plant species list developed as a means for direction on what 
would be appropriate to plant in City Rights-of-Way.  Planner Cawley stated they had directed 
residents to that list as a means to help establish some sort of direction on Water Wise Species.  
They realized that this list was for Fire Wise Plants; and, although there is some crossover, they 
heard at the last meeting that although some of these plant species are water-wise, they were 
not great options for the climate or location.
