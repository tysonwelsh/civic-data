3. Preliminary Subdivision Approval, The Commons at Ogden 3rd Amendment, approximately 12th & Wall
   Avenue. (Attachment 3)

  As the applicant was not present, Ms. Patrick explained the property is on the southwest corner of 12 th
  Street and Wall Avenue. She stated a previous amendment had created Lots 5 and 6 but left an
  unlabeled lot which does not meet Ordinance requirements. This proposal is to divide Lot 6, modify Lot
  5 and create one new lot as Lot 14 which will allow for separate ownership of development pads. Other
  existing lots are re-labeled and correct the previous amendment, with new lot lines for Lots 15 and 16.
  She stated as Lot 16 does not have frontage on a dedicated street, it is not legal, and the unusual shape
  of Lot 15 does not meet the Subdivision Code. She suggested Lot 15 be combined with Lot 16 to create
  frontage on a dedicated street, and that the northern piece of Lot 15 be combined with Lot 14, and
  recommended approval subject to those revisions.

  Commissioner Safsten asked if Staff is aware of the purpose of the odd-shape of Lot 15. Commissioner
  Sandau asked about the location of the new property line for Lot 14. Chair Graf asked of there is
  currently a building on Lot 14. Ms. Patrick stated the only purpose she is aware of is so each pad can
  have separate ownership. She stated the combination of the north portion of 15 to Lot 14 would create
  a lot with right angles and meet the Code, while the combination of the south portion of Lot 15 to Lot 16
  would create frontage on a dedicated street in order to meet the Code.

 MOTION:       A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to recommend approval of the proposed
               amendment based on the findings the subdivision is in the best interest of the public and
               in harmony with good development of the area and will comply with City Ordinances by
               combining the lots as proposed and meeting all departmental staff and private utility
               conditions of approval. Approval is subject to the following conditions:
               1. Obtaining and satisfying all staff and private utility corrections and requirements;
               2. Combining lots 15 and 16 to provide needed frontage; and
               3. Modifying the unusual shape of Lot 15 by combining the norther piece with Lot 14.
               Motion was seconded by Commissioner Safsten and passed unanimously with
               Commissioners Blaisdell, Safsten, Sandau, Schade, Southwick, Stoker and Graf voting aye.

4. Preliminary Subdivision Approval, for Sycamore Cove Subdivision, 2335 Quincy Avenue. (Attachment 4)

  Mr. Jeremy Smith stated Neighborhood Development has been in the process of acquiring property in
  this area for some time as it is one of the target neighborhoods for redevelopment. The City has
  renovated several homes in the area which have been sold as owner-occupied single-family homes.
  This proposal is to create eight new lots with access from a new cul-de-sac on the west side of Quincy
  Avenue in order to develop new single-family homes.

  Commissioner Schade asked about the area shown as cross-hatched. Mr. Smith stated it had been an
  original concept of the engineer in providing storm water detention, but since that time they have
  determined to use other methods in order to make each lot more desirable. Commissioner Blaisdell
  asked about the existing home on Lot 2. Mr. Smith answered it is to be renovated. Commissioner
  Safsten asked if there is enough information provided relating to the building styles and materials. Mr.
  Smith stated these will comply with the standards of the East Central neighborhood and styles will likely
  be bungalow or arts and crafts. Chair Graf noted he had understood the homes will be designed to
  accommodate the option of an accessory dwelling unit and asked if they were to be single-family
  homes or duplexes. Mr. Smith answered the homes will be single-family, but may be designed to
  accommodate an option for an accessory unit, indicating it is hoped the project can become a model
  for the development of accessory units as well as water-wise landscaping for the surrounding
  neighborhood. Commissioner Stoker asked about the existing trees on the site. Mr. Smith answered
  care will be taken to preserve majestic trees if possible, and he will work with the urban forester to
  determine the health and stability of the existing trees. He also clarified the lot sizes are insufficient for
  use as a duplex and any home with an accessory dwelling unit is required to be owner-occupied.

  Mr. Rodriguez stated the consideration of the Commission is to determine whether the proposed lots
  meet the Ordinance lot area and width requirements and whether utilities can be provided. He stated
  the request is for the approval of an eight-lot subdivision and dedication of a public right-of-way from
  Quincy Avenue. The existing property consists of five parcels containing 1.9 acres in the R-3EC zone.
  Each of the lots proposed meet the standards of the R-3EC zone for a single family home, and some
  have adequate lot area for two-family or three-family. He stated the applicant has been requested to
  provide additional information relating to stormwater. There are no undevelopable or unserviceable lots
  being created and the proposed plat complies with the Subdivision and Zoning Codes. Staff
  recommendation is for approval subject to City departmental staff recommendations being satisfied.

  MOTION:       A motion was made by Commissioner Stoker to recommend preliminary approval based
                on the findings it complies with City ordinances subject to satisfying all departmental and
                private utility requirements. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed
                unanimously with Commissioners Blaisdell, Safsten, Sandau, Schade, Southwick, Stoker
                and Graf voting aye.

5. Final Group Dwelling Approval, for 1100 Townhomes at approximately 1150 Childs Avenue.
   (Attachment 5)

  As the applicant was not present, Mr. Simpson explained the request is for final group dwelling approval
  for a mix of row housing and apartments consisting of 19 units. Preliminary approval had been granted
  last October, and this review is to determine whether the conditions identified at that time have been or
  can be satisfied. Addressing these conditions, he indicated fencing along Childs Avenue is to be limited
  to 4’ in height but the entryways facing the street should not be enclosed. He stated some of the
  architectural features proposed earlier have been abandoned such as an archway and wing walls, which


                                                                                                 Page 3 of 9
have now been replaced with other options to satisfy the group dwelling code by adding wood siding to
the façade and providing individual patio areas for the apartment tenants with extended the balconies
on the upper levels. Covered porches are provided for the row housing, with light fixtures and
decorative lintels around windows and doors to add variety. Fencing detail has been provided, limited to
4’ in the front along Childs Avenue and the rear and sides to be enclosed with a 6’ vinyl fence. Due to
topography of the site, a terraced retaining wall will provide some of the fencing enclosure. Additional
information is needed to assure the 6’ fence does not encroach into the setback areas. Walkways are
provided to allow access from tenants to amenities and to the parking area. There are some
corrections to be made as the architect plan and engineering plan do not match, including the fencing
and drive widths between the buildings meeting City regulations. He stated while there are amenities
identified, detail is not provided to assure the group dwelling standard are satisfied. Both a dumpster
enclosure and bicycle racks are to be provided, but there is no entry sign or street trees proposed. He
suggested the applicant could work with Staff to satisfy these standards and recommended approval
subject to the following conditions:
  1. All fencing within the 20’ front yard setback along Childs Avenue be limited to 4’ tall.
  2. The engineer site plan reflect that the driveway between the buildings will be at least 28’ wide.
  3. The engineer site plan show that the main entrances of the row-housing along Childs Avenue will
       not be enclosed with fencing.
  4. The applicant work with staff to finalize and approve the details of the project entry sign, and the
       outdoor seating, table, and playground equipment.
  5. All department staff comments being satisfied.

Chair Graf expressed concern with the accessibility of the dumpster from the sidewalk, suggesting it
might be set back so there is more walking room. Mr. Simpson responded while it would provide a
better service to the dumpster for residents, it also would remove green space from the project. He felt
the visibility of vehicular traffic is adequate for the tenant use of the dumpster, but that the Commission
could determine whether its relocation is desirable. Commissioner Stoker noted the code indicates
amenities should be centrally located and felt the proposed location is more towards the edge. Mr.
Simpson stated the intent is that all residents can easily access amenities, and due to the smaller size
of this project, he feels the intent is satisfied. While amenities could be moved, they are not allowed in
the front setback areas, and the alternative location could affect the design of the terraced retaining
wall.

Ms. Karen Coburn, 4311 Spring Drive expressed concern with multi-family apartments, stating more
than 75% of Utah residents are women and children and this type of people warehousing is not
desirable. She also felt the architecture proposed is not desirable and should be more community
oriented suggesting a courtyard design. She stated 12th Street is a corridor to Ogden Canyon and is
becoming compromised by additional traffic and high-density housing and that every vacant property
need not be developed into multi-family housing. She also had understood the project would consist of
1100 units while it is proposed at 19. Mr. Simpson explained the 1100 refers to the property address
and is part of the title chosen for the project by the developer. He also indicated a courtyard design had
been considered in the early design but due to the limited lot area and required setbacks, it was not
practical.

6. Preliminary Subdivision Approval, for Continuity Subdivision at approximately 4274 Spring Drive.
   (Attachment 6)

  Mr. Stratford explained while the Planning Commission is to review subdivisions, they are bound to the
  regulations of the City codes, as established by the City Council and it is not within their scope to vary
  from these codes.

  Mr. Tom Moore, 2342 Washington Boulevard, stated this request is to amend Spring Drive subdivision
  which has previously been recorded, to include an adjacent parcel to the east which was originally a part
  of Beus Hills Subdivision but had illegally been created and recorded as a separate parcel. As it is
  currently in the same ownership as Lot 1 of Spring Drive Subdivision, this amendment is simply to add
  that sliver of property to the existing Spring Drive Subdivision. No new lots are proposed to be created,
  but the lot area for Lot #1 would be increased. While Lot 1 is already a buildable lot and has necessary
  utilities, the proposal would eliminate the existing illegal parcel to the east.

  Mr. Rodriguez stated while this is listed as a preliminary approval, it is actually a subdivision
  amendment to include the illegal small parcel which had been recorded from Beus Hills Subdivision to
  become part of Lot 1 of the Spring Valley Subdivision. He stated while the application was for a
  subdivision amendment, the proposed plat had identified a new name which led Staff to believe it was a
  new subdivision. The consideration of the Commission is to determine whether the proposed lots
  meet the regulations of the Subdivision and Zoning ordinances, whether services can be provided and
  whether hazardous areas have sufficiently been identified and mitigated. The request is to combine an
  existing illegal parcel to become part of Lot 1 of the Spring Drive Subdivision. He reviewed the history of
  this subdivision indicating a concept plan had first been presented in 1986 and the revised in 1988. The
  original approval identified portions of lots which were undevelopable as being 150’ from the Spring
  Drive right-of-way. In 1988 additional geotechnical information eliminates this restriction from Lots 1
  and 2 due to the amount of flat surface available. The recorded plat now identifies areas 150’ east of
  Spring Drive for Lots 3 and 4 as unbuildable, but there are no restrictions on Lots 1 and 2. The proposed
  amendment will simply correct the illegal creation of a recorded parcel by adding it to Lot 1. Each of the
  lots meet the requirements of the Sensitive Area and R-1-10 regulations in terms of lot area. Additional
  information has been requested by both Engineering and Building Services, to increase the utility
  easement along Spring Drive and to provide a site-specific geotechnical study to address any potential
  hazards. The City Attorney also has noted property taxes are delinquent and will need to be up-to-date
  before the amendment can be recorded. Staff recommendation is for approval, subject to City
  departmental requirements being satisfied. He clarified the remnant parcel and Lot 1 are currently
  under the same ownership.

  Ms. Kelly Lowrey stated she lives in Lot 80 of the Beus Hills Subdivision, and the remnant parcel was
  between her lot and Lot #1. She expressed concern about a retaining wall which has been built on the
  property which does not appear to be safe or properly engineered and expressed concern with ongoing

                                                                                               Page 5 of 9
grading on the property. She stated there is a high potential of rockfall in this area and was concerned
about damages caused by boulders coming off the mountainside. She felt the integrity of the existing
retaining wall is insufficient to contain any rockfall. She asked the item be tabled and allow neighboring
residents to do more research relating to the area.

Mr. Charles Iverson, 4260 Spring Drive stated at the time the subdivision was approved, there was a no
build line established 150’ east of the Spring Drive right-of-way, stating construction has occurred in
that area. While he understands this was later amended to exclude Lots 1 and 2, he felt all four lots are
identical and should contain the same restriction.

Ms. Karen Colburn, 4311 Spring Drive expressed confusion as the Staff Report had included a plat for
the nine-lot Sycamore Cove Subdivision, and neighbors had understood the property was to be re-
subdivided.

Mr. Elliott Lewis, 1817 Beus Drive, felt all four lots on the east side of Spring Drive should be considered
together. He stated the existing home had been illegally built within the no-build area, and expressed
concern that emergency vehicles would have difficulty providing services to this home as it is serviced
by a shared service road between Lots 2 and 3, fearing he will create another similar access for Lot 1.
He also expressed concern the site work would affect the springs which feed into Beus Pond. He also
indicated Weber County had adopted hillside development review standards in 2009 and felt these lots
should be subject to their review. He felt the 150’ no-build line should extend onto each of the four lots.

Mr. Tom Moore clarified the proposed amendment is not to create any new lots, and affects only
existing Lot #1, adding the illegal parcel to this lot, creating one larger lot to accommodate one single-
family home. He indicated the retaining wall is a pile of boulders and is not an effort to retain the
hillside. He stated recent digging has been done in an effort to provide soil analysis for an updated
geotechnical report. He stated the current resident of the home is not the owner, but owns a tractor
which is used to clear snow from his driveway. He stated the shared driveway is shown on the
subdivision plat and was legally created.

Commissioner Sandau asked if consideration had been given to deed the illegal parcel back to its
original owner as it was originally part of the Beus Hills Subdivision. Mr. Moore responded there has
been signage listing the property for sale. He clarified Lot 1 is legal as it now exists and a home could
be built without the amendment, but this may be the last opportunity to include the illegal parcel to
make it part of a subdivision and make it legal. He stated one property owner had owned all four lots
but had financial difficulties, and the lots are now owned by his creditors.

Commissioner Sandau asked if the action also would include amending the plat for Beus Hills
Subdivision. Chair Graf stated when the parcel was created, it came to be owned by the owner of Lot 1
and has since been identified as part of the Spring Drive Subdivision due to its ownership.

Mr. Rodriguez stated the access road was recorded and appears to have been done legally. He stated
he has yet not been successful in finding original building permits for the home, but it was constructed
on Lot 3 east of the no-build line. He clarified Lot 1 is buildable as it currently exists according to the
regulations of the R-1-10/SA regulations. Mr. Stratford indicated the Weber County Hillside standards
apply only to unincorporated areas of the County, and Ogden City is governed by its own Sensitive Area
regulations.

Commissioner Sandau asked why the remnant parcel would not go back to its original status as part of
Lot 80 rather than to be added to Lot 1 of Spring Drive Subdivision. Mr. Montgomery stated it appears
the original owner of Lot 80 sold this back piece of his property to the owner of Lot 1 and recorded the

                                                                                              Page 6 of 9
  illegal lot and due to this purchase, it is now associated more with Lot 1. When the City became aware
  of the action, a Certificate of Noncompliance was placed on the parcel identifying it as an illegal lot. As
  it is currently under the same ownership as Lot 1, the application is to combine it into that lot. The City
  has no authority to require it to be sold back to the owners of Lot 80, and this action is to determine

7. Public Hearing, Petition to Rezone from M-2 to C-3 property at 149 West 3175 South. (Attachment 7)

   Mr. Tyler Nielsen from Gardner Engineering represented Tom Baur. He indicated the owner desires to
   sell a portion of his property so that it can be developed, indicating zoning lines appear to cut through
   existing parcels. It is desired that the front piece be sold to the adjacent landowner in order to expand
   the existing auto dealership.

   Mr. Simpson stated the property in the petition is currently vacant, and split between zoning of
   commercial and industrial, with the boundary line appearing to have been created following the old


                                                                                                 Page 7 of 9
   railroad right-of-way. This request is to rezone the front piece from M-2 to CP-3 to allow auto sales
   along the Wall Avenue frontage which is not allowed in the existing M-2 zone. The new boundary line
   between the commercial and industrial zone will be near where Stephens Avenue would connect to the
   property. As the property currently is in one ownership, the request would still be in violation of the
   policy, and suggested a condition of approval be added that the property be subdivided at the proposed
   zone boundary line. He also discussed the CP/C-3 zoning distinctions, stating the uses are the same in
   both zones. He reviewed the Jefferson Community Plan as well as the Wall Avenue Corridor Plan and
   indicated the use proposed is consistent with each of those. Staff recommendation is for approval
   subject to the lots being subdivided at the zone boundary line.

   Commissioner Safsten asked about the existing 32nd Street right-of-way. Mr. Simpson stated it is
   recorded but not developed as a typical right-of-way and remains as a small, rarely used street.

   As there were no comments from the public, a motion was made by Commissioner Schade to close the
   public hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Sandau and passed unanimously.

   MOTION:       A motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to recommend approval of the petition to
                 rezone as described based on the findings it will provide a definite edge to development
                 and buffering between types of uses, will reflect the prevailing character of the overall
                 district, will not cut across lots or developments once the subdivision recommended by
                 Staff occurs, and is supported by both the Jefferson Community and Wall Avenue Corridor
                 plans. Approval is for the northeast portion of the property to be rezoned from M-2 to C-3
                 to allow future subdivision and development of a commercial vehicle sale lot, and the
                 southern portion be rezoned from CP-3 to M-2 to connect with existing M-2 zoning to the
                 west and accommodate future industrial use, subject to the following conditions:
                 1. The petitioner obtain approval and complete a subdivision plat process to split the
                      property so the zone boundary will follow the property line; and
                 2. The portion of the property to remain commercial be rezoned to C-3 so there is not a
                      split between the CP-3 and C-3 zones within the same property.
                 Motion was seconded by Commissioner Stoker and passed unanimously with
                 Commissioners Blaisdell, Safsten, Sandau, Schade, Southwick, Stoker and Graf voting aye.

Review of Meeting
Mr. Montgomery noted while there were some mistakes, some confusion was based on misinterpretation
rather than misinformation. He agreed with the comment there are beginning to be cookie-cutter
apartments with little architectural detail, but there are no regulations in many areas. He stated the
subdivision amendment is interesting in that there is a recorded subdivision with existing lots that can be
sold as they currently exist, with a note on the plat that any construction requires detailed information due
to the slope of the area. It was approved under the Sensitive Area regulations, and geotechnical reports
were completed prior to the subdivision being approved and standards established. Because this was

                                                                                                      Page 8 of 9
more than 25 years ago, the Building Official is asking for more geotechnical information in order to
address any changes which may have occurred. While the existing lot could be sold as it currently exists,
there is danger that the remnant parcel be under a separate ownership as it is illegal. He stated the
comment by Legal Staff at the onset of the agenda item was an effort to caution the Commission and
educate the public that the action of the Commission is bound by ordinances which have been adopted by
the City Council and should not be concerned about past action by the developer or property owner. While
residents may be somewhat pacified with the additional time period, there is little gained in the
Commission request for more information as these are to be satisfied according to both the recorded plat
and the Sensitive Area regulations. Mr. Stratford felt the action to table the item allows more details to be
identified on the plat. Discussion continued relating to the lack of building permit records, with the
Commission being concerned there was a disregard for the City’s regulations. Mr. Montgomery stated he
was hoping to find a site plan for the home, and not necessarily whether or not there was a building permit,
but there is a void of building permit records during that time period and he is aware that the home had
received inspections by Ogden City which would have been tied to a permit.

Mr. Stratford announced he has accepted a position with Jordan River Conservancy District and will be
leaving Ogden City at the end of January and thanked the Commission for the work they do. Commission
indicated he has been instrumental in educating them relating to their roles on the Commission.

Mr. Montgomery stated there will be an agenda item for the as a special meeting on January 19th to be
followed by a work session.

As there was no additional business before the Commission, the meeting was adjourned at 7:47pm.
