Council Member Jackson started the meeting by welcoming the public.

1. Introduction by Council Member Cheri Jackson and Millcreek Department Heads

The Millcreek Department Heads briefly introduced themselves. John Miller said he is over the
171 miles of road in the City. He said anything road, sidewalk, and storm drain related falls
under his purview as the Public Works Director. Rita Lund, Communications and Programs
Director, said she works with the community councils, Venture Out, special event permits, and is
the catch-all department.

Francis Lilly said he is the Community Development Director and planning and zoning is his
main emphasis. He works with 3 planners and they coordinate certain approvals that go through
the Planning Commission and City Council. He said the future land use map for the general plan
was on a table at the meeting for the public to view. The Promise Program is predicated on 3
promises; health and safety, education, and economic wellbeing. The goal of Promise is to get all

Mayor Jeff Silvestrini said on behalf of Millcreek, we express condolences to Jill Robinson’s
family and West Valley. She was a West Valley code enforcement officer who was murdered
today. He said Millcreek has officers out doing the same kinds of things she did.

Mayor Silvestrini said he is the ceremonial head of the City, is the Chair of the City Council and
votes on the Council as well. He represents the City at the legislature and sits on various boards;
Chair of Unified Police Department Board, on the Unified Fire Authority Board, the Chair of the
Fire Service Area, Commissioner on the Wasatch Front Regional Council, Vice Chair on County
Council of Governments, on the Central Wasatch Commission. He makes sure the City gets

residents can contact him with any concern or complaint. At Millcreek, we “Play to Win.”

Kurt Hansen, Director of City Services, said he manages the building department, code
enforcement, emergency management, fleet and facilities. Mike Winder, Commerce and
Economic Development Director, said he focuses on the 3 R’s: retaining great businesses in
Millcreek, recruiting new businesses to come to Millcreek, and redevelopment of tired areas to
make better places. Elyse Greiner, City Recorder, said she takes minutes for the City Council and
oversees the City’s records. John Geilmann, City Manager, said he takes the vision the Council
puts together, and he acquires resources to make sure that vision can be accomplished. He said
there is a great Mayor and Council in Millcreek and it is a pleasure to work with them.

Council Member Jackson recognized Bev Uipi from Council District 4.

2. Open Question and Answer Period

Ralph Morelli said he was concerned about the commercial nodes in the general plan draft. The
first map in the general plan seems like a contradiction. He said he is in Neighborhood 2, but
would feel better if he was in Neighborhood 1. He said the commercial nodes said something
about revenue generating and Neighborhood 1 does not talk about commercial development. He
asked how he resolves this and how is the criteria used to include a particular neighborhood in
the sphere of the commercial node. He wanted to know how the radius of that node was
determined.

Francis Lilly said the City’s goal is to promote the creation of town centers while protecting the
integrity of surrounding neighborhoods. There is not a center on the plan that changes the
characteristic of an existing single-family neighborhood zoned R-1 or R-2. One exception is in
the area in Olympus Cove between Wasatch Blvd. and I-215 because it is an area the City does
not want single-family homes. The commercial nodes are intended to call out the intersection of
where existing commercial is. It does not mean that neighborhoods will turn into commercial.
The future land use map is what really matters for sake of policy.

Mayor Silvestrini said the City is seeking comments from the public on the future land use map.
Neighborhood 1 is principally single-family dwelling; Neighborhood 2 reflects more duplexes.
The map largely reflects what is on the ground now and the input received from the public. The
City has data that has been gathered from over 20 meetings with the public. He said to the
audience that it’s important to let staff know if you have a problem with the map. Lilly said the

goal of the centers is not to take out an existing a single-family neighborhood, it identifies
locations and focuses on tired commercial, misplaced light industrial or underutilized properties.
The master plan goals include creating the right type of center to help neighborhoods thrive and
prosper.

A resident asked in terms of “the wedge,” has the City thought about making sure there is space
for a farmer’s market. Lilly said yes. Winder said, “the wedge” is starting to be called,
“Millcreek Center.” Winder said there are proposed developments that include a pocket park
where a farmer’s market could be held. The future of the center is to have a walkable street and
plazas. Lilly said the City put out an RFP for a town center consultant. Mayor Silvestrini said the
City got a grant to pay for that. Lilly said the selected consultants have worked on local projects
that include walkable streets and plazas.

A resident asked what Neighborhood 3 consists of. Lilly said there are Neighborhoods 1, 2, 3
and Mixed Use 1, 2, 3; the higher the number the higher the density and intensity gets. The
difference in mixed use is building height. Mayor Silvestrini said the idea is to transition height.
He said there is a housing shortage and the City wants to protect single-family neighborhoods
and keep taxes low by building up a tax base by putting the growth in town centers.

A resident asked about acquiring Brickyard. Mayor Silvestrini said he talked to Salt Lake City
Council about a boundary adjustment. Millcreek has asked for it and have not heard back. He
said people have to drive on a Millcreek street to get to it. Winder said there are three ways the
City can get it back; 1) mutually agreed upon boundary adjustment, 2) property owners petition
to disconnect, and 3) legislative action through the State legislature.

Ralph Morelli asked about the plan for the property where city hall now sits and where the
permanent location for city hall will be. He suggested putting in green space where it is now.
Mayor Silvestrini said the property that city hall sits on is owned by Woodbury Corp. and the
City is leasing it for 5 years with an option to renew for another 5 years. He said Woodbury
Corp. may redevelop the area in that time span, and if they do they have to relocate city hall.
There is no plan right now for a future city hall. He agreed that the City should own its city hall
and would like to see the resident’s vision realized with green space. Mayor Silvestrini said there
is a possibility of Granite School District property opening up that the City could look at
purchasing. There are new plans for an elementary school in South Salt Lake to be built and they
may close Roosevelt Elementary in Millcreek. Mayor Silvestrini said the immediate priority for
the City is to be careful about how money is spent. Lilly said his idea would be great input for
the city center master plan.

A resident said powerlines on 3300 S., close to Highland Dr., are always getting knocked down
and asked if there was a plan to get them out of the road. John Miller said 3300 S. is a state route.
The poles are in the right-of-way with an easement to be there. He said around 5 years ago it
would have cost about $5 million to move them and it does not offer any enhancement to the
road.

Miller said there is a service request link on the City’s website. He told resident if they see a
problem or have a suggestion, to fill out a request. His staff is 6 people and the City has over
60,000 people, so the residents see more than his staff. He said the link is on the home page and
under the public works page. There is also an app called iWorQ and residents can download it on

their cellphone to report issues. The app is helpful because it gives a geo-reference point for
requests.

A resident asked if there have been any major changes to the Parleys EIS. Mayor Silvestrini said
UDOT is studying the interchange around Parleys Canyon for improvements. There are four
proposed alternatives, but the proposals do not address the alternatives’ impact on Millcreek and
surrounding trailheads. He said the improvements could take up Wasatch Blvd., houses, and
yards. Mayor Silvestrini said today he received a petition with about 20 signatures expressing
concern and he will put the issue on UDOT’s radar. He will also work with a legislative team
and lobbyist to get a good alternative that works for Millcreek.

A scout asked about plans for a public swimming pool in the future. Council Member Jackson
said some residents have requested a splash pad at Canyon Rim Park and there is a study being
done to see if it’s feasible. She said the Holladay Lions Recreation Center has a pool and it is
located in Millcreek. Mayor Silvestrini said he would love to have an outdoor swimming pool
but the City cannot afford it right now.

Council Member Jackson said the City is starting a Millcreek Youth Council. It is for Millcreek
youth from 9-12" grade and if there is any interest, people should contact Rita Lund.

Council Member Jackson asked Kurt Hansen to talk about code enforcement. Hansen said there
are two code compliance officers. The idea of code enforcement is to make sure properties look
good. Residents can submit code compliance concerns on the website. There is a process of
warnings and fees to get the issues resolved.

Mayor Silvestrini took a moment to recognize Michelle Quist, who is a candidate for County
Council, and Alan Monsen, who is a candidate for Utah State Senate District 4, from the
audience.

Council Member Jackson asked John Miller to explain evaluation for sidewalk and street lights.
Miller said residents can fill out the service request form on the website. The requests are based
on data and the City ranks priorities. He said there are 3 secrets to a good road; drainage,
drainage, and drainage. He also said fixing curb and gutter helps road treatments last longer.

Council Member Uipi asked how the City considers the impact on neighbors with traffic
calming. Miller said the policy on traffic calming is that over 50% of the neighbors must buy in
to the solution. He said City ordinance says that the resident is responsible to fix and maintain
curb, gutter, and sidewalk in front of their property. Mayor Silvestrini said the City is putting in
sidewalk on Upland Dr. because that street serves 3 schools. The City will try to put in sidewalks
where safety warrants them. The City is getting grants for putting sidewalk on parts of 3300 S.
He said if residents want to replace their sidewalk, the City will participate 50/50 on cost for
installation. There is a sidewalk plan that the County helped Millcreek do, and that is roadmap
for where Millcreek residents said they want sidewalks first. Miller said the City Council went
through a budget activity and they put more money towards sidewalk improvements.

Council Member Jackson said residents could mingle with staff for the remainder of the meeting
and thanked the audience for attending.

DISTRICT 4 MEETING — 7:30 p.m.
TIME COMMENCED: 7:30 p.m.

Council Member Uipi started the meeting by thanking the audience for coming. She said she
hoped to hear what their concerns were and to educate them on the City’s processes. She
introduced Council Member Cheri Jackson.

1. Introduction by Council Member Bev Uipi and Millcreek Department Heads

The Department Heads introduced themselves in the same fashion as they did in the District 3
meeting. Mayor Silvestrini recognized Michelle Quist who is running for County Council. He
then offered condolences to Jill Robinson’s family, who was a code enforcement officer who
was murdered today in West Valley City. Mayor Silvestrini also shared information about what
he does, similar to that in the previous meeting.

Council Member Uipi commended the City Manager for hiring great leaders for the City.

2. Open Question and Answer Period
Council Member Uipi asked the audience for all their questions up front.

A resident asked what the City was doing about fireworks during July. He said he is a veteran
and has a wooden roof. He would like them banned in the City.

A resident asked about the intersection at 2300 E. and Claybourne Ave. He said there are close
calls with cars when people are trying to cross on the crosswalk.

A resident said he lives in the Woodland Ave. area around 1575 E. and a road improvement has
not been done there in 25 years; there is not any drainage and water collects at driveways.

A resident who lives on Keller Lane said there is an ongoing discussion with neighbors about
speeding vehicles. He said about every five minutes speeders go by. Another resident said high
schoolers call it the “Keller Challenge” to see how fast they can get on that stretch of road. A
third Keller resident said it is an ongoing problem. She recommended a stop sign at 2175 E. and
more police presence because she is worried a child will get hit.

A resident said what is good for one part of Millcreek is not good for another part of Millcreek.
She has lived in the Cove for 40 years and the focus should be where the need is for any project.

A resident said he has had interactions with various departments and offered compliments to
code enforcement, the building department, and the City Recorder. He asked if the Planning
Commission could present applications to the City Council when their opinion differs from the
planning staff because nuances get missed.

Council Member Uipi asked John Miller to answer the public works related questions that were
raised. Miller said on the City’s webpage there a service request link for residents to submit
concerns and comments. He said there are 3 E’s when it comes to road safety; enforcement,
education, and engineering. He said he makes decisions based on data. There is a process for
traffic control. The Federal Highway Administration said stop and yield signs cannot be used for
traffic calming. He can do a warrant study to see if traffic and speeds warrant a stop sign on 2175
E. If there is no warrant, there are liabilities the City would take on. He said Evergreen Ave. got
speed bumps two years ago. Over 50% of the neighbors signed a petition to get a study then the
project was ranked based on data. He said the secret to good roads is drainage, drainage, and
drainage. The City is trying to add curb and gutter where it can. The decision was made a long
time ago to not install them, so the City is trying to correct that. If there is flooding in homes or
in the neighborhoods, that priority goes to the top of the list.

A resident asked what the process would be to get the ball rolling on Keller Lane. He told the
residents to submit a comment in the Citizen Service Request. He will then do research on what
was supplied to the County; the residents may have to submit another petition. Mayor Silvestrini
said he gets complaints about speeding all over the City. He said some of the people who were
more vocal about speeding on Jupiter Dr. were the ones who got pulled over when more patrol
was added. He said the City does not make money on speeding tickets. He proposed to the
residents that he would get more enforcement on Keller Lane. He said a lot of residents ask for
traffic calming solutions in their neighborhoods, but they are driven by data.

Council Member Uipi asked Mike Winder to update the audience on the closed Dan’s. Winder
said Canyon Rim Commons is a proposed town center. The old Dan’s has had a $2 million face
lift. Joanne’s from 3300 S. will move there and DownEast will be in the other half. There will
also be a drive-thru coffee kiosk. There is not anything planned for the vacant RiteAid yet.

Council Member Uipi thanked code enforcement for their work. She recognized Joan Haven on
the Mt. Olympus Community Council and Linda Milne with emergency management. She then
continued the meeting by asking department heads to answer their department-related questions.

Lilly said the Planning Commission’s recommendation gets transmitted to the Council and if
nuances are missed, that is on him for not communicating it fully. Lilly said the City has
lengthened the time applications go from the Planning Commission to the Council. He is
working with the Planning Commission about making their motions clearer. The Chair and Vice
Chair of the Commission are now taking their own notes during meetings.

Mayor Silvestrini said there were far more complaints about fireworks last year than this year.
He said there were 16 fireworks-caused fires last year. He said this year they passed a bill
limiting lawful discharge days from 14 to 8 days and the penalty of unlawful discharge

increased. He wished they could do more but there are several obstacles at Capitol Hill lobbying
against harsher laws. He said there was only 1 firework-caused fire this season, and that was with
bottle rockets and children. He said the City will do what they can.

Mayor Silvestrini said the Planning Commission is welcome to send a representative to Council
meetings and there has only been one time where the Council did not approve a recommendation
from the Planning Commission. The one time involved a change of policy made by the
Commission and the Council felt that the application should be treated equally, and they thought

the Planning Commission had made a mistake. Mayor Silvestrini respects the work the Planning
Commission does. Council Member Uipi said with that application, she called a community
meeting for residents to discuss the project because it was the Council’s job to help inform the
residents.

Council Member Uipi asked the audience if they receive the City’s newsletters. She said the
newsletters are to inform the residents about what is going on in the City. She mentioned that the
City Council broadcasts their meetings through Facebook live. She said the City looks to the four
community councils for recommendations on issues facing the City. She said the community
councils will be going through elections soon and that the City gives community councils
funding.

She turned the time over to residents to mingle with city staff.

Mayor Silvestrini said the land use plan map on the table will be adopted as part of the general
plan. There is time for the public to comment on it by going to millcreekgeneralplan.com. The
future land use map will be the roadmap for the City, so staff and the Council want to make sure
they get it right. It reflects what is currently here and what Millcreek residents have said they
want from over the last 2 years of input.

Adjourned at 8:28 p.m.
