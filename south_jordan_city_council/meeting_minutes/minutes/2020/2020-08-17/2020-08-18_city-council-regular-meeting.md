         E. Mayor and Council Reports

Council Member Shelton reported on the Association of Municipal Council and Legislative
Policy Committee meetings. He attended both electronically. He noted that Police Chief Carr is
on the Love, Listen, and Lead task force making recommendations for policing
changes/modifications in the state. He thanked Strategic Services Director Tingey for his email
following up on questions he asked at the last meeting.

Council Member Zander said she met with the Mosquito Abatement District Board meeting via
zoom. She said Salt Lake County has had their first positive west nile virus cases. She said the
city will be doing a ribbon cutting for the pickle ball courts. She thanked the police department
for helping with a parking issue in her district.

Council Member McGuire said he met with the Arts Council. They approved some cross walk
murals in the Town Center area. There will be artists painting those during the farmers market.

Council Member Marlor said he met with the Architectural Review Committee on the 12th. He
noted the passing of his mother in law, at 100 years old.

Council Member Harris said the committees that he serves on have not met since the last City
Council meeting. He said he has been working on various issues with CM Whatcott.

Mayor Ramsey said at today’s Salt Lake County Council meeting, they approved an extension of
the current health order including the mask mandate. It is anticipated it will go through January
5, 2021. Mayor Ramsey said she and Police Chief Carr met with Representative Pierucci, and
representatives from Herriman and Riverton for a round table discussion on domestic violence.
They are trying to determine policies, potentially at the state level, that make it easier to address
domestic violence and help curb the consistently rising numbers. She said they have a special
Legislative session Thursday. There are several issues they are watching closely. She said she
had a phone meeting with Senator Romney. Mostly he discussed a potential 4th stimulus. They
are watching that close. They also have Senator McAdams visiting a small business in South
Jordan evaluating how the CARES funding is helping. She said the Covid numbers are coming
down, and that is a positive thing. She said she attended the EDCU staff summer outing. She has
been asked to co-chair the EDCU Board starting in September. She has visited with several
candidates. She said they need to learn about all of the candidates and vote. She said she serves
on the board of Resilient Utah to help families teach their kids resilience. She said there is an
initiative being started called Serving South Jordan to help with some service opportunities.
There will be collection bins available at City Hall and the public works building. It is a home
grown idea, and they want to give people that chance to serve.

         G. Presentation Item: Project Empathy (By John Hansen and Chase Hansen)

John and Chase Hansen were introduced.

Chase Hansen indicated that he and his dad have been working with the homeless community to
lift them up and make them feel human. He said they have focused on how to make them feel
heard. He talked about a man that they helped get reconnected with his sons. They helped that
individual for 2 years and got to see the evolution of him. That man recently moved into his own
house. He said Project Empathy is based around human interaction. He and his dad have given
130 meals. They are trying to impact problems that they see in Salt Lake City and to see the
homeless individuals as friends and people.

John Hansen said Project Empathy is their attempt to organize outreach and help them find
connection. He said their greatest success story was in South Jordan, because they gave someone
their time. He said empathy is listening at its best. They have interviewed addicts. Project
Empathy is a prescription for human connection, and deliberate intention. It is about how to take
a base of people and infect others by asking questions of what they need.

         H. Public Hearing: Zoning Ordinance 2020-10-Z, Rezone for Skye Drive 3-Lot
            Subdivision located at 9811 S. Dunsinane Drive. (By Planning Director, Steven
            Schaefermeyer)

Planning Director Schaefermeyer reviewed the background information on this item (Attachment
A). He said Dale Hull is the applicant. The proposal is a 3 lot subdivision, but that includes the
existing home. The lots will be close to ½ acre.

Mr. Hull said he has owned this property for 30 years. They desire to develop this to its highest
and best use. He said they went with staff’s recommendation.

Mayor Ramsey opened the public hearing. There were no comments. She closed the public
hearing.

Council Member Zander asked if there were objections from the surrounding property owners,
including the school and church. Mr. Schaefermeyer said not that he is aware. It was noted that
the masonry wall is required because the adjacent property owned by the church is zoned A-5
and has animal rights.

Council Member Zander asked if staff is concerned with traffic impacts in this area? Mr.
Schaefermeyer said the planning staff is not. Engineering Director Klavano said it would be a
minor change in traffic. He does not have a concern with traffic.

Council Member Zander made a motion to approve zoning Ordinance 2020-10-Z. Council
Member Marlor seconded the motion.

Council Member Harris noted that these are big lots being proposed.

         I. Staff Reports and Calendaring Items

CM Whatcott said staff would like to schedule a Strategic Planning meeting with the Council.
They determined to hold the meeting October 27, 2020 at 5 p.m.

CM Whatcott said they would have a budget update the first meeting in October. He said right
now, things are not bad in terms of the budget. He said they submitted their first CARES
reimbursement to Salt Lake County. They will probably do 1 more submission. It does not
appear that they will use all of the money provided to them. They have been careful to follow the
guidelines.

Public Works Director Rasmussen said they have been working on a snow event parking
Ordinance. He said Council Member Zander had asked if staff was willing to host a meeting to
get additional feedback from the residents. He said they will be putting something together; it
will be done electronically.

Council Member Zander said the city road that is the worst for a snow event is Oakmond. She
said she would like the residents on Oakmond invited to that meeting.

City Attorney Loose indicated that masks with the South Jordan logo are available for the City
Council.

City Attorney Loose said legislative interim is today and tomorrow. The special session is
Thursday. Staff will be watching and will report back to the City Council.
