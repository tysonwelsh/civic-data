Financial Report

bringing attention to the Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security (CARES) Act and the

Rezone Request

                 Mayor Peay reviewed the following request for a rezone:

Whitaker Construction requesting approval to rezone approximately 17.56 acres of property
located at 7581 West 2100 South from an A-20 (agricultural) zone to an M-2 (manufacturing)
zone.

               Paul Ashton stated the Legislature modified the Utah Code such that the Council
no longer needs to have a separate planning public hearing on a zoning issue after a planning
commission has held a public hearing. It can just be scheduled on an agenda as an action item.
That is to expedite the development process, and it will save time and money. This is the first
zoning item that has come to the Council since the amendment to the Code.

            Council Member Peel stated he would like to get a report from the Magna
Planning Commission on the public comment it received and its recommendation.

             Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to set
September 14, 2021, as the date to consider the rezone as an action item. The motion passed
unanimously.

Cyprus High School Update

                 Ben Horsley, Communications Community Outreach Director, Granite School
District, stated Granite High School representatives were here today to give the Council an update
on the Cyprus High School.

                 Donald Adams, Assistant Superintendent, Granite School District, delivered a
presentation on the rebuild of Cyprus High School. The school district went out to the voters with
a bond, and voters overwhelmingly supported it. The new Cyprus High School will be the finest
high school building in the state. The presentation included a site plan, on a 60-acre site, which
makes it the single largest high school site in the school district. The school district just finished
the bidding process, and now has a contractor on board who is going through the school district’s
internal contract review. Construction will begin in September. It will be just shy of a 4-year build.
The school district expected the contractor would turn the building over to it in May 2025, and
students will start school in August 2025. The capacity of the school will be approximately 3,000,
but it can flex a little larger if the demand is there.

                 Mr. Horsley stated the Council can find regular updates on its website at
gsdfuture.org.

               Council Member Hull complimented the Granite School District on the way it
presented the bond to voters and was able to get it passed. A lot of other districts did not succeed
with their bonds. She asked if the school district did that inhouse or hired a consultant.

               Mr. Adams stated it was done by the school district, along with donors who were
interested in improving buildings in the school district, since Granite has some of the oldest
buildings on average in the state. Many businesses, entities, and individuals were helpful and
instrumental in helping to get that passed.

               Mayor Peay asked what the Granite School District was going to do with the old
building.

                 Mr. Adams stated the school district does not yet know what it will do. There are
a lot of options to consider, and it needed time to mull things over. Once it gets direction from its
board, it will come back to the Council to let it know.
