 3.      Public Hearing and Recommendation to the City Council for a Zoning Map
         Amendment for the Property at 4768 S Redwood Road in Taylorsville, Utah
         from Community Commercial ( CC) to Boulevard Commercial ( BC); Applicant:
         Elias Canas ( 5Z24 - DCA- 000481- 2024 / Terryne Bergeson,     Planner)



 e Ms. Bergeson explained that the property located at 4768 South Redwood Road
was a   deep commercial   lot with   a   small
                                         building that had been used for small retail-- most
recently Incredible Toy. Applicant Elias Canas contacted the city to inquire about opening
a used car dealership at the location. The property was zoned Community Commercial
which prohibited that type of use. The applicant ( potential tenant) had applied for a zoning
map amendment with the permission of the property owner, and requested the property
zoning designation be changed from Community Commercial to Boulevard Commercial,
which would allow the operation of a used car dealership with the approval of a non-
administrative conditional use permit and business license.

The subject property was a single parcel fronting Redwood Road and containing
approximately 25, 700 square feet. An existing 1, 800 square foot building with an attached
rear garage fronted on Redwood Road, 30 feet from the property line. The back half of
the parcel was undeveloped and was an open lot screened by fencing. The front half of
the lot was paved and striped, used in the past as parking for the retail use. Required
improvements would be determined during a site plan review process. The application
did not seek any change to the General Plan designation. The application was not for
permission to open a used car lot. Instead, it was for a rezone. If the rezone were to
eventually be approved by the city council, the applicant could then apply to operate such
a  business.  Ms. Bergeson pointed out that forwarding a positive recommendation

regarding the zoning request would not approve the concept development plan. Instead,
a zoning change simply established the legal mechanism for the applicant to pursue
development approvals based on the underlying zoning.

Commissioner Quigley asked how soon the applicant could try again if the city council did
not approve the rezone once it got to them. Mr. Spung responded that it would be one
year.

 4.       Public Hearing and Recommendation       to the City Council for a Zoning Text
        Amendment       to   Chapter   13. 38     of    the    Municipal
                                                              Taylorsville Code,   Updating
        Regulations in the SSD- X Bennion Point District( Summit Vista), Related to Density,

        Landscaping, and the Table of Uses; Applicant: Andy Hubbard of Great Basin
        Engineering; ( File 4Z24 — DCA- 000479- 2024; Terryne Bergeson, Planner)

 IE     Ms. Bergeson explained that the Summit Vista retirement community project first
received    approvals    in 2015 through       the adoption      of a Development        Agreement     and site-

specific development standards, Chapter 13. 38 SSD- X Bennion Point Zoning District.
Construction on the project had continued since then, with the final building for phase one
almost complete. Permitting for phase two was now underway, with the property owner' s
agents receiving preliminary approval for a non- administrative conditional use permit on
August 13, 2024. The applicants were working through the building permit process for the
first neighborhood 2 building, RB2. 1. Construction was anticipated to begin at the end of
2024.


As the project had incrementally developed over the years, the developers had made
changes in response to market feedback and resident preferences such as providing
larger gathering spaces and redesigning some landscaping. One significant change had
been an increased demand for 2- bedroom units, resulting in the total unit count falling
below the minimum density.

Updated information for the overall project was provided with the non- administrative
conditional    use   permit    application.     Staff   review    determined      that   the   new   anticipated

density at full build- out would not be achieved. To comply with the minimum density listed
in the ordinance, a very significant increase in unit count would be needed in phase three.
After discussion     with    applicants   and     staff, it had      been    determined    that the minimum
density listed in the governing ordinance could be amended so the project would be on
track to comply with density requirements     at completion, while still maintaining the

commitment to create a quality, high- density senior living project. Amendments to the
SSD that were proposed were intended to allow an easier application process for
development of future phases, clarification and flexibility for the overall density and future
development, and minor updates to landscaping.

Currently, each time a change was proposed, a review was conducted by the planning
commission.    Staff wished to streamline the process so that the commission would
approve the master plan for each phase, but then allow sub- phases would be reviewed
and approved by staff.

Although      Commissioner      Wendel        understood      that   Summit     Vista    was   not   considered
affordable     housing," she wondered if there was any reason to believe that if the density
were to be increased, the costs could be lowered.

Mr. Spung responded that would be a reasonable thing to consider, although staff had
not spoken to the developer about that possibility. However, the buildings themselves
were quite expensive, so adding a floor would certainly increase the costs.

Chair Wilkey asked if the applicant was present, but he was not. She then opened the

Jackie Larsen asked if any public roadways would be blocked off once this phase was
complete.    Ms.     Bergeson responded in the negative. Then in response to an earlier

There was no one else who expressed a desire to speak either in person or online, so
Chair Wilkey closed the public hearing.

Commissioner         Wright    expressed    concern       about   the   recommended     administrative

changes     in the    review   process,    and said he could       not recommend      approval   of the
application because of those.


Mr. Spung wished to clarify that the draft would still require the planning commission to
approve each phase as a master plan, but instead of returning for each building within
those phases, staff would provide future approvals.

Commissioner Wendel was very adamant that the planning commission retain their
current approval authority. Since Summit Vista tended to add only one building per year,
she pointed out that it would not be overly burdensome for the commission. She also felt
the city should hold firm on the density requirement as it was what was agreed upon.

Commissioner Quigley felt that the proposal was in opposition to what Mark McGrath had
previously told the commission regarding the city' s needs. Ms. Bergeson responded that
the entire community development had reviewed the proposal, including Mr. McGrath.

Chair Wilkey commented that when the developer appeared the previous month, it was
made very clear that there were concerns about the density. So for them to not even
attend this meeting " kind of feels like we got blown off."

Commissioner         McElreath said he was leaning towards requiring that the developer
maintain the original agreement.


When the possibility of modifying the recommendation was raised, Commissioner Wright
