1. An Ordinance Regarding the Northeast Neighborhoods Plan Approval
(PLGPA20240278) 0:06:25

Jessica Dahneke, Planner, presented the Northeast Neighborhoods Plan, which encompasses the
Higher Bottoms, North Timpview, and Sherwood Hills neighborhoods. She detailed the
comprehensive planning process undertaken, including public input sessions and collaboration
with an advisory committee. The plan addresses eight primary objectives:

    1.   Understanding land use needs.
    2.   Preserving neighborhood character.
    3.   Identifying gateway opportunities.
    4.   Evaluating annexation areas.
    5.   Providing developer education.
    6.   Enhancing safety and transportation.
    7.   Creating an implementation plan.
    8.   Ensuring consistency with the city’s broader goals.

Ms. Dahneke highlighted proposed changes in the future land use map, particularly along
University Avenue, aimed at aligning with city goals while maintaining the neighborhood’s
character. Public feedback was incorporated, with residents expressing concerns about accessory
dwelling units (ADUs), design corridor requirements, annexation desires, trail access, and
housing density placement.

Councilor George Handley raised questions about the inclusion of small-scale infill
development, a point of contention in the Planning Commission’s split vote. Ms. Dahneke
acknowledged the concerns and suggested adjustments to the language to better support small-
scale infill while upholding neighborhood preservation goals.

Councilor Becky Bogdin addressed specific recommendations from a community member,
Sharon Memmott. These included clarifications on road classifications and the designation of
Canyon Road as an arterial road. Gordon Haight, Public Works Director, explained that road
classifications are determined based on functionality rather than the number of lanes, and he
recommended addressing transportation designations within the Transportation Master Plan
rather than individual area plans.

Members of the neighborhood committee provided additional context regarding the development
of the plan and the distinctive features of the northeast area. They emphasized the importance of
accommodating diverse housing types, supporting local businesses, and addressing challenges
such as critical hillsides and natural hazards.

Motions and Votes:

    1. Councilor Becky Bogdin moved to strike the sentence “As shown on the map.” from the
       plan document. Councilor Craig Christensen seconded the motion. No votes taken.
    2. Councilor George Handley moved to revise the motion’s language to include “LDR
       projects within the northeast area are planned in the areas as shown on map Figure 3.3.”
       Councilor Craig Christensen seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously 6-0
       Hoban Excused).

The Council expressed general support for the Northeast Neighborhoods Plan while
acknowledging the need for potential refinements in the future. The ordinance was advanced
with the recommended changes.

2. An Ordinance Regarding a Zone Map Amendment (PLRZ20240174) 1:04:24

Nancy Robison, Planner, presented a request to rezone property at 2000 North Canyon Road
from R1.8 (Single-Family Residential) to MDR (Medium Density Residential) to facilitate the
development of a proposed 100-unit apartment building. She noted that the Planning
Commission had recommended approval of the rezone and stated that a development agreement
template had been provided to the developer.

Councilors raised questions regarding several aspects of the proposal, including the development
agreement’s details, parking provisions, sewer capacity, and the inclusion of for-sale units in the
project.

Rob Reynolds, the developer, provided additional context about his plans for the development.
He indicated that the project might include townhomes or condos for sale, addressing Council
concerns about homeownership opportunities. He also assured the Council that parking spaces
would be included with unit rentals and that additional spaces could be made available for rent if
needed.

The Council discussed the broader implications of the project, highlighting the importance of
fostering homeownership opportunities while balancing the city’s need for diverse housing types.
Councilors deliberated on whether the development agreement should mandate a specific
percentage of for-sale units within the project.

No final decision on the rezone request was made during this work meeting. Councilors
emphasized the need for more detailed provisions in the development agreement before moving
forward with approval of the zone change.

3. A Discussion Regarding a Rewrite of the Noise Ordinance (24-102) 1:38:56

Michael Sanders, Policy Analyst presented proposed revisions to the city’s noise ordinance, with
a focus on exemptions for athletic stadiums. He shared data indicating that noise complaints
related to stadium activities represented a significantly small proportion of overall complaints in
the city.

Three options for exempting athletic stadiums were outlined, with staff recommending Option
1(a): exempting official athletic competitions from noise restrictions regardless of time of day,
while requiring permits for other nighttime activities.

Eric Davis, representing BYU Athletics, provided input on non-athletic events held at BYU
facilities and the potential impact of the proposed ordinance on these activities.

Motion

Following discussion, Councilor George Handley proposed a motion to exempt athletic stadium
activities from noise restrictions between 7:00 AM and 10:00 PM. Activities occurring after
10:00 PM would require a permit if they exceed noise limits. Councilor MacKay seconded the
motion.

The motion passed unanimously 6-0 (Hoban Excused).

5. A Presentation Regarding Disclosure Document Implementation (24-016) 2:27:06

Scott Johnson, Zoning Administrator, presented updates on the implementation process for
disclosure documents required for rental properties. He explained that the new procedure
mandates applicants to submit completed disclosure documents as a prerequisite for rental
license approval.

Councilors recommended adding information about the disclosure document requirement to the
online application portal to ensure applicants are adequately informed during the application
process.

6. An Ordinance Regarding the Increase of Water Impact Fees (24-109) 2:34:17

Susie Becker, Zions Public Finance, presented a proposal to increase water impact fees. She
outlined the methodology used for calculating the fees, which included an analysis of existing
facilities, future infrastructure needs, and growth projections.

The proposed fee structure varies based on lot size and meter size, aiming to allocate costs more
equitably according to water usage. Councilors discussed the rationale for the differentiated fees
and the potential implications for housing affordability.

John Borgett, Administrative Services Director, noted that the administration recommended
implementing the new fee structure on July 1, extending beyond the typical 90-day notice period.
This extension is intended to give developers additional time to adapt to the changes.

The Council expressed general support for the proposed fee structure and the adjusted
implementation timeline. A formal vote on the ordinance will be scheduled for a future meeting.

7. An Ordinance Amending City Code Chapter 2.05, the Election Code (24-110) 2:57:35

Gary Millward, Deputy City Attorney, and Heidi Allman, City Recorder, presented proposed
amendments to the city’s election code. The revisions aimed to update references to state code
and clarify language within the ordinance. A key focus of the discussion was on campaign
finance reporting requirements and the rules governing the disposal of unused campaign funds.

Corporation (24-108) 3:27:43

Justin Harrison, Council Executive Director, presented proposed amendments to the bylaws of
the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) of Provo City Corporation. The updates focused on revising
officer titles and clarifying leadership roles between the City Council and the RDA Board.

Motion

Board Member George Handley moved to approve the bylaw amendments with the inclusion of

cases, the Board would vote to appoint replacements. Board Member Christensen seconded the
motion.

The motion passed unanimously 7-0.
