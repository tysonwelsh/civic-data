CORNERSTONE 1-LOT COMMERCIAL SUBDIVISION FINAL PLAT APPROVED:

   City Planner Shauna Talbot reviewed the final plat for Cornerstone 1-lot Commercial Subdivision. This
project is located at approximately 1610 S. and 130 W. (Orgill Lane) just South of the Maverik
convenience store on the West side of Hwy 28. It consists of parcel # XA00-2518-21, and a portion of
parcel # XA00-2518-1E. The developers are 4SI Group LLC, A UT LLC with Joe Nielson and Josh Nixon
representing the company. Their final plat was recommended by the Planning Commission at their April
12th meeting.
   As part of this development, Korey Wright has deeded a triangle piece to the West of this lot to be
incorporated as part of this one lot subdivision. In conjunction with the deeding, a notice was recorded
at the county recorder’s office in accordance with Utah Code that stated the breaking off of the triangle
is in anticipation of a land use approval, and no approval has of yet been granted. Finally, 4SI group has
deeded to the City a portion of ground that could become a road in future on the south of this property
heading West. They also recorded the necessary notice required by Utah Code.
  The city and applicants are also still working with UDOT for a potential access south of the Maverik
access, more in alignment with the Southern boundary of their property, that would allow for more
feasible traffic flow onto Orgill Lane, and subsequently to the West of the area, but this will likely not
happen before this development is completed. The property is in the Highway Commercial 1 zone and
has potential to provide complementary businesses to the Crimson Flats development and the Maverik
gas station.

DEFINITION ORDINANCE TABLED FOR FURTHER REVISIONS:

  Ms. Talbot reviewed some definition changes in Title 10 of the Nephi City Code. The Planning
Commission recommended some more specific definitions in regards to various residential types in an
effort to make uses allowed in residential zones, and types of ownership in regards to separate lots
more clear and concise. Also, to be more definitive as to the city’s requirements regarding internal
accessory dwelling units or IADUs. This will also help as zoning clarifications and additional zones are
recommended.
  City Attorney Kasey Wright had some additions and changes to the draft definitions. The council
decided to table adopting the ordinance and directed staff to bring the ordinance back with the
suggested revisions.

ORDINANCE 04-18-2023 TITLE 11 UPDATES TO EXCEPTION TO SUBDIVISIONS:

   Ms. Talbot updated the council that the recent request for a development to be an exception to the
subdivision code has made it necessary to clarify the city’s code. The City Council recently
recommended, and the Planning Commission approved a change to allow an existing home to be
exempt from installing curb, gutter, and sidewalk. New parcels that are subdivided will still need to
install this infrastructure. The changes proposed in the ordinance below enable this for existing homes.
In addition, the state code allows for exemptions to be made for up to ten (10) units in the exceptions
code. The Planning Commission recommended that the city’s code be changed from three (3) to the
amount allowed by state law. Other restrictions apply in the city’s exceptions code that will likely
prevent a subdivision of 10 lots in the original platted part of the city. In addition, land is scarce in the
original platted part of Nephi which would also prevent a subdivision of 10 lots.
   Councilor Worwood moved to adopt Ordinance 04-18-2023 as presented. Councilor Callaway
seconded the motion. The motion carried on a unanimous vote.

AMENDING TAYLOR WARBURTON DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT DISCUSSED:

  Ms. Talbot updated the council that In November of last year, the city entered into an agreement with
Taylor Warburton dba/Tompkins Street, Inc. This agreement allowed Mr. Warburton to sell off divided
parcels on the corner of 300 North and 400 East, issue building permits, and receive certificate of
occupancies on parcels that were being split off the original parcel containing an existing home. In
return, Mr. Warburton agreed to install curb, gutter, and sidewalk along the entire parcel, including the
section with the existing home. He also agreed to install a storm drain inlet/drop box to allow for
drainage to the Hollow.
  With the recent direction by the Council and the recommendation of the Planning Commission to
exempt existing residences from curb, gutter, and sidewalk improvements, Mr. Warburton would like to
remove the requirement to install these improvements from the agreement. These infrastructure
improvements would still need to be installed on the new parcels that have been subdivided from the
original parcel.
   The installation of a storm box to collect water and disburse it to the Hollow would function much
better if all three parcels had curb and gutter, especially if it was all installed at the same time and the
elevations from the three parcels were designed to work together. If the existing home is missing curb
and gutter, then the curb and gutter will not function as effectively and if these improvements are ever
installed on the existing home, they may not match up as easily with the curb and gutter on the new
parcels.
   Despite this, it would be consistent with the recent direction of the Council to exempt the existing
home from installing curb, gutter and sidewalk on the parcel containing the existing home. Mr. Talbot
recommended amending the original agreement to state that curb, gutter, and sidewalk must be
installed only on the new parcels and not on the existing home.
  Mr. Warburton asked if the storm drainage provision of the agreement could also be removed. Ms.
Talbot recommended that the storm drainage provision be left in the agreement but did suggest our
Public Works Director Kyle Marchant look at that issue.
  The council directed staff to bring an amended agreement back to the council for approval. Mayor
Seely highly complimented Ms. Talbot for her expertise, knowledge, and her desire to follow code. Mr.
Warburton also complimented Ms. Talbot for being up front and clear about expectations and her being
able to clarify Nephi City code issues.
