3.7 COVID-19 and Earthquake Emergency Response Updates
The Council may Take Action, Including Votes, on any 
Necessary Legislative Matters Related to the Ongoing States 
of Emergency
20-0405
Attachments: Staff Report
Presenters: Mayor Jenny Wilson; Jill Miller, Associate Deputy Mayor; Clint 
Mecham, Salt Lake County Emergency Manager; Gary Edwards, Health 
Department Director; Rory Payne, Facilities Director  (Approx. 20 mins.)
Mayor Jennifer Wilson  updated the Council on the COVID -19 pandemic.  
She stated the County is seeing progress, which allows it to plan for the next 
phase to reopen additional businesses.  The County is working closely with 
the State on its reopening plan, so hopefully this will avoid any confusion. To 
have success with reopening, testing must be available, case tracing,  hospital 
stabilization, and a decline in the number of cases have to all be in place. 
Mr. Gary Edwards , Director, Salt Lake County Health Department, 
updated the Council on the COVID -19 pandemic.  He stated over 33,000 
residents of Salt Lake County have been tested for the virus.  The highest 
group for positive cases falls within the 30- to 40-year-old range; however, 
this is down from a high of 48 percent to 40 percent of the total cases.  The 
20-year-old and under range only has 7 percent of the overall cases. Hospital 
rates increase with age, with the 65- to 85- plus-year-olds having the highest 
hospitalization rates.  Cases have been found at the jail and the men ’s 
resource center. Data, which has not been verified, shows that the suicide

rate is down from this time last year, number of cases of child abuse and 
neglect has decreased, and there is a significant drop in the number of youths 
coming to the receiving centers or crisis centers.  However, there is a 33 
percent increase in the number of domestic violence calls. 
Mr. David Schuld , Hagerty Consulting and Intelligent Section Chief for 
COVID-19, stated his job is to look at real data and analyze it so Uniform 
Command can make decisions based on that data.  Even though there is a 
higher percentage of individuals being tested, rates of COVID -19 are not 
increasing. This is still a fluid situation and plans are in place to respond 
quickly to any increase of the virus. 
Council Member Burdick asked if there was any data on false negative 
testing.
Mr. Edwards stated the test that is being used in Utah is in the 
99-plus-percent range for accuracy. 
Council Member Ghorbani  asked if there was any antibody testing being 
done.
Mr. Edwards stated there are a lot of labs working on it, but no trial tests 
have been granted emergency use authorization by the Food Drug 
Administration (FDA), so the tests are not recommended for use by the 
Centers for Disease Control (CDC). 
Mr. Clint Mecham , Salt Lake County Emergency Manager, updated the 
Council on the 5.7 earthquake that occurred on March 18, 2020.  He stated 
there has been over 1,400 aftershocks, with two bigger 4.2 aftershocks 
occurring last week.  The aftershocks damaged additional buildings, 
including Fire Station 102 in Magna.  This fire station has been temporarily 
closed due to concerns over the viability of the structure.  Engineers are 
looking into the structure. Damage assessments will wrap up on April 22, 
2020, so the State can forward that assessment to the Federal Emergency 
Management Agency (FEMA).  These assessments will provide an overall 
picture of structure damage and total dollar lost, which will help the State to 
determine if a Major Disaster Declaration is needed. 
3.8 Legislative Update 20-0404

Presenter: Kara Trevino, Council Legislative Director (Approx. 20 mins.)
Ms. Kara Trevino , Intergovernmental Relations Manager, Council Office, 
stated the Legislature called themselves into a special session to discuss bills 
that were needed for the budget and other issues within the State.  The 
following bills were passed:
-SJR 301 Joint Resolution
This resolution allows the State to accept federal funds in response to COVID -19.  The 
Legislature is working on how those funds will be distributed. The U .S. Treasury is 
providing guidance on how the money can be used. 
-HJR 301 Urging Fiscal Responsibility
Ms. Trevino stated this resolution urges counties to practice fiscal responsibility. Salt 
Lake County has already taken action to deal with this.
-H.B. 3006 Election Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated the primary election is scheduled for June 30, 2020, but this bill 
allows counties to do the primary elections by mail.  It provides the counties to be a 
mobile voting county, which means on election day voters can drive up and drop off their 
ballot.  
-H.B. 3007 COVID-19 Workers' Compensation Amendments for First Responders
Ms. Trevino stated this bill allows first responders who get COVID -19 to be eligible for 
Workers' Compensation.  It is hoped that the CARE Act would help with the cost of this.
-S.B. 3004 COVID-19 Health and Economic Response Act

Ms. Trevino stated this bill sets up a 10-member commission to give recommendations 
on how to take the next step in reopening the economy.  The commission has been 
working with Utah Leads Together to come up with recommendations by April 22, 2020.  
If the Governor does not take the recommendations, then by the end of the month he has 
to say why he did not.
All these bills passed.  The Legislature will be back in session on Thursday, April 23, and 
Friday, April 24, to take up additional bills, which include:
-H.B. 3009 Local Government Emergency Response
Ms. Trevino stated the League of Cities and Towns and Utah Association of Counties 
worked on this bill. The bill tried to strike a balance between what local leaders and state 
leaders could do. More coordination and better messaging on health orders is wanted . 
This bill got put on the sidelines because the Senate came down with its bill.  Besides 
coordination, there are other things the local governments need so it is hopeful this bill will 
come back up.  
-H.B. 3004 Annexation Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill deals with an annexation and incorporation in Weber County; 
it does not impact Salt Lake County.
-H.B. 3005 Pandemic Response and Consultation Act
Ms. Trevino stated this bill would require the Governor to provide some notice to the 
Legislature before he could declare an emergency.  This bill is on hold in the Senate.
-S.B. 3007 Contagious Virus Provisions
Ms. Trevino stated this bill provides immunity to business owners who might have a 
contagious person come into their business or property and infect other people. This bill 
will probably be discussed on Friday, April 24.
