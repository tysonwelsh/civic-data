               Later in the meeting.

               Laurie Stringham, Council Member, Salt Lake County, stated she is working with
Legislatures on H.B. 374 – County Sheriff Amendments. The bill would disband Unified Police
Department (UPD). If the bill passes, she will work with the Metro Township’s with whatever needs
it has moving forward.

               Council Member Hull asked if the Salt Lake County Council is going to keep its
investment.

                Ms. Stringham stated the Salt Lake County Council has only discussed and voted
on opposing the bill. There have been discussions on creating a group to look into what the future
of countywide and shared services will look like. In addition, and at the request of Sheriff Rosie
Rivera, a committee was formed, led by Mayor Silvestrini, to examine countywide services. She
participated in those discussions, and it became evident that a lot of people do not understand
how UPD works.

               Council Member Barney asked what the worst case scenario will be if the bill
passes and what the funding mechanism will be for the Metro Townships since it does not have
property tax authority.

              Ms. Stringham stated Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area
(SLVLESA) is the current funding source for UPD. If the bill passes, SLVLESA does not go away
and UPD will not immediately be dissolved. SLVLESA was created to fund UPD and can stand
on its own.

                Council Member Hull stated with there being three days left in the legislative
session, will the bill pass?

                Ms. Stringham stated she does not know. There is a perceived conflict of interest
with Sheriff Rivera. After researching Sheriff Rivera’s role in UPD and SLVLESA the only conflict
she had was her participation on the SLVLESA Board. That has since been resolved and she was
removed. There is a possibility that the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD) will
be able to take over the administration of SLVLESA and do it at a savings.

Action/Discussion Items

Purchase of Flock and Plate Readers for Magna Unified Police Department (UPD)

               Lieutenant Shane Manwaring, UPD, stated Flock Safety is a company that was
created to help law enforcement eliminate crime while maintaining privacy. Cameras are used to
capture license plate numbers, vehicle model, unique alterations, number of doors, stickers,
among other identifiers. It is a subscription based service, costing $2,500 per camera per year
with a $250 set-up fee. Midvale City has 15 cameras throughout its city and has seen an increase
in the recovery of stolen vehicles. This technology has helped in solving various types of cases
like, homicides, stolen vehicles, missing persons, vehicle burglaries, and stolen credit cards.

               Council Member Barney stated when officers have a vehicle it wants to find, they
put the information into the system and then the cameras start looking for the vehicle.

                Lieutenant Manwaring stated yes, officers have this program up and when a
vehicle is flagged it will notify officers.

               Council Member Prokopis asked if it is more beneficial to have cameras in a
mobile unit rather than a fixed unit.

               Lieutenant Manwaring stated the fixed cameras are more beneficial. UPD has
cameras installed in traffic cars and they help catch revoked registration and insurance. They
have the capability to capture revoked and expired license plates; however, the State of Utah will
not provide access to that data base.

                Council Member Prokopis asked if a camera installed at a four-way intersection
captures all directions.

               Lieutenant Manwaring stated no. The company guarantees the capture of two
lanes. To cover an entire intersection, there would need to be four cameras. He would like the
Council to fund cameras for Magna.

               Council Member Hull asked how many cameras would be needed.

Capital Improvement Projects Timeline

Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD) Planning and Development Services and

2023 Legislative Session and Proposed Bills

               Paul Ashton stated 1,500 bills were filed at the Legislature this year with 182 of
those being tracked for the metro townships. The bill being most focused on is H.B. 374 – County
Sheriff Amendments. There is a short term rental bill that will start a pilot program to allow short
term rentals. People can register their rentals, get licensing, and municipalities can impose a tax
on it. The administrative land use bill is the same program that the MSD is already using, it will
not have a great impact on Magna.

Representatives Tueschers H.B. 374 bill will have some consequences, especially for himself. He
represents both Magna and White City Metro Townships. Magna is on the cusp of becoming an
independent city. Magna will have more options on what it wants to do with law enforcement.
Because of that conflict of interest, he is ceasing his representation of Magna.

             Council Member Hull asked if Mr. Ashton will stay on as its attorney for a few
more months until it can hire a new attorney.

               Mr. Ashton stated he would consider staying on until a conflict arises. He wants
to make it clear for the record that he brought this matter to the Council’s attention.

Form of Government Change

                Paul Ashton stated if the Council decides to move forward with the form of
government change it will need to consider the timing. The Council must pass a resolution more
than 75 days before the next election. The 2nd meeting in July is 78 days prior to the election.
After the resolution is approved, the Council needs to hold two public hearings within 45 days.
The last day the Clerk’s Office needs the information is September 1, 2023. The Council may
want to look at adopting the resolution during the 2nd meeting in May. That would provide a couple
of months to hold the public hearings. From previous conversations, it appears that the Council
wants to adopt a mayor and five-member council.
