Citizen Comments:
Teresa Holmes, West Jordan resident, expressed concern about the Community Cat Program, stating
that in reality the program had increased the cat population in her neighborhood. She spoke of
property damage, health concerns, and property value concerns. Ms. Holmes commented that cat
food left outside for the community cats also attracted rats and racoons. She asked that the
Community Cat Program be reviewed, discussed, and amended.

Dave Holmes, West Jordan resident, agreed with Ms. Holmes’ comments. He stated that some of the
yards in the neighborhood smelled of cat urine and feces. Mr. Holmes asked, if changes could not be
made to the Community Cat Program, that residents be allowed to treat the feral cats the same as
skunks and racoons.

Stephanie Chavez, West Jordan resident, read aloud a statement from Duane and Vicki Sorensen,
stating the number of cats constantly in their yard was destroying their lawn and yard furniture,

their children and grandchildren did not want to visit because of the conditions, and Mr. Sorensen
was allergic to cats, making it difficult for him to be outside. Skunks, racoons, and rabbits were now
in their yard all the time looking for food, and they had found several dead kittens. Boats and trailers
in the neighborhood had been destroyed by the feral cats. The Sorensen’s did not bring the element

Aurelia Redd, West Jordan resident, said she was speaking on behalf of a group who recently had a
neighborhood meeting. Ms. Redd said there was extreme concern and deep frustration in her
neighborhood related to the Community Cat Program, with cats destroying yards, gardens, and the
community. She said the number of cats in the neighborhood had reached unhealthy levels and was
causing thousands of dollars in property damage. Ms. Redd said the Community Cat Program had
done the opposite of reducing the number of feral cats. She said she had a severe cat allergy and had
needed to be administered emergency medication because of the situation. Ms. Redd said her
cameras had caught 18 different cats in one night, in flower beds, sheds and garages, and on cars,
boats, and trailers. She provided a packet that included a list of 17 different diseases found in the
flower beds from cats. Ms. Redd said the Health Department said the neighborhood had an unhealthy
number of cats, causing unhealthy conditions in yards and gardens. She pointed out there was no
liability written into the Community Cat Program and implored the City Council to make changes.
She suggested the City: define the number of cats per neighborhood and per caretaker; allow
homeowners to capture more than one cat per day on their property, and for Animal Control to
relocate the cats somewhere else; provide cat deterrents in the neighborhood; provide a service to
clean up the cat feces and neutralize the cat urine in flower beds in the neighborhood to save plants
and children; determine liability for property damages and provide a process to submit claims for
reimbursement; update requirements for caretakers to provide food and water indoors; and create
a permitting process. She asked the Council to please act now.

Ann Marie Barrett, Bingham Creek Library Manager, reported on a successful summer reading
program. She thanked everyone who participated. Ms. Barrett spoke of an upcoming program at the
library.

 7. PUBLIC HEARINGS
       a. Highlands Landing Rezone
          Jeff Seamon with Peterson Development explained the request to rezone 7.26 acres of
          property located at approximately 7662 South 5490 West from R-3-20 Senior Housing
          Overlay (SHO) Zone to R-3-20 Zone. He said Peterson Development planned to develop
          45 single-family homes. A map of the area was shown. Mr. Seamon reported of the senior
          housing element proposed in 2017 and noted that the applicant had since obtained
          additional property they believed would be a better location for senior housing. He
          showed a layout of the proposed 45-home development, planned with a moderate-income
          price point. The proposed neighborhood would not have a traffic impact on existing
          residential streets in the area and would have less impact on the area than a three-story
          senior-living product. Mr. Seamon emphasized that a bait-and-switch was not the
          intention of Peterson Development.

           Mr. Seamon commented that development agreements were usually involved in
           situations requiring installation of utilities or infrastructure, which was not the case with
           the current application. He noted that the applicant had no intention of coming back with
           a denser product if the rezone was approved.

           Responding to a question from Council Member Pack, Mr. Seamon said a tot-lot would
           most likely be placed in the open space detention area. Council Member Worthen
           expressed the opinion that West Jordan did not have enough senior housing options, and
           said she believed both locations could be successful with senior housing. Mr. Seamon said
           a market study showed there was probably not enough demand for senior-specific
           housing at both locations.

           Barrett Peterson with Peterson Development spoke of multiple Peterson Development
           senior housing developments in Utah, and said he was excited to bring the product to
           West Jordan. He confirmed that a market study found that two senior housing projects so
           close together would oversupply the market in that area. He emphasized that changes
           were made thoughtfully, and he did not take the decision to change development plans
           lightly.

           Chair Whitelock opened a public hearing at 8:03 pm

           Comments:
           Amy Martz, West Jordan resident, had objected to the three-story apartment complex, and
           was pleased to find lower-density housing proposed instead. She was not in favor of the
           roundabout.

           June Christiansen, West Jordan resident, drove past the subject area every day, and was
           concerned about putting 45 houses in that space. She anticipated every home would have
           3-4 cars and hoped there would be enough space for the cars to not be parked on the
           street. Ms. Christiansen was worried about the impact on the Utah Natural Meat farm and
           felt there would probably be complaints about the smell and noise that went along with
           cows. She believed everything needed to be firmly in writing before anything was
           approved.

           Chair Whitelock closed the public hearing at 8:07 pm

           Chair Whitelock struggled with the request. She expressed concern with the request to
           entitle the developer with 20 units per acre without restrictions. Chair Whitelock said she
           would be more comfortable with a plan that would lock the development at 45 units. She
           said she hoped when it was time for the homes to be purchased that homebuyers would
           be required to acknowledge in writing that they knew they were purchasing near a
           working ranch.

           Council Member Jacob said he understood Chair Whitelock’s concerns about what would
           be entitled versus what was proposed. However, he said he was willing to approve
           because, for the most part, Peterson Development had shown themselves to be a group
           that would develop what they said they would develop in good faith. He said he liked the
           proposed concept and liked that it was walkable.

           Vice Chair Green understood the concerns expressed but agreed with Council Member
           Jacob that Peterson Development put their money where their mouth was in West Jordan.

           commercial, and the proposed moderate-income housing better than the three-story
           building. He would vote in favor. Council Member Bloom understood that changes
           happened over time, and liked the changes proposed. She would vote in favor and looked

       d. Moderate Income Housing Element Amendment
          City Planner/Zoning Administrator Larry Gardner said the Planning Commission
          recommended eight strategies for inclusion in the Moderate-Income Housing element of

          recommended eight and added three additional for a total of nine. Mr. Gardner said State
