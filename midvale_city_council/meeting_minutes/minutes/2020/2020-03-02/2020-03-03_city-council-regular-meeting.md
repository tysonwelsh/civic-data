IX.	ACTION ITEM
	A.	CONSIDER PROPOSED TEXT AMENDMENT TO REPEAL THE 	HISTORIC COMMERCIAL ZONE AND ADOPT THE MAIN STREET 	CODE (ORDINANCE NO. 2020-O-04) AND PROPOSED REZONE TO 	REZONE THE MAIN STREET NEIGHBORHOOD TO THE MAIN STREET 	FORM-BASED CODE (ORDINANCE NO. 2020-O-05)
Alex Murphy said following the City Council’s adoption of the Main Street Small Area Plan, City Staff has been working with the consultant, Planning Commission, City Council, and property owners in the Main Street neighborhood to develop the Main Street Form-Based Code (“MS-FBC”). The MSFBC establishes new standards concerning the development and redevelopment of the Main Street neighborhood with the intent to revitalize the area and see it transform into a connected, walkable town center for the City. 

The FBC was presented to the Council for discussion on January 7, 2020 and January 21, 2020 and then for a public hearing on February 18, 2020. Following the latest meeting, Staff has revised the FBC to establish a minimum parking standard for residential uses of 1 stall per unit. All other standards, including parking maximums as originally proposed, remain in place. Staff has prepared two ordinances for the Council to consider which accomplish the following:

1.	Repeal the Historic Commercial Zone and replace it with the Main Street Form-	Based Code (MS-FBC; affecting Chapter 17-7-11 of the Midvale City Municipal 	Code) and 

2. 	Apply the MS-FBC to all properties in the Main Street neighborhood.

ORDINANCE CONSIDERATIONS:
Section 17-3-1 of the Midvale City Municipal Code requires the Council adopt at least one of three findings to support a rezone:

1.	The proposed rezoning is necessary either to comply with the general plan 	proposed land use map or to provide land for a community need that was not 	anticipated at the time of adoption of the general plan;

2.	The existing zoning was either the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact, or 	that it failed to take into account the constraints on development created by the 	natural characteristics of the land, including but not limited to, steep slopes, flood 	plain, unstable soils, and inadequate drainage; or 

3.	The land or its surrounding environs has changed or is changing to such a degree 	that it is in the public interest to encourage redevelopment of the area or to 	recognize the changed character of the area. In this case, the first finding would 	apply. The 2016 General Plan identified the Main Street neighborhood as an 	Opportunity Area and established goals for the area, including the following:
	1.	Preserve and build on the character of Historic Main Street; …
	3.	Enhance the Main Street and Center Street corridors to create a vibrant, 			walkable town center that could become a regional attraction;
	
	4.	Diversify the land use mix to develop a compact full-service pedestrian 			scale neighborhood, particularly in the area of Main Street, Center Street, 			and Holden Street;…

	6.	Connect Main Street to surrounding neighborhoods and to Bingham 			Junction and Jordan Bluffs through pedestrian and bicycle pathways and 			urban design techniques to create a cultural center; … and

	11. 	Develop design guidelines for new development and alterations on Main 			Street. 

The 2018 Main Street Small Area Plan used those goals as a framework and established additional goals specific to the Main Street neighborhood:
	•	Attract new investment;
	•	Support existing businesses;
	•	Strengthen residential areas;
	•	Improve connectivity; and
	•	Improve neighborhood identity.
The Main Street Form-Based Code was written with these goals in mind and customized to address specific needs within the neighborhood.

PLANNING COMMISSION RECOMMENDATION:
Section 17-3-1 of the Midvale City Municipal Code requires the Planning Commission hold a public hearing and adopt a written recommendation to the City Council, advising the City Council to approve, disapprove, or modify a request on all zoning code and map amendments.

The Planning Commission conducted a public hearing on November 13, 2019 to consider the MS-FBC and ultimately issued a positive recommendation to the City Council to approve the MS-FBC text amendment and rezone on December 11, 2019.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION:
Based on the public input and planning efforts completed of the past two years and compliance with the General Plan and Main Street Small Area Plan, Staff recommends the City Council approve Ordinance No. 2020-O-04, repealing the Historic Commercial zone and replacing it with the Main Street Form-Based Code, and Ordinance No. 2020-O-05, rezoning the Main Street neighborhood to the Main Street Form-Based Code zoning district.

	C.	DISUCSS MIDVALE CONNECT CITIZEN ENGAGEMENT PROGRAM
Laura Magness said Midvale City strives to provide timely, effective communication to the Midvale community with a goal of increasing resident’s understanding and awareness of local government. Sharing relevant information is the cornerstone of improving government transparency and gaining community participation in the decision-making process.

To that end, Midvale City is launching the new online forum, Midvale Connect, in order to
increase civic engagement. Because it may be difficult for residents to attend City Council
meetings or stay current on city issues, we are implementing the short and easy to use online survey system that enables residents to voice their opinions on topics that impact our community. Utilizing the Public Input platform, we will be able to reach out to the community through text, email, and social media.

In order to receive and participate in these surveys, residents must opt-in. We will invite residents to opt-in via email (utilizing the utility billing database), social media, the Midvale City Journal, Midvale City’s website, and through the school district’s online newsletter, PeachJar. According to Public Input, residents are more likely to respond to short surveys that take approximately five minutes to complete. They also suggest using a soft issue as the first survey.

Therefore, the first Midvale Connect survey will be a Communications Survey to determine how residents receive information about Midvale City and resident’s perceptions of Midvale City’s communication efforts. With the goal of continuous improvement, we will utilize the results to improve the City’s communications with the community and increase community participation in the decision-making process.

	A.	PUBLIC HEARING AND PRELIMINARY SUBDIVISION PLAT 	APPROVAL FOR 3-LOT JORDAN BLUFFS LOT 201 AMENDED 	SUBDIVISION LOATED AT 983 WEST CENTER STREET
Mark Murdock, representing Gardner Jordan Bluffs LC, is requesting approval to subdivide property located 983 West Center Street (7800 South) into three lots to dedicate a public right-of-way and separate parcels Gardner is required to retain from other developable parcels in the northern portion of Jordan Bluffs Subareas 1-3 area. The lots created by the subdivision include Pods D&E (Lot 1), Pods F-H (Lot 2), and Pods I-N (Lot 3) as laid out on the pod map in Section 17-7-10.12.1 of the zoning ordinance. The subject property is approximately 150 acres in size and is bounded by Center Street (7800 South) on the north, Bingham Junction Boulevard on the east, the Jordan River on the west, and the cap slope south. No new development is proposed as part of this subdivision request.

The proposed subdivision is subject to Title 16 (Subdivisions), and Chapter 17-7-10.12 (Jordan Bluffs Subareas 1-3 Development Zone). Because of the project’s proximity to the Jordan River and associated special flood hazard area identified by FEMA, Chapter 8.11 (Flood Damage Prevention) also applies.

Because the proposal includes the dedication of a public road, the large subdivision process laid out in Section 16.04.040 applies. Staff has found the proposed subdivision generally complies with the design standard requirements of Title 16 Subdivisions. No lot standards apply for the zone and the proposed street layouts meet the minimum right-of-way requirements of the ordinance. Redesign of the planted median in Bingham Junction Boulevard will be required to accommodate the proposed intersection but can be postponed until the new street is built in conjunction with the adjacent pod(s). Street tree requirements have been overridden by the development zone and will be addressed during the site plan review. Public and private infrastructure improvements will be included as part of individual site development plans. Street trees, public infrastructure, and reconstruction of the 	Bingham Junction Boulevard median will
need to be addressed at the time of site development.

The subdivision ordinance requires that duty to serve letters from the water and sewer provider (Midvale City) be provided and courtesy notices be sent regarding utility easements to Dominion Energy, Rocky Mountain Power, Xfinity, Utopia, and CenturyLink prior to final plat approval. The proposed subdivision also complies with the requirements of the development zone. The zone allows a subdivision plat to identify and dedicate roadways between pods and to establish park areas prior to submission of the required Large-Scale Master Plan. Further development on the site will require the applicant follow the Large-Scale Master Plan review process identified in Chapter 17-7-10.12. Chapter 8.11, Flood Damage Prevention, establishes additional requirements for development within identified special flood hazard areas, also known as floodplains. The applicant has
addressed these requirements by including Base Flood Elevations and the boundary of the 100- year special flood hazard area on the plat with a note indicating future development within the special flood hazard area will be required to comply with the development standards of Chapter 8.11.

The subject property currently contains a construction trailer that was placed by the owner last year near the intersection of Ivy Drive and Bingham Junction Boulevard. A permit request for that structure was denied and removal of that structure and its associated improvements is required. This trailer will need to be removed prior to recording of the final plat.

The proposal was sent to the City Engineer and Unified Fire Authority for review and both
approved the preliminary plat with technical items to be addressed on the final plat. All subdivisions require a review and recommendation from the Planning Commission and approval from the City Council. Public hearings are required to be held by each body. The
Planning Commission conducted a public hearing on this subdivision plat on February 12, 2020 and forwarded a recommendation to the City Council to approve the preliminary subdivision plat for the Jordan Bluffs Lot 201 Amended Subdivision with the following findings and conditions or approval:	

Findings:

2. 	With the conditions included in this approval, the proposed subdivision complies 	with the requirements of Title 16; Chapter 17-7-10.12; and Chapter 8.11 of the 	Midvale City Municipal Code.

Conditions of Approval:
1.	The applicant shall prepare a final subdivision plat to be reviewed and approved 	by the City Engineer, Fire Marshal, and City Council.
2. 	The applicant shall complete the required review processes described in the 	Jordan Bluffs Subareas 1-3 Development Zone ordinance prior to any construction 	on the site. A note to this effect shall be included on the plat.
3. 	Street trees and public infrastructure within and adjacent to the subdivision and 	reconstruction of the Bingham Junction Boulevard median at the new intersection 	with Binary Way shall be addressed during site development plans. A note to this 	effect shall be included on the plat.

5. 	The plat shall include the Base Flood Elevation and boundary of the special flood 	hazard area identified on the applicable Flood Insurance Rate Map.
6.	Future development within the special flood hazard area shall be required to 	comply with the development standards of Chapter 8.11 including, but not limited 	to, elevating residential structures at least 1’ above BFE, elevating or floodproofing 	nonresidential structures at least 1’ above BFE, and designing utility systems to 	minimize or eliminate the potential for flood damage.
