      a.     Glass Recycling - Momentum Glass.

Jason Utgaard from Momentum Glass gave a presentation on glass recycling. He
recognized individuals present in the meeting who had visited the facility and welcomed
any desiring to take a tour. He summarized the history and mission of Momentum Glass
and stated that they are the only facility in Utah and the Rocky Mountain Region. They
maintain another facility in Colorado.

Mr. Utgaard reviewed the process glass goes through to be recycled into fiberglass
insulation. They currently have 700 commercial customers and are approaching 9,000
residential customers, who span the Salt Lake Valley as well as Park City. He explained
that glass assists in creating jobs. He gave an example of the benefit of recycled glass
with the Owens Corning facility in Nephi.

Mr. Utgaard reviewed the Curbside Glass Recycling Program, stating that it is a monthly
opt-in service where residents can sign up on the Momentum Recycling website. The

program also includes their municipal partners, who have sign-up forms on their websites
as well. This is a month-to-month program and does not require a contract. Pick-up
reminders for the glass recycling bins are sent by text, phone, email, and through a mobile
app. After sign-up, the bin is delivered within a week and the glass collection service is
provided.

Mr. Jeppesen highlighted the mobile app, which consists of the Waste Wizard that
educates residents as they learn how to recycle. The app was launched in June and

         c.        Emergency Management Discussion.

Emergency Management Coordinator, Scott Chapman introduced Emergency
Management Director, Bob Jeppesen from the Salt Lake County Health Department who
discussed the current coronavirus situation. Mr. Jeppesen reviewed the history of
COVID-19 (Coronavirus) and the details of how it is spread. He reviewed the symptoms
as well as the incubation period of the disease. He compared the Coronavirus to similar
diseases and their fatality rates. The case fatality rate for the Coronavirus is currently
estimated at 0.7% which is slightly higher than seasonal influenza.
Page | 5 Minutes – City Council Meeting
March 3, 2020




There is currently no vaccine, however, it is under development. Generally, vaccines take
up to one year to 18 months to produce. Mr. Jeppesen suggested individuals carry out
the same practices as during the influenza season to prevent illness. To date, there is no
anti-viral treatment for this disease, however, there is a new product that is being tested
on patients in Nebraska. Currently, patients can only be treated with supportive care for
this disease.

Mr. Jeppesen advised the residents of Riverton to wash their hands frequently, refrain
from touching faces, having physical contact with others, disinfect surfaces, stay home if
symptoms become worse, contact health care providers, and remain home for 24 hours
after sickness to prevent the contraction and spread of this disease.

Public health interventions began in China and travelers arriving in the United States after
February 23, 2020, were closely screened. The individuals were closely monitored for 14
days, required to take their temperature every day, and report to those monitoring them.
The Health Department urges communities to be sensitive to those individuals.

The Health Department has developed an Infectious Disease Emergency Response Plan.
Mr. Jeppesen reviewed the plans for various diseases and situations. Both Infectious
Disease Epidemiologists and Environmental Health professionals have a 24/7 emergency
call number. Mr. Jeppesen reviewed the process if an individual were to call that number
in the event of an emergency.

Mr. Jeppesen defined the terms quarantine and isolation and described the distinction
between them. He explained that all local public health officers in the State have the
statutory authority to order both quarantine and isolation as well as close mass
gatherings.

Mr. Jeppesen stated that the Council should consider how best to prepare Riverton
residents as well as determine what the City would need to do to maintain daily
operations.

Coronavirus is not currently in the State of Utah. The individual who is under quarantine
is asymptomatic and has not had the sickness. The Council should plan for possible
increased numbers in staff absences due to illness, possibly up to 30% of employees.
Mr. Jeppesen suggested the City monitor the absenteeism in the workplace and
presented ideas of how the City could accomplish this.

Mr. Jeppesen reviewed the vectors from the Middle East Respiratory Syndrome in a slide.
It is currently uncertain how the COVID-19 virus originated, but researchers speculate it
could have originated from bats. The Centers for Disease Control widened their case
definition of this disease recently, which results in a broader scale of individuals who may
currently have the disease. Currently, there are 105 cases of individuals with the disease
in the United States, however, the risk of contracting the disease remains low. The Health
Department has screened individuals who have been cooperative, which was a process

estimated to take 72 hours. The State Lab currently has kits for testing for the virus that
only take an estimated 24 hours. 17 individuals were tested in Utah of which 15 tested

Mr. Jeppesen reviewed a map from John Hopkins showing the current total confirmed
cases around the world and the total number of deaths, which changes several times in

         a.        Resolution No. 20-15 – Amending Section 2.4 and 4.1 of the City
                   Council Rules of Order and Procedure – Ryan Carter.

Mr. Carter presented two items proposed to be amended in the City Council Rules of
Order and Procedure. The first was that two Council Members need to work together to
put an item on the agenda. This will assist in making preparations for the agendas more
efficient.

The second would address how information will be communicated to and from the
Riverton Police Department. The goal was for staff to make the City Council aware of
issues in the City as soon as possible. The concern was with how much information
should be disclosed to an elected official during an investigation. Staff wants to refrain
from giving sensitive information to elected officials, including names of victims,
witnesses, and suspects. However, if there is an incident of a certain nature at a certain
location, this information is appropriate to share. The policy being proposed is designed
to allow police to provide pertinent information to Council Members, but not all
information.

Council Member Stewart asked if the Mayor could be added with the Council Members
to the first amended item proposed. Mr. Carter stated that he could and explained that
the Mayor can add an agenda item at his request. Concerning the second amended item
proposed, Council Member Stewart requested that Council Members be notified at the
same time as the Mayor if an incident affects their district. He was also concerned about
being restrained from communicating with the Police Chief or Officers.

Council Member McDougal stated that it is the Mayor’s duty to represent the entire City.
During an emergency, the Mayor and Police Chief have jurisdiction and the role of a
Council Member and the information given to them in this situation would be determined
by the Mayor and Police Chief. His concern pertained to the Council’s responsibilities
both now and in the future. People always want to be involved but do not necessarily
have the expertise. He preferred to leave the proposed amended item as written.

Council Member Wells approved of the proposed amendment.

Council Member Buroker approved of the proposed amendment. She related her
experience as a nurse in a Psych Ward and the importance of keeping information
confidential. As Council Member, their responsibility ensures that the district receives the
information they need, which does not always include the information they want. She
agreed that the Police Department has the right to determine which information needs to
be disseminated to elected officials.

Council Member McCay agreed with Council Member Stewart in that emergency
notifications should be sent to the Council Members in addition to the Mayor. Names do

         b.        Discuss the Transfer of a City Street to UDOT: 12600 South, from
                   Bangerter Highway to the Western Municipal Border of Riverton City.

Public Works Director, Trace Robinson, reported on the 12600 South Bangerter Highway
to Mountain View Corridor Project. He stated that Senator McCay has been assisting in
getting 12600 South turned over to the Utah Department of Transportation (“UDOT”).
There has been communication with UDOT over the last few years and entitlements for
Page | 13 Minutes – City Council Meeting
March 3, 2020


major accesses along the corridor between Mountain View Corridor and Bangerter
Highway have been retained.

Mr. Robinson stated that staff has been diligent in obtaining funding from the Wasatch
Front Regional Council (“WFRC”) for projects and improvements on this stretch of road.
He explained that the purpose behind acquiring the funding was that Riverton did not
want to give up their bike lanes along 12600 South.

Mr. Robinson reported that Riverton City has been awarded $1.8 million to widen the
road. Staff also received money from Corridor Preservation to purchase 10 feet on the
south side of 12600 South from Bangerter Highway to Mountain View Corridor.
Mr. Robinson stated they have an estimated three-fourths of that taken care of currently
by receiving various awards of right-of-way money. Staff also received $70,000 to
purchase the Rocky Mountain Power parcel on 4575 West.

With the approval of Olympia Hills, staff decided to wait on further projects for the
Southwest Vision Study to be completed to ensure that impacts on the road are
determined. Mr. Robinson reported that staff has currently returned the money to the
WFRC, the purpose being, if the money is used to widen the road now, the City’s options
for expanding the road in the future would be limited. The City is currently planning to
receive future funds from WFRC because of this action.

UDOT is asking for $3.3 million to upgrade the road to meet their requirements.
Mr. Robinson stated that if Riverton pays UDOT for that service and also receives the
Federal funding in five years, improvements will be made as the road is widened. He
stated that there is an option to give the road to UDOT to widen. Another option would
be to partner with Region 2 in receiving Federal funding.

Mayor Staggs thanked Mr. Robinson and expressed appreciation to Senator McCay for
his efforts as well. This support could relieve Riverton City of ongoing maintenance and
operations on 12600 South. The Mayor stated that Riverton City is always dealing with
finite resources and suggested that they instead take the money and put it toward other
projects. Majestic on 13200 South, east of the canal, is an option and the most important

Highway is a State route. Mr. Robinson confirmed that was the case. Council Member
McCay asked what the expense would be to maintain the section of road between the
Mountain View Corridor and Bangerter Highway along 12600 South. Mr. Robinson stated
that the City spends an estimated $5,000 to $10,000 per year without major maintenance.
Council Member McCay asked if the Federal money could be transferred to the State.
Mr. Robinson responded that he would like to research it. If it was a State road UDOT
would finance the match of the project. Council Member McCay commented that if that
were possible, she would support Federal funds going to the State unless Riverton City
needs to pay the $3.3 million. Mr. Robinson stated that there was a possibility that the
City will not have to provide the funds.

anything strategically and allow Mr. Robinson to pursue the five-year Federal funding to
finish the road and build it to accommodate traffic flows. Two, if possible, transfer the
