         The purpose of the Joint Work Session was to review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00
p.m.; discuss the Ogden Wellbeing Project with Utah State University; discuss the proposed name for the new Marshall White
Fieldhouse – The Nathan James Lyday Fieldhouse; discuss Council business, including a Council Member Initiative to allow
shipping containers for use as residential accessory buildings; discuss Board business, including Redevelopment Agency Mission
and Values; hear Council/Board comments.

Agenda Review
        Chair White and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Ogden Wellbeing Project – Utah State University
          Dr. Courtney Flint from Utah State University used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to present the results of the Utah
Wellbeing Survey conducted in Ogden in October 2024. Ogden is one of 51 cities that participated in the Utah Wellbeing Survey
Project in 2024. This project is designed to assess the wellbeing and local perspectives of City residents and to provide
information to City leaders to inform general planning processes. In October and November 2024, Ogden City advertised the
survey for residents largely through social media, newsletters, emails, and utility bills. All City residents age 18+ were encouraged
to take the online Qualtrics survey; 780 viable surveys were recorded in this 2024 survey effort. The adult population of Ogden
was estimated at 66,129, based on the American Community Survey by the U.S. Census and the 780 survey responses in 2024
represent 1.2 percent of the adult population and have a conservative margin of error of 3.49 percent. Dr. Flint provided
demographic statistics of the survey respondents, personal wellbeing feedback, community connectedness, participation in
recreational and nature-based activities in Ogden, perceptions of the pace of economic development in Ogden, top concerns and
values for Ogden residents, and feedback regarding what could be done to improve wellbeing in Ogden. For a copy of the
presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion between the Council and Dr. Flint centered on how Ogden’s survey results compare to results in other
communities.

Proposed Name for New Marshall White Fieldhouse – The Nathan James
Lyday Fieldhouse
       Recreation Manager Bridge explained that City Administration is recommending that the fieldhouse located within the
new Marshall N. White Community Center be named the “Nathan James Lyday Fieldhouse” in honor of Officer Lyday’s sacrifice
for and devotion to his community. Ronald White, son of the late Marshall N. White, recommended this honor, and Officer
Lyday’s family have given their approval and support. The Administration believes that naming the fieldhouse in honor of Officer
Lyday will serve not only as a tribute to him, but also as a powerful reminder of the bravery and dedication of all the City’s first
responders.
         The Council also heard from Mr. Ronald White regarding his feelings about Officer Lyday’s service to the community
and expressed his support for honoring him by naming the Fieldhouse after him.
         Council members expressed their gratitude to Mr. White for his input tonight and thanked him for his family’s sacrifice
as well.
         Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reported that action on the proposal is scheduled for April 1.


Council Business
         Council Member Initiative – Allowing Shipping Containers for Use as Residential Accessory Buildings: Council
Senior Policy Analyst Burton explained Council member Richey has submitted an initiative to discuss the expansion of land use
codes to allow shipping containers to be used as residential accessory buildings. He used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
provide background information regarding the initiative process, as well as information he found in his research regarding the
growing trend of using shipping containers as residential accessory buildings. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the
information packet for the meeting. Mr. Burton also played a video regarding the manner in which some contractors have used
shipping containers as residential structures.
         Council member Richey added his input to Mr. Burton’s presentation; he has noticed the growing trend of using shipping
containers as residential structures and wanted to explore the option with the Council. Discussion among the Council and staff
centered on appropriate regulations that should be imposed on shipping containers in the event the decision is made to approve
them as use for residential structures; differences between an accessory building and a backyard storage shed.
         Council members Hyer and Blair indicated that the Council could consider an ordinance that would allow and
appropriately regulate shipping containers as residential buildings; Council member Richey agreed and stated he feels it
appropriate for the City to respond to shifts in the market and provide a use that there is a demand for. Council member Choberka
agreed as well. Chair White asked Mr. Burton what the next steps would be in the process. Mr. Burton indicated he feels there is
sufficient interest in moving forward with discussions with planning experts to develop a draft ordinance that would provide for
the requested use.

Council/Board Comments
         City Council Email Accounts: Chair White facilitated a conversation between the City Council and Information
Technology (IT) staff regarding what steps Council members can take to ensure they are receiving all emails that are sent to them
by constituents. IT Director Martinson provided the Council with guidance regarding how to adjust their filtering settings in their
email account to ensure that emails they should be receiving are not diverted to junk or clutter folders.
         Council Calendar: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reviewed the Council calendar for March and April.
         Housing Stability: Council member Choberka cited an email the entire Council received from AmeriCorps regarding
housing stability in the region. She would like to explore options for communicating housing support resources to the public to
help them prevent a situation that could result in loss of their home or rental property; low-income households have a lot of
optimism regarding how much their local government cares about them and she would like to work to address that challenge.


Board Business
          Discussion – Redevelopment Agency Mission and Values: Board Administrator Eller-Smith used the aid of a
PowerPoint presentation to facilitate discussion among the Board regarding the lack of adopted mission and values statements for
the Redevelopment Agency. The mission statement and values that have been published in recent Redevelopment Agency plans
and documents have been utilized by Agency staff but have not been formally adopted by the Board.
          The Board received input from Community and Economic Development Director Johnson regarding his desire to work
with the Board to modify vision statements for the Redevelopment Agency and pursue formal adoption of a mission, vision, and
values document. The Board agreed it is appropriate to formally adopt guiding principles for the Redevelopment Agency and
briefly discussed their ideas for adjusting or expanding the vision statements that have been utilized by Agency staff. Ms. Eller-
Smith stated Council staff will work with Agency staff on that matter.

         The meeting adjourned at 5:56 p.m.
