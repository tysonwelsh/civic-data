       2. PUBLIC COMMENTS/QUESTIONS.
          Cottonwood Heights resident, Ken Davis, spoke about his campaign as the
          Republican nominee for the Salt Lake County District Attorney. He expressed his
          commitment to listening to the community, acknowledging that the City houses the
          county jail and resource centers, and therefore feels the impacts of the justice
          system acutely.

           South Salt Lake resident, Haisel Stout, shared comments regarding the recent
           primary elections and celebrated the defeats of three Republican incumbents who
           supported a data center in San Juan County. They urged the Mayor and the City
           Council to publicly condemn the data center, arguing it would consume vital water
           and energy resources during an ongoing drought, and expressed frustration that
           foreign billionaires were influencing state infrastructure.

       3. MAYOR COMMENTS.
          Mayor Wood read a City proclamation recognizing America 250, observing the
          250th anniversary of the United States. The proclamation highlighted the diversity
          of the City and its commitment to building a community where everyone feels they
          belong. The Mayor indicated that the City planned to celebrate further at the
          upcoming Celebrate South Salt Lake event in September.

       5. CITY COUNCIL COMMENTS.

          everyone a happy Pioneer Day.

           Council Chair Bynum thanked the public commenters and explicitly stated on the

       6. INFORMATION.
             a. Advancing Opportunities for Adolescents
             Promise Director, Kelli Meranda, presented a new initiative funded by the
             Wallace Foundation called ‘Advancing Opportunities for Adolescents’. She
             explained that the program is a cross-sector partnership aimed at transforming
             youth development in the City and Millcreek. She detailed that the City is one of
             seven communities nationwide selected for the program, which focuses on
             youth between the ages of ten and twenty-five, with a primary focus on junior
             high and high school students. Ms. Meranda outlined the program's phases,
             starting with a design phase in 2026, a pilot phase in 2027, and full
             implementation from 2028 to 2031. She highlighted the importance of positive
             out-of-school-time settings in improving academic achievement and decreasing
             negative behaviors. Ms. Meranda also shared that the Digital Youth Network is
             administering a survey to gather feedback from local youth regarding service
             gaps.

               Mayor Wood added that securing this funding was a multi-year process and
               praised Ms. Meranda and her team for their long-term efforts in building the
               City's youth infrastructure.
