3.0   Public Hearing: Resolution RDA #19-05, amending the current Fiscal Year 2020
      Redevelopment Agency of Draper City Annual Budget.

3.1   Bob Wylie, Finance Director, explained that this was a continuation of the 11900 South
      road extension project. This item adjusted after the year-end expenditures that were made,
      and took into account that an additional $100,000 that was put back into the General Fund.
      The project would now be paid for through the RDA Fund instead.

3.2   Boardmember Green opened the public hearing, no one came forward, so
      Boardmember Green closed the public hearing.

3.3   Boardmember Walker moved to approve Resolution RDA #19-05, amending the
      current Fiscal Year 2020 Redevelopment Agency of Draper City Annual Budget.
      Boardmember Summerhays seconded the motion.

3.4   A vote was taken with Boardmembers Green, Lowery, Summerhays, Vawdrey,
      Walker and Weeks voting yes. The motion passed unanimously.

4.0   Action Item: Resolution #RDA 19-04, approval of the 2019 Annual Report.
4.1   Bob Wylie, Finance Director, explained that every year the RDA was required to submit
      an annual report. This report for State compliance was sent out to all the taxing entities
      that were in Draper’s project areas, for which the City was getting a portion of tax
      increment money. The overall report had been attached to the agenda and was available
      for public review.

      Mr. Wylie gave two quick highlights. First, there had been six project areas within the
      City. In this current year, two of them were expired: the Gateway and the West Freeway
      projects. Next year, east Bangerter would also be closing. This had a cap on sales tax and
      the City had since reached that cap this year. Therefore, between the six total areas, three
      of them would close. The three areas that were still open would have an increment of about
      $8.1 million next year.

      Secondly, Mr. Wylie provided an overview of the three project areas remaining. The oldest
      area was Sandhills and would expire in 2025. In Sandhills, there was a total of 69 acres
      that were undeveloped. The Frontrunner was a big area, as there were already 214 acres
      of developed acreage with a value of $428 million; that area was just triggered in 2016. He
      stated that this was a dramatic increase that would continue until 2035. There were still 86
      acres of undeveloped acreage for a total of 300 acres.

4.2   Boardmember Weeks moved to approve Resolution #RDA 19-04, approval or the
      2019 Annual Report. Boardmember Walker seconded the motion.

4.3   A vote was taken with Boardmembers Green, Lowery, Summerhays, Vawdrey,
      Walker and Weeks voting yes. The motion passed unanimously.
