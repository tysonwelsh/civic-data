1.     MINOR SUBDIVISION, RYAN RUDD IS REQUESTING APPROVAL OF RUDD
       ESTATES, A THREE-LOT-RESIDENTIAL SUBDIVISION LOCATED AT
       APPROXIMATELY 1642 WEST 13200 SOUTH.

Zoning Administrator, Tim Prestwich, presented the staff report and stated that the proposed
subdivision will create two additional building lots. The parcel will be divided into three lots
with one of the lots remaining with the barn. There are two existing lots on the private lane,
which will be widened to meet the criteria for four homes. The house or barn and the school
on the west side will be accessed by the driveway. There was a Conditional Use Permit on
the property in 2016 when application was made for a subdivision, however, challenges
prevented the approval from being granted. The application has remained active since that
time and staff has been involved in reviewing the request on all levels. Staff recommended
approval.

Mr. Prestwich explained that in the process of reviewing the request, a stormwater trench
was dug and currently the parking lot drains into it. Each lot will have individual drainage
and the irrigation ditch will be piped. A neighbor submitted a letter expressing concern with
the irrigation issues. One of the conditions of approval was that the irrigation ditches be
piped or abandoned.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the north property line contains an existing six-foot masonry wall,
which is located on Lot 1 as well as on the applicant, Ryan Rudd’s property, which is Lot 2.
Because the property is owned by Mr. Rudd, an agreement was reached on the shared
private lane.

Mr. Prestwich reviewed the purpose for condition number eight and stated that this may be
the only six-foot masonry wall in this area.

It was noted that the former Crane home currently is a school. When the property is removed
to create the building lots, Lot 1 will not have adequate frontage to be approved as a building
lot and will be a private parking lot. However, an agreement had not been formalized for
shared parking access. Staff requested that the agreement be formalized before the
property owner is authorized to subdivide. This was the purpose of condition number 10.

In response to a question raised, Mr. Prestwich stated that the condition of approval is that
the applicant pipe the existing ditches and coordinate with the Engineering Department. This
is the final approval. If the Commission acts tonight, staff will rely on the engineer’s plans
provided in the conditions of approval.

Mr. Prestwich confirmed that Lot 1 will not have access to the private lane. He explained
that the lane will be limited to Lots 2 and 3 as well as the existing two lots to the east within
the Beasley Farms development. He noted that what is proposed exceeds the required road
width. Chair Cannon thanked the Warrs for being diplomatic about the water drainage issue.
He approved of the action the City is taking to resolve the issue.

Mr. Prestwich identified the properties and owners on a map displayed and stated that
Mr. Rudd will act as the developer and applicant.

Commissioner James asked what will be used to prevent traffic between Lot 1 and Lot 2.
Mr. Prestwich stated that a fence is currently there and that there is no restriction or
easement right. Commissioner James suggested that the motion specify that there will be
no access between Lots 1 and 2. Mr. Prestwich explained that the residents have their own
drive approach, however, there is an open asphalt lane leading to the northeast corner of
Lot 1. A condition can be included to prevent access and require fencing to be included.
Commissioner James asked if development is allowed on the back parcel and if access
could be approved. Mr. Prestwich stated that further development would be prohibited as
only four homes can access the lane.

2.     MINOR SUBDIVISION, RICHARD NAYLOR IS REQUESTING APPROVAL OF
       MONTE MEADOWS AMENDED, A THREE-LOT RESIDENTIAL SUBDIVISION
       LOCATED AT 1608 WEST 13400 SOUTH.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the request was presented
previously as a two-lot subdivision. Because of a misunderstanding, a settlement was made
that transferred the title of the lane to someone else but left the two building lots. The
proposed subdivision will not result in additional building lots; however, it creates a new lot
that is a private lane.

Mr. Prestwich reviewed the history of the property and identified it on a map displayed. Lots
1 and 2 meet the minimum requirements. Lot 3 does not meet the minimum requirements;
however, it is for access only. Mr. Prestwich reviewed the language proposed in the plat.
He explained that typically with a subdivision for a private lane an easement is more
common. However, the intent of the proposed application is to create a parcel.

Mr. Prestwich reviewed the ordinance and stated that the owner insists that what is proposed
is required to settle disputes. The proposal was reviewed by staff who recommended
approval. Mr. Prestwich reviewed Conditions 6 and 7.

Commissioner James asked about access to the existing lots. Mr. Prestwich explained that
a lot is shared with an access and utility easement that also crosses Lots 1 and 2.

Commissioner Rushton counted four lots on the private lane, however, staff referred to three.
Mr. Prestwich explained that there are three homes currently in the development with
another buildable lot that could access the lane. The project has been approved for the four
homes to access the lane. The road includes a 40-foot access and drainage utility easement
and meets all Code requirements.

Commissioner James asked why the utility access cannot be recorded on the plat.
Commissioner Russell responded that the person purchasing Lot 2 does not want to share
in the ownership of the lane. They are splitting and will renegotiate later.

In response to a question raised, Mr. Prestwich stated that the way the plat is recorded
meets the ordinance requirements, however, the arrangement is not acceptable to all of the
parties involved. The new arrangement makes the lane a separate parcel. If approved, the
new property owner will split the parcel and individually pay the taxes.

The applicant, Richard Naylor, commented that he is trying to clean up a parcel that was
originally not intended to be part of the sale. They are correcting the issue now by returning
the road to the way it was originally and separating it from the back lots. The access
agreement will be in place before recordation.

Mr. Prestwich suggested that Condition 7 could be clarified to be more specific.
Commissioner James asked if Condition 6 could be removed from the motion. Mr. Prestwich
did not recommend it be removed since the intent is to specify that Lot 3 will not be buildable.
City Attorney, Craig Bott, stated that from a legal perspective it is problematic to create a
precedent in this circumstance.

There were no further public comments. Chair Cannon closed the public hearing.

3.    MASTER SITE PLAN, STEVE MARX IS REQUESTING APPROVAL OF A MASTER
      SITE PLAN FOR “RIVERTON BUSINESS PARK” TO BE LOCATED NEAR 12650
      SOUTH 4150 WEST.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and shared the history of the Commission’s
exposure to the report. He explained that 4150 West is a City road that is currently being
designed and built with Utah Department of Transportation (“UDOT”) funds. UDOT will
serve as the Project Manager, however, it will be a City road. The property was identified
on a map displayed and the dynamics of the property and the surrounding land use were
described.

Lot 1 contains a smaller building and has several bay doors. Previously, staff was concerned
that the bay doors did not meet the ordinance criteria. The applicant returned with a plan
including landscaping that currently meets all requirements.

Mr. Prestwich identified the phasing plan on a map displayed and explained that a remnant
parcel remains that does not currently have a building plan and is too small to be
developable.    Mr. Prestwich reviewed the PPC zone requirements and identified his
concerns. Staff recommended the applicant use the space for landscaping. The trees and
grass were determined to meet the minimum requirement.

Engineering staff recommended that the western drive approach be shifted to the north to
allow for a drive approach that allows full access for both sections of the property. The
ordinance for commercial development requires interconnectivity. Currently, the applicant
has no drive approaches and no shared accesses for the parcels to the north. However, if
this is moved to the north, interconnectivity and shared access will be achieved. There is
an additional drive approach near the Ear Nose and Throat Clinic (“ENT”) that can also be
accessed; however, it may not be desirable. Mr. Prestwich asked the Commission to decide
on the interconnectivity of the property.

Commissioner Hansen asked for clarification on the access to the property. City Engineer,
Greg Wilson, stated that it is recommended and anticipated that the applicant will move the
access to the north and that it will be shared; however, there was no recommendation on
the width. Mr. Prestwich stated that because the City owns the north piece, a portion of the
width on the business park side as well as a portion of the width on the City’s property could
be widened. The applicant desired to open the island in the most optimal place, which will
result in a loss of landscaping.

Commissioner James reported that interconnectivity is realistic when there are similar uses.
This project will involve a different vehicle type with traffic entering and exiting.
Interconnectivity is more applicable to specific situations. He commented that this is not an
appropriate site to apply it. He asked if there is anything in the ordinance that clarifies this
or if it can be determined by the City’s discretion. Mr. Prestwich stated that the ordinance
requires interconnectivity; however, it is determined at the Commission’s discretion.
Mr. Prestwich agreed with Commissioner James that the properties are different uses,
however, placing an access in this area simplifies the traffic patterns. Commissioner Hansen
asked if the median can be shortened to make a wider crossover point. Mr. Prestwich
explained that shortening the median and extending the width may accomplish what is
required. Commissioner James commented that there are ways to design the area so that
both have the left-hand turn capability and still protect the crossover.

Commissioner James stated that what is proposed will create a non-conforming parcel.
Mr. Prestwich explained that commercial development is slightly different than residential.
There is no other option for this parcel unless it is set aside as a future use. Commissioner
James asked if another person can apply for a use if it is approved and constructed, or if
they must wait for the property owners to the south to acquire it. Mr. Prestwich reviewed
Condition 5 and the requirements for the remnant parcel.

Development Services Director, Jason Lethbridge, stated that this is an unfortunate
circumstance in terms of the location of the road and the existing property lines. The
applicant is not seeking to create something that does not conform to the City’s ordinances.
Staff wanted to prevent the parcel from becoming a barrier to development or being left as
an unused parcel.

Chair Cannon asked if the detention area has specific landscaping requirements.
Mr. Prestwich reviewed the landscaping requirements in the ordinance. This was
acknowledged to be a significant observation since the property will be visible from major
roads.

The applicant agreed to be mindful of the landscaping on the southeast corner when it is

4.    REZONE, RD DEVELOPMENT IS REQUESTING A REZONE OF ABOUT 12.05
      ACRES LOCATED NEAR 11800 SOUTH AND REDWOOD ROAD.

Mr. Lethbridge presented the staff report and stated that the above matter was included on
the agenda to provide an opportunity for the public comment period to be extended. No
additional comments were received, however, via the City’s electronic system. Any emails
received were forwarded to the Commission.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that if the applicant’s proposal is denied, the Commission has been
asked to identify options. The City Council invited additional recommendations from the
Commission.

Commissioner Hartley commented that the density is too high, however, he approved of the
road going through and to the light.

Commissioner James commented that the small parcel along Redwood Road should be left
in a CPO to allow it to be more compatible with what will be developed to the north and
south. Area 1 should be assigned an SD zone and there should be a clear understanding
of a realistic wetland configuration delineation. The density should be compatible with the
existing subdivision to the south. Based on comments from the neighbors, Area 2 should
be in the SD zone but the density should be compatible with the RM-4 zone.

Mr. Lethbridge indicated that the application will come back for site approval if approved.
Commissioner Rushton asked why the access to Redwood Road at Melba Lane differs from
commercial development. He gave an example of the roadway behind Starbucks.
Mr. Lethbridge stated that it will be similar and explained that the development of the
property will not necessarily result in more traffic on Melba Lane. Commissioner Rushton
stated that traffic on Melba Lane is an issue, however, the access at Area 3 is of concern
because there is no access to Redwood Road.

Mr. Lethbridge addressed Commissioner James’ concern with the wetlands and clarified
that there is water storage in the area. The zone has the potential for the maximum number
of lots that can be accommodated on the property. If the wetland area needs to expand,
there will be fewer lots; however, if the wetland area contracts, only the prescribed maximum
number of lots will be allowed. Mr. Lethbridge commented that the wetland issue is
complicated. It is necessary to determine the maximum number of lots but it is premature
to determine how large the wetland area needs to be.

The applicant, Brook Anderson with RD Development, participated in the meeting via cell
phone and thanked the Commissioners for their time. He understood the frustration from all
parties concerning the proposed development and recognized that the Commission has
received letters from landowners. He explained that there have been efforts over the last
20 years to determine the correct use of the property. Commissioner James was concerned
about a lot that has been a challenge to determine a use based on the size of the parcel. A

Mr. Anderson believed their approximation concerning the size of the wetlands area is
accurate and there will not be any lots in the wetlands. The road alignment is not a simple
solution and there are multiple options to consider. The determination of the roads has been
turned over to the City to identify the most efficient solution.

Commissioner Hartley asked if the density in Area 3 can be reduced. Mr. Anderson stated
that the parcel is narrow and a single-family home does not meet the Code requirements. It
was noted that Area 3 is .87 acres in size.

Commissioner Hartley asked if a density reduction in Area 2 is possible. Mr. Anderson
stated that the current proposal makes the most sense but he was willing to consider it.
Mr. Anderson stated that due to traffic in the area, smaller lots make more sense. The
neighbors to the south, however, are concerned about smaller lots. It was noted that the
proposal for Area 2 was created by using the footprints from the townhomes homes directly
across the street.
