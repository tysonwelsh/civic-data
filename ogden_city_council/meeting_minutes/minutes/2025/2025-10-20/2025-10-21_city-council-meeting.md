Proposed Ordinance 2025–33 approving public art projects for Ogden City
for the Fiscal Year 2026; and
Proposed Resolution 2025–20 awarding funding for the Olympic Public Art
Mural Project and setting forth findings pursuant to Utah Code Annotated
§ 10–8–2
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding public art projects for the Fiscal
Year (FY) 2026 and awarding funding for an Olympic Mural at approximately 2450 Kiesel Avenue came before the Council
for consideration. The memo stated the Ogden City Cultural Services Division is requesting Council approval to allocate
$110,000 from the Public Art Fund for a proposed Olympic mural on Kiesel Avenue. Of this amount, $62,500 represents the
City’s base contribution to the mural itself, $22,500 is budgeted as contingency to cover potential shortfalls in partner
contributions, and $25,000 is reserved for mural lighting. The project, developed in partnership with Visit Ogden, Snowbasin,
MarketStar, and the MarketStar Foundation, is estimated to cost $210,000 in total, with the balance expected from partner
contributions and in-kind support. If approved, the mural would be designed and installed beginning in 2026, with the intent of
marketing Utah’s 2034 Winter Olympics.
          Arts Administrator Buckley summarized the memo and stated Administration is proposing two actions for Council
consideration: adoption of an ordinance and adoption of a resolution. The ordinance approves Ogden City’s Public Art Projects
for Fiscal Year 2026, which consist solely of this Olympic mural project, and will take effect immediately upon posting after
final passage. The resolution then formally awards $110,000 in public art funds to the Olympic mural, finding that the project
serves a substantial public purpose, aligns with the City’s general plan, and provides value equal to or greater than the public
investment. Together, these actions authorize the funding and formally incorporate the mural project into the City’s approved
public art program for the upcoming FY. Ms. Buckley then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide a more detailed
project overview. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Brief discussion among the Council and Ms. Buckley centered on the scope of the project and specific characteristics
of the mural site; there was also discussion about costs associated with ongoing maintenance of the mural.
          Council member Richey asked if there has been any discussion about building a sidewalk along Kiesel Avenue, given
the potential for the mural to attract visitors who are interested in taking a photo in front of it. Ms. Buckley stated that it is her
understanding that sidewalks are not required on both sides of the street, but that is something that may be heavily considered
because the art will be a public draw during the Olympic celebration. Mayor Nadolski stated there is a blind exit from the
nearby parking garage and any infrastructure improvements will require professional engineering reviews.

Proposed Resolution 2025–21 adopting the preferred street design
concept from the Wall Avenue Corridor Plan for the downtown section of
Wall Avenue, defined as 22nd Street to 27th Street
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed Wall Avenue Corridor
Plan came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Planning Division is requesting that the City Council
adopt the preferred street design concept for the downtown section of Wall Avenue (22nd –27th Streets) in preparation of the
Wall Avenue Corridor Plan.
          Jake Farnsworth representing Kimley-Horn and Associates, the project consultant for the development of the Wall
Avenue Corridor Plan, summarized the memo and introduced the preferred street design concept for the downtown section of
Wall Avenue, 22nd to 27th Streets. The preferred concept — Traffic Calming with Landscape Median and Shared Use Path —
was developed through public input, staff analysis, and coordination with the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT), and
has been unanimously recommended by the Planning Commission on June 4, 2025. He used the aid of a PowerPoint
presentation to review the concept, which includes four travel lanes with a left turn lane, a landscaped median, street trees, on-
street parking, curb extensions, mid-block crossings, and an off-street shared use path along the west side. This concept is an
important step for the future of downtown Ogden because it will guide investment, development, and transportation decisions
that shape how people experience the City’s core. The final draft of the entire Wall Avenue Corridor Plan is expected to be
submitted to and reviewed by the Council in the next few months. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the
information packet for the meeting.
          Chair White stated that she personally does not believe on-street parking should be permitted along Wall Avenue and
that other traffic calming measures should be considered. Mr. Farnsworth stated that it is a concern he has heard, and it has
been taken under advisement; consideration has been given to other options such as making the median in the road smaller to
increase the space for parking along the road. Chair White stated she still does not believe on-street parking is appropriate for
roads like Wall Avenue. Mr. Farnsworth stated that concern has been noted. This is also an issue that was raised by UDOT
when they reviewed the current version of the proposals and there are ongoing discussions with UDOT regarding this and other
matters.
          Council member Myers stated he understands Chair White’s concerns but noted that he parked his vehicle on
Washington Boulevard tonight to attend this meeting; some people prefer the convenience of on-street parking versus finding
a spot in a crowded parking lot. Mr. Farnsworth agreed and stated there are pros and cons of both on-street parking and lot
parking.
          Chair White introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2025-21, entitled:
                    “A resolution of the Ogden City Council adopting the preferred Street Design Concept from the Wall Avenue
                    Corridor Plan for the downtown section of Wall Avenue, defined as 22nd Street to 27th Street.”
          Chair White then called for public input regarding the proposal.
          Laura Lewis stated she has attended every meeting regarding this project; at the last meeting, there was a very good
turnout, and the current proposal does not represent the desires of the public. The public prefers the option of a bypass tunnel
that routes through traffic underground. The current proposal is biased in favor of desired projects. If the subject section of
Wall Avenue is narrowed, which is what has been proposed, on-street parking will be very dangerous. She feels the current
proposal will increase the likelihood of accidents and tragedies along the roadway. She also referenced potential issues
mentioned with the underground option but noted that WonderBlock has dealt with the same issues successfully and that Salt
Lake City is currently working on a similar project and she advised the Council to examine what is happening in Salt Lake to
see how they are addressing it.
          Travis Pate stated there has been a great deal of focus on this area in past studies and project plans; there is already a
pedestrian bridge at 24th Street and all that is needed is a down ramp on the west side of the viaduct to allow individuals to land
on the west side of Wall Avenue. This would require some work with the Utah Transit Authority (UTA) or the Union Pacific
Railroad, but it can be done. He feels the current plan is good, but some adjustments can be made to address current conditions
in the area that are limiting safe vehicle and pedestrian circulation.
          Paul Burt stated that once the traffic on the road is subjected to a pinch-point, traffic will begin backing up and drivers
will become more reckless. He feels a bridge over the street would be fantastic, though he had not thought of utilizing the
current bridge at 24th Street, but he wondered if other configurations could be considered to move pedestrians diagonally across
Wall Avenue to ensure that pedestrians arrive in the location they desire.
          Theresa Bramwell stated this area reminds her of 900 South in Salt Lake City where a similar traffic calming project
was recently implemented. The final outcome includes parking in the middle of the road, and it is atrocious; traffic is backed
up into intersections every day at all hours, and cars that park in the middle of the road have a very difficult time exiting the
parking spot and getting back into the lane of traffic. She feels it is the worst design that could have been imagined, and she
hopes that a similar outcome will not occur on Wall Avenue in Ogden. She also likes the idea of routing through traffic
underground instead of what has been presented tonight.
          Linda Sanchez stated she also attended some of the public meetings regarding this project and Ms. Lewis is absolutely
correct in her communication of the public’s desires for this area. The final proposal does not represent the public’s desires.
She expressed her concerns about wasting money on something that will not be the final solution for the area. On–street parking
is very problematic for the elderly population and other options for easier access to one’s final destination need to be considered.
She is also concerned about a median in the middle of the road, especially during winter months when driving conditions may
be impacted by snow and ice. She referenced the significant burden of ongoing maintenance of any improvements decided
upon for the area and asked the Council to carefully consider the issues that have been raised tonight.
          No additional persons came forward to be heard.
          Chair White then referenced comments made by Planning Manager Brierley during tonight’s work session regarding
ongoing discussions with UDOT and the need to delay final action on this proposal for a period of up to six months until those
discussions conclude and a final agreement is reached by both parties.
          VICE CHAIR HYER MOVED TO EXTEND CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION 2025-21. MOTION
WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER GRAF.
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith advised the Council of the next steps to be taken upon approval of a motion

Proposed Ordinance 2025–32 amending the Ogden Municipal Code by
amending Chapter 4 of Title 9 to remove backflow and cross connection
control; by readopting Chapter 4 of Title 9 to adopt cross–connections,
make technical changes, and to appropriately revise the Index
          A memo from the Public Services Department regarding proposed culinary water cross connection ordinance
amendments came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Ogden City Administration is proposing that the
Council approve amendments to the City’s backflow and cross connection control ordinance. As required by state code, all
culinary water purveyors are required to implement and maintain a Cross Connection Control Program. Ogden’s ordinances
regarding backflow and cross connection control need to be updated to comply with state code as well as requirements of the
Division of Drinking Water and the Utah Plumbing Code. The proposal is to repeal the existing Title 9, Chapter 4, and replace
it with the newly proposed language.
          Backflow Technician Royer summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide additional
information regarding the proposed ordinance amendments, the purpose of which is to ensure compliance with State and
Federal requirements pertaining to safe drinking water. The regulations included in the ordinance apply to backflow testers,
contractors, citizens, the City, plumbing supply companies, and inspectors. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see
the information packet for the meeting.
          Council member Choberka acknowledged the need to update ordinances to preserve safe drinking water, but she
understands there will be some financial burden for certain property owners and residents to comply with the updated
regulations. She asked if residents will be able to apply for any financial assistance to help them achieve compliance. Mr. Royer
stated the HELP program is a funding option for those who need to update their backflow devices.
          Vice Chair Hyer stated that most residents in Ogden City do not have access to secondary water, and they maintain
their landscaping with culinary water; he asked if this ordinance will be imposed on those that do not have secondary water
and, therefore, the risk of cross contamination is lower. Mr. Royer stated that many people believe the trip valves in their valve
boxes will prevent back siphonage, but many of those are not compliant with engineering standards and this is why backflow
prevention is so important. He would be happy to participate in a site visit with any resident or property owner to help them
understand their system and how to achieve compliance with the ordinance.
          Chair White introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2025-32, entitled:
                   “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Chapter 4 of Title 9
                   to remove backflow and cross connection control; by readopting Chapter 4 of Title 9 to adopt cross-
                   connections; to make technical changes; to appropriately revise the index; and by providing that this
                   ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final passage.”
          ON A MOTION BY VICE CHAIR HYER AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER BLAIR,
ORDINANCE 2025-32 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE –
COUNCIL MEMBERS BLAIR, CHOBERKA, GRAF, MYERS, RICHEY, VICE CHAIR HYER, AND CHAIR

thinking of where she will find the money to retrofit her system to be compliant with the ordinance. She then asked why the

recent tragedies that have occurred in the community that have been very difficult for the youth in the community; things like
this should put things into perspective for everyone and, for him, it reinforces the need for him to do more to make sure things
like this do not continue to happen. He then noted there are many federal employees who live in the community that are
struggling due to the federal government shutdown; he is working on a utility bill relief program for government employees
who are struggling. He will also be announcing some partnerships with non-profit partners that can improve community
resources during this time. He addressed the conversation surrounding the Wall Avenue Corridor Plan and stated that what Ms.
Lewis is asking of the City is to ignore best practices and engineering standards that have been implemented in other projects
and have yielded good results. He will rely upon factual data and will not avoid progress because the City would rather wait
for perfection. He feels misalignment is coming from people arguing over things that they actually agree upon; everyone wants
what is best for downtown Ogden and the Union Station, but that is being lost in the thought that nothing should be done until
the City can spend $200 million for an underground option. He then addressed the comments made about recycling programs
and expressed a willingness to be as transparent as possible about the City’s research and exploration into whether to continue
offering curbside recycling. He also addressed Mr. Satow and stated that he knows that he did not call him a fascist; he has
been called that word by others online regarding other issues, but Mr. Satow did not call him a fascist. He stated there is a great
deal of negative rhetoric online and he shared some philosophical views and thoughts that have come to his mind after
participating in a Circles USA Conference aimed at eradicating poverty in communities. He believes that everyone should do
what they can to help those who are struggling and that is not happening in Weber County; there are some cities in Weber
County that provide zero subsidized or affordable housing units while Ogden City has more than its share as well as homeless
shelters and a Veterans shelter. Ogden City is home to 82 percent of the County’s subsidized housing units, even though Ogden
makes up just 32 percent of the County’s population. It is not possible to pack poverty into a space and expect prosperity; the
negative impacts of homelessness have been born by Ogden City alone for far too long, but Ogden City has been maligned for
rejecting a housing authority project from an entity that did not have the City’s blessing to operate in the City. He will continue
to work with and support the Ogden Housing Authority but has encouraged Weber Housing Authority to do work outside of
Ogden City to make sure that all cities are participating. He provided employment statistics for the City to highlight the
significant resources that have been dedicated to responding to homelessness issues in the City; the annual cost of these
employment positions is $3.2 million. This does not include the additional costs spent on mitigating homelessness issues;
Ogden is doing more than any other city in the State and that is negatively impacting the taxpayers and businesses of Ogden
City. It is important that people recognize the contributions of the City’s Staff, which are made possible due to the support from
the Council and City Administration.

Council member Comments

to have those things recognized by people from outside of the community.
