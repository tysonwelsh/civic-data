Update on Short Term Rentals Presenter: D. Jacob Summers, Deputy City Attorney

Mr. Summers presented an update on the Short Term Rental discussion from a previous
City Council work session. Mr. Summers referred to a court case that was brought before the
Orem City Administrative court. He explained that there were 3 violations of the Orem City
Code: 1) Improper Maintenance of Property by violating Code 22-6-10(C) - Keeping of Animals
in violation of ordinance. 2) Improper Maintenance of Property by violating Code 22-6-8(F) -
Renting of Property in violation of Single Family Definition, and 3) Improper Maintenance of
Property by violating Appendix A of the Orem City Code - Renting of property for a
non-permitted use. In the case, neighbors provided much of the evidence about the frequency and
number of guests that were renting out this property. At times more than 50 guests. With their

help the City was able to show that the owners were in violation of the above listed codes. In the
case the City submitted that Short Term Rental is not listed in Appendix A, but the following
uses are prohibited in Residential Zones; Rooming & Boarding Houses, Fraternity & Sorority
Houses, Membership Lodging, All Transient Lodging and Hotels, Tourist Courts & Motels. The
Ruling in the Administrative Court was in favor of the City. The Court held that Short Term
Rental (the GOAT House) operations violated City Code and the Court ordered the Short Term
Rental to cease operations and that they may not operate until City regulations are adopted and
operation is in compliance with said regulations. Ms. Millett wanted to know how neighbors
were informed about the ruling. Mr. Summers explained that many of the neighbors attended
court that day and some of them testified.

Mr. Summers explained that the next step is to decide how the city would want to move
forward with any sort of regulations or regulatory scheme, if any. Mr.Summers has looked at
other cities and other ways that the city could branch out. He suggested the city could expand the
existing landlord tenant program. In the expansion theCity could adopt a licensing fee,
restrictions and requirements. Mr. Summers was asked if Short Term Rentals were legal in Orem.
Mr. Summers, in his legal opinion, says they are not a permitted use in Orem. He explained the
idea is not to prohibit short term rentals but to have them operate consciously of their neighbors
and provide safety for the renters. The city should manage in a way that is responsible and
cognizant of the residential neighborhoods. Mayor Young questioned why the process took time
to stop the “Goat House” activities. Mr. Summers explained that it takes time to collect evidence
and to bring it before the judge and City Council.

Mr. Earl spoke about the upcoming Utah State Legislature General Session and that there
is a draft bill addressing Short Term Rentals and requirements for municipalities to adopt
ordinance or regulations. Mr. Summers suggests that waiting for the Legislative Session to
conclude before changing our code might be a wise decision. After discussions with the City
Council, the direction is to track the bill but the City of Orem should continue to move in the
direction of updating our code concerning Short Term Rentals.

Independent Financial Audit - FY 2022-2023 Presenter: Brandon Nelson, Finance Director
and Jon Haderlie, Partner, Larsen & Co., PC

Mr. Nelson reported that the City of Orem has completed the Financial Audit for FY
2022-2023. He explained some key points. First, the general fund increased by $5.4 million
comparative to the prior year. Normally this increase would be sales tax, but this past year the
sales tax was lower than previous years. The biggest contributor to the increase in the general
fund is interest rates. The city went from 1% to 5% interest and that resulted in $3.7 million
dollars. One fund that is in a deficit is the facilities maintenance fund. The fund’s deficit will be
eliminated in the current fiscal year. Mr. Peterson asked about sales tax for online purchases. Mr.
Nelson explained that this is done by audit from the State Tax Commission. Ms. Lauret
commented that the city was wise and refinanced their loans while interest rates were low and
now that the interest is higher the City is benefiting from proper planning. She thank Mr. Nelson
for his management.

Mr. Haderlie recommends the City Council read the report that Mr. Nelson has provided.
They offer 3 opinions. The first opinion pertains to the financial statements. This is an
unmodified or an unqualified opinion. In layman‘s terms that is a clean opinion that means that
the financial statements are material correct. The second opinion is for the federal government.
That is an opinion on Orem’s compliance and internal controls of how Orem spent federal

money. This is a dual opinion, first, they looked at the compliance with those programs. Federal
programs have conditions on how that money is spent, when that money can be spent and if there
is matching funds required. There were no issues with compliance in this area. They also looked

City Council Minutes -December 12, 2023, (pg.6) 4 complete video of the meeting can be found at www.orem.org/meeting

at the City’s internal controls on tracking of that money and no issues were found. The third
opinion is for the Utah State Auditor. There was a federal single audit report and the compliance
report. They found no significant issues that would need to be reported. Mr. Nelson had to
complete an internal control spreadsheet outlining every compliance. No concerns with the
controls that are currently in place.

Mr. Haderlie did speak about a couple of findings from the reporting process. First, the
facilities maintenance fund is currently in a deficit position as Mr. Nelson has previously
mentioned. The requirement is that the City needs to reduce that deficit by 5% of the revenue
coming into that fund on an annual basis. The City transferred funds into that fund last year and
the city has plans to eradicate that deficit. Mr. Haderlie expects this will not be a concern by next
year. The second finding is concerning the general fund balance. This fund is required to
maintain only a 35% maximum fund balance and that’s 35% of all the revenues of the general
fund for the year. The City of Orem is currently at 40%. This is against the state code but those
funds are planned for future projects including the new city building. Mr. Haderlie stated that this
doesn’t not cause him concern.

Mr. Macdonald moved to accept and approve the audit for the fiscal year ending June

motion. Those voting yes: David Young, Jeff Lambson, Tom Macdonald, Terry Peterson, Debby
Lauret, LaNae Millett, and David Spencer. The motion passed.

New Orem City Seal Resolution Presenter: D. Jacob Summers, Deputy City Attorney

Mr. Summers is presenting the adoption of a new City seal. This seal includes the name
of the city, the year of incorporation, the Orem slogan (Family City USA) and the United States
motto (In God We Trust). Mr. Summer spoke on the inclusion of the National Motto of “In God
We Trust” to the Orem City seal. Many other cities and states have added similar mottos to flags
and seals. The courts have supported the “In God We Trust” motto being displayed on coins and
in government buildings. This motto was found to have a secular purpose. It symbolizes the
historical role of religion in our society, it formalizes our medium of exchange, it fosters
patriotism and it expresses confidence in the future. This motto was also found to have no
theological or ritualistic impact. It has spiritual and psychological value and inspirational quality.

Ms. Lauret asked if a challenge to this decision would be defendable. Mr. Summers is
confident that it is. He believes that the City is on good footing based on prior rulings and
multiple different federal district circuit courts of appeal as well as the statements made by US

Supreme Court and other establishment clause cases.
Mr. Spencer moved to adopt by resolution the new Orem City seal. Ms.Millett seconded

the motion. Those voting yes: David Young, Jeff Lambson, Tom Macdonald, Terry Peterson,
Debby Lauret, LaNae Millett, and David Spencer. The motion passed.

Historical Marker Resolution Presenter: D. Jacob Summers, Deputy City Attorney
Mr. Summers explained that this resolution is to direct the City of Orem in the creation of

a historic marker program. The contents of the resolution are to identify, preserve and create
educational material regarding the community history of the City of Orem. The Women’s and
Children’s Monument will be part of this resolution as requested by the City Council.

Mr. Macdonald asked about funds for this project, Mr. Bybee responded that the costs are
not known at this time and he would suspect that a fundraising effort may come into play at a
later date. Currently the only funds required would be for staff time and the cost to hire a design
company.

Mr. Summers answered the question of why now? The City has made efforts to preserve
Orem History in the following ways: the creation of a Historical Preservation Advisory
Commission, the sponsoring of the Orem Heritage Museum by CARE tax and support of
citizen-led efforts to educate and preserve Orem history.

Mr. Summers spoke specifically about the Women’s and Children’s Memorial. The
creation of this memorial is to honor the role of women in the history of the City of Orem. The
exact location and monument will be determined at a later date. The parcel at 1600 N is located
within the PF zone and the resolution calls for the development of plans for a landscaped open
space, installment of a historical marker and acknowledgement of future development of a well
house on the parcel.

Mr. Summers continued to speak on the proposed resolution and issues that will need to
be resolved, such as what is considered historic, the audit of needed markers and a refresh of the
historic marker design. The last part is the inclusion and installment of the Heritage Park tank
site.

Mayor Young believes the city council is going back to core values including preserving
history. Mr. Peterson would like to see this program expanded to include a larger memorial at
another more accessible location. Ms. Millett confirmed that this program will include more
locations and thanked those who brought this opportunity to the City Council. She is excited and
honored to be a part of this project. She mentioned that Quick Quack has offered support of this
memorial.

Ms. Millett moved to approve the resolution for the historical memorial for the City of
Orem. Ms. Lauret seconded the motion. Those voting yes: David Young, Jeff Lambson, Tom
Macdonald, Terry Peterson, Debby Lauret, LaNae Millett, and David Spencer. The motion
passed.

Rezone Amending Article 22-5-3(A) and the zoning map of the City of Orem by enacting

located generally at 1350 East 740 North from the R12 zone to the PD-52 zone
(approximately 6 acres) Presenter: Jason Bench, Assistant Development Services Director
Mr. Bench explained this is a current facility that is looking to rezone the property to
allow for the expansion of the facility, known as the Provo Canyon Behavioral Hospital. This
will result in the creation of the PD-52 zone. The purpose of the PD-52 zone is to allow an
existing behavioral hospital to remain and allow an additional 24-bed expansion to be
constructed. The property in the PD-52 zone shall be developed in conformance with the concept
plan. Hospital Services and Secondary Schools are permitted uses in the PD-52 zone. All other

maximum of 154 beds. Any final plat must conform to all development standards and
requirements. All development standards and site plan requirements of Section 22-14-20 shall

apply to any development in the PD-52 zone. The Provo Canyon Behavioral Hospital is
proposing a one-story addition.

their intention is to get going on this project after approval. Mr. Bench explained they will need

Considering a proposed agreement between Wright Development Group and the City of
Orem regarding development of the property located generally at 841 South State Street
(former Meadow Gold Property) Presenter: Steve Earl, City Attorney

Mr. Earl is here to discuss a proposed settlement agreement about the property commonly
known as Meadow Gold located at 841 South State Street. Mr. Earl gave a history of the
property and the Arts District where the property resided. In 2021, Wright Development
submitted a proposal for a mixed use development. After the proposal, there was a dispute about

the application. Shortly after this, there was a moratorium put on the development proposal.
Wright Development decided to sue the City of Orem. There have been discussions between
legal counsels to come up with a settlement. The proposal settlement is that the project would go
forward but reduce the height, number of units and remove the commercial area.

Mr. Bench reviewed the proposed settlement agreement elements. A maximum building
height of 52 feet and four stories with a limited exception for a rooftop structure enclosing the
stairway, elevator and lobby which have a maximum height of 64 feet. A maximum of 311
residential units. The requirement of at least 650 parking stalls. Mr. Bench showed the site plan
for the proposed development.

Mr. Earl recognizes this is a painful and difficult decision to make. This is an opportunity
to resolve the lawsuit. The decision is if we want to mitigate the lawsuit and what is in the best
interest of the City? Ms. Lauret wants to know what happens if they do not build the property in
the allotted time. Mr. Earl explained that they have three years to complete an approved site plan.
Then they have 2 years to pull the building permits.

Mr. Spencer asked if 900 South could be a one way street to stop traffic from going into
the neighborhoods. Mr. Earl replied that a traffic study will be required for the project and that
option could be looked at with the traffic study.
