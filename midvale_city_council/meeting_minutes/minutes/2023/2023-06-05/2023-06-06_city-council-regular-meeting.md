        C. UNIFIED FIRE DEPARTMENT REPORT
Chief Brad Larson said their recruits just graduated from firefighter training and are now
being assigned to crews throughout UFA. UFA has also had lateral hires, who have a lot
of experience. There are two paramedics and six fire fighters starting orientation on June
'1
   . He discussed the strategic planning session that will be held with stakeholders and
personnel. As a flooding update, the critical portion has passed, but they are still holding
media events and asking if people choose to recreate near water to designate "Water
Watchers" to prevent accidental fall ins. Battalion Chief Scott McBride has announced his
retirement. Also retiring is Chris Morrison who served in Midvale. There was also a captain
promotion in Midvale. The June safety message is with summer upon us, caution around
campfires in backyards. There is also a message regarding fireworks and when they're
allowed which can be found at www. unif iedfire. orq. The safest way to have fireworks is
to attend a professional shoot. He recognized Captain Chad Green's crew that was in
attendance and stated they will be taking blood pressures at the senior center on an
ongoing basis throughout the summer.

teachers. Council Member Dustin Gettel and Mayor Marcus Stevenson read the
proclamation declaring June as Pride Month into the record.

       A.    CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 2023.R.22 APPROVING AN
             AGREEMENT BETWEEN MIDVALE GITY AND NOLAND AND SON
             CONSTRUCTION, FOR THE INSTALLATION AND CONSTRUCTION OF
             A 20" WATERLINE AND METER VAULT CONNECTION ALONG 7OO
             WEST FROM WINCHESTER AVENUE TO 6980 SOUTH
Glen Kennedy said Midvale City (City) engaged Hansen, Allen and Luce (HAL) to
prepare a Water Master Plan (WMP) that provides direction on decisions and actions
that need to be made to the City's water system to adequately provide drinking water,
fire suppression, and emergency storage for both now and in the future. This plan was
adopted in 2020 and the City is moving forward in implementing the projects identified in
this plan.

The 700 W Pipeline and Vault project are labeled as projects 31 and 34 in the Master
Plan and will provide a source/connection from Jordan Valley Water's system on the
City's west side. Currently there are no reliable back-up sources west of State street. ln
addition, SCADA will be installed in the meter vault, which will allow for the monitoring
and control of both pressure and flows remotely and will alert staff of abnormalities or
issues in real time.

Per the City's purchasing policy, a Request for Bid was released with four respondents
bidding on the project. Proposals were weight rated on approach, scope, and cost by a
selection committee made up of three public works representatives, two representatives
from the engineering department, and one outside consultant.

Noland and Son Construction (Noland) scored the highest in scope and approach and
was the lowest bid. The selection committee seeks to award Noland for the project.

        C.      DISCUSSION OF PROPOSED AMENDMENT TO MIDVALE MUNICIPAL
                CODE 10.15.160 OVERNIGHT PARKING OF CERTAIN VEHICLES
              PROHIBITEO
Garrett Wilcox said over the last several months, several concerns have been raised
about Midvale's existing overnight parking ordinance for oversized and nonmotive
vehicles and have highlighted certain difficulties about its enforcement. One identified
problem is that some RVs, semi-trucks, and trailers have been running generators,
condensers, and other equipment after10 PM in front of residential properties along
Bingham Junction Blvd. and Center Street. While this is actionable under Salt Lake
County Health Regulation #21 Community Noise Pollution Control and Midvale Municipal
Code 8.01A.010, an officer must use a calibrated and certified sound level meter to
enforce this regulation.

These meters are not readily available to officers. As a result, officers have not been able
to readily enforce this regulation and address potential noise violations, The second
identified problem with the current overnight parking ordinance enforcement has been
with UPD staffing. Right now, only one shift of officers is on duty during the prohibited
parking period of 1 AM to 6 AM. These officers are generally responding to calls that are

                                             4L
Proceedings of City Council Meeting
June 6, 2023

regarded as being more pressing for public safety. This has resulted in sporadic
enforcement of this ordinance.

The proposed amendment attempts to alleviate these two identified concerns by
expanding the prohibited parking hours to match the quiet times identified in the Salt Lake
County Health Department noise regulation. By expanding the prohibition of overnight
parking on public streets to 10 PM through 7 AM, officers will be able to indirectly address
the noise complaints by requiring some of the worst offenders to move their vehicles
through enforcement of the overnight parking ordinance.

Additionally, by expanding the prohibited parking hours, two shifts of officers, including
dedicated motor and code enforcement officers, will be available to enforce the overnight
parking ordinance without requiring an overhaul of UPD's existing shift schedules. Staff
hopes that this minor amendment will improve the affected residents' quality of life and
improve UPD's ability to enforce this ordinance.
