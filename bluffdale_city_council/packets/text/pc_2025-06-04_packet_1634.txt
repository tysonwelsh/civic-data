13   8.     Planning Commission Training – Daniel Tracer, Staff Presenter.
14
15   Mr. Tracer conducted stormwater regulation training on Low-Impact Development, which
16   minimizes the impact development has on stormwater.
17
18   Stormwater is water that runs off the landscape during storm and melt events. Pre-development,
19   approximately one-quarter of the stormwater soaks deep into the ground, one-quarter soaks in at a
20   shallower level, and 40% is soaked up by plants or evaporates back into the atmosphere.
21   Approximately 10% of the stormwater runs off into rivers and streams. Post-development, the
22   amount of runoff increases to approximately 55%.
23
24   Traditional stormwater treatment involves collecting the water in pipes and moving it away from
25   the location and into water bodies. Issues with that method include the distribution of pollutants
26   like oil, gasoline, and trash into rivers and streams. Quantity of water is also an issue as it causes
27   flooding. Impacts include algae blooms in Utah Lake due to nitrogen and phosphorus loading
28   from fertilizers and insufficient sewer treatment from older treatment plants as was seen in recent
29   years. Those issues led to a change in philosophy.

 1   The current strategy is to reduce the impact of stormwater on receiving waters through low-impact
 2   development, keeping the water onsite, and letting it out slowly to lessen the impact on water
 3   bodies. Keeping the water onsite also keeps pollutants onsite.
 4
 5   Low-Impact Development is a strategy to mimic natural stormwater systems with natural-looking
 6   features that hold stormwater so it can be soaked up by plants and the ground and utilize filtration
 7   to remove contaminants. It can require a bigger park strip with a depression to hold stormwater
 8   and let it soak into the ground. Low-Impact Development for parking lots would be to include a
 9   depressed landscaped area between asphalt parking stalls to capture stormwater. This type of
10   development provides a large benefit without being obtrusive.           Landscaped areas include
11   stormwater storage to allow stormwater to soak into the ground, and certain plants also soak up
12   pollutants in the water.
13
14   Low-Impact Development also helps preserve open space. Developers often see landscaping
15   requirements as a hindrance, but Low-Impact Development features fulfill the stormwater
16   treatment requirement while beautifying the area.
17
18   Cluster development can also preserve open space to mimic natural pre-development hydrology.
19   Instead of developing an entire parcel, some natural features are retained, and people will often
20   pay a premium to have those features in their neighborhood.
21
22   Impervious areas can be reduced by utilizing permeable pavement and pavers in driveways and
23   parking lots. Bluffdale has successfully implemented this strategy in Vintage Park and other areas.
24   The City has also implemented grass and vegetated swales along roadways that convey water on
25   the surface rather than in a pipe below ground, which provides an opportunity for the water to be
26   slowed down and filtered out by existing vegetation. The vegetated swales on Bringhurst
27   Boulevard have been very successful. The developer was hesitant to install them, but they now
28   plan to utilize them in all future phases of Bringhurst Station.
29

31   bioretention, rain gardens, green roofs, and rainwater harvesting and reuse. Limitations of Low-

EFFECT ON MODERATE INCOME HOUSING GOALS

The City has adopted a Moderate Income Housing Element (MIHE) and associated goals as required
by State Law. As part of this Element, Bluffdale has chosen three strategies to further housing
affordability within the community. One of these strategies is to “[z]one or rezone for higher density
or moderate-income residential development in commercial or mixed-use zones near major transit
investment corridors, commercial centers, or employment centers.” Staff opines that approving
additional units in this location would strongly support this strategy due to the site’s proximity to
the future Point development and other job and commercial centers along 14600 South.

The intent of this special district zone is to provide a land use pattern that provides for a
complementary and compatible mix of commercial and residential uses. This zone allows
for the establishment of commercial, residential, and other uses consistent with a

to Porter Rockwell Boulevard, Rising Star Way, and Bluffdale Boulevard. Additional internal
