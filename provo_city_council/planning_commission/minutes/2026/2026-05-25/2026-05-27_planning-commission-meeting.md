PROJECT SUMMARY
Provo City owns and operates a water reclamation facility and a wastewater collection system.
The wastewater collection system consists of approximately 310 miles of gravity sewer
pipelines, ranging from 6‐inch sewer lines to 42‐inch sewer lines and 15 sewer lift stations
owned and operated by the city. The City’s new membrane bioreactor reclamation facility is
rated at 21 Million Gallons per Day (MGD) with a hydraulic capacity of 16 MGD.

The Wastewater Collection System Master Plan evaluates the City’s wastewater pipeline
infrastructure and reclamation facility in several criteria. The key points of the Master Plan are:

    1. Hydraulic Analysis (sewer model) – Analyze the existing system for current pipe
       capacity deficiencies and future growth to determine future capacity deficiencies, based
       on current zoning and current planned developments.
          a. Key points of concern
                   i. Northeast Area has near future sewer pipeline capacity deficiencies.
                  ii. West of I-15 has future sewer pipeline capacity deficiencies.

    2. Condition Assessment – Determine deficiencies in the current pipelines, lift stations,
       and Treatment Plant.
          a. Key points to note
                   i. Large portion of the system has older pipes that are failing.
                  ii. Treatment Plant is updated to meet new EPA standards.
                 iii. Determine Locations of inflow and infiltration to our sewer pipelines.

    3. Capital Improvement Program
          a. Addresses projects that will eliminate system deficiencies.
          b. Discusses rate increases to fund future projects in order to maintain existing
               system.

PROJECT SUMMARY
The Provo City Storm Drain Master Plan (SDMP) is a comprehensive long-range planning
document that evaluates the existing storm drain system and identifies current and future

managing stormwater based on projected land use, anticipated development patterns,
hydrology, and accepted engineering standards.

flood risk, and protect public infrastructure. Adoption of the plan will provide Provo City with a
guiding document for stormwater planning, development review, and long-term budgeting.

PROPOSAL SUMMARY
The Development Services Department is proposing a variety of amendments to the parking
code in an effort to attract redevelopment near campus and bus rapid transit stations. The
specific language proposed is shown in “Attachment 1” of this report but are all related to this
goal.

The first proposed amendment is to Provo City Code (PCC) 14.37.050, the section that
describes options for reduction in off-street parking requirements. The proposal in this section is
to allow a by-right fifty percent (50%) reduction for any new multifamily or commercial
development if the property is located within one-quarter mile of a bus rapid transit station.

The second proposed amendment is to PCC 14.37.060, which enumerates parking spaces
required for different uses. The proposal here is to remove the “Joaquin South Campus
Planning Area” language under the “baching singles” section and replace it with two simpler
standards that can apply to any new baching multifamily or mixed-use development within one-
eighth mile and one-quarter mile of BYU campus. The current standard is an overly complicated
paragraph that leaves room for variation based on what occupancy is told to the city. Staff seek
to replace that with a simple standard based on maximum allowable occupancy for this type of
development adjacent to campus, and keep the current standard for projects between the two
distances.

The third section with proposed amendments is PCC 14.37.080, General Provisions, but is
specifically seeking to amend the location standards in subsection one to allow multifamily
developments to count off-street parking spaces on a separate property, subject to certain
criteria. Off-site parking has been permitted for commercial uses for years. In effort to use
parking infrastructure more efficiently, the ability to count off-site parking for residential has
merit.

Approval of any of the proposed amendments should help to develop areas of the city that are
expected to have higher density since parking can be a financial or logistical obstacle to new

CONCERNS RAISED BY PUBLIC
 Any comments received prior to completion of the Staff Report are addressed in the Staff Report to the Planning
 Commission. Key issues raised in written comments received subsequent to the Staff Report or public comment during
 the public hearing included the following:
     • No public comment was made at the meeting

PLANNING COMMISSION DISCUSSION
 Key points discussed by the Planning Commission included the following:
    • Commissioner Wheelwright expressed concern about bringing the area from one acre to five acres based on
        reasoning that one acre does not seem likely to be able to generate a lot of profit for a property owner farming it
        but could create nuisance for neighbors. Staff pointed out that one of the requirements is that there is a reasonable
        expectation of profit.
                                                         Page 1 of 4


---

   •   Commissioner Wheelwright asked how many parcels staff would expect to apply for a protection area. Staff was
       unsure of an exact number to expect.
   •   Commissioner Lyons asked why the city was reducing the acreage requirement now and why the proposal
       removes the greenbelt tax requirement. Staff explained that the proposed text amendment of one acre is because
       that is the smallest lot size allowed in an agricultural zone. Removing the greenbelt tax benefit requirement is
       because the land size requirement to receive greenbelt tax is 5 acres.
   •   Commissioner Jensen asked about the use of the farmland assessment act, Staff explained it is a tax benefit. She
       then asked if this text amendment was approved would the five state requirements for creating an agricultural
       protection area still be required, Staff explained that they would. She then asked about a setback requirement.
       Staff explained for buildings it is based on the setback requirement of the underlying agricultural zone but there
       is no setback requirement for where animals could graze on the property. Staff highlighted that this is set up to
       protect existing farms from future development as opposed to creating a path for farms to be built in an existing
       neighborhood.
   •   Commissioner Gonzales asked if there was an application process, which staff explained there is an application
       process. Commissioner Gonzales stated that he would like there to be more restrictions on the property owner
       once in an agricultural protection area.
   •   Commissioner Jensen suggested we keep the standards in line with the greenbelt standards.
   •   Commissioner Gonzales asked if an agricultural protection area can shield owners from other nuisance complaints
       like noise if the property owner decided to host a concert. Commissioner Jensen stated that wouldn’t be allowed
       because that is not related to agricultural use. Staff agreed with her explanation.
   •   Commissioner Lyons expressed concern that if someone got an agricultural protection area established raising
       chickens for eggs but then switched and started raising goats this would have an unfair impact on surrounding
       properties. Staff explained that in an agricultural zone a property could do that already; however, as part of the
       application process for a protection area a property owner has to list the agricultural uses at the property.
   •   Commissioner Wheelwright stated that there are only a few areas where this really could apply and he is generally
       in favor of it.
   •   Commissioner Lyons disagrees stating that three acres is a reasonable size, one acre seems too small.
   •   Commissioner Wheelwright commented on having joint parcels being able to apply.
   •   Commissioner Lyons made a motion to recommend denial based on the finding that reducing from five acres to
       one acre is too drastic a reduction and is not sure that such a small farm needs additional protection. The motion
       did not get a second.
   •   Commissioner Wheelwright made a motion to recommend approval which was seconded by Commissioner
       Gonzales.
   •   Commissioner Gonzales stated that as part of the discussion on the motion he would like more information about
       why this is necessary. Staff explained that this is a tool to protect existing farms.
   •   Commissioner Jensen stated that this made her feel more comfortable with the motion when considering this is
       for existing agricultural land.
   •   Commissioner Gonzales and Commissioner Lyons expressed additional concerns about the possibility of
       landowners changing the agricultural use of the property and then the city not being able to enforce on issues.
       Staff explained that agricultural protection areas valid for 20 years, that a change of use to something not listed
       on the application would require the property owner to go through a new agricultural protection area review, and
       that even without the agricultural protection area, a property owner in an agricultural zone can change from
       growing crops to raising animals since both are permitted uses in the agricultural zone.

FINDINGS / BASIS OF PLANNING COMMISSION DETERMINATION
The Planning Commission identified the following findings as the basis of this decision or recommendation:
   • While the majority of the Planning Commission in attendance agreed with the findings of the staff report,
       Commissioner Gonzales had concerns regarding future oversight of agricultural protection areas and wanted the
       requirements to be more along the lines of a conditional use permit.
