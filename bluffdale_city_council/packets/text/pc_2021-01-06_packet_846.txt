2    Slide # 2 explained why cities have regulations and provide a basis by which the needs of citizens are
 3   best met. Approaches include the use of zoning and the creation of a General Plan. The slide included
 4   renderings of what can happen when zoning goes awry.
 5
 6   Slide # 3 described the use of a Land Use Table that specifies which uses are compatible with the
 7   different zones.
 8
 9   Slide # 4 provided a historical review of the drafting of the Home Occupation Ordinance being
10   considered. The City Council passed a “Notice of Pending Ordinance” on June 24, 2020. The City
11   then had a legal obligation to finalize the ordinance within 180 days. The process had been substantial
12   and included many different types of meetings with a wide range of stakeholders.
13
14   Slide # 5 reviewed what constitutes a Home Occupation Business, which involves the use of a
15   residential dwelling for commercial activities. Mr. Sheeran enumerated what a home occupation is
16   not. Consequently, the proposed ordinance has no bearing on activities that do not constitute a home
17   occupation.
18
19   Slide # 6 identified the purpose of home occupations, which includes the opportunity to operate as an
20   accessory use when compatible with the neighborhood; encourage appropriate forms of economic
21   and community development within a residential zone; to guide inappropriate businesses to locate in
22   commercial zones; and to protect the peace, quiet, and safety within all residential neighborhoods.
23
24   Slide # 7 enumerated a list of General Regulations. Mr. Sheeran emphasized that all regulations must
25   be met. In particular, the business owner must be a resident of the dwelling where the business is
26   being conducted.     Vehicle advertising has limits to minimize distractions in neighborhoods.
27   Deliveries must be limited to the hours between 9:00 a.m. and 6:00 p.m. Hours of operation are
28   flexible and can be from 7:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. Outdoor storage, if needed, must be screened or
29   covered so that it is away from the public view. Signage must be non-illuminated and cannot exceed
30   four square feet. Mr. Sheeran stated that yard space has been debated by staff. It will not be regulated
31   by percentage; however, it will be determined on a case-by-case basis. He clarified that “yard space”
32   refers to the use of back and side yards for the business.

11   Slide # 12 specified the allowances for employees of home occupations. Minor Home Occupations
12   would still be zero and Major Home Occupations would be one. The Special Exception would allow
13   up to four employees, provided that number does not have a major impact on the neighborhood.
14   Mr. Sheeran next reviewed the regulations that are in force in Riverton, Draper, and Sandy.
15
16   Slide # 13 addressed the allowed number of customers. Currently, for childcare businesses, if there
17   are three or fewer children, it would be considered a Minor Home Occupation. If there are four to 12
18   children, it would be considered a Major Home Occupation. The proposed numbers would be three
19   clients per day for a Minor Home Occupation and six per day for a Major Home Occupation.
20   Childcare facilities would be considered a Major Home Occupation and allow 12 children per day
21   and eight children at the same time.
22
23   The third row of the table was for Seasonal Child Group Activities. It would allow 16 per day and
24   eight at the same time. The Special Exception for regular Home Occupations would be 12 per day.
25   The Child Care Special Exception would be 16 per day and 12 at the same time. The Special
26   Exception for Seasonal Child Group Activities would be 24 per day and 12 at the same time. The
27   traffic numbers were kept a bit lower for the Seasonal Child Group Activities to foster carpooling and
28   walking/biking to minimize the impact on traffic in a neighborhood. Mr. Sheeran likewise reviewed
29   the regulations established in Riverton, Draper, and Sandy.

15   used to encourage the use of garages for the parking of vehicles. It was noted that accessory buildings

SUMMARY
The applicant proposes to create a 29-lot subdivision, amending one existing lot near 14400 South and
3450 West. The R-1-43 minimum lot size requirement is one acre. The lots meet the minimum lot size
requirement. Many lots have unique constraints because of various easements. The applicant proposes
two public access roads, providing two cul-de-sacs, connecting to the northwest corner of the
subdivision, from 3450 West, as well as from 14400 South to the south.

A public hearing is required before the Planning Commission acting as the Land Use Authority, and they
may consider a proposed Preliminary Subdivision Plat Application. This application is an administrative
action before the Planning Commission to review the compliance with zoning and ordinance
requirements. Following preliminary plat approval, the final plat will be administrative and completed
based on the conditions of approval.


ANALYSIS
Layout and Access. Lots in the proposed subdivision can be accessed off of 52’ public streets, with two
cul-de-sacs provided. Several parcels have utilities running through them which cannot be moved,
similar to Palisade Acres Subdivision, to the south. The applicant has identified buildable areas on the
lots, with non-buildable areas shown on the proposed plat.

The south side of the subdivision will be accessed from 14400 South. The road is proposed to be located
on property currently owned by Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District and Rocky Mountain Power.
Both companies will need to provide an easement for a public right-of-way for that portion of the road,
which is a condition of approval. The northwest side of the subdivision is proposed to be accessed from

3450 West. The R-1-43 Residential zone requires a minimum lot size of one acre or 43,560 square feet. All
of the lots meet this minimum requirement.

The applicant is proposing to connect to 3450 West which exists as a public right-of-way, according to
various City records and plats on file. For example, the 1990 Boulden Acres #3 plat shows a 50’ right of
way for 3450 West, and a 25’ section of road on 3450 West was shown on the 1987 Boulden Acres non-
regular approved subdivision by the City. On that plan, the 25’ section of road was deeded to the City.
West of that parcel is another 25’ wide parcel which is not shown in private ownership, according to the
County Recorder’s records. Connecting to both 14400 South and 3450 West address subdivision
ordinance requirements to not have cul-de-sacs or dead end streets be longer than 1000 feet.

Lot Requirements. The size of the lot, dimensions, setbacks, and other development standards shall meet
the requirements for the R-1-43 Residential zone.
