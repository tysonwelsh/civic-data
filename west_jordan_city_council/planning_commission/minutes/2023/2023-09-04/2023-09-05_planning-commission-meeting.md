Zo Sportsman’s Warehouse Pole Sign Relocation; 1475 West 9000 South; Pole Sign
Relocation; P-C Zone; Yesco (applicant)
Megan Jensen explained that the existing pole sign is being moved approximately 20 feet to
accommodate the 9000 South road widening. The code allows a legal nonconforming sign to be
moved/replaced with added enhanced architectural features and no increase in size or height. The sign
has to be approved by the Planning Commission. The sign incorporates a decorative aluminum cap and
a cement tile base that matches the building materials. The sign was reviewed by the Design Review
Committee who forwarded the item to the Planning Commission without any other recommended
changes.
Staff recommended that the Planning Commission approve the sign materials and design for the
Sportsman’s Warehouse sign relocation located at 1475 West 9000 South in a P-C Zone, with the
Requirements of Approval listed below.
Requirements of Approval (Requirements of the City Code and Regulations)
A. Building Permits shall be obtained for the signs per §12-2-3 of City code.
B. The overall height, sign area, and width of the new sign shall not exceed that of the existing
sign. Permit plans shall provide these dimensions for the existing sign.
& New materials applied to an existing sign shall not increase the size of the existing sign beyond
the thickness of the new materials.

4. Text Amendment — Material Change Definition and Stormwater Impact Fee

Clarification; Amend the 2009 West Jordan Municipal Code Sections 14-2-2 and 14-2-3
and add a definition for “material change” in Section 13-2-3; City-wide applicability; City
of West Jordan (applicant)

Larry Gardner said changes to State Law will come into effect in February 2024. The main change is

the number of reviews a city can do on a subdivision plat, which are two at preliminary and two at

final. However, if there is a material change to the plan sets then the review period can start again. He
reviewed and explained the proposed ordinance. The review team has to be diligent in its
determination that an application is complete prior to beginning the review. The review team will also
determine if a change is considered a material change. He provided some examples of what a material
change might be, including a change in use or a change from a development plan that was approved by
the Planning Commission. That decision can be appealed to the department head and then to district
court, if necessary.

Duncan Murray explained the amendment to 14-2-3 is a clarification to have the code match the

current practice of charging stormwater impact fees with the subdivision approval. All other impact

fees are charged at the time of the building permit.

Trish Hatch asked if there is an additional fee involved with a material change.

Larry Gardner said the application fee include two reviews of a project. Additional reviews are charged

an hourly fee per reviewer, as set in the fee schedule.

Ammon Allen opened the public hearing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

MOTION: _ Trish Hatch moved, based on the information and findings set forth and the
evidence and explanations received today, to forward a positive recommendation
to the City Council for the Text Amendment ~— Material Change Definition and
Stormwater Impact Fee Clarification as provided in the staff report. The motion
was seconded by Matt Quinney and passed 7-0 in favor.
