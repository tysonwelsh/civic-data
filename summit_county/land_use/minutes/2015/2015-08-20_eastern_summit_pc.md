Director Putt explained to the public that the Commission is trying to get to the point 
where they can agree on the proposed zoning to move it to a public hearing.  This would 
allow the public to comment.  Following that meeting, the Commission would take their 
comments into account and make a decision.  That decision would be passed on to the 
elected officials who will then make the final decision.    
 
Director Putt said when they talk zoning, there are two pieces.  There is the written 
part, which is the Code.  That is the what, the how much, and the where.  The second 
part is the map, which is the illustrated part of it.  By state law, the Code and the map 
have to be justified and come out the General Plan.  The General Plan is important 
because it is the document that explains why they are going to create the rules.  Zoning 
is the tool they are going to use to implement that plan.   
 
The General Plan is the “what” they are going to do and zoning is the “how” they are 
going to do it.  If the zoning map or an ordinance is amended, they have to go through a 
process so that it is predictable and hopefully everyone is involved.   
 
Director Putt said state law requires some pretty rigorous tests that the county has to 
meet.  A letter has to be sent out to every individual affected property owner.  In that 
letter, an explanation must be provided outlining the current and the proposed zoning.  
If there are any changes made to the zoning rules, those changes must be described and 
how the property owner may be affected.   
Before that explanation can be given, the Commission has to decide what it is they are 
going to lay out for the public.  It has to go through a public process and then ultimately 
to the Council.  This is for both the map and the Code.

If the Commission is going to change somebody’s zoning (up or down) they have to 
conclude certain things exist.  Among those are the following: 
1. The changes are generally consistent with the goals of the General Plan; 
however, that doesn’t mean the changes have to meet every goal.   
2. The changes have to be compatible with adjacent land uses and will not be 
overly burdensome on the local community. 
3. The changes have to be in compliance with all of the applicable standards and 
criteria of the zoning.   
4. The changes cannot adversely affect the public health, safety, or welfare.   
 
Director Putt said these are pretty broad conclusions.  They are broad on purpose to 
allow some latitude.  The General Plan is a series of goals and policies.  He has prepared 
a matrix for the Commission to use.  In the matrix, he has taken the policies and goals 
and put them in the form of a question.  Over the next couple of weeks, he would like 
each Commissioner to answer the questions and determine if the proposed zoning 
strategies (meaning the map and the Code) implement these standards.  There is a box 
to check yes or no and a place to add a brief explanation of why.   
 
Director Putt suggested that at the next meeting, Staff will put on the wall both the map 
and the description of all the zones.  This will include the ones they have now and the 
ones they are proposing.  Everyone will understand what these zones mean and what 
they include.  They will discuss if there are any additional lines they need to make.   
 
Director Putt further recommended that following this exercise, they will then discuss

map and the Code before they get to the public hearing process.
