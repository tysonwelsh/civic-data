following Facebook post, reminding everyone how important search and rescue 
was and to be careful:
“Salt Lake County SAR was called out for two injured rock climbers in Big 
Cottonwood Canyon. A party of two were climbing Goodro ’s Wall in Storm 
Mountain when a large rock on the route gave way. The rock fall seriously 
Injured one climber and killed the other. Salt Lake County SAR responded along 
with Unified Fire, Unified Police, AirMed, and DPS. Due to the location of the 
patients a technical lowering system was setup and the surviving patient was 
brought down to the parking lot and flown by AirMed to the hospital. 
Goodro’s is a well -traveled route that was first climbed over 70 years ago. Due to 
the wet cool Winter and Spring this season, people need to be hyper aware for 
the potential of loose rock even in well -traveled areas. Along with this risk, we 
will see snow on the trails well into the summer and cold fast flowing creeks.”

6.3 Informational Update Concerning Salt Lake County’ s 
Declared State of Emergency Due to Flooding
23-0546
Attachments: Staff Report
Presenters: Deputy Mayor Catherine Kanter. Scott Baird, Public Works 
Director. Amy McCormick, Engineering & Flood Control Fiscal Manager. Clint 
Mecham, Division Chief/Director & Emergency Manager.(10 mins)
Informational
Mayor Jennifer Wilson  thanked the Public Works & Municipal Services 
Department, and Flood Control Engineering Division whose teams have been 
Page 3 of 21
County Council Meeting Minutes May 16, 2023
working day and night to make sure the needs in the community are met.  She 
expected the next six to eight weeks to be eventful and that the County would 
have a busy Memorial Day weekend.  She encouraged residents to visit 
slco.to/runoffready  for flood information, which provides information about 
various streams, impacts in communities, sandbag questions, and all basic 
information.  She wanted to ensure everyone knew the dangers of the rivers 
and creeks during this year ’s high runoff.  Currents are swift, which means 
there is more risk.  
Mr. Kade Moncur , Director, Flood Control Engineering Division, updated 
the County on the current flood control activities on the east side.  The 
emergency contractors are on standby now, but the County will reactivate 
them as it heads into peak runoff, which will be around Memorial Day 
weekend.   Flood control crews are currently working normal and extended 
hours, but they will go to 12-hour shifts, day and night, during the heavy 
runoff period.  The hazard assessment teams are also on standby, but ready 
to be deployed.  These teams are paired with a policeman and firefighter to 
help gain access to the back of properties in the event there is an issue along 
a creek, which includes Big and Little Cottonwood Creeks.  Flood Control is 
also staging equipment along the banks on Big and Little Cottonwood 
Creeks, and in other areas, such as the debris and detention basins.  
The weather for the next 10 days is expected to be in the low to mid 80s.  
Those temperatures will bring snow down the mountains, while keeping 
creeks manageable.  When temperatures get into the high 80s and 90s, or if 
there is a rain on snow event, there will be more issues.  He reviewed the 
creeks stating Emigration Creek is winding down and running out of snow .  
Next up is City Creek for higher flows, and that peak should happen next 
week, but it should be manageable.  Flood Control is meeting with Salt Lake 
City weekly to talk about that.  This weekend and next week, flows will kick 
up in Mill Creek and Big Cottonwood Creek.  If Flood Control starts to see 
flooding, it may hold water back on Big Cottonwood Creek.  Then, there 
could be flooding into Creekside Park, but it is a detention basin designed to 
hold water.  Flood Control will work with the Parks & Recreation Division to 
get information out to the public if that happens.  Lastly, Little Cottonwood 
Creek is expected to peak in June.    
The County is working with municipalities and Emergency Management to 
get a centralized sandbag  disposal location for each city and trying to figure 
out a dumping fee to dispose of sand at the landfill.  That is being planned 
Page 4 of 21
County Council Meeting Minutes May 16, 2023
for June and July.  It is important for the public to know how to dispose of 
the sandbag correctly.  Flood Control does not want bags opened and sand 
dumped back into the creek.  The sand could be contaminated, and it would 
have to be dredged back out.    
Mr. Clint Mecham , Division Chief /Director, Salt Lake County Emergency 
Manager, stated Emergency Management has activated the Emergency 
Coordination Center to a level three, which means it has in place all 
emergency management staff and various emergency support teams, 
including public works, emergency management logistics, fire fighting, search 
and rescue, and law enforcement to help coordinate and respond to requests .  
Every Friday, those teams get together, so everyone has a clear 
understanding of what their roles and responsibilities are for the weekend and 
upcoming week.  Emergency Management has also been meeting with 
affected municipalities to help coordinate actions that might need to be taken 
and to coordinate communications so people can talk to each other, and it 
has put together prepackaged missions in case it had to mobilize the National 
Guard.  In addition, Emergency Management is working with the State ’s 
Division of Emergency Management (DEM) and its Department of Public 
Safety (DPS) and has been asked to look at potential impacts to critical 
infrastructure and how it could protect that.  Tomorrow, the State Legislature 
kicks off a special session, where it will consider bills to address potential 
funding for emergency response and recovery efforts.   
Mayor Wilson  stated the command staff has been meeting once a week, but 
it will increase that to twice a week if necessary.  It is also opening an 
operational site at Wheeler Farm for the warmer months.  
Ms. Catherine Kanter , Deputy Mayor of Regional Operations, stated the 
current Flood Control Division workforce does not have time to do 
everything, so the County has augmented it through contracts.  This will help 
mitigate County crews from being overworked.  The money the Council 
appropriated to fight this emergency will be going to things like that.
Ms. Amy McCormick , Fiscal Manager, Flood Control Engineering 
Division, delivered a presentation on the costs of the flood emergency from 
mid March to present, reviewing details for payroll expenses of $ 84,110.87, 
operating expenses of $ 364,389.91, and encumbrances of $ 1,378,060.93, for 
a total cost of $1,826,561.71.

RECITALS
A. Salt Lake County (“the County”) and City of Taylorsville (the “City”) 
are “public agencies” as defined by the Utah Interlocal Cooperation Act, 
Utah Code Ann. §§ 11-13-101 et seq. (the “Cooperation Act ”), and, as such, 
are authorized by the Act to enter into this Agreement to act jointly and 
cooperatively on the basis of mutual advantage in order to provide facilities 
in a manner that will accord best with geographic, economic, population and 
other factors influencing the needs and development of local communities.
B. The County receives funds (“TRCC Funds ”) pursuant to the Tourism, 
Recreation, Cultural, Convention, and Airport Facilities Tax Act, Utah Code 
Ann. §§ 59-12-601 et seq. (the “TRCC Act ”).  The TRCC Act provides that 
TRCC Funds may be used, among other things, for the development, 
operation, and maintenance of publicly owned or operated recreation, 
cultural, or convention facilities.
C. City requested TRCC Funds from the County to help it fund the 
project described in its TRCC Application.  More specifically, the City 
requested TRCC Funds to construct 10 pickleball courts in the City (the
