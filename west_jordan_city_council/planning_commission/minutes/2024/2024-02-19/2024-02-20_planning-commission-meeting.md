2. Utah Xtreme Cheer; 8224 Industry Circle Suite 310; Conditional Use Permit for Personal
Instruction Services; M-1 Zone; K & M Uyetake Enterprises, Inc/Kory Uyetake
(applicant) [#31912; parcel 21-31-176-012]
Kory Uyetake, applicant, said their business has been operating in Sandy for the last 15 years, and now
they would like to move to West Jordan in the proposed building, which fits their needs for cheer
instruction and training.
Megan Jensen said the proposed personal instruction service use requires a conditional use permit in
the manufacturing zone. The business will take one tenant space within the building. Activities include
coaching of competitive cheerleading. Typical hours of operation are Monday through Thursday from
3:00 p.m. to 10:00 p.m. There are nine employees and customers will range between 25 to 60 per hour.
The applicant provided a traffic circulation plan that shows vehicles entering through the north access
going to the drop off and pick up location in front of the space and exiting through the southern access.
The property is located at the end of a cul-de-sac, so there should not be much impact on traffic. She
reviewed the condition of approval, which the applicant has agreed to.
Staff recommended that the Planning Commission approve the Conditional Use Permit for Utah
Xtreme Cheer, located at 8224 South Industry Circle in an M-1 zone, provided that all Conditions of
Approval can be fully adhered to at all times.
Conditions of Approval
i. Follow the traffic control plan. For the first two weeks of every new cycle of classes, have
one designated person at both the entrance and exit of the parking lot directing traffic flow.

3. Carroh Way Retail; 2877 West 7800 South; Preliminary Site Plan and Conditional Use
Permit for a two-tenant, Restaurant, fast food (general) location; SC-2 (Community
Shopping Center) Zone; Iron Horse Real Estate/Tate Baxter (applicant) [#30729; parcel
21-33-127-100]
Wes Graham, representing the development, said this is the second time they are presenting the item
for a dual drive-through retail building. At the meeting in November a lot of community concerns were
raised. Since that time, they have met with the Applecross community and their HOA board to address
ways to mitigate the concerns. The biggest concern was the amount of traffic onto Carroh Way and
difficulty turning onto 7800 South. They propose to mitigate this concern by removing the bollards at
the dead end on Dornie Lane to provide a second location where the residents can exit. Cal Ranch is
the owner of that lane, and they have agreed to allow access. Another concern dealt with potential
odors and impacts from the dumpster. The proposed mitigation is to construct a taller than required
dumpster enclosure that will be fully enclosed and a lid to block smells. Potential light pollution from
cars entering and exiting the property was a concern especially for the homes directly south of the
property. The lighting plan has been changed so that no lights are facing south, and hedges of at least
headlight height will be installed to mitigate the possibility of headlights shining onto the adjacent
properties. The last thing they have agreed with the community members to do is to install new signage
at the entrance to the community on Carroh Way and Dornie Lane stating that there is no outlet. This
will notify commercial customers that there is no access through the neighborhood. With these
arrangements they have made with the HOA board they feel that they have addressed the major
concerns, and the project is ready to be presented for approval. Regarding road maintenance, currently
the property south of the bollards is maintained by the HOA and north of the property line is Cal
Ranch, and that is how it will remain. They will share the existing public access easement that is on the
property. The retail development is part of the HOA, and they will participate in the maintenance as
stated in the HOA documents.
Tayler Jensen explained that this item was able to return to the Commission because the motion to
approve in November failed and no official action was taken. The applicant took that opportunity to
address mitigation of the concerns. He provided a diagram that better explains how traffic circulation
will work for the dual drive-throughs. He showed the updated landscape plan with the hedge row, and
he pointed out that there is already a landscape buffer and 6-foot wall that will remain. He reviewed
the three detrimental effects that were raised at the last meeting and the proposed mitigation: A. Access
to the lot due to proximity of the intersection at 7800 South. The access from the subject parcel will be

Paul Brockbank said the requirement for the bollards was due to a fire code that says the development
cannot have more than 30 lots on a single access. At the time of the subdivision approval, the
developer did not want the connection at Dornie Lane, so the limited access was accepted. He said, as
the Fire Marshal, that he would actually prefer that the bollards be removed.

Ammon Allen asked if snow removal would block that entrance even without the bollards.

Paul Brockbank said it will have to be worked out to see where snow can be pushed so the access

George Winn opened the public hearing.

George Winn referred to an email that was sent to the commission today from the traffic engineer that
reevaluated the findings and the statement was that the additional traffic would have minimum
conflicts between existing and proposed traffic. There are many who have subjective opinions about
traffic in the area, but these findings are based on an objective statement.

George Winn acknowledged that a comment was received from resident Byron Johnson stating he still
had concerns about traffic and parking.

Cindy Chatwin, West Jordan resident, said even if signage directs customers to make a right turn, they
will still go through the neighborhood if the bollards are removed. This will put their children and pets
at risk. There are going to be cars stacked in the mornings wanting to exit and they will figure out that
they can go through the other side. Even UDOT told her that it was poor planning when the project
was originally built.

Cody Jenkins, West Jordan resident, said he and his wife Haley live directly south of the proposal.
They were grateful that the applicant listened to a lot of the concerns, especially providing bushes and
improvements to the garbage disposal. However, the proposal hasn’t done enough to mitigate the main
traffic concern. The only solution presented will open an additional way that allows people to go
through the subdivision. He said that people won’t pay attention to small signage, but they will just
want to use whatever is the quickest way to get in and out.

Court McMullin, West Jordan resident, speaking remotely, said opening of the bollards will increase
traffic through their development. He said the left turn lane can usually only hold three vehicles, which
they often exceed in the morning. It will be difficult for people to get into the business when cars are
stacked in the left turn lane. There is not a way that there will not be traffic congestion on Carroh Way,
even if the bollards are open. He expressed appreciation for what the developer has tried to do to
mitigate other issues, but there is no way around the traffic issue.

Clifford Stevens, West Jordan resident, said he lives close to the bollards. He said adding the
additional ingress and egress would be helpful, but that access to 7800 South is already very busy with
traffic from Arby’s and the daycare. Some parents park in the Cal Ranch area and walk with their
children to the daycare so they are crossing traffic, which will increase the safety risk to children. The
preference of the community is for something that does not generate so much traffic.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

Ammon Allen asked the applicant to address the comments regarding snow removal and what the

conversations with the HOA have been like specifically regarding traffic.

Wes Graham said they met multiple times with the HOA board and discussed what could be done to

prevent cars from entering into the community. They considered installing gates at their expense, but it

was decided that gates would open more issues than they would solve. The only reasonable solution
was to install signage stating that there was no outlet through the neighborhood. They are willing to
install as much signage as needed to make it clear. They will be sure to address the snow removal

Ammon Allen said he was against approval at the last meeting, but he appreciated how the applicant

had looked for solutions and engaged with the HOA. He read through the traffic study and appreciated

that staff conducted trip counts. He noted that this property was specifically set up to be a commercial
use when the subdivision was approved and there is no other alternative to exit onto 7800 South. He

and the applicant has done their due diligence and shown that this is viable.

Trish Hatch agreed and thanked the applicant for working with the community. This is a tough spot to

develop, but it was originally planned as a commercial use. She has experience living in a small HOA

community and it is challenging to make things work between residential and commercial. She liked
the proposal.

Jay Thomas stated to the public that this property was already designated as a commercial area and the

commission’s job is to mitigate what they can. The applicant had presented mitigation, and the

commission considers if the proposal meets the law and the intent of the zoning. He appreciated the
comments made tonight and previously in November.

George Winn said when considering a conditional use permit, it is the duty of the Planning

Commission to identify potential detrimental effects and to consider any remedies offered. If that has

been done, then they are obligated to approve the application. As far as he could tell there were no

additional detrimental effects identified by the comments tonight.

5. Jones Landing North; 6835 West New Bingham Highway; Future Land Use Plan
Amendment from Parks and Open Space to Southwest Quadrant, Rezone 52.4 acres from
A-20 (Agriculture 20-acre lots) and M-1 (Light Manufacturing) Zone to SWQ Mixed Use
(Southwest Quadrant) Zone, and Master Development Agreement; Gardner Group/Ben
Seastrand (applicant) (#31043, 30753, 30948; parcel 26-10-100-007]
Ben Seastrand, applicant, representing the Gardner Group and Mike Jones, expressed gratitude to
everyone involved for getting to this point. They are taking a high-level look at the best way to develop
the uses in the Southwest Quadrant zone and how to integrate those uses. They will come back for site
plans approvals and the master development agreement. This area should provide a strong commercial
area for jobs while being a good neighbor with the existing community by creating some buffering.
Tayler Jensen explained the three applications. A future land use map amendment requests a change
almost entirely to the southwest quadrant and a strip of park between Echo Ridge subdivision and the
development, a rezoning from A-20 agriculture and M-1 manufacturing to SWQ mixed use, and a
master development plan for 19.2 acres in park and residential with 17.4 du/ac, and 34.2 acres in office
warehouse/flex space and a gas station location. The residential component will be stacked flats with a
strip of park to the east. Parking for the stacked flats will be interior to that component. More than 70
parking stalls are provided exclusively for the park. The park will be built by the developer and then
turned over to the city as a public park for the city to maintain. One of the biggest concerns they
anticipate from the residents is traffic going through their neighborhood. However, the traffic impact
study shows that won’t be the case. He illustrated the more likely route to be taken to New Bingham
Highway, which is with the new connection and not through the existing neighborhood. They
anticipate the existing neighborhood will use the new connection. The Southwest Quadrant zoning has
added design standards that will be applied to the industrial buildings. He showed examples of
architecture for the commercial/industrial and stacked flats project. He reviewed parking for the
apartments and for the park. Road projects in the future will increase connectivity to this area.
Based on the analysis contained in the report, staff recommended that the Planning Commission make
a recommendation of Approval (positive recommendation) to the City Council for the proposed Master
Development Agreement, General Plan Amendment, and Rezone.

Tayler Jensen said the Southwest Quadrant Mixed Use zone is exempt from the balance housing
ordinance. This is partly because it provides workforce housing for the industrial areas and lifestyle
housing for different life stages. It is mass-planned on a large scale with several thousand acres overall

Duncan Murray provided an explanation of the differences between a master development plan and a

6. Discussion - Proposed Amendments to the Planning Commission Bylaws and Rules of

Procedure

Duncan Murray said the Planning Commission can review and consider changes to their bylaws as

needed. He had initiated some changes, which he reviewed. Most of the changes deal with issues of tie

and failed votes.

There was a discussion regarding how if an inverse motion would have been promoted in November

for the Carroh Way project, then the applicant would not have had the opportunity to work with the

residents and come back tonight. Several suggestions were given for that type of situation, which Mr.

Murray said could be reviewed at the next meeting. The important thing is to be cognizant of all of the

options and to provide clarity and closure. The proposed changes leave some flexibility.

There was a discussion regarding why in tonight’s meeting the public hearing allowed for residents to

make the same comments they did at the previous meeting. If the application and key elements are

virtually identical then they legally don’t need to take comment. If it is substantially different or if
there is a significant passage of time, it is a good idea to have a public hearing. This item had some
newly proposed solutions, so it is best to err on the side of more due process. The public could be
encouraged to only give new comments.

Ammon Allen liked the clause with up to 90 days without additional action and then it is considered

denied.

Trish Hatch said she was happy with the changes.
