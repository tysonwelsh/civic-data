>» Proposal to Amend County Boundary with Weber County — Craig Sears, Real
Property Business Manager at Weber State University (WSU) indicated land on the
border of Cache and Weber Counties was donated to the University in 1987. Some 2.5+

acres are in Weber County (#23-012-0109) and approximately one acre is in Cache
County (#16-001-0011). WSU desires to sell the property. WSU requests that Cache
County authorize a minor adjustment to the county line so that #16-001-0011 will be in
Weber County to facilitate its marketing and sale. WSU understands the Utah code
17-2-209 and is willing to pursue the requirements.

Council members White, Worthen and Zilles are not in favor of the boundary change.

ACTION: Motion by Council member Zilles to table the discussion until more information
is available. Ward seconded the motion. The vote was unanimous, 6-0. Erickson
absent.

To: Cache County Council, Executive and Finance Director
From: The Cache Daughters of Utah Pioneers Museum Board

RE: 2016 RAPZ Allocations and the Cache DUP Museum

The Cache Daughters of Utah Pioneers Museum is asking the County Council to extend the use of RAPZ funds
for this fiscal year due to circumstances beyond our control. The funds were allocated to add a new subpanel
and install internal electrical metering for the DUP in the Cache Chamber building where the museum is
located. We are very disappointed that we have run into a major roadblock in using the funds in a timely
manner when they are extremely critical to both our current and long-term operations.

The Cache Chamber of Commerce, which owns the major share of the museum building, has informed the
museum board and the contracted electrician that we must wait for the results of the lawsuit that they have
filed against Sharon Johnson before the electrical work can begin. The electrician was present in the building
with his staff, ready to begin the job when the Chamber staff sent him away.

The RAPZ request was written to solve the portions of the lawsuit, and the funds will take care of the
underlying issues, however the timing has now become a problem. You may ask, why is the Chamber suing
Sharon Johnson, the Board Chair of the DUP Museum? It is a bit complicated but the following is a brief
summary.

* The Cache Daughters of Utah Pioneers Museum has shared a location with the Cache Chamber of
Commerce since 1966. Originally, the two entities shared the construction and operating costs of the
building they shared on 200 North in Logan City. The DUP contributed 40% to the building’s construction and
paid for their own operating costs with separate utility meters. A change was made to transfer DUP’s
ownership interest to a 99 year rent free lease in lieu of ownership that included information on sharing costs
of the building. In 1990, Cache County wanted the land where the museum was located. The Cache
Chamber moved to the present building and the County provided financial assistance by purchasing 20% of
the current building and the Chamber purchased the remaining 80%. The DUP moved as well since they had a
lease guaranteeing occupancy for another 75 years. The County still owns 20% of the building but has no
current management interest that we are aware of.

¢ The DUP was given space in Chamber building but it was not wired for separate meters as the previous
building had been. This has led to many years of contention between the Chamber and the DUP. A lease
addendum was hastily adopted in 1990 to address concerns made in the move but it was unclear and subject
to many interpretations. The DUP Museum board has been paying an annual assessment to the Chamber for
over 25 years for operating costs, and the amount has not been satisfactory to the Chamber.

* In May 2015, Sharon Johnson and five other DUP members replaced the prior DUP Museum board and
approached the Chamber about resolving these differences by clarifying the lease and determining actual
costs incurred by the DUP. In July of 2015, the Chamber responded to these efforts by suing Sharon Johnson,
an individual member of the board, for all costs and damages the Chamber felt they were owed.

* In June of 2016 the Cache DUP Museum was awarded $5000 in RAPZ funds to separate our utilities from
those of the Cache Chamber of Commerce. The Chamber and DUP both wanted separate utility readings and
the technology now exists to do that with internal metering.

The Chamber was notified in July of 2016 that funding was now available to do that work and the
DUP arranged the electrician to do the work. As time passed, the original electrician had other
obligations so the Chamber and Sharon Johnson met with two additional electricians to review the
work needed for separating the utilities.

The DUP contacted the electrician, Eclipse Electrical, about starting and finishing the work before the
RAPZ deadline. Dave Buttars from Eclipse Electrical came to the building on Feb. 13,2017 to begin
the work and was told by the Chamber that no changes to the electrical system would be allowed
until the lawsuit is settled. This is a problem for the DUP.

© A quick resolution of the lawsuit has not been possible due to the actions of the Chamber. There have
been four hearings scheduled that were all postponed by the Chamber. The first hearing was scheduled in
June 2016, then delayed to August, then to October, then January 3 of 2017 and finally the fifth scheduled
hearing took place on Jan 26, 2017. The judge has not yet ruled on the January hearing but stated he will do
so by March 26", At that time, he will rule on whether the Chamber should have sued Sharon Johnson, an
individual, instead of the entire DUP board and its International governing entity. He will not rule on the
concerns underlying the lawsuit.

¢ The current lawsuit (Motion for Summary Judgment) states that the DUP is to be evicted from the building
and space that is owned by Cache County because liability insurance was not paid by the DUP. The DUP has
liability insurance and it was in place when the Chamber filed their motion. The DUP occupies the 20% of the
building owned by Cache County as stated in annual tax abatement documents. The County provides a 20%
tax abatement to the Chamber for the portion of the building occupied by the DUP which is a 501 c3
nonprofit entity. The Chamber has previously expressed their desire to use that abatement for their general
purposes, evict the DUP, and then charge rent to a new tenant.

°The Museum Board has approached the Chamber many times with the solutions to resolve the basic
differences between us. At this point we are not that far apart on the terms of the lease and the DUP has
sought for mediation; however, the Cache Chamber has refused that option. The current DUP board has
earnestly tried to clarify the lease and solve the dispute to prevent future contention.

We realize that this narrative is lengthy but the County is indirectly involved in the matter as the County owns
20% of the Chamber building and the County authorizes the abatement of the building. The position of the
Chamber is having a direct negative effect on the use of County RAPZ funds by the DUP Museum and could
jeopardize future RAPZ funds for the DUP Museum.

This letter is a request to hold the funds for the DUP until we reach a resolution with the Chamber. It is also
an update to the lawsuit information that was previously given to Craig Buttars, James Swink, Kathleen
Howell, Greg Merrill and Cameron Jensen when the suit was first filed in July of 2015.

The DUP Board feels that we provide a valuable resource to the community and that the Museum is worth
supporting. The Cache DUP Museum has made great strides in the last two years to improve its exhibits,
increase the hours of business and obtain state funded museum grants and private donations to manage our
operations. The museum has undergone a transformation since 2015 and made a positive impact to
downtown Logan and the residents of Cache County. Visitation has increased significantly from local
residents and tourists. The DUP board has also created connections with trained museum staff from the
State of Utah and Cache community partners, including USU, to increase opportunities for education and
growth.

We ask the County to please consider our request to extend the use of the RAPZ funds. The DUP is grateful
for the previous support we have received from the County as we continue to operate our museum which
promotes the history of Cache County and its early settlement. Please feel free to contact Sharon Johnson if
you have additional questions.
