DISCUSSION REGARDING THE FISCAL YEAR 2021-2022 BUDGET:
     Mayor Pike explained that city Manager Adam Lenhard began having this budget
     strategy session a couple of years ago. This is an opportunity for the Council to
     voice their opinion on what their priorities are.

      Councilmember Hughes mentioned Councilmember Randall’s comments regarding
      the next fiscal budget. Her priorities include Police and Fire.

      Mayor Pike also mentioned that Councilmember Randall’s concerns include staffing of
      firefighters on each engine. She also commented to him that the planning
      Department needs to be staffed enough to help the builders as well as staffing in the
      Legal Department.

      Councilmember Hughes noted he has heard from some citizens who comment about
      staffing in the Building Department, not complaints, just things are moving slow.

      Community Development Director John Willis outlined staffing in his department.
      After being asked by Councilmember Larkin, he stated it takes approximately a
      month to get a permit for a new home. He spoke about what workflow can be
      outsourced.

      City Attorney Shawn Guzman explained state law has changed to require all cities to
      review a residential building permit within 14 days of submittal. Staff works hard to
      get the reviews complete. If there are redlines on the application, staff can return it
      to the applicant which starts the clock over.

      Public Work Director Cameron Cutler outlined staffing his department.

      Mayor Pike asked Mr. Cutler to reassess staff and request additional positions, if
      needed.

      Councilmember McArthur asked if resources can be put into the master plan as he
      believes this is crucial for the community.

      Mr. Willis spoke about how the master plan update will include stakeholder groups.
      The consultants are currently providing guidelines on who should be included in the
      groups.

      Councilmember Hughes commented one of his priorities is transportation. He stated
      3000 East as well as other roads in the Little Valley area need some improvements.

      Mr. Cutler provided an update on the road improvements in the Little Valley area
      that will be completed in the current fiscal year and future improvements.

      A discussion took place on additional transportation needs in the Little Valley and
      surrounding areas.

PUBLIC HEARING/AMEND FISCAL YEAR 2020-21 BUDGET/RESOLUTION:
     Public hearing and consideration of a resolution to review and approve
     amendments to the Fiscal Year 2020-21 Budget.

      Budget and Financial Planning Manager Deanna Brklacich outlined the proposed
      amendments as follows: 1) To reinstate a Full-time Collections Officer position that
      was converted to Part-time in Fiscal Year 2016 as increasing numbers of collections
      accounts and workload has surpassed projections. This will increase the number of
      Collections Officers from 1.5 to 2; 2) To budget for 5 new police officer positions (4
      Police Officer I's and 1 Sergeant), 5 vehicles, and associated equipment and
      uniforms. The amount represents funding for 6 months of the current fiscal year
      from January 2021 to June 2021; 3) To budget for employee market and merit
      increases for full-time employees effective in December 2020 as compared to original
      budgeted amounts for General Fund departments; 4) To budget for anticipated
      employee market and merit increases for part-time employees as compared to
      original budgeted amounts for General Fund departments; 5) To increase the
      Dispatch Center's budget to provide for tables and chairs for the Emergency
      Operations Center/training room and cabinets for the supervisors room at the new
      911 Center (Dispatch Relocation Project). Funding will be provided from reserves
      accumulated in past years from net revenues over expenditures received specific to
      the Dispatch Center. The Dispatch Center's reserve funds are restricted for
      these types of projects; 6) To increase St. George Golf Club's budget for the
      emergency replacement of a greens mower which was over 20 years old which failed
      and parts are no longer available for repair. The purchase is covered by Green Fees
      and Cart Fee revenues which are anticipated to finish higher than budget by the end
      of the fiscal year; 7) To increase the Police Dept. budget for a new federal 2020
      Justice Assistance Grant (JAG) to be used towards: 1. Active Shooter Response
      equipment, 2. training for Field Trainers and Fitness Program Development, and 3.
      command boards for Patrol Supervisors; 8) To budget for reconstruction of the #1
      Point fairway at the Sunbrook Golf course in order to correct drainage and flooding
      problems. This project was approved in the prior year’s budget but postponed due to
      COVID19 and is requested to start in January 2021 to take advantage of the slower
      months for the golf course such that interruption to golfers is mitigated; 9) To
      budget for our final allocation of State Coronavirus Relief Funds to assist the City
      with mitigating and responding to the current COVID-19 pandemic. We are working
      on finalizing the FY2021 allocation with Public Safety wages & benefits, the City's

      Small Business Relief Grant program, and the Economic stimulus relief loan program
      administered by the St. George Chamber of Commerce; 10) To rollover COVID19
