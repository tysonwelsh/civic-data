MONA CITY NATURAL GAS GATE STATION AGREEMENT APPROVED:

  Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the Council that Nephi City through agreement with the Juab Rural
Development Agency, operates the main line natural gas system that feeds the Nephi City, Mona City,
Levan Town, and Rocky Ridge distribution systems. As part of that system, Nephi City also maintains the
gate stations that feed these distribution systems. Each year all distribution systems undergo a state
inspection, and it was important to each of these entities to clearly define where Nephi City
maintenance ended and where other distribution system maintenance began.
  A draft agreement between Nephi City and Mona City was presented to the Council that clearly
defined liability roles and established maintenance responsibilities. Mona City had already signed the
agreement.
   Councilor Skip Worwood moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the agreement with Mona City.
Councilor Parady seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

RESOLUTION 03-19-2024 -ON-CALL COMPENSATION POLICY ADDED TO THE PERSONNEL POLICY :

   A newly revised Personnel Policy and Procedures document along with Resolution 03-19-2024 was
presented to the Council. The revised Personnel Policy included a new On-Call Compensation section
that replaced the previously titled Stand-by Compensation section. The new section clarifies what the
pay is for on-call status and adds an additional two hours of compensatory time for each full weekend
an employee is on-call.

MASTER FEE RESOLUTION 03-19-2024-A ADOPTED:

  Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the Council that in the 2024-25 Biennial Budget, it was assumed that
there would be a $4.00/month increase in the city’s sewer rates. It was anticipated that there would be
several sewer projects forthcoming and rates would need to increase to cover the costs for these capital
projects. These projects include:
             Sewer lining or replacement of older sewer lines. This includes the sewer mainline that
                runs through the airport and under the runway. The Nebo Heights project is also
                included.
             A new primary sewer collection cell with space and infrastructure for headworks. A
                headworks is a machine that gathers larger materials that have made their way down
                the sewer lines and extracts them from the main flow of wastewater.
             Power lines to the sewer pond facilities to operate the headworks and eventually,
                aerators. Aerators increase the oxygen flow to the ponds and increase the number of
                helpful bacteria that break down solids.
             Lining replacement for the third sewer collection cell. It appears that the third cell may
                be leaking and will need a new liner system to prevent additional leeching into the
                soil. The state is aware of this issue, and they are encouraging a fix in the near future.

   Mr. Atkinson also stated that the Solid Waste Collection utility had seen some increases in costs for
larger equipment. The latest garbage truck purchase was a 48% increase over the cost of a truck
purchased 4 years ago. Rates had been calculated to replace trucks every 4-5 years with an assumption
in cost increases. It was not anticipated that inflationary cost increases would be as high as they have
been. An additional rate increase of $.60 per can for both primary and additional cans was
recommended.
   He also recommended that the police report copy fee be increased from $6.00 to $10.00. He
presented Resolution 03-19-2024-A that would make the following fee changes:
