Recommendation 
Staff recommends that the Planning Commission review the proposed amendments to the 
Eastern Summit County Development Code as a work session item and provide direction.  
 
Proposal 
Staff are requesting feedback from the Planning Commission on a Code Amendment to the 
Eastern Summit County Development Code.   
 
This proposal would: 
• Create definitions in Appendix A (Definitions) for several uses related to addiction 
recovery including: 
o Recovery Residence 
o Residential Support Program 
o Residential Treatment Facility 
o Social Detoxification Facility 
o Substance Abuse Treatment Program 
• Add the uses to the Use Table in 11-3-6 of the Eastern Summit County Development 
Code.  
• Create specific criteria for each use in Chapter 11-6 of the Development Code.  
 
Background 
Recently the County accepted an application for a Conditional Use/Master Planned 
Development for the Hope River Recovery Ranch, proposed to be in the AG-20 zone between 
Henefer and Echo. As part of the preapplication process, the applicant was informed that the 
proposed Use did not fall under any existing definition in the Development Code. The applicant 
responded that they would like to apply for a code amendment to make the use allowed. Staff 
25
2 
 
countered that it would be better if the amendment were initiated by the County since it would 
be applied County wide.  
 
Discussions with representatives from the County Health Department indicate that these 
facilities are needed and necessary on both sides of the County. The crux of the issue is making 
sure that the various uses are distributed appropriately and that safeguards are in place to 
ensure any impacts of the uses are mitigated.  
 
Staff is requesting a discussion and direction on whether the Planning Commission would like to 
proceed with this Code Amendment. Staff has included the Definitions used in Utah State Code 
26B-2-101 as Exhibit A. If the Planning Commission is in favor of the proposed amendments, 
Staff will return with a draft of the proposed definitions as well as criteria specific to the new 
Uses.

1. Recovery residence: a home, residence, or facility that meets at least two of the 
following requirements: 
a. provides a supervised living environment for individuals recovering from a 
substance use disorder; 
b. provides a living environment in which more than half of the individuals in the 
residence are recovering from a substance use disorder; 
c. provides or arranges for residents to receive services related to the resident's 
recovery from a substance use disorder, either on or off site; 
d. is held out as a living environment in which individuals recovering from 
substance abuse disorders live together to encourage continued sobriety; or 
i. receives public funding; or 
ii. is run as a business venture, either for-profit or not-for-profit. 
Recovery residence" does not mean: 
e. a residential treatment program; 
f. residential support program; or 
g. a home, residence, or facility, in which: 
i. residents, by a majority vote of the residents, establish, implement, and 
enforce policies governing the living environment, including the manner 
in which applications for residence are approved and the manner in 
which residents are expelled; 
ii. residents equitably share rent and housing-related expenses; and 
iii. a landlord, owner, or operator does not receive compensation, other 
than fair market rental income, for establishing, implementing, or 
enforcing policies governing the living environment. 
 
2. Residential support program: a program that arranges for or provides the necessities of 
life as a protective service to individuals or families who have a disability or who are 
experiencing a dislocation or emergency that prevents them from providing these

b. psychological; 
c. developmental; or

e. a recovery residence; or

The mission of the Hope River Ranch is to Empower Individuals and Families in thefight against addiction.  Hope River Ranch (HRR) stands apart in the world of addiction treatment through our unwavering commitment to evidence-based medicine, compassionate care, and a comprehensive, family-centered approach.  This will be a renowned treatment center for addiction that will bring individuals from all over the country to experience healing in a beautiful nature setting.Tax BenefitsHRR will bring an increase in tax revenue to the community. This increase will provide the community with the opportunity to make improvements such as schools, green space, public transit, and health care. Employment OpportunitiesThe operation of HRR will require many employees of varying skill levels and experience. Job opportunities will include health care providers, mental health therapists, administrative staff, group facilitators, activity supervisors, recovery technicians, nutrition specialists, a chef, maintenance technicians, fitness instructors, social workers, grounds maintenance, equestrian staff and more. HRR will create opportunities for community members to work near their homes and participate in a fulfilling career.  These jobs will also attract good people to the community who work hard and care for the welfare of others.Community Economic BenefitsHRR will benefit the local community in its purchase and use of goods and services.  The Services needed will range from: Grocery, catering, Gas, vehicle maintenance, window cleaning, Florist, basic repairs, and activities.  Along with serving the patients at HRR, their families will be a big part of rehabilitation.  Hotels, Restaurants, and other hospitality services will see an increase in businesses. Community PartnersHRR will bolster community involvement through volunteer work, charitable donations, and participating in community festivals and events.  HRR will also participate in local sponsorship of community groups and will work with local school district to support education for substance abuse prevention.  A priority of HRR will be to maintain the beautiful environment. The property will include an outstanding mountain lodge, equestrian center, and small cabins.  The surrounding land will be improved but maintained in its natural state as a large open mountainous region. Wildlife will flourish and add to the natural beauty. Equestrian Center HRR will have an equestrian barn and riding arena with horse stalls on-site.  Equine therapy will be integrated into the treatment program at HRR.  The riding arena and boarding stalls can be rented out to the community as there is a need currently.Future Development Opportunities Recreation center with swimming pool, basketball courts and other sports areas to support community sports programs and fitness/recreation.HRR will also offer Individual cabins for nightly rentals.Not a Detox CenterHRR will not include a detox center.  This means all patients participating will have already attended a certified drug detox or medically managed withdrawal program, if required.

Summary 
Today we will be discussing how to use the recently completed visioning plan in the creation of 
an updated Eastern Summit County General Plan.  The current General Plan was adopted in 
2013.  The intention of updating the Eastern Summit County General Plan is to create a plan 
that is representative of the community and that is a highly usable guide for decision making. 
The final plan will be actionable, clear, and community directed.  
 
In the fall of 2022, the County began a year-long process to engage community members with 
the hope of identifying a clear vision for all of Summit County through Our Summit Community 
Visioning. The resultant vision statement, as adopted by the Summit County Council, is as 
follows: 
Summit County embraces growth thoughtfully and navigates change with integrity by 
upholding community identities through proactive planning.  Decisions that affect our 
natural resources, lifestyle, unique identities, communities, and future built 
environments are made responsibly to ensure communities remain affordable, healthy, 
and inclusive to current and future generations.   
 
Over the course of the visioning process, we gained insight into how Summit County wants to 
move forward collectively, and we began to identify ways in which communities within the 
County may wish to proceed. One ‘next step’ coming out of the visioning process is to update 
both the Eastern Summit County and Snyderville Basin General Plans. As staff and planning 
commissioners, our task is to continue refining information in order to identify how we can 
reach our collective vision for the future.   
 
Data collected during the visioning process is invaluable.  We now have an excellent place to 
start for further exploration into the what, where, and how of community planning. Our goal is 
to craft a General Plan that is representative of the various communities individually and of 
Eastern Summit County as a whole that still advances the vision of all of Summit County.   
 
 
50
2 
 
Why Now? 
In order to build off the momentum gained during Our Summit Community Visioning, the time 
is right to harness community involvement so that strategic and intentional planning decisions 
can be made to help shape the future of Eastern Summit County.   
 
The County embarked on Our Summit with the understanding that it was the first step needed 
to kickstart a series of processes. The information gained through that process is high level and 
relatively general. In order to now create a General Plan that is specific and actionable, we will 
need more information.  In terms of specificity, we can think of the relationship between a 
vision, a general plan, and the development code like this: 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
  
 
What can we expect?  
As a part of a comprehensive update to the General Plan, staff, planning commission, and 
community members should expect a plan that includes: 
1. Analysis of data from sources including the most current Census and American 
Community Survey regarding  
a. Current and projected population 
b. Housing needs 
c. Job and market trends 
d. Transportation 
2. Analysis of data related to recreation, water conservation, and hazard mitigation 
3. Thorough and inclusive community engagement opportunities that build off the data 
and feedback gained during the visioning process. The opportunities may include, but 
are not limited to: surveys, focus groups, open houses, and stakeholder interviews with 
the intention of diving deeper into areas of focus revealed through the visioning process  
4. A comprehensive development of goals, strategies, and priorities for Eastern Summit 
County 
5. Actionable strategies for each goal that can be assessed, reviewed, and, if necessary, 
revised annually  
6. Future land use map(s) that identify areas for growth, protection, and restoration.

members who attended a meeting, event or open house; responded to a poll or online 
questionnaire; participated in a one-on-one interview; or completed and returned a mail-in 
questionnaire.  
 
During this process, we identified themes that span the Snyderville Basin and Eastern Summit 
County boundary. For example, we heard from community members across the county that 
what they love most about living in Summit County is the access to recreational opportunities, 
the open space, and the rural character. Similarly, we heard shared sentiments that some of the 
biggest concerns for the future include balancing growth with community values, mitigating 
traffic and congestion, and enhancing community character. We heard that what could improve 
Summit County as a whole is increasing housing options and transportation connections, 
highlighting and encouraging elements that promote community character, strategically 
preserving agriculture, proactively planning for the protection of water quality and quantity. 
 
Based on all the feedback received, we identified five community-wide objectives: 
1. Manage Natural Resources: Strategically acquire, preserve, and maintain an 
accessible and connected open space system for community enjoyment and 
ecosystem vitality. 
2. Promote Healthy Living: Equitably increase opportunities for recreation, safe walking 
and bicycling, access to services, and availability of amenities for people of all ages 
and abilities. 
3. Preserve Unique Identities: Celebrate the unique identity of each community while 
recognizing the need for strategic, comprehensive planning countywide. 
4. Create Inclusive Communities: Elevate the quality of life for Summit County 
community members through accessible housing and employment opportunities 
that diversify the economy. 
5. Grow Responsibly: Promote sustainability best practices through multi-modal 
transportation options, building aesthetic, site design, and local and regional policy. 
 
Under each of those objectives, we identified strategies and priorities for the County as a 
whole. The Planning Commission and staff must now take the strategies most appropriate for 
Eastern Summit County and plan for their implementation. As an example, consider the

Perhaps Eastern Summit County chooses to focus more on the strategy of implementing 
a cluster development zoning district in order to strategically preserve agricultural uses. 
During the General Plan process, we would then need to determine what that looks like 
and where that might be appropriate, which will require future engagement from the 
community.

What is the structure of a General Plan?   
General Plans can take on a variety of forms and include a variety of information.  Based on the 
Utah State Legislature, a General Plan must, at minimum, include the following (for more 
information, visit 17-27a-402): 
1. A land use element that: 
a. designates the long-term goals and the proposed extent, general distribution, 
and location of land for housing for residents of various income levels, business, 
industry, agriculture, recreation, education, public buildings and grounds, open 
space, and other categories of public and private uses of land as appropriate; 
b. includes a statement of the projections for and standards of population density 
and building intensity recommended for the various land use categories covered 
by the plan; 
c. is coordinated to integrate the land use element with the water use and 
preservation element; and 
d. accounts for the effect of land use categories and land uses on water demand; 
2. a transportation and traffic circulation element that: 
a. provides the general location and extent of existing and proposed freeways, 
arterial and collector streets, public transit, active transportation facilities, and 
other modes of transportation that the planning commission considers 
appropriate; 
b. addresses the county's plan for residential and commercial development around 
major transit investment corridors to maintain and improve the connections 
between housing, employment, education, recreation, and commerce; and 
c. correlates with the population projections, the employment projections, and the 
proposed land use element of the general plan; 
3. for a specified county as defined in Section 17-27a-408, a moderate income housing 
element that: 
a. provides a realistic opportunity to meet the need for additional moderate 
income housing within the next five years; 
b. selects three or more moderate income housing strategies described in 
Subsection (2)(b)(ii) for implementation; and 
c. includes an implementation plan as provided in Subsection (2)(e); 
4. a resource management plan detailing the findings, objectives, and policies required by 
Subsection 17-27a-401(3); and 
5. a water use and preservation element that addresses: 
a. the effect of permitted development or patterns of development on water 
demand and water infrastructure; 
b. methods of reducing water demand and per capita consumption for future 
development; 
c. methods of reducing water demand and per capita consumption for existing 
development; and 
d. opportunities for the county to modify the county's operations to eliminate 
practices or conditions that waste water.
