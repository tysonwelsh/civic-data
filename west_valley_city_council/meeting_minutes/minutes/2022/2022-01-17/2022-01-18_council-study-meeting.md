      ORDINANCE 22-02: AMEND SECTION 1-2-107(3) OF THE WEST VALLEY
      CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO UPDATE ADMISSIONS FEES FOR THE WEST
      VALLEY CITY FAMILY FITNESS CENTER
      Jamie Young, Parks and Recreation, presented proposed Ordinance 22-02 that would
      amend Section 1-2-107(3) of the West Valley City Municipal Code to Update Admissions Fees
      for the West Valley City Family Fitness Center.

      Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
      follows:

             The WVC Family Fitness Center has seen a significant increase in expenses since
             its last fee increase 10 years ago. The proposed membership and drop-in admission
             fee increases outlined below will help cover upsurges in maintenance costs,
             personnel and benefits, property insurance and overall cost of services. Staff
             compared membership fees with other similar facilities and found that the proposed
             increases remain comparable to most nearby recreation facilities. With these
             changes, it is recommended that the Centennial Outdoor Pool be included in the
             memberships.

             It is proposed that all membership rates be increased by 15% apart from senior
             membership rates which will be raised 5%. This reflects a monthly membership
             fee increase of:

RESOLUTION 22-12: AUTHORIZE THE EXECUTION OF AN INTERLOCAL
COOPERATION AGREEMENT, A PARK EASEMENT AGREEMENT, AND A
PERPETUAL WATERLINE EASEMENT AGREEMENT BETWEEN WEST
VALLEY CITY AND SALT LAKE COUNTY
Jason Erekson, Parks and Recreation, presented proposed Resolution 22-12 that would
authorize the Execution of an Interlocal Cooperation Agreement, a Park Easement Agreement,
and a Perpetual Waterline Easement Agreement Between West Valley City and Salt Lake
County.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       West Valley City owns a water meter north of the Utah Cultural Celebration Center
       that is used very little. Salt Lake County will be building and maintaining Pioneer
       Crossing Park and have asked for this agreement allowing them to utilize the
       existing water meter and water line. By utilizing existing utilities there will not
       need to be a new agreement with Rocky Mountain Power.

       West Valley City also owns a portion of land on which Salt Lake County would
       like to build a portion of Pioneer Crossing Park. This agreement will allow them to
       build part of the park on this piece of property.

       As part of the agreement, Salt Lake County will grant the city use of 227 square
       feet of space in their maintenance building. West Valley City police department
       will be able to stage equipment in this space for Jordan River patrols.

The City Council will consider Resolution 22-12 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled

RESOLUTION 22-13: APPROVE AN AMENDMENT TO THE INTERLOCAL
COOPERATION AGREEMENT BETWEEN WEST VALLEY CITY AND SALT
LAKE COUNTY FOR SERVICES AT THE HARMAN SENIOR RECREATION
CENTER
Nancy Day, Parks and Recreation Director, presented proposed Resolution 22-13 that
would approve an Amendment to the Interlocal Cooperation Agreement Between West Valley
City and Salt Lake County for Services at the Harman Senior Recreation Center.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Salt Lake County Division of Aging and Adult Services pays West Valley City an
       annual amount totaling $18,786 to offset the costs of operating the congregate meal
       program including $700 for specific meal supplies. Additionally, Salt Lake County
       provides transportation services for seniors which takes them to and from the
       Harman Senior Recreation Center.

       A new interlocal agreement with Salt Lake County was approved and signed by
       West Valley City on July 27, 2020 and was then extended through December 31,
       2021. This new amended agreement will extend services through December 31,
       2023.

Wayne asked if the funding amount is the same. Nancy replied yes and noted it has stayed
consistent for the last 7 or 8 years. Wayne asked if conversations have been had with the
County about a potential increase. Nancy replied yes but noted that the County is not
amenable to an increase. Wayne noted that the City still finds the contribution important
and has continued renewing the agreement. Nancy agreed and added that the funding is
helpful and pays for the cost of all the food as well as 75% of the part time employee salary.
She stated that this is a great service to the community and senior’s are thankful for it.

The City Council will consider Resolution 22-13 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
January 25, 2022 at 6:30 P.M.

RESOLUTION 22-14: ADOPT THE STATE LEGISLATIVE AGENDA OF WEST
VALLEY CITY FOR THE 2022 UTAH STATE LEGISLATIVE SESSION
Nicole Cottle, Assistant City Manager, presented proposed Resolution 22-14 that would
adopt the State Legislative Agenda of West Valley City for the 2022 Utah State Legislative
Session.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
    MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JANUARY 18, 2022
                           -6-


follows:

       As the second largest city in the state it is imperative that the City has a significant
       presence at the state legislature. The City enjoys a particularly strong presence and
       maintains that presence each year through the process of setting guidelines for
       representation and executing on those guidelines.

       Due to the city’s unique position, it has been essential to maintain this presence and
       to proactively work on legislative issues throughout the year. Each January, the
       Council sets forth the guidelines for this work. These guidelines are found in the
       attached resolution. The guidelines allow staff and contractors to adequately
       represent the City on any topic in a constantly changing environment and provide
       protection to the City from any misunderstanding of the City’s position.

       Due to the extremely dynamic nature of the legislative session, staff must be clear
       about the direction of the City Council. This resolution assists in that effort.

       Of the various issues that will be discussed during this legislative session, of
       particular interest are policing, land use, emergency operations, taxation, funding,
       local control, economic development matters, and transportation funding.
