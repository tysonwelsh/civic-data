A. Discussion and Possible Action to Consider Ordinance 2024-O-16.
MSD Planning Manager Brian Tucker gave a brief overview of the previous discussion and the
information included in tonight’s packet.
Council Member Sudbury asked if they could now require weed barriers underneath gravel
surfaces.
Mr. Tucker responded that is not in the proposed code change but could be added.
Council Member Prokopis asked for the planning commission and staff's recommendations on
the proposed change.
Mr. Tucker responded that both staff and the planning commission recommended approval of
the ordinance as presented in the staff report. Similar ordinances are used throughout the
valley with success and this version was created from researching other areas’ ordinances.
The 60% coverage was used in creating the Kearns ordinance and was used as starting point.

He reviewed the specific coverage requirements including setbacks, noting residents can only
cover 60% of their yard, and he gave the details of what is allowed as coverage.
Mayor Barney asked if a resident could, in theory, concrete their entire front yard if nothing was
done in the back.
Mr. Tucker responded they can go up to 60% total, but no more than 60% in the front.

allowing more parking in yards. If a resident is planning to cover more than 50% of the yard,
the project will require a stormwater analysis by Engineering. They can go all the way to 60%
coverage in the front yard, but going over 50% will probably require some type of onsite
detention of rainwater.
Council Member Prokopis noted this is a step in the right direction, as it can always be
revisited if needed in the future. With any motion of a recommendation for approval, he would
like to see a requirement for a weed barrier under any gravel surfaces.
Council Member Pierce was okay with how the ordinance is written but noted she would not be
opposed to the recommended addition of a weed barrier requirement.
Council Member Prokopis moved to approve Ordinance 2024-0-16, with the edit
requested requiring a weed barrier under gravel surfaces. Council Member Sudbury

D. Vacating Dora Street

City Attorney Paul Ashton noted there have been numerous discussions in front of the council
on this matter, including a public hearing held two weeks ago, and reviewed background
information from the staff report and previous discussion. The council has two options at this
point. He doesn’t believe, based on the staff report in front of them tonight, that the council can
approve the vacation. The council can either deny the petition to vacate at this time with the
option to revisit in the future, or they can approve the vacation of the street subject to
conditions they choose such as water and sewer letters showing development is possible. One
problem with adding conditions is that the approval with conditions stays with the property.
Even if the current developer doesn't meet the conditions, a new developer can come in later
and meet the conditions without it being reviewed by the council. There has been a lot of
discussion and consideration on this topic, and he noted they cannot vacate any street if harm
could fail to individuals, including property owners surrounding this street and development. If
the intent is to vacate the street and move the development closer to the school, that would
cause the property owners on 8400 to have the street in front of them with the access road
behind them.
Council Member Prokopis asked how this could be done better next time, avoiding a developer
making a significant investment in the hopes of a vacation. Possibly a development agreement
up front could help avoid this problem in the future.
Mr. Ashton shared some history of the subdivision, noting when these streets were developed
there was an intention of continuing homes where the school is. Dora Street was just one of
the streets in the development. When the apartments were developed, access should have
been maintained throughout the development for water and sewer with the future in mind.
Developers should always do their due diligence on water and sewer connections.

Council Member Pierce asked if this is done with conditions or restrictions, is there a time limit

to develop with those conditions before the project has to go through the process again.

Mr. Ashton believes they could add a time limit for those conditions to be met, and it could be

added in the motion.

Council Member Pierce believes there needs to be conditions added before approving this, but

she doesn’t know what the appropriate amount of time would be to give the developer; she

doesn’t want it left open-ended.

Council Member Prokopis noted that even if the property changed hands after the fact, a plan

was agreed upon and would still have to be followed.

Council Member Pierce doesn’t have concerns about it changing hands. If the city is giving up

land there should be compensation, she just doesn’t know what that number looks like.

Mr. Ashton responded that would be subject to an appraisal, and the developer has already

agreed to comply if that condition is placed by retaining an appraiser agreed upon with the city

to obtain the fair market value of the property.

Council Member Pierce is in favor of moving forward with this in lieu of any conditions.

City Manager David Brickey noted that different areas have different timelines. In similar

situations, Summit County generally allows no more than seven years, while West Jordan

allows five years from the council's decision.

Council Member Pierce believes the time frame should be shorter than five years.

Council Member Sudbury noted that they are trying to fix a known problem, as the council is

aware of some blight and misuse of property. This would be a benefit to develop, but the city

should be helping not hindering that process. If it takes additional conditions that are

reasonable to get them there, they need to work towards that.

Mr. Ashton suggested delaying their decision while they wait to work out what those conditions

should be.

Council Members Pierce and Sudbury expressed their agreement with delaying a decision as

noted above.

Council Member Sudbury moved to delay any decisions on vacating Dora Street until

such time the council can get advisement on appropriate conditions to move forward.

Council Member Prokopis seconded the motion. AYE: Council Member Sudbury, Council

E. Discussion of Possible Action to Reconsider Resolution R2024-12-01.

City Manager David Brickey gave a brief review of the staff report regarding approval of an
interlocal cooperation agreement with the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District related
to the use of specific funds to design, mobilize, and construct certain road and sidewalk
projects within the area known as The Webster Center. They wanted to adjust language to
capture the ability to purchase property, and the packet contains the specific amounts along
with where they are coming from. In reviewing the information in the packet, he noted the cost
of Item 8 is going to double with the amount currently listed being the amount already paid,
and still owing twice that to finish the project. The Pleasant Green copper sign is also showing
half of what they paid to finish the project, with approximately another $45,000 due. The CW
Farms Light Improvement Project could be as much as $45,000 to $51,000 to complete as
well. Those amounts will likely come off the available $1.8 million by the end of the year,
leaving the rest to be utilized next year on projects to benefit the city like playgrounds, roads
and other projects around The Webster Center. The language has been reviewed with three
additional attorneys and they were all okay with the recitals as written.
Mr. Ashton added that they would prefer to stay with the recital because it mirrors the Kearns
version.
Mr. Brickey added the money must be identified and committed by next year, spending it all by
December 31, 2026. The extension on committing the funds is the purpose of this resolution. If
approved tonight, it will be presented to the MSD Trustees tomorrow night.
Council Member Sudbury moved to approve Resolution 2024-12-01 as published.
Council Member Prokopis seconded the motion. AYE: Council Member Sudbury, Council
Member Prokopis, Mayor Barney, Council Member Pierce EXCUSED: Council Member Hull
FINAL RESULT: 4-0 Motion Passes
