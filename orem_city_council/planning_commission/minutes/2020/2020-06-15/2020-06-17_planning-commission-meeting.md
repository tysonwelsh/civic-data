                                                        two (2) small commercial buildings on the site which will each

The south building will be 1,700 square feet and will have two primary entrances. One that faces 1600 North and

from the back of the curb on 1600 North.

The north building is 1,503 square feet and will have primary entrance facing the east parking lot. The side of the
building that faces State Street is primarily composed of the same “tier 1” materials approved for the North Village
District noted above. The other faces of the building will have the same “tier 2” materials approved for the North
Village District noted above. This building will have a drive through on the south face. This drive through will go
between the two buildings on the site and will meet the drive-through requirements of the North Village District.

Both buildings are a part of the existing Cross Point plat and be meeting their parking requirements noted through the
shared parking as part of that plat. At the time of the original plat, the development was proposed to have 53,072
square feet of retail space which would have required 266 parking stalls. This site and the one just to the west of it
were originally proposed to have 5,600 square feet of retail space each. The Swig went in on that west site with 1,052
square feet and the proposed Xpointe Retail Plaza will only have 3,450 square feet, both are well below their allotted
maximum and have decreased the required parking for the entire area by 35 spaces. The overall area requires 231
spaces and 285 are provided.

The applicant will be meeting the landscaping requirement. The required three trees (1 per 40’) are provided along the
street frontage. There is an eight foot (8’) wide sidewalk provided along 1600 North.

Recommendation: The Development Review Committee determined this request complies with the Orem City
Code. Staff recommends the Planning Commission approve the site plan of Xpointe Plaza located at 575 West 1600
North in the North Village District Edge (NVD-E) Zone.

                                       Staff Presentation: The applicant’s existing business is located nearby this
                                       site on State Street. In order to expand his current business, he is proposing to
                                       move it entirely to this new location. He is not proposing any changes to the
                                       site, it would remain as is. However, because the site is in a residential zone, he
                                       is required to receive conditional use approval.

                                       Commercial daycares seeking conditional use approval within a residential
                                       zone are required to have an outdoor area in the rear yard that will be enclosed
                                       by a 6’ site obscuring fence with self-closing, self-locking gates. There is an
                                       area existing in the rear yard that is currently surrounded by a 6’ wood fence.
                                       As per State Code, this area will be required to have self-closing, self-locking
gates. The applicant will be providing these.

Commercial daycares seeking conditional use approval within a residential zone are required to submit a professional
traffic study that shows the following:

    1.   No more than two car trips per hour shall be generated before 5:30am or after 9:00 pm.
    2.   No vehicle will back out onto a dedicated street.
    3.   Persons who arrive at the facility shall be legally parked on the lot or on that part of the street which
         immediately abuts the lot.
    4.   Ingress, egress, drop-off areas, traffic flow, and parking spaces will be contained on the site and will not
         adversely affect surrounding properties.

The submitted traffic study showed that the facility was going to require a minimum of twenty-four (24) stalls based
upon on hundred (100) children attending the day care. The study also found that they would not generate more than
two trips in the unpermitted times and that one of the stalls in the parking lot would be required to be used as a back-
up area. If you combine the total stalls in both the existing parking lot and the on-street parking and remove the
required back-up stall, twenty-six (26) stalls are available.

Recommendation: The Development Review Committee determined this request complies with the Orem City
Code. Staff recommends the Planning Commission forward a positive recommendation to the City Council for the
approval of the conditional use of the commercial daycare, Smiling Faces, located at 410 S Main Street in the R8
zone with the following conditions:
    1. That all residents, visitors and employees who arrive at the facility in connection with the Commercial Day
        Care facility shall be legally parked on the lot occupied by the facility or on that part of the street which
        immediately abuts the lot.
    2. That signage be provided in the existing off-street parking lot that clearly indicates pick-up/drop-off stalls.
    3. That one stall in the existing off-street parking lot shall be used as a back-up area for vehicles in the off-
        street parking lot.
    4. That the facilities have a maximum occupancy of one hundred (100) children for both buildings combined.
    5. That two ADA stalls be provided in the existing off-street parking lot.

Public Comment received 6.16.2020:

I’ve lived for 30 years in the home that borders 410 S Main Street on the west. I have several concerns about the
conditional use permit for the day care. 1) Traffic: I’m not sure the traffic study accurately reflects the difficulty of
that multiple intersection area (400 S \ Main and 400 S \ Orem Blvd). Both high traffic numbers and traffic
congestion (backup) from the Orem Blvd lights impacts turning both left and right onto 400 South from Main Street,
not to mention poor westbound visibility when cars are parked on 400 South (along the property frontage). No
center turn lane on 400 South anymore (removed to accommodate bike lane) impacts turning off of 400 South onto
60 West (left turn) and onto 400 South from 50 West (right turn) which will both be done by westbound cars
needing to turn around and use the property’s frontage parking stalls on 400 South. These are just two examples of
the already difficult (and dangerous) traffic in the area.

2) Parking: The request says that there are 24 parking stalls. It also says they have 8 employees. In reality, that
means 16 parking stalls for up to 100 children. The parking study says that the current daycare parking never
exceeds 10 stalls used even at the busiest time of the day. But the current daycare has around 60 children. The math
doesn’t add up for me. So, if that is the case, and there are parking issues, who is going to enforce the conditional
use requirement that “Persons who arrive at the facility shall be legally parked on the lot or on that part of the street
which immediately abuts the lot”? Is the business going to train their clients on appropriate parking areas? Are they
going to monitor and police themselves and be firm with their clients when they complain? In short, are they going
to be a good neighbor or a negligent neighbor that requires external policing? I recognize that this is a negative,
worst-case scenario, but it seems to be a real possibility which is why I’m asking.

I want to make it clear. I have nothing against this business. I’ve looked at their website and they offer a great
service to the community. I just don’t feel like it is a good fit based on the property’s traffic and parking limitations.

Thank you.
Darrin VandenBos
