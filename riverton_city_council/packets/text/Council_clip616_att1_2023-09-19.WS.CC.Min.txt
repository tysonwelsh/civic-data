47            South Valley Sewer District Presentation
48
49   Joel Thompson, General Manager of the South Valley Sewer District, provided an overview of
50   the district's slip lining project, which involves rehabilitating sewer pipes using a trenchless
51   technology called CIPP (Cured-In-Place Pipe) lining. He explained that the district covers the
     Page | 3 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     September 19, 2023




 1   south end of Salt Lake County, providing wastewater collection and treatment services to
 2   several cities.
 3
 4   Mr. Thompson discussed the importance of addressing defects and damage in sewer pipes,
 5   which can lead to infiltration and reduce system capacity. He highlighted the benefits of using
 6   CIPP lining, such as cost-effectiveness and reduced disruption to property owners.
 7
 8   The presentation included images of damaged sewer pipes and the CIPP lining process, where
 9   resin-soaked liners are inserted into pipes and cured in place. Mr. Thompson also shared a map
10   showing completed and planned lining projects within the district.
11
12   Councilmember Buroker asked why the work would continue until late into the evening. In
13   response, Mr. Thompson explained that late-night work during the curing process is primarily
14   due to the inability to stop once it has started. Once the curing process begins, it must continue
15   until the liner is fully cured and hardened. Additionally, the contractors aim to time the work to
16   complete it efficiently, but there may be challenges related to bypassing or diverting flows, which
17   can make it necessary to work late into the evening. The contractors understand the importance
18   of completing the job and restoring sewer services to residents, so they work diligently until the
19   project is finished and the quality meets the required standards.
20
21   Councilmember McDougal asked about Riverton City’s inspections and whether certain lines
22   are at the end of their life. Mr. Thompson responded by saying that the most critical and urgent
23   repairs to Riverton's sewer lines have been addressed as a top priority. These critical repairs
24   took place between 2021 and 2022, and the current phase of the project focuses on less severe
25   and less urgent repairs, as well as the slip lining process to extend the operational life of the
26   sewer lines. The sewer lines in Riverton have received necessary attention to ensure their
27   functionality and minimize potential issues.

37            Riverton Police Department Presentation
38
39   Chief Barfuss shared several important updates and statistics regarding the recent transfer of
40   Animal Services and Code Enforcement to the police department's supervision. This transition
41   occurred on July 1, and the data presented covered the two months following this change.
42
43   Chief Barfuss noted that there has been a significant increase in call volume for Animal Services
44   since the transfer, with calls going from seven in July to thirty-three in August. This surge in calls
45   has put pressure Officer Watson, impacting her ability to proactively address issues. Regarding
46   licensing, it was mentioned that there has been a decline in pet licensing, raising concerns
47   about public safety and disease control.
48
49   Chief Barfuss said there was a notable increase in calls for service in Code Enforcement, from
50   61 in July to over 100 in August. Officer Juarez is struggling to keep up with the rising demand,
51   and cases typically take over 30 days to address.
     Page | 4 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1
 2   Chief Barfuss explained that the records division experienced a complete turnover in personnel.
 3   The division is responsible for daily transcriptions, and the statistics showed a significant
 4   workload increase, with over 1,700 daily transcriptions from May to August. The division also
 5   faces the challenge of fulfilling governmental records requests within the mandated 10-day
 6   timeframe.
 7
 8   Lastly, Chief Barfuss discussed broader police department statistics. Traffic citations were noted
 9   to have increased by 34% compared to the previous year, likely due to increased traffic in the
10   area. Arrests, while on the rise, have not increased as much as expected, which was attributed
11   to proactive efforts and community-oriented policing.
12
13   Councilmember McCay asked why there was such an increase in Animal Control calls. In
14   response, Chief Barfuss explained that both Animal Services and Code Enforcement have
15   always been active but may not have been as comprehensively documented in the past. He
16   noted that due to the high volume of incoming calls, there hasn't been much time for proactive
17   work, which is an issue that is being addressed.
18
19   Councilmember McCay questioned whether the City should continue requiring dog permits,
20   given the limited number of residents who obtain them. She suggested that those who do
21   obtain permits might be responsible pet owners who are already ensuring their dogs are
22   properly vaccinated and cared for.
23
24   Mayor Staggs acknowledged Councilmember McCay’s suggestion as a topic worth discussing
25   with the City Council. He noted that revenue from dog permits could help fund additional code
26   enforcement officers. He expressed surprise at the drop in the number of dog licenses issued
27   after the City transitioned to the Stone Ridge Animal Center partnership. He emphasized the
28   importance of this revenue for supporting code enforcement officers and acknowledged that the
29   way calls are tracked shows an increase in demand for code enforcement services.
30
31   Councilmember Wells expressed concern about reducing or eliminating dog permits, citing
32   several serious dog bite incidents in his district over the past four years. He emphasized the
33   importance of dog permits in ensuring that dogs are immunized, preventing the spread of
34   diseases, and addressing potentially dangerous situations. He mentioned that the goal of
35   bringing animal control and code enforcement under the police department was to have more
36   active enforcement rather than relying solely on complaints. However, he recognized that it's
37   only been a couple of months since the change, and there have been efforts to address record-
38   keeping and other issues.
39
40   Councilmember Wells suggested that in the future, they could discuss the level of active
41   enforcement in the City, not just related to animals but also concerning other issues such as
42   problem properties. He expressed interest in receiving a report on the City's progress in this
43   regard.
44
