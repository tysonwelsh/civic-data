2. 24-170 Council Office recommending the Council receive a quarterly update on the
Medically Vulnerable People (MVP) Facility

Attachments: M\VP Quarterly Report

Laurie Hopkins with Shelter the Homeless, provided a quarterly update to the Council on
the Medically Vulnerable People Shelter located in Sandy City. Ms. Hopkins provided
information regarding security, staffing, shelter residents, and program impact. Currently,
there are two security guards on site 24 hours a day, 7 days a week and they plan on
continuing this. Program staff meet with Sandy Police and Fire weekly to encourage
communication and collaboration. 201 people have been served since the shelter opened.
The average age of the resident is 63 years old and range in age from 33 to 86. Staff from
the Road Home and Fourth Street Clinic were also present and provided information to
the Council. This program works with over 35 different referral services. They all
expressed their appreciation to the Council and Administration, Fire and Police for their
continued support and collaborative efforts to make this program a success. Council
questions followed.

3. 24-177 Police Department providing the council with the 2024 crime report

Attachments: 2024 Property Crimes Report.pdf

Police Chief Severson provided the Council with a crime report overview based on crime
statistics and incidents that occurred last year. He also provided prior year comparisons.
Overall, crime is very low in Sandy. Chief Severson provided the following details
regarding property thefts: Business Burglary - 135; Residential Burglary - 110; Larceny -
1,188; Vehicle Burglary - 455; Vehicle Theft - 180. Over a ten year comparison, property
crime in Sandy City has decreased. He also provided maps detailing theft hot spots.
Chief Severson provided an overview of violent crimes committed in Sandy in the prior
year: Aggravated Assaults - 93; Homicide - 0; Rape - 35; Robbery - 23.
Chief Severson also provided an overview of policing strategy and crime prevention. The
Police Department focuses on crime prevention and sponsors many community
programs: Sandy Secure, Volunteers in Police Service, Neighborhood Watch. The Police
Department utilizes the following policing strategies: Crime Suppression Unit, Property
and Person Crimes Detective Unit, Community engagement, partnerships with state and
federal task forces, data driven policing strategies, use of technology, and real time crime
center. Recently, Sandy Police was awarded with a multi-agency award for their service.
Council questions followed.
Council expressed their appreciation and support of the Sandy City Police Department for
their dedication and commitment to our community and residents. Council thanked
Police for all their service to our city.
Council went to General Citizen Comment.

8. 24-171 Public Hearing to consider the tentative adoption of the Mayor's FY
2024-25 Tentative Budget for Sandy City and the Alta Canyon Recreation
District

Attachments: 24-16C Tentative 2025 Budget.pdf

Presentation_Tentative 2025. pdf
Public Hearing:
Mayor Zoltanski pariticpated in the Council meeting virtually from Washington DC, where
she is a member of the South Valley Chamber Delegation. Mayor Zoltanski presented the
FY 2025 Tentative Budget to the Sandy City Council. Mayor Z spoke about the priorities
of the community that are reflected in this tentative budget: public safety, protecting open
space, community events, city services, and employee compensation. A modest cost of
living adjustment of 1% for city employees was proposed. No tax increases have been
requested. The General Fund has been thoroughly reviewed by Administration, Finance,
and city leaders to provide a clarity of costs. All expenses have been scrutinized. The
goal of this year's tentative budget is to spend only what is necessary. We have made
great strides on city capital projects with the bond approval to fund Fire Station 31
supporting our city's public safety priorities. Mayor Z spoke about a plan for Alta Canyon
Recreation Center to rebuild the center in phases rather than all at once. She reviewed
details of the plan for Alta Canyon. She also spoke about the city's ongoing commitment
to continue providing our residents with the highest level of services.
Shane Pace, CAO, introduced Brian Kelley, Administrative Services/Finance Director,
who provided an overview of the city's Tentative Budget. Mr. Kelly reviewed the vision,
mission statement, foundational values, guiding principles, and revenue policies of the
city. He provided an overview of the Budget Process, key challenges and opportunities,
and timeline. Adoption of the Tentative Budget by the Council initiates the budget
process. The adoption of the final budget for FY 25 must occur by the last Council
meeting in June of 2024.
Mr. Kelly reviewed details and strategies contained in the FY25 Tentative Budget. No
property tax increase was requested. General fund subsidies associated with the bulk
waste program have been eliminated or reduced which will result in fee increases. Indirect
subsidies associated with administrative charges have been eliminated. Total staffing has
decreased by 1.3 equivalent full-time employees (FTE). He provided details regarding fleet
replacement and proposed plans for Alta Canyon Recreation Center and provided an
overview of operating expense increases related to core city services and capital projects.
Cost reductions contained in the proposed budget include $303K reduction to the General
Fund and $424K reduction in other funds. The proposed budget does not include any
increases to storm water fees, substantial fleet replacement needs, certain capital
projects, and many department requests.
Department presentations to the Council will begin on May 21, 2024. The budget tool can
be found on the city website. Mr. Kelley recognized and thanked Administration and
department heads for their participation on the budget team.
Katrina Frederick, Human Resources Director, presented the FY 2025 Compensation
Plan Proposal. The Key Takeaways from the Compensation Study are as follows: 1)
Recent police and fire pay plan revisions have significantly helped recruitment and
retention for those positions and have made us market competitive; 2) Significant
progress has been made for public positions; 3) High inflation and low unemployment are
still driving up salaries. She reviewed the Compensation Plan Goals: keep pay ranges
