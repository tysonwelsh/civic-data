BAR W AGRICULTURE SPLIT, SUBDIVISION (2 LOTS)- FINAL PLAT AND SITE PLAN APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council that the Bar W subdivision had been approved and
recommended by the Planning Commission at their last meeting. It comprises two lots along with an
agricultural split of three lots along the eastern boundary of the subdivision. An agricultural subdivision
with a “record of survey map” was completed to clean up the boundaries of this development and will
remain in agricultural use. Agricultural splits are allowed to be exempted from a municipality’s regular
subdivision code by Utah State Code, but they still have to go through the same channels they would
have if they went through the full required process essentially still requiring a survey that is recorded
and going through our processes. The record of survey map will be recorded in the County Recorder’s
office prior to the recordation of the Bar W subdivision plat.
  The final plat submission is for a two-lot subdivision on recently annexed parcels. Lot 1 has been
rezoned to the ID-2 zone to facilitate slaughterhouse use, but Lot 2 remains in the HC-1 Highway
Commercial zone. A quiet title action on the property is also still pending along Hwy 132 and will need
to be cleared up before the plat can be recorded.
  It had been determined that 1150 West will now be a city right-of-way rather than a private road. It is
proposed as a cul-de-sac at this point but is planned as a connector to a road going westward to Airport
Road. A site plan for a slaughterhouse has been approved by the Planning Commission at the last
meeting, contingent on subdivision approval. The slaughterhouse is proposed for Lot 1 of the
subdivision.
   Councilor Parady moved to approve the Bar W agricultural split. Councilor Memmott seconded the
motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.
   Councilor Parady moved to approve the final plat of the Bar W Subdivision. Councilor Ostler
seconded the motion. The motion carried on a unanimous vote.

NEBO MEADOWS SUBDIVISION (42 LOT) FINAL PLAT APPROVED:

  Mr. Atkinson presented the final plat submission is for a forty-two (42) lot Nebo Meadows
Subdivision. The zoning for this area is R1 or ¼ acre lots minimum. The city had previously negotiated
with Terry Messersmith of the Northridge Plat A development to the north of Nebo Meadows for a
potential road extension, and this can now go through on Lot 17 of Northridge Plat A if the city exercises
their right to that lot based on an executed development agreement.
 The city has negotiated with the Nebo Meadows developer to enter into a development agreement
that would include a fee-simple dedication to Nephi City from the developer of a fifteen-foot right-of-
way on the far east side of the development. The right-of-way would about the Nebo Heights
development to service the sewer in the rear of those properties. The dedication would also include a
landscaped area, on Lot 42 of the subdivision, that would allow the city’s large sewer truck to have an
appropriate amount of turning space. The city, in exchange, will allow the developer to collect
stormwater runoff through a diversion of stormwater drainage with some off-site improvements to
corners on 50 East and 150 East, which will remove the requirement to install a retention pond for the
development. The stormwater would then be diverted to the retention pond in Hunter Meadows.
Because of the future closure of 1450 E and Main Street, the pond could potentially be expanded if
needed.
   Councilor Memmott moved to approve the final plat for Nebo Meadows Subdivision. Councilor
Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

OUT-OF-STATE TRAVEL FOR POLICE OFFICERS APPROVED:

  Mr. Atkinson explained to the council that the Police Department has two different training
opportunities for their staff that will be out of state. The first opportunity is for the 2022 Utah Narcotic
Officers Association Conference and will be held in Mesquite, NV. The Drug Task Force will be paying for
the expenses but our officers will need permission from the Council to attend an out-of-state training.
  The second opportunity is the 2022 FBI Intermountain Law Enforcement Executive Command College
in West Yellowstone, MT. The deadline to apply is August 3rd and Chief Morgan is seeking a grant in
order to send Officer Deeben. The college will instruct on subjects such as: credibility, command
discipline and liability, dealing with problem employees, media, diversity, officer safety issues, leading
change within an organization – and other subjects important to today’s law enforcement leadership.
   Councilor Callaway moved to approve the two requests for out-of-state travel for the police
department with the contingency of grant approval for the FBI regional training. Councilor Parady
seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

“GREENHALGH PROPERTY” LAND SALE AGREEMENT AND REAL ESTATE PURCHASE CONTRACT
APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council that the city put out notification that offers on a city-owned
property would be accepted. Specifically, this property is on the south end of the city and is called the
Greenhalgh property. An offer was made for this property that also came with some conditions. These
conditions were written in an agreement attached to this staff report.
   The agreement states that, if the property were developed with infrastructure improvements using
the funds allocated for the purchase of the property, that certain properties would be deeded back to
the city. The infrastructure would, by and large, follow the recent master plan the city put together for
the property. In addition, the money for the purchase of the property would be used to develop 4 ball
fields to be used as an “anchor” tenant for the surrounding lots. The purpose of an anchor tenant would
be to draw sales to the area. A second property would also be deeded over to the city once certain
improvements are put in that would serve a future police station. There is a provision that if the costs
associated with the infrastructure improvements are above $2,700,000, then the investor of the
property would cover the overage.
   The city agrees to also allow for all stormwater from the property to be drained to a stormwater
facility constructed by the city. This facility would most likely be a large pond next to the railroad right-
of-way. Another condition would be to reimburse the city’s electric fund for the original purchase of the
property along with a reasonable rate of interest return. As part of a real estate purchase agreement,
the city would also sell 30 shares of the South Irrigation Company’s pond and delivery system and lease
30 water shares held by the city for water from the Nephi Irrigation Company. $300,000 worth of parks
impact fee credits would also be given to the property investor.

DIVISION OF WILDLIFE RESOURCES (DWR) AGREEMENT APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson explained that the City had been in touch with representatives from the Utah Division
of Wildlife Resources (DWR) to work out a draft agreement for a potential land swap. The properties
are similar in size and landscape. The current Nephi City property doesn’t currently hold much in value
for the city but acquiring the similar DWR property could allow for an expansion of the city’s golf
course.
    The agreement requires two closing dates to accommodate the federal agencies involved in the land
swap. The Bureau of Land Management has a reversionary clause on the property that requires the
property return to BLM ownership if transferred. Staff is working to keep the reversionary clause on the
property but allow the swap to take place. This same reversionary clause applies on the existing golf
course property. The city also has submitted for a grant to allow for the land swap to take place since
the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service initially purchased the property and deeded it to the state of Utah.
Once the agreement has been signed, the city can then carry on with appraisals and the due diligence
process. DWR has agreed to pay half of the costs of these services. The cost to the city would be
around $30,000 and we can use the funds from the golf course savings.
   Councilor Memmott noticed that on page 13 of the draft agreement it said Mayor Nielson instead of
Mayor Seely. Councilor Memmott moved to accept the land exchange agreement with DWR with the
change of Mayor Seely’s name as the signer instead of Mayor Nielson on page 13. Councilor Ostler
seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.
