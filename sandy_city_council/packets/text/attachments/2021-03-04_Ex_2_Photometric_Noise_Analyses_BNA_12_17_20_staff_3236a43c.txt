Re:     Photometric and Noise Analyses – 7-Eleven at 711 W. 900 S., Sandy, UT 84070

1       INTRODUCTION

        Sandy City issued an RFP requesting an analysis of the existing site lighting and noise
of the 7-Eleven property located at 711 W. 900 S., Sandy, UT 84070. Comprised within this
report are the results of the light and noise study, including light spillage onto six (6) adjacent
properties, an analysis of the possible impacts on the residences of vehicular traffic from the
site, and possible mitigation efforts for both lighting and noise.
        On Wednesday, November 18, beginning at 10pm, light and noise level readings
were taken in the backyards of six (6) residences directly to the south of the 7-Eleven store and
along the north side of the property wall (the 7-Eleven side). Height above finish grade
measurements were taken of the building-mounted fixtures on the south, east, and west
exterior walls of the 7-Eleven as well as the pole-mounted fixtures. Visual observations were
made of the impact of headlights on the homes from vehicles entering the property from the
west and east entrances.
        A second visit was made to the site on Friday, November 20, beginning at 10pm, to
continue and finish the noise analysis.

2       RESULTS

        A light meter that measures the levels of light-exposure to a surface or area in foot-
candles (fc) was used for the lighting portion of the analysis. For noise, a Type I microphone
that meets the ANSI IEC 61094 requirements was used to measure the amount of ambient
sound in A-weighted decibels (dBA) and processed with the AudioTools software.
        The following are the results of the photometric and noise studies and their
corresponding images, figures, graphs, and tables can be found in the Appendices at the end
of this report.

2.2     Noise (refer to Figure 3, Graphs 1-11, and Tables 1-5)
        According to guidelines set forth in Salt Lake Valley Health Department Health
Regulation #21 COMMUNITY NOISE POLLUTION CONTROL section 4.2, Table 1a,
between the hours of 10:00 pm – 7:00 am the maximum permissible sound pressure level
shall be 5 dBA above ambient sound not to exceed 50 dBA – referring to extraneous noise by
any group or adjacent property. The measurements that were taken during these site visits
were all ambient sounds issued by 700 W. and the main road of 9000 S., but there were no
vehicles, groups, etc. that added to the existing ambient sounds. The results of these
measurements can be found in Graphs 1-11 and Tables 1 & 2 in APPENDIX B and the testing
locations can be found in Figure 3 of APPENDIX A.
        Considering the graphed results, no significant, only slight, differences between the
sound levels of the two measurement dates/times can be determined. The main difference
between the results are whether or not a loud vehicle drove past during the time of
measurement.
        For example, measurement location number 9 is located between the south exterior
wall of the 7-eleven building and the property wall. This is a natural barrier for traffic noise
issued from 9000 S. and should have considerable low noise levels. Referring to the
Wednesday night (11/18/20) average of 52.2 dBA (Table 1), this hypothesis is confirmed.
However, referring to the Friday night (11/20/20) average of 60.8 dBA (Table 2), this

3       RECOMMENDATIONS

3.1     Photometric
        There are several options to help reduce the glare and the level of foot-candles on the
south side of the property, predominately coming from the building-mounted fixtures:

Option 1
    •   Reduce the number of building-mounted fixtures.
        o   There are four (4) fixtures on the south side of the building. At least one of
            these could be removed – possibly (2) – and still have adequate lighting for
            driving, walking, and safety. However, this solution could possibly be at odds
            with 7-Eleven corporate requirements.

Option 2
    •   In addition to removing fixtures (refer to Option 1 above), or as a stand-alone
        solution, the fixtures on the south, northwest and southwest halves of the building
        could be dimmed to at least 50% of output after 10pm. The two pole lights on the
        north side could also be dimmed.
        o   Further investigation would be required to determine if the fixtures have dimmable
            drivers already installed and if a different lighting control system would be needed
            to provide this functionality.

Option 3
    •   The building-mounted fixtures could be lowered a foot or so but this would leave
        unsightly, blank cover plates on the exterior of the building and likely significant work
        on the interior. The pole fixtures could possibly be lowered as well.
        o   Communication with the manufacturer would be required to see if this is a
            viable option.

Option to reduce the impact of headlights on the homes are limited:

Option 1
    •   Provide taller, more mature plantings along the wall to help with the screening of the
        headlights.

Option 2
    •   Provide some kind of planting or barrier at the northwest entrance and along the
        east/west portion of the drive, to reduce the impact of the headlights.

        Considering the site is sloped upward from 9000 S towards 700 W and from west to
east, the impact from headlights would be an issue regardless of the 24-hour nature of the
business, but that exacerbates the problem, of course. The slope upward from the west to the
east also provides the homeowners to the west direct view of the LEDs of both fixture types.
However, as the distance to west increases the glare decreases.

3.2     Noise
        By definition, noise is sound that has become a nuisance, which is relative to the
beholder. What could be pure sound, including background sounds, to one person could be
perceived as noise to another. Therefore, I recommend one of the following options:

Option 1
    •   Provide taller barrier walls at the property line in order to limit the line-of-site between
        9000 S. and residents.
        o   The confines with this are costs for constructing such a high barrier as well as
            near limitless height definition to satisfy the need to remove line-of-site sound
            directivity.

Option 2
    •   Instead of providing a taller wall at the property line, provide a barrier wall along
        9000 S.
        o   This will be closer to the source and limit the line-of-site better than
            constructing a massive, unsightly wall close to the properties.

Option 3
    •   Provide large, matured trees or other greenery that will limit the line-of-site as also
        referenced in the lighting recommendations.
        o   The plants would need to be dense in order to effectively break the sound up.
            However, during winter when the leaves have fallen, the effects of the dense
            leaves will limit performance.

4       CONCLUSIONS

        The main issues with any excessive lighting and noise is the line-of-site between the
sources and residences. Essentially, if the source can be seen, it can be heard. Distance and
frequency bands also play a role in the ability of hearing noise sources; however, these cannot
be controlled. Providing barriers, eliminating or dimming some lighting sources, or a
combination of these as indicated in the "RECOMMENDATIONS" section above should help
mitigate many of the issues.
        The limitations to the test measurements are those pertaining to the particular days
and times indicated in this report and any extraneous circumstance is not included.
