1. Consent
Commission Chair Jackson welcomed everyone to the meeting. Kim Struthers introduced Gary
Ellis, the new City Engineer, to the Commissioners.

2. Regular Agenda

2.1) Public hearing and consideration of Dominion Energy's request for conditional use
approval of the Dominion Regulator Station Site Plan located on 0.13-acres at
approximately 11775 West Frontage Road.
Annie Reid presented information on the item.

The applicants with Embridge Homes, were present to represent the item. They mentioned they
will not be moving forward with the power extension and will be working with Rocky Mountain
Power. The conditional use for the fence is still being looked at.

Commission Chair Jackson opened and closed the public hearing.

        Motion: Commissioner Everett moved to approve Dominion Energy’s request for
        conditional use approval of the Dominion Regulator Station Site Plan located on
        0.13-acres at approximately 11775 West Frontage Road; including all findings stated
        in the report and all DRC comments, also approving the conditional use for the fence.
        Commissioner Eyre seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes;
        Commissioner Lockhart, yes; Commissioner Roberts, yes. The motion passed
        unanimously.

*2.5, 2.6, 2.7, and 2.8 were all heard together to be clear and concise*

Brittney presented information on items 2.5, 2.6, 2.7, 2.8

Commissioner Everett asked about the purpose of changing the density. Kim Struthers stated that
the density was roughly closer to R1-15 rather than R1-12. He mentioned that Marlin Eldred had
worked out the price of the development.

Steve Maddox, representing Edge Homes, was the applicant. He requested that Walter Plum be
excused. Marlin Eldred procured the development agreement, with the residential section
managed together and the commercial section by the city. Economics was also included in the
agreement. The flexibility regarding water dedication and layout needed to be addressed. There
would be connectivity with the trail corridor, with shared costs. He believed they would be
connected and aimed to maintain transparency.
4o

Commission Chair Jackson opened the public hearing for items 2.5, 2.6, 2.7 and 2.8

Todd Reese (Lehi resident) - He stated that he felt it was a bit like an attempt to wear down the
residents. His concern and intent were that a General Plan was in place, and he did not agree with
amending it to suit the developer's objectives. From what he understood about STRATA, they
were a mess, traffic was bad, and there was excessive noise. He felt it did not blend well with the
surrounding property. He wanted to quote Mayor Johnson (Listen to quote). He stated that the
development to the west couldn't be controlled, and adding density wouldn't help. He respected
development and property rights but did not support amending the General Plan to achieve it.

Jill Elmont (Lehi resident) - When they bought their property, she questioned what that would
do to their privacy and mentioned they paid out of pocket for it. The neighborhood did not have
enough outlets, and she believed the outlets needed to be reconsidered. Since the property had
been taken away, trees had grown, and raccoons were causing issues. She wanted this addressed
and mentioned the trees were taken down for her family's safety. She agreed with land ownership
but wanted to keep the area as nice as it had been.

Colton Smith (Lehi resident) - He had been to all three agencies to try to facilitate the purchase
of the property. A power line ran through it, and currently, there were not enough outlets from
the neighborhood. The neighborhood couldn't handle the traffic, and the General Plan clearly
stated it could not be done in good faith. He was happy to pass along the ground he had acquired.

Patrick Tread (Lehi resident) - He echoed the previous comments. He stated that 2300 West
was a mess, as was 700 South. Adding all the homes would create more problems. The
congestion was horrible and would cause huge issues. He mentioned that getting in and out of
the neighborhood was hardly feasible and that it was already dangerous. He suggested that if the
lot sizes were increased by half an acre, it would be more cohesive with the neighborhood. There

were no sidewalks for kids walking to and from school, and he was frustrated with the lack of
safety.

Brittney read the online comments that would be included in the record.

Commission Chair Jackson closed the public hearing on items 2.5, 2.6, 2.7 and 2.8

Mr. Maddox stated that the trail was intended to meet the neighbors' requests and would be
included in the contract. He agreed that land use changes were necessary to service the residents
in that area. They had asked for the same considerations with STRATA and wanted to protect
their community and the surrounding communities. They did not want a messy site for the
community.

Luke Seegmiller stated they would require traffic studies, noting 880 trips a day. The
connections would be to the existing stub roads and at Grays Drive. There was a current MAG
project to widen 2300 West to five lanes between Main Street and 2100 North, projected to be
finished by August 2025. South of Main Street, 2300 West would require a traffic signal at
Gray's Drive and 700 South. Many issues stemmed from regional traffic coming from Eagle
Mountain and Saratoga Springs. UDOT had recently received federal funding and would start the
2100 North corridor, with construction beginning in 2026. Pioneer Crossing would be installing
flex lanes starting in 2026 as well. These changes should alleviate traffic. He stated relief was
coming, though it was not immediate. The project would not add significant traffic to the area.
Kim Struthers applied to finish improvements on 700 South. MAG had provided funding for
pedestrian and bicycle activity, but this would all take time, with funding expected in two years.

Commissioner Everett stated they couldn't start construction until water dedication was resolved.
There was no sidewalk on the 700 South Island. Kim Struthers mentioned a ribbon road would
be connected, and Richard Chu owned this property. Commissioner Everett asked if there was an
ingress and egress on Main Street. Luke stated he was not sure what the developer had planned
for the site, but a traffic study would be conducted. Mr. Maddox stated the City did not want the
road stubbed out by limiting access to Main Street. He mentioned that traffic was a concern and
wait times were an issue. Mr. Maddox stated their traffic study presented a global solution. Luke
stated signal spacing would not allow another light. Commissioner Eyre asked if the City was
requesting an exception for the road access. The only access to the City would be from Main
Street. Commissioner Eyre asked if Edge would need an exception due to block length
requirements. Kim stated that was a fair assessment and would need to be included in the
development agreement. He also mentioned that aesthetics per code needed to be added. Mr.
Maddox stated that engineering met the block length requirements. Commissioner Kunze asked
if STRATA had a limited time in Lehi or if this was long-term. Kim stated they did not solely
operate in Lehi and were not exclusively for Lehi City, indicating it would be a long-term site.
This would facilitate the Lehi City Public Works building, and public facilities allowed for
outside storage. Commissioner Eyre asked about the development on 700 South and if there
would be connectivity. A cycle track would be added above the road. Commissioner Eyre stated
it was a challenge and safety issue because of 700 South and asked about aligning the roads for
connectivity. Kim stated the bike track would be separated by physical protection. Commissioner
Jackson stated it upset him because you could not charge for a view. He stated property rights
ended where the land ended.

Commissioner Everett looked at the numbers and, if it was developed as it currently stood, he
didn't see it as a huge distraction. He liked that the City and STRATA would hold architectural
standards. He acknowledged there would be traffic issues and hoped drivers could be respectful
and safe. He saw the benefit for each applicant and did not feel that Lehi was the sole source of
traffic or the lack of planning.

Commissioner Jackson stated there was a general plan for a reason and held it in high regard but
was not opposed to amending the agreement. If it benefited the city, they were open to
amendments. He wanted more conversation on ingress and egress and outlets. He urged the
public to engage with the City Council members.

Commissioner Lockhart did not love everything about the agreement but wanted to recognize the
compromise. She acknowledged it could be frustrating but commended the hard work from staff,
the developer, and the neighbors. She favored the idea of finding high growth and being more
proactive.
Commissioner Kunze also wanted to recognize the developers for compromising. She stated they
had no idea what would happen to Lehi and felt this was a good option that would work for a
long time.

Commission Chair Jackson reopened the public hearing for all items.

Colton Smith asked for clarification regarding the green space on the plat map. He inquired
about the interest in placing a public facility in that area and wanted to know why it was allowed
to be located on that piece of property.

Jill Elmont echoed Mr. Smith's concerns and asked if the properties behind the homes would be
larger. She also had questions about traffic and wondered why this particular property was
chosen. She expressed significant concern about traffic issues.

Todd Reese asked for clarification about the triangle at the public facilities. He mentioned that
the ground had been reduced to allow for more homes. He noted that people race on Main Street,
which could create issues for STRATA if they are heading west, leading to wrecks and posing a
safety concern, along with the increased traffic from the public facility.

Megan Trent expressed high concern about traffic as well. She asked for clarification on the
three exits and their locations. She echoed concerns about the effectiveness of traffic studies,
noting that it didn't seem like anything changed even after a traffic study was concluded. She
acknowledged the compromise and felt better about what was being proposed that night.

Robert Peck (Lehi resident) noted that his street was stubbed and expected a road to be
punched through. He wanted to know when the road would be stubbed through near the cattle
trail property. He stated that not requiring three exits in and out of the neighborhood could lead
to a potential disaster.

Commission Chair Jackson closed the public hearing for all items.

Steve Maddox wanted to address some questions. He thanked the Commissioners for their time
and explained that the reason for the public facility was due to the City's division and large size.
Kim clarified that as part of the agreement, there would be smaller lot sizes, but the overall
density had to match the R1-15 zone. Not all the lots would be smaller; there would also be
larger lots. Luke stated that there were existing stub roads at 2500 West and River Way for
ingress/egress and that there was a stub road on Gray's Road that could not be built until it was
acquired. He mentioned that the traffic study would assist with this. Commissioner Lockhart
asked why the facility was being placed on Main Street and sought clarification for those with
public comments. Kim explained that with the growth on the west side, part of the need for the
facility was for emergency purposes, as all current facilities were on the east side. More space
was needed due to outgrowing the current facilities.
