Recognizing Ogden City Recreation Supervisor Ginger Myers for saving a

life while working at the Golden Hours Center came before the Council for consideration. Council member Hyer read the

Proposed Resolution 2020-33 Amended adopting Amendment #1 to the
Consolidated Plan for the period July 1, 2020 through June 30, 2025, which
includes Amendment #1 to the Annual Action Plan for the period July 1,
2020 through June 30, 2021
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the Fiscal Year (FY) 2021-2025
Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) and Annual Action Plan Amendment #1 came before the Council for consideration. The memo
stated the Council approved Amendment# 1 to the FY2021-2025 Consolidated Plan and FY2021 Annual Action Plan (AAP) on
January 5, 2021. However, it was discovered after that approval that the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development
(HUD) required 5-day comment period had not been noticed properly. As such, the Council will need to hold a second public
hearing in conjunction with there-noticing of the HUD-required comment period. There are no changes proposed from that which
the Council approved on January 5, 2021.
          Community Development Manager Ogden summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
discuss the ConPlan and AAP amendments. He noted that an initial public hearing regarding this item was held January 5, 2021,
but the public comment period for the proposed amendments did not end until after that public hearing date. He reported the
ConPlan and AAP are a combined document in the HUD system and no changes are being proposed to the actual ConPlan;
Amendment#} only changes the AAP for the period July 1, 2020 to June 30,2021. The ConPlan is the mechanism for the City to
receive HUD funds and the application to renew entitlement status for the CDBG and HOME grants. It is also a strategic planning
tool to identify and prioritize housing, community development, and economic development needs. It outlines the City's plans to
utilize HUD funds for the next five years and if a project is not in the ConPlan, it cannot receive HUD funds. The AAP for FY
2021 is the first-year implementation plan for the five-year ConPlan; it is the City's application for CDBG and HOME entitlement
funds, defmes the programs and projects to be funded in the fiscal year, outlines the goals and accomplislunents for the year, and
sets a detailed budget for the use ofHUD funds. Amendments to the AAP are required to add or delete a program; change a

program's purpose, scope, location, or intended beneficiaries; or implement a budget change of20 percent or greater of the City's
HUD budget. The proposed FY 2021 amendment would:

               •    Adjust entitlement amounts;
               •    Update program income estimates for the year;
               •    Revise carryover budget from estimates to actual;
               •    Allocate the additional funds; and
               •    Modify goals for the year.
          The total proposed budget increase is $2.2 million and is broken down as follows:
               •    $337,595 Increase in Entitlement (Includes recent award of additional CDBG-CV);
               •    $955,792 Increase in Program Income (Updated PI estimates for FY21); and
               •    $727,038 Entitlement Carryover (Budget updated from estimate to actual).
          The goal for the Quality Neighborhoods Program will change by adding a "new housing construction" goal and applying
that goal to one new home in FY 2021. He noted the overarching goal of the Quality Neighborhoods Program is to rehabilitate and
sell seven homes to low-moderate income households and he presented photographs ofhomes that have been part of this program
in the past. In relation to infill housing, the proposal includes a budget increase to $150,000; the goal of this program is to
construct and sell six new homes in the Stone Hill project in FY 2021. A budget amendment of $1.4 million is also proposed for
the Home Exterior Loan Program (HELP); the goal of this program is to complete 20 loans for home exterior repairs. For the Own
in Ogden program, which provides down payment assistance loans to low-moderate income households, the proposed change is to
increase the goal to provide 50 loans rather than 45 in FY 2021. The Microenterprise Loan Program provides loans to low-
moderate income micro enterprise owners and the proposed change is to provide loans and/or technical assistance to applicants. A
budget amendment is proposed in the Microenterprise Loan Program; the budget will increase to $426,197 and is funded with
CARES Act monies. Budget amendments are also proposed for the Small Business Loan Program and Small Business Loan
Program CV by increasing budgets $343,852 and $426,197, respectively, ofCDBG funding for loans to businesses to create and
retain full time equivalent jobs, assist businesses in urgent need, and/or mitigate the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic. No
changes have been proposed for the Emergency Home Repair Program, the Target Area Public Improvement Program, the
Business Information Center (BIC) Program, and special economic development projects. The total proposed budget increase in
Community Development programs is $1,596,076, with the increase in Business Development programs of$424,348.
