             The purpose of the joint work session was to review the agendas for the Special Redevelopment Agency (RDA) and
    regular City Council meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.; participate in Council/Mayor/Fire Department discussion; discuss
    Fiscal Year (FY) 2021 Budget amendments pertaining to miscellaneous grants, storm sewer utilities, and the Continental
    Community Reinvestment Area (CRA); a FY 2021 RDA Budget amendment pertaining to the Continental CRA; the FY 2021
    Capital Improvement Plan (CIP)- and more specifically, water meters; storm water low impact design development standards;
    discuss Council/Board business; and hear Council/Board comments.

    Agenda Review
              Chair Choberka and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agendas for the special RDA and regular
    City Council business meetings. There was a brief focus on the Council agenda item dealing with design standards for residential
    development in commercial zones; Council Deputy Director Symes facilitated discussion among the Council regarding changes
    and adjustments that have been made to the draft ordinance since the Council's last discussion of the issue and since the
    publication of the packet materials and ordinance document. He presented optional amendment language for the Council to
    consider during the business meeting in regard to inclusion of elech·ic vehicle charging stations in multi-family projects of a
    certain unit size; bonus density based upon a project's proximity to a bus rapid transit (BRT) stop; and adding multiple-family
    dwellings with commercial on the ground floor as a permitted use in the C-P/CP-2 and C-3/CP-3 zones. There was also a brief
    discussion about the reasoning behind selecting certain Enterprise Green development standards, but not all, in the multi-family
    design standards. Councilmember White stated that it feels counterintuitive to not incorporate all Enterprise Green standards,
    especially when developers are given advantages based on their proximity to tr·ansit. Planning Manager Montgome1y stated that

Fiscal Year 2021 City Budget Amendment- Miscellaneous Grants
          The City Council will be considering a proposed budget amendment in the amount of $876,999, to recognize and
appropriate various grant funds and adjust other current fund accounts.
          Senior Accountant Jensen explained the proposed budget amendment will impact a few funds; it includes fund transfers
and recognition of grants for the following:
               •   Carry-forward of $20,000 for 2020 Census funds;
               •   Transfer of $69,257 of City funds for a grant match increase for 20 th Street improvements;
               • Various sidewalk, park, amphitheater and trail improvements; and
               •   Combining and moving various Harrison Boulevard project funds into the Capital Improvement Plan (CIP)
                   roadway account;
          All required grant matches are proposed to be addressed through existing account funds relative to the various projects.
          Council Policy Analyst Mabey noted that a few of the projects that are proposed for funding through this budget
amendment are not included in the adopted CIP document; if a project has never been in the CIP or has not been fully funded in
the past, a CIP amendment will be necessary, in addition to the budget amendment. If the project has been fully funded in the past
and additional funded is needed to continue the projects outlined in the initial CIP brief, the City's Legal Division has determined
that a CIP amendment is not required. Council staff is working with Administration to ensure proper record keeping for CIP
projects and adjustments in funding allocations for certain projects. She then reviewed the proposed timing for action on these
budget amendments; ifthere are no objections from the Council, the public hearing can be scheduled for October 20, 2020. No
objections were voiced.

         Fiscal Year 2021 Redevelopment Agency (RDA) Budget Amendment -
         Continental Community Reinvestment Area:
                  Administration has requested Council consideration of a proposed budget amendment which would allocate $600,000 of
         Business Depot Ogden (BDO) Lease Revenue funds to the RDA for the Continental Project Area. This would increase the project
         account to $866,231. If approved, the proposal is for $570,000 of these funds to be used to pay off the current Ogden Industrial
         Development Corporation (OIDC) loan and for the remaining account balance of$296,23 l to be used for plat work, architectural
         and civil design work for the old Browning Ice Cream Building and for additional environmental remediation at the site.
         Additionally, the RDA Board will review and discuss a proposed budget amendment that will increase the FY2021 Agency budget
         by $600,000. Tue proposed budget amendment recognizes additional revenues transferred from Ogden City BDO Lease Revenue
         ($600,000) and appropriates those revenues to the Continental CRA to retire debt owed to the OIDC ($570,000) and for additional
         environmental and other costs associated with the site ($30,000).
                  Comptroller Stout summarized the budget amendment proposal and also provided an overview of the balance available of
         BDO Lease Revenue retained earnings; the balance as of July 1, 2020 was $6. 9 million. Tue FY 2020-2021 budget contemplates
         allocations to various projects, including the Hostess Site/Continental CRA; Gramercy Square; Gibson Grove, and the Swift
         Building site. If these allocations are approved, the uncommitted balance will be reduced to $4.6 million.
                  Community and Economic Development Deputy Director Cooper then provided an overview of the expenditures that
         have been made to date in the CRA, including property acquisition, site work and demolition, and debt service payments. One
         third-party property owner that the City has been working with to ensure the success of the project area plan is the Utah State
         Courts; they own a piece of the western parking area that is vital for the success of the property and Administration is working
         with them to negotiate a land transfer and development agreement to protect the interests of both parties. The City is seeking this
         $600,000 appropriation to repay the principal due to OIDC, fund plat work, architectural and civil design work for the old
         Browning Ice Cream Building, and additional enviromnental remediation at the site. He noted he will provide more detailed
         information regarding future anticipated costs associated with improvements of properties in the CRA.

         Fiscal Year 2021 Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) - Water Meters
                   Tue Council received a transmittal from the Administration with a requested amendment to the Fiscal Year 2021-2025
         CIP plan. The city is need ofreplacing old water meters with new meters that can be read remotely. The intention is to increase
         meter reading efficiency, accuracy and access to water information for residents. If approved, outdated water meters are
         anticipated to be replaced in Fiscal Year 2021. The equipment costs are $1,000,000 and water bonds are anticipated to be used for
         project funding.
                   Public Utilities Manager Herd explained this proposed CIP project would provide for upgrades and service improvements
         in the Water Division; it will make it possible to replace and upgrade water meters to allow for remote reading. It will also
         improve data collections from new meters. He explained the Planning Commission voted unanimously to recmmnend approval of
         the CIP amendment with a determination that this project is consistent with the General Plan by creating a more reliable means to
         obtain water usage infonnation and providing an ability to better detect system usage problems.
                   Council member Hyer inquired as to when the updated meter reading system will be fully functional throughout the
         entire City. Mr. Herd stated that efforts are underway to increase the coverage area for upgraded meters and remote reading, but
         he does not have concrete data regarding the coverage areas as this time. He believes it will take approximately one year to
         complete the water meter project. The focus of the Water Utility has been on the accuracy of the data that is being collected via
         remote reading.
