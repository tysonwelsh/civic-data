My Hometown Initiative Presenter: Jennica Jones, Strategies and Innovations Division
Manager, Craig and Linda Burton, My Hometown Initiative Utah Association Directors

Mr. Burton introduced himself and his wife. They are the associate directors of the
MyHometown initiative program. Mr. and Mrs. Baum, the Provo City Chairs, are in attendance.
Mr. Burton explained that the purpose of MyHometown is to organize city, neighborhood and
multi-faith resources to revitalize deteriorating areas in the cities where we serve. The origin of
the program comes from the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints. The Burtons have been
called as church service missionaries. The plan is to become partners with the city. The
MyHometown program works in three ways: Community Resource Centers (CRC),
neighborhood services and help with education.

Mr. Burton showed a video of an overview of the program. The video illustrates the
program as it is running in West Valley City. The program began in 2021 in West Valley City in
one community. In 2023, 11 communities were added from Ogden, West Valley City, Salt Lake
City and Provo. The second video shown was of Mayor Kaufusi explaining the benefits of the
program in Provo. The 2023 statistics for the program are 608 MyHometown weekly volunteers,
12,400 Days of Service volunteers, 65,458 Days of Service hours, 1,302 City and Home
Improvement projects, 1,125 teachers and volunteers, 2,651 attending classes and 33,843 CRC
hours. Mr. Burton explained that the LDS church is the springboard for the programs and they
use the churches for the programs because they are often vacant during the week, but the desire
is to open it up and be part of the community.

The CRC will provide classes that the community needs, such programs can include
English for Spanish speakers classes, piano lessons, sewing classes, computer classes, family
history, health and fitness, art, music, home repair/home maintenance, finance/budgeting, etc.
The neighborhood resources part of the program includes Days of Service. On a Day of Service
typically there will be 15 projects and 120 to 150 volunteers. Provo City does 6 services days a
year. The MyHometown Organization was displayed with the Area Presidency at the top and
then volunteer missionaries for the organization of the program.

The City Council asked questions about the program and its needs. Mr. and Mrs. Baum
explained that the classes in Provo catered to the needs of the neighborhood. They asked the
neighborhood what classes they would like to see in their area. They found that this serves the
needs of the area best. The City Council is interested in the program and would like to see this
program come into the Orem community. The program supports the idea of neighbor helping
neighbor. Questions were raised about the cost of the program. Mr. Burton explained that upfront
there are no costs. The church provides money for the CRC but the city will provide funds for
the community project. Provo, for example, gives $50,000 to each of the communities being
served per year for Days of Service, dumpsters, employee help, equipment rental, and
community activities. Other funds come from private donations, church funding, partnerships
with Habitat for Humanity, United Way and Community Action. Mr. Bybee believes this
program would enhance the effort already started in the City and that the City would be able to
fund this program.

Hillcrest Video Tour Presenter: Bryce Merrill, Library and Recreation Director

Mr. Merrill presented a video tour of the Hillcrest Center. He explained that the center is
nearing completion of its transformation into a resource for our city. The tour will be presented
by Mr. Lane Gray and Mr. Merrill. The tour began in the classroom which will host many of the
preschool classes, after-school programs and evening community programs. The gymnasium has
been painted for a full length basketball court, volleyball court or three pickleball courts. The
family room area will include a snackbar, pool tables, video games etc. The patio area will have
table tennis and a nine square court. Pickleball courts will be painted when the weather warms

and the splash pad will open this summer, as well. The Hillcrest Center will allow for programs
for the residents. The park plans to be fully open in the Spring of 2024.

Amending Article 22-5-3(A) and the zoning map of the City of Orem by rezoning the
property located generally at 519 South Geneva Road from the R20 zone to the C2 zone
Presenter: Jason Bench, Assistant Development Services Director

Mr. Bench explained that the applicant has requested a rezone of the outlined area located
at 519 South Geneva Road. The two parcels to the north are currently zoned as C2. The parcel in
question is zoned R20. The applicant plans to put in an overall cohesive shopping center at that
location to replace the nursery currently at the location. The proposed center will include a
grocery store, bank and a fast food restaurant. Mr. Bench reported that the planning commission
recommended that the City Council approve the rezone of the parcel.

Mr. Holdaway, the applicant, answered questions about the planned project. He explained
that he is not under contract with any potential businesses. He wanted to get the C2 zone
approval for the entire area before signing contracts. Mr. Spencer expressed concerns about only
having one entrance for the development. Mr. Bench supported that one entrance is sufficient for
the project. There are two lanes to enter and two lanes to exit. This is a consolidated larger access
that is further from the intersection and will be more efficient and less congestive than the two
smaller entrances that are currently on the property.

Ms. Millett asked about the option of a C3 zone, since this project backs up to a
residential area. Mr. Holdaway has been focused on the zoning approval, when that is complete
then he will begin approaching developers and tenants. He sees no reason to go any taller in his
development than what is currently illustrated in the drawing. He explained that the plan is to
add trees along the mason wall between the shopping center and the homes. Mr. Bench explained
that a development agreement could be used to set limits. Mr. Earl said this is allowable but it
would have to go back to the Planning Commission.

Ordinance Amending Fiscal Year 2023-2024 Budget Presenter: Brandon C Nelson, CPA,
Finance Director

Mr. Nelson is here to amend the FY 2023-2024 budget. He showed the revenues and
expenditures spreadsheet. He focused his discussion on the revenues. The first adjustment to the
revenues comes from grants and donations received. Some of the larger grants received are the
DOJ-COPS hiring program for mental health officers for $132,704. Major crimes received the
HIDTA Grant for $298,756. The second revenue is the CIP fund transfer for Northridge Park in
the amount of $250,000. The third revenue discussion item is for the vehicle maintenance fund to
replace the column jacks in the fleet department. The funds have been saved and are now being
transferred to fund the purchase. The fourth revenue is for the Self Insurance Fund to replace a
vehicle that was totaled. The fifth revenue is the CIP fund transfer for the Hillcrest property. The
project is impact fee eligible and this will reimburse the fund used by the city to purchase
Hillcrest. The sixth revenue discussion item is the money paid by UPS to rent the Public Works
parking lot and the seventh revenue is from the City of Lindon to use our victim advocate
services. This is the budget amendments that are proposed at this time.

Ms. Millett asked about the victim advocate services provided to the City of Lindon.
Captain Speith explained that this agreement allows the City to recoup some of the funds used to
services the needs in Lindon. He explained that this service has only been provided for one year
and so the evaluation of fees has not been done yet. The data is being collected to facilitate that
evaluation in upcoming months. Mr. Bybee said this is a concern that Exec Staff has been
discussing. They are regularly looking at the different agreements Orem has with other agencies
to make sure that what we are charging keeps up with our rising costs.
