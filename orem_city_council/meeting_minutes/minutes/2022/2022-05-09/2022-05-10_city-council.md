31          DISSCUSSION - Municipal Wastewater Planning Program
32
33   Mr. Price introduced the Municipal Wastewater Planning Program (MWPP) to the Council and
34   stated the MWPP uses an annual survey to assist owners of municipal sewerage systems and
35   wastewater treatment works in evaluating and summarizing the technical, operational, and
36   financial conditions of and requirements for these facilities. He expressed the purpose of MWPP
37   is to help communities better understand these business operations, and to help identify and
38   resolve potential problem areas before they become serious and costly. After this, Mr. Price
39   presented the Water Resources Division organizational chart, and went through the roles of

19          DISSCUSSION - CARE Major Grant Applications
20
21   Mr. Bybee provided handouts to the Council which showed funding amounts requested from
22   Hale Center Theater, SCERA Center for the Arts, and the Utah Metropolitan Ballet (UMB). This
23   handout also included how much CARE funding the city has given them in past years. For the
24   2022-2023 fiscal year, Hale requested $1.27 million dollars, Scera requested $759 thousand, and
25   UMB requested $76 thousand.
26
27   Representatives from Hale, Scera, and UMB detailed how their operations changed, and how
28   their companies were affected during the COVID-19 pandemic.
29
30   Mr. Bybee continued with a presentation detailing CARE applications for fiscal year 2023. He
31   stated there were around 21 different requests totaling 4.4 million dollars, and expressed the city
32   has a little over $3 million dollars of CARE funding to distribute. Mr. Bybee then went through
33   city-related arts applications. These included historic photo digitization, upgraded equipment for
34   the Library Hall, a new Scera Pool, branding, art, and public art displays in the city’s Arts
35   District. Mr. Bybee continued to discuss the city recreation applications, which included items
36   such as a “swimwall” for the Family Fitness Center, upgraded equipment for the Scera pool, and
37   funding for co-sponsored groups. Mr. Bybee concluded by reviewing the city parks applications,
38   which included funding for Hillcrest Park, field lighting and artificial turf at Lakeside Park, and
39   City Center Park WIFI improvements.

38          DISSCUSSION – Residential Occupancy Limits
39




           City Council Minutes – May 10, 2022, (pg.3)           A complete video of the meeting can be found at
                                                                            www.orem.org/meetings
 1   Mr. Spencer stated this topic is a follow up to two young women who came and spoke to the
 2   Council during the public comment portion of a past Council meeting. He stated during their
 3   comments, they expressed concern regarding residential occupancy limits, and asked Mr. Earl to
 4   explain how the city has gotten to the current standard of three unrelated people maximum living
 5   in a house at one time.
 6
 7   Mr. Earl stated this regulation comes from a state code which says cities can limit residential
 8   occupancy to three unrelated parties if there is a major university within the city. He also stated
 9   that if there isn’t a major university in the city, you cannot limit occupancy to less than four
10   unrelated parties. Mr. Earl concluded the city has the residential occupancy limit set to three
11   mainly due to parking concerns.
12
13   Mayor Young stated he understands where these women are coming from, and is sympathetic to
14   their problem. He then expressed how it seems really tricky to make a change like this actually
15   happen.
16
17   Mr. Downs agreed with Mayor Young’s comments and expressed the city is trying to not make
18   buying single family homes and renting them out to college students any more attractive than it
19   already is to investors. He then explained how he feels just raising the unrelated parties limit
20   straight across will entice investors to do this. Mayor Young responded and expressed how
21   increasing the occupancy limit could be going in the opposite direction of affordable housing.
22
23   Mrs. Millett stated some of the potential problems which could come with raising the occupancy
24   limit. These included parking four cars while still maintaining the 50% landscaping requirement,
25   enforcement issues, and encouraging the use of single-family homes as investment properties,
26   thus diminishing housing stock available for first-time-buyers and raising housing prices
27   throughout the city, creating an affordable housing issue.
28
29   Mrs. Lauret and Mr. Spencer asked if the city could just give the young women in this example
30   an exception. Mr. Earl responded they need a rule that applies generally to everybody, and
31   should not give exceptions to that rule.
32
33   The young women who brought up this topic asked about various different possibilities for
34   raising this occupancy limit while still addressing parking concerns.
35
36   Coming to no definitive answer, the Council expressed they would have to discuss this issue with
37   the legal department further, and see what possibilities they could produce.

29          PUBLIC HEARING / RESOLUTION – Adopting the City of Orem Fiscal Year 2022-
30          2023 Tentative Budget
31
32   Mr. Nelson started by expressing that the Council is not voting to adopt the tentative budget
33   today, but are just voting to accept it, followed by a vote to adopt the final budget in June. Mr.
34   Nelson presented the Council with various taxes and fee changes. He discussed changes and
35   projected increases in sales tax, property tax, franchise tax, and building/business service
36   changes.
37
38   Mayor Young stated when he moved into his house, his property taxes were $800 dollars, and
39   are now $2,800 dollars. He then asked Mr. Nelson which parts of his property tax have been
40   going up. Mr. Nelson responded and expressed that more than likely the portions of property tax
