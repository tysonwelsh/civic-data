Director Rae noted an amendment requested in the Police fund to accommodate anticipated raises and
added this item had not been translated into the previously presented budget. The revised budget had
been adjusted to reflect those numbers. Director Rae offered to answer questions.

Councilmember Shields asked about revenue from the summer events like the rodeo and enduro cross.
He asked where the line item for this revenue was outlined. Finance Director Rae oriented the Council
as to where to find the corresponding revenue amounts. Councilmember Shields asked if these were
conservative revenue figures to which Director Rae responded the events staff provided expected
revenue amounts based on prior year actual numbers. Councilmember Shields clarified events exceed

there were increased budget requests for seminars, training, and office supplies. Finance Director Rae

Driver’s License holders.

Councilmember Shields reiterated the increase for office supplies and asked how the number had come
to fruition. Assistant City Manager Moody replied the Administration Department ordered supplies for
the all departments and due to inflation, costs have increased across the board. She also observed the
previous two years reduction of office supplies due to the closure of City Hall during the COVID-19
pandemic and there weren’t as many people in the office. The increase of office supplies also takes
into consideration the additional staff being brought on board. Assistant City Manager Moody offered
specific instances on supplies where the cost had doubled over the last year. Councilmember Shields
expressed his appreciation for explaining the thought process behind the budgeting allocation request.
City Manager Cherpeski explained there seemed to be purchases lumped into general line items and
staff had been working to outline software purchases for different departments to bring more
transparency to the budget document. His desire was to change the way the City was budgeting to
separate capital projects versus operational costs.

previous years expenditures and revenues. He acknowledged some departments outlined their

Operations Director Johnson discussed the bids for the damaged wires in the park vault pavilion. He
showed a picture of where the damage had occurred and said the vault had been a problem for the
park for a few years. He offered a brief synopsis of the damage and recommended switching out the
600-amp breaker for a 400-amp breaker due to the cable not being adequately protected which caused
the need to replace the entire line from the meter to the pavilion. Director Johnson reported the
anticipated cost for the project was slightly more than $170,000, and other contractors have been
contacted to get additional opinions.

Operations Director Johnson highlighted the “brown out” and staff preformed tests and realized there
had been a loss of power. Rocky Mountain Power come out and isolated the problem. Director Johnson
explained the power ran out to a vault in the road, Vault A, then turned and went down to the sidewalk
to Vault B, and there it split and went to the ball diamonds and south to Vault C just before the pavilion,
then it fed into the main vault in the pavilion. The problem was identified in Vault B. Historically,
vaults lasted for twenty years, although power usage at the pavilion had changed over the years which
caused some problems. Director Johnson reported other companies had suggested replacing the leg
of power from Vault A to Vault B, which would be a significantly lower cost. The two received were
$45,000 and $57,000. He said the companies recommended replacing the existing line from Vault A

the insulation test revealed any additional issues. He reported he had received estimates on the other

    2.3. High Speed Internet Task Force update - Blake Thomas, Community Development Director

community would allow the City to effectively manage flood control, traffic signals, detention basins,
fire services, and police services. The Task Force reviewed the best option for the community as a

      9.1 Discussion and consideration of amendments to Chapter 10-3 to specify trails in the
definition of a Public Park, and Chapter 10-16 to allow a Public Park as a permitted use in all
Agricultural, Residential, Recreational, and MU-2 Mixed Use Zones in City Code (City File No.
Z2022-058) - Clint Spencer, Planning Manager
Planning Manager Spencer presented the amendment and provided a timeline. There were two
requests with this proposal, one being to include a trail as a public park, and the second, to amend the
land use table. The land use table had been brought to the Planning Commission and it showed public
parks as a permitted use in all zones except commercial and manufacturing. The Planning
Commission’s motion was the change this to accept the trail as a public park in definition but to keep
the parks as a conditional use in all zones, including commercial and manufacturing. He noted the City
was implementing policies for all public parks to go through the public notice process and the Planning
Commission would hear it in a work session meeting for their consideration and input. He showed the
zoning map, the open space map, and the parks master plan.

Councilmember Shields clarified a comment had been made earlier about making impulsive decisions,
and how the process would vary from what the Planning Commission recommended and said this
would have notification, public hearing, and the Planning Commission would still review and submit
comments to the Council. When something like this was a budgetary item, City Council was the body
which made the decisions over budgetary concerns. He didn’t like public funds being spent when they
weren’t allocated by public officials which was why he was in support of this. He said this was not a
shortcut process and asked City Attorney Andrizzi to speak to the comment. City Attorney Andrizzi
stated there was plenty of room for public input to speak on park amenities, location of trails, etc. This
would not circumvent a public process. Councilmember Henderson said they wanted the Planning
Commission’s recommendations but ultimately it was the City Council which made the decisions on
these kinds of things. City Attorney Andrizzi said one of the things he had brought up to the Planning
Commission was the conditional use process did not grant authority to the body to implement
whatever changes desired. They could only impose changes to mitigate concerns of the use and
