REVIEW AGENDA FOR REGULAR CITY COUNCIL MEETING OF
FEBRUARY 21, 2023

this night.

COMMUNICATIONS
A.  LEGISLATIVE UPDATE
    Lincoln Shurtz, Government Relations Director, provided an update on current bills
    being discussed during the current Legislative Session including: homeless
    services, highway patrol funding on Bangerter Highway and Mountain View
    Corridor, Olympic venue funding for the Maverik Center, charter schools, and land
    use Development Agreements.

       The Mayor and Council discussed the shelter/homeless service temporary and
       permanent shelter bill. They agreed to accepting a temporary shelter at the old
       liquor store building on Redwood Road and approximately 3400 South but noted
       that they would like the agreement to include a statutory statement that precludes
       West Valley City from offering the service or providing a permanent shelter again
       in the future.

       The Mayor and Council had no further questions or concerns.

B.     BANGERTER HIGHWAY DISCUSSION
       Dan Johnson, Public Works Director, provided an update on the Bangerter
       Highway expansion project. He indicated that UDOT is moving forward with an
       interchange system and noted that this would eliminate access to 3100 South from
       Bangerter Highway. Dan stated that this is contrary to what the City requested in
       2021 via resolution.

       Dan stated that 4700 South is currently going through the design process and unless
       there is a significant concern found, this will be designed with the freeway going
       under. He noted that once everything is solidified, the Council will be reviewing a
       funding agreement for the City’s contribution.

       Dan indicated that ground water is much shallower at 4100 South and so shallow
       further north that going under will not be possible at 3500 South and further. He
       displayed two options proposed by UDOT for 4100 South. Dan stated that the first
       would be a hybrid option that would require UDOT to build the ground up 10 feet
       in order for the road to go under. He indicated that the second option would be to
       have Bangerter go over 4100 South. Dan displayed images of what these two
       options may look like. He noted that the hybrid option would cost the City an
       additional 4 million dollars. Councilmember Whetstone asked if one of these
       options has less impact on homes and businesses. Dan replied that they are both
       about the same as far as acquisition goes. Councilmember Christensen asked if the
       church on 4100 South and Bangerter will remain. Dan replied no and indicated that
       the entire parcel would be acquired. Mayor Lang stated that residents in that area
       felt strongly about the highway going under so she would support that continuing
       to be the goal. Councilmember Fitisemanu asked if funding can be obtained from
       grants or other possibilities. Dan replied that it’s possible and the City would try to
       pursue funding but noted it would be best to budget for that full amount.

       Mayor Lang asked if additional public meetings will be provided to residents since
       UDOT has committed to the interchange system. Dan replied yes and stated that
       these will begin in the next 2-3 months.
