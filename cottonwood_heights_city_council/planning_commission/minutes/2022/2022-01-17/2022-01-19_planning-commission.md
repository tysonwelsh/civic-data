38   Project ZTA-22-001 was overviewed by Associate City Planner, Samantha DeSeelhorst. She
39   explained that the zoning text amendment related to the Water Efficiency Landscaping Standards.
40   Rick Malloy from the Central Utah Water Conservancy District was available to answer questions
41   and share additional information. Ms. DeSeelhorst reported that the City was looking at water
42   because water is limited in Utah and the population in Utah is growing. There are many ways to
43   conserve water, but the presentation would focus on landscaping. Utah State University estimated
44   that Utah residents use approximately two-thirds of the drinking/culinary water for landscaping.
45   That figure was largely due to overirrigation.
46



     Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 01/19/2022                                      5
 1   Some State level planning further illustrated the need to look at water conservation. For instance,
 2   there were a lot of bills related to water conservation being considered at the 2022 Utah Legislative
 3   Session. Two of the proposed bills would prevent cities from requiring lawns in order to remove
 4   lawn minimums. In addition, State agencies were looking at water conservation methods as well
 5   as a coordinated action plan.
 6
 7   One of the goals identified by the Governor for water conservation was to expand incentive
 8   programs. Ms. DeSeelhorst explained that two existing programs that fulfilled that need were the
 9   Flip Your Strip program, which is a rebate for park strip conversions, and the Localscapes Rewards
10   Program, which is a rebate for yard conversions. Historically, those programs were only available
11   for households served by the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District (“JVWCD”). Cottonwood
12   Heights has several commercial properties that were serviced by the district, but the bulk of the
13   City is under the Central Utah Water Conservancy District. A lot of residents were interested in
14   the programs but were not eligible. In August 2021, the incentives were expanded to households
15   in Salt Lake County. Communities that want to continue to be eligible for the incentive programs
16   need to adopt water conservation landscaping standards by July 2022.
17
18   Ms. DeSeelhorst highlighted the proposed standards. The language states that the standards would
19   be applicable to commercial and residential new construction. The irrigation standards require the
20   use of drip irrigation, except for in lawn areas as well as the use of efficiently designed irrigation.
21   There were also standards related to landscape beds and lawns. Additionally, there were
22   Localscapes Principles for Residential Landscapes listed. Chair Coutts asked about the Central
23   Open Shape requirement. Mr. Malloy explained that historically, as homes were developed, the
24   default landscape was lawn. Lawn would be placed from the curb to the house and as an
25   afterthought, an area would be carved out for a planting bed. That created challenges with
26   irrigation efficiency. Rather than follow that direction, the requirement would encourage the lawn
27   to be designed for functionality. Ms. DeSeelhorst shared an image for reference.

34   Associate City Planner, Samantha DeSeelhorst overviewed Project ZTA-22-001. The zoning text
35   amendment was related to the Water Efficiency Landscaping Standards. Rick Malloy and
36   Savannah Peterson from the Central Utah Water Conservancy District (“CUWCD”) were available
37   to answer questions and share additional information. Ms. DeSeelhorst explained that the City
38   was looking at water because water was limited in Utah. Additionally, there was a steadily
39   climbing population rate in Utah. Those two issues created scarcity concerns and it was important
40   to conserve water.
41
42   There were many different ways to conserve water, but the presentation would focus specifically
43   on landscaping. Utah State University estimated that Utah residents used approximately two-thirds
44   of the potable water for landscaping irrigation. That figure was largely due to overirrigation. As
45   a result, a focus on landscaping could potentially address water conservation goals. When the
46   water used for landscaping was reduced, it was free for other important life-dependent functions.

 2   Ms. DeSeelhorst explained that this was an important topic that was being discussed throughout
 3   the State. In the current Legislative Session, which started on January 18, 2022, there were a
 4   handful of water bills related to water conservation. Additionally, an action plan was being
 5   developed by the Governor and other State entities. One of the goals identified by the Governor
 6   as it related to water conservation and landscaping was to expand incentive programs.
 7   Ms. DeSeelhorst explained that two existing programs fulfilled that need: Flip Your Strip, which
 8   was a rebate for park strip conversions, and the Localscapes Rewards program, which was a rebate
 9   for yard conversions. Historically, those programs were only available for households that were
10   served by Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District (“JVWCD”).
11
12   Cottonwood Heights has several commercial properties that are serviced by the district, but the
13   bulk of the City is under the CUWCD. To facilitate the expansion of those incentives, CUWCD
14   began offering the incentive programs to all of the households in Salt Lake County. Communities
15   that wanted to continue to be eligible for the incentive programs needed to adopt water
16   conservation landscaping standards by July 2022.
17
18   Ms. DeSeelhorst highlighted the proposed standards. The language stated that the standards would
19   be applicable to commercial and residential new construction. The irrigation standards required
20   the use of drip irrigation, except for in lawn areas and the use of efficiently designed irrigation.
21   There were also standards related to landscape beds and lawns. Additionally, there were
22   Localscapes Principles for Residential Landscapes listed. Those principles related to the increased
23   efficiency and functionality of Utah yards. An example image was shown to highlight a traditional
24   yard and a yard that incorporated water-efficient strategies.

27   would be conservation standards. He asked for additional information about the standards. Ms.
28   DeSeelhorst explained that the standards would apply to new construction and the standards would
29   be recommended for residents that wanted to electively update their yards. For the latter, residents
30   could take advantage of the incentive programs, if they were eligible. Commissioner Allen
31   wondered if other municipalities had adopted similar standards. Ms. DeSeelhorst stated that
