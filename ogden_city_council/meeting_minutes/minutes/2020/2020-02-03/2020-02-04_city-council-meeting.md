Justice Court Operations Report
          Representatives from the Ogden City Justice Court will review and discuss Court operations. Council members requested
this presentation during the recertification process in October 2019.
          Justice Court Judges Junk and Stucki approached the Council and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide
the Justice Court Operations Report. Judge Junk reported the mission of the Utah Justice Courts is to improve the quality oflife in
the State's communities. The Ogden City Justice Court has adopted this mission statement; the Court aims to provide hope, a
positive experience, and rehabilitation to the people it services. It also aims to reduce recidivism. He discussed the history of the
Court, which was established in July of2006; prior to that time, cases were heard at the District Court in Ogden. Cases handled by
the Justice Court are all Class Band C misdemeanors that occur in Ogden City. This includes all traffic infractions and small
claims up to $11,000. The Justice Court hired seasoned Court Clerks who were instrumental in establishing quality and effective
processes that are intended to serve those who find themselves in the Court. He then stated that he and Judge Stucki both moved to
the Justice Court in 2017 and they have very similar philosophies about their role in helping and serving the community. Rather
than focusing on the matter that brought an individual before them in court, they focus on what they can do to make sure they do
not come before them again. They treat everyone with respect and try to be very clear with the expectations they have regarding
the punishment they order. Both judges have met with program coordinators in the community to see what they can offer to
individuals being sentenced by the Justice Court. Some of the community partners that provide support for these individuals
include Cottages of Hope, Your Community Connection (YCC), Lantern House, Weber Mental Health, and Private Probation
Services (PPS). Each of these partners share a common goal ofrehabilitation. A new program that has recently been implemented
in the area is a sobriety program; individuals in the program are tested during morning and evening hours with the goal of keeping
them from consuming alcohol for a one-year period. The program is still in its infancy in this area, but 14 people have completed
the program and there are 44 people cmTently enrolled. Over a one-year period, 21,755 breath tests were conducted and there were
just 18 positive tests. This led to a discussion regarding the difference between mandatory and voluntary programs aimed at
rehabilitating an offender; there was a brief focus on the cost associated with various rehabilitation programs.
          The Council then inquired as to what support or resources the Justice Court needs from the City. Judge Junk stated
technology upgrades would be very helpful given that there have been so many technological advancements in policing and
presentation tools.
          Judge Stucki proceeded with the PowerPoint presentation, focusing on why the Justice Comt is important for addressing
misdemeanor crimes; proactive enforcement of misdemeanor crimes significantly reduces all crime and improves community
quality of life according to the Committee on Law and Justice, National Academies of Sciences Engineering and Medicine.
Additionally, most citizens cmTectly prosecuted for misdemeanor crimes do change the unwanted bad behavior. He also discussed
the following key elements of procedural justice:
                • Voice: Participants must be given a voice and an opportunity to be heard.
                • Neutrality: People want decisions to be made by an unbiased, neutral authority based on the facts and the
                    impmtial and consistent application of reasonable and understandable rules and policies.
                •   Transparency: Authorities such as judges need not only to be fair but also to be seen as fair. This means that
                    they need to have transparent procedures and to explain those procedures and the decisions that develop
                    through them in ways that allow people to understand both what they need to do before and after the decision
                    and how the decision will be made.

Proposed rezone of 206 13th Street
          Council Deputy Director Symes explained a petition has been filed by Jonathan Oliver to rezone property at 206 13°1
Street from the cun-ent R-1-6 zone to the R-3 zone. The petitioner has indicated that the purpose of the rezone is to develop multi-
family housing. The proposed rezone is supported by the Mountain View Community Plan and the Planning Commission has
forwarded a recommendation of approval to the Council.
          Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the Council with additional
information about the rezone petition. The subject property is 18,295 square feet with an existing single-family home; the home
has been 'gutted' and the owner would like to demolish it to facilitate the construction of multi-family townhome structure. The
Planning Commission considered the General Plan policies for guidance in consideration of the petition; zoning must reflect
prevailing charter of the area, provide a transition from single family and commercial uses, and follow parcel lines. He noted that
the Planning Commission felt the petition is consistent with those policies and they noted the property to the east of the subject
property is a non-conforming group dwelling containing two duplexes. They also found the proposed use is consistent with the
Mountain View Community Plan; it will support multi-family development at the north end of Lincoln Avenue, provide for the
integration of multi-family uses, and allow mixed residential development to meet a variety of housing types and income needs.
Mr. Montgomery then provided an aerial photograph of the subject property and sun-ounding area to orient the Council to the
location of the 'transition area' according to the Mountain View Community Plan. He concluded the Planning Commission voted
unanimously to recommend approval of the zone change.
          Vice Chair Blair inquired as to the type of project that can be built on the property according to the R-3 zoning
designation. Mr. Montgomery noted that any multi-family less than eight units could be constructed.
          Council member Hyer disclosed that he personally knows the petitioner.
          Council member White asked if there is public transit service on Lincoln Avenue. Mr. Montgomery answered no;
residents would need to access public transit from 12th Street.
          Council member Hyer asked the petitioner to summarize his development plan. Mr. Oliver stated he will build
townhomes on the site; he has considered piping the ditch on the site to maximize the number of units he can build, but others
have recommended that he keep it open. He will erect fencing around the ditch that does not talce away from the aesthetics from
that feature of the property. He has worked to acquire additional properties on the street and the surrounding area to replace
existing housing with the townhome product that he typically constructs.

Proposed rezone of 117 Doxey Street
         A petition has been filed by Manuel Vazquez to rezone a small single lot at 117 Doxey Street from the current C-2 zone
to the R-4 zone. The petitioner indicates that the rezone is being requested for the development of a new single-family home on
the property. The Planning Commission reviewed the petition and is recommending approval of the rezone.
         Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the Council with additional
infonnation about the rezone petition; the petitioner would like to build a single-family home on the property, which is currently a
vacant lot. The property is 6,534 square feet and is too small to develop multi-family housing according to regulations for the C-2
zoning designation. It is also too small to develop a substantial commercial use. The parcel fronts along Doxey Street, which is
mostly R-4 and developed with single-family homes. The Jefferson Community Plan supports maintaining the single-family use in
the core of the community and with the extension ofDoxey Street, additional residential connections were created. The Planning
Commission voted unanimously to recommend approval of the zone change.
         There was a brief discussion about the dimensions of the lot and the development standards included in the R-4 zone,
after which Mr. Symes indicated that once the proposed rezone has been properly noticed, an action item will be included on a
future City Council meeting agenda.
