26   CED Director, Michael Johnson reported that the first agenda item is related to a request from AJ
27   Rock, LLC for an ordinance and zone map amendment of approximately 21.5 acres of property
28   located at 6695 South Wasatch Boulevard. The proposal is to utilize the City’s Planned
29   Development District (PDD) ordinance and change the zoning designation from F-1-21 (Foothill
30   Residential) to PDD-2, a zoning designation prepared specifically for the subject property by the
31   applicant, within the guidelines of Chapter 19.51 of the City’s Zoning ordinance.
32
33   Mr. Johnson shared the updated site plan and stated that between the native hillside and trail, newly
34   landscaped areas, and plaza space, open space will account for 53.9% of the total site. He noted
35   that parking information had not been changed but charts related to building coverage were added.
36   The site plan provided both pervious and impervious coverage, with the total building footprint
37   amounting to 18.9%, hardscape at 27.2%, and the landscape comprising 53.9% of the total site.
38   The lot sizes were currently unknown but the square footage of the footprint was included as part
39   of the update.

 3   Mr. Johnson discussed the grading plan and noted that the PDD ordinance states that a 10-foot
 4   berm is required between public streets and parking areas. The applicant proposed landscaping
 5   and gabion walls to visually obstruct the surface parking area from the public street, rather than a
 6   10-foot berm. With the grade change between the proposed parking area and Wasatch Boulevard,
 7   the vertical impact of the landscaping would be comparable to a 10-foot berm. Mr. Johnson
 8   reported that the ARC recommended the gabion wall treatment.
 9
10   Commissioner Wilde asked about outstanding issues with the proposal. Mr. Johnson reported that
11   the staff memo detailed all outstanding issues but staff felt that the proposal was ready to move
12   forward to the City Council. One of the issues pertained to a comment from the Public Works
13   Department and the City Engineer who needed additional analysis of the projected impact of
14   stormwater runoff on the existing City infrastructure. The Public Works Director was comfortable
15   forwarding the proposal to the City Council as long as the information is gathered before final
16   approval is granted. Another outstanding issue had to do with the portion of the primary access
17   point that encroaches on the City of Holladay’s right-of-way. Mr. Johnson stated that it must be
18   properly approved and reviewed but felt this could be done while waiting for a decision from City
19   Council.
20
21   Mr. Johnson reported that the City Engineer and the contracted Geological Hazard Consultant
22   requested additional slope stability analysis, specifically where the apartment project is proposed
23   to be located. The review was completed after the last Planning Commission Meeting and was
24   currently under review by the Geological Hazard Consultant. Mr. Johnson noted that many
25   geologic and engineering studies had been performed and reviewed. Staff did not believe that all
26   technical analyses needed to be completed at this point but it would need to be done before
27   anything is built. At each phase of development, the applicant will be required to complete all
28   necessary studies. No phase will be approved for vertical construction until additional trenching,
29   soil samples, and foundation analysis have been completed.
30
31   Staff recommended that emergency access onto SR-190 (Wasatch Boulevard) become a temporary
32   access point that can be used by the development. Mr. Johnson noted that there will be a meeting
33   between the Utah Department of Transportation (“UDOT”) and the applicant next week to
34   continue discussions. The additional access will alleviate concerns from the adjacent
35   neighborhood to the north regarding traffic coming through the northern intersection. Having two
36   intersections will spread out that traffic. Mr. Johnson reported that when the southern gravel pit
37   property begins redevelopment, a full intersection will connect to the site, and temporary access
38   will no longer be necessary.

21   Commissioner Allen noted that there is an outstanding item in the staff report related to the
22   geologic engineering hazards. Mr. Johnson stated that public comment was received and
23   distributed to the Planning Commission. The person who made the comment felt that all site
24   engineering should be completed prior to a zone change. This would include digging out
25   foundations for each building, trenching, clearing every building location, doing a soils analysis,
26   and performing geotechnical work at each footprint. Staff did not feel that those steps were
27   necessary at this point in the project. The current focus was on clearing the site, addressing any
28   issues, and determining how those issues would be mitigated. Language was built into the
29   ordinance to specify that studies would be required at each phase of development. If one of the
30   proposed buildings required substantial changes, the ordinance would need to be amended.
