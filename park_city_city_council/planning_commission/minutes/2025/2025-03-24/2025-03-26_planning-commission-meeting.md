 Commissioner John Frontero asked for additional details about the AMPD provision that
 is considered for child care. He asked if there is a square footage ratio anticipated.
 Planner Klopfenstein explained that the current provision in the code allows the Planning
 Commission to require an applicant for an MPD or AMPD to designate a site within the
                      ed


 MPD or AMPD for a child care center. The threshold for the Commission’s ability to
 require that is simply if the Commission determines there is additional demand for child
 care that the MPD or AMPD would create. There was extensive research conducted to
 find more specific criteria in other communities. However, there is not anything that is
            v



 comparable. Without a better set of criteria in place, the recommendation is to eliminate
 that provision from the code. Commissioner Frontero understands the explanation but
         ro




 pointed out that this seems to be in opposition to the goal to create more child care in the
 City. It seems that the suggestion is to soften the requirement to provide child care.
   p




 Senior City Attorney, Mark Harrington, explained that in order to require something or
Ap




 exact space out of a project, there needs to be a reason and a standard. To date, there
 is not an example where that has been justified legally. As a result, the recommendation
 is to eliminate that as a mandatory requirement. Director Ward reminded Commissioners
 that there have been some changes since these discussions first came before the
 Planning Commission. In December 2024, the City Council adopted the ordinance
 recommended by the Planning Commission to update the accessory uses for MPDs.
