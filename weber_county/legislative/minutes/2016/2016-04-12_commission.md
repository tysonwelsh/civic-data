    2. COOPERATIVE AGREEMENT WITH UDOT FOR CONVERTED TAP FUNDS TO BE USED FOR THE DESIGN AND
       CONSTRUCTION OF THE WOLF CREEK TO EDEN PATHWAY PROJECT ALONG SR-158

         Charles Ewert, of County Planning, stated that there has been a great planning effort in Ogden Valley
         regarding active transportation. UDOT approved a transportation alternatives program funding request from
         the county for the trail that will go from the 4-way stop up to Wolf Creek. This contract executes the
         obligation for the funds in the amount of $67,986. The county’s portion is $100,000 ($50,000 to come from
         the trail impact fee fund and $50,000 from an in-kind donation from Wolf Creek Resort). Chair Gibson
         thanked Mr. Ewert for finding the funds. Commissioner Ebert expressed thanks to Wolf Creek for being a
         great community partner.
         Commissioner Bell moved to approve the Cooperative Agreement with Utah Department of Transportation for
         the TAP funds for the design and construction of the Wolf Creek to Eden pathway project along SR-158;
         Commissioner Ebert seconded.
         Commissioner Ebert – aye; Commissioner Bell – aye; Chair Gibson – aye

    3. INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT WITH WEBER AREA DISPATCH 911 AND EMERGENCY SERVICES DISTRICT FOR
         THE PROVISION OF A FINANCIAL ADVISOR AND BOND COUNSEL

         Commissioner Bell stated that the 911 services has been looking into constructing a new building and
         approached the county about three years ago about bonding for $8-10 million. The county elected not to bond
         and other options have been researched. One of the options may be accomplished without increasing taxes and
         is still in the works. This agreement is to obtain a financial advisor and bond counsel.
         Commissioner Bell moved to approve the Interlocal Agreement with Weber Area Dispatch 911 and
         Emergency Services District for a financial advisor and bond counsel; Commissioner Ebert seconded.
         Commissioner Ebert – aye; Commissioner Bell – aye; Chair Gibson – aye

    5. ACTION REGARDING NOTICE OF TERMINATION OF THE JUSTICE COURT AGREEMENT WITH ROY CITY
       David Wilson, Deputy County Attorney, stated that the County Attorney and County Sheriff have been
       researching alternatives to the current justice court to make it more convenient for both those who have to
       appear in court and the officers. The current interlocal agreement requires one year’s notice to terminate and
       this is to give that notice.
       Commissioner Bell moved to approve sending the notice of termination of the Justice Court agreement to Roy
       City; Commissioner Ebert seconded.
       Commissioner Ebert – aye; Commissioner Bell – aye; Chair Gibson – aye

    6. MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING WITH HILL AIR FORCE BASE 75TH SECURITY FORCES SQUADRON
       FOR HOUSING OF MILITARY MALE AND FEMALE DETAINEES AND INMATES

       Seffani Ebert, of the County Sheriff’s Office, stated that this is a renewal and that there were no changes in this
       contract. She addressed Commissioner Bell’s question stating that generally there are one or two of these
       detainees/inmates in the county’s facility at any given time.
       Commissioner Bell moved to approve the Memorandum of Understanding with Hill Air Force Base 75th
       Security Forces Squadron for housing of military male and female detainees and inmates; Commissioner Ebert
       seconded.

G. PUBLIC COMMENTS:
       Miranda Menzes, Eden resident and Weber Pathways board member, expressed thanks from the Eden
       community to UDOT, the county and commissioners for assisting with funding of the important Wolf Creek to
       Eden pathway project. She expressed thanks for the good work being done by the County Engineering and
       Planning departments. She said that a lady with a blind person’s cane was seen last week walking along the
       edge of the roadway; the community is looking forward to a safe place for people to walk. Notice has gone
       out to all landowners to discuss how to fit the trail into the available right-of-way. Commissioner Ebert
       thanked Ms. Menzes for the effort she puts into strengthening the community. She addressed Commissioner
       Bell’s question about the eradication efforts of puncture vine and other noxious weeds in Ogden Valley stating
       that the county’s right-of-way up to the antenna tower is close to being free of puncture vine, the port ramp
       and the road edges are a concern; this is an ongoing battle. She stated that John Lewis, of Wolf Creek, has
       shown wonderful leadership in the community with the trail and other issues, such as controlling dyers woad
       in Wolf Creek.
       Daryn Westergard, Weber County Farm Bureau, invited the commissioners and other elected officials to the
       Bureau’s annual banquet on Saturday. He expressed thanks to the Commission for supporting agriculture in
       our county and the commissioners noted the importance of agriculture to our economy. Commissioner Ebert
       stated that agriculture is an anchor, which our county was founded upon and continues to be so. Chair Gibson
       explained that the banquet recognizes and highlights farming families as well as important agriculture issues.
