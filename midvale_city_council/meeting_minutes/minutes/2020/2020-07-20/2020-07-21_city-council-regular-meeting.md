¢ Discuss Midvale Police Department Feasibility Study [Matt Dahl, Assistant City
Manager]
Matt Dahl said Midvale City joined the Unified Police Department in 2011. At that time the
Unified Police Department (UPD) model of operating local precincts and sharing specialty
services (SWAT, K9, investigations, forensics, evidence) with UPD Member Communities
allowed for Midvale to continue the high level of service expected by its residents and
achieve significant savings for the City’s general fund. After realizing the anticipated
savings in the initial years of membership, Midvale City, as well as the other UPD Member
Communities, grew concerned by the significant annual increases in the cost of the
shared services, the persistently low-pay received by officers and detectives, and budget
cuts in local precincts. As a result of these concerns, among others, Midvale City felt it
would be appropriate to consider the feasibility of withdrawing from UPD and providing
its own police services.
In April 2020, Midvale City hired Lewis Young Robertson & Burningham Inc. (LYRB) to
prepare a Feasibility Study that would include budget scenarios for the establishment and
on-going operations of a Midvale Police Department. The budget scenarios would be
used to evaluate the range of police services that Midvale City could provide and the
corresponding costs, for the purpose of comparing the outcomes with the service level

and costs currently provided by UPD. In preparing the Feasibility Study, LYRB compiled
. benchmark data, including calls for service, current budget expenses, and officer counts
from community police departments in Salt Lake County. Additionally, City staff consulted
with several law enforcement experts to establish a range of expectations for police
services.
The Feasibility Study was scheduled to be completed in mid-July 2020, with updates on
the preliminary findings in late June. On June 16, 2020, Midvale City Staff presented
preliminary findings from the Feasibility Study to the City Council. Based on the data in
the study and recommendations from law enforcement experts the budget scenarios that
were developed incorporated a staffing range of 52-56 officers, which resulted in annual
costs for police services of $8.1 — $8.9 million dollars. Additionally, the range for
department start-up costs was $5.0 - $7.3 million. The biggest contributors to the startup
costs were the construction of a new evidence storage facility and pension liabilities.
These cost estimates indicate there could be modest savings if Midvale were to operate
its own police department. During the June 16th meeting, staff indicated that it would
continue to refine the budget estimates, with a focus on leasing, insurance, and start-up
financing costs.
Following the June 16th City Council meeting, Taylorsville indicated that it would be
withdrawing from UPD. This decision has significant budget and service level implications
for the remaining UPD Member Communities. Any increase in cost to Midvale resulting
from the withdrawal of Taylorsville, would increase the anticipated savings realized by
L Midvale for self-providing police services. UPD has indicated that it is their intent to make

Taylorsville’s departure have a neutral financial impact on the remaining Member
Communities, though a detailed proposal has not been presented.
On June 30, 2020, Midvale City Staff presented updated findings from the Feasibility
Study to the City Council. Significant changes were made in the costs estimates to
address the financing of the start-up costs. As a result of these changes, the potential
outcome of leaving UPD could range from modest savings for Midvale City, to moderate
cost increases. Following the update on the Feasibility Study, the City Council instructed
staff to look further into the impacts of Taylorsville’s withdrawal from UPD, the impacts of
increasing officer pay, and ways of decreasing the start-up costs.

e Discuss public gatherings ordinance amendment [Lisa Garner, City
Attorney]
Lisa Garner said in light of current events, Staff has reviewed the City’s laws for use of
City property for public gatherings, events, and demonstrations. Currently, the City only
regulates events that require tickets with 500 or more attendees under Chapter 5.16.
Other special events on public property are currently unregulated.
Many communities have adopted regulations to mitigate the impact of special events on
traffic, noise, security, health, and safety of the public. Staff is in the process of drafting
Chapter 12.02 “Special Events on Public Property.” The current draft includes regulation
of athletic events, block parties, demonstrations, entertainment events, and other events
that occur on public property with 50 or more participants. It would also regulate events,
regardless of the number of participants, that would prevent the general use of public
) property.
1 The proposed chapter would require a permit for these activities. The organizer of one of
these events would submit an application to the City. The City would review the
7

Proceedings of City Council Meeting
July 21, 2020
application to ensure that adequate measures are in place to mitigate any impact on
traffic, noise, security, health, and safety of the public. Additionally, if City support services
are necessary to mitigate the impact of the special event, the City would be able to recover
the costs of those services. These services include such things as police, fire, and
emergency medical protection, road closures, traffic control, cleanup, water, power, and
other services directly related to the special event. Only spontaneous events, as required
by the First Amendment, would be completely exempt from the proposed ordinance.
It is anticipated that this ordinance will be limited to establishing the procedures for special
event permits on public property. Specific guidelines for special events, such as the
number of police officers per a number of participants, will be established by a City policy
available to the public. Staff believes this approach will provide the City flexibility to
appropriately update its mitigation measures without burdening the City Council with
every change.
As Staff drafts this ordinance, it would like some guidance on the following issues:

The area of law surrounding public forums is currently evolving. Staff does not anticipate

manner restrictions to change. However, issues on the periphery are being explored.
Staffs final proposal may have noticeable differences from the discussion tonight as
courts issue holdings regarding current events.
FISCAL IMPACT:
It is anticipated that adoption of Chapter 12.02 will create a mechanism to ensure that the
City’s costs are recovered to the extent the law permits when special events are held on
public property.

A. CONSIDER ORDINANCE NO. 2020-0-09 APPROVING PROPOSED
TEXT AMENDMENT TO MODIFY DEVELOPMENT STANDARDS IN THE
JORDAN BLUFFS SUBAREAS 1-3 ZONE
Alex Murphy said in 2019, the City adopted a zoning ordinance for Subareas 1-3 of the
Jordan Bluffs zone. This ordinance established goals for the district, review process
requirements, and standards for commercial, residential, and mixed-use development
based on a preliminary “pod” concept put together by the property owner, Gardner
Company. To date, one project has been approved under this ordinance (Holmes Homes’
Jordan Bluffs Condo project).
Following an agreement between Zions and Gardner to develop the northern pods, D&E,
Gardner developed a new concept for the Jordan Bluffs area that would, over time and
subject to market conditions, develop an urbanized core in the project area with Zions
serving as the northern anchor and future development incrementally contributing to the
vision.
To implement the new concept, certain requirements of the zoning ordinance require
amendment and Gardner has submitted this request to update the text. Pursuant to the
text amendment process described in §17-3-1 of the Midvale City Municipal Code, Staff
and Gardner have been discussing the required changes and presented a draft text
amendment to the Planning Commission for recommendation. In the meeting on June 10,
2020, the Planning Commission recommended the Council adopt the proposed changes
with a revision to the proposed Figure 2 to add required bike lanes.
PROPOSED CHANGES:
The text has been revised to include the updated Figure 2 recommended by the Planning
Commission and now proposes changes to the following sections:
17-7-10.12.1 Purpose.
The new concept requires a new layout of the overall pod map. The changes results in a
reduction in the overall number of pods (14 to 12) and new boundaries for the pods. This
change also includes a revision to the central linear park location and will require
modifications to the Master Development Agreement for Jordan Bluffs, which will be
| reviewed separately by the City Council.

17-7-10.12.5 Development requirements.
Proposed changes to this section include a new road layout, new road standards, revised
landscape, building, and parking lot setbacks for the “core,” and increased building
heights in certain pods.
The new concept proposes a new street system on the west side of Bingham Junction
Boulevard. The new street system provides a central boulevard-type street as a primary
street serviced by secondary and tertiary streets with standards designed based on
expected traffic volumes. On street parking is required for the primary boulevard and an
expanded bike network is provided on the primary and secondary streets. Figure 2 in this
section has been updated to include bike network details as recommended by the
Planning Commission In conjunction with the new road layout, building and landscape
setback along streets in the new core have been modified. For Pods E-K, which include
all pods west of Bingham Junction Boulevard, minimum and maximum setbacks are
determined by street type (primary, secondary, or tertiary) and priority. Outside of those
pods, landscape setbacks for minor roads have been decreased from 15’ to 10’. Major
roads (Bingham Junction, East lvy, Center Street) remain as originally adopted.
Following the Planning Commission’s public hearing, a provision was added to this
section clarifying that the new frontage requirements only apply to the first street of each
type for a development. This provision is necessary to provide flexibility in the timing of
buildout in the core area.
Following some forays into marketing the site, Gardner is also requesting the building :
height east of Bingham Junction Boulevard be increased from 80’ to 95’ and that it be
measured from finished floor elevation to top of exterior wall (excluding parapets) instead
of to the parapet. This results in a maximum building height increase of approximately 20’
overall for this area.
17-7-10.12.6 Parking and access standards.
The proposed changes to parking standards include a reduction in the minimum
requirement for Office uses, a modification to access requirements, and the addition of
on-street loading areas.
The current language requires a minimum of 3 parking stalls per 1000 SF of net leasable
floor area for general office uses or 5 stalls per 1000 SF for intensive office uses. This
standard does not account for proximity to TRAX stations or shared parking opportunities
that may eventually develop in the project area, so a modification to establish a minimum
of 3 stalls per 1000 SF and maximum of 6 stalls per 1000 SF is proposed.
The City Engineer expressed some concern about mandating minimum access
separation requirements in the zoning ordinance, specifically indicating that it limited his
ability to consider traffic patterns and street layout when reviewing development
proposals. The proposed language 3 changes the requirements for separation to
recommendations and reverts approval authority for
road network modifications to the City Engineer. l

roads or a public park are required to be screened with landscape material. For the
purposes of these requirements, elevations are reviewed individually and considered
“within clear sight of” if they are (1) approximately parallel to a street or park, (2) within

change. Parking garages in Pods E-K are also required to comply with the landscape
setback requirements of §17-7-10.12.5(L).
17-7-10.12.11 Commercial developments.
Proposed changes to this section match the building height and parking garage changes
proposed in §17-7-10.12.10 above.
17-7-10.12.13 Signage.
The proposed change within this section would adjust the standards for a project area
monument sign, increasing maximum allowable height from 9’ to 10’ and maximum
allowable area from 150 SF to 180 SF. No other changes are proposed.

3.3 Project Amenities.
The biggest change in the proposed MDA amendments is to the central community park.
As part of the new concept, the park has been relocated from the central linear park
configuration originally proposed to the top of the cap slope. This change strengthens the
connection between the new park and the Jordan River while maintaining the area of the
original proposal. While the minimum area required by the park remains the same (15
acres), the required width (125’) is not provided throughout the park. An exhibit of the
proposed improvements has been included in the proposed amendment and will need to
be fleshed out as adjacent projects are developed.
A minor change to the trail connections from the cap to the Jordan River Parkway Trail is
proposed that would expand the allowable types of lighting. Instead of requiring only
bollards, the lighting would need to be “pedestrian-scaled,” which can include bollards,
short poles, or other configurations that enhance the pedestrian experience. The other
design requirements remain as-is and the City is still required to accept maintenance
responsibility for the trail connections.

OF CITY-OWNED RIGHTS-OF-WAY LOCATED WEST OF BINGHAM
JUNCTION BOULEVARD (IVY DRIVE AND BINARY WAY)
Alex Murphy said as part of the Jordan Bluffs Lot 201 Amended subdivision that was
recorded in April, the City received dedication of rights-of-way for the extension of Ivy
Drive west of Bingham Junction Boulevard and a new street, Binary Way. The property
owner at the time, Gardner Jordan Bluffs
LC was required to dedicate the rights-of-way to create a parcel to sell to Zions Bank prior
to completing a Large Scale Master Plan (LSMP) for the Zions Bank project. The Zions
LSMP proposes public streets in a different location than originally identified on the
recorded plat, so Zions and Gardner have requested a vacation of the original rights-of-
way to allow new ones to be dedicated. An updated plat has been prepared and will
include dedication of new public rights-of-way according to the updated Jordan Bluffs
concept Gardner has developed.
The City’s Municipal Code dictates the process vacation requests must go through. Per
§12.16.050, this request was forwarded to other affected City departments to determine
potential impacts to the City. The various City departments concluded vacation of the
right-of-way was appropriate and, because of how recent the rights-of-way were
dedicated and the fact that no construction work had begun on them, did not express any
L concerns with the proposal.
All vacation requests require a review and recommendation from the Planning
Commission and approval from the City Council with public hearings held before each
body. The Planning Commission conducted a public hearing regarding this request on
June 10, 2020 and forwarded a recommendation to the City Council to approve the
request. The Council considered the request on July 7, 2020, and adopted a motion
approving the request and directing Staff to prepare an ordinance to formalize the
vacation. The requested ordinance has been prepared for Council to consider.
FISCAL IMPACT:
No immediate impact. This vacation will allow for the proposed public roads to be
redesigned and the City will be responsible for future maintenance of public roads on
Jordan Bluffs after the improvements are dedicated, designed, and accepted according
to City policy.

Staff recommended the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 2020-O-10, vacating the Binary
Way and Ivy Drive rights-of-way located west of Bingham Junction Boulevard.

D. CONSIDER ORDINANCE NO. 2020-0-11 AN ORDINANCE ESTABLISHING
TEMPORARY LAND USE REGULATIONS REGARDING ALLOWED MULTI-
FAMILY USES IN THE SINGLE FAMILY RESIDENTIAL ZONE (SF-1) AND
SINGLE FAMILY RESIDENTIAL ZONE (SF-2)

Nate Rockwood said On July 7, 2020 City Council and staff held a work session to discuss

the allowed uses in the Single Family Residential SF-1 and SF-2 zones. This work session

was the first step in understanding and assessing the Single Family Zones and evaluating
some strategies which might help City Council align the regulations with the Council’s
objectives for single family neighborhoods. Among several goals stated in the code is that
the zones are to “maintain existing, predominately single family detached residential
neighborhoods”, “allow for single family development that is compatible with existing

development”, “and maintain the character of neighborhoods with compatible residential l

design’.

As part of the discussion, staff presented several strategies which Council and staff will

continue to explore and develop as ways to further the goals of the single family

residential zones. During the discussion, a majority of City Council indicated that the
allowed use of multi-family development in single family zones, as part of an MPD, should
be temporally repealed until these strategies were further developed and implemented.

Therefore, the attached ordinance includes the following findings which constitutes a

compelling, countervailing public interest:

The allowed uses of multi-family development incentives for Master Planned

Development design in the single family residential zones could lead to projects that are

fundamentally incompatible with the purpose of the SF-1 and SF-2 Zones because of they

may change the nature and characteristics of existing single family neighborhoods which
are to be predominately single family detached residential neighborhoods.

In cases where there is a compelling, countervailing public interest, both the Midvale City

Municipal Code and Utah Code allow the City to adopt an emergency or temporary zoning

ordinance, effective for no longer than 6 months, without going through the typical

ordinance amendment process. To avoid potential adverse outcomes associated with

allowing multi-family development in single family residential zones, staff has prepared a

temporary ordinance that would repeal sections 17-7-1.10 (D)(2) and 17-7- 2.10 (D)(2)
