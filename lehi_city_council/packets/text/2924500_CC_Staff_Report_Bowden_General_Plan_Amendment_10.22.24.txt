  APPLICABLE DEVELOPMENT CODE REGULATIONS

Section 04.060 Criteria for Approval
The Planning Commission should consider the following factors, among others, when reviewing a General Plan
amendment:

    1.   The effect of the proposed amendment on the character of the surrounding area;
    2.   Consistency with the goals and policies of the General Plan;
    3.   Consistency and compatibility of the proposed zone with the General Plan land uses of nearby and adjoining
         properties;
    4.   The suitability of the properties for the uses requested and their suitability for the existing uses identified
         by the General Plan;
    5.   Whether a change in the uses allowed for the affected properties will unduly affect the uses, or proposed
         uses for nearby and adjoining properties;
    6.   The gain to the public health, safety, and welfare from the existing classification to the proposed amend-
         ment; and
    7.   The overall community benefit of the proposed amendment.

Bowden General Plan Amendment
  REPORT ANALYSIS

The applicant is requesting to amend the General Plan land use designation of this property from Very Low-Density
Residential Agriculture (VLDRA) to Low Density Residential (LDR). This is to facilitate the construction of third-
acre lots. In their narrative, the applicant stated that this request is beneficial to the city in that it will increase the tax
revenue of the city and help with housing affordability. The VLDRA designation requires half-acre lots, but under
the LDR designation with third-acre lots, the homes would be more affordable at just under a million dollars sale
price rather than two million for the half-acre lots. The applicant has also stated that the third-acre lots would be in
character with the nearby College Farms Planned Residential Development as some lots within the subdivision are
around 0.33 acres in size.

It should be noted how changing the General Plan Designation affects the density of this parcel. The VLDRA desig-
nation allows for a density of 1.75 units an acre, while the LDR designation allows for 3.25 units an acre. Under the
current VLDRA designation, the 5.18 acre parcel could support nine lots. If the amendment were approved to LDR,
the maximum number of lots that could be built on this parcel would be 16.8 lots.

There were three comments from the DRC, all from the Planning Division. They include:
    1. LDR lets you go down to 0.18 acre lots.

        should be near existing and future transit service or other land use centers.

  POSSIBLE MOTIONS AND FINDINGS

The City Council may move to approve or deny the proposed general plan amendment. Any motion should include
findings. Possible findings include:

    1.   The proposed General Plan amendment is/is not in conformance with the purposes, intent, and provisions
         of the General Plan and its various elements.
    2.   The proposed General Plan amendment is/is not consistent with the land uses and zoning of nearby and
         adjoining properties.
    3.   The proposed General Plan amendment will/will not have a substantial adverse effect on surrounding prop-
         erty.
    4.   The affected site is/is not physically suitable in terms of design, location, shape, size, operating characteris-
         tics, and the provision of public services to accommodate any potential future uses, including all landscap-
         ing, loading areas, open spaces, parking areas, setbacks, buffering/screening, walls, fences, yards, and other
         required features.
    5.   Additional findings based upon information presented during the public hearing.


  PLANNING COMMISSION RECOMMENDATION

The Planning Commission reviewed the proposed general plan amendment on September 26, 2024. The minutes
and motion from this meeting are as follows:

3.2) Public hearing and recommendation of Corey Bowden’s request for review of the Bowden General Plan
Amendment on 5.18 acres located at 9861 West 9600 North (1500 N 2800 W) changing the land use designation
from VLDRA (very low-density residential agriculture) to LDR (low density residential).
Commissioner Kunze asked if this was a county or city property and staff clarified that it was annexed into the city.

Jill Jones was there on behalf of the applicant. She stated that they requested a change based on a plot map of Coolidge
Farms, proposing smaller 0.33-acre lots to offer more affordable homes, which would better meet local demand and
generate more tax revenue for the city.

Commissioner Everett asked Ms. Jones why she opted to change the general plan to LRDR instead of pursuing a PUD,
which would allow for more green space and was suggested by the city. Ms. Jones responded that while they would
consider it, it wasn't their first preference.

Commissioner Chair Jackson opened the public hearing.

There were comments on Granicus in opposition some of the reasons where the lot size not matching with the neighbor-
hood. schools and traffic.

Candace Jones- expressed opposition to the proposed general plan amendment changing the land use from VLDRA to
LDR. She argued that the amendment was inconsistent with surrounding properties, which had larger lot sizes, and
would negatively impact home values and neighborhood cohesion. Ms. Jones also cited concerns about increased traf-
fic, overcrowded schools, and inadequate city infrastructure, including the lack of public transit and bicycle lanes. She
urged the city to deny the amendment and collaborate with the landowner on solutions that would benefit the broader
community.

Ashley Frampton- expressed concerns about the proposed zoning change, echoing points made by Ms. Jones. Her pri-
mary worry was the overcrowding at North Point Elementary, which already has nearly 1,100 students, with more hous-
ing developments planned in the area. She emphasized that adding more homes would exacerbate large class sizes and
strain the school's ability to support students, particularly those with English as a second language.

Jared Marbuji-opposed the amendment, expressing concerns about increasing traffic and overcrowded schools. After
moving to Lehi in April 2023 to raise a family, he was disappointed by the rising traffic, particularly from 2100 North,
which funnels through his neighborhood. He also worried about the impact of large class sizes on his children's educa-
tion.

Camille Nielsen-opposed the amendment, sharing similar concerns as the previous speaker. She has children at multiple
schools in the district, including a child with special needs, and noted the increasingly chaotic traffic in the area. While
she values the family-friendly community they moved to, she felt the rising traffic and changes were disrupting the
peaceful environment they sought.

Cassie Meyers- opposed the amendment, citing concerns about heavy traffic on unsafe roads and the dangers around
North Point Elementary. She noted the high demand for larger half-acre lots and stressed the importance of preserving
them in the community. As a realtor, she believed the designation change wouldn’t significantly impact the property’s
value.

Commissioner Chair Jackson closed the public hearing.

Commissioner Lockhart noted that school population issues are outside the planning commission's jurisdiction, but
acknowledged the concerns raised. They encouraged residents to research the upcoming November ballot proposition
about splitting the Alpine School District, which could provide more local representation and faster responses to school
overcrowding issues. She than followed up cornering the amendment stating that while a diverse range of housing types
is important for addressing Utah's affordable housing crisis, half-acre lots should not be sacrificed in every situation.
She emphasized preserving these lots, as there is a market for them, and encouraged the applicant to explore other op-
tions without resorting to a general plan amendment.

Commissioner Eyre asked about amount of lots depending on size to staff and those were discussed with staff member
Jacob Curtis.

Commissioner Everett expressed concern about the minimal affordability difference between half-acre and third acre
lots, stating that it doesn't significantly impact affordability for most people. He emphasized the importance of preserv-
ing half-acre lots for hobby farms and animals, which he believes is part of Lehi's legacy, and viewed the general plan
amendment as a step backward for the city.

Commission Chair Jackson acknowledged the validity of Ms. Jones' points on the importance of zoning changes but
emphasized that amendments should offer significant benefits to the city rather than just favoring the developer. He
viewed the proposed change as neutral to beneficial for the developer and felt it would disrupt the surrounding area,
concluding it wasn't in the city's best interest.

         Motion: Commissioner Lockhart moved to recommend denial to Corey Bowden’s request for review of the
         Bowden General Plan Amendment on 5.18 acres located at 9861 West 9600 North (1500 N 2800 W) changing
         the land use designation from VLDRA (very low-density residential agriculture) to LDR (low density residen-
         tial) including all findings that the proposed general plan amendment is not in conformance with the purposes
         intent and provisions of the general plan and its various elements. In addition to that, the amendment is not
         consistent with the land uses and zoning of nearby, adjoining properties and all DRC comments. Commissioner
         Eyre also wanted to draw attention to the minimum of the AG fencing noting that they can make an agreement
         if they so choose. Commissioner Kunze seconded.

Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes; Commissioner Lockhart,
yes; Commissioner Kunze, yes. The motion passed unanimously.
