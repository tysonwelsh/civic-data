2.  Public hearing and possible action regarding a proposed Rezone and Master 
Planned Development (MPD) for a campground on lot 1 of the Wilderness Acres; 
Roger Eggett, Applicant. – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner shared the background for this application. The applicant has requested 
a rezone from AG80 to Commercial for a 25-space campground. If the rezone is approved, 
the applicant would like to condominimize each space to sell each pad separately.

Roger Eggett, the applicant stated he understands there is strong opposition to this 
proposal. A landowners’ association would be formed to take care of the property and be 
an asset. There is an overwhelming demand for this type of property.  
Chair Sargent stated the Planning Commission has been considering campgrounds as a 
Code Amendment for some time. The last discussion included considering eliminating 
commercial campgrounds and including them in the RC (Recreation Commercial) Zone. 
This current application is a rezone request that MAY be approved only after four criteria 
are met. A rezone is at the sole discretion of the County and not a given right. 
Commissioner Wilde asked who will manage the sewer. There is nothing in the 
application about water. There is no room to park extra cars, and a sixty-foot pad is not big 
enough for a truck and trailer. 
Commissioner Benson stated this use is not consistent with the neighboring property. 
Commissioner Sonntag stated there are serious concerns with compatibility, particularly 
for twenty-five spaces. 
Commissioner Clyde stated this type of property would work well if it was closer to the 
Bear River Lodge, similar to something seen in the Zion Park area. The road connection 
seems to cross other properties and needs to be better explained. Outside storage would 
not be allowed and is undesirable. There needs to be more explanation about water, 
storage tanks, and fire flow. This area should not be zoned AG80, but should be a 
residential zone. 
Commissioner Wheaton stated there is no grading plan or stream protection.

Chair Sargent stated if the property were along Mirror Lake Highway near other 
commercial properties, this application could be good, but the character of the Wilderness 
Acres area should not be changed. 
 
The Public hearing was opened.  
 
Lance Pooler stated there are issues with how many people are coming through the 
property. The property is not for four-wheelers. There are water issues and trash issues. 
Larry Wagstaff is the President of the East Fork Canal Company and is concerned with 
how to protect the ditch from people and keep it running. There is judicated water that is 
assigned and not open for people to take. Chair Sargent stated this hearing is preliminary 
to see if the concept could be supported before the applicant spends a lot of money. 
John Adamson stated that access to the property is through a private road west of the 
property across a lot that is owned by Union Telephone and Bridger Valley. Those 
landowners were not notified of this hearing, but access is a problem. The ditches are over 
100 years old. Trout Unlimited already tried to eliminate the ditches that provide water to 
local ranchers. The impact use of fifty trailers would be sixty gallons of water per trailer. 
Some access would be needed through other properties. There is no trail access. The 
historical use at Bear River Lodge is overused and the site cannot be controlled. 
Troy Olson stated the LLC the application was submitted with is expired. Twenty-five 
people buying a 60-foot by 60-foot lot will have their dreams crushed when there is no 
sewage plans or water available.

Diane Christensen is a recreational resident of Uinta Lands and asked if the development

WORK SESSION 
 
1. Discussion regarding the Eastern Summit County General Plan and Code 
Amendments. – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner stated that infrastructure and transportation will be addressed by 
Derrick Radke and Caroline Rodriguez.  
Derrick Radke stated the County is first working on road projects. The Eastern side of the 
county is in pretty good shape. The capacities are there, and good planning and zoning 
prevent the need for monster roads. 
Mike Kendell, the County Engineer stated the County is working on a transportation 
impact fee study. Some projects are eligible for fees. The existing infrastructure needs to be 
evaluated, and some will need improvements. Some maintenance projects will not be 
eligible for fees, but will still be done. This will be the first time that impact fees will be 
assessed for transportation in Eastern Summit County. 
Derrick Radke stated that the County has hired a firm to look at Eastern Summit County to 
see what infrastructure is in place and what the capacity is to 2050. There has been some 
limited participation from the utilities, and the study has not been as comprehensive as the 
County wanted. The level of detail on the utility study depended on the participation of the 
utility provider. There is a tool on the County website to see what infrastructure is in place.  
Commissioner Clyde stated the big concern is that there are a few fairly large systems, but 
there are a zillion little microsystems that should be regionalized. The County should not be 
approving subdivisions with little water systems that are designed to fail. 
Caroline Rodriguez shared that the County has contracted with Park City Transit, and as 
of July 2021 the County will be having their own transit services. There will be some route

2. Discussion regarding the Cedar Crest Village Overlay application reviewing the 
Village Residential character area, constraints, lot and unit types, and densities; 
Cedar Crest Village Overlay Committee, Applicant. – Kirsten Whetstone AICP, County 
Planner 
 
Planner Whetstone encouraged the Commissioners and the public to read the Staff Report 
and look at the website. The committee has met and primarily looked at the Village 
Residential area. Staff has begun to review the proposal with the committee to look in more 
detail what the project would mean for the local area. 
Peter Barnes shared a 3D model of the village area. The Village Residential Zone is where 
the bulk of the residential development would be located. Considerations include what type 
of residential use is appropriate, and what density is appropriate. Larger lots would be 
located toward the edge of the residential zone with more density near the commercial 
core of the village. One consideration is how to not overwhelm the existing neighborhood 
and infrastructure systems. Does the Planning Commission believe the committee is on the 
right track? Are there any missed opportunities or anything that should be removed? 
Commissioner Wilde stated there is some value in having two to three story buildings in 
some locations. Ranchettes would be appropriate on the outskirts of the village. It would be 
better to have a riding area in Open Space.

Commissioner Clyde stated he likes the direction of the plan and it is consistent with the 
overall vision. Feathering is important for blending with a variety of lot sizes. It is 
important to feather the densities to blend with the existing homes. Be respectful and plan 
around the existing homes. Having amenity packages somewhat in common is appropriate. 
To get the number of rooftops to be functional three- to four-story buildings would be 
appropriate in the village core and some into the hillsides. 
Commissioner Sonntag stated that the ability to have four stories in the Village core is 
needed to make the density work. It is helpful to give a list of what is allowed, but the 
developer should be able to decide without requiring a ratio of uses. 
Commissioner Wheaton stated she is excited and looking forward to seeing Cedar Crest 
move forward. 
Commissioner Benson stated that three-story buildings could easily be done in the 
western core area. This project is comparable to Daybreak and could squeeze a lot of 
development in a small area with a community feel. Larger lots should be feathered to the 
outside of the core. 
Chair Sargent stated that feathering is important. Residential height could be three stories. 
This project lends itself to rural community design and the desires of the community. rural 
design principles need to be used. Comments that have been received include that 
residents are afraid of change, and this project is an alternative to strip development. The 
committee needs to present the positives of avoiding strip development. 
Director Putt stated that Staff will give updates to the Planning Commission every second 
meeting of the month.
