The purpose of the joint work session was to receive presentations from the Ogden School District regarding recent
successes and ongoing challenges; receive presentations from Ogden City regarding the WonderBlock project, Union Station
Campus and the roadmap for development, the new Marshall White recreation center, and homelessness response efforts; and to
discuss other topics of interest.
Ogden School District Presentations
Superintendent Rasmussen, Assistant Superintendent Carpenter, Business Administrator Woolstenhulme, Executive
Director Roberts, and Human Resources Director Bennington used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss specific goals
of the District’s Strategic Plan relating to improved graduation rates and improved literacy rates; system-wide accreditation for the
District, only the second district in the State of Utah to reach this status; college and career readiness; college concurrent j
enrollment; early literacy systems and literacy measurements; diversity of District staff; and financial facts relating to student
costs and employee compensation.
There was a brief discussion among the group regarding the benefits afforded to the District after achieving accreditation,
> including increased access to funding sources for educational programs. There was also a brief discussion about the demographics
— and ethnicity of the students in the District. i
Mr. Woolstenhulme then reported that in Fiscal Year (FY) 2022, the total revenue in the District’s general fund was $114 t
million; sources of funds include federal revenue, local revenue, and State revenue. Total expenditures in FY 2022 were $112 :
million and included functions like transportation, plant services, District administration, instructional support, and instruction.
Based upon current debt, the District has a rating of AA3. Outstanding voter authorized debt is $97 million and outstanding lease ;
revenue debt is $46 million. The District’s debt capacity is $328 million. He then highlighted major Board approved building
program projects, including the Child Nutrition Center, Pathways at Ogden Weber Technical College (OWTC), Sped/Annex
building improvements, and the Hillcrest Elementary rebuild project. The current taxable value of property in the District is $8.3 .
billion, which equates to a district taxable value per student of $819,278. Budgeted property tax revenue for the District in FY :
2022-2023 is $59 million. ,
Ogden City Presentations
WonderBlock: Community and Economic Development Director Cooper used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
discuss the WonderBlock project; one of the purposes of local government is placemaking and the MAKE Ogden plan has been
developed to establish a vision for downtown Ogden and ongoing economic development initiatives. There are several goals
included in the Plan, one of which being a 175 percent increase in overall taxable value, which will help generate the income to :
return services to citizens. The WonderBlock project is a significant mixed-use project that captures all of the values of the MAKE
Ogden plan in terms of density, providing services, creating jobs, quality housing, and creating a sense of place that residents can
enjoy. He discussed the components to be included in the project and provided renderings of the project from different viewpoints.
The WonderBlock investment represents a $188 million total investment via a joint venture between a developer and the
Redevelopment Agency (RDA) of the City. Both parties are investing in the project and the RDA’s investment contribution will
not require an increase in property or sales tax. He indicated more information about the WonderBlock project can be found on the
City’s website.
Union Station: Mr. Cooper then discussed the Union Station campus project; he presented a map to orient the group to
the properties that make up the campus and plans to assemble all properties into one to create a unique and vibrant transportation
. hub. Engagement regarding the project will begin this year and the City will communicate all planned phases of the project.
} Marshall White Recreation Center: Public Services Director Lowder then discussed the Marshall White Center (MWC)
project; progress is being made on the design and engineering of the project and City Administration is developing a funding plan
for the project. The design incorporates much of the public feedback the City has received about the amenities to be included in
the project, such as a large field house to serve as the anchor of the project; he is excited about the project, and he believes the end
project will be beautiful.
Chair Choberka asked when the project may be advertised for bids, to which Chief Administrative Officer Johnson
answered in the next two weeks in hopes of securing bids early in March. Mr. Lowder added that the construction of the project
could take 12 to 18 months.
There was brief discussion among the group regarding the programming that will still be available to the public during .
the construction project and any plans to relocate certain activities or amenities to other areas of the City.
Homelessness Response Efforts: Police Chief Young discussed the impact of homelessness on the entire community,
including the school district properties throughout the City. The Department has assigned school resource officers to various
schools and one of their duties is to monitor and address homelessness issues on school property. Other officers are regularly
monitoring school and other public properties during the evening hours to mitigate the impact of homelessness. It is difficult to
deny the impact that the increased homeless population has on the entire community, but Ogden City is doing the best they can to
police the environmental issues of homelessness. Homeless advocates are employed by the Police Department, and they work to
address the root cause, rather than just the impacts, of homelessness. Homelessness has a very real fiscal impact on the entire City
and many Departments across the City have been working together on a collective approach to responding to the matter.
The group discussed the value of the Police Department’s response to homelessness in neighborhoods throughout the
community. They also discussed access to shelters in the community and plans to increase the number of beds available in the
shelter. There was an emphasis on the value of the partnership between the Police Department and local schoo! administrators in
the common goal of addressing the impact of homelessness on students and their families.
