Citizen Comments:
Mickey Phelps, West Jordan resident in Copper Valley Estates, said he believed the planned gas
station at 5600 West near the overpass was a bad idea that was not needed, not wanted, would cause
light and traffic problems, and would be a place young people would congregate. Mr. Phelps
expressed the opinion that the planned gas station would lower property values.

said she could see accidents and backed up traffic at the intersection of 5600 West and New Bingham

she did not trust the studies that had been done and was concerned about the six-foot wall height.

station built at that location.

community. Ms. Barrett reported on summer activities and spoke of upcoming events at the library.
Ryan Wilkinson, West Jordan resident, believed the City Council did not care about ambient noise in
the city and wished Mr. and Ms. Phelps luck. Mr. Wilkinson reported that he had contacted Council
Members via email about aircraft flying below the required height but had not received a response.

 5. BUSINESS ITEMS
       a. Water Report
          As the West Jordan representative on the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District
          (JVWCD) Board, Council Member Jacob presented the 2023/2024 JVWCD Tentative
          Budget and a proposed 9.3% property tax increase. The additional property tax revenue
          would support: treatment plant upgrades and expansion; two new storage reservoirs;
          new southwest aqueduct segment; new wells and groundwater development; and debt
          service from $190 million of new bonds to be issued.

           Public comment for the proposed change opened at 7:02 pm
           Ryan Wilkinson, West Jordan resident, spoke against any increase in taxes, and asked why
           the Council had not spoken up against the proposed increase.
           Responding to a question from Council Member Whitelock, Dave Martin with JVWCD
           reported that the two new storage reservoirs would most likely serve Kearns and West
           Jordan. He spoke of long-term water service plans. Responding to a question from Council
           Member Green, Mr. Martin said another 5% water rate increase would be necessary, as
           well as cutting some of the planned projects, if the proposed property tax increase was
           not approved.
           Council Member Jacob asked for Council feedback. Council Member Green was not
           familiar with the JVWCD budget, so was not aware if there were expenses that should be
           postponed or cut. He said West Jordan needed water, and hoped the Board would look at
           the budget completely and identify places to cut to be able to reduce the proposed
           property tax increase. Council Member Whitelock agreed.
           Council Member Pack acknowledged the increasing cost of servicing more people and did
           not want to have to pay higher taxes, but was in favor of having water. He spoke of the
           delicate balance involved. Chair McConnehey could see the benefit to West Jordan, and
           expressed support for the proposed property tax increase.
           Council Member Jacob said the roughly $6 more per year in property tax for an average
           household would barely keep up with inflation from the last year. He appreciated the
           comments and would take them back to the JVWCD Board.
