Discussion Items

       Town Days Fireworks Display Location

Sheril Garn, Director of Events and Operations, explained that the Council had decided during
the Strategic Planning Meeting to have fireworks at two different locations during Town Days.
The added cost for the second show would include overtime for City staff, lights, restrooms and
fencing.

Councilmember Stewart said that he would choose Monarch Meadows or Western Springs Park
as a location for the second show.

Councilmember McDougal said that he appreciated the work done to present these options to
the Council. He said that Town Days had been something unique to Riverton City that residents

really enjoy. He would like to consolidate everything to the Main Park and said none of the sites
seem optimum for the second show. He expressed concern for the extra costs and employee
time associated with having a second show.

Mayor Staggs asked if the firework shows were already budgeted. Ms. Garn replied that the
firework shows had already been out to bid and the vendor required a three year contract for a
second show.

Councilmember Wells commented that just like the Spring Cleanup, once the City does
something, it is really hard to take it away. He would like to only have one firework show and
commented that no other cities have more than one show.

Mayor Staggs asked for clarification on the fireworks contract. Ms. Garn said the preferred
vendor is Firestorm and she will see if the contract can be pulled back and only include one
fireworks show.

Councilmember Buroker said that in order to provide a second show, they should consider
practical things such as safety, restrooms and parking. She does not believe that any of the
sites have all three things. She prefers fireworks at the Main Park only.

Councilmember McCay was concerned about the expectation for it to continue. She believes
Monarch Meadows would be a good option but is okay if the council decides on just one
fireworks show.

Councilmember Stewart said he would like the second show to be at Monarch Meadows if the
contract cannot be pulled back. Councilmember McDougal agreed and added that he would
like new development on the west side to accommodate future City events.

Discussion Items

          Day of Service, September 10

Ms. Garn explained that she understood that the Day of Service was very important to
Councilmembers during the Strategic Planning Meeting. She said the City would pick and
prioritize all of the projects and all parts of the City would be equally represented. They will
solicit volunteers through the City website and volunteers will come from all backgrounds and
religions. The Main Park is reserved for this day and she does not anticipate a humanitarian
project.

Mayor Staggs commented that this Day of Service is well worth it for the City and he is
supportive. He wants to make sure that it is open to everyone and across denominations. Ms.
Garn agreed and said she would like to involve the Events and Inclusions Committee members.

Councilmember McCay expressed excitement for the Day of Service and said she would like to
have a humanitarian project. She believes the humanitarian project could be handled by
volunteers and does not think the City needs to plan it.

Councilmember McDougal said that the success from last year was because of the

Councilmembers discussed language in the Strategic Priorities about maintaining enterprise
fund balances.

Mr. Hicks explained that when referring to maintaining fund balances in culinary, secondary and
sanitation funds, the Council should use “days on hand.” The bond rating agencies recommend
382 operating days on hand. He thinks this is extreme but feels 270 is a better goal.

Mr. Hicks reported that at the end of fiscal year 2023, Fund 51 will have 136 days on hand and
will need $800,000 to get to 180 days on hand. Fund 53 will finish fiscal year 2023 with 140
days on hand and will need $400,000 to get up to 180 days on hand. Because there are no
assets in Fund 55, there are no days on hand.

Councilmember McDougal said he would support working towards 270 days on hand. In
response, Mayor Staggs said the Council should be aware of consequences such as fee
increases when trying to maintain days on hand. Mayor Staggs also commented that the City
delivers the service, has 150-180 days on hand, and maintains a $4 million fund balance in
Fund 10 and a $7 million fund balance in RDIIF. He believes that having general fund balances
at a certain level is fine, which could support the enterprise fund balances.

Councilmember Buroker appreciates having standards but doesn’t want to structure the fund
balances so tightly that the Council doesn’t have autonomy to make decisions every year. She
would like to soften statements and use “work towards” as the language so that it isn’t finite.
Councilmember Wells said he agrees with Councilmember Buroker and would like to use “work
towards” and have small increases over time.

Councilmember Stewart commented that having 270 days on hand would mean that the City
could function for 270 days without collecting any additional fees. There has never been a
moment in his life where cities have stopped collecting fees and he has never seen it drop
below an 80% collection rate. He is more comfortable with 180 days and does not feel that 270
would be an attainable goal. Once the City hits 180 days then they can reassess.

Councilmember McCay also liked the language “working toward” and said she would like to
exclude sanitation from the 180 days on hand goal. In response, Mr. Hicks said that sanitation
was already excluded because it does not have assets. If sanitation were to be brought in-
house, he recommended that the City have 180 days on hand in this fund.

Councilmember McDougal asked for clarification on bonding. Mr. Hicks replied that there are
two ratings for bonding: franchise and sales tax revenue bonding and water revenue bonding.
The water revenue bond rating includes pledged revenue from Fund 51 and 53 combined.

Councilmembers agreed to 180 days on hand for the enterprise fund balances.

Mayor Staggs explained that in his five-year budget the County would pay $3 million for the
Greenwell and the State will contribute to the secondary water meters. He does not see any
other major capital projects that would require bonding. Mr. Hicks agreed and said that major
water line projects are anticipated, but those will be paid out of RDIIF. He also anticipates a
property tax increase for RLESA and isn’t sure what will happen with sanitation when it goes out
to bid in the fall.

Councilmembers discussed language in the Strategic Priorities relating to debt per capita.

Mayor Staggs explained that the City has $35 million in total debt, equating to $800 debt per
capita.

Councilmember McCay said the City’s goal should be to reduce debt over time. Since it will be
19 years until all debt is paid off, she would like the Strategic Priorities to state that the City will
be debt-free in 20 years and work towards a debt per capita of $700. In response, Mr. Hicks
explained that if the Council adopts a rigid rule about debt per capita, he worries that if
something catastrophic happens, the Council will need to do truth in taxation or raise fees.

Councilmember McCay suggested wording that says “work toward becoming debt-free in 20
years.” Councilmember Stewart suggested that the City only incur debt when it is absolutely
necessary.

Councilmember Buroker cautioned about putting definitive numbers in the priorities. She wants
to have the freedom to continue to improve the City when opportunities arise. She doesn’t think
the Council should have any sense of shame that they aren’t a fiscally prudent city already. She
would support $1,000 debt per capita. Mayor Staggs agreed with Councilmember Buroker.
Councilmember Wells also agreed and said he wants to be careful not to box into an amount.
He commented that if the City explores self-providing sanitation, it could mean that the City has
to incur debt and buy sanitation trucks.

Councilmember McDougal said that the reality is that the Strategic Plan is a governing
document but it has flexibility. He likes a firm number because it expresses the Council’s
attitude about budgeting. He believes the City took on unnecessary debt over the years and
this frustrated residents. He explained that it is the attitude that matters and he suggested that
the Council reduce debt and focus on the essential needs of the City. He is comfortable with
$1,000 or $850 debt per capita.

Mayor Staggs suggested the objective read “lower debt per capita and ultimately become debt-
free.” Councilmember McCay said the goal should be more specific and attainable. She also
recommended removing the item about “exploring self-providing sanitation.”

Councilmember Buroker suggested that the City should use unplanned revenue to reduce debt.
Councilmember Stewart agreed and said that 25% of new unforecasted revenue should be
allocated to reduce debt. Mayor Staggs suggested that it read “reducing callable debt.”
Mr. Hicks clarified that callable debt would need to be refinanced. The City has a duty to the
bond holders to pay based on the schedule that was established.

Councilmember Stewart recommended that the percentage of unforecasted revenue be placed
in a fund balance and then used to refinance the debt at the appropriate time. All
Councilmembers agreed that 50% of new unforecasted revenue would be placed in a debt
service fund.

Councilmembers discussed whether to remove the language about exploring self-providing
sanitation. After discussion, the decision was made to leave the language in there.

The Strategic Priorities were adopted by all Councilmembers. The final document will be
included in the packet.
