Station Area Plan (SAP) and Technical Assistance to Governments (TAG) – LaNiece Davenport and Dan Wayne, MAG (6:33) LaNiece Davenport reported that MAG recently created the TAG program that is a new ongoing funding source for city-led plans and projects that integrate transportation and land use. It is modeled after UDOT’s Technical Planning Assistance (TPA) Program and WFRC’s Transportation and Land Use Connection (TLC) Program. Dan Wayne provided an overview of the SAP Technical Assistance Program. The first deadline to submit applications for technical assistance was July 18, 2022. Applications from American Fork, Vineyard, Orem, and Provo were submitted. Funding requests for the first round of applications totaled $520,000. Cities that apply that have multiple stations will be coordinated together as one Station Area Plan because of close proximity. A Policy Committee has been established to provide peer-review and certifications of SAPs and to receive updates on SAP Technical Assistance awards. The Committee will meet when there are recommendations to present. The next steps for the SAP technical Assistance Program include: MPO Regional Planning Committee August 4, 2022 Page 3 • Work with applicants to finalize scopes and budgets for procurement • Future rounds of technical assistance to be administered bi-monthly • Work with cities to ensure that SAPs are certification-ready • Hold SAP Policy Committee meetings to review progress and certify SAPs as they are completed The Technical Assistance to Governments (TAG) Program is patterned after the WFRC’s Transportation and Land Use Connections (TLC) Program. The TAG Program will provide technical assistance to cities in Utah, Wasatch, and Summit County for plans and studies that integrate transportation and land use. A broad range of planning activities are eligible that may include, but not limited to addressing specific hurdles or gaps that are preventing the implementation of local plans and/or discouraging developers from carrying out a transformational project in a community. Examples of community and area studies were provided. MAG is currently developing an online application tool and program guidelines to identify eligible projects, the application process, and how applications will be scored. MAG’s roll will be to procure from WFRC-MAG Consultant Pool, provide contract management, and act as a technical advisor on city-led projects. The TAG Program is ongoing and can fund a variety of land use connect project. The motion passed unanimously. 2023 Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) Approval – Bob Allen, MAG (22:09) The Metropolitan Planning Organization (MPO) of MAG has the responsibility of preparing and approving a Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) for the area. The TIP is MPO Regional Planning Committee August 4, 2022 Page 4 prepared and approved every year and lists transportation projects and programs within the MPO for the fiscal years 2023–2027. Conceptual projects are listed through 2030. Municipalities, Utah County, UDOT, UTA, and others are eligible for MPO funds. All regional projects, regardless of the selection agency, must be approved by the MPO committees and be listed in the TIP. The MPO funds projects in the TIP every two years. The Technical Advisory Committee (TAC) and Regional Planning Committee (RPC) created a final prioritized project list on June 2, 2022, following the process established by RPC. That process included: • Reviewing submitted concept reports • MPO staff scoring each project • A field review • Voting to create the ranked priority list of projects RPC must approve the funding scenario for the final prioritized project list and add those projects to the TIP. MPO staff have created a funding scenario. Funds are approximately $117M and will be primarily available in 2025-2026. The scenario includes an additional $20M to be added to the contingency fund due to rising construction costs. Specific information regarding TIP projects can be found at https://mountainland.org/tip MAG Staff and TAC recommends RPC approve the attached funding scenario and approve the TIP presented. Mayor Larson moved to approve the 2023 TIP as presented. Mayor Staheli seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Safe Streets For All (SS4A) Funding Opportunity Discussion – Jim Price, MAG (51:15) In May 2022, the US Department of Transportation released a Notice of Funding Opportunity for the newly created “Safe Streets For All” (SS4A) program. SS4A provides $1 billion annually, nationwide, for the next five years, for planning and implementation of projects and programs designed to directly address transportation safety issues in local and regional areas. A Safety Action Plan will be established to provide an in-depth analysis of safety problem areas; identify solutions for projects, policies, and programs; and develop a plan to implement solutions. Examples were provided of ways that the grant can be implemented. The Committee discussed transportation safety issues in their communities that may fall within the guidelines of SS4A funding. The funds are only available at the MPO and local government level rather than being administered at the state level. Due to the complexities of administering federal grants, MAG staff suggest that the region partners collaborate on the planning and implementation phases that this funding opportunity may provide. Complete information may be found at https://www.transportation.gov/grants/SS4A MPO Regional Planning Committee August 4, 2022 Page 6 MAG Staff and TAC recommends that MAG members collaborate on efforts to obtain and use planning and implementation grants provided by SS4A. MAG would be the lead agency in this effort.
