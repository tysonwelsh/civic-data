Proposed Resolution 2022-7 approving amendments to the Water Shortage

Proposed Ordinance 2022-19 amending Ogden Municipal Code to address
water rates and restrictions and revisions to definitions related to the Water
Shortage Management Plan

A memo from the Public Services Department regarding the Water Shortage Management Plan and City code
amendments came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Utah state code requires each city or entity that
provides culinary water to more than 500 residents to establish a water conservation plan. The plan must be adopted by the
legislative body and is to be updated every five years. Ogden City’s Water Conservation Plan was last updated in 2020 and
included as an addendum the city’s Water Shortage Management Plan. The Water Shortage Management Plan is specific to low
water conditions rather than addressing overall water usage in the city. The proposed amendments to the Water Shortage
Management Plan primarily deal with updating the categories for the declarations of water shortage, what factors may lead to the
declaration and which indices are used to determine water conditions. In addition to the proposed amendments to the Water
Shortage Management Plan, the Administration is proposing amendments to Title 9, Water and Sewer, of the City code to reflect
the changes proposed for the Water Shortage Management Plan and to clarify the Mayor’s discretion when increasing water rates
in times of low water availability.

Public Utilities Manager Herd summarized the memo and stated Administration is proposing changes to the city’s Water
Shortage Management Plan as well related changes to Title 9 of the City’s code. The proposed amendments to the Water Shortage
Management Plan include changes to the four phases of water shortage. The proposed amendments also update the triggers for
each phase. Currently, the trigger for Phase II is tied to water levels at Pineview Reservoir and Ogden River and the trigger for
Phase III is tied to additional sources such as the Eden valley wells. The proposed amendments would tie the trigger for each
phase to the state’s drought index. In addition, wording to allow the triggering of a particular phase based on water system failure
or contamination is added to Phase II and Phase III. Lastly, the amendments also add limits on maximum irrigations per week for
each of the water shortage phases. Mr. Herd also detailed changes to Title 9 of the City code; this section of the code hosts the fee
structure for the water and sewer utilities as well as other important regulations regarding the water utility. Title 9 includes in it the
authority for the Mayor to temporarily increase water rates up to 20% in times of drought or other emergency conditions affecting
the availability of water. The proposed amendments provide some clarification on how extraordinary drought conditions are
determined and how the Mayor may implement increases for different user classes. The changes also update the definitions
included in the code to reflect the updated water shortage phases proposed in the Water Shortage Management Plan amendments.

Mayor's Proposed Fiscal Year 2022-2023 Budget

The Mayor’s Proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2022-2023 Budget came before the Council for consideration. A memo from
Council staff explained each year during the first Council Meeting in May, the Mayor is required to present a proposed budget for
the upcoming fiscal year. The Council accepts the proposed budget for review and, through a series of work sessions, reviews the
proposal over the next two months. A tentative budget is adopted after holding an initial public hearing. A second public hearing
is held at the time of adoption of the final budget. During the budget hearings, the Council outlines their proposed changes to the
budget and accepts public input. Once the final public hearing is closed, the Council makes its changes and adopts the final City
budget. By State law, a budget must be adopted by June 30. An exception to the timing is allowed if the Council is considering a
proposed property tax increase.

Mayor Caldwell used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide his proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2022-2023 budget to
the Council. The top priority in the FY2023 budget is the City’s employees and he has recommended a compensation increase for
general employees, sworn Fire employees, and sworn Police employees. He offered a comparison of the proposed FY 2023 budget
and the FY2022 budget, identifying a 17.16 percent increase in FY2023. He indicated that the proposed budget includes a
property tax increase to capture an additional $2.8 million in revenue, noting that if the City had increased property taxes over the
past three years, the City would have captured additional revenue of nearly $4.5 million. It is important to consider a tax increase
each year to capture revenue associated with inflation and he feels that the City can educate residents of the importance of that
type of action to avoid higher tax increase in single years. Failure to consider appropriate property tax rates reduces the City’s
purchasing power and its ability to respond to typical inflationary increases. He then reiterated the importance of City employees
and discussed the impact of employee turnover; the current fiscal year to date turnover for full-time employees is 11.76 percent
and 76 full time employees have left the City since July 1, 2021. On average, it takes 8 to 12 weeks to replace a knowledgeable
worker and then six months to a year before the replacement gets to full productivity mode. The cost of replacing an individual
can range from one half to two times the employee’s annual average salary. Ogden City and many other cities have struggled to
recruit new employees. He expounded on his employee compensation increase:

e 13 percent cost of living adjustment (COLA) for general full-time employees: Pay tables (minimum and
maximum) would shift by 13%. All full-time general employees would receive this increase.

e 14 percent COLA for sworn Fire employees: Pay tables (minimum and maximum) would shift by 14%. All
full time sworn fire personnel would receive this increase and be eligible for the next step on their
anniversary date.

e 8 percent COLA for sworn Police employees: Pay tables (minimum and maximum) would shift by 8%. All
full time sworn police personnel would receive this increase and be eligible for the next step on their
anniversary date.

Update and review of communications plan for the proposed right-of-way
vacation at 1st Street between Washington Boulevard and Adams Avenue
and proposed rezone at 101 and 131 Adams Avenue extended from the
April 19, 2022 meeting

A memo from Council staff explained this item was discussed during the April 19, 2022 meeting, and it was decided that
it should be extended. The petitioner is working on a housing development at 120 Washington Blvd and would like to expand the
property to the north and east. To this end, the petitioner is requesting to rezone portions of two properties along Adams Avenue
and the Ist Street right-of-way to the north. The requested rezones would total approximately .84 acres, which would be added to
the 120 Washington Boulevard development.

Council Policy Analyst Watkins summarized the discussion that took place during the April 19, 2022 Council meeting;
although that property is already zoned C-3, and it is not part of this petition, the development raises concerns for some Council
members. It is unclear what the development would look like, and how commercial uses would be incorporated into the project.
The Council decided it would be best to get these questions answered before allowing an expansion of the project boundaries with
this rezone petition. In order to ensure the Council’s questions are addressed, this item will be considered at a future work session.
The Council requested to see a site plan that shows the apartment development, future commercial development, and how the
rezone plays into the development. As soon as Council staff receives this information, the item will be scheduled to be heard in a
work session. The petitioner and the public will be notified when this item returns to a Council meeting.

Council member Hyer stated he attended a recent meeting with all parties interested in this project and it was very
positive; he is not sure there is a need to further extend the application and he asked that leadership place the matter on the May
17, 2022 agenda. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith indicated that she will work with Council leadership to respond to that
request.
