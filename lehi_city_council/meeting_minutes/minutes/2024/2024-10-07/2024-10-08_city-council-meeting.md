2. Presentations and Reports
2.1 Emergency Management Presentation
Scott Dabell, Lehi Emergency Manager, presented to the Council. The emergency management
team had updated the emergency operations plan to align with recent directives and regulations.
They collaborated with various agencies and hospitals to conduct tabletop trainings and exercises
to support first responders. The team had provided emergency preparedness training for city
staff, including active shooter and hazmat scenarios. They had also organized community
preparedness events and outreach, such as promoting the Great Shake Out earthquake drill.
Overall, the discussion focused on public outreach, community engagement, and continuous
improvement of the city's emergency preparedness capabilities.

2.2 Presentation and Discussion of Railroad Safety Protocol
Luke Seegmiller, gave an update on the quiet zone, the temporary suspension of the quiet zone
and how to reinstate the quite zone. The city had originally established the quiet zone in 2012, as
part of a collaborative effort with several other cities along the FrontRunner commuter rail
corridor. Lehi City had taken on the responsibility of representing the group and coordinating
with the Federal Railroad Administration (FRA) on maintaining the quiet zone.
The city had recently received an inspection letter from the FRA, outlining deficiencies that
needed to be addressed to keep the quiet zone in compliance. Lehigh City had worked with the
other participating cities to develop an action plan and timeline to fix the issues, such as ensuring

issues, leaving the train horn noise to continue impacting residents along the entire FrontRunner
corridor.

   acres located at 635 West State Street, changing the designation from Commercial to

   Brad Tronson was the applicant. Tronson explained he purchased the property a few years

    and residential uses.

7. Consideration of Ordinance #59-2024 a Development Code Amendment to Chapter 26,
   Accessory Uses, revising the required side yard setback for accessory buildings on a
   corner lot.
   Planning Director Kim Struthers explained the amendment aims to provide more flexibility
   for property owners with corner lots to place accessory buildings like detached garages or
   storage sheds. Under current code, corner lots have more restricted placement options
   compared to interior lots. The proposed change would allow structures to be placed closer to
   the side street, maintaining a 20-foot setback only for garages requiring street access, while
   other structures could follow interior lot setback requirements.

    explaining his personal situation with a corner lot where he hopes to build a home addition.

10. Consideration of Ordinance #61-2024 a Development Code Amendment to Chapters 12-
    A and 12-B, adding some additional requirements to grading.
    The amendment addressed grading issues by adding requirements for slopes over 30%,
    including geotechnical reports and retaining wall plans. It also established penalties for
    unauthorized grading on city property as a Class B misdemeanor.

    The Council engaged in significant discussion about enforcement mechanisms and penalties.
    Ryan Wood, City Attorney, provided important context about the Class B misdemeanor
    designation, explaining it carries up to six months jail time versus 90 days for a Class C. He
    noted this gives prosecutors more flexibility in resolving cases and signals the Council's
    seriousness about violations.
    A key debate centered around the inclusion of a 30-day notice period before enforcement.
    While the Planning Commission recommended including this provision, Wood pointed out it
    was unusual to codify a grace period for violations, as it essentially permits illegal activity
    during that time. He explained the city's standard practice is already to work with property
    owners to achieve compliance before pursuing penalties, regardless of whether it's specified
    in code.

    Mayor Johnson raised a concern about post-construction issues, particularly regarding
    homeowners modifying drainage swales after taking possession of their properties. This led
    to discussion about enforcement challenges. The Council also discussed liability issues,
    particularly regarding who would be held responsible for violations - property owners,
    contractors, or equipment operators. Wood clarified that enforcement typically focuses on
    property owners, as investigating contractor responsibility would be impractical.
