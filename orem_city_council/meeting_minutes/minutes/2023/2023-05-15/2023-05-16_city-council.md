Transportation Master Plan: Presenter: Sam Kelly, City Engineer and John Dorney, Horrocks
Engineering

Mr. Dorney from Horrocks Engineering went over updates to the Transportation Master
Plan from 2022. Updates include access management standards, traffic calming guidelines,
traffic impact study guidelines and Southwest Street Network Plan. Struggles they are dealing
with are that highways have high mobility and low access, arterials have high mobility and low
accessibility, collectors balance between mobility and access, and local roads have low mobility
and high degree of access. The transportation master plan needs to have projects that will meet
the future demands. Major streets in Orem, Center Street, State Street, 8300 N, University Pkwy
etc. are part of future projects. Projections show the need to update many of the main streets.

Ms. Millett asked questions about 1600 North. She is wondering if we can take that off
the master transportation plan and put it in later as needed. Mr. Lambson asked about the option
to leave it in the plan but could the City Council make decisions about how it will be handled.
Mr. Kelly explained that both are an option. Discussions continued about 1600 North. The plan
will have a public hearing for the Master Transportation Plan on June 27, 2023. The plan will go
to the Planning Commission tomorrow. Mr. Bybee suggested that a better priority list will help
in the planning process.

Presenter: Jason Bench, Assistant Development Services Director and Ryan Clark, Acting

Assistant City Manager/Development Services Director

Mr. Bench is proposing an amendment to portions of the City code. Some reasons for the
rezoning are preserving public land for future generations, city and school lands have been paid
for with public dollars, and to ensure appropriate due diligence is followed before redeveloping
public lands. Mr. Bench showed the parts of the City Code that would need to be updated as
well as showed an updated zoning map. Mr. Clark illustrated 5 elements that should be
considered as per the LYRB (Lewis Young report) Research Study, preserving land for public
use, multi-generational living, housing turnover as population ages, increased density due to new
accessory dwelling units, and increased general population due to continued growth along the
Wasatch Front.

Mr. Bench reported that the Planning Commission is in support of this zoning amendment.
Ms. Lauret asked how the public hearings were received. Mr. Spencer asked about the concerns
some have expressed about Orem is doing this to devalue the property and that the City is not
communicating with the Alpine School District (ASD). Mr. Bybee reported he met with the
school district in March and the Orem staff has monthly meetings with the ASD. Mr. Bybee also
explained that ASD would be willing to meet in a small group with the Orem City Council
members. Ms. Lauret asked what this change does to future school closures that Orem may not
want to purchase. Mr. Bench explained that rezoning could happen at that location. Discussions
continued about the zoning changes.

ORDINANCE for CARE Tax Allocation Presenter: Brandon Nelson, Finance Director and
Trevor Bell, Budget Manager

Mr. Earl confirmed this is an ordinance not a resolution. Mr. Nelson explained this is a
follow up to the work session where there were discussions about the CARE recipients. The
main change was increased funds for the Timpanogos Symphony Orchestra. Mr. Peterson had
concerns that the split of money is not a 50-50 match. Mr. Merrill and Mr. Bybee explained that
the decision going forward for the next 10 years will be decided on the ballot for future CARE
tax.

Mr. Bybee explained the SCERA park improvements will include digital signage that will
include the SCERA announcements. Mr. Merrill explained that over the 10 years of the CARE
tax the money was split 50/50 even if individual years were not.

Mr. Spencer moved to approve the Fiscal Year 2023-2024 CARE Tax awards as outlined
in Exhibit A of the ordinance. Ms. Millett seconded the motion. Those voting yes: David Young,
David Spencer, Debby Lauret, LaNae Millett, Jeff Lambson,Terry Peterson and Tom Macdonald.
The motion passed.

Council to retain separate legal counsel Presenter: Steve Earl, City Attorney

Mr. Earl explained that this ordinance allows the City Council to hire a separate legal
counsel and this attorney is an “at will" employee and can be removed by the City Council. Mr.
Lambson is concerned this is a redundancy of jobs. Mr. Young, Mr. Peterson, and Ms. Millett
agree that a separate legal counsel is a benefit to the council.

Mr. Peterson moved by ordinance to amend Section 2-3-10 (Functions of City Council)
to authorize the City Council to retain separate legal counsel. Ms. Millett seconded the motion.
Those voting yes: David Young, David Spencer, LaNae Millett, and Terry Peterson. Those voting
no: Jeff Lambson, Debby Lauret and Tom Macdonald. The motion passed.
