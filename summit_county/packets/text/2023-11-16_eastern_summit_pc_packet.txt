Background 
 
The Promontory Development Agreement was approved on January 2, 2001. It provided for 
885 estate lots as part of a base density, 716 incentive density lots, two hotels/300 resort units, 
equestrian facility, up to five golf club houses, and other accessory uses on approximately 6500 
acres with no less than 3900 acres remaining as passive open space. Of the 1901 total units in 
the density pool, 1,462 units have been approved. The development parameters for this project 
are specifically set forth in the Development Agreement. 
 
Analysis and Findings 
 
Density 
 
The applicant is using density from the existing pool of density.  
 
Critical Lands 
 
Promontory Development Agreement Section 4.4.4 states; “...All structures which are built 
within the retention and ridgeline viewsheds, defined in Section 1.52 and as designated on 
Exhibit E, shall comply with the Design Guidelines and the Supplemental Design Guidelines for 
Retention and Ridgeline Areas in Exhibit C. Additionally, all base residential units located within 
Ridgeline Areas shown on Exhibit E must comply with the ridgeline requirements of Section 
4.8.6.1.6.” 
15
3 
Promontory Mountain Crest Subdivision – November 16, 2023 
 
Promontory Development Agreement Section 4.8.6.1.6 states; “In no instance shall any 
development be permitted to encroach onto a ridgeline area designated on Exhibit E in a 
manner that is inconsistent with the requirements of this paragraph. For purposes of this 
Agreement, ridgeline protection means that on a ridgeline area designated on Exhibit E, building 
forms shall not break or detract from the natural lines of the hillside or ridgeline. A building that 
is located a minimum of 50 vertical feet below the ridge will generally be regarded as meeting 
the ridgeline requirements herein provided. Further, residential units which comply with the 
approved Supplemental Design Guidelines in Exhibit C and do not break a Ridgeline Area skyline 
when viewed from U.S. Highway 40, Interstate 80, Highway 248, and the Browns Canyon Road, 
shall be deemed to fulfill all ridgeline requirements. The County may require at subdivision plat 
review additional computer imaging to ensure compliance with this benchmark requirement.” 
 
The applicant has provided an overlay map which identifies the Promontory Development 
Agreement designated Ridgeline and Ridgeline Retention areas. Based on the overlay, Staff 
confirmed that portions of Lots 1 and 2 are within the Ridgeline Retention. The applicant has 
also provided a ridgeline measurement exhibit which confirms that Lots 1 and 2 are ~175 feet 
below the Ridgeline.  
 
Promontory Development Agreement Exhibit C Supplemental Guidelines states;  
“HEIGHT AND MASSING OF STRUCTURES  
The purpose of building height restrictions in the Promontory Design Guidelines is to minimize 
the visual effect of the Community at full development. With that end goal in mind, and in order 
to address the increased sensitivity to this goal in Retention and Ridge line Areas, any 
construction in Retention and Ridgeline Areas can have no portion of a structure (except for 
chimney elements) exceeding a maximum height 28 feet above original natural grade directly 
below the point of measurement. Additionally, all homes must have a one story massing 
element on the downhill side of the western viewshed. Offsets or indentations in wall planes 
create visual interest and add depth via shadow lines. No building wall may extend more than 
30 feet in length without an offset in the vertical plane of at least four feet.” 
 
Staff has included a condition of approval to require that a plat note be added to reflect the 
Exhibit C language above.  
 
Building Pad Locations 
Park City Fire Department expressed concern over the long driveways associated with the 
proposal. They stated that; “Based on the documents provided, both lots will require driveways 
greater than 150 feet in length which will result in the requirement for a fire vehicle turnaround. 
We have had several drawings submitted to us for approval in the Pinnacle subdivision that the 
designer was unable to provide a turnaround on because of slope and lot design...”   
The applicant is proposing the Lot 1 building pad location in the back portion of the property. 
The applicant has stated that; 
 
“The reasoning for the driveway configuration are several fold: 
• View corridor – we have done sight analysis on these two lots and the views are achieved 
by setting the building footprint higher up on the lot. We anticipate this will be the 
16
4 
Promontory Mountain Crest Subdivision – November 16, 2023 
request of the lot owner developing this property anyway, so we’re attempting to avoid 
the potential request to revise the enevelope. 
• Trails along the road – members in the past have expressed concern over people walking 
on the trails in proximity to a home and being able to see into people’s living areas. This 
building envelope addresses that concern. 
• The Dye Course Cabins across the street will most certainly have complaints about 
construction happening in front of their existing homes, lots that have been well 
established for 15+ years. Moving this building area back more than 200 feet takes away 
some of the nuisance (noise, dust, etc) from their immediate front door.” 
 
Promontory Development Agreement states; “Viewshed refers to Policy 3.12 in the Snyderville 
Basin General Plan, which require four different approaches to development and open space 
stewardship.” 
 
Promontory Development Agreement Section 3.7 states; “Promontory contains outstanding 
features which advance the policies, goals and objectives of Eastern Summit County and its 
General Plan beyond mere conformity, including the following: (i) agreements with respect to 
design controls and limitations to minimize the visual impact of the development; (ii) the 
clustering and appropriate location of density, (iii) the creation of significant Open Space areas, 
trail connections and recreational improvements, (iv) the restoration of upland sage habitat...” 
 
Promontory Design Guidelines Section 2.4 states; “2.4 ACCESS DRIVES 
Each homesite may be accessed by a single driveway or shaded drive as outlined herein. 
Access drives shall be located to preserve and avoid important natural features, such as 
large or significant plant materials, drainageways, rock outcroppings, so as to minimize 
disruption of the existing landscape.” 
 
Promontory 2008 Design Guidelines Section 3.4 states; “3.4 Access Drives 
Driveway surfaces are the only improvement allowed outside the building envelope, except for 
underground utilities servicing the Homesite; thus, driveway designs are carefully analyzed and 
regulated by the Committee. Each Homesite may be accessed by a single or a shared driveway 
as detailed below. Circular drives are generally not allowed unless they enter and exit the 
building envelope at a single point. Access drives shall always be located so they preserve and 
avoid disturbing important natural features, such as large or significant plant materials,  
drainageways, and/or rock outcroppings; and such drives shall always minimize disruption of 
the existing landscape as well.” 
 
Promontory 2008 Design Guidelines Section 5.6.4 states; “During any and all salvage 
operations, special care must be taken to minimize Homesite disruption as much as possible and 
to ensure the Site’s Natural Area always remains untouched.” 
 
Eastern Summit County General Plan Goal 2.1c states; “Work to ensure that single-family 
residential development minimizes disturbance, and is clustered where appropriate.” 
 
The proposed building pad and driveway locations would result in longer driveways which 
would lead to unnecessary disturbance on a visible hillside. Staff has included conditions of 
approval to require that Lot 1 building pad be moved to the front of the lot and that a plat note 
be added to limit driveways to 150-foot maximum length. NOTE: Most driveways surrounding 
the proposed development are significantly less than 150 feet.

Proposal 
 
The Summit County Community Development Department has received an application for a 
Conditional Use Permit for a ~4,700 sq. ft. Accessory Building on Lot 2 of the Cottonwood 
Canyon Subdivision, located at 1485 Dog Ear Dr. (Parcel CCAN-2-AM), Hoytsville, Summit 
County, UT. The applicant has stated that the building will be used for private purposes which 
include storage of; “a couple camping trailers, a RZR, a couple trucks, a tractor, and hopefully a 
boat that we will be keeping in there.”  The applicant has stated that the building is meant for 
private, non-commercial purposes.   
Background 
 
The Cottonwood Canyon Subdivision was platted in May of 2021 and consists of ten lots. Lot 2 
(Parcel CCAN-2-AM) is currently vacant.

Findings of Fact: 
 
1. The Cottonwood Canyon Subdivision was platted in May of 2021 and consists of ten lots.  
2. Parcel CCAN-2-AM is located at 1485 Dog Ear Dr., Hoytsville, Summit County, UT, in the 
AG-5 Zone.   
3. Parcel CCAN-2-AM is currently vacant.  
4. SKO Enterprises Inc. (Justin Taylor) is the owner of Parcel CCAN-2-AM.  
5. The applicant is requesting a Conditional Use Permit for a ~4,700 sq. ft. Accessory 
Building on Parcel CCAN-2-AM. 
 
6. The applicant has stated that the building will be used for private purposes which 
include storage of; “a couple camping trailers, a RZR, a couple trucks, a tractor, and 
hopefully a boat that we will be keeping in there.”   
7. The applicant has stated that the building is meant for private, non-commercial 
purposes.   
8. Section 11-3-16 of the Eastern Summit County Development Code requires that 
Accessory buildings and uses to the principal use, exceeding 2,000 square feet in the AG-
5 Zone be reviewed as a Conditional Use Permit.  
9. The building is located a relatively flat area.  
10. The building meets all Eastern Summit County Development Code setback and height 
requirements.  
11. Uses proposed in the building are compatible with those in the general area (residential, 
agriculture).   
12. The location of the building and the size of the lot mitigate potential negative impacts of 
the accessory building on surrounding uses and the natural environment.  
13. The use will neither hinder nor eliminate any existing agricultural operations in the area 
and will not hinder the existing irrigation systems in the area. 
14. Water and septic are not being proposed.  
15. The impact of the building on County infrastructure as it relates to traffic volume, and 
other public services will be minimal.    
16. Due to the small scale of the use, it is not anticipated that any maintenance or 
improvements will be necessary on Dog Ear Dr.
