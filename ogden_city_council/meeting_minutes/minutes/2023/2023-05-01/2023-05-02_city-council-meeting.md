A memo from Council staff came before the Council to consider a bond parameters resolution for the Marshall White
Community Center project.. The memo stated the Council will consider authorizing the issuance of Sales Tax Revenue Bonds in
an amount not to exceed $31 Million. The bond proceeds will be used to fund demolition of the current Marshall White Center
and construction of a new Marshall White Community Center. Funds will also be used to complete repairs to the parking
structures at The Junction. Debt service on the bonds will be covered by Business Depot Ogden (BDO) lease revenue. The total
project is estimated to cost $34.3 Million with funding coming from grants, ARPA funds, city investment, debt and donations. A
public hearing on the matter will be held May 23, 2023.

Nate Roberts, Lewis Young Robertson Burningham, the City’s Bond Counsel, summarized the memo and provided an
overview of the key terms of the resolution as follows:

e Principal amount not to exceed $31 million;

e Bond term of not more than 40 years;

e Bond sold at a price not less than 97 percent of the total principal amount;
e Interest rate not to exceed six-percent annum.

The resolution also authorizes designated officers to make final determinations regarding method of sale, principal
amounts, terms, discounts, and other decisions relating to the bond issuance, including alterations, changes or additions.
Designated officers consist of any four of the following six: the Mayor (or designee), the Management Services Director, the
Chair and Vice Chair of the Council (or their designees), the Council’s Executive Director (or designee), and the Treasurer of the
Issuer. At least two of such designated officers shall be from the Chair or Vice Chair of the Council (or their designees) or the
Council’s Executive Director (or designee). Finally, the resolution also sets the public hearing for final action on the bonds for
May 23, 2023 at 6:00 p.m. Closing on the bond could occur on June 21, 2023.

Council member White inquired as to the repayment term of the portion of the bond associated with the Junction Parking ;
Structure. Mr. Roberts indicated there will be one bond sale for the two projects, but there will be two repayment schedules; the
parking structure repayment term is just five years, and the rest of the bonds will have a 25-repayment term. This means the bond
payment for the first five years will be $1.6 million and that amount will be reduced to $1.2 for the remaining term.

The Mayor’s proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2023-2024 Budget came before the Council for consideration. A memo from
Council staff explained each year during the first Council meeting in May, the Mayor is required to present a proposed budget for
the upcoming fiscal year. The Council holds a series of work sessions over the next several weeks to review the proposed
expenditures for all departmental accounts and all major and minor funds. A tentative budget is adopted after holding an initial
public hearing. At least one public hearing is held before adoption of the final budget. During the budget hearings, the Council
outlines proposed changes to the tentative and final budgets and accepts public input. Once the final public hearing is closed, the
Council makes changes and adopts the final City budget. By State law, a budget must be adopted by June 30 unless a property tax
increase is under consideration, then the final budget must be adopted by September 1.

City Attorney Departmental Budget: City Attorney Williams indicated the City Attorney Department includes both civil
and criminal divisions; the Department is funded primarily through the General Fund. The department also has two positions that
are partially funded by grants through the Victims of Crime Act (VOCA) and the Violence Against Women Act (VAWA). He
noted he has compared the City’s case load with the case load of several other cities and has found that Ogden’s criminal and civil
