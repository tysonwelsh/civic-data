stated that he has been very impressed by Mr. Cook in his opinions and participation in city affairs. He

about how the city is run.
  Councilor Parady moved to ratify Terry Cook as an alternate member of the Planning Commission.

ANIMAL TRANSPORT PROGRAM AGREEMENT APPROVED:

  City Attorney Kasey Wright explained to the Council that Nephi City’s animal shelter is considered a
“no kill” shelter. To achieve this designation, animals occasionally need to be transported to other areas
so they can be adopted out. There are certain organizations who provide this transport service and one
such organization has asked the city to enter into an Animal Transport Program Agreement.
   The agreement specifies the methods of transportation for animals, the cost associated with
transport, and responsibility for the animals during transport. It also contains a termination clause that
allows either party to terminate the agreement at any time with notice.
   Councilor Worwood moved to authorize the Nephi City Police Department to enter into the Animal
Transport Program Agreement. Councilor Parady seconded the motion. The motion passed
unanimously.

AGREEMENT WITH STORM ENGINEERING FOR GAS GATE STATION DESIGN APPROVED:

  Finance Director/City Recorder Lisa Brough reminded the Council that In the last City Council work
session, the Council made the administrative decision to authorize the Mayor to sign an agreement with
Storm Engineering (dba Tempest) to design the Natural Gas Gate Station for the power plant on the
south end of the city. They were selected as the engineer for this project at the City Council meeting on
June 6th. Storm Engineering has been doing some preliminary work on the project and the Council, in
the interest of time, authorized the Mayor to sign the agreement and then to ratify it at this Council
meeting.
   Councilor Pardy moved to ratify the signing of the agreement with Storm Engineering. Councilor
Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

AGREEMENT FOR THE 138 KV TRANSMISSION LINE APPROVED:

   City Attorney Kasey Wright updated the council that the negotiations with several property owners to
obtain easements and property associated with the 138 KV transmission line had been completed and
the agreements and easements have been signed by the property owners. Each of the property owners
were also interested in annexing their properties. If annexed, they will be allowed to be served with the
city’s electricity and other utilities. It also allows them to develop their properties easier in the future.
   Mr. Wright explained that the terms in the agreements are consistent with the direction of the
Council and that they would further the city’s ability to meet the need for more electrical capacity.
    Councilor Worwood moved to approve entering into the following agreements:
                   Easement agreement for Dale & Vicki Jackson
                   Easement Agreement for Brown Management, LLC
                   Easement Agreement for HCPI Holdings, LLC
                   Easement and Property Purchase Agreement for George & Jill Jackson
   Councilor Parady seconded the motion. The motion carried on a unanimous vote.

Work Session 8:09 pm

 1.     The Council reviewed a staff report written by City Administrator Seth Atkinson about a request
      that originated from the Six County Association of Governments to help Juab County pay for senior
      services. The services included Meals on Wheels, senior trips, and meals held at the Senior Center
      in the county building. Costs are increasing and Six County informed Juab County that some of the
      costs of these services will soon be directly charged to Juab County.
        The county contacted city staff to see if there were any funds from the city budget that could be
      allocated to these services in the short term and for future ongoing operations for senior
      services. These are valuable services to have for the elderly in our community and the county has
      done well in providing them.
         Because the county has historically provided senior services, there are no funds in the current
      budget that have been allocated to this service. If the Council decided to help fund senior services,
      funds from other department(s) would need to be reduced and reallocated to a senior services line
      item or new department.
        Mt. Atkinson Staff has evaluated the county’s request and had the following concerns about
      funding senior services.
      1) The first concern was that Nephi City has several other operational concerns that are
      underfunded including, but not limited to, police staffing, the Hive operations, street maintenance,
      bridge improvements, additional parks & recreation staffing, swimming pool repairs and upgrades,
      technology needs, and wage parity with the market. Before the city were to take on new services,
      these should be addressed.
      2) If the city were to help with funding senior services, the city should have a say in how those
      services should be provided, or simply provide the services completely as a new city department.
      Right now, the request is to subsidize the county services that are being provided without any
      supervisory control.
      3) Another problem with subsidizing county services is that the same services would be provided
      to seniors who did not live in the city limits as those who do. These individuals that receive the
      same services who live in the county, are only taxed once with their county tax rate to receive
      those services. If Nephi City were to subsidize these services, then a Nephi City senior would
      essentially be taxed twice for the same service, once through the county and again through their
      Nephi City tax rate.
       The council agreed that they would need more information before they directed staff on how to
      proceed. They wanted to know the amount of funding the county would need. The council also
      wanted to know the participation rates. The discussion was tabled until more information can be
      gathered.
