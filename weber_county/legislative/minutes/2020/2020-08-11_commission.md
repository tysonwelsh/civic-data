     5. RECONSIDERATION OF THE DECISION TO AMEND COUNTY CODES §104-7 & 108-7 TO ALLOW
        CULTIVATION & PROCESSING OF CANNABIS CULTIVATION IN THE A-2 ZONES DATED AUGUST 4, 2020 –
        ORDINANCE 2020-12
         Chair Froerer noted that last week there was great discussion on this item and there had been a little
         confusion on the motion and this is to correct that, and that this allows cultivation in an A-2 Zone but only
         one production establishment, unless there was application for more. The State has only approved eight
         licenses. Charles Ewert, of the County Planning Division, stated that the amendment clarifies that
         processing is not allowed in the A-2 Zone; this ordinance is for cultivation only. Christopher Crockett,
         Deputy County Attorney, explained the county’s rules of order in detail regarding a motion to reconsider,
         including the issue of timing and motions.
         Commissioner Jenkins moved to approve the reconsideration Ordinance 2020-12 of the decision to amend
         Weber County Codes §104-7 and 108-7 to allow cultivation and processing of cannabis cultivation in the
         A-2 zones dated August 4, 2020; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye
         Commissioner Jenkins read the following amendment to this ordinance as requested by the Commission
         that, “No more than one cannabis production establishment is allowed to be in operation in an agricultural
         zone at any one time.”) and asked about the A-3 Zone. Mr. Ewert said that currently the code does not put
         a limit on how many can operate in the A-3 or the M-1 through M-3 Zones. In discussing this today, there
         was a question whether the county could make the restriction on the A-3 Zone according to the State’s
         regulations and Seth Gomm, Attorney and the applicant’s representative, who said that he has knowledge
         of the related State Code stated that each jurisdiction needs to provide one location in both an industrial
         and an agriculture zone for each of the uses and believed that the commissioners would be in compliance
         with what they were suggesting. Commissioner Jenkins asked to include that no more than one cannabis
         production establishment is to be allowed in operation in the A-2 and the A-3 Zones at any one time. Mr.
         Ewert and Mr. Gomm said that they were in agreement with this language.
         Commissioner Jenkins moved to adopt Ordinance 2020-12 to amend Weber County Codes §104-7 and
         108-7 to allow cultivation and processing of cannabis in the A-2 zones dated August 4, 2020 with the
         language, “No more than one cannabis production establishment is allowed to be in operation in the A-2
         and the A-3 Zones at any one time.” Commissioner Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye
