CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE NO. 2024-1250-O, AN ORDINANCE AMENDING
THE GENERAL PLAN BY ADOPTING THE WASHINGTON COUNTY

TRANSPORTATION MASTER PLAN AS AN ELEMENT OF THE GENERAL PLAN

Public Works Director Todd Edwards explained this is an ongoing project. The Utah Department
of Transportation (UDOT) and Dixie Transportation Executive Committee (DTEC) issued funding
to the County in order to finish the Transportation Master Plan (TMP). Avenue Consultants was
contracted to undertake the study which provides general locations of existing and proposed
freeways, arterial and collector streets, public transit, active transportation facilities and other
modes of transportation. The study also addresses proposed land uses, residential and commercial

CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE NO. 2024-1251-O, AN ORDINANCE AMENDING
THE WASHINGTON COUNTY CODE, VARIOUS TITLE 10 ZONING REGULATIONS,
AND TITLE 11 SUBDIVISION REGULATIONS, TO COMPLY WITH NEW STATE
LAW__REGARDING _ SINGLE-FAMILY, _TWO-FAMILY, AND TOWNHOUSE
DEVELOPMENT, AND MISCELLANEOUS CHANGES (TITLES 10 & 11
AMENDMENTS — FEBRUARY 2024)

Deputy County Attorney Victoria Hales stated that in 2023 the State Legislature enacted Senate
Bill 174 requiring all jurisdictions in the state to streamline the processing of subdivisions for
single-family, two-family and townhouse developments with the stated purpose of stimulating
more housing development. The Planning Commission discussed the changes to Titles 10 and 11
in detail and unanimously recommended approval of the proposed amendments.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Iverson to Adopt Ordinance No. 2024-1251-O, An
Amending the Washington County Code, Various Title 10 Zoning
Regulations, and Title 11 Subdivision Regulations, to Comply with New State
Law Regarding Single-Family, Two-Family, and Townhouse Development,
and Miscellaneous Changes (Titles 10 & 11 Amendments — February 2024).
Motion seconded by Commissioner Snow and carried by unanimous vote, with
Commissioners Almquist, Iverson and Snow voting aye.

CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. R-2024-3262, A RESOLUTION ACCEPTING
FUNDING FROM _ THE STATE OF UTAH FOR CERTAIN TRAIL AND SEWER
IMPROVEMENTS IN ZION NATIONAL PARK

Regional Tourism Regulatory Affairs Director Heath Hansen stated that Zion National Park has
seen a large increase in visitors, which has created challenges that include failed septic systems
and inadequate restroom facilities. The Park has plans to install a sewer system to resolve these
concerns, but the federal funding would not be available until Fiscal Year 2027. In order to avoid
visitor restrictions, the Utah Legislature appropriated $18 million to be used for trail and sewer
improvements.
