                 – Nathan Cherpeski, City Manager
         *This item was discussion prior to Item 2.1*

          City Manager Cherpeski discussed they had seen an unusually wet winter that year,
          which led to issues at drainage sites. He said the easements were designed to go around
          properties, but they have since had impacts on homes. City Manager Cherpeski said the
          landscape does not maintain the drainage ways, which has led to issues. He outlined
          homeowners had two things to ask of the Council: one, they asked if the City would
          participate financially, and also asked if and how they can build improvements on City
          property. City Manager Cherpeski relayed that the Staff’s recommendation noted these
          drainage sites were approved a certain way and had been based on a historical flow of
          the area. City Manager Cherpeski said he was sympathetic to these issues but felt this
          was a once in a decade issue. City Manager Cherpeski said the Staff recommendation was
          sympathetic to the owners but felt the Council should decide what to do move forward.

          private property. City Manager Cherpeski said he believed this was due to pre-existing

          2.1. Discussion on the City’s Water Rate Structure – Justun Edwards, Public Works
               Director
      Finance Director Kyle Maurer introduced Keith Larsen with Bowen Collins and Associates
      to discuss the water rates and different tier structures. Finance Director Maurer said they
      were still working on the water portion of the master plan and stressed this was just the first
      step.
April 26, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                                Page 5 of 18



      Mr. Larsen recapped the past meetings and noted he was looking for basic feedback to help
      move forward. Mr. Larsen walked the Council through a graph on expected revenue and fees.
      Mr. Larsen said there was a huge drop in sales in 2022 for conservation and reduced water
      use. Mr. Larsen indicated there had been a bounce back in 2023, but inflation had created
      different problems. Mr. Larsen also noted they were not bringing in enough revenue to cover
      expected costs.

      Mr. Larsen said they were looking for ways to spend less. He reported they had assessed the
      value of their water system and came to a total of $750 Million. Mr. Larsen indicated the team
      devised a new system which would tell them how much they should spend annually. He
      discussed operational costs were fixed, so they calculated annual costs of $8 to $13 Million.
      Mr. Larsen said they were looking at $93 Million, which needed to be spent in the next ten
      years.

      Councilmember Shields asked if they were dividing up secondary improvement needs and
      culinary improvement needs. Mr. Larsen responded with the plan accounted for both types
      but they could do an in-depth look into both types.

      Mr. Larsen said they drifted below the sustainable level of funding at the current budget,
      though said he believed there would be some ongoing maintenance.

      Mr. Larsen said they had been told about three alternatives. Mr. Larsen stated the rest of the
      presentation was structured around alternative three. Councilmember Shields relayed his
      issue was not knowing what they were paying for. Councilmember Shields said they may
      change their strategy moving forward. Councilmember Shields was not sure they would go
      with alternative three. Mr. Larsen said he appreciated the idea of providing more plans as the
      details were fleshed out.

      Councilmember Henderson echoed these sentiments. Mr. Larsen said a lack of investment in
      a secondary site would be an issue. Mr. Larsen said the focus should be on long term
      sustainable funding. Councilmember Hodges asked if bonds would be the best way to pursue
      the project. Finance Director Maurer said bonding had a lower interest rate and would be
      more palatable for residents.

      Mr. Larsen switched gears and discussed the changes in collection. Mr. Larsen said the goal
      was generating revenue, improving water usage, simplifying rate structure, equitably allocate
      costs, while encouraging but not forcing secondary use for those who have it available.

      Mr. Larsen recommended changing the water rate tiers. Mr. Larsen referred to a visual aid
      which demonstrated the complicated existing systems. Mr. Larsen said they have taken the
      rates and divided them by the costs of the tier one water in the efforts of normalizing the
      rates of all the other tiers. Mr. Larsen said this would allow for comparison with other rates

      including base and maximum use. Mr. Larsen said they will do a cost service analysis on water
      usage. Mr. Larsen said doing this shows how they are charging less for water than the cost
      of production and distribution. Mr. Larsen said the bottom water users are actually providing
      a subsidy for those at the top. Mr. Larsen said they are paying both a base rate and an added

          11.2. Discussion and Consideration of an Ordinance Adopting a Proposed Rate of Tax
                and Levying Taxes Upon All Real and Personal Property Within the City of
                Herriman, Utah – Kyle Maurer, Finance Director
      Finance Director Maurer began by stating the certified tax rate does not include inflation or
      property value increases. Finance Director Maurer explained changes only ensure the city
      receives the same amount of money it received in years past. He referred to a chart which
      noted revenue lost due to inflationary increase, and said the average new growth was about
      a 7-percent increase per year, but acknowledged this was going down as development slows.

      Finance Director Maurer said the certified tax rate is 0.000194, which would generate
      approximately $190,000. Finance Director Maurer said the average tax of the average home
      was approximately $74. Finance Director Maurer offered a brief overview of the budget.
      Finance Director Maurer said they began with an initial deficit of $1.2 million. Finance
      Director Maurer said he would be bringing a fund balance policy by August for the Council
April 26, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                                 Page 16 of 18


      to consider. Finance Director Maurer said they have a City Hall Capital Project fund which
      had ambiguous use guidelines. He added the plan was to close this fund which currently has
      $213,000.

      Finance Director Maurer summarized they had an excess of $50,000, and they were able to
      balance the budget and keep the capital project list intact. Finance Director Maurer said they
      would eliminate any new positions proposed in the 2024 budget which includes the decision
      to not fill an account position and several internships. Finance Director Maurer said they also
      converted a few positions and changed some duties. Finance Director Maurer said they have
      proposed eliminating the economic development director position.

      Mayor Palmer asked if staff had a recommendation on this. City Manager Cherpeski said he
      was not aware of an increase, and the challenge was making significant changes. Finance
      Director Maurer said staff recommendation was to allocate any additional tax revenue to
      Public Safety.

      Councilmember Henderson stressed the need for people to look at their property tax and see
      where it goes. Councilmember Shields added that no increase would yield a significant value.
      Councilmember Shields said inflation is eroding any benefit.
