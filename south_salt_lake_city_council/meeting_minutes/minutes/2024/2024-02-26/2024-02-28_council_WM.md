   3. Council Code of Conduct
      Council Chair Bynum gave a recap of this matter and thanked the Council Members for
      submitting their feedback based on Salt Lake City’s Code of Conduct that was used by the City
      Attorney as a template.

       A few of the changes to the Council Rules include removing the ‘Serious Moment of Reflection’
       and limiting the time frames for City Council Comments to five minutes.

       She asked the Council Members to share any concerns or questions they had over the draft that
       was prepared by the City Attorney and to refer to them by number or section so that everyone
       could follow.

       Council Member Pinkney asked about the ‘Conduct Guidelines’ that goes over how elected
       officials should conduct themselves with colleagues and City staff and if it also applied to Mayor
       Wood.

       Both Council Chair Bynum and City Attorney, Josh Collins, clarified that since this Code of
       Conduct is for the City Council it doesn’t apply to the Mayor as she doesn’t sit on the Council but
       that any conduct guidelines that are in place, or can be looked at for revision, fall under the
       personnel policy for the administrative elected official.

       Council Member Sanchez gave a statement that expressed his disagreement with there being a
       Code of Conduct policy in place. He then addressed the Council Members and said that if this
       Resolution was passed at the Regular Meeting, he would send each one of them into litigation.

       When asked what part of the Code of Conduct he had an issue with, in order to pinpoint what
       could be discussed or clarified, Council Member Sanchez said that he had an issue with all of it.

       Council Chair Bynum then took a straw poll so that the Council could have an idea of where
       everyone stood on the matter.

       Council Members Williams, Mitchell, Huff, Thomas, Pinkney all said that they were in favor of
       the Code of Conduct that would potentially be passed by Resolution at the Regular Meeting.

       Council Member Pinkney said that she doesn’t believe that this Code of Conduct stops her or
       anyone from disagreeing with anyone or anything.

       Council Chair Bynum said that she also stands in favor of this Code of Conduct as there has been
       a lot of threatening behavior on and off social media recently and finds that there is a true need
       for it.
