10.     ADOPT (OR DENY) AN ORDINANCE TO AMEND SECTIONS 2-2 AND 3-25 OF THE UTAH
        COUNTY LAND USE ORDINANCE TO ADD REQUIREMENTS FOR EARTH EXTRACTION
        OPERATIONS RELATED TO AIR AND WATER QUALITY, REQUIRED LANDSCAPING, HOURS
        OF OPERATION, NOISE, AND OTHER RELATED IMPACTS (Continued from the September 5 and
        19, 2017 Commission meetings)
(Track A, 23:05)
Commissioner Ivie pointed out a sentence and requested the words “in Utah County” be added for specificity. Utah
County Deputy Attorney Robert J. Moore reminded the Board this ordinance will only apply to Utah County property.
Commissioner Graves expressed offense at using ‘road destruction’ as an excuse for limiting and condemning an
entire industry, when the farmers who use the same roads do not pay full taxes and therefore do not contribute to the
repairs. He noted agricultural use disturbs the land and can contaminate water as much as mining, and he will be
sponsoring an ordinance to place similar restrictions on farmers.

(Track A, 27:10)
Benjamin resident Parley Hall is a licensed real estate agent and appraiser who is reminded of the current Bears Ears
National Monument situation during this discussion: government protecting the interest of a specific party. Mr. Hall
spoke of a proposed subdivision in West Mountain several years ago, where no data could conclusively prove
residential properties near gravel pits have decreased value. He stated it is banking and lending issues, and not gravel
pits, which have caused the property values to decline in West Mountain. Mr. Hall emphasized there can be ways to
manage dust, reiterating the solution should not be based on emotional unwarranted information regarding property
values. Commissioner Graves agreed with Mr. Hall’s statements.

(Track A, 33:30)
Julie Sainsbury of West Mountain is grateful to the commissioners and for the legislative process. Ms. Sainsbury
explained she has nursing education, and in that field, a care plan is developed by assessing, planning, implementing,
and evaluating. She noted this is similar to the legislative process, explaining nothing changes if all steps are not
followed. Ms. Sainsbury talked about South Utah County Community Voice, and the need for local control. She
pointed out public input can be dismissed as clamor, but what is the purpose of open meetings if not to encourage
feedback? She encouraged the Board to adopt this ordinance.

(Track A, 37:55)
Paul Washburn of Clyde Companies agreed with Julie Sainsbury that this is a complicated issue. He encouraged the
Board to think of long-term impacts, and to recognize there will be ongoing debates in finding solutions. Mr.
Washburn spoke of the necessity of gravel and reiterated it may take months or years to find the right balance. He
warned against creating additional burdens on taxpayers.

(Track A, 41:35)
Benjamin resident Vernal Shepherd referred to a comment made by Commissioner Graves at a previous meeting,
wherein it was mentioned that re-zoning this area for agricultural use will cause an influx of mink farms. Mr. Shepherd
noted since mink operations have been moving in from Lehi, and the Utah County Sheriff’s Office can no longer use
sirens in the area because of the negative impact the noise has on breeding. Since exceptions can be made to
accommodate those farms, similar regulations could be implemented to satisfy orchard operations.

(Track A, 42:50)
Commissioner Graves explained limiting the area to agricultural use would landlock the residents, and prevent future
generations from selling the property. He pointed out Alpine City residents should be offended because the county
consistently advises them to annex, yet the Commission sits here considering an exception to the General Plan.
Commissioner Graves talked about the importance of open meetings: the fruit farmers, residents, and gravel pit
operators have not been brought together on this matter. He stated this is the kind of decision that will raise prices
and change this county negatively in one or two decades – this is about regulating the whole county to address specific
truck drivers. Commissioner Graves stated he understands the West Mountain residents want safer roads, and the
Board can provide that, but not through this action. When he equated this ordinance with restricting grocery store
business hours to coincide with youth being in school, Commissioner Lee countered this amendment does not address
grocery stores. Commissioner Graves stated, ‘But that’s how it’s started – where do we stop?’ Commissioner Ivie
added the legislative process is to work through those concerns.

(Track A, 47:25)
Utah County resident Robert Stevens thanked both Vernal Shepherd and Commissioner Graves for their comments,
calling Mr. Shepherd one of the wisest people he has ever met and stating Commissioner Graves is also right: “the
more we regulate, the more we discount free market principles.” Mr. Stevens pointed out everyone wants to use
government as their personal force but they do not consider the consequences; he related this example to farmland
being taxed at a lower rate but mutually being valued lower.

(Track A, 50:30)
Commissioner Ivie expressed the fundamental role of government is to protect life, liberty, and property. People
should be able to do what they want with their property unless it is causing harm to another individual; government
has a responsibility to set reasonable standards. In this case, increased activity has created conflict and caused damage
to a valuable industry in the county. Commissioner Ivie reiterated the need for balance and talked about his
experiences in addressing this issue, including presentations, gravel pit visits, and meetings with mine owners. He
noted the original draft of the ordinance was amended and revised to protect both sides of the argument. Commissioner
Ivie explained the ordinance can be instituted and then evaluated; working through it might provide opportunities to
improve the process.

(Track A, 53:15)
Commissioner Graves stated it is a problem to think owning property is a right. That is a federal government mentality,
like designating health care as a right – Commissioner Graves explained both are a privilege. He talked about the
Founding Fathers establishing God-given inalienable rights: they believed in electing God-fearing men to enact the
will of the people. Commissioner Graves reminded the Board it is important to consider the impact on all county
residents when adopting an ordinance, and not just the immediate community. He pointed out taking away any
person’s right, including corporations, is equal to taking away their freedom.

Commissioner Lee asked who grants the privilege of owning property, and he and Commissioner Graves debated
rights versus privileges. Commissioner Lee talked about responsibility to protect and be considerate of all property
owners, and the proper government role to create guidelines and plans. He reviewed the wording of the proposed
ordinance and Commissioner Graves reiterated his earlier points regarding government regulations, addressing safety,
and infringing upon a specific industry.
