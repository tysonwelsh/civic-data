Public Comment (00:03:47) Acting Chair Jill Spencer opened the meeting to the public. Brent Hall, representing the neighborhood group in the area of 820 North in Provo, expressed concerns about a proposed five-lane road project that would go through their neighborhood. He stated that they have been trying to convince people for the past 20 years that this project would be detrimental to their neighborhood and would rip it apart. He further emphasized that the neighborhood does not want this five-lane road and has repeatedly voiced their opposition during public hearings. He pleaded with the committee for help, noting that the neighborhood needs assistance and that they are friendly but truly do not want this road project to go through their community. Minutes - Action (00:05:48) Jason Bond moved to approve the minutes from October 28, 2024. Richard Nielsen seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Vice Chair Nomination (00:07:04) There were three names suggested for the Vice Chair position; Richard Nielson, Ben Hunter, and Michael Florence. After a brief discussion Richard Nielson withdrew his name as a nominee. There was a vote between Ben Hunter and Michael Florence. Ben Hunter received the majority of the votes. Brad Kenison nominated Ben Hunter to be the vice chair of the MPO TAC committee, knowing that the vice chair will rotate into the chair position in two years. Vern Keeslar seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Page 2 of 5 Congestion Management Process Adoption (00:29:40) Calvin Clark presented the proposed Congestion Management Process (CMP) by MAG. He outlined that the CMP is required to meet federal regulations and includes goals such as reducing congestion, improving mobility, enhancing transportation options, improving air quality, and enhancing safety. Performance measures in the CMP focus on congestion, travel time reliability, transit, active transportation, and safety. Key highlights included: ● Analysis of travel demand reduction and operational management strategies before capacity expansion. ● Integration with MAG's other planning processes, such as the Regional Transportation Plan (RTP). Vern Keeslar moved to recommend that the MPO Board approve the proposed CMP. Noah Gordon seconded the motion, and the motion passed unanimously. Corridor Preservation, Provo 820 North (00:42:15) Calvin Clark presented a request from Provo City to use $675,575 from the Corridor Preservation Fund to acquire a property on Camelot Drive backing 820 North. This acquisition is part of an ongoing corridor preservation project in Provo. Brent Hall, a local resident, expressed concerns about the project's potential negative impacts on the community. Brad Kenison moved to recommend that the MPO Board approve Provo City's request for $675,575 from the Corridor Preservation Fund, confirming its adherence to the fund's requirements. Chris Trusty seconded the motion, and the motion passed unanimously. Corridor Preservation, Mountain View Corridor, Saratoga Springs (00:55:27) Jason Bond moved to continue Saratoga Springs’ request for $970,000 from the Corridor Preservation Fund until additional information is provided to MAG staff. Vern Keeslar seconded the motion, and the motion passed unanimously. RTP Amendments 2 / AQ Conformity Determination (00:56:02) Shawn Eliot presented the second amendment to the RTP, which included changes affecting 10 projects: Level 1 Amendment ● A88 Highline Canal: Moved from Phase 2 to Phase 1. Level 3 Amendments ● H31 Cory Wride Freeway (west): New routes added, Phase 2 moved to Phase 1, and increased costs. Page 3 of 5 ● Cory Wride Freeway and Cedar Valley Freeway: Area network changes, projects shifted to Phases 2 and 3, cost increases, and additional legislative funding. ● H114 Geneva Road Widening: New project added to Phase 1, including additional lanes and STIP funding. ● H115 I-15 Freeway Passing Lanes: New project added with STIP funding. Ben Hunter moved to recommend that the MPO Board approve TransPlan50 Amendment 2 and the associated Air Quality Conformity Determination Report, subject to any significant comments during the public comment period. Richard Nielsen seconded the motion, and the motion passed unanimously. TIP Modifications (01:09:20) Bob Allen outlined the changes related to the RTP Amendment 2 recommendations stating the Amendments would need to be adopted and reflected in the TIP. Jason Bond moved to recommend that the Cory Wride Freeway, I-15 Passing Lanes, and Geneva Road projects be added to the TIP, confirming their alignment with Phase 1 of the fiscally constrained and air quality-conforming RTP. Eric Ellis seconded the motion, and the motion passed unanimously. Wasatch Choice Vision Goals and Technical Process (01:12:03) Dan Wayne and Ryan Beck from Envision Utah presented the technical process for updating the Wasatch Choice Vision (WCV). The update includes: ● Developing a single land use vision through two phases: gathering local plans and extending the planning horizon to 2055. ● Collaboration with the WCV Work Group, economic development partners, and MPO TAC members. Refinements include: ● Identifying and facilitating interagency projects. ● Balancing all four WCV strategies. Brad Kenison moved to recommend that the MPO Board approve the WCV Update technical approach (Attachment A) with Work Group refinements and continue existing WCV goals and strategies (Attachment B). Ben Hunter seconded the motion, and the motion passed unanimously. RTP 2025 Work Plan (01:24:02) Shawn Eliot provided an overview of the 2025 work plan for the RTP. Key elements include: 1. Transparent project selection process. 2. Reviewing plan goals in alignment with the Wasatch Choice Vision update. 3. Collecting land use data. Page 4 of 5 4. Travel Demand Model Version 9.1 (01:34:02) Jared Lillywhite provided updates on the joint travel demand model developed by MAG and WFRC. Notable updates include: ● Incorporation of RTP Amendment #2 network changes. ● Addition of a Provo Airport trip table. ● Refinement of the Salt Lake Airport as a trip generator. ● Visualization tool (VizTool) for improved accessibility of model outputs. Concerns were raised about inconsistencies between the VizTool and the 2023 RTP. Jared Lillywhite and Shawn Eliot requested detailed feedback for further investigation.
