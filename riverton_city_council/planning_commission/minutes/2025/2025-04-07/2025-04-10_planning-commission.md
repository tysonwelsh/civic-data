posted to the State website as required. Although affected property owners received
notices, failure to post on the State website could result in challenges to the Commission’s
decision. It was recommended that this item also be continued. He provided background
on the application for the Commission’s consideration.

The property’s zoning was established in 2000 as the Western Springs Specific Plan, SP-
Commercial. The developer was granted density for apartments, cluster homes, and both
small and large lot development. A five-acre portion of the property was also designated
commercial, and Big-O Tires was built in 2014. The application was for a Master Plan for
the remaining 3.8 acres of commercial property. Mr. Prestwich noted that there is a park
area in the zone, some of which is owned by the City and some was traded to the Jordan

Herriman, which will require the developer to install an island to prevent left turns into the

paths, two right-turn accesses to 12600 South, and full access to 4570 West. The developer

previously applied to rezone the property to Commercial Gateway (“C-G”). At the City
Council meeting, City Attorney, Ryan Carter suggested that they amend the Western
Springs Development Agreement rather than changing the Zoning Map.

Commissioner Cluff moved that the Planning Commission Continue Application PLZ-
24-8021, Commercial Site Plan and Conditional Use Permit for “Western Springs
Commercial Master Site Plan” to the April 24, 2025 Planning Commission meeting.

3.     DECISION ITEMS

       A.     “ARETE EVENTS CENTER”, PLZ-24-8008, AN AMENDED COMMERCIAL
              SITE PLAN TO ALLOW AN EVENTS CENTER IN REMODELED PET
              STORE AT 11968 SOUTH REDWOOD ROAD. APPLICANT – JOSEPH
              WHITE.

Mr. Prestwich presented the Staff Report and indicated that the Commission previously
considered an application to rezone the subject property from Commercial Neighborhood
(“C-N”) to C-G. The current application was the final step in the repurposing of that building.

An aerial view of the property was presented, indicating that it is a dual-purpose building
with both a dance studio and the proposed Arete Events Center. The amended Site Plan
was necessary due to minor exterior changes, including a lighted arbor over the entrance to
the reception center. Staff recommended approval of the application as presented.

Commissioner Rushton asked why this item did not require public notice as it is a different
use and impact from the dance studio. Mr. Prestwich clarified that the dance studio will
remain. The events center will be in the section previously occupied by Pet Planet. Site

Plan Amendments are evaluated by Staff to determine if the changes are substantial enough
to require notice. Slight changes to the exterior of a building like those in the current
application are posted on the City website, but no mailers are sent.

Commissioner Rushton expressed concern that the business use is changing from a
daytime use with a certain traffic volume to one that includes nighttime events with parking,
noise, lighting, etc. Mr. Prestwich clarified the Table of Uses change was posted on the City
website, and public notices for the rezoning request were sent to property owners within
1,000 feet of the subject property. He noted that the changes to the awning were made in
the Fall of 2024, and no complaints had been received since that time.

In response to a follow-up question from Commissioner Rushton, Mr. Prestwich stated that
the original Site Plan was approved when the building was initially constructed. If the
Planning Commission desired that all amended Site Plans be noticed for public hearings,
the Ordinance would need to be changed to indicate that requirement.

Regarding the Use, if the property owner applied for a Conditional Use with no architectural
changes to the site, that would only require Staff approval. City Code dictates that if an
existing commercial site functions essentially the same with a different use, the property
owner is not burdened with public noticing and hearing requirements. In those instances,
the application is discussed with Development Services Director, Jason Lethbridge.
Regarding the site, any changes to the exterior of a building, parking lot, or landscaping
require Planning Commission approval.

Commissioner Rushton asked if the C-G Zone includes restrictions on hours of operation.
Mr. Prestwich confirmed that the hours are restricted.

Chair Park asked if something like the installation of parking lot lighting would come back
before the Planning Commission due to potentially adverse impacts on surrounding
properties. Mr. Prestwich stated that if they were simply replacing existing fixtures, it would
not be brought back before the Commission. However, if they wanted to install new fixtures
in the rear of the property near the residential area, Planning Commission approval would
be required.

Commissioner Cluff stated that the minutes of the City Council Meeting in which the rezoning
was approved specify that the proposed change was to rezone the property to Commercial
Gateway, which allows for a broader range of Uses, including reception centers, a Use that
is not permitted in the current zone but Conditional in the C-G Zone. Mr. Prestwich confirmed
that it is a Conditional Use in the C-G Zone.

Commissioner Cluff asked if the Planning Commission would be able to rule on the matter
before them if the Use required additional Commission approval. Mr. Prestwich confirmed
that they could rule on the amended Site Plan, and the Conditional Use could come back
before them if Mr. Lethbridge determined that it was necessary.

Commissioner Rushton stated that he visited the site and the rear parking lot is not lighted.
If they have an event at night, they will need lighting. Commissioner Cluff noted that the site
would have to comply with lighting regulations. Mr. Prestwich confirmed that a Site Plan
Amendment application would be required for additional lighting.

Commissioner Rushton stated that he would like to know the hours of operation for the
business. He asked if retail is a conditional or permitted Use in the C-G Zone. Mr. Prestwich
clarified that the matter before the Planning Commission was an amended Site Plan. They
could continue the matter for Staff to determine if it should have been noticed for a
Conditional Use, or they could act on the Site Plan and the Conditional Use could be
addressed separately.

Commissioner Rushton referred to City Code § 18.215.020 and asked if the site was a Class
A development. Mr. Prestwich stated that he did not know. Regarding § 18.215.050(3), he
confirmed that the item was publicly noticed on the City website as required for amended
Site Plans. Only Final Site Plans require written notice to nearby property owners.

Chair Park stated that the Site Plan covers architectural and structural specifications, not
Use. Their discussion was focused on whether the Conditional Use should be noticed due
to its potential impact on the surrounding neighborhood. Commissioner Rushton stated that
the subject property was rezoned to a zone that allows Uses that are very different from the
previous one. He believes it would be reasonable to send notice to the neighboring property
owners that it will now be an events center, and they need a full understanding of things like
the hours of operation.

Chair Park asked if the neighbors would have recourse regarding the Use. Mr. Prestwich
reported that a Conditional Use can only be denied if it creates a negative impact above and
beyond that of the allowed Uses in the Zone and reasonable conditions cannot be placed
on it to mitigate that impact. The Conditional Use of this site was approved by the Planning
Director.

In response to a question from Commissioner Park, Mr. Prestwich stated that he did not
know if the public notice for the rezoning request stated that the property would be used as
an events center. The events center had been in operation for a few months before Staff
became aware of the changes, and it is still in operation. He believes the Noise Ordinance
applies to both residential and commercial properties, but he is unsure of any specified hours
of operation.

Chair Park stated that the amended Site Plan was not related to the Conditional Use, which
had been approved by Staff. As it was approved, the Planning Commission could not take
action on Commissioner Rushton’s concerns regarding the Use. Mr. Prestwich reported that
if the Planning Commission believed reasonable concerns had not been addressed, they
should table the matter so it could be noticed and brought back in a public hearing. They
could then consider both issues at the same meeting.

In response to a question from Commissioner Rushton, Mr. Prestwich clarified that City
Code specifies restrictions for Home Occupations, and those are often listed in motions for
the applicant’s benefit. For commercial Conditional Uses, they can only consider impacts
beyond any normally anticipated impacts.

Chair Park asked that Staff provide the Commission with any Conditions of Approval placed
on the Conditional Use Permit that was granted to this property. Mr. Prestwich clarified that
no specific conditions were placed on the property because it was deemed to have
essentially the same impacts as the previous Use. If Staff had determined that additional
conditions were warranted, the matter would have been brought to the Planning Commission
for consideration. If the Planning Commission disagreed with Mr. Lethbridge’s decision,
they could publicly notice the matter and bring it back before the Commission for discussion,
either with or without the amended Site Plan.

Commissioner Rushton stated that Staff had made the determination, so it was outside the
Commission’s scope and Staff could address any concerns that come up. Mr. Prestwich
reported that if Code Enforcement issues arise, they can be brought back to the Planning
Commission at a later date.

             CONVENIENCE STORE TO BE LOCATED AT 4076 WEST 13400 SOUTH
             IN THE SLR ZONE.

Mr. Prestwich presented the Staff Report and indicated that the subject property is located
in the SLR Zone, which specifies that public notice and a public comment period are required
for the Community Scale Plan, Block Scale Plan, or a combined Community and Block Scale
Plan, but the Detail Site Plan is a decision item.

The Vicinity Map was displayed. The 7-Eleven will be located on the corner of 13400 South
and Old Liberty Way. Several businesses in the area were previously approved and will be
opening soon including Wingstop, Quick Quack Car Wash, Dutch Bros, Apollo Burger,
Raising Cane’s, and Freddy’s. The final inspection for 7 Brew Coffee was conducted earlier
that day. Two other sites are pending construction.

As a side note, Mr. Prestwich reported that westbound access from Bangerter Highway to
13400 South will be restored soon. Construction will then move to the east side of Bangerter
Highway, and a portion of 13400 South will be under construction during the summer.

Mr. Prestwich reported that Land Reserve, formerly SLR, had approved the Detail Plan for
the site, which is located on the last remaining pad in Village West. There are two vacant
pads in Village East. He noted that one Condition of Approval was changed from the staff
report to indicate that the storm pond is now operational.

Architectural drawings and landscaping plans were reviewed. There will be canopies along
the front and sides of the building. The canopy and dumpster enclosures meet all SLR Zone
requirements. Access will be via right turn only from 13400 South and a private access drive
at the rear of the development. There will be no direct exits onto 4050 West or Old Liberty
Way. Due to an eight-foot difference in grade between the building and parking area, a
switchback ramp will be installed. Stormwater from the rear storm drain will be held in
underground tanks. Waterwise landscaping will be installed that meets all standards for
area and trees, and all park strips will be maintained by the property owner.

Mr. Prestwich reported that all relevant City departments reviewed the Detail Plan and
recommended approval.

In response to a question from Commissioner Matheson, Mr. Prestwich confirmed that
access from 13400 South will be via right-in only. Signage will be installed indicating that
vehicles cannot exit onto 13400 South.

Chair Park stated that the angled access near Lowe’s is extremely effective due to its angle
and expressed concern that this access will have a very shallow angle. The Apollo Burger
drive-thru ends near that access and there is a double-lane approach, which will invite
people to use it as an exit.

In response to a follow-up question from Commissioner Matheson, Mr. Prestwich clarified
that traffic onto 13400 South is regulated by the City, not Suburban Land Reserve, and as
such the Planning Commission could include a Condition of Approval that the drive approach
be narrowed. He did not know if it would be practical to increase the angle. They could also

defer to the City Engineer, who has already reviewed the Detail Plan, or ask the Applicant
to remedy the issue.

Commissioner Rushton agreed that the existing right-in is very effective because it has a
narrow approach and a dramatic angle, and expressed concern that the access into 7-
Eleven will be a potential collision point. He asked if there would be additional accesses for
the other businesses in the development. Mr. Prestwich reviewed the accesses on the
Master Site Plan and noted that the intention is for left-turn traffic to exit via 4130 West.

Commissioner Cluff stated that there will be a dedicated turn lane and traffic will be slower
in that area, so it should be less problematic if people do try to turn right onto 13400 South.

The Applicant’s representative, Rick Magnus of AWA Engineering, expressed his
appreciation for Riverton City Staff and stated that he understood the Planning
Commission’s concerns about the right-in access from 13400 South. AWA Engineering
worked with both City Staff and a traffic engineer at the ingress point. He noted that Apollo
Burger will have signage clearly indicating that cars must turn left to exit, and they will install
additional signage if the need arises. He clarified that the approach is wider to accommodate
fuel trucks entering and exiting the 7-Eleven.

Mr. Magnus asked that the Planning Commission consider conditioning approval on the
Applicant working with Staff to identify how narrow the approach can be while still allowing
for fuel truck circulation. He did not believe they could meet the width and angle of the
referenced access, but they would review the plan and address the concern with Staff. They
also concurred with all Staff recommendations.

Commissioner Rushton asked if there would be enough room for the fuel trucks to access
the site via the road to the north. Mr. Magnus stated that the option was considered but was
not feasible and asked that they be allowed to work with Staff on the matter. If necessary,
he could provide a directional template for the access. There is a good throat depth on the
access adjacent to Apollo Burger, but the approach is necessary to accommodate the fuel
trucks’ turning radius.

In response to a question from Chair Park, Mr. Magnus stated that they may be able to
further angle the initial approach, and committed to angling and narrowing the access as
much as possible.

Commissioner Rushton noted that Maverik is directly across from the subject property, and
it would be reasonable to look at their access. Mr. Prestwich reported that the drive
approach for Maverik is approximately 42 feet wide. Mr. Magnus stated that the 7-Eleven
access is 30 to 35 feet wide. Commissioner Rushton asked if it would behoove the
Commission to consider accepting that people will try to exit from the right-in access and
simply widen it to allow vehicles to exit as well. Chair Park indicated that the access is from
a right-turn-only lane, and cars will be entering that lane to turn onto 4130 West. If other
vehicles are trying to exit and merge into through traffic on 13400 South, the resulting cross
