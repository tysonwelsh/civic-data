3. Consideration of proposed public art for BRT stations. (Attachment 3)

  Ms. Christy McBride indicated Ogden City Arts has partnered with UTA, Weber State University,
  and IHC who have approved an enhancement plan for the stations to service the Bus Rapid
  Transit route. The proposal is for artwork to be laminated between glass fixtures, with each to
  be designed to fit the specific location. The artwork will be 25% transparent and will extend to
  fill all panels at each location. It is anticipated artists will be selected and have the ability to
  research the history and identity of each particular area these stations will serve. She
  identified the location and sizes of proposed stations.

  Mr. Montgomery stated the Commission is charged to determine whether the proposed
  artwork is consistent with the General Plan and is an appropriate use of the right-of-way
  without impacting future development. He stated as each location will have their own design,
  it creates an identity for the surrounding community. He stated three stations are in the
  center of the street, with the remaining to be on the sides of the right-of-way. He stated these
  stations will not affect existing facilities or utilities or with vehicular or pedestrian routes. Staff
  recommendation is for approval.

  Commissioner Schade asked if the local citizens would be allowed to have input relating to art
  for their specific community. Ms. McBride stated it is anticipated artists will reach out to the
  surrounding community as well as local historians to attain information and stories of the
  particular area before designing their artwork.

  MOTION:      A motion was made by Commissioner Sandau to recommend approval of the
               proposed public art for the wind screens for each of the BRT locations based on
               the findings the projects are consistent with the General Plan and are located in
               areas that have sufficient space for development of the projects and do not
               interfere with any future planned development areas. Motion was seconded by
               Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously with Commissioners Blaisdell,
               Safsten, Sandau, Schade, Stoker and Graf voting aye

6. Special USE Permit to allow Change of Nonconforming Use, for two apartments at 795 24th
   Street. (Attachment 6)

  Ms. Sue Wilkerson stated she owns the historic Scowcroft Mansion at 795 24th Street. The
  structure was initially built as a single-family home with a finished basement but was later
  converted to become nine apartments. In 1985 it was purchased and renovated by Paul
  Stockdale and converted to office space, retaining two residential units in the basement. Due
  to the location of wiring and utilities, these spaces became unusable and has since become a
  storage area for the office use. She is requesting approval to create two residential units in
  the basement while retaining office space on the upper levels. No walls will be moved and
  each of the units already have a separate outside entrance which will remain. There are 17
  existing parking spaces to service the building. She indicated the Landmarks Commission had
  previously approved replacement of metal windows and removal of bars which will make the
  basement space more usable. She identified areas which will continue to be used as storage


                                                                                    Page 8 of 16
  as well as service items such as modems and wiring which will be relocated. A common
  laundry will be installed to service the two units.

  Ms. Corbridge explained this is to be reviewed as a special use permit as the use desired is
  both office and multi-family in the R-3EC zone and the office use is nonconforming but the
  residential uses have been abandoned. It is eligible for this review because it is a historic
  building of specific influence to the community. The review by the Commission is to
  determine it complies with zoning regulations and General Plan, and is compatible with
  surrounding land uses. The property has sufficient lot area to accommodate the added use of
  two apartments, and adequate parking is available on site. The preservation of historic
  buildings is supported by the General Plan and the use is compatible with proposed mixed use
  projects in the area. Staff recommends approval subject to the owner obtaining necessary
  building permits for the interior remodeling.

  Commissioner Schade noted an outdoor entry does not now have sidewalk and asked if
  sidewalk will be added. Ms. Wilkerson indicated she intends to install sidewalk to service all
  entryways. Commissioner Blaisdell asked if the building is ADA accessible. Ms. Corbridge
  stated while the Building Code may require an ADA parking space, she is not aware as to
  whether the building will be required to meet these but the use of stairs is currently required
  for access to all levels.

  MOTION:     A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to approve the special use to allow
              two additional dwelling units based on the findings the proposed building is on the
              local historic register, the use as a duplex complies with the zoning ordinance and
              is compatible with surrounding land uses. Approval is subject to building permits
              being obtained for interior remodeling work. Motion was seconded by
              Commissioner Sandau and passed unanimously with Commissioners Blaisdell,
              Safsten, Sandau, Schade and Graf voting aye. Commissioner Stoker was not
              available at the time of the vote.

10. Continuation of Public Hearing, to consider amending ordinance to allow painted brick in the
  East Central Community. (Attachment 10)

  Mr. Brett Rogers stated he had painted a brick home and upon receiving notice from Code
  Enforcement, had tried to remove the paint but the test patch indicated the removal of the
  paint would damage the brick. He desires the Ordinance to be amended so the existing paint
  can remain on the home.

  Mr. Montgomery stated the request on the petition is to amend the code so that painting on
  brick can be approved if the brick is damage, defaced or deteriorating. The removal method
  shown by the applicant indicated the glazing of the brick was being removed along with the
  paint and he has petitioned the ordinance be revised to allow the existing paint to remain. In
  considering the petition, the Commission is to determine whether the request is consistent
  with the General Plan and the East Central Community Plan, whether conditions have changed
  or whether there are situations where painting is appropriate, and whether the historic
  character of the community

  He stated the Commission had reviewed the petition in July and has since held a work session
  to discuss the effects of the proposed amendment. The Central Bench Historic District was
  created in 2005 as National Historic Districts to provide tax credit incentives for the
  rehabilitation of structures in the area as well as to preserve the character of the historic
  community. In 2009-2010, the East Central Community Plan was updated at which time
  residents had expressed concern that the historic integrity of the community needs to be
  preserved and several goals and policies were adopted. Then in 2010, the Zoning Ordinance
  was amended to regulate specific items in the East Central community, including the
  prohibition of covering existing brick with any material or paint. He noted people might paint
  structures for several reasons, including to change the color, to cover flaws and to preserve
  the brick. The preservation of existing brick structures in the East Central Community is
  important as the exterior building material shows the character of the area. Painting removes
  the contrast of textures between mortar and brick, can speed up the deterioration of brick,
  adds maintenance for future owners to repaint or touch up as paint peals and cracks over
  time. There is additional concern that if buildings were painted since the district was
  established, its right for historic tax credits are forfeited.


                                                                                 Page 13 of 16
He reviewed existing study areas within the East Central community where it appears
approximately 25% of brick homes had been painted prior to the establishment of the district
and are considered legal, and new owners may notice these and assume the painting of brick
is acceptable. Reviewing comments from the July meeting, Mr. Montgomery stated concern
had been noted that permits are not required for exterior painting and the web site does not
clarify painting brick is prohibited. Removal of paint can damage brick and is costly, yet some
have already removed paint at a personal expense and there is concern that all are treated
fairly. Efforts need to be made to improve awareness of the regulation. At that time, the
Commission felt additional information is needed and tabled action, followed by a work
session which was held August 18th. Commission discussed the historic character of the
East Central community and determined it is important to be preserved and the ordinance
regulations are appropriate but felt efforts could be made to better notify property owners.
They also discussed the ability to remove paint from brick and the cost involved as well as
whether there may be conditions where painting is appropriate to preserve existing brick.

Based on information from both previous meetings, Staff is suggesting several solutions,
including the following:
    • Changes to the website have been made to improve communications, identifying
        unique requirements of the East Central Community.
    • Additional noticing be provided to include information on property titles identifying
        conditions, recording noncomplying certificates for structures which were painted
        prior to the district being created, recording illegal certificates on properties in violation
        and continue the citation process for newly painted brick.
    • Existing regulations are to be retained as the Central Bench District is important to
        preserve and architecture is more important
    • Language proposed to be added that the Planning Commission may approve painting
        of brick as a last resort if it is found to be required to provide a protective surface and
        limit erosion so long as the paint will match the existing brick color
    • Amend other chapters in the East Central Community so application of the regulation
        is uniform, including the R-4, NC and PI chapters
Staff recommends these items be approved in response to the petition and that the petition to
amend the Ordinance be denied.

Commissioner Blaisdell asked about clear coat painting, and whether it would be allowed. Mr.
