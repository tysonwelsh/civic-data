39          Sycamore Glen Pond Discussion
40
41   Matt Cassel, City Engineer, presented three alternatives for the Sycamore Glen detention pond,
42   also known as Walker Pond, seeking direction from the Council. Alternative one, the "Do
43   Nothing" option, involves minimal intervention, but poses the risk of overflow into the
44   neighborhood during storm events due to reduced capacity. Alternative two entails maintaining
45   the existing design, which lacks landscaping and aesthetics, while still posing overflow risks.
46   Alternative three, the recommended option, involves utilizing the north half of the existing pond,
47   deepening it, and landscaping to enhance aesthetics. This alternative closely aligns with the
48   original design, offering improved capacity and reduced overflow risk.
49
50   Mr. Cassel emphasized the need to address concerns raised by neighbors and outlined the
51   budget estimate for implementing the recommended alternative. The Council was invited to

 3   Councilmember Buroker highlighted historical concerns raised by residents during the initial
 4   development phase of the area around the detention pond. Residents expressed concerns
 5   about preserving the natural landscape and wildlife habitat, including a stream and deer
 6   population, in the area before the development. She emphasized the importance of considering
 7   residents' perspectives and reaching out to them to gather feedback on the proposed changes
 8   to the pond.
 9
10   Mayor Staggs inquired whether the plan includes plans for a buildable lot or if it is intended as
11   open space to be maintained by the Toll Brothers community. In response, Mr. Cassel said
12   there is potential for the front piece of the area to be utilized as a benefit to the community and
13   expressed a need for further consideration regarding its management, including the possibility
14   of fencing for safety reasons.
15
16   Councilmember Pierucci expressed a preference for not putting up a fence around the pond if
17   efforts are made to beautify the property. He suggested engaging the community to gather
18   feedback and help them understand the necessity of the project. Additionally, he expressed
19   support for option three but emphasized the importance of considering the costs and comparing
20   them to the initial budget investments.
21
22   Councilmember McDougal asked about vegetation surrounding the pond. Mr. Cassel said it will
23   be low-lying vegetation and could incorporate a meandering stream-like feature.
24   Councilmember McDougal highlighted the importance of considering the durability of vegetation
25   around the pond, citing an example with Midas Creek where flooding caused damage to grass
26   and landscaping. He emphasized the need to factor in the costs of potential replacement due to
27   occasional flooding to avoid ongoing expenses.
28
29   Councilmember McCay expressed a preference for not having a fence around the pond and
30   suggested instead putting up signs to remind people to watch their children. She also mentioned
31   the possibility of creating a path around the pond, which aligns with requests from residents for
32   more paths in the City. She expressed support for option three but suggested further discussion
33   and site visitation before making a final decision.
34
35   Mayor Staggs discussed potential traffic improvements along 12600 South between Bangerter
36   Highway and Mountain View Corridor. The proposed plan includes expanding the road to three
37   lanes from Bangerter Highway to 4150 West to alleviate traffic congestion during peak hours.
38   There was also mention of seeking funding through the transportation omnibus bill to further
39   address traffic issues in the area.

 7            Ordinance No. 24-01 – Adopting Section 17.35, Review and Approval Process in
 8            Compliance with Utah State Code, regulating processing, review and approval of
 9            subdivisions in compliance with adopted Utah State Code, Ordinance proposed
10            by Riverton City
11
12   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, discussed an amendment to the subdivision
13   ordinance, which aligns with changes in the Utah State Code mandating a standardized process
14   for reviewing residential subdivisions. The changes include setting maximum review cycles for
15   staff, limiting the time for each review cycle, and specifying the land use authority (Planning
16   Commission in Riverton's case). The ordinance also outlines the preliminary and final approval
17   processes for residential developments. While the adoption brings the City's code in line with
18   state requirements, there may be additional changes pending based on ongoing legislative
19   sessions. The recommended motion was for the adoption of the revised subdivision ordinance
20   as dictated by Utah State Code.
21
22   Councilmember McDougal asked if the only decision making power the City Council would have
23   is the original development decision or preliminary zoning. Mr. Lethbridge said this was correct.
24   The Council's involvement in development decisions would primarily pertain to zoning changes,
25   while the Planning Commission and City staff would handle the review and approval process for
26   subdivisions according to the standards set forth in the ordinance. The Council would still have
27   authority over any changes to the code itself, but the day-to-day approval process for
28   development packages would be managed by the Planning Commission and staff.
29
30   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained that according to the development agreement act, any
31   amendments to land use within a development agreement would require approval from Riverton
32   City through the same administrative or legislative process as other changes. He clarified that
33   this process would involve a subdivision process and a public hearing before both the Planning
34   Commission and the Council prior to execution.
35
36   Mr. Carter said this legislation is not cause for celebration. He believes it doesn't significantly
37   benefit the Council's interactions with developers. He highlighted that the legislation affects the
38   staff's ability to address various issues that arise during land development processes, such as
39   ensuring community safety and welfare. However, he clarified that the legislation doesn't alter
40   the Council's handling of matters when brought before them appropriately.
41
42   Councilmember McDougal expressed frustration with the state's involvement in local-level
43   decisions, particularly regarding land development. He emphasized the importance of decisions
44   being made by those closest to the affected residents, and expressed disappointment that
45   current regulations appear to favor developers over residents.
46
47   Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.
48

 8            Resolution No. 24-19 – Conveying Real Property from Riverton City to the Riverton
 9            Redevelopment Agency
10
11   Mr. Carter provided an overview of a transaction involving the Council’s adopting a resolution in
12   April 2022 which established the Community Reinvestment project area for Redwood Road. He
13   explained that Riverton City currently holds title to certain properties outlined in red in the
14   designated area. To facilitate development in the region, he proposed transferring title of the
15   properties to the Redevelopment Agency (RDA). This transfer would enable the RDA to better
16   collaborate with the development community and potentially initiate development projects. If no
17   project materializes before the Community Reinvestment project area expires, the title can
18   revert to Riverton City.
19

25   motion. Mayor Staggs called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: Buroker-yes,
26   Haymond-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes, and Pierucci-yes. The motion passed unanimously.
