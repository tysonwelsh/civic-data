 1.           A presentation of the Giving Machine donations to local non-profit organizations. (23-011)
              (9:00)

Presented by Karen Ellingson, Utah County Communications Council for the Church of Jesus Christ of
Latter-Day Saints. Ms. Ellingson introduced other members of the Communications Council.

Carl Cheney, of the Missionary Department of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints, said his
primary responsibility is to manage the worldwide efforts of the ‘Light the World’ giving machines
initiative. He shared that worldwide there were approximately 425,000 people who purchased goods
from a giving machine. There were approximately 75,000 people in Orem who donated and that 38,000
items were purchased. He explained the items purchased included meals, hygiene kits, clothing, school
supplies, etc. He presented plaques to Bill Hulterstrom from the United Way of Utah County, Tom Hogan
from Community Action Services and Food Bank, and Brent Crane from Provo Food and Care Coalition.

Chair MacKay thanked Carl for the presentation and extended her gratitude to members of the
community who donated.

 3.           An ordinance amending Provo City code regarding standards for conditional uses- dance
              halls. Citywide Application. (PLOTA20220404) (20:17)

        Motion:       An implied motion to approve an Ordinance, as currently constituted, has been made
                      by council rule.

Aaron Ardmore, City Planner, presented. He discussed that the property owner applied for a business
license for an event center and dance hall but were denied the license. After working with the
Development Services staff on different options, the owner decided to apply for the text amendment.
Mr. Ardmore explained that the text amendment would apply to more than just the property applying
for the change. He then discussed the proposed amendments and the staff concerns associated with the
request.

Councilor Ellsworth asked how frequently the city gets complaints from or about dance halls.

Mr. Ardmore responded that there is another event center that has caused frequent police calls,
littering, and nuisance with noise complaints for the last few months. He said the city is trying to figure
out strategies to combat and resolve the issues. He added that while not all are problematic, the
potential to be is there.

Councilor Shipley asked for the rationale for the expansion of the boundary by a few hundred feet and
what it would accomplish.

Mr. Ardmore said he believes it is due to noise and parking concerns.

Councilor Whipple asked why the outermost corners were included in the border. She said by doing so,
it has a larger impact on homes.

Councilor Handley asked for clarification between a dance hall and a dance studio.

Councilor Fillmore asked about the vote from the planning commission and asked for the reasoning
behind their vote.

Mr. Ardmore said the vote was five to two votes against. He said the reasons included best use of
property for this area and the adverse impacts on the neighborhoods of residential properties nearby.

Vice-Chair Whipple asked if there was feedback provided from the neighborhood chair.

Mr. Ardmore said there was not any feedback given but notice was sent. He added that it was sent
around the restructuring of the neighborhood program so that could be a reason why feedback was not
provided.

Chair MacKay invited the applicant to share.

David Kang discussed the plans for property and shared it would be used for ballroom dance lessons and
competitions.

Councilor Shipley asked if the applicant recommended the change of 200 to 100 ft barrier.

Councilor Fillmore asked if the applicant had explored other options besides a dance hall given the
prime location of the property.

Mr. Kang explained they are open to other options. He expressed his desire to help build the future of
this area, especially once something is done with the property of the old city center, but as a small
business owner, it makes the most sense to start with something that the building could be used for as it
currently stands. He said they have owned the property for eighteen months and they have obligations
to make payments on the building.

Sean Kang discussed the process of how they got to this point and the difficulties they have faced along
the way.

Councilor Handley asked Mr. Ardmore why it is not considered a dance studio instead of dance hall.

Mr. Ardmore said the applicant would be using the space as a dance studio during the day and as an
event center on nights and weekends.

Councilor Whipple asked if it would be permitted for the applicant to use the space for dance lessons.
After a nod from Mr. Ardmore, she asked if the applicant could use the space for dance lessons with
competitions. Again, Mr. Ardmore nodded yes. She determined the text amendment request was only
for the use as an event space.

Councilor Ellsworth asked if there are other event spaces downtown. She then asked for some examples
of some others for comparison.

Chair MacKay stated there is The White Shanty and The Blake.

Councilor Ellsworth asked for others along Center Street.

 4.           A request for approval of a Zone Map Amendment from the ITOD zone to the DT1 (General
              Downtown) zone in order to construct a new apartment building, at 466 South 100 West.
              Downtown Neighborhood. PLRZ20220220 (1:04:27)

        Motion:       An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2023-4, as currently constituted, has been
                      made by council rule.

Dustin Wright, City Planner, discussed the proposed amendment.

Chair MacKay opened public comment. With none, she closed public comment and brought discussion
back to council.

Councilor Ellsworth asked about the advantages or disadvantages of having an ITOD zone vs the DT1
zone.

Mr. Wright said they are comparable. He said the applicant chose DT1 due to most of the property
already being DT1. He added that the developer will be combining the properties into one parcel and
this change would allow for one congruent zone for the entire parcel.

Chair MacKay asked for the recommendation of the Planning Commission.

Mr. Wright said the Planning Commission recommended approval.

Councilor Shipley asked why a developer would want a different zone and what is their motivation.

Mr. Wright said they submitted their plan, the staff realized there were multiple zones and instructed
