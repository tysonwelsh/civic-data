       a.       Update on Road Projects.
Manager Chamness shared updates related to 3900 South. Work was scheduled to begin on the
reconstruction project on February 15, 2021, with pre-work starting beforehand. Ms. Chamness
reported that a consulting firm will handle public information and targeted outreach to ensure that all

Ms. Chamness also reported that the recent issue of The Holladay Journal contained information from
the City of West Jordan rather than the City of Holladay. The inserts were inadvertently switched.
New issues with an explanation of what happened were to be mailed out soon.

Ms. Chamness provided a legislative update and noted that there was a piece of legislation that would
allocate quarter-of-a-quarter funding based on population for the next 15 years. If approved, that
funding would provide the City of Holladay approximately $250,000 on an annual basis. The funds
could be used for long-term capital needs associated with the street system. Council Member Petersen

confirmed.

Ms. Chamness also discussed Senate Bill 98, which would allow developers to opt-out of the City
inspection process and prohibit cities from imposing design standards on developments. She reported
that the bill in its current form was unlikely to pass. Council Member Petersen wondered if current
zones would be grandfathered in and asked about the Holladay Village Zone. Mr. Godfrey explained
that based on the drafts of the bill he has seen thus far, the Holladay Village Zone was not the target
of the regulations. He believed the regulations were more oriented toward communities with strict
design regulations.

Ms. Chamness reported that there was momentum for a billboard related bill in some form. For

were at least three bills pending currently. Ms. Chamness reported that the outcome from the
Legislative Policy Committee was that if cities decide to take that kind of a position, the end result
could be far worse. The Committee felt it would be beneficial to work with legislators to craft
something that is easier to live with. Mr. Godfrey discussed House Bill 82, that would make an
internal ADU a permitted use in any residential zone. He reported that the proposal included the
ability to impose standards related to parking, entrances off streets, and an additional enforcement
tool. Ms. Chamness reported that City staff prepared a comparison of the pending ADU-related bills.
There were comparisons made by zone and lot size.

X.     Reconvene City Council in a Work Meeting.
       a.     Discussion on Previous Public Hearings.
              •      Proposed Rezone
Chair Durham reported that the City Council recently discussed two public hearings consisting of the
proposed rezone on Highland Drive and the proposed Holladay Crossroads Zone.

Council Member Fotheringham asked what constitutes growth as it relates to property tax. He
wondered if there was anything that would increase property tax revenues other than annexing land
or a Truth in Taxation Hearing that would increase the tax rate. For instance, adding additional height.
He used the example of the proposed rezone from the R-2-10 Zone to the RM Zone which would
create more livable units and wondered whether it would add growth or whether it simply
concentrated additional value in that parcel. Council Member Fotheringham also used the example
of the Holladay Crossroads Zone. He wondered if additional height north of Highland Drive would
create more taxable property vertically. Ms. Chamness reported that the Salt Lake County Auditor’s
Office informed her that vertical growth would be considered new growth.

Ms. Chamness shared an example with the City Council. She reported that each year for the last 10
years, the City has received approximately $60,000 in new growth on an annual basis. When the
budget is adopted at the end of June, there is normally an increase of approximately $60,000 because
of new growth. If calculations determine that increasing the verticality of a particular parcel will
generate an additional $250,000 in new growth, Council Members should not necessarily rely on that
$250,000 to appear instead of the $60,000 the following year. believed it was the responsibility of
the County Assessor to make those types of determinations.

Mayor Dahle did not believe that decisions should be driven by maximizing property tax revenues,
but based on whether the request is appropriate and consistent with the General Plan. Council
Member Petersen noted that Council Member Fotheringham raised solid questions regarding property

tax. However, she agreed with Mayor Dahle and felt it was important to focus all decision-making
on what will be the best fit for the area.

Mayor Dahle asked about building height and setbacks in the R-2-10 Zone compared to the RM Zone.
Senior Planning Advisor, Paul Allred clarified that in the RM Zone there is a 40-foot maximum height
allowance for a property over one acre. Since there is not a full acre of land connected to this
particular rezone, the development would be limited to 35 feet. Mr. Allred reported that the building
setbacks in the RM Zone are more restrictive than in the R-2-10 Zone. He discussed graduated height
and noted that graduated height prevents any building near the property line from being too imposing
on properties next door. He reported that the ordinance requires a traffic study for any project that
would involve 100 trips being generated during the peak hour. This project was well below that
threshold.

Council Member Petersen asked about the number of units available for the proposed rezone.
Mr. Allred reported that there could be approximately 14 units on the property in the RM Zone. There
could be approximately seven units on the property if it remains in the R-2-10 Zone. Council Member
Petersen noted that there are currently three units on the site. The applicant could more than double
the density within the existing R-2-10 Zone.

Mayor Dahle reported noted that the building height restrictions in the R-2-10 and RM Zones are the
same. As a result, he was not compelled by the argument that the units will tower over other abutting
residences. He was also not compelled by the arguments related to traffic. Mayor Dahle also did not
believe that the increased number of units would bring more affordable housing to the City.

Council Member Gibbons commented that the applicant could already double the potential density
without a rezone. He agreed that there are RM Zone parcels within one-half to one block away, but
there is no RM in the immediate neighborhood. Council Member Gibbons made note of the negative
reactions from neighbors in the surrounding area. Council Member Quinn expressed concern that the
increased density will not fit in the area. She commented that a large building that filled up most of
the property and contained 14 units would look very out of place.

Council Member Fotheringham agreed with many of the points raised by the City Council. However,
he noted that the Highland Drive Small Area Master Plan allows for more density and more intense
uses along a major corridor. He believed that if an upzone is not appropriate in that particular area,
there was no reason to upzone anywhere else in the City. Council Member Fotheringham pointed out
that Highland Drive was a major corridor, there was mass transportation capability and commercial
property nearby.

Mr. Allred noted that the Planning Commission has a similar view to Council Member Fotheringham.
Mr. Allred pointed out that higher-density projects tend to be located on major streets. Mr. Allred
noted that the more units put on an area of land, the lower the cost of each unit. Mayor Dahle believed
the applicant insinuated that allowing additional density would help the City meet the SB-34
requirements for affordable housing. He did not believe the units would be considered affordable.
Council Member Fotheringham agreed that it would not create affordable housing right away.
However, he pointed out that any addition to housing, particularly multi-family housing, would
eventually become more affordable.

       •        Proposed Holladay Crossroads Zone.
Chair Durham opened up discussions related to the Holladay Crossroads Zone. Council Member
Fotheringham discussed his earlier comments related to vertical expansion and property tax revenue
growth. He wondered if there were benefits connected to extra height north of Highland Drive
Council Member Gibbons noted that the original Planning Commission proposal was a maximum
building height of 65 feet to the north and south. After talking to his constituents, he determined that
height was the biggest concern. As a result, the proposal was a 58-foot maximum height to the north
and a maximum height of 40 feet to the south. However, Council Member Gibbons was not opposed
to the height to the north being less than 58 feet.

Mayor Dahle noted that the General Plan identified the area as an important commercial node for the
City. The reason the height had been brought down from 65 feet was because it was out of character
with the area as it relates to the buildings across the street. Additional height was allowed on the
north side because there was more of a buffer between commercial and residential. Mayor Dahle
believed that in order to have a vibrant commercial node, there needs to be density and traffic
connections to ensure that the businesses there can thrive.

Council Member Gibbons presented a redline document of the Holladay Crossroads Zone ordinance
during a previous City Council Meeting. Mayor Dahle and Council Member Gibbons had discussions
with staff related to the redline document. He summarized some of the changes made to the redline
document as follows:

   •   The original proposal was a 200-foot setback from the commercial property line, which would
       give residents 200 feet plus an additional 60 feet from the canal. The solution presented by
       staff was 200 feet from the residential property line rather than from the commercial property
       line;
   •   The setback for 6060 South, which does not have the canal between it and the commercial
       property, was changed to 150 feet from the residential property line;
   •   No trash receptacle or storage area shall be located closer than 50 feet from a residential
       property line unless otherwise allowed by the Planning Commission.

        c.     Proposed Sidewalk Ordinance Discussion.
Ms. Smith reported that there were draft amendments to sidewalk-related sections of the ordinance in
the packet. Staff had been working to update sections of the Code regarding the sidewalk policy in
an effort to improve the safety of sidewalks in the City. Ms. Smith noted that redline changes were
made to sections of Code from Chapters 13 and 14 and reviewed those changes.

Ms. Smith discussed the difference between 14.32.010 D: Reimbursement by City (if the owner of a
parcel or property wanted to replace the curb and gutter, drive approach, or sidewalk on public
property due to deteriorated conditions, there may be a 50% reimbursement of the cost of the eligible
project) and 14.32.030 B: Fifty Percent Paid by City (upon receiving a notice to replace public curbs,
curb ramps, gutters, and sidewalks due to deteriorated conditions, 50% of the cost may be covered.
The City would make the replacement or hire a contractor and the owner would contribute the
remaining 50% of the funds).

Council Member Fotheringham noted that 14.32.010 D outlined that an eligible participant must be a
resident of the City. He wondered how that would impact a property owner who is not a Holladay
resident but owns or rents a property in Holladay. She reported that staff would work to clarify the
language. Council Member Quinn noted that there is a monetary cap of $1,000 for 14.32.010 D but
no monetary cap for 14.32.030 B. Ms. Smith explained that the program was structured that way in
the past and that clarifying language could be added.

Ms. Smith outlined next steps and reported that comments from the City Council Meeting would be
incorporated into the draft ordinance. The Planning Commission would look at changes and make a
recommendation. The item would likely come back to City Council for approval in March 2021.

       d.      Budget Update Options – Based on Council and Business Feedback on COVID
               needs.
Ms. Chamness reported that there had been previous discussion regarding the use CARES Act funds.
CARES Act funds were used to cover first responder services that were previously budgeted for police
and fire services. There was now an option to use General Fund dollars to provide support to the
business community in the aftermath of COVID-19. Based on City Council guidance, staff came up
with several possible items to add to the budget, which included:

   •   Holladay Bucks program. Ms. Chamness explained that the program would encourage
       residents to visit Holladay businesses, including retail, restaurants, and personal services.
       Approximately $15 per household would be given and could be used at businesses in Holladay
       that choose to participate. The mechanics of the program had not been worked out but there
       were suggestions that the program be app-based or include printable gift certificates. Ms.
       Chamness reported that the estimated cost of the program was approximately $200,000;
   •   An estimated $130,000 for rental assistance, targeted to residents between 60% area median
       income (“AMI”) and 80% AMI. Ms. Chamness noted that residents that fall within those
       percentages were not currently served by other rental assistance programs;
   •   Business grant program. Based on the results of the business survey earlier in the year, the
       program would be designed to assist businesses in making long-term technology
       improvements to continue operations. Assuming a grant of approximately $5,000 per
