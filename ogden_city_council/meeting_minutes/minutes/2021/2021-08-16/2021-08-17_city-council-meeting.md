          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed amendments to the Lynn
Community Plan came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated a developer has petitioned to amend the Lynn
Community Plan at an area near 2nd Street and Wall Avenue. The developer has also applied to rezone the property from R-1-6 to
R-3/CO to be able to build a series oftownhomes. However, the rezone is contingent upon the approval of the Lynn Community
Plan because the R-3/CO zone is not compatible with the current community plan. The Planning Commission has recommended
approval of the petition to amend the community plan despite the fact that a similar plan amendment was denied in 20 19. The
Plannil;tg Commission placed a condition that all properties located within the amended area must be acquired by the developer
and developed under a single development agreement.
          An additional memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a petition to change zoning
at 140-142 West 2nd Street and 139 North Wall Avenue came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the
northwest comer of 2nd and Wall is in the R-3/CO zone, and the property around it is in the R-1-6 zone. A developer is
petitioning to extend the R-3/CO zone further into the R -1-6 zone, beyond the comer of 2nd Street and Wall A venue. The purpose
of the rezone is to allow a multi-family townhome development. A similar request to rezone these properties to R-5 or R-3 was
denied by the City Council in February 2020. This rezone is contingent upon approval of the Lynn Community Plan amendment,
which will be considered by the City Council first. This rezone is also contingent upon recording of a development agreement,
which is attached to this report. The Planning Commission recommended approval of this rezone with a unanimous vote.
          Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
Plan amendment petition. He noted the petitioners are John Strickler, Renae Woods, Betty Jones, Shawn Strong, and Bob Giboney
and he identified the locations of the properties they own in the Community Plan area. He added that Mr. Strong has acquired Mr.
Strickler's property since the petition was first filed. The group proposes to amend the Community Plan to include the option to
rezone from R-1-6 (single family) to R-3/CO (multiple-family/conditional overlay). There is a separate petition to change the
zoning of properties at the comer of2nd Street and Wall Avenue. The group also proposes the same conditions apply to property
under a conditional overlay zoning, with a development agreement, at the property at 120 West 2nd Street, but that the conditions
extend beyond the corner property. He discussed the history of previous petitions submitted for zoning of these subject properties,
noting that they were seeking R-5 zoning in 2019. Additional petitioners have signed on to this current petition and additional
surrounding support has been obtained. In November of2020 the Council considered a development proposal from Mr. Strong
that included 50 townhomes in the initial phase of the development project; he presented a rendering of that proposed
development and noted that the project would actually be a second phase to current developments in the area. The project would
address properties that have fallen into disrepair by providing new construction that reflects the historical architecture of the area.
However, staff was concerned about the isolation of remaining single-family homes in the neighborhood as the City has a policy
prohibiting the creation of peninsulas or islands of zoning. The Planning Commission considered the Community Plan amendment
in November of2020 and recommended amendment to the Community Plan by allowing the option to rezone properties beyond
the comer parcel from R-1-6 to R-3/CO, with the recommendation that the plan language include conditions that rezoning does
not isolate single-family properties; that there is an appropriate transition between single-family neighborhoods and family
developments; and that all properties between Fort Bingham Subdivision, Wall A venue, and the north side of2nd Street must be
acquired and developed under one development agreement. The dissenting Planning Commissioners cited the following concerns:
              4. The Lynn Community Plan should be revisited before adding more amendments.
               5. R-1-6 zoning already provides for moderate housing and offers the best opportunity for home ownership.
               6. The amendment would isolate homes, and the extension oftownhomes would not create a good transition to the
                   existing single-family homes.
          Mr. Montgomery then noted the zone change petition is the companion item to the petition to amend the Lynn
Community Plan; the petitioners are seeking to rezone their property from R-1-6 to R-3/CO with a development agreement. He
presented the conceptual plan for the development, identifying one single-family property that would remain and be surrounded
by the proposed townhome development. He provided a project breakdown, noting there would be 34 single family townhomes
and five single family homes on 5.02 acres. The townhomes/row-housing would be subdivided under a private subdivision for
owner occupation. The proposed development would match the townhomes recently approved for 120 West2nd Street; new multi-
family and group-dwelling standards would need to be applied. Staffs analysis included the policies to avoid isolating
developments and neighborhoods and not create peninsulas or islands of zoning. The proposed concept has changed several times
since it was originally presented in January of this year; these changes are responsive to staffs concerns about the creation of
peninsulas of R-1-6 zoning, isolation of an existing single-family home, double fronting lots, and historical preservation. The
latest concept would retain the Baldwin property access at it currently exists; retain the 140 West 2nd Street structure; not include
the Giboney parcel; and contain 34 townhomes versus the 42 included in the original development proposal. The Planning
Commission voted to approve the petition with a development agreement requiring the following:
