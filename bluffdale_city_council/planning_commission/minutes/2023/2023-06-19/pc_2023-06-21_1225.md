Associate City Planner, Ellen Oakman presented the proposed text amendment to the R-1-10
Infill Overlay ("10") Zone for Quail Ridge and explained that the zone allows for flexibility in
terms of the density and development standards. The proposed modification to the setbacks and
a couple of other minor standards would allow for duplexes. The location of the 1.96-acre
property was identified on a map displayed. The property owner attended a Work Session before
the City Coimcil to propose duplexes and made minor modifications. The City Council was
supportive and the applicant submitted an application.

The current concept shows two single-ownership duplexes with the rest being single-family
homes. The proposal is for a fi'ont setback of 20 feet for the garage and 10 feet for the home.
Lots 1 and 6 to the south require a minimum setback of 20 feet for the 14600 South or Bluffdale

rear setback of20 feet, which currently exists.

Single-ownership duplexes would be allowed with the proposal. A small modification would be
made to the lot with 60 feet of IBrontage measured from the front setback. The sidewalk and park
strip would also be updated to the current standard.

Chair Cragun asked for clarification on the changes made. The existing front setback of 20 feet

would be changed from 10 feet and eight feet to five feet. There was no change to the 20-foot
rear setback. Duplexes will be allowed and the standard sidewalk and park strip will be required
rather than the existing integral sidewalks with no park strip. They will do the standard park
strip and sidewalk rather than street trees.

Mr. Crowell reported that the Planning Commission does not often get involved in Development
Agreements as there is a legal aspect that is contractual in nature. The Planning Commission is
allowed to modify land use and zoning provisions with Development Agreements. If what is
created differs firom the underljdng zone it becomes a land use ordinance. For that reason, it falls
under the purview of the Planning Commission which makes a recommendation to the City
Council.


Mr. Crowell identified the Light Industrial ("1-1") and the Heavy Commercial("HC")areas. The
entire industrial park includes these types of attributes with Industrial and Heavy Commercial
being difficult to discern. The area between 14600 South, Bluffdale Boulevard, Independence
Village, and the East Jordan Canal provides a horizontal buffer where redevelopment has begun
to occur. The Redevelopment Agency ("RDA") had a vision years ago of redevelopment in
Eastern Bluffdale, which is now happening.


Mr. Crowell reported that the applicant, Shivam Shah, and his partners have submitted
applications. The historic gravel road was 50 feet wide and used for private purposes. The City
offered to widen it. The City is trying to spur investment using RDA investment, which created
some zoning implications, which are reflected in the site plan. With the north-south section near
Covent Garden was that 50 feet feels tight. The Council and the RDA Board wanted to see if
City Engineer, Michael Fazio and the project engineers could come up with a wider cross-
section. They tried to make it as wide as possible considering yard areas and settled on 59 feet.
The decision was to lay out the proposed design. The goal of all acquisitions is to have willing
sellers. This situation is complicated because of ongoing development with a road on top and
multiple property owners. The RDA also has goals to incentivize investment and clean up the
area.   They want to see modem, new development that creates assessed value and job
opportunities.


Mr. Crowell suggested that the City take responsibility for the impact of encroaching nine feet
into an ongoing project, the amount of real estate required, and the reduction in landscaping.
The applicants felt other things might be justified due to the site location. They questioned the
need for windows on the backs of industrial buildings to create more light and whether the
