BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Land Use Map Amendment, Zoning Map
Amendment, and Development Agreement for approximately 27.37 acres located between
Pioneer Road, 12600 S, 12650 South, and the Salt Lake County Library Senior Center
Complex. The property is located at approximately 1060 East Pioneer Rd (Exhibit B). The



Fitzgerald Subdivision Land Use and Zoning Map            App. # TEXTMAP-0052-2021, TEXTMAP-0051-2021,
Amendment, Development Agreement Request              1          DA-0050-2021
property is currently zoned TC adjacent to Pioneer Rd, and RA1 and RA2 to the south. The
applicant is requesting that a Land Use Map Amendment, Zoning Map Amendment, and
Development Agreement be approved to develop the property with for 111 single-family
residential lots and roughly 30,000 square feet of commercial space.

There are seven parcels included in this application. Most have historically been used for
agricultural purposes. There are two homes, built in 1890 and 1980. There is also a large
agricultural building on the property.



ANALYSIS

General Plan and Zoning. The Land Use Map of the General Plan calls for the Town Center
and Residential Low/Medium Density land use designations for the subject property
(Exhibit C). These categories are characterized as follows:

The property has been assigned the TC, RA1, and RA2 zoning classifications (Exhibit D).
According to Draper City Municipal Code (DCMC) Section 9-8-020 the purpose of the TC
zone is to “provide a method for implementing special provisions found within the general plan
and the Draper City conservation area master plan regarding the establishment and promotion
of a historic core area of the community characterized by stringent design standards for
buildings, public spaces, site design and landscaping with a harmonious mix of commercial uses,
limited high density residential uses and civic areas.”

Per DCMC Section 9-8-020, the purpose of the RA1 and RA2 zones are to “foster low density
development with little impact on its surroundings and municipal services; to generally preserve
the character of the City’s semi-rural areas; and to promote and preserve conditions favorable to
large-lot family life, including the keeping of limited numbers of animals and fowl. This zone is
intended to promote a combination of retail and service facilities which, in character and scale,
meet day to day needs of nearby residents.”

The property abuts the TC zone to the north. To the west is the TC and RA2 zones. Located

Town Center area about 125-140-feet wide. The Town Center designation area will be 1.73
acres in size.

Zoning Map Amendment. The applicant is proposing to rezone the property into three zones
which correspond to the requested land use designation (Exhibit F). The TC zoned area
would equal 1.73 acres. The 5.98 acres designated for high density would be zoned RM2,
supporting up to 12 dwelling units per acre. The RM2 zoning classification has the following
purpose per DCMC Section 9-8-020 “permit well designed apartments, townhomes, twin
homes, and condominiums at relatively high densities that are appropriately buffered from and
compatible with surrounding land uses.”

The remaining 19.67 acres is proposed for the R4 zoning designation, supporting up to four
dwelling units per acre. According to DCMC Section 9-8-020 the purpose of the R4 zone is
to “permit medium to medium-high density residential developments with single-family homes.
These zones are intended to foster thriving and well maintained neighborhoods that allow
upscale single-family homes on smaller lots that require less maintenance and provide a
balanced lifestyle.”

Streets within the development will be a mix of public and private. The public roads will be
through the R4 zoned area, with a public road going north to Pioneer Road. These public
roads would meet the City’s standard of 60-feet wide. The private roadways would be in the
RM2 area. The private roadways would be 37-feet wide and 24-feet wide, and comply with
DCMC standards for private roads.

Traffic Impact Study. The Traffic Impact Study looks at the Level of Service (LOS) for area
intersections with existing conditions and with the proposed development (Exhibit G). The
study found that the LOS for intersections is acceptable both under existing conditions and
with the proposed development. Intersections were found to have the same level of service
in both existing and proposed conditions, with the exception of one. The intersection of
Manfield Way and 1300 East is anticipated to go from a LOS C to a LOS D with the proposed
development. There are no mitigation measures recommended in the study. The study
shows the development will generate approximately 2,260 weekday daily trips, including
138 trips in the morning peak hour, and 169 trips in the evening peak hour. Staff review
comments on the study can be found in Exhibit A of this report.

Fire Division Review.

   1. Fire Department Access is required. An unobstructed minimum road width of
      twenty-six (26) feet exclusive of the shoulders and a minimum height of thirteen (13)
      feet six (6) inches shall be required. The road must be designed and maintained to
      support the imposed loads of emergency apparatus. The surface shall be able to
      provide all weather driving capabilities. The road shall have an inside turning radius
      of twenty – eight (28) feet. There shall be a maximum grade of 10%. Grades may be
      checked prior to building permits being issued.

       D103.6.1 Roads 20 to 26 feet in width. Fire lane signs as specified in Section D103.6
       shall be posted on both sides of fire apparatus access roads that are 20 to 26 feet
       wide (6096to 7925 mm).

          This section requires that parking be prohibited on both sides of narrower fire
           apparatus access roads. Twenty feet (6096 mm) is the appropriate width needed
           for two average-size fire trucks to pass one another. If that width is reduced by
           parking even on one side, it will be potentially difficult for a fire department to
           undertake emergency operations in that area.

       D103.6.2 Roads more than 26 feet in width. Fire lane signs as specified in Section
       D103.6 shall be posted on one side of fire apparatus access roads more than 26 feet
       wide (7925 mm) and less than 32 feet wide (9754 mm).

          Because this width is more than sufficient for maneuvering at least two fire
           department vehicles by one another, parking would be allowed on one side.

       Fire apparatus access road. A road that provides fire apparatus access from a fire
       station to a facility, building or portion thereof. This is a general term inclusive of all
       other terms such as fire lane public street, private street, parking lot lane and access

lessen the character and charm of Draper. It would be extremely short-sighted for you folks, who are only

allowing such a change in the literal and figurative heart of Draper City.

traffic and drastically change the character of Pioneer Road, which is already suffering mightily from the

government. It would be a tragic thing if Pioneer became another 123rd South, and it's already well on the
