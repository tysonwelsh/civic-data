Reports from other Municipal Service Providers

Unified Police Department (UPD)

                 Chief Del Craig stated the 4th of July event was successful and uneventful from a
police perspective. With regard to crime, the Magna Precinct had about a 200 per month call
increase March through May. Most notable, were three homicides, which is an unprecedented
amount. Homicides are investigated by the UPD’s Violent Crime Unit. The one on 8270 Westbury
Drive was intensive, and also needed the Canine Unit as well as the Magna Precinct. All the
homicides were solved, and the suspects charged. Then, property crimes and thefts have been
down a little bit. However, it is normal for crime to pick up in the summer. As soon as the UPD
transitions to its new system, he will have a better view of the crimes and will report on them then.
Currently, the Magna Precinct is fully staffed with officers and supervisors, which is the first time
it has been in quite some time. It also has a new office coordinator who is doing a great job.

Unified Fire Authority (UFA)

                Council Member Prokopis stated the UFA will be building five or six new stations
and has started construction on the Midvale fire station. It has also scheduled construction for
the Eagle Mountain fire station, but the rebuild on Magna Main Street has been bumped down
the list. He thought that was at the top of the list and did not know why that had been postponed,
but crews will be here in August and will be sharing the station for the construction time.

               Council Member Hull stated the UFA is waiting on some zoning changes on that.

Code Enforcement ~ Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District

               Paul Ashton stated Tom Zumbado has received a promotion, so will not be here.

               Olivia Uesi, Code Enforcement Officer, stated the GSLMSD has not assigned
anyone yet to report to the Magna Metro Township, but since the township’s last meeting, 42 new
cases have been opened and 32 cases have closed. Magna now has 91 cases open, 46 of which
are zoning issues and 45 are because of weeds.

Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District

                Pam Roberts, Director, WFWRD, announced that Ryan Jones, veteran with 13
years, won the National Waste and Recycling Association’s Driver of the Year Award for 2021 for
the public sector. Then, on June 17th, WFWRD celebrated Waste and Recycling Workers Week.
Tomorrow, it is wrapping up the 2021 Area Cleanup. The changes it made to that program have
been tough on residents, but they were made due to low staffing levels. WFWRD needs 21 part-
time Commercial Driver’s License (CDL) drivers to run the cleanup program, but last year and this
year, it only averaged 10-12 drivers because of a nationwide shortage of CDL drivers. Every
year, WFWRD increases wages to help recruit and retain drivers, but then so does everyone else.

               Council Member Prokopis asked if the cleanup program would ever get back to
what it used to be.

               Ms. Roberts stated going into 2022, the board will be looking to see if it can bring
back the program in front of houses, but she did not see how that could happen and have it
successful for everybody. She thought this would be the new normal

               Council Member Peel asked about the budget.

                Ms. Roberts stated it will be about $300,000 in 2020. There will not be that big of
savings this year because WFWRD increased wages, and in fact, it increased wages going into
2020 too. She could report back to the Council on wages later. The good news is WFWRD is
not anticipating doing a fee increase for 2022, so there will be some savings there.

Resolution

              Paul Ashton reviewed Resolution 21-07-01, regarding Wasatch Front Waste and
Recycling District’s (WFWRD) request for Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security Act
(CARES) funds:

RESOLUTION NO. 21-07-01                                                   DATE: July 13, 2021

       A RESOLUTION OF THE MAGNA METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL APPROVING
       AND AUTHORIZING A PAYMENT TO WASATCH FRONT WASTE AND
       RECYCLING DISTRICT IN THE AMOUNT OF $4,478.40 FOR COVID-RELATED
       EXPENSES

       WHEREAS, the Magna Metro Township (“Magna” or the “Municipality”) recognizes its
need for municipal-types services; and

      WHEREAS, the Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District (“WFWRD”) is an Interlocal
Cooperation Agency that provides fire protection services to Magna;

        WHEREAS, Salt Lake County is a County pursuant to Utah Code Title 17: COUNTIES
and is the County where Magna is located; and

       WHEREAS, due to the impacts to state and local governments nationwide, the United
States Government passed the CARES Act which, through Salt Lake County, provided Magna

                                              4
DATE           TUESDAY                                 JULY                            13, 2021

with certain funding that could be used to cover unbudgeted COVID-related costs for the delivery
of municipal-type services; and

       WHEREAS, as Magna’s municipal services are provided by local districts and interlocal
agencies, it is prudent for Magna to assist them with their unbudgeted COVID-related costs; and

        WHEREAS, WFWRD has presented Magna with an invoice and purchase information to
request $4,478.40 from Magna’s CARES Act funds to cover WFWRD’s unbudgeted COVID-
related costs, hereinafter known as Attachment A; and

      WHEREAS, providing this financial assistance benefits Magna and softens the impacts
caused to a service provider due to COVID-19; therefore, be it

       RESOLVED, the Magna Metro Township Council approves, and authorizes the Mayor to
approve the attached invoice for Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District (ATTACHMENT A)
and authorize the payment of $4,478.40 to WFWRD to assist them in covering their unbudgeted
COVID-related costs.

      APPROVED AND ADOPTED this 13th day of July 2021 by the Magna Metro Township in
Magna Metro Township, Salt Lake County, Utah.
