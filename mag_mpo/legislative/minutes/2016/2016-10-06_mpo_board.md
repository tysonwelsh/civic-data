Public Comment No comments Open and Public Meeting Training - David Church, Utah League Cities and Towns Public bodies are required annually to have training on the Open and Public Meeting Act. Actions and deliberations are to be taken openly. This training helps to understand the policies behind the Act. A few definitions: Convening: calling of a meeting of a public body for the express purpose of discussing or acting on a subject which the public body has jurisdiction or advisory power. Meeting: the convening of a public body, with a quorum present, for the purpose of discussing, receiving comments for the public about or acting on a subject which the public body has jurisdiction or advisory power. A meeting does not mean a chance meeting or social meeting. Public Body: any administrative, advisory, executive, or legislative body of the state or its political subdivisions. A public body does not include a political party, group, or caucus, also a legislative conference, rules, or sitting committee. Quorum: A simple majority of the membership of the public body Recording: An audio, or an audio and video, record of the proceedings that can be reviewed needs to be available 3 days after meeting Written Minutes: Minutes and recording shall be taken for all open meetings and be permanently kept Public notice of meetings should be given at least 24 hours or more prior to a meeting and must include the agenda, date, time, and location. All public meeting notices should be posted on the Utah Public Notice Website, organizations website and office. A public body may not take final action on a topic in an open meeting unless the topic is listed under an agenda item and included with the advance public notice, with the exception of an emergency meeting. An emergency meeting may be held if necessary for the public body to consider matters of an emergency or urgent nature. The notice should include time, place, location, and topics to be considered. A meeting is open to the public unless closed for discussion of the character, professional competence, or physical or mental health of an individual; session to discuss collective bargaining, pending or reasonable imminent litigation, purchase, exchange, or lease of real property. Individuals Social Security Numbers, Welfare, and other private information will not be discussed. A recording of a closed meeting shall be completed and unedited and should be permanently kept. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning October 6, 2016 Page 3 Minutes for an open meeting should list the date, time, place, and members present. Minutes should include a summary of the agenda topics and a record of votes taken. Minutes should be available to the public within a reasonable amount of time. The public body has the authority to take action on motions, resolutions, or ordinances. A public body can establish its own rules or bylaws, over and above the Public Meeting Act. Electronic Meetings can be convened if the public body has adopted a rule or resolution pertaining to electronic meetings. Electronic communication (text or calling) during a meeting, about the item being discussed is not allowed. For more information contact Dave Church, Utah League of Cities and Towns. MPO FHWA FTA Federal Certification of the MAG Planning Process Response - Shawn Seager Every four years FHWA and FTA jointly review and evaluate the MAG MPO, UDOT, UTA transportation planning process. The certification process ensures the federal planning requirements are being satisfactorily implemented. The review process provides advice and guidance to help enhance the planning process and improve the quality of transportation investment decisions. MAG, UDOT and UTA activity coordinate the planning issues and activities involved with the development of a well maintained transportation program. The federal certification review committee reported that MAG is fully certificated and are were no corrective actions were issued.
