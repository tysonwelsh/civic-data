1.    Briefing Session to Review the Agenda. (Jim Spung/AICP/Senior Planner)

Mr. Spung explained that the July 23rd Planning Commission meeting had been canceled
due to the Pioneer Day holiday. The Commission would next meet on August 13.

Planner Terryne Bergeson then provided an overview of Agenda Item #4, explaining that
the application was for a new reception center. The applicant was requesting extended
business hours, and her property was within 250 feet of residential housing. Notice of a
public hearing regarding this matter had been sent out on June 26th which resulted in only
one phone call. That caller indicated she had no concerns if there were no police calls.
The applicant originally asked to operate until 1:30 a.m. but later indicated she would
accept whatever time would be deemed permissible by the City. She also indicated she
would not allow alcohol to be served on site.

Commissioner Wendel wondered if 200 people in a 5000 square foot space was
excessive. She asked Commissioner McElreath to weigh in as a former firefighting
professional. He responded that it depended on the layout of the property. He also
mentioned out that the fact that the building had a fire suppression system made that
occupancy level less concerning. Ms. Bergeson stated that a building permit would be
necessary due to the recent change of use, and occupancy limits would be addressed
during that process.

Mr. Spung explained that staff was working on a text amendment to address reception
centers. The intent was to standardize conditions of approval which would minimize the
number of applications for that use which the Planning Commission had to address and

Commissioner McElreath asked that a condition be added that the rear door remain

department’s attention. The two issues, while not necessarily related, were in the same
section of the city code.

vehicles and trailers. Mr. Spung responded that everything would need to be behind the
residence (shielded from view at the front).

Commissioner Wendel pointed out that although she would not have voted in favor of the

                            CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT

 4. Request for a Conditional Use Permit to Operate a Reception Center and
    Extended Hours of Operation at 4870 S. Redwood Rd; Applicant Monica
    Padilla (2Z24 – CUP-000465-2024 / Terryne Bergeson, Planner)


Ms. Bergeson explained that the applicant was requesting a non-administrative
conditional use permit to operate a reception center in a tenant space within the True
Value Plaza. The space was within 250 feet of homes and therefore required additional
Planning Commission consideration to grant approval for a business to operate past 10
pm. The applicant had submitted a narrative with details of the proposed business
operations (Exhibit H). The intent was to provide a space where customers could host
family events.

Conditional use permits were governed by Utah State Code and were required to meet
the provisions outlined in 10-9a-507. If the Planning Commission were to deny the CUP
request it must be determined that “reasonably anticipated detrimental effects of a
proposed conditional use cannot be substantially mitigated by the proposal or the
imposition of reasonable conditions to achieve compliance with applicable standards, the
conditional use may be denied”.

The land uses to the adjacent property were as follows: To the north was Mixed-Use (MU)
Auto Repair; to the south was Boulevard-Commercial (BC) Self-Storage; the property to
the east was zoned as Boulevard-Commercial (BC) Shopping Center, and the area
directly west of the subject property was zoned as Residential (R-1-8) Single Family
Neighborhood.

A public notice was mailed to property owners within 300 feet of the subject property on
June 28, 2024. As of July 3, 2024, staff had received only one inquiry. The caller lived
directly behind the venue and stated that she did not have any concerns unless “there

are wild parties and a lot of police calls”. If any additional comments were received, they
would be noted in the public hearing presentation.

The parcel was zoned Mixed Use and had a General Plan designation of High-Density
Mixed Use. The center itself was not consistent with the General Plan which defined a
high-density mixed-use zone as consisting of at least 12 dua. in a mixed-use setting.
However, the Land Development Code did identify the use as permitted with non-
administrative approval and the request was consistent with the personal and retail
services provided at the existing shopping center.

Section 13.33.050 of the LDC states the following related to the City’s authority to impose
conditions of approval on a conditional use permit application:
In order to achieve compliance with the standards set forth herein, the city may impose

B. Proposed site ingress and egress to existing and proposed roads and streets.

D. Design, location, and amount of off-street parking, loading areas and solid waste

K. Measures designed to protect the natural features of the site including wetlands and

HOURS OF OPERATION: In the submitted narrative (Ex H) the applicant listed proposed
hours to allow events to run until 1:00 am. The location was within 250 feet of a residential
zone, which required Planning Commission approval for hours of operation past 10 p.m.
(Section 13.23.200). The Planning Commission and staff reviewed several applications
for reception center use with similar location (shopping center abutting residential
neighborhood to the rear). Staff recommended that modified extended hours of operation
be granted in consideration of the residential neighbors to the west. The recommendation
was based on applications with similar site conditions, but in consideration of the distance
between the building and the homes to the rear, which were approximately 200 feet from
the reception center.

NOISE: All businesses were required to abide by Salt Lake County Health Department
noise regulations. The reception center was surrounded by properties that the county
ordinance designated as type A (single-family residential) and C (commercial retail type
uses). Quiet hours were between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m. To ensure compliance with noise
regulations staff recommended any doors at the rear of the building remain closed during
events to prevent noise spill into the residential area, and that the applicant was
responsible to ensure that the operation did not exceed the decibel levels listed in the
county regulations. If complaints were reported, the applicant might be required to submit
verification of decibel levels and obtain building permits to make sound-proofing
improvements.

Monica Padilla introduced herself and made herself available for questions.

brought on premises. She responded that her clients would have to sign a contract

Commission Quigley asked the applicant what type of events she anticipated having. She

Commissioner Wright asked if it would be problematic to keep the rear door to the facility

that would be included in client contracts.

Commissioner Russell inquired about parking. Ms. Padilla responded that there was
plenty of parking, particularly in the evenings when most events took place. Ms. Bergeson
interjected that there were more than 100 parking stalls on site.

                             ZONING TEXT AMENDMENT

 5. Public Hearing and Consideration of a Zoning Text Amendment to Several Sections
    of the Taylorsville Municipal Code, to Align City Standards with Utah Senate Bill 13
    Related to Home-Based Microschool and Micro-Education Entities as well as
    Amendments Related to Vehicles and Trailers Associated with a Home Occupation
    / 2Z24 / DCA-000435-2024 / Jim Spung, Senior Planner

Mr. Spung explained that the City of Taylorsville was requesting a text amendment to
several sections of the Taylorsville Land Development Code (LDC) to align city standards
with recent legislation passed by the Utah Legislature related to local government
regulation of home-based microschools and micro education entities. The primary
changes proposed by this amendment were:

• New land use types and definitions for “home-based microschool” and “micro-education
entity.”
• Updated land use tables to allow home-based microschool, micro-education entity, and
charter school as a permitted use in all zoning districts (except the open space zone).
• New special use standards for home-based microschool and micro-education entities.
• New and updated minimum vehicle parking standards for education uses.
• Updated definition for “charter school.”

Staff was also proposing modifications to the standards related to vehicles and trailers
used in association with a home occupation. The primary changes proposed included:

• New modified standards to allow more vehicles and trailers for some large residential
lots:

                                            6
o Within the R-1-20 or R-1-40 zoning district.
o Primary access via collector or arterial street.
o Vehicles and trailers parked behind primary residence and screened from public
view.
o Parking and maneuvering area limited to 35 percent of the rear yard area and
located at least 50 feet from neighboring dwellings.
o Maximum of 5 vehicles and 10 trailers at any given time.
o Registered to the business owner or current resident.
o Storage of equipment, materials, etc. not allowed.
• Gravel surface could be used when:
o Parking and maneuvering area had been designed and constructed to support weight
of vehicles.
o Gravel was clean, compacted, free of weeds, dust, etc.
o Minimum 30-foot paved track-out pad was installed between the gravel surface and
public street.
o Area was maintained to remain compliant with standards.

In the 2024 General Session, the Utah Legislature passed Senate Bill 13 (S.B. 13),
resulting in revisions to how cities and counties regulated education entities. The intent
of S.B. 13 was to provide home-based microschool and micro-education entities with
certain similar duties, requirements, waivers, and rights as private and charter schools. A
summary of S.B. 13 was provided below:

• Defined terms “home-based microschool” and “micro-education entity.”
• Required a county and municipality to consider a home-based microschool and micro
education entity as a permitted use in all zoning districts;
• Identified the occupancy requirements to which a micro-education entity was subject;
• Required a local school board to excuse a student who attended a home-based
  microschool or micro-education entity under certain circumstances;
• Provided that an instructor of a school-age child who attended a home-based
microschool or micro-education entity was solely responsible for instruction, materials,
and evaluation;
• Prohibited a local school board from requiring a home-based microschool or micro-
education entity to provide teaching credentials, submit to inspection, and conduct testing;
• Prevented government entities from regulating home-based microschool and micro-
education entity food preparation and distribution under certain circumstances;
• Required a home-based microschool and micro-education entity to register as a
    business;
• Exempted a student who attended a home-based microschool or micro-education entity
    from immunization requirements; and
• Made technical and conforming changes.

Regarding the use of vehicles and trailers associated with home occupations, Mr. Spung
explained that it had become more common in recent years. In an effort to support
business-owners and entrepreneurs within the city, the city’s administration requested
that staff research potential amendments to the zoning code to allow for added flexibility


                                             7
regarding the parking of business vehicles and trailers while also ensuring proper
safeguards were in place to minimize potentially negative impacts on surrounding uses.

S.B. 13 authorized local governments to adopt additional standards for these uses related
to:

• Parking, traffic, hours of operation;
• city business license requirements;
• standards within each zone pertaining to setback, height, bulk and massing regulations,
offsite parking, curb cut, traffic circulation, and construction staging; and
• imposing regulations on the location of a project that were necessary to avoid risks to
health or safety.

In addition to the mandated requirements of S.B. 13, city staff was recommending
additional standards be considered to ensure home-based microschools and micro-
education entities operated safely and that potentially negative impacts of those uses
were mitigated or minimized to the maximum extent practical. The following was a
summary of additional standards for consideration:

Home-Based Microschool
• 13.11.040(G)(3) Minimum Parking. The draft was proposing that a minimum of one
vehicle parking space be provided for every non-resident employee, plus one additional
space beyond that required for the primary dwelling.
• 13.11.040(G)(3) Secure Outdoor Play Area. This standard required that a secure
outdoor play area be provided in the rear or side yard, similar to the requirement for a
home daycare and home preschool.

• Location Standards. A new table note was included with each land use table prohibiting
a home-based microschool within 660 feet of a sexually oriented business or an
establishment that sold alcohol.

Charter School
• Location Standards. A new table note was included with each land use table prohibiting
a charter school within 660 feet of a sexually oriented business or an establishment that
sold alcohol.

Micro-Education Entity
• 13.11.250(C) Secure Outdoor Play Area. This standard required that a secure outdoor
play area be provided that was located a minimum of five feet from a vehicle parking,
access, or circulation area. The area must also be secured with a self-latching gate and
be well-maintained.
• 13.11.250(D) ADA Loading Zone. A minimum of one ADA compliant passenger loading
zone be provided on-site.
• Location Standards. A new table note was included with each land use table prohibiting
a micro-education entity within 660 feet of a sexually oriented business or an
establishment that sold alcohol.

education entities.

                         PART TWO OF AGENDA ITEM NO. 5

 5b. Public Hearing and Consideration of Amendments Related to Vehicles and
    Trailers Associated with a Home-Occupation / Jim Spung, Senior Planner

Mr. Spung explained that the second half of this Agenda Item was related to home
occupation standards. He said residents with home businesses had shown quite a bit of
interest in having the City address the number of vehicles and trailers allowed in rear
yards (out of sight from the front of the home). Code Enforcement had reached out to
discuss one specific home business which was non-compliant with city code as currently
written.

The proposed amendment was intended to accommodate more intense home
occupations in areas of the city where such uses might be deemed acceptable. Staff had
performed GIS analysis to determine which existing properties in the city would qualify
for the amended vehicle and trailer standards. These findings were summarized in Exhibit
C of the staff report. In short, the primary changes proposed included the following:

   o Within the R-1-20 or R-1-40 zoning district.

   o Primary access via collector or arterial street.

   o Vehicles and trailers parked behind primary residence and screened from public
     view.

   o Parking and maneuvering area limited to 35 percent of the rear yard area and
     located at least 50 feet from neighboring dwellings.

   o Maximum of 5 vehicles and 10 trailers at any given time.

   o Registered to the business owner or current resident.

   o Storage of equipment, materials, etc. not allowed.

   Gravel surface could be used when:

   o Parking and maneuvering area had been designed and constructed to support
   weight of vehicles.

                                             12
   o Gravel was clean, compacted, free of weeds, dust, etc.

   o Minimum 30-foot paved track-out pad was installed between the gravel surface
   and public street.

   o Area was maintained to remain compliant with standards

Mr. Spung explained that there were approximately 20 properties in Taylorsville that
would qualify to have additional vehicles and trailers based on the listed criteria.

Commissioner Quigley expressed concern that the maximum of five vehicles plus ten
trailers was too high. Mr. Spung pointed out that because residents would be limited
vehicles taking up no more than 35% of the rear yard area, the resulting number of
vehicles might or might not equal that many.

Commissioner Quigley spoke against the proposed amendment, expressing concern for
homeowners situated behind such lots. He suggested it made more sense for the
homeowner prompting this amendment to bring his property into compliance rather than
change the entire city’s code to accommodate his individual situation.

Commissioner Wendel wondered how this would impact an R-1-10, R-1-20 or flag lot
property owner who wished to subdivide behind one of these situations. Mr. Spung
responded they would not be able to do so. Commissioner Wendel suggested it would
then be potentially decreasing the value of such neighboring lots. Commissioner Quigley
agreed.

Commissioner Wright felt that requiring increased vegetation at the 6’ fence might be a
more appropriate screen/buffer than the fence alone. Commissioner Young asked if such
a business owner would be required (for example) to plant trees that were 30’ today or
that would eventually grow to be 30’.

Mr. Spung clarified that the proposed code did not require the vehicles to be screened so
as not to be visible from neighboring homes. Instead, they could not be visible from the
public right-of-way. That is, from the front of the home.

Commissioner McElreath felt that the minimum 50’ distance from neighboring dwellings
should be increased. He also wondered about possibly adding some restrictions
pertaining to noise from these vehicles.

Commissioner Wendel was not comfortable restricting a homeowner’s opportunity for
future residential development in favor of a commercial use in a residential neighborhood.
