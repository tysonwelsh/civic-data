Guiding Our Growth, A Statewide Conversation about Utah's Future -
Presented by the Governor's Office of Planning & Budget .

Laura Hansen of the Governor's Office of Planning and Budget used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss
growth projections for the State of Utah; she indicated that from 2010 to 2020, Utah was the fastest growing state in the nation and
continued growth is projected through the entire state. While growth is projected statewide, expectations vary for each county in
the State, and she presented a map illustrating 2060 population projections by county. Weber County is expected to have 133,000
new residents by 2060, while other areas are not projected to grow as fast. The focus of the presentation was how to manage this
growth in a responsible way; capitalizing on the economic benefits of growth; and acknowledging and planning for the challenges
brought by growth. The State has identified goals for guided growth, including:

e Educating Utahns on the effects of growth decisions;

e Encouraging Utahns to support quality growth principles;

e Soliciting input on Utahns' growth preferences;

e Generating a list of Big Ideas and guidance for decision makers; and
e Supporting existing local and regional planning efforts.

Ms. Hansen discussed phase one of public engagement regarding growth and indicated some of the questions that have

arisen throughout this phase include:
: e What can be done now to preserve Utah’s water resources?
Z e What can be done now to ensure future housing availability and affordability?
e What can be done now to make sure future Utahns can get from place to place efficiently?
e What can be done now to maintain the identity of communities?
e¢ What can be done now to protect open space for future Utahns to enjoy?

Answering these questions will be the focus of the State’s “Guiding Our Growth” project and the next phase of public
outreach efforts will be launched in phase two of the initiative. ( )

Throughout Ms. Hansen’s presentation, she engaged in discussion with the Council about topics like the ability of the .
State to pursue water conservation efforts during times of extreme growth; the relationship between housing density and water
use; the ability of each community to participate in the State’s initiative; the need for the State to consider laws that encourage
more owner occupancy and regulate uses like vacation rentals that detract from owner occupancy units in a community; the
impact of concentrated wealth in certain areas of the State; shifting demographics in some Utah communities; and collaboration
among multiple State agencies in responding to the impacts of growth.

FY2022-2023 Budget Amendment — New Revenues and Capital
Improvement Plan Project Reappropriations

Comptroller Stout provided an overview of revenues and expenditures involved in a proposed budget amendment totaling
$4,033,825. In the General Fund, the proposed budget amendment recognizes revenues for police operations and appropriates
those revenues to police uniform wages and benefits to provide an additional sergeant over school resource officers. General Fund
contingency funds are being re-appropriated to purchase five bullet-proof vests for code enforcement officers. B & C Road
contingency funds are being reappropriated to cover the bond payment for the Energy Savings Companies (ESCO) project. In the
CIP Fund, multiple CIP projects are being closed out with any remaining funds being transferred to cover overages in other
completed projects. Funds from an unexpended Airport project are being transferred to the CIP Fund for street reconstruction
projects. Remaining funds from completed CIP projects in the Airport, Water, Sanitary Sewer, and Storm Sewer are also being re-

appropriated to other projects. Internal Services funds—IT and Fleet--are recognizing revenue from current operations and
appropriating those funds for needed equipment.

There was brief discussion among the Council and City Administration regarding the implications of a few of the items
listed in the budget amendment, with Ms. Stout noting she can provide more detailed descriptions for a few of the proposed
adjustments at the time that the Council is scheduled to take action on the proposal; she concluded the recommendation is to hold
a public hearing on May 16.

Proposed Rezone — 236 Porter Avenue

Planning Manager Brierley explained a petitioner, Steve Diamond, has requested approval to rezone three parcels of
property at 236 Porter Avenue, from R-1-6 to R-1-5, to accommodate a single-family development comparable to the current | }
surrounding neighborhood. The proposed rezone sits on a 2.18-acre lot, hosting only a vacant church building which will be
demolished in the future. The petitioner desires to develop a single-family development on the property with similar lot widths
and lot area as the surrounding single-family neighborhood. The boundary of the rezone lays entirely within 2"! and 3™ Streets,
and Porter and Jefferson Avenues, respectively, and will follow the centerline of those adjacent streets. The petitioner also
requested that two smaller properties be rezoned, which separated from the area specified above, located on the south side of 3
Street. However, it is recommended. by the Planning Commission that these properties remain R-1-6, as the General Plan does not
support created isolated pockets of zoning. Consequently, there is no proposed action for these two smaller parcels of property as
a result of this petition. He concluded the Planning Commission reviewed the proposal to rezone the property at 236 Porter
Avenue from R-1-6 to R-1-5 and forwarded a unanimous recommendation of approval to the City Council.

Council Executive Director Eller-Smith noted that action on this issue is scheduled for May 16.

Proposed Text Amendment — Vacation Rentals

Planning Manager Brierley explained the petitioner, Snell & Wilmer Law (represented by Jason Boal, AICP), is
proposing to amend the residential vacation rental text to be able to use the newly restored Bristol Manor at 2480 25" Street as a
non-owner-occupied residential vacation rental. The current owners (Snell & Wilmer) want to sell the units and advertise them as
potential vacation rentals. Short-term vacation rentals have become a popular way to travel, including but not limited to the
following reasons: vicinity to attractions, convenience for large groups wanting to stay together, location is within a neighborhood
and not a busy commercial area. Many private property owners use their properties as a short-term rental unit to supplement their
income and reduce housing costs. Like Ogden, several cities along the Wasatch Front that are located near ski resorts and are
looking to balance the demand for short-term vacation rentals while protecting needed housing options such as motels and hotels.
This proposed zoning ordinance amendment is specifically intended for the Bristol Mansion, located at 2480 25th Street. The
intent is to amend the residential vacation rental ordinance to allow the newly renovated eight (8) unit condominium to be rented
as eight (8) individual nonowner-occupied residential vacation rentals. The current owners want to sell the individual
condominiums and want to advertise the condominiums as potential vacation rentals. Currently, the residential vacation rental
ordinance would only allow one of the condominium units to be used as a residential vacation rental due to the ordinance’s
spacing requirements, which allows one rental per block face. he proposed zoning ordinance amendment would allow uptoeight —{
(8) condominium units in a single building to be treated as a single residential vacation rental based on meeting specific
requirements. The petitioner’s proposed requirements are as follows:

The Planning Commission reviewed the proposal to amend the zone text amendment and forwarded a unanimous
recommendation of denial! to the City Council.
Chair Choberka acknowledged the petitioner and noted that it was her understanding that they only planned to convert a
few of the units to vacation rentals; she asked why they applied to use all eight units as vacation rentals. The Jason Boal,
representing the applicant, indicated that he did not apply for eight vacation rental units, but he was seeking flexibility when
pursuing the amendment and the change will apply to a building with up to eight units. If there is a desire to limit the number of
units that can be used at vacation rentals, he is happy to comply with that restriction. He added the owner has invested quite a bit ;
to restore this beautiful home and exploring the economic viability of retaining home ownership. It is condominiumized and the
owner does have the right to sell each unit separately, but that is not their intent, and they would like to maintain ownership if
possible.
Council Deputy Director Symes stated that Council staff has done some research into the property subject to this
application and found it is currently a legal non-conforming use. Mr. Brierley stated that is correct. Mr. Symes asked if it is
included on the local historic register, to which Mr. Brierley answered no. One of the possible reuses of the property is as a bed
and breakfast, but the applicant did not want to pursue that option. The owner could apply to be on the local historic register and :
would probably qualify and that could create some other options for them.
Chair Choberka inquired as to the history of the condominiumzation of the property. Mr. Brierley indicated he is not
familiar with the property’s history; in the East Central Neighborhoods, there are many homes that have been added onto in a poor
fashion or divided into different units in very odd ways. Council member White asked if the building could legally be used as
apartment units, to which Mr. Brierley answered yes. Council member White noted she is trying to figure out why the City would
allow apartments, but not short-term rentals. Discussion among the Council and staff centered on the reasoning behind the
Planning Commission’s recommendation of denial, with Mr. Brierley reminding the Council that this is a legislative item, and
they have the ability to act in a manner different than that recommended by the Planning Commission.
Council member White inquired as to whether the City receives complaints about existing vacation rental properties in
‘| the City. Mr. Brierley answered yes; those complaints are typically related to parking, noise from a party being held at the
} vacation rental, and owner’s not living on-site even though the City’s ordinance requires owner occupancy for units located in
single-family neighborhoods.
The Council then discussed and debated the merits of the application; they also allowed input from the applicant and
property owner, who communicated that he would be comfortable with operating just one vacation rental per each floor of the
building. Chair Choberka concluded that this item has not been scheduled for a future Council meeting based upon the Planning
Commission’s recommendation of denial and she encouraged the Council to consider the application further in preparation for
continued discussion of the issue.
