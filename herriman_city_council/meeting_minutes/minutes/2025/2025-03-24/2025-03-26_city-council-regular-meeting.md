      6. Discussion and Action Items
         5.2 Consideration to Approve the 2025 Mowing Services Contract with Huskie’z
              Landscaping
      Councilmember Shields voiced his reservations about approving the contract without seeing
      more concrete data, particularly regarding the claim that the current contract with Huskies
      was 20-30% cheaper than alternatives. He questioned how this figure was derived and
      expressed concern that the ongoing lack of formal reviews or RFP processes for contracts
      might result in missed opportunities for better pricing or services.

      Councilmember Henderson shared similar concerns and added that the public feedback
      received should be taken seriously but stating that he would still consider approving the
      current year's contract, given the timing. He emphasized the need for a formal review process
      for future contracts, whether through an RFP or an internal review, and mentioned that
      residents’ concerns about service quality also weighed on his decision-making process. City
      Manager Nathan Cherpeski mentioned the City's new purchasing policy, which mandated
      that contracts exceeding five years must go through an RFP process, though this contract
      was negotiated under the old system. Finance Director Kyle Maurer also explained that the
      contract was under the previous process but mentioned that pricing had been regularly
      reviewed by reaching out to neighboring agencies and contractors.

      Deputy Director of Parks, Recreation and Events Anthony Teuscher addressed some of the
      concerns by explaining that, despite not conducting formal RFPs, the City had continuously
      sought informal bids and found that the current pricing was highly competitive, even when
      compared to nearby agencies. He noted that one quote received was 20% higher and another
      was 50% higher. He also shared that Huskies' pricing had impressed even vendors offering
      autonomous mowers, who could not compete with the current contract. He suggested that
      moving forward with the current contract was beneficial but acknowledged that a formal
      review process would be valuable in the future. He raised concerns that going out to an RFP
      could cause the contractor to inflate their prices, ultimately leading to higher costs for the
      City.

      Councilmember Shields continued to stress that the contract should not be approved simply
      because it was the status quo. He argued that the City needed more solid evidence to justify
      the pricing and service levels before entering into a long-term agreement. Councilmember
      Henderson concurred with this sentiment but pointed out the importance of adhering to the
      new purchasing policies and incorporating a more structured process for reviewing
      contracts. Councilmember Ohrn noted that while it might be easy to stick with familiar

          5.4 Letter of Support for the Utah BEAD program to expand services into
                underserved areas in Herriman
      Councilmember Shields noted for the past five years, he has advocated for a better internet
      solution for Herriman residents. He relayed his deep concern regarding the request of the
      letter when the for profit organization was opposed to the City pursing the establishment of
      a municipal broadband service, arguing that taxpayer funds should never be used to build
      internet infrastructure. He opined their position was presented as morally objectionable and
      fiscally irresponsible. Yet, less than a year later, the same organization is requesting taxpayer
      money to build out their own network, which would allow them to profit from service the
      Herriman community. Councilmember Shields found this contradiction problematic. He
      acknowledged the request being made but did not believe that taxpayer funds should be used
      to support the expansion of a for-profit network, especially when the idea of a municipal
      broadband service was previously framed as morally unacceptable. Councilmember Shields
      relayed his desire not to hinder the residents’ access to service but could not support the
      letter and requested the Council consider the inconsistencies carefully before moving
      forward.

      Councilmember Henderson asked for clarification of the program to which City Manager
      Nathan Cherpeski responded that the request was for a Federal Grant Program and explained
      that any private company or entity could apply for the funds, which is the reasoning behind
      the request being in front of the Legislative body for consideration.

      Mayor Palmer noted that the program only reimbursed service providers for 30% of the cost,
      and the beneficiaries would have to cover the remaining 70%. Councilmember Shields pointed
      out that the state had identified 36 underserved areas, but the benefits of the program were
      not clear, especially since some of those areas might be non-residential, like storage facilities.

      Councilmember Henderson noted the challenge of understanding how these 36 areas would
      actually benefit from the proposed infrastructure. There was a push to understand the details
      of how the expansion would affect residents, as the City had put considerable time and effort
      into past efforts to improve the City's internet service, which had faced opposition from
      providers. He was particularly skeptical about granting a provider exclusive access to the
      funds, fearing it would limit future options for residents.

      Comcast Business Development Manager Steve Porter explained the process, clarifying that
      the funds would only be used for infrastructure in the designated 36 underserved areas. He
      also emphasized that providers, including Comcast, were competing for the funding, and if
      another provider were to win, they would handle the expansion instead. He reassured that
      the funds would go only toward building out in these areas, although the exact number of
      impacted residences or businesses remained unclear. He mentioned that Comcast had
      committed to providing substantial funding beyond the required 20%, promising speeds of up
      to gigabit levels for five years in the expanded areas. He also highlighted Comcast’s long-
      standing "Internet Essentials" program, offering discounted service to qualifying households.

      Councilmember Shields expressed concern that the lack of clarity about the areas and the
      actual impact on residents made it difficult to fully support the proposal. He reiterated the
      city's past issues with the provider but stated that his priority was to provide residents with
      better internet options moving forward.

      Councilmember Ohrn moved to continue the Letter of Support for the Utah BEAD program
      to expand services into underserved areas in Herriman to the April 9, 2025 City Council
      meeting. Councilmember Shields seconded the motion.

      Councilmember Teddy Hodges                     Absent
      Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn                     Aye
      Councilmember Steven Shields                   Aye
      Mayor Lorin Palmer                             Aye

      The motion passed unanimously with Councilmember Hodges and being absent.

          6.5. Consideration of a cooperative agreement with the Utah Department of
               Transportation regarding the funding and construction of Herriman Boulevard
               from 6800 West to U-111 – Blake Thomas, Community Development Director
      Community Development Director Blake Thomas presented information about the
      cooperative agreement with UDOT for the construction of Herriman Boulevard. He explained
      that UDOT would transfer responsibility for this section to Herriman City, along with up to
      $19 million in funding. He oriented the Council to the location of the project noting it would
      include a five-lane section and three signalized intersections, one of which will be built by
      UDOT, while the other two will be constructed as part of the agreement. The newly
      constructed road would serve the development in Olympia and the commercial center north
      of the road, with plans to tie into the Rose Basin area via 7300 West.

      Director Thomas addressed questions about potential cost overruns, stating that any
      additional costs would be covered by the Olympia Public Infrastructure District. In response
      to concerns about budgeting, he relayed his confidence in working with them and the funding
      would adequately cover the project. He explained the project aligned with the City's strategic
      plan of seeking outside funding sources for infrastructure projects, which would allow
      Herriman to manage the construction work effectively while adhering to UDOT's design and
      construction rules.

      Councilmember Shields moved to approve Resolution No. R18-2025 approving the execution
      of a cooperative agreement with the Utah Department of Transportation providing for the
      transfer of up to $19,000,000 for the design and construction of Herriman Boulevard from
      6800 West to U-111. Councilmember Henderson seconded the motion.
