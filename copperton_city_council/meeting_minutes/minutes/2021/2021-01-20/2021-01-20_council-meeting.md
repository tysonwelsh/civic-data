Council Goals for 2021

                Mayor Clayton stated he would like to set at least three goals for 2021. He
recommended the first goal of reviewing all County ordinances and making them specific to
Copperton. The Council could hold some working sessions with everyone in the same room while
social distancing.

               Council Member Sorensen stated that was an expedited way of doing it.

               Nathan Bracken stated the Council could work with the Municipal Services District
(MSD) staff to review land use ordinances. Most of Title 19 could be deleted to make it more
Copperton friendly. It might be easier to take smaller town codes and use them as the starting
point.

              Council Member Stitzer asked if the Council met in a working session, would it
be considered a regular meeting that needed to be noticed.

               Mr. Bracken stated yes. The specific definition of a meeting is one where a
quorum is present, and the meeting involves something that will come before the Council for a
decision. He suggested the Council designate a committee of no more than two Council Members
to work on the ordinances and report back to the Council.

               Mikala Jordan, Municipal Services District (MSD), stated they were looking to
create a working group to deal with all Title 19 land use ordinances.

               Mr. Bracken stated the ordinance will be different between the metro townships.
He is looking at general changes with the MSD that will work for all metros and then getting into
the Copperton specific issues.

              Mayor Clayton stated he will put together some ideas and the Council can decide
at the next meeting how it would like to proceed.

              Council Member Stitzer stated she would like to set a goal of working with Rio
Tinto to expand Copperton through annexations.

              Council Member Bailey stated the arborist will be submitting a plan to upgrade
the park. She would like to set a goal of implementing some of the plan.

            Mayor Clayton stated he would draft an email listing these goals and asked
Council Members to consider which committees they would like to serve on.

COVID Update

                 Mayor Clayton stated he spoke with Salt Lake County Mayor Jenny Wilson last
week. She reached out to ask what the County could do for Copperton’s aging population. She
is willing to set aside vaccines for Copperton’s over-70 population. They also discussed the
frustration of trying to register for an appointment on the website.

A second tranche of CARES funding may be coming from the federal government. It would be
the same amount and same provisions as last time. He asked that the County send Copperton
an agreement if the funding materializes.

               Council Member Stitzer asked about the location of a vaccination clinic in
Copperton.

              Mayor Clayton stated the Lion’s Club is closed to the public but perhaps it can
open for one day. The water board may also have some space. If none is available, the County
Health Department can bring a mobile unit.

               Council Member Stitzer stating putting links to vaccine information on the
township website was a good idea, but the Council should be non-biased with its approach. They
are providing the vaccine, not pushing it. She found the vaccine manufacturers’ websites to be
the most informative.

              Council Member Olsen stated any vaccine event should be promoted in the
newsletter and on social media.

               Mayor Clayton stated the township religious organizations probably have phone
numbers for all senior citizens within their group. Help could also be offered to fill out the forms.

              Council Member Stitzer asked if the Council would be enforcing restrictions on
age and residence if and when vaccines are distributed.

                Nathan Bracken stated the Salt Lake County Health Department would be doing
all the administrative work. The Council would only have to get the word out.

            Mayor Clayton stated next week he is volunteering at a vaccine clinic at the
Mountain America Expo Center to become familiar with the process.

                Council Member Bailey noted that Frequently Asked Questions about vaccines
is available on the County Health Department website.

Committee/Board Updates

        Legislative Research Committee

               Nathan Bracken reported on the following bills being proposed to the State
Legislature:

                                                 4
DATE            WEDNESDAY                             JANUARY                             20, 2021


SB 58: Sponsored by Senator Karen Mayne, this bill would give metro townships the authority to
impose municipal energy or telecommunication taxes. This could help the Municipal Services
District to close a $35 million structural deficit.

HB 98: This is a bad bill. It is being pushed by the Home Builders Association and would allow
builders to do their own site plan reviews and inspections. There would be no independent
inspector. It would also remove the ability for municipalities to enact building design requirements
or elements or landscaping requirement. This bill is opposed by all municipalities.

HB 94: This bill concerns micro enterprises and home kitchens. There are health concerns with
this bill because the local health department would not be involved in regular inspections.

Due to the pandemic, this legislative session will be a weird one with the potential for bad bills to
get farther along. He will meet regularly with the Southwest Mayor’s group to follow the legislation.

            Council Member Bailey stated she emailed the USA’s Legislative Watch list to all
Council Members. If anyone sees any triggers, please let her know.

Other Announcements

               Nathan Bracken stated the point of most annexation efforts is business
development and economic growth. He suggested the Council work with the MSD and Rio Tinto
and the finance folks. Creating a Redevelopment Agency (RDA) will quell most fears about giving
the metro townships more taxing authority.

He reminded Council Members that they must do an annual Open and Public Meetings Act
training. Doing it online will produce a certificate that is often requested by the Auditor’s Office.

                 Mayor Clayton stated he would send out the link for training. He will also send
out conflict of interest forms and would like them back by the end of the month.

                                 −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
               Council Member Stitzer stated there is a pole installed across the street from the
Amazon warehouse on Old Bingham Highway in South Jordan. This has created a dangerous
situation. She witnessed a truck that had to move onto the media to avoid hitting a bicyclist. She
would like to call South Jordan authorities but wonders if that should be done as a private citizen
or as a Copperton Council Member.

              Council Member Olsen stated in a different incident he called the West Jordan
Mayor about a spill from a dump truck that had covered the road. It was cleaned up that afternoon.
As a Council Member she can call and discuss a problem.

                 Nathan Bracken stated if Council Member Stitzer was commenting in her personal
capacity, she should not mention that she is a Council Member. Council cannot take action
tonight, but this could be put on the next agenda for Council consideration.

                 Mayor Clayton stated he will put this matter on the agenda for next month and
write a letter to South Jordan. He asked Council Member Stitzer to work on the language for the
letter.

               Council Member Bailey stated this falls in line with the desire to have an active
transportation route between Copperton and the West Jordan Trax line.

               Mayor Clayton said such a trail would keep people and bikers off that portion of
the road.
