Proposal 
The applicant is proposing to modify the existing building pad located at 7068 Canyon Drive 
(PB-PR-110) in order to reduce driveway length and keep the construction footprint closer to 
the road, thus limiting the disturbance to the natural environment and lessening the size and 
number of retaining walls.  The proposed amendment would meet front, side, and rear 
Snyderville Basin setback requirements and would maintain existing public utilities easements 
as they were originally platted.   
 
The proposed plat, Amended Plat of Lot 110 of Pineridge Subdivision, is attached in Appendix A.

Background 
On March 3, 2023, the Community Development Department received a Plat Amendment 
application to modify the building pad of Lot 110 of the Pineridge Subdivision.  The Pineridge 
Subdivision was originally recorded in 1995. Lots within the Pineridge Subdivision include 
“Line(s) of Building Zone Typical” which amount to building pads, as defined by the Snyderville 
Basin Development Code: 
BUILDING SITE, BUILDING PAD: That part of a parcel of land designed to be occupied by 
the principal building and any accessory buildings. 
 
Lot 110 contains significant slopes and much of the lot is sloped greater than 30%.  Typically, 
development would not be allowed in areas where slope exceeds 30%, but there is an 
exception in section 10-4-3 which states “In the event a legally defined parcel or access to a 
legal lot of record consists of critical lands and there are not permissible locations to develop on 
the parcel, then the property owner will be allowed to locate development on the parcel in the 
most sensitive manner for both access and structure location.” Both the existing and proposed 
building pads contain slopes greater than 30%.  The only areas of the lot (outside of the existing 
building pad) that do not contain slopes greater than 30% are within a snow storage easement. 
 
The applicants’ goal is to locate development toward the western edge, or front property line, 
of Lot 110 in order to limit the length of driveway needed to access the build site, minimize the 
amount excavation, reduce the number and size of retaining walls, and lessen the amount of 
disturbance to native vegetation.  See Appendix B for a slope analysis of the site and Appendix 
C for a site constraints illustration.   
 
The applicant has provided the following information: 
“Based on multiple studies, we’ve determined that the current build envelope is not 
best suited for the most sensible construction, when taking into consideration the 
existing topography. 
 
With the current build envelope starting at roughly 7353, approximately 20’ below the 
road, and dropping to roughly 7324 at the east end, development will require 
substantial fill as well as massive retaining walls. Building this deep into the valley will 
also result in extensive disruption to the natural vegetation. 
 
We propose a substantially narrower and slightly longer build envelope, limiting 
development closer to the road. This will result in a much shorter driveway, more 
compact retaining walls, and substantially less disruption to natural vegetation. The 
proposed build envelope will be +/- 7,900 sf, similar in size to the current +/-7,906 
envelope” and will meet all required setbacks.   
 
The Pineridge HOA has expressed support of this proposed change.   The applicant has also 
worked extensively with the Summit County Engineering Department to ensure that access is 
safe and suitably located.

Analysis and Findings 
Section 1 0-3-18 of the Snyderville Basin  Development Code sets the following “Grounds For 
Vacating Or Changing A Plat:” 
 
1. If the county manager or land use authority is satisfied that neither the public nor any 
person will be materially injured by the proposed vacation, alteration, or amendment, and 
that there is good cause for the vacation, alteration or amendment, the count y manager 
or land use authority may vacate, alter, or amend the plat, any portion of the plat, or any 
road or lot. 
 
Analysis: The proposed amendment would locate development toward the western edge, 
or front property line, of Lot 110 in order to limit the length of driveway needed to access 
the build site, minimize the amount excavation, reduce the number and size of retaining 
walls, and lessen the amount of disturbance to native vegetation. 
 
2. No plat amendment shall be approved which results in an increase in density. 
 
Analysis: The amendment adjusts an existing building pad.   No new density is  being 
created. COMPLIES

Summary 
The project area consists of approximately 30 acres adjacent to Interstate 80 and accessed from 
Silver Creek Road at the Interstate 40 interchange. The development itself is connected via 
frontage road and is otherwise divided into an Eastern section and Western section. The 
Eastern section was originally plated for Commercial uses, is zoned Rural Residential, and is 
proposed for mixed uses, as identified and supported in the Snyderville Basin General Plan. The 
Western section was originally plated for Industrial uses, is zoned Rural Residential, and is 
proposed as residential use.

project for both the Eastern and Western parcels.  The updated proposal is outlined below:

Per section 10-2-17 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code, the purpose of the 
Neighborhood Mixed-Use zone (NMU-1) is to “create development and redevelopment areas 
that are: mixed use, pedestrian-focused, and transit-oriented.”  Additionally, “the desired 
development pattern of the NMU-1 zone shall be facilitated through flexible site and 
community design principles that: 
a) Result in a mix of land uses that are compatible, mutually supportive, well connected 
through a variety of transportation choices; 
b) Result in convenient, safe, direct, and well-connected transit and pedestrian linkages; 
c) Result in a reduction in the need for parking;

d) Result in compact development that creates public spaces, including but not limited to 
plazas and other outdoor amenity areas; and 
e) Result in sustainable infrastructure and building design consistent with best practices.” 
 
 
Section 10-2-17 also describes rezoning eligibility for consideration for a rezone to NMU-1: 
1. The property(s) must be designated for mixed-use on the Snyderville Basin General Plan, 
Future Land Use Map; 
2. The property(s) must be adjacent to (or a redevelopment of) existing commercial, 
mixed-use, or institutional/civic development: 
3. The property(s) must be located along an existing transit system or within the County 
Master Planned Development process, receive a written commitment to provide service 
from the County Regional Transportation Planning Director. 
4. Property subject to the terms and conditions of any prior development agreement, 
settlement agreement or similar instrument shall remain subject to those terms and 
conditions until such time as the prior agreement is amended or terminated as part of 
the Master Planned Development Process.

a) The amendment complies with the goals, objectives and policies of the general plan, the

burdensome on the local community;

Kimball Junction Plan.  The most recent comprehensive update was in 2015.  As with all General 
Plans in the state of Utah, the Snyderville Basin General Plan serves as an advisory document 
that provides direction and focus for the community.     
 
Section 10-9a-403 of the Utah State Code requires that a general plan include, at minimum,  
- A land use element 
- A transportation and traffic circulation element 
- A moderate-income housing element  
- A water use and preservation element (recently added) 
 
Chapter 2 of the Snyderville Basin General Plan discusses land use.

EXHIBIT A 
 
PROCESS OPTIONS FOR THE MPD / REZONE APPLICATION 
1. A single MPD application with two separate zoning districts. 
o  NMU-1 and RR zones 
o  NMU-1 and NC zones 
2. Two separate MPD applications. 
3. A single MPD application with one zoning district, which includes a text amendment to allow for 
detached single family dwellings. 
Option 1 [PREFERRED OPTION] 
A single MPD application with two separate zoning districts. 
 
Based on 10-2-18-C and 10-2-18-E-2-b, the MPD application can include more than one zoning district.   As a result, 
the proposal could include a rezone for the Eastern portion to NMU -1 and the Western portion could either 
remain Rural Residential (see below) or be rezoned to Neighborhood Commercial to allow for the single family 
detached dwelling use. 
 
If the Western portion remains under the Rural Residential zone, the additional density could be relocated to the 
zone via 10-2-18-E-2-a/b.  In other words, the density could be granted under the NMU-1 zone via a site suitability 
analysis, then transferred to the adjacent Rural Residential zoning district, as permitted in the code.   Under 10-2-
18-E-2-d, any density associated with deed restricted affordable housing unit s within an MPD is exempt from 
density calculations.  Note that this approach is more consistent with similar neighborhoods in Summit County, 
such as Blackhawk Station and all of Pinebrook/Jeremy Ranch (all of which are zoned RR and have similar 
densities).  This approach has the added value of retaining the restrictions in the RR zone without any additional 
language in the Development Agreement. 
 
If the MPD application contemplates rezoning the Western portion to Neighborhood Commercial instead, this zone 
would allow single family detached dwelling units under a low impact permit, provided that the floor area does not 
exceed 5,000 SF (10-2-9-B).  Theoretically, the other non-desirable neighborhood commercial uses (such as Retail 
Sales) could be further restricted from this area via Development Agreement.  This approach also appears viable as 
an Option 1 alternative.  
 
Option 2 
Two separate MPD applications. 
 
While this approach could be viable to separate the rezoning applications, we would need the planning 
commission to waive 10-2-18-E-1, which appears to require a single MPD application for all contiguous affected 
properties owned by the same legal owner.   
 
Provided that the planning commission waived 10-2-18-E-1, the concept of two applications would still need to 
address the rezoning options noted above.  In other words, it's double the process with the same end result.  That 
said, this is a viable option to consider if two separate applications are determined to be the right approach.   
 
Option 3 
A single MPD application with one zoning district, which includes a text amendment to allow for detached single 
family dwellings. 
 
A text amendment to the NMU-1 zone could be processed simultaneously with a single rezone MPD applicati on for 
the entire property to NMU-1.  However, such text amendment, if approved, would then be available to any other 
developer processing an NMU-1 rezone application.  We believe the County got the NMU-1 zone right and 
changing the text to allow for single family detached dwelling units that are likely a unicorn scenario in our case 
does not feel appropriate for the overall zone.  In other words, the physical configuration of our project, effectively 
separated by a single family home subdivision and connected by a privately -owned roadway, is unlikely to be 
encountered in any other development projects that could be rezoned to NMU -1.
