 1   Affordable housing is defined by the United States Department of Housing and Urban Development
 2   (“HUD”) as housing that the occupant only needs to spend up to 30% of their gross monthly income
 3   on. That 30% should cover the rent or mortgage, property taxes or escrow, insurance, and utilities.
 4   The median income in Salt Lake County is $115,500 per household. Bluffdale’s median income is
 5   approximately $4,000 higher, but County figures are used for reporting purposes. An affordable
 6   housing expense is just over $2,400 per month, which equates to a $450,000 home. Ms. Tubbs
 7   reported that it is less and less common to see homes offered at that price point.
 8
 9   The General Plan is required to have a Moderate Income Housing Element. The State mandates that
10   Bluffdale report on moderate income housing, which is defined as housing that is reserved for
11   households with a gross income of up to 80% of the area median income. As part of the reporting
12   requirements, cities must choose three menu items to focus on. Bluffdale focuses on the following:
13
14          E.      Create or allow for and reduce regulations related to internal or detached
15                  accessory dwelling units in residential zones. The accessory dwelling unit (“ADU”)
16                  Code was recently updated to allow for detached units, and Staff is working on an
17                  ADU Toolkit.
18
19          F.      Zone or rezone for higher density or moderate income residential development in
20                  commercial or mixed-use zones near major transit investment corridors,
21                  commercial centers, or employment centers. Several special district zones have
22                  come before the Council to request entitlements for higher density housing types that
23                  are close to Redwood Road or Porter Rockwell Boulevard, where Staff anticipates
24                  major transportation investments and transit improvements to be made in the future.
25
26   In response to a question, Ms. Tubbs reported that the minimum size for a Mixed-Use Special District
27   Zone is 10 acres. There is not a minimum size for reporting purposes. As long as the City was making
28   progress toward that goal, the State has accepted it in the past. Mr. Crowell added that reviewers ask
29   a lot of detailed questions about how the City is furthering each goal, and it can be challenging to
30   determine what they are looking for. Ms. Tubbs clarified that the City is able to report on the matter
31   for one year after improvements or progress has been made.

31   Assistant City Engineer, Dan Tracer, presented the Staff Report and noted that the City’s Quality
32   Assurance Program allows Staff to inspect and test construction work that will be dedicated to the


                                                        23
                             DRAFT – FOR DISCUSSION PURPOSES ONLY

                                       BLUFFDALE CITY COUNCIL
                                          MEETING MINUTES
                                       Wednesday, February 12, 2025

 1   City. Mr. Tracer gave the analogy of baking cookies: If you do not follow the recipes, the cookies
 2   will not turn out. The same applies to concrete.
 3
 4   If any of the multiple ingredients in concrete is off in quantity or quality, it can ruin the entire mix.
 5   To mitigate that, the ingredients list is reviewed before the mix gets to the jobsite. Concrete will
 6   always crack, but they try to minimize and ensure that it cracks in the right location. Mr. Tracer
 7   presented a photograph of a crack in concrete and noted that it was repaired by grinding and filling
 8   with an epoxy crack sealer to prevent water from getting into it.
 9
10   Concrete also needs a sturdy bed. It is great in compression, but not in tension. If it sits on a cliff,
11   the edge piece is in tension and will crack and fall off. It needs a proper, level base to provide the
12   necessary strength. Mr. Tracer presented an image of a bed that was prepared with improperly
13   compacted topsoil that included organic material that should have been removed, and reported that
14   the concrete would fail immediately. That is mitigated by the City inspectors inspecting the base to
15   ensure that it is properly compacted and has the strength it needs. Additional quality testing is also
16   done to verify the results.
17
18   Concrete often contains rebar, which does not do well with moisture as it rusts and expands when
19   wet. Mr. Tracer presented a photograph of severely cracked concrete that resulted from wet rebar.
20   To mitigate that, the inspectors verify that the rebar is epoxy coated so that it will not rust if exposed
21   to moisture.
22
23   Poor mixing and curing is another issue. If the workers are continuously troweling or brooming the
24   concrete, it can cause the ingredients to separate close to the surface. After a few months or weeks,

29   Program. It requires that the contractor provide submittals to verify that all materials are adequate for
30   the job, conduct independent testing, and provide proof that the testing was completed by a certified
31   laboratory.

EXHIBIT A
                                        Metro Fire Agency
                                       Interlocal Agreement
        This Interlocal Agreement (the "Agreement") is entered into by and among Bluffdale
City, a municipal corporation of the State of Utah; Draper City, a municipal corporation of the
State of Utah; Murray City, a municipal corporation of the State of Utah; Sandy City, a
municipal corporation of the State of Utah; City of South Jordan, a municipal corporation of the
State of Utah; South Salt Lake City, a municipal corporation of the State of Utah; West Jordan
City, a municipal corporation of the State of Utah; and West Valley City, a municipal
corporation of the State of Utah, which may hereinafter be collectively referred to as "parties" or
individually as a "party."
        Whereas, the parties have determined that a separate interlocal entity known as the Metro
Fire Agency (the "Agency") will assist in furthering the protection of the citizens of their
respective cities and neighboring communities; and
        Whereas, Agency will allow for increased benefits regarding purchasing, mutual aid
assistance, and efficient use of resources to the parties and the citizens of their cities and
neighboring communities; and
        Whereas, the parties' currently have mutual aid and automatic agreements and systems in
place to assist with the provision of fire service to citizens and such agreements have
demonstrated the parties' ability to work together in an effective and efficient manner; and
         Whereas, the parties desire to broaden the scope of their cooperation to other beneficial areas
of their operations including the formation, sponsorship and operation of task forces, as needed and
permitted by law, to complement the services otherwise provided under this Agreement; and
      Whereas, each of the parties have participated in the discussion and negotiation of this
Agreement;
       Now therefore, in consideration of the mutual promises and covenants herein, the parties
agree as follows:


  1. Purpose. The purpose of this Agreement is to promote the health, safety, and welfare of the
     collective citizens of the parties, to provide improved fire protection for the participating
     municipalities and to provide immediate unified and cooperative action to guard against
     potential multiple threats to individual cities.

          a. The parties declare that there is a community-wide need to provide for an
             interlocal fire agency and declare that this compelling need requires a state-of-the-
             art "all hazards" emergency response system. Such a system requires the creation of
             a Metro Fire Agency under the Utah Interlocal Cooperation Act, Title 11, Chapter
             13, Utah Code Annotated (Interlocal Act). This Agency will allow all
             participating municipalities to combine and share their collective capabilities and
             resources for themselves and their neighbors.

          b. This Agency will further allow the parties, as well as non-participating public agencies
               to this Agreement outside the geographical limits of the Agency, to form, sponsor and
               continue task forces as needed, including a specially trained urban search and rescue
               team known as Task Force 5, all of which are intended to complement the services
               otherwise provided under this Agreement.

           c. The Agency and its associated task force(s) may operate anywhere within or
              without the boundaries of the State of Utah, subject to governing state or federal
              laws, rules, regulations or compacts, in furtherance of the purposes outlined in this
              Agreement.

       It is the purpose of this Agreement to provide for the accomplishment of these purposes
       in a manner provided herein.

   2. Authority.

           a. The Interlocal Act permits local governmental units to make the most efficient use of
              their powers and to provide the benefit of economies of scale; authorizes
              municipalities to enter into cooperative agreements with one another for the purpose
              of exercising, on a joint and cooperative basis, any powers, privileges, and authority
              exercised by such public agencies individually; and authorizes such public agencies,
              pursuant to such agreements, to create a separate legal entity to accomplish the
              purposes of their joint cooperative action.

           b. This agreement supersedes all prior interlocal agreements concerning the
              Agency.

           c. This Agreement ratifies all actions taken by the parties to this Agreement since
              2016 in conformity with and pursuant to the express, implied, or apparent
              authority of the Agency not to include illegal or intentionally wrongful conduct.

3. Consideration. The consideration of this agreement consists of the mutual benefits and
   exchange of promises provided herein.

4. Effective Date. This Agreement shall become effective when all the parties have signed it.
   The date the Agreement is signed by the last party to sign it (as indicated by the date stated
   opposite that party’s signature) will be deemed the Effective Date of this Agreement. As
   soon as practical after the last party has signed, the host agency shall advise the parties in
   writing of the Effective Date.

5. Term. The term of this Agreement shall commence on the Effective Date and shall
   terminate fifty (50) years from the Effective Date unless dissolved earlier pursuant to
   paragraph 16.

6. Name. The name of the legal entity is the Metro Fire Agency (the “Agency”).

7. Governance. The Agency shall be governed by a Board of Trustees (“Trustees”) which shall
have sole authority to conduct the business of the Agency.

       a. Trustee membership shall consist of each party's chief executive officer or
          designee. At the discretion of a party's chief executive officer each party may
          designate an alternate trustee.

       b. Trustees are responsible for all decisions related to the organizational, operational,
          and financial conduct of the Agency. Trustees will have responsibility for
          overseeing reciprocity within the Agency.

       c. Trustee decisions shall be based on a majority vote of its members. Each
          member city shall have one vote.

       d. If a Trustee representing a party does not agree with a majority decision made by the
          Trustees, that Trustee may (through written notice provided pursuant to paragraph
          25) abstain from participating in the specific issue being addressed and that party
          will not be bound by that decision. Any written notice of abstention shall be
          provided within thirty (30) days of the Trustees voting to take the action.

       e. Trustee officers shall consist of a chair, vice-chair and secretary, and shall be
          elected by the Trustees among its members and shall serve for such terms and
          perform such duties as provided in the bylaws

       f. The chair shall be the presiding officer of the Trustees, and the vice-chair shall
          serve at the request of the chair or in the absence of the chair. The secretary shall
          keep minutes of the Trustees' meetings and shall attest to the signature of the
          chair as needed. Trustees may also appoint additional officers and
          representatives, and may assign duties to existing officers, as it deems necessary
          for the administration of the Agency.

       g. Trustees shall have regular meetings as needed or as provided in any bylaws and
          may have electronic meetings subject to the requirements of state law and any
          bylaws. Meetings will be held at a location to be determined by the Trustees.

       h. The chair shall give reasonable notice to all Trustees of the time and place of each
          meeting.

       i. Trustees may establish from time to time standing or ad-hoc committees as shall be
          deemed appropriate or necessary to carry out the business of the Agency.

       j. There shall be one host agency for administrative purposes. Such administrative
          purposes include, but are not limited to, assisting the elected chair of the Board
          of Trustees, hosting Agency meetings, providing public notice of Agency
          meetings, preparing and maintaining Agency documentation and records, and
          administering record requests pursuant to the Utah Governmental Records
          Access and Management Act. The host agency shall be the City employing the
          elected chair of the Board of Trustees unless otherwise designated by the
           Trustees.

       k. Trustees is a public body and its members shall in all respects follow the
          requirements of the Open and Public Meetings laws, Title 52, Chapter 4, Utah
          Code Annotated, the Government Records Management Act, Title 63G, Chapter
          2, Utah Code Annotated, all other applicable laws.

       l. Trustees shall have the power to adopt, amend, and repeal rules, bylaws,
          policies and procedures to regulate the affairs and conduct the business of the
          Agency.

8. Operations Advisory Committee. In addition to any standing or ad-hoc committee that
   the Trustees may deem appropriate or necessary to carry out the business of the
   Agency, an Operations Advisory Committee ("Operations") shall be established under
   the direction and supervision of the Trustees.

       a. Operations membership shall be composed of a designee from each party.

       b. Operations shall provide advice and recommendations to the Trustees for
          planning, budget preparation, system coordination, policies, procedures, and
          standards utilized by the Agency, and may be given other responsibilities and
          authority as approved by the Trustees.

       c. Operations decisions shall be based on a majority vote of its members. Each
          member city shall have one vote.

       d. Operations officers shall consist of a chair and vice chair, who shall be elected
          by Operations from among its members and shall serve for such term and
          perform such duties as shall be provided in the bylaws. Operations may also
          appoint additional officers and representatives as it deems necessary for the
          administration of its duties

       e. Operations shall meet as provided in the bylaws or as determined by the
          Operations membership.

       f. Operations is a public body and its members shall in all respects follow the
          requirements of the Open and Public Meeting laws, Title 52, Chapter 4, Utah
          Code Annotated, and Government Records Access Management Act, Title 63G,
          Chapter 2, Utah Code Annotated, and all other applicable laws.

9. Party Control. Each party shall continue to control, own, and maintain its individual fire
   facilities, apparatus, and equipment at its sole expense. Although Agency-wide
   purchasing processes and Agency-wide contracts may be used, each party shall
   continue to maintain its separate purchasing processes. In addition, purchasing alliances
   may be formed among some or all of the parties for submitting bids to vendors. Each
   party shall continue to handle its own human resource functions to include payroll or
   benefits, personnel and staffing decisions, and employee compensation with respect to
   its own employees.

       a. The Agency, in making decisions that impact the organizational and functionality
          of local fire departments representing each party, acknowledge the right of each
          party to choose whether or not to participate in recommendations that are made
          and accepted by the Trustees.

       b. The Agency may contract with any person or entity for the provision of services
          and materials in compliance with contracting and purchasing policies established by
          the Trustees, including legal and accounting services

10. Agency Services.

       a. The Agency, as determined by the Trustees, may provide to each party
          emergency and non-emergency services which the Agency has the capability of
          providing, to include, but not limited to, firefighting, emergency medical
          response, hazardous materials response, bomb response, search and rescue,
          technical rescue, fire and safety prevention, environmental protection, and public
          education.

       b. It is acknowledged and agreed that the parties have previously been operating a task
          force known as Task Force 5. Task Force 5 consists of individuals with special
          skills, qualifications, training, knowledge and/or experience required for urban
          search and rescue operations.

               i. Task Force 5 is hereby officially recognized and is authorized to participate
                  in interstate and intrastate urban search and rescue operations in accordance
                  with the Utah Emergency Management Act, Utah Code Ann. §§ 53-2a-101,
                  et. seq., and other governing state and federal law.

              ii. The Agency shall serve as the sponsoring agency for Task Force 5. The
                  Agency shall operate Task Force 5 consistent with the purposes of the
                  Agency. Task Force 5 shall be subject to and operate in accordance with
                  this Agreement, Agency bylaws and applicable state and federal laws.

              iii. Task Force 5 is authorized to recruit from the parties to this Agreement
                   and from non-participating public agencies outside the geographical
                   limits of the Agency. Task Force 5 shall recruit individuals with the
                   requisite skills, qualifications, training, knowledge, and/or experience to
                   participate with Task Force 5. Non-participating public agencies who
                   want to join Task Force 5 shall execute a memorandum of understanding
                   with the Agency that will govern their participation

              iv. The Board of Trustees shall designate periodically a Task Force 5
                  Program Manager.

11. Financial Matters.
       a. Budget Adoption. All financial matters of the Agency shall be conducted in
          accordance with applicable Utah State laws and generally acceptable accounting
          principles. Trustees shall prepare an annual budget which shall include: (1) a
          proposed staffing schedule identifying all positions and titles of employees and
          officers employed and paid by the Agency; (2) a compensation schedule with
          pay grades for each employee or officer paid by the Agency; (3) a reasonably
          detailed identification of the source and amount of each anticipated revenue
          source of the Agency, including each fee or assessment to be made up the
          parties; (4) an organizational chart of the Agency; and (5) the Agency's mission
          statement, a brief summary of the immediate last year's accomplishments, and a
          statement of the budget year's goals and objectives.

       b. Fees and assessments. Fees and assessments may be proposed as part of the
          budgeting process by a 2/3 vote of the trustees in a duly noticed public meeting
          and thereafter, forwarded in writing to each party's legislative body no later than
          May 15th, before the party’s budget for the following year is adopted. No fee or
          assessment against a party will be valid or enforceable until that party has made
          an appropriation of funds to pay such an assessment.

       c. Rules and Procedures for Fees and Assessments. The Trustees, by a 2/3 vote,
          may adopt rules and procedures to assure the proper collection of approved fees
          and assessments, consistent with the Agreement.

       d. Party Non-appropriation as Withdrawal. In the event a party fails to appropriate
          and timely pay to the Agency the fees and assessments adopted by the Trustees
          and as provided in the Agency's duly adopted rules and procedures, such non-
          action shall be deemed to be a withdrawal of that party from the Agency,
          effective as the last day of December of the fiscal year in which the fees and
          assessments are due, notwithstanding the provisions of Item 15 below.

       e. Independent Audit. The Trustees shall obtain an independent audit of the
          Agency for each budget year in which the estimated case revenues exceed
          $10,000, which sum shall include fees, assessments, taxes, grants, but exclude
          in-kind or donated services, staffing, or operational support.

                         INTERLOCAL COOPERATION AGREEMENT

                              Urban Wildlife Assistance Program

      THIS AGREEMENT is made and entered into this 4th day of February 2025, by and
between SALT LAKE COUNTY, a body corporate and politic of the State of Utah (the
"COUNTY"); and BLUFFDALE CITY, a municipal corporation of the State of Utah (the "CITY").
COUNTY and CITY may collectively be referred to as the "parties".

                                         R E C I T A L S:

       WHEREAS, UTAH CODE ANN. § 11-13-202 provides that any two or more public
agencies may enter into an agreement with one another for joint or cooperative actions; and

     WHEREAS, the COUNTY and the CITY are "public agencies" as contemplated in UTAH
CODE ANN. § 11-13-101, et seq. - Interlocal Cooperation Act; and

         WHEREAS, the COUNTY and the CITY are desirous to take part in a multi--
jurisdictional effort originally proposed by the Salt Lake Council of Governments ("COG") to
create and fund an ongoing, regional program for urban wildlife control in the greater Salt Lake
County metropolitan area; and

        WHEREAS, it is beneficial for the COUNTY, the CITY and their respective citizens that
the parties cooperate in accomplishing the foregoing.

                                       A G R E E M E N T:

NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the mutual promises contained within this Agreement,
the parties hereby agree as follows:
I.      Scope of Services

   a.        The COUNTY agrees to:
        i.      Establish and administer a special revenue account for the “Urban Wildlife
                Assistance Program" (the "Fund").
        ii.     Expend all monies received from the CITY under this Agreement as directed, and
                shall promptly reimburse the CITY for any such funds not so expended. The
                COUNTY shall provide the CITY a detailed accounting of all funds received from
                the CITY upon request of the CITY.
        iii.    Consult with representatives of the CITY and other participating local jurisdictions
                in making decisions concerning administration of the Fund.
      b.          The CITY agrees to:

           i.        Participate in the Fund.
           ii.       Make an annual contribution in the amount of three thousand one hundred
                     seventy hundred dollars ($3,170) to the COUNTY for deposit and use in the
                     Fund. The amount shall be adjusted annually upon other cities’ participation in
                     the Fund and the city population.
           iv.       Consult with representatives of the COUNTY and other participating local
                     jurisdictions in making decisions concerning the administration of the Fund.

      c.          The parties mutually agree:

           i.         The Fund will serve program goals as developed by the program participants.
                      The program participants will make recommendations to the COUNTY for the
                      expenditure of Fund monies.
           ii.        The Fund will not supplant any existing COUNTY programs or funding for wild
                      animal control, nor shall monies contributed by the CITY to COUNTY hereunder
                      be diverted or used for other COUNTY programs.
           iii.       The program participants shall make recommendations concerning how monies
                      contributed to the Fund are spent.
           iv.        Funding will be allocated by the parties as part of their respective annual
                      budgeting processes. The CITY's initial contribution shall be paid to the
                      COUNTY by July 31,2025, for the term of service through June 30, 2026. The
                      CITY shall allocate and pay each subsequent annual contribution to the
                      COUNTY by July 1 of each succeeding year of this Agreement.
           v.         Pursuant to Section VI. of this Agreement, entitled "Non-funding," nothing in this
                      Agreement shall be construed to bind the decision of the future legislative
                      bodies of either party to continue funding or participation in the Fund.
           vi.        The COUNTY's role under this Agreement shall be limited to those services set
                      forth in Section I.a. Except where agreed to otherwise in writing, the COUNTY
                      shall not be required to provide any additional money or resources to fulfill the
                      objectives of the Salt Lake County Urban Wildlife Assistance Program. Nothing
                      in this Agreement shall be construed as to require the COUNTY to ensure the
                      success of the program goals developed through the program participants.

Bluffdale Quality Assurance Program
1   Introduction
The Bluffdale Quality Assurance Program defines policies, elements, activities, and guidelines
required for all City construction projects (vertical as well as horizontal) to assure that the materials
and workmanship conform to the requirements of the approved plans and specifications. The
program includes the Materials Acceptance Program and Independent Assurance requirements. All
quality assurance personnel, under the direction of the City Engineer, are expected to follow all
aspects of the Quality Assurance Program (QAP) and the Independent Assurance (IA) requirements
as included in this manual and in the City Standard Specifications. This includes procuring all
required documents, submittals, and inspection reports needed to assure that the materials and
construction practices comply with the quality assurance program.


2   Quality Assurance Program Overview
The Bluffdale Quality Assurance Program implements a schedule of activities and requirements to
cover testing personnel competency and equipment operations during both owner-performed, and
contractor-performed testing programs. The program defines the level of assurance based on the
project type. All projects constructing infrastructure that is owned or will be owned by the City at the
completion of the project, whether built by a developer or by a contractor working for the City, are
City projects and subject to the QAP. Some examples of these projects are:

            a. Development projects that will dedicate public infrastructure to the City
            b. Capital Projects designed, bid, and managed by the City

2.1 Materials Acceptance Program
2.1.1 Materials Acceptance Decision based on Manufacturer Information
This requirement is applicable to off-the-shelf or off-site manufactured materials where production
of products/materials is independent of project considerations. Typical products covered under this
approach include piping and fixtures, geotextile fabric, landscape materials, electrical wiring, and
equipment, etc.

2.2 Submittals
City Inspectors must verify that all materials and products installed in the project meet City
standards. Inspectors must obtain submittals describing the product characteristics of all required
products prior to product installation to ensure compliance with City and project plans and
specifications. Inspectors must not allow any materials or products to be installed without written
submittal approval by the City. Appendix A includes the submittal form to be included with all
submittals. Inspectors must provide these forms to the contractor prior to the preconstruction


                                   Bluffdale Quality Assurance Program
                                                Page 2 of 13
Quality Assurance Program                                                    January 2025

conference. The contractor should have all project submittals ready for City review at the
preconstruction meeting and at least two weeks prior to installation to allow for sufficient review
time.

The City Construction Manager will complete a list of required submittals for each project to be
included in the project Bid Package. This list will be used as a guide to ensure that all required
submittals are provided by the Contractor prior to installation.

2.2.1 Material Acceptance based on UDOT Quality Management Plans (QMPs)
This category includes products and materials that are commercially produced. These are normally
produced in large lots or batches that can be tested and used on multiple projects. Materials covered
under this requirement include Portland cement, asphalt binders and emulsions, steel
reinforcement, hydrated lime, pavement marking paint, etc.


2.3 Testing Program and Applications
2.3.1 Material Acceptance using Sampling and Testing
Sampling and testing is required on all mass-produced materials where large lots or batches were
manufactured and where testing is practical. Materials covered under this category include Portland
cement, asphalt binder and emulsions, steel reinforcement, hydrated lime, pavement marking paint,
etc.

2.3.1.1 Quality Assurance Testing
City Engineering Staff or their representatives (trained and certified hired consultants), perform
Quality Assurance/Acceptance testing according to the Bluffdale Minimum Sampling and Testing
Requirements (BMS&TR) and project specifications. Sampling and testing activities are validated by
independent assurance from qualified testing labs (UDOT Approved Laboratories for roadway
projects for example).

2.3.1.2 Quality Control Testing
Contractors and suppliers are required to perform quality control (QC) sampling and testing to
control processes and quality of the products being installed for the City. Material acceptance is not
based on quality control testing.

2.4 Independent Assurance (IA) Program
This program is specific to federally-funded projects. The IA program requires that the acceptance
sampling and testing is performed according to the UDOT Transportation Technician Qualification
Program (TTQP) and the UDOT Laboratory Qualification Program (LQP). This program assures that
the testing personnel perform the tests properly and that testing equipment is properly calibrated
and certified.

2.4.1 UDOT Construction and Inspection Training Program (CITP)
City Inspectors, or contracted representatives, are required to be certified through CITP program to

2.4.2 Western Alliance for Quality Transportation Construction (WAQTC) and American
      Concrete Institute (ACI)
All testers that perform quality assurance testing on City projects must be certified through WAQTC
for the specific testing to be conducted and ACI for all concrete testing.

2.4.3 Laboratory Qualification
Contractors and City Inspectors must use testing laboratories that are certified through the AASHTO
Materials Reference Laboratory (AMRL).


3   Materials Acceptance Requirements

3.1 Materials Certifications
Contractors must provide manufacturer’s certification at least two weeks prior to installation for all
off-the-shelf or off-site manufactured materials including, but not limited to: piping, fixtures,
geotextile fabric, landscape materials, electrical wiring and equipment, precast products, etc.

The certification must include at least the following: manufacturer’s product data, specifications,
product testing, recommended installation instructions, and manufacturer’s labeling.

The paving contractor is required to submit a paving operation Quality Control Plan to the City
Engineering Department prior to paving. Submittals and inspection forms must identify all pertinent
product data indicating conformance with standards and specifications.

3.2 Material Acceptance based on UDOT Quality Management Plans (QMPs)
Projects that have construction materials with large lots are under the QMP acceptance program.
Typical examples of materials covered include Portland cement, asphalt binder and emulsions, steel
reinforcement, hydrated lime, pavement marking paint, etc. These materials must be supplied by
qualified suppliers and producers under the UDOT Qualified Supplier List based on the following
UDOT Materials Quality Management Plans:

    ▪   503 Reinforcing Steel Epoxy

    ▪   509 Asphalt Binder

3.3 Material Acceptance using Sampling and Testing
Mass-produced materials where large lots or batches can be tested and can represent multiple
projects (typical examples of materials covered under this approach include Portland cement,
asphalt binder and emulsions, steel reinforcement, hydrated lime, pavement marking paint, etc.) are
under this testing program.

3.3.1 Source Testing
The contractor is required to submit a Certificate of Compliance to the City before use of any
materials. The City may verify the certificates at their own discretion.

3.3.2 Field Testing
Field testing is required for all field placed/installed/constructed items including Portland cement
concrete, hot mix asphalt, and soil/aggregate applications. Only qualified technicians meeting
qualifications of the Independent Assurance Program (Section 4) using equipment also meeting the
requirements of the Independent Assurance Program can perform field testing.

3.3.2.1 Acceptance Testing
        The City Engineer’s authorized representatives can perform acceptance testing.
        Acceptance testing is according to the Bluffdale Minimum Sampling and Testing
        Requirements (BMS&TR) and project specifications. Sampling and testing activities are
        validated by independent assurance (IA) activities. This approach is traditionally used for
        capital projects, but may be used for other projects at the City’s discretion.

3.3.2.2 Contractor Quality Control Testing
        The Contractor/Supplier can provide their quality control (QC) test results. These results
        should be used as an additional source of information and not in place of the City
        acceptance testing data. The product acceptance must be based on un-biased testing the
        City conducts independently from the contractor quality control testing. On rare occasions
        the City can use the Contractor’s quality control testing as part of the acceptance decision
        when the City cannot perform quality acceptance testing because of unavailable
        independent testing services, when material quality is validated by the City through
        additional testing and data verification, or if the City witnesses the testing.

3.4 Rejection of Materials
The City will not accept materials that do not meet City standards and specifications. Non-compliant
materials may be evaluated and analyzed by additional methods. The contractor is responsible for
the costs of the additional evaluation of the non-compliant materials. The evaluation must be
completed by a Professional Engineer with experience in materials quality.

The engineering analysis must include an evaluation of the in-situ quality of the materials in
question, their expected impact on performance, and any proposed mitigation procedures or
financial remunerations to the City to account for substandard performance. For development
projects, financial remunerations are not acceptable. Corrective measures must be used for
substandard performance.

maintain their competence through adherence to the UDOT Independent Assurance Program.

sampling, testing, and inspection during roadway construction through the qualification of industry
and City Quality Assurance personnel. Qualification of Inspectors and Test Technicians is
accomplished through adherence to the UDOT Transportation Technician and Inspector

Re:   Amendments to Insurance Requirements for Encroachment Permits


        The City has established requirements related to insurance for contractors and
other entities doing work in the public rights of way through City-issued encroachment
permits. These insurance obligations protect both the City and the permittee from liability
issues related to accidents or injuries during the course of construction.
        These amendments are intended to assist reviewers to efficiently evaluate the
adequacy of insurance. The amended language better matches standard insurance industry
language found on Certificates of Insurance and within insurance policies. The additional
clarity from the amended language will also benefit the permittees and contractors doing
work pursuant to City encroachment permits.

RECOMMENDATION: On February 5, 2025, the Planning Commission unanimously forwarded a positive
recommendation of the proposal for the City Council’s consideration based on findings listed in the February 5,
2025 Staff Report, with an additional finding that the proposed Zone Map Amendment helps with the city’s
affordable housing measures by providing additional density.

SUMMARY: Bringhurst Station is a 102-acre mixed-use development along Redwood Road at the southern end
of Bluffdale City. The Applicant proposes amendments to the General Plan Map and official Zoning Map to
include a 5.06-acre parcel located at 16473 South Redwood Road in the Bringhurst Station project area.
Additionally, the Applicant proposes amendments to Section 11.110.100 of Bluffdale’s City Code to update the
approved project plan to allow 99 townhome units where 13 detached single-family homes are currently
entitled. Finally, the Applicant proposes a Development Agreement with the City of Bluffdale to enumerate the
installation, ownership, and maintenance of amenities, connections to regional trail systems, sitewide grading
and storage agreements, and additional Developer monies for future public park amenities.

BACKGROUND: The City Council established the SD-X Bringhurst Station Zone through Ordinance 2018-21 on
August 22, 2018. The original Bringhurst Station project included approximately 102 acres of land and was
entitled to the development of 350,000 square feet of commercial space, 341 residential units, and 31.23 acres
of active open space, including parks and other amenities. As noted in 11.110.100.010, the Bringhurst Station
Project Plan (BSPP) was adopted as part of the enacting ordinance and remains in effect with the ordinances in
Section 11.110.100.
The Applicant is proposing the inclusion of a 5.06-acre parcel of land into the Bringhurst Station project area.
The Bluffdale City General Plan designates this parcel as a Very Low-Density Residential use, anticipating a
maximum density of less than one dwelling unit per acre. The subject 5.60-acre property is currently zoned
Agricultural (A-5), in which agricultural uses, single-family developments, and parks and open space are allowed
uses.

Section 11.110.080.050 of the City Code requires applicants to participate in a Work Session with the City
Council prior to submitting an application to rezone a property to an SD designation. On May 22, 2024, the
Applicant met with the City Council to discuss the potential amendments 1 and a conceptual design for 99
townhomes. During this meeting, City Council members stated the inclusion of active open space and
protection of the existing trail systems and users are of paramount importance in this area of the city.

During the May Work Session, the Applicant detailed the need for a sewer lift station in the area and a road to
provide access to the Apostolic United Brethren (AUB) property to the east. The Applicant has worked with the
Jordan Basin Improvement District (JBID) to determine a location for the sewer lift system and this new system
will allow neighboring property owners to abandon the existing septic systems and potentially develop in turn.

ANALYSIS: The Applicant and City Staff have worked through several iterations of the project design. The
proposed development includes 99 townhome units on a mix of public and private streets with neighborhood
connections to the Jordan River Trail. The Applicant has also proposed the installation of two resident amenity
spaces: a playground in the northeastern portion of the project and a mixture of tables and seating in the
northwestern portion. The land around the playground slopes down toward the east and is covered in existing
mature native vegetation, which the Applicant will retain. The Draft Development Agreement provides that the
Developer will install a play structure for ages 2-5 and 5-12 in this amenity.

A public street will connect to Bringhurst Boulevard and cross the Jordan River Trail and the Utah Lake
Distributing Canal over a culvert as the primary access to the project. A public road will also stub down to the
southern boundary of the project as well as eastward to the AUB property, providing access and egress to
abutting property owners. Private streets will provide access to the remainder of the project.

The proposed townhomes each have a dedicated 2-car garage and a 20-foot-deep driveway. Per BCC 11.120.030
one guest parking space is required per 4 dwellings. The proposal surpasses the 25-guest space requirement
and provides 32 guest parking stalls.

The Applicant has proposed a trail connection between the neighborhood and the Jordan River Trail which will
be owned and maintained by the homeowners association (HOA). An additional access to the Jordan River Trail
is proposed at the northwesternmost portion of the project where the Applicant has proposed picnic tables and
seating. While the proposed amenities are located on the Applicant’s property, the proposed trail connection to
the Jordan River Trail crosses land owned by Salt Lake County. If the project is approved, the Applicant will be
required to obtain permission from Salt Lake County to make this trail connection.

Bringhurst Station is not entirely built out, however, several phases have been completed including the
detached single-family phases to the north of the subject property. A model train park and bluff park were
anticipated amenities in these phases. Meandering pedestrian trails and disc golf were also proposed as
amenities between the townhomes and commercial uses. Plats for Phases 1 – 3 of the Bringhurst Station
development were recorded between 2020 and 2022 and were constructed over the past few years. While the
majority of the residential and commercial uses have been constructed at this point, Staff conducted several
site visits and determined multiple amenities outlined in the BSPP and in Section 11.110.100 which are missing
from the development.

The Applicant has acknowledged these absent amenities in the draft Development Agreement and proposes
paying the City $2,000 per dwelling unit (in addition to required impact fees) to fund parks and recreation needs
in the community, totaling $198,000. If the City accepts this proposal, the Applicant will be absolved from the
requirement to install the amenities from prior project phases.

At the time of the May 22nd Work Session, City Staff was engaged in ongoing discussions with Salt Lake County to
consider the possibility of exchanging City-owned property at 1490 W Rock Hollow Drive for a 2.68-acre parcel
owned by the County south of the proposed development site. At that point it was unclear whether the parties
would come to an agreement for an exchange, however, since then Staff and Salt Lake County have continued
discussions and have drafted an Interlocal Agreement (ILA) to facilitate the property exchange. While the Salt
Lake County property abuts the proposed site, a potential park space on this parcel should not be considered a
part of this project’s parks and open space contribution. The City anticipates constructing a small park on the
exchanged property in the future.

FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL:

   1) The proposed concept plan complies with adopted parking requirements for townhome units and
      surpasses adopted guest parking requirements;
   2) The proposed development protects delineated wetland areas;
   3) The proposed development protects existing hillside open space and mature native vegetation;
   4) The proposal provides connections to a regional active transportation facility (Jordan River Trail) and
      the rest of Bluffdale’s trail network;
   5) The proposal will bring road and utility infrastructure farther into an area of the city that needs fire
      egress, water, and sewer services;
   6) The proposal will enable abutting or neighboring properties to develop in the future if they choose;
   7) The proposal will advance Strategy #2 (Utah Code § 10-9a-403 “menu item” F) of the City’s Moderate
      Income Housing Element, to zone for higher densities to increase housing affordability within Bluffdale;
      and
   8) The Planning Commission made an additional finding that the proposed Zone Map Amendment
      supports the City’s affordable housing measures by providing additional density.

11.110.100.010 Intent And Purpose

The intent of this special development zone is to provide a land use pattern that provides
for a complementary and compatible mix of uses and a diversity of dwelling unit types. This
article allows for the establishment of necessary supporting commercial, light industrial,
and other uses consistent with a convenient and pedestrian friendly development layout. It
is the intent of this article to allow for ﬂexibility and creativity in the arrangement of uses
while promoting efficiencies in the delivery of services.

The purpose is to develop Bringhurst Station, a mixed-use community consisting of up to
three hundred ﬁfty thousand (350,000) square feet of commercial buildings and up to three
hundred forty one (341) four hundred forty (440) units on public and private streets, an HOA
owned and maintained swimming pool and amenities, and park and trail amenities
accessible to the public.

The Bringhurst Station Project Plan (BSPP) is also adopted as an accompanying document
to the adopted ordinance. Design guidelines, standards, and speciﬁcations in this SD-X
Zone are created with this article and shall supersede any conﬂicting design guidelines,
standards, and speciﬁcations in any and all applicable City ordinances. Design standards
not included in the SD-X Bringhurst Station shall be governed by this Code.



11.110.100.020 Land Use Authority For Site Plans And Subdivisions

A site plan approval is required for all commercial and non-residential buildings, with the
exception of townhome projects, within the SD-X Bringhurst Station Zone. All applications
are subject to chapter 15 of this title and are required to be approved by the Bringhurst
Station Architectural Review Committee (BSARC) prior to review by the Land Use Authority.
City of Bluffdale staff shall be the Land Use Authority to review and approve all
commercial, non-residential (i.e., schools, churches, parks, etc.) site plans. The Planning
Commission shall be the Land Use Authority to review and approve all townhome site
plans for the zone.

The adoption of the SD-X Bringhurst Station Special Development Zone and BSPP does not
constitute a subdivision of the Bringhurst Station property. All future subdivisions of land
within the SD-X Bringhurst Station Zone is subject to all requirements of title 12 of this
Code and the BSPP and shall be reviewed and approved by the designated Land Use
Authority as stated

4. SD-X Parks, Open Space, And Trails Plan: Within a mixed-use community such as
   Bringhurst Station, active open space needs to be provided to beneﬁt the overall
   community, provide connectivity to existing trail systems, and in the residential
   areas, to compensate for the smaller, private rear yard areas. Each lot contains
sufficient private rear yard open space for small family gatherings, outdoor
entertaining and small children play areas. Active park and trail areas will provide
the opportunity for larger, family and neighborhood gatherings, picnics and informal
play areas for sports and similar recreation.

Bringhurst Station's active open space includes 31.23 34.76 acres in private trails
and parks. The trails will connect with the County and/or City owned and maintained
trails adjacent to and within the project area.

Bringhurst Station proposes privately owned and maintained HOA parks and trails of
approximately 31.23 34.76 acres. It is anticipated that there will be a public
trailhead area dedicated to the City providing access to the Jordan River Parkway
Trail and connectivity to the overall trail system throughout the development. The
developers will work with City staff and Salt Lake County for detailed designs of
public road crossings using best practices or other safety techniques for the
crossing of the Jordan River Parkway Trail. The parks and trails will be informal play
areas that include at a minimum turf, trees and an automatic irrigation system
supplied by a secondary water system. As a privately owned and maintained park,
the developer will not request reimbursement for the value of the park land and the
improvements.

The private parks and public trails shall be included in construction drawings for all
subdivision applications and installed per adopted City standards prior to the City
Council's acceptance of the subdivision phase in which those improvements and
amenities are included.

adjacent to the canal prior to the City Council's acceptance of the subdivision
phase in which the fencing is located.

31.23 acres and will contain a variety of recreational amenities. Possible future park
elements or amenities to be constructed are provided in the BSPP.

16. Minimum Standards For Commercial Architectural Design:

      1. Commercial Architectural Design Guidelines: Consistent with the
         requirements of the SD-X Bringhurst Station Zone requires enhanced
         architectural elements and features that will result in commercial buildings
         that are thematic and include enhancements to visible side and rear facades
         from either public streets or the private park or trail. The following
         architectural design guidelines shall dictate the design and construction of
         all non-residential buildings within Bringhurst Station.

             1. Building Material:

                    1. At least eighty percent (80%) of a building's facade along all
                       streets shall be ﬁnished in one of the following materials:

                           1. Masonry (brick, stone, stucco utilizing a 3-step process,
                              cast stone, glass, or glass block).

                           2. Split face concrete block or pre-cast or poured in place
                              concrete.

                           3. Wood or architectural metal panel, batt and board
                              siding (no vinyl), or lap siding (no vinyl).

                    2. No more than twenty percent (20%) of a building's facade
                       along all streets shall use other accent materials: split-face
                       concrete block, tile, EIFS or pre-cast concrete panels.

             2. Building Massing:
                    1. Building masses shall avoid long ﬂat-plane expanses and
                       incorporate horizontal relief no less than two feet (2') in depth.
                       The maximum length of a single facade expanse is one
                       hundred feet (100').

                    2. Building masses shall be greater at areas with more pedestrian
                       activity, such as street corners and building entrances.

                    3. Single story building masses that exceed twenty feet (20') in
                       height shall incorporate an overhead structure element above
                       building entrances to provide pedestrian scale interest.

                    4. Load-bearing materials should touch the ground.

             3. Arcades And Storefront Structural Bays:

                    1. Storefront structural bays shall be orchestrated to appear as
                       phased construction over a long period of time.

                    2. Substantial structural bays shall be composed of vertically
                       oriented piers and horizontal spandrels with intervening
                       storefront windows.

                    3. Arcades are encouraged as semi-public spaces between the
                       street and storefront, sheltering pedestrians from the
                       elements.

                    4. Multiple storefronts with separate storefront windows can be
                       massed together within a single facade expanse with the same
                       architectural style.

             4. Roof Form:

                    1. Predominant roof shapes should be ﬂat or slightly pitched,
                       with limited use of gable or canted roof elements to provide
                       breaks in facade type and/or add visual interest.

                    2. Roof pitches should be typically low (1:12 - 4:12).

                    3. Moderate overhangs up to three feet (3') should be supported
                       by substantial dimensional steel braces that deﬁne the top of
                       the building.

17. Other Commercial Requirements: The following requirements are in addition to the
    requirements found in this title or any other applicable resolution or ordinance:
      1. Landscaping: Individual lots or parcels located in this SD-X development are
         not subject to a minimum landscaping requirement so long as ﬁfteen

         space.

      2. Screening Of Outdoor Storage Or Display Areas And Mechanical Equipment:
         All outdoor storage or display areas and mechanical equipment and other
         appurtenances, including electrical boxes, gas meters and other utility
         facilities shall be located or screened so as not to be visible from streets,
         pedestrian areas and adjacent developments. Screens shall be aesthetically

         fences, mounding and/or 4-season vegetative screening. Screened areas

         round. Rooftop mechanical equipment and other appurtenances shall be

         subject buildings. All mechanical equipment and utilities shall in all cases be
         either enclosed by outer building walls or parapets or grouped and screened

      3. Trash, Junk, Storage And Other Debris: No trash, used materials, unsightly

         an open area. All such materials shall be enclosed in a building or, if deemed
         appropriate by the City, by a sight obscuring fence.

ANALYSIS

In addition to the purpose sections of each zoning district, the City should consider whether the
proposal to amend the General Plan Map and Zoning Map furthers the goals of the city’s adopted
General Plan and the Parks, Trails, Recreation and Open Space Plan. The proposed General Plan
Map Amendment is consistent with the General Plan’s call for center-focused growth: the proposed
development is immediately adjacent to an existing neighborhood with similar density and is within
an anticipated growth area.

If the City chooses to amend the General Plan Map, the Applicant’s proposal to locate additional
residential development at the edge of the existing A-5 Zoning area is compatible with the purpose
sections of the A-5 Zone and the SD-X Bringhurst Station Project Zone. The proposed project would
concentrate density in an area with access to the city’s trail network and nearby an existing
neighborhood with a similar density. Further, the proposal maintains and protects the mature
native vegetation at the northeastern corner of the project area to offset the increased density.

The majority of land remaining in the A-5 Zone is not utilized for large-scale farming uses and is

Bringhurst Station project would result in continuous, not leapfrog, development as supported by
the purpose of the A-5 Zone and would be accompanied by extensions of utilities and other public
infrastructure.

Per BCC 11.110.80.010, the purpose of a special development district (SD) zone is to:

              “allow the most efficient and creative development of lands that have
              unique or unusual characteristics. The SD zone is intended to be used
              for development when it can be shown that no other zone
              classification would be adequate for reasonable development that
              otherwise conforms to or furthers the goals and objectives of the city’s
              general plan, economic development strategic plan, and/or the city’s
              parks trails, recreation, and open space plan.”

The subject property is unique due to its topographical and utility challenges. Additionally, it is
located in an existing growth center but still provides opportunities for preservation of open space
and native plant materials. It also places higher-density housing development in an area of the city
where road and utility infrastructure has been installed in anticipation of higher traffic and demand.
The increase in rooftops will make abutting and nearby commercial developments more attractive
to a variety of businesses looking to serve the community.

In accordance with Utah Code § 10-9a-537, Staff reviewed the proposal alongside the West Traverse
Mountain Compatibility Area Study (WTMCAS) and has determined that the proposal would not
impact the communications, flight patterns, or cause significant sound or light pollution impacting
the activities of Camp Williams.
