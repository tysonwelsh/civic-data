Public Hearing

            THIS BEING THE TIME heretofore set for a public hearing to receive public
comment regarding the following request:

Application #OAM2021-000282 – Ordinance #21-05-01 to consider amendments to Chapter
19:74 – Floodplain Hazard Regulations of the Magna Municipal Code to bring it into compliance
with Federal and State law.
                                             2
DATE           TUESDAY                                    JUNE                               8, 2021


                 Travis Hair, Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, reviewed the
amendments, showing a map of where floodplain areas were in Magna. Magna does not have a
lot of floodplain areas, and only one area in the 100-year floodplain has buildings on it. That area
is along the channel. That is also where the Ivory Homes project is being built, so this ordinance
will affect that development.

The GSLMSD is not as concerned from a flood control or wastewater/stormwater standpoint as it
is about other potential disasters. If the ordinance is not in compliance with National Flood
Insurance Program (NFIP) standards, it could result in the Federal Emergency Management
Agency denying resources for other disasters. The GSLMSD has vetted this ordinance with
FEMA’s regional and state offices, and both signed off on the ordinance. That ensures the Magna
Metro Township will be able to get assistance in the event of a disaster and it covers NFIP
requirements. It also allows the Magna Metro Township to charge fees to keep the existing or
future floodplains, and it outlines the steps someone would need to take in order to do any kind
of development in floodplains.

               Council Member Hull asked if someone living in a floodplain could get flood
insurance.

              Mr. Hair stated yes, having the ordinance in place allows landowners to participate
in the NFIP and get coverage through that, and most lenders will require that or offer it. Even if a
landowner is outside the floodplain area, but is along the channel, they can get floodplain
insurance.

               Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to open the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

               No one spoke in favor of or in opposition to the ordinance.

               Council Member Hull, seconded by Council Member Peel, moved to close the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

             Council Member Hull, seconded by Council Member Peel, moved to approve
Ordinance 21-05-01, as follows. The motion passed unanimously.

Magna United / Magna Community that Cares

                 Kellen Schalter stated the vape detectors were installed in Matheson Junior High,
and administration is being trained how to use them. They should be in full use by the time school
starts in the fall. She expected to have an action plan to present to the Council by the middle of
July, showing the next phase of the coalition’s work.

               Council Member Hull asked if the vape detectors could also be installed in Cyprus
High School.

                Ms. Schalter stated the coalition asked the Granite School District about doing
that, and the school district said no. It said that would require too much work because there was
mold in the ceiling. Vape detectors could be installed in other community areas, such as libraries.
She would also explore having them put in the new high school once that is built and start looking
for grant money now.

Code Enforcement ~ Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District

                Tom Zumbado, Code Enforcement Officer, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services
District, stated since the last Council meeting, Code Enforcement opened 37 new cases and
closed 32 cases. Of those new cases, 14 are zoning related and 23 have weeds. Currently, there
are 67 open cases, an increase of five cases since the last meeting. Code Enforcement has
made its second appearance at the homeless encampment on California Avenue, and saw only
a modest improvement. He told people there that time was running out to find an alternative
location and what resources were available to them. Code Enforcement has scheduled a one-
month mark, and is currently meeting with the UPD, the Salt Lake County Health Department,
and Kennecott Utah Copper about that.
