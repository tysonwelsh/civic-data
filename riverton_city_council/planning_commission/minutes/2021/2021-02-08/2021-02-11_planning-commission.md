In CPA 1, residential and commercial are allowed. Mr. Prestwich stated that this
application is only requesting residential. CPA 1 allows all types of residential. Within
250 feet of the north property line is limited to single-family detached residential. Mr.
Prestwich reported that an MDA was executed by the City and recorded against the SLR
property. The SDD is available on the City’s website. The review process is different as
they are following a Master and Final Site Plan. For a large residential project, they would
do a preliminary plat and a final plat. With the SDD, they follow a three-step process
starting with a Community Scale Plan, followed by a public hearing. The applicant then
prepares a Block Scaled Plan with increased detail, showing project feasibility. It then
comes back to the Planning Commission as a decision item. Noticing requirements were
addressed. The final step will be a detailed plan that may be considered as a final plan.

discussion will focus only on the Community Scale Plan.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the proposal is for a mix of single-family detached units along

community space in the middle with open space and green space. The community center
and open spaces are proposed to be private and will be privately owned and maintained
by the developer.

The project has three access points onto Eagle Flight Road (4570 West) and one onto
13200 South. There is no bridge across the Welby Jacob Canal but there is one on 13200

SDD was adopted. Mr. Prestwich reported that the SLR was part of a special agreement.

Mr. Prestwich reported that this is the second SLR project the City has done. The
Development Agreement with the City specifies that the master developer, SLR, has to
grant the number of units to the next developer that comes in. Staff will keep track of the
units to ensure that the threshold is not reached.

discretion under the MDA does not extend to putting limitations on the overall density.

which they are not. The governing number is the number of units allocated by SLR.

      a.      RIVERTON WEST BUSINESS PARK, SCREENING OF LOADING
              DOCKS FACING A RESIDENTIAL ZONE.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the above matter is for discussion only and no decision was
to be made tonight. Similar nearby uses were described. The property location was
identified on a map displayed and is zoned PCC. The intent was to develop flex
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                                14

warehouse space similar to what was approved on the opposite side of the road. There
have been issues with the PCC zoning, which specifies that loading docks shall be
oriented away from residential or other incompatible uses and major roadways. Mr.
Prestwich stated that due to the shape of the parcel, there is no way to develop a building
that meets both requirements and orients it away from the residential and the new City
street. On the opposite side, there is a similar problem where the parcel is narrow. The
developer was moving forward with a proposal and would like guidance in order to
proceed. The matter was to come back to the Commission as a site plan with additional
details and is currently in the conceptual stage.

Mr. Prestwich oriented the Commission to the site. Currently, the building is oriented with
the docks in the back with parking and storefronts in front. The flex warehouse space will
include storefronts in front and warehouse space in the back. A 50-foot setback was
proposed in addition to 11 feet of landscaping and an eight-foot masonry wall. An
architectural rendering was displayed of the north building. 10 feet of landscaping is
required by ordinance with an eight-foot masonry wall.

The larger building to the south will include a depressed dock with a taller loading area.
Mr. Prestwich asked if the Commission would agree to allow the access doors to face the
residential. If so, he asked if it would be acceptable to have the dock ramp in the proposed
location or if it needs to be located on the side and further away from the residential.

Commissioner Lefgren inquired about the location of the dumpsters. Mr. Prestwich stated
that the plans have not progressed that far.

Commissioner Breinholt reported that years earlier he represented a supermarket that
was developed in an existing neighborhood. He commented that a masonry wall is the
best way to collect noise, however, when it faces a two-story building it creates an echo
chamber that feeds the noise directly into the neighborhood. That can be mitigated
through landscaping or shrubbery that covers the masonry wall. A two-story masonry
building nearby will result in noise coming over the wall. He was concerned about
mitigating the noise and suggested that cloth awnings be used to deflect the noise.

The applicant, Jeremy Day, identified himself as one of three partners on the project.
Together they have over a decade of experience developing flex/warehouse space. They
were excited to be in Riverton. He noted that because of the shape of the parcel, it will
be difficult to face the dock in a manner that works. He believed that the best option was
the one proposed. They envision 10 feet of landscaping with larger trees and shrubs to
mitigate the visual and noise impacts. Because of the quality of the space, they plan to
bring in larger tenants who will be closed on weekends.

The possibility of locating the dock on the north side was mentioned. Mr. Day stated that
it is infeasible due to the size of the space.

      b.     PRIVATE LANES.

Mr. Prestwich reported that previously there was discussion of potentially incorporating
work session time into the Commission schedule. The Mayor identified it as a priority.
The intent would be to dedicate the first portion of Commission meetings, from 6:30 p.m.
to 7:00 p.m., as a work session. Doing so will allow items to be addressed in advance of
the actual application and avoid substantial investment in time and planning on the part
of the City and the developers. It was suggested that work meetings take place during
the first Planning Commission Meeting of each month.

Mr. Prestwich reported that staff has been studying revisions to the Elderly Housing
Overlay Zone that was discussed at the previous meeting. There is an outstanding
application for a rezone to the Elderly Housing Overlay that would be heard by the Council
at their next meeting.

Mr. Prestwich explained that one of the ordinances that are ready for revisions is the
Private Lane Ordinance. The intent was to get feedback from the Commission before
preparing the text. The current ordinance contains limitations and allows a maximum of
four lots on a private lane. The width is scaled based on the number of users and the
number of lots. A maximum length of 450 feet was set. Setbacks are measured to the
property line. One of the issues with private lanes was to determine whether it is
something they want to encourage or discourage. Criteria are in place that the private
lane should be permitted where there is no ability to bring in a public right-of-way. The
ordinance states that public rights-of-way are encouraged, however, it needs to be
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                                  16
February 11, 2021


demonstrated that there is not a feasible option to develop off of a public right-of-way or
that there are other constraints.

Mr. Prestwich pointed out that as a City they are moving toward more infill and
increasingly property owners are looking to utilize private lanes. Currently, the ordinance
allows an easement right-of-way to float relative to the home. A question for the
Commission was if they would prefer to see the ordinance treat the edge of the private
lane as the measurement point for setbacks. Currently, in terms of accessory structures,
they discount or remove the private lane area from the calculation. There was discussion
regarding how to measure minimum lot size. Requiring the home to be set back from the
private lane will accomplish much the same thing and force the dimensions of the lots to
be larger.

Commissioner Cannon commented that he lives on one of the most utilized private lane
experiments in the City. In many ways, the private lane was created to circumvent full
subdivision improvements. His street is a classic example of a good private lane. He
noted that most of the homes at the end of his lane are large homes on large lots.

Commissioner Lefgren felt strongly that with respect to setbacks, the end of the lane
should be where the setback is. He did not think they should discourage lanes as they
create seclusion and have an appeal. He would not support an ordinance that would
push against a private lane. If they feel it is overly restrictive, they should change the lot
size rather than force people to find ways to reduce the size of the lots from a usability
standpoint.

In response to a comment made by Commissioner Cluff, Mr. Prestwich confirmed that
there are no restrictions on a concrete pour.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the current subdivision ordinance includes a minor subdivision
category of up to three lots. With regard to lane width and layout, he suggested that it
may be beneficial to differentiate between a private drive and a private street. A
distinction would give the Commission the ability to make allowances for infill or private
drives that may not be appropriate for development. Sidewalk issues were also
addressed as well as places where they would be necessary and more difficult to
accommodate.

Signage and addressing issues were discussed. Mr. Prestwich stated that because
Accessory Dwelling Units (“ADU”) are allowed, the intent was to ensure that private lanes
are appropriately signed and the units addressed. In the ordinance, they would want to
make the requirements much more clear and explicit in terms of what is required.
Metering and infrastructure on private lanes would also be addressed.

Mr. Prestwich reported on the annual Planning Commission Training, which would be
scheduled in the near future.
