Agenda Review

Council/Mayor Discussion
         Mayor Caldwell started the discussion by providing the Council with an update on adjusted operations in response to the
COVID-19 Pandemic; the majority of the adjustments are based upon the advice and guidance of local health departments and
State government. He noted City publications regarding COVID-19 response are printed in English and Spanish. He then
indicated that City Administration is working closely with the Utah League of Cities and Towns (ULCT) and other partners to
closely monitor the economic impacts of COVID-19; the City is in a fairly positive financial position, but Administration will be
very conservative in their budget recommendations for the coming Fiscal Year (FY). He then engaged in discussion with City
Council members regarding any adjustments to City personnel policies in order to support employees that may contract COVID-
19 or are at higher risk of contracting the illness. There was a discussion about the City's role in enforcing any social distancing
regulations or stay at home orders that may be in effect in the City.

the Planning Commission's role to evaluate the Plan's conformance with the City's General Plan. They found that projects AR002

analysis of the needs at the Marshall White Center and they will provide a recommendation to the City Council regarding that

its consistency with the General Plan.

includes a classroom space for pilot training and an area for maintenance of aircraft for mechanic training. The hangar plays a
pivotal role in operations at the field and there is no other facility available to replace it if the roofis not replaced; it is a true asset
that can meet needs of airport users for the next 20 years, which means that the useful life of the new roof would be realized.

assigmnents for federal aviation funds.

Redevelopment Agency Project Updates
           Community and Economic Development Deputy Director Cooper discussed the impact that the COVID-19 pandemic
could have on redevelopment efforts in the City; all of the ingredients of a strong economy are still in place and the City still has
many competitive advantages that will make it possible to continue to pursue development and redevelopment efforts in the
community. There will likely be a shift in the office space market given the ease of facilitating tele-working for employees, but
satellite office space will be in demand for businesses that are located in larger metropolitan areas. The retail market has stalled,
but the housing market is still moving. Administration's biggest concern relates to the equity market; the lending capacity of
certain fmancial institutions may be limited based upon reduced investment, but there is still movement in the construction
industry. He then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide updates on major projects in the City, including the Swift
site redevelopment, Hostess site redevelopment, and Rite-Aid site redevelopment. There was a brief discussion about the
completion of the demolition of the Rite-Aid building; Council member Nadolski stated he was surprised to learn the stlucture had
been demolished as it was his understanding that would not be completed until an investor had been selected for the project. Mr.
Cooper stated the urgency of the demolition was based on the fact that the City could not secure the building well enough to
totally stop break-ins and squatter activity. It is yet to be known how much the demolition will influence the future acquisition
cost for any investors interested in the property. Council member Nadolski asked how the demolition of the Rite-Aid building is
being funded. Comptroller Stout stated she is preparing a budget opening to identify the funding source for that component of the
project.

Fiscal Year 2021 Financial Principles
          Council Deputy Director Symes explained each year the Council and Mayor jointly adopt fmancial principles to guide
and direct budgetary and financial management decisions throughout the fiscal year. Prior to FY2015, the Principles were adopted
with the budget. Beginning in FY2015, the Council began adopting the financial principles prior to receiving the Mayor's budget
as way to provide additional guidance during preparation of the Mayor's proposed budget. No significant changes are proposed
for the FY2021 fmancial principles when compared to the FY2020 principles. If there are no objections to proceeding, Council
staff proposes Council consideration of adoption of the Financial Principles on April 21.
