Proposed Ordinance 2024-6 amending Section 3-14-6 of the Ogden
Municipal Code to extend the sunset provision to April 30, 2026
        Camille Cook, Chair of the Marshall N. White Center Advisory Committee, presented the sunset report for the Marshall
N. White Center Advisory Committee. For a copy of the sunset report in its entirety see the information packet.
        Discussion among the Council and members of the Advisory Committee and City staff centered on various elements of
the Marshall N. White reconstruction project; programming of the new Center; timing of the re-opening of the Center; and
community engagement opportunities.
        Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-6, entitled:
                 “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending Section 3-14-6 of the Ogden Municipal Code to extend the
                 sunset provision for the Marshall N. White Center Advisory Committee to April 30, 2026; and by providing
                 that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final passage.”
        ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS,
ORDINANCE 2024-6 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL
MEMBERS CHOBERKA, GRAF, HYER, MYERS, VICE CHAIR WHITE, AND CHAIR RICHEY. VOTING NO –
NONE. Council member Blair was not present when this vote was taken.

Public Comments
          Angel Castillo stated she is excited to hear about the progress of the Marshall N. White Center. She then referenced
tonight’s work session discussion of the proposed creation of a Flex Industrial Zone and noted the key point missing in the
proposed ordinance is reference to clear span construction. This is important as the community is moving into an uncertain time
relative to office space development. Flex space needs to be truly flexible to provide reuse opportunities to ensure it is not cheaper
to demolish a building than to convert it to a different use. She is grateful the Council asked for more discussion of the matter and
asked them to research market trends relative to office space development.
          Heath Satow stated that he does support the creation of the Flex Industrial Zone but found it ironic that the text explicitly
prohibits any smells detectable outside of the building while there is still a business present in Ogden City that regularly ‘stinks
up’ the entire City. He then referenced the recent sale of the Brown Ice Cream Building to a realtor who misrepresented the size of
the building by half; the realtor is a business partner of the person who purchased the building at an undervalued price. Six weeks
ago, he started asking the Council about the legal responsibilities of the new owner and he has not received an answer. He read the
statement he made during the last meeting regarding this matter and asked for an answer this evening. He reiterated the area is
blighted and the whole reason for accelerating the sale of the building was to remove blight as quickly as possible. The reason this
matters is because if the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) is making contracts with developers, he wants to ensure those contracts
cannot be entirely overridden by selling a property to another party.
          Teresa Bramwell added to the comments made by Mr. Satow; she has reached out to the owner of the Brown Ice Cream
Building who told her that he is not doing anything with the property until interest rates come down. He asked her if she has a
suggestion for the reuse of the property, to which she answered no. The individual told her that he could not come up with any
ideas of what to do with the building and if this is the case, she wonders why he owns it. She suspects he will sit on the property
and sell it for a profit when possible. She then echoed Ms. Castillo’s comments about the value of a Flex Industrial zone; she
would like a zone assigned to her property that would allow for different uses depending on market conditions. Her property is
zoned neighborhood commercial, but the uses allowed in that zone are very limited and those who have been interested in opening
a business in her building have been refused a business license by the City.

Mayor Comments
          Mayor Nadolski stated he is unsure when Ms. Bramwell spoke with the owner of the Brown Ice Cream building, but it is
his understanding that the new owner is moving forward with development plans. He stated that he wants to see the property and
the building redeveloped. He addressed Mr. Satow’s question about the legal restrictions on the new owner of the building and
reiterated a statement previously made by the City Attorney that the new owner is committed to following the legal terms of the
original purchase agreement.
          Chair Richey suggested that the City’s contract forms be reviewed, and that language be included to communicate that
any legal requirement placed on a property owner will be transferred to any new owner of the property. Mayor Nadolski stated he
is not sure that is necessary, and he does not believe the legal requirements of the contract have been eliminated by the sale of the
property. He expressed a willingness to discuss the matter further with Mr. Satow to ensure he is answering the questions he has.
          Mayor Nadolski then thanked the entire Council for attending the recent Fire Department banquet; the Fire Department
noticed and appreciated support from the elected officials of the City. He then shared an email he received today regarding the
banquet; it was from the father of a City employee who was pleased by the City’s response to a fallen tree on City-owned
property. He has witnessed City employees work on the property over the past 20 years and he spoke to the work ethic and
dedication of those employees. Mayor Nadolski stated that it has been his experience during his short tenure as Mayor that the
caliber of City staff is very high and is noticed by City residents. This has made for a very fulfilling experience on his part.
