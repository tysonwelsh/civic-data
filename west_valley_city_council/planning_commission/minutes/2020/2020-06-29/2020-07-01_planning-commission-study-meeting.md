GENERAL PLAN/ZONE CHANGE APPLICATION
GPZ-6-2020 Petition by RUSSELL PLATT requesting a General Plan change from General
           Commercial to High Density Residential (12 to 20 units/acre) and a zone change from C-2
           (general commercial) to RM (multiple unit dwelling residential). The property is located at
           4565 West 3500 South on 3.07 acres. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545)


Discussion: Steve Pastorik presented the application. Steve explained that the applicant is looking to
        redevelop the subject property into townhomes. He explained that the car lot currently
        located on the site would be demolished. Steve explained that the concept plan includes
        both front-loaded and alley loaded townhomes. He explained the density of the proposed
        development is higher than other townhome developments at 16 units/acre. Steve stated the
        applicant would need to work with Building Inspections on the open stairs to the rooftop
        patio to make sure they meet code, where this is a new concept.

        Commissioner Winters asked where the closest bus stop is to the subject location. Steve
        said he would check on the bus stop location and have the information at the next meeting.
        Commissioner Fuller thought there was a park and ride just up the street. Steve stated it is
        at least a mile away.

        Commissioner Woodruff pointed out the proposed site seems maxed out. He asked Steve if
        the applicant was planning on offering amenities or if the residents will need to use the park
        across 3500 South. Steve explained there would be green space between the alley loaded
        townhomes, the rooftop patios on the all townhomes, a playground and BBQ area. Steve
        explained the possibility of losing some of the units for more open spaces this could be
        addressed in a development agreement.

        Commissioner Wood expressed her concern about the dangers in crossing 3500 South to
        access the park. She also asked Steve what the staff thought of the modern design being
        proposed with the surrounding area being traditional. Steve explained there is some
        residential area to the south of the subject area, however having this development on 3500
        South and having the commercial property on both sides, he doesn’t see the modern look as
        a concern.

        Commissioner McEwen stated this being a strange location for rooftop patios. He
        explained they would be looking down at commercial building rooftops. Steve agreed they
        would see the roofs; however, on top of a 3-story building, they would still have a great
        view of the mountains on both sides.

        Steve explained that any decision on land use should not be based on just revenue, but he
        did find that a project of this density would generate the same, if not more, revenue as a
        used car lot. Steve explained this being due to the increase in townhome prices.

        Commissioner Lovato asked Steve if the project on Redwood Road was built using the
        same developer. Steve confirmed it is the same developer. Commissioner Lovato stated he
        had driven by the development on Redwood Road, and there must have been a party in one
        of the units causing spillover parking into the street. His concern is this new development
        will have the same parking issue. Steve explained that the front-loaded townhomes would
        have a two-car garage and a driveway that could fit two more cars. He explained the alley
        loaded townhomes would have parking spots on the street as well as a two-car garage.
        Steve stated there would be between 3.5 and 4 parking spaces/unit. Steve Lehman then
        explained that Cobalt Landing has 3.9 spaces/unit, and he has not heard of any parking
        issues.



ZONE CHANGE APPLICATION
Z-4-2020        Petition by JEFFERY AND KRISTINE NAYLOR requesting a zone change from A
                (agriculture, minimum lot size ½ acre) to BRP (business/research park). The property is
                located at 1078 W Parkway Avenue on 0.8 acres. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545)



  Discussion:   Steve Pastorik explained the applicant owns the house to the east of the subject
                property, and they are looking to rezone this parcel to allow for a small warehouse and
                shop space. Steve pointed out the curb, gutter, and sidewalk have not been installed,
                and if the zoning is changed and they develop the property, this will be a requirement.
                Steve explained the applicants are looking to add 37 feet of the proposed lot to the
                adjacent lot containing their home. The 37 feet would remain in zone A.

                Steve stated another thing for the Planning Commission to consider is if the applicant
                builds just a warehouse on the subject property without a shop. If the applicant
                includes a shop, they would need to follow the commercial design standards; a
                warehouse alone would not need to meet them. Steve stated this could be addressed in
                a development agreement.
                Commissioner Porter asked Steve if the applicant is planning on running a business out
                of the proposed building. Steve stated they are; they run a cabinet shop.

                Commissioner Fuller asked if Steve knew what type of equipment the applicant would
                have there. Steve stated he would follow up with the applicant on what kind of
                equipment they will be using. Commissioner Fuller stated his concern is the possibility
                of sawdust and noise close to the Jordan River Parkway.

DISCUSSION ITEM
WVC Active Transportation Plan


Discussion:    Steve Pastorik presented the active transportation plan. He explained how in 2018, the
               City secured a grant through the Wasatch Front Regional Council for $75,000. Using the
               grant money, the City retained an engineering firm to help develop the active
               transportation plan. Steve pointed out the three goals of the plan; enhance pedestrian
               traffic safety, increase the amount of active transportation activity, and increase the lane
               miles for active transportation. Steve explained this discussion item is to allow time for
               the Planning Commission to review the transportation plan before the July 22, 2020,
               public hearing, when this will be a General Plan Change application on the agenda.


There being no further business, the meeting adjourned at 4:35 p.m.
