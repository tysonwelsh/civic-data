WORK SESSION 
 
1.  Discussion/update regarding the Eastern Summit County General Plan, Chapters 
1-6. – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Chair Sonntag stated that suggested edits were sent into Director Putt. The suggested 
edits were shared and discussed. Planner Milliner stated that some chapters are still 
being edited. 
Commissioner Wheaton stated she questions the obligation of any municipality of 
balancing private property rights. It is not the purview of any municipality to enforce 
private property rights. Attorney Strachan stated that State statute states that a 
municipality can give consideration to private property rights. Commissioner Clyde stated 
there is a need to respect existing property owner rights versus those who want to change 
the land use. Language should be included stating, “balancing private property rights of 
existing and proposed land uses.” 
Commissioner Sargent stated that the Mission Statement needs to include, “Small-town 
rural character…” and agriculture. 
Commissioner Peterson suggested changing “foundation” to “heritage.” Chair Sonntag 
suggested that word changes mirror the Cedar Crest planning exercises.

Commissioner Wheaton asked if not having farming and ranching listed as primary 
activities would take away from agricultural protection. We do not want to downplay 
agricultural protection. Director Putt answered that farming and ranching are no longer 
primary economic drivers, but they should still be respected and protected. Add language: 
“…and need to be protected.” Commissioner Wilde stated that three of the major 
ranchers/farmers in Hoytsville are trying to sell out. 
Director Putt clarified there is added language to help define rural character. Cradle-to-
grave is explained as the community evolves and changes, the opportunities to stay in the 
community are limited and it is getting harder to find a variety of housing. Creating more of 
those opportunities would make it easier to stay in the community for a lifetime. 
Commissioner Peterson asked what the agricultural opportunities are that are the 
hallmark of Eastern Summit County living and suggested replacing “opportunities” with 
“uses.” Planner Milliner answered that agriculture is changing and new agricultural 
uses/opportunities are the future. Commissioner Wilde stated that Mike Brown is an 
example because he is looking at other uses for his dairy. 
Planner Milliner stated that Chapter 2: Land Uses is still being worked on. Chapter 3: 
Transport will include information from Derrick Radke and Caroline Rodriguez that needs 
to be added. Chapter 4 changes were discussed. 
Commissioner Clyde stated that there is not anything on the map for any commercial 
zoning, which is not consistent with encouraging economic development. Transportation 
issues should include roadways. Development should not be approved if it depends on 
UDOT improvements. Approvals and planning should be consistent with infrastructure.

Chapter 5 changes were discussed. Commissioner Clyde stated that regionalization of 
water and sewer systems needs to be addressed. People should be encouraged to extend 
and connect to existing systems. The County should try to anticipate growth and develop 
regional systems for that growth. Secondary water for irrigation and watering lawns where 
available should be considered. There is a need to protect and enhance public land access, 
including State Parks and waterways.  
Commissioner Peterson stated that some objectives do not have underlying policies. 
Commissioner Clyde stated it should be clarified who manages open space areas and what 
is done with those open space areas that are part of subdivisions. If that is not clarified 
those open spaces could become orphaned remainder parcels. Commissioner Sargent 
stated that the open spaces should be incorporated into the development. Commissioner 
Wilde is concerned that those open spaces may be open for development in the future. 
Commissioner Clyde stated that wood-burning boilers should be banned and the number 
of wood-burning fireplaces per dwelling should be limited. Commissioner Wheaton 
stated that some no idling zones should be established. 
Chapter 6 changes were discussed. 
 
 
Director Items 
Director Putt shared that the May 6th meeting will have an application for the Marathon 
pipeline and a water tank in Woodland. The Cedar Crest public hearing will be postponed.
