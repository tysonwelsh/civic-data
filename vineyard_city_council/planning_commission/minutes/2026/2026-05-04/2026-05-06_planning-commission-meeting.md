      5.2. Conditional Use and Site Plan Application- Tire Auto Body Shop and Retail at
           430 S Geneva Road, Regional Commercial Zone

            Guilford Rand requests approval of a site plan for a two-building commercial
            development at 430 S Geneva Road in the Regional Commercial Zone, including a
            tire/auto body shop, general retail space, restaurant, parking, landscaping, and related
            site improvements.

            The Planning Commission reviewed a request from Guilford Rand for approval of a
            site plan and conditional use permit for a two-building commercial development at
            430 S Geneva Road in the Regional Commercial Zone, including a tire/auto body
            shop, retail space, restaurant use, parking, landscaping, and related improvements.

            Planner Fletcher presented the project and noted that the applicant added additional
            parking as requested. Commissioners discussed access at 400 South, roadway
            alignment, potential corner barriers, signage, and the design of the two-story office
            space. Concerns were raised regarding the use of honey locust trees, with both
            commissioners and a resident, Russell Evan, noting they are messy; the Commission
            recommended consultation with the City Arborist on tree selection. The City Engineer
            stated that two site entrances are required for safety and that a crosswalk or signal will
            be installed in the area. Chairman Steele confirmed that the City is not responsible for
            tree maintenance and requested that a maintenance and cleanliness clause be included
            to ensure the applicant maintains the parking lot and landscaping.

            MOTION: Commissioner Pierce motioned that the conditional use request is
            approved with the conditions that we’ve stated in the recommendations.
            Commissioner Evans seconded the motion. Yes: Commissioners Evans, Fagg,
            Huntington, Ostler, Pearce, and Steele. No: None. Motion Passed 6-0.

            Site Plan Amendment: Utah City Wellness Center and Resort Pool Phase 2
     5.3.
            Planning Commission – Meeting Minutes Summary
            Utah City Wellness Center and Resort Pool, Phase 2 Site Plan Amendment

          The Planning Commission reviewed a request from Flagborough for approval of a site
          plan amendment for the Utah City Wellness Center and Resort Pool Phase 2. The
          amendment was limited to updates to building elevations and landscaping, with no
          other changes to the previously approved site plan.

          Planner Fletcher presented the proposed revisions, outlining updates to the building
          elevations and landscape plan. He confirmed that the applicant has agreed to meet all
          pedestrian-friendly landscaping requirements. Construction-related parking and safety
          concerns were discussed.

          Representatives Bronson Tatton and BJ Laterveer, on behalf of Utah City
          (Flagborough), addressed questions from the Commission. They explained that the
          exterior elevation changes to the changing rooms and concessions were intended to
          better align with the surrounding architectural styles and to ensure an authentic
          appearance.

          Commissioner Pearce clarified that the pool is anticipated to open for public use in
          2027. Commissioner Evans asked about the removal of demolition materials; Mr.
          Tatton stated that excavated soil was relocated for fill needs in other areas of Utah
          City. Commissioner Evans also requested consideration of glazing or decorative
          treatment on a wall, and Mr. Laterveer reiterated the intent for the building to be
          visually authentic. Commissioner Evan suggested Monet’s Water Lilies be included in
          the artwork. Both Mr. Tatton and Mr. Laterveer agreed that it was a great idea.

          Commissioner Marina inquired about ADA accessibility during construction. Mr.
          Tatton confirmed that ADA access would remain available throughout construction
          and that the number of ADA parking stalls would meet State code requirements.
          Commissioner Graden asked about ownership and public access; Mr. Tatton stated
          that the facility will operate as a not-for-profit, will be open for public enjoyment, and
          that the City will retain ownership of the land.

          Public Comment:

              •   Karen Corneilous, resident, expressed concerns regarding parking, particularly
                  related to parking availability at the All-Abilities Park.
              •   Jane Pearce of Cascade asked about zero-scaping and water usage.
              •   Terry Ewing of Villas inquired about the length of the pool’s seasonal
                  operation, swim meets, and swim lessons.

          The Chair reminded attendees that the scope of the agenda item was limited to
          building elevations and landscaping.

          In response to public comments, Mr. Tatton stated that waterwise landscaping would
          be implemented. He explained that the upper pool would be open year-round with

          heated decks, while other pools would be closed during the winter months. Mr. Tatton
          concluded by thanking Utah City for donating funds to support the project.

          MOTION: Commissioner Steele motioned to accept the site plan amendment for
          Utah City Wellness Center and Resort Pool phase 2, with the conditions as
          presented, with the suggested modification for the last one in the Spa building.
          Commissioner Pearce seconded the motion. Yes: Commissioners Evans, Fagg,
          Huntington, Ostler, Pearce, and Steele. No: None. Motion Passed 6-0.

    Planning Commission – Meeting Minutes Summary
Floodplain Regulation Update – National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) Compliance

The Planning Commission reviewed a proposed update to the City’s floodplain management
ordinance, which is required to maintain compliance with the National Flood Insurance Program
(NFIP) following updated FEMA (Federal Emergency Management Agency) flood hazard
determinations.

Public Works Director and City Engineer Naseem Ghandour explained that the update is part of
a public input process requested by the State. He stated that the purpose of the ordinance update
is to reduce flood risk, protect public safety, maintain the City’s eligibility for flood insurance,
and preserve access to federal disaster assistance in the event of a flood-related emergency.

Chairperson Steele confirmed that the Planning Commission’s role at this stage was to provide
input on the proposed ordinance. Mr. Ghandour confirmed that an ordinance would be presented
to the City Council in June. He noted that the ordinance is based on a State-provided model and
is intended to ensure compliance with FEMA and the NFIP, while aligning with City and State
of Utah requirements.

Mr. Ghandour informed the Commission that a public comment period would be held from May
1 through May 31, 2026. Public comments may be submitted through the City’s website, and
notice of the comment period would be provided through public meetings, social media, and the
City’s website.

Commissioner Martina asked about public awareness and education related to emergency
procedures. Mr. Ghandour stated that public education was not addressed within the scope of
this ordinance; however, he noted that the Utah Department of Public Safety offers resources for

public access. He encouraged commissioners and members of the public to contact his office
with any questions or concerns regarding the floodplain regulations.
