89/91. The proposal is for a 2,540 SF addition to the east side of the existing, 2,570 SF building.

connect the south and east sidewalks in front of the new addition.

As proposed the south fagade exceeds the 30 percent, but the east and west facades do not meet
the requirement. Aimee discussed options for meeting the intent of the fenestration requirement,
including additional landscaping or trellising along those facades. As conditioned, the project meets
this minimum requirement.

Staff/Proponent: Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director, reviewed the Planning
Commissions role when reviewing code amendments, which included:
e Consider information and weigh fact versus fiction.
Represent the good of the many versus the good of the few.
Make decisions based on the law and good planning, not public statement or public pressure.
Understand and respect constitutional rights on all sides.
Don’t pre-load individual decision making process (come in with an open mind).

M. DeSimone reviewed general property owner rights and said that while property owners have the
right to use and enjoy their property and a right to engage in any legal activity on their property, that
legal right of use can’t infringe on neighboring owners and their rights of use and enjoyment.

M. DeSimone reviewed the basic assumption of the rights behind a single-family residence zone is
that it is residential in nature, one house on one lot, typical accessory uses, e.g., garage, pool, patio,
etc., are permitted, and generally no commercial uses.

Through the legislative process, residential uses have evolved to allow more institutional uses such
as churches, parks, daycares, or schools, things typically associated with residential uses. Further
evolution of the rights behind residential zones have allowed for some limited commercial uses such
as home occupations, short term rentals, accessory dwelling units, and increased occupancy levels.
The rights that are encompassed in the legal use of a property are typically defined through zoning
and the legislative processes. Homeowners have the right to own a property or a home, but do not
have the right to turn that home into a STR, home occupation, or daycare unless local zoning allows
for it.

M. DeSimone discussed the three different LDC amendments and who or what the impetus behind
the proposed changed. The proposed changes to the Home Occupation standards were staff initiated
and the result of operational issues. The proposed changes to the Accessory Dwelling Unit standards
were directed by the City Council and based on earlier workshops with staff and public input into
ADUs. The proposed changes to the Short Term Rental standards were directed by the City Council
and were a result of input and resident feedback the Council has received about STRs in residential
neighborhoods.

Based on the August 8" Planning Commission public hearing and Commission direction, staff
presented the additional LDC changes to the Commission for the August 22"4 hearing.

M. DeSimone reviewed the proposed code amendment for Short Term Rentals (STR) and explained
that the Mayor and City Council initiated this code amendment questioning if short term rentals should
be permitted in all residential neighborhoods based on complaints received regarding the noise,
strangers, commercial use, and parking associated with STRs.

Some of the concerns that have been raised about STRs center around the current housing market.
Every residential dwelling used as a short term rental removes that dwelling from housing a long term
family which contributes to a residential neighborhood. It is believed that STRs drive up housing
prices although there is no data to support that. And, in the larger housing picture, 21 — 37 STRs in
a City with approximately 18,590 dwelling units, it seems the effect on housing is minimal or
unmeasurable.

Some of the concerns that have been raised about STRs center around neighborhood impact
including the belief that ST’s destroy the integrity of the neighborhood, that residents are forced to
shoulder the impact of STRs while the commercial gain is realized by the property owner, STRs are
an impact on residents’ rights to enjoy their property, STRs bring nuisances, strangers, and a
potential loss in property values.

Some of the benefits brought up include tourism/economic benefits, homeowners able to afford a
house with income generated by the STR, STRs provide an alternative to traditional lodging, and
STRs bring diversity to neighborhoods.

The proposed STR code amendments are not based on City operational issues or enforcement
issues but are based on resident feedback to the Council and Mayor after some STRs have been
approved through the CUP process.

M. DeSimone reviewed the current CUP permitting and business licensing procedures for STRs
stating that the current system is sound. The code enforcement process was also reviewed, stating
that the City receives very few complaints about licensed STRs. There was also discussion about
the City enforcing private HOA CC&Rs. M. DeSimone stated that it isn’t the City’s role or responsibility
to enforce private CC&Rs; rather, it is the responsibility of residents choosing to live in those
neighborhoods to do so. The Commission asked about informing applicants about the need for an
applicant to seek an approval of their HOA before applying to the City.

The Commission determined that the 1,000 foot distance between STRs should only apply to single
family residential zones, and STRs in the multi-family zones should be at least 500 feet apart.

The Commission discussed requiring STRs to be owner occupied. M. DeSimone said that owner
occupancy means that it is the owner’s primary legal place of residence whether they stay there a
certain number of nights per year or not. Commissioner Doutre said that requiring owner occupancy
wouldn’t allow STRs to be investor properties for out of state residents.

Commissioner Guth said that the code we have is working, and if it is too restrictive people will not
bother going through the permitting process.

Commissioner Doutre said that the opposition received from the neighborhoods when an STR applies
for a permit is data that needs to be considered.

Commissioner Doutre asked if it can be required in the business license process for applicants to
verify that STRs are allowed by their HOA, if applicable.
