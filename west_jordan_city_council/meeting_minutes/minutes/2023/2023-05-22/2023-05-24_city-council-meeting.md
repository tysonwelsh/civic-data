       b. Resolution No. 23-032 approving a substantial amendment to the Program Year
          2022 Annual Action Plan for transfer of funds from Contingency to Emergency
          Housing Repair and Accessibility Improvements and authorizing the Mayor to sign
          associated agreements
          Associate Planner Lisa Elgin briefed the Council regarding an agenda item aimed at
          assisting applicants in increasing their expenditure beyond the usual amount. In 2020,
          the applicants received a higher funding allocation than usual, but had a reduced
          timeframe for spending it. As a result, they operated under an extended performance
          period that gradually decreased each year. However, last year’s allocated funds only
          covered expenses for nine months, leading to increased costs. Additionally, the applicants
          had undertaken costly accessibility projects. Having exhausted all funds provided to
          them, they requested an additional $175,000 to be sourced from the contingency fund, to
          support their operations until June 30, 2023.

       a. Resolution No. 23-040 authorizing the Mayor to execute an amended interlocal
          agreement with Salt Lake County, allocating TRCC Grant funds towards capital
          costs for the West Jordan Performing Arts Building
          Senior Management Analyst Jeremy Olsen presented a proposed amended interlocal
          agreement with Salt Lake County allocating Tourism, Recreation, Culture, and
          Convention (TRCC) Grant funds toward capital costs for the West Jordan Performing Arts
          Building. Responding to a question from Council Member Whitelock, Mr. Olsen said the
          County would contribute the expected $2 million even if the total project costs were less
          than the $13 million listed on the form she referenced submitted by the City. He said the
          County’s contribution would be guaranteed when the agreement was signed. Council
          Member Worthen commented that she did not believe the agreement correctly
          documented project costs and would vote against it. Council Member Whitelock
          expressed concern with the numbers included in the amended interlocal agreement.

           Chair McConnehey oriented those present with history of the project. Council Member
           Worthen repeated her concern with the numbers, not the wording of the document. She
           pointed out that the $5.6 million from the City and the $2.2 million from the County
           together totaled $7.8 million, which was short of the $8 million projected project cost.

           Council Member Jacob felt the numbers did add up noting $800,000 was spent on
           architect services, leaving $7.2 million. He commented that $13 million would be the cost
           to build the original design, but the design had been simplified to stay within the $8
           million budget. Council Member Pack agreed with Council Member Jacob. He expressed
           the opinion that when you were gifted money, you took it and made things work, and
           pointed out the project had been scaled down. He believed the County would be
           amenable to providing more money when they saw the City was serious about moving
           forward.

           City Administrator Korban Lee believed Council Member Worthen was looking at a
           document from the original project proposal. He said the TRCC Committee approved the
           amended scope on April 28, 2023 for $8 million. The Council discussed the many
           documents attached to the meeting agenda, and the TRCC approval of the amended
           scope.

           Responding to a question from Council Member Whitelock, Mr. Olsen said there was no
           deadline to approve the agreement, but the agreement should be signed before getting
           started on the project. Council Member Worthen asked to postpone the issue to obtain
           the correct documents with numbers that lined up. Mr. Lee said the Council could table
           the issue, but tabling would potentially slow down the project. He expressed a desire to

       8b. Overview and Discussion of the FY2024 Tentative Budget
           Chair McConnehey explained that Council Members had provided a list of items for
           discussion and asked for a brief presentation of their items and determine whether or
           not there was consensus from Council to adjust the budget accordingly. Administrative
           Services Director Danyce Steck prepared slides in the order she received them from the
           Council.

             Council Member Pack returned at 7:18 pm

             Regarding the topic of a possible property tax increase, Chair McConnehey suggested
             waiting to discuss it until the County provided FY2024 numbers. Council Member Jacob
             viewed the issue as a policy discussion, and expressed the opinion that if the City did not
             implement a small regular increase, a large increase would be necessary in a few years
             to catch up. It was noted that the Council voted at a previous meeting to implement truth
             in taxation for FY2024 with a possible property tax increase of up to 5%. Council
             Member Worthen expressed support for a regular inflationary tax increase; however,
             she said she would not vote in favor of an increase in FY2024 with a “want” included in
             the budget.

            Council Member Jacob believed if the budget included increased property tax revenues,
            he would be in favor of doubling the Urban Forestry Fund to increase ongoing tree
            maintenance (currently $100,000). Council Member Whitelock and Chair McConnehey
            expressed support for adding $50,000. Council Member Green was in favor of adding
            $25,000-$30,000. Chair McConnehey said he would want to increase the Urban Forestry
            budget in general, without clarifying the amount for ongoing tree maintenance. Council
            Member Jacob was willing to compromise on an amount that received consensus from
            the Council. After further discussion and answers from staff, a majority of Council agreed
            to allocate $150,000 to the Urban Forestry Fund.

            Council Member Jacob expressed interest in funding a public arts program at a
            suggested amount of $75,000 in the FY2024 Budget. Council Member Worthen would
            be in favor if the program could be started without increasing property taxes. She
            emphasized that she would be in favor of an increase to pay for “needs”, but not “wants”.
            Council Member Jacob said the Council had expressed a desire to focus on how the City
            looked for years, and suggested a public arts program would contribute to that goal and
            would be an investment that would add interest and pay off in the long-term. Council
            Member Green suggested adding art to the construction budget for a planned
            roundabout project. Staff explained there were restrictions on how federal money could
            be spent for such projects. Chair McConnehey would be supportive at a lower dollar
            amount, closer to $25,000 to begin with, and suggested working with sponsors. Council
            Member Pack suggested beginning with $50,000. Council Member Jacob expressed
            support for $50,000. Council Member Whitelock expressed support for getting sponsors
            and commented that art meant different things to different people. Council Member
            Worthen was willing to discuss a program. The Council continued discussion, and a
            majority of the Council appeared to support adding $25,000 to the budget.

            Police Chief Wallentine answered questions about estimated costs of an Explorer
            Program and a Citizen Academy. He expressed the desire not to add more to the
            workload in his department without additional staff support. The Chief said his
            department was trying to connect with the community in ways the community wanted
            to connect. Chair McConnehey suggested the Police Department employ someone to
            focus on community engagement and voiced support of $100,000 for the Chief to use for
            personnel and community engagement. Chief Wallentine spoke of federal grant
            possibilities. Council Member Jacob wanted to add funding to the Police Department
            Budget for programs not currently in the Budget if a property tax increase were
            implemented. Council Member Whitelock expressed support. Chief Wallentine
            described planned programs and programs he would implement with sufficient funding.
            He said being able to provide the best training in the State was important to maintaining
            the Department and expressed support for making sure the Fire Department also
            received needed training.

            Council Member Whitelock said she would support $40,000 for the Explorer Program
            and Citizen Academy with the personnel already included in the budget. Council
            Member Worthen agreed. Council Member Jacob felt there were always more needs and
            reiterated that his idea was to provide additional funding to the Police Department if a
            property tax increase was approved. A majority of the Council expressed support for
            including $40,000 in the budget for the Explorer Program and Citizen Academy.
