Recognizing President and CEO of Visit of Ogden. Sara Toliver, for being
named to the Utah Tourism Hall of Fame
          A memo from Council staff regarding recognition of President and CEO ofVisit Ogden, Sara Toliver, for being named to
the Utah Tourism Hall of Fame came before the Council for consideration. Vice Chair White read the certificate of recognition
issued by the Ogden City Council and Mayor Caldwell and presented Ms. Toliver with the certificate.
          Ms. Toliver thanked the Mayor and Council for this recognition; she was shocked by the initial recognition from the Utah
Tourism Hall of Fame and noted that Ogden City's recognition doubles the impact of the award for her. She is grateful to her staff
for their support and for nominating her for this award. The tourism industry has evolved over the years, but one thing that has
been constant is that she has always felt welcomed and supported by the City Council and City Administration. She appreciates
the City's acknowledgement of the impact that tourism has on the community and for Ogden's leadership for making this such a
great community to live in and to visit.
          Council members spoke to their personal experiences with Ms. Toliver and Visit Ogden; they commented on the passion
of Ms. Toliver and her team and their great representation of the community.

Weber State University's Division of Student Affairs
          Dr. Enrique Romo, Weber State University's Assistant Vice President for Student Affairs, discussed access and diversity
programs offered through his office. The Office of Access & Diversity's (OAD) mission is to increase higher education, access,
persistence, and graduation rates for under-represented students. OAD strives to transform lives through the power of higher
education. The goal is to give students the best experience possible and to provide the tools and resources to help them be
successful. By taking students seriously and validating their experiences, OAD can help them achieve their academic and personal
goals. He also spoke to the University's goal to become an emerging Hispanic serving institution and efforts at meeting the needs
of the community. He thanked University President Mortensen for his support of this endeavor and invited President Mortensen to
address the Council.
          President Mortensen announced that Allied Bank has agreed to sponsor the Emerging Hispanic Serving Institution
Initiative; they will provide $500,000 over the next five years for the Initiative. He thanked the Council and Mayor for their
partnership as well.
          Dr. Romo then introduced students who have been successful as a result of these program, a few of which spoke to the
meaning of the Council and Mayor's recent adoption of a joint proclamation declaring September 15 to October 15 as Hispanic
Heritage Month in Ogden City.

Update and review of communications plan for the proposed rezone of 550
East 900 North extended from the October 5, 2021 meeting
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the rezone of property at 550 East 900
North came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated a developer has petitioned to rezone a vacant church property
from R-1-6 (single-family residential) to R-3 (multi-family residential). The purpose of the rezone is to demolish the vacant
church and build townhomes. The Planning Commission determined that this rezone request does comply with the General Plan,
and that townhomes would create an effective transition from higher density housing at Washington Boulevard to single-family
housing to the east of the property in question. It was also noted that a new development would be preferable to a vacant church
lot, which could fall into disrepair and negatively impact the neighborhood. The Planning Commission unanimously
recommended rezoning the property from R-1-6 to R-3. The Council voted to extend this item during the October 5, 2021 Council
meeting. Due to extensive public comment in opposition to the petition, as well as misunderstood language in the community
plan, the Council felt it would be best to take some extra time to understand the plan and address the concerns of the
neighborhood.
          Council Policy Analyst Watkins summarized the memo and provided a review of the communications plan developed by
Council staff. All residents who spoke during the October 5, 2021 meeting provided their contact information in order for Council
staff to communicate with them regarding future discussions ofthe agenda item. It is scheduled for a work session discussion on
October 19, 2021 and the petitioner and all residents will be invited to participate in that discussion. From that point, the Council
can determine the next action to be taken, which could include placement of the item on another meeting agenda.

Public Comments
           Tawney Renee stated she is presenting as "We the People of the United States of America, unincorporated"; Tawny
Renee is an unincorporated person. Her lawful person is the American State National, which means she is not a federal, territorial,
or municipal corporate citizen or employee. She is here tonight to petition for redress for two cases she has with Weber County
regarding her private property and an unconstitutional trespass. Weber County is trying to make her responsible for corporate
statutes and codes, whereas only corporate citizens are responsible for such corporate statutes and codes. She would like to work
with City Administration to resolve the issues so that the trespasses of domain no longer take place in local courts and so that there
is no extortion, fraud, or maladministration with regard to the legal statuses she has referenced. She is seeking advice from the
City on the best way to resolve her legal issues with the County and the local court system. Chair Blair advised Ms. Renee to work
with Chief Administrative Officer Johnson regarding the matter.
           Heath Satow stated that listening to the public comments regarding the development occurring at Business Depot Ogden
(BDO) on the border of Marriott- during the last City Council meeting was heartbreaking. He visited the area the next day to gain
his own understanding of the situation and he encouraged anyone unfamiliar with the situation to do the same. It is much worse
than he imagined; a place that once had beautiful mountain views and morning sunshine now literally feels like the inside of a
cold, dark prison. It is horrifying and is no place that he would ever consider living. He stated the Mayor indicated there is literally
nothing he can do about the matter besides schedule meetings for the residents to yell at him. Some Council members have said
that other cities in the· area should assume some of the responsibility for providing affordable housing for the area, but he
wondered how those cities should be willing to engage in those discussions when Ogden has been willing to destroy those
communities for its own profit. The City of Ogden would be bankrupt without the partnership with The Boyer Company and
BDO; every problem that the City encounters is paid for with BDO lease revenue, but as such, the expansion that was agreed upon
years ago that is ruining the lives of the people who live on 1200 West is the City's responsibility. To not do anything about the
issue is the same as accepting 'blood money' from the past; the City of Ogden ruined the lives of those people for profit and in his
mind that is immoral. The Swift Building was contaminated decades ago by a local family that got wealthy partly by using the
building for a chemical dump; the City decided that even though it was not at fault, it was the right thing to do to take on millions
in costs for liability ofremediating the site. Righting these wrongs are the best that the City can do. It is necessary to determine
how much harm the residents in Marriott-Slaterville are suffering because of something that directly benefits Ogden and
compensate those residents accordingly. Even if the City is legally able to get away with doing nothing, doing nothing would be
immoral at best; he encouraged the Council to consider the moral path on this issue.
