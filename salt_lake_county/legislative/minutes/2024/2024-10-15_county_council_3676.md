Ms. Rashelle Hobbs, County Recorder, read the following statement:
Good afternoon, 
I want to take a moment to directly address the inclusion of the Property 
Watch informational insert in the 2024 tax notices. 
I hope everyone here is familiar with Property Watch, a free notification 
service that I designed and implemented in 2019. After being elected, I 
worked closely with state Senator Karen Mayne on this initiative to better 
inform homeowners of recorded changes to their property - namely, owners 
wanting to confirm their mortgage loans were paid in full and they owned 
their homes outright. 
Page 3 of 24
County Council Meeting Minutes October 15, 2024
However, I quickly became aware of predatory companies running 
commercials and mail ads for a similar service. These ads targeted some of 
our most vulnerable community members -our seniors. These companies 
were promising to "lock and monitor home titles" to prevent fraud, using 
scare tactics to convince seniors that their homes could be stolen if they 
didn’t sign up -and pay steep fees -while most already live on fixed incomes . 
It has only become more important to me that property owners know about 
the Property Watch service. 
For the past four years, my team and I have been urging Salt Lake County 
property owners not to fall for these fees and to use our free service instead . 
I also shared the Property Watch program with my colleagues in other 
counties so they could offer this vital service to their communities. Due to 
the program's success and the significant rise of title fraud cases in the real 
estate industry, the Utah legislature decided to require all county recorders 
to offer similar services and ensure all property owners are made aware of 
them. 
The informational insert included in the property tax notice this year is Salt 
Lake County ’s implementation of Senate Bill 165, "Recording Notice 
Requirements Amendments," which was sponsored by Senator Harper and 
Representative Stenquist. The bill passed with unanimous support in both 
chambers, underscoring the Utah legislature ’s shared commitment to 
protecting property owners and raising awareness of serious scams like 
title fraud. 
Per the bill's requirements, county recorders must announce property alert 
systems for the next three years in the annual tax notices - beginning in 
2024, again in 2025, and finally in 2026. The goal is to ensure property 
owners are aware of this free government service meant to keep them 
informed about any documents recorded against their properties. 
This year, my team and I originally considered adding a few lines of text 
directly onto the tax notice itself. However, the Treasurer ’s office counseled 
us that that route would likely be difficult for recipients to notice and read . 
We initially considered the smaller format due to budget concerns, but after 
a careful second review, we found that we could cover the cost this year 
without impacting other operations. Additionally, upon Treasurer staff 
advice, we determined that an insert would be far more effective. This 
decision was made because inserts are more noticeable than fine print, and

the spirit of SB 165 is to encourage sign -ups for Property Watch, 
something I believe is best achieved through clear, accessible information. 
The insert was designed in -house to match the marketing materials we use 
during community outreach, including visits to county libraries and senior 
centers, where Property Watch is particularly critical. My picture was 
included in the insert, and is included in Property Watch, Data Services, 
and other Office of the Recorder designs and materials, because I believe 
it’s important for residents to recognize and feel connected to their elected 
officials and to be able to identify the duties that different elected officials 
perform. I want them to feel assured this service is offered by a trusted, 
legitimate source. 
There was no special request from, or treatment granted to, me in the 
creation and inclusion of this insert. Other elected offices have used this 
method to share important information with the public because it works. 
The return on investment is outstanding. Since the electronic tax notices 
went out on October 1st, we have had more than 2,000 registrations for 
Property Watch. That’s more sign -ups in the first two weeks of October - 
than January through September of this year . Let’s note that the median 
home price in Salt Lake County is around $ 500k. For just under $ 10,000 - 
paid for out of my budget - invested in the insert, hundreds of millions of 
dollars’ worth of property value in Salt Lake County have an added level 
of security against fraud. This is an incredible feat for Salt Lake County ’s 
good governance. I am glad to share this success with all of you. Paper tax 
notices have also gone out and I would be happy to make a report to this 
body after a couple weeks with the most up to date data. 
In closing my remarks, I must emphasize that the Council ’s attorney and 
the District Attorney ’s office have already addressed this topic, both 
confirming that there is no inappropriate use of county resources involved 
with the insert. The sole purpose of the insert is to promote Property Watch, 
a service designed to protect our residents and their property from fraud. 
Thank you for your attention, and I look forward to continuing our efforts 
to protect the interests of Salt Lake County property owners.

Rep. Gay Lynn Bennion , Utah State Legislature, stated in September, she 
was given a tour of the canyons by the Central Wasatch Commission and 
later met with the directors of all the ski resorts and UDOT.  They all 
concluded that there had to be a law to change the current Cottonwood 
Canyons Sticker Program to make it enforceable.  Alta and Snowbird receive 
1.3 million ski visits a year, which was comparable to the largest national 
parks, and Little Cottonwood Canyon has more avalanches than any other 
canyon in the country.  UDOT’s current Traction Law is applied statewide

be effective from about November 1st to April 15th. 
Council Member Theodore read the following statement:
Traction Law and Public Safety in the Cottonwood Canyons 
On October 17, 2024, UDOT will be holding a press conference to 
announce the 2024/2025 UDOT Cottonwood Canyons Sticker Program .  
The sticker program was created in an effort to increase safety in the 
Cottonwood Canyons during the winter months.  Vehicles with a tread 
depth of 5/32 are able to receive the sticker at certain tire shops. 
Unsafe traction compromises the safety for all canyon users by slowing and 
blocking traffic flow, including blocking emergency services vehicles. 
UDOT has established an agreement with the Sheriff ’s Office, Sandy City 
Police, and Cottonwood Heights Police to fund additional officers at the 
base of the Cottonwood Canyons.  On traction required days, vehicles with 
the traction sticker will be allowed through while those without the sticker 
will be pulled over to ensure that their tires meet the minimum 
requirements or that they have chains. 
We are also supportive of efforts by the Utah Legislature, including those 
by Representative Gay Lynn Bennion, to increase traction enforcement as 
well as to modify and update the traction laws and reconciling tread depth 
requirements to provide greater safety in the Cottonwood Canyons. 
As part of this effort, the Council will also direct Kara Trevino, its 
legislative affairs coordinator to work closely with Representative Bennion 
and other stakeholders during the upcoming legislative session, and to 
update the Council about progress on improving vehicle safety and 
traction laws in the Cottonwood Canyons.

A RESOLUTION OF THE SALT LAKE COUNTY COUNCIL

RECITALS
A. Salt Lake County (“County”), has an ownership interest in real 
property (the “Property”), located in Kearns Metro Township, in an area 
known as Camp Kearns, at approximately 6162 West Lightning Lane, 
Kearns, Utah, Parcel No ., 20-12-256-004, and as more specifically described 
in the quitclaim deed attached to the Remainder Property Purchase 
Agreement as Exhibit A.
Page 20 of 24
County Council Meeting Minutes October 15, 2024
B. County cooperated with several adjacent property owners to dedicate 
land for the development of public roads in Camp Kearns, resulting in the 
recording of the Camp Kearns Road Dedication Plat (the “Road Plat ”) on 
May 3, 2021.
C. After recording the Road Plat, County still owned property in gaps 
between parcels owned by adjacent property owners and the dedicated 
public road (the “Gap Parcels”).
D. As part of the agreement with the adjacent property owners to create 
and construct the public roads, County had agreed to convey its interest in 
these Gap Parcels to certain adjacent property owners.
E. After the construction of the public roads and recording the Road 
Plat, additional discrepancies were discovered concerning ownership 
interests adjacent to the public roads, specifically, a parcel owned at the time 
of the recording of the Road Plat by Andermien Enterprises, LLC.
F. In order to address this ownership discrepancy and to complete the 
transfer of County ’s interests in the Gap Parcel, County desires to enter into 
a Remainder Property Purchase Agreement attached hereto as Exhibit 1, and 
to quitclaim any interests in the Gap Parcel to Eric Verharst the successor in 
interest to Andermien Enterprises, LLC.
G. The quitclaim deed to be signed by County (the “Quitclaim Deed ”) is 
attached to the Remainder Property Purchase Agreement as Exhibit A.
H. It has been determined that the best interests of County and the general 
public will be served by executing the Remainder Property Purchase 
Agreement and the Quitclaim Deed which are attached to this Resolution.
NOW, THEREFORE, IT IS HEREBY RESOLVED by the Salt Lake 
County Council that the sale and conveyance of the Property by Quitclaim 
Deed to Eric Verharst, as provided in the Remainder Property Purchase 
Agreement is hereby approved; and the Mayor is hereby authorized to 
execute the Remainder Property Purchase Agreement, and the Mayor and 
County Clerk are hereby authorized to execute the Quitclaim Deed, attached 
to the Remainder Property Purchase Agreement as Exhibit A, and to deliver 
the fully executed documents to the County Real Estate Section for delivery 
to Eric Verharst.
