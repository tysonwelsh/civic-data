A.     Discussion and consideration of the Landscape and Irrigation Policies and Design Standards and
       Drawings
Parks Division Manager Dave Naylor presented a PowerPoint to the Council regarding proposed
updates to the Landscape and Irrigation Policies and Design Criteria. Updates had not been made
since 2007. Mr. Naylor explained that the Planning Department, Engineering Department, and Water
Division reviewed sections that pertained to their areas of responsibility, and the Planning
Commission approved the proposed updates.

Mr. Naylor suggested that image PK-025 regarding park strips would not align with Jordan Valley
Water Conservancy District’s recommendations regarding water conservation, and suggested the
Council could exclude PK-025 in their motion and direct Staff to continue working with Jordan
Valley Water Conservancy District.
    City Council Work Session APPROVED Minutes - June 24, 2020                                             Page 2

     Mr. Naylor spoke of water conservation, and suggested there was more to look at than just the amount
     of turf area in a landscape: plant health; aesthetics; ease of maintenance; quantity of acreage and
     linear feet of park strip; soil structure; functional uses; and irrigation systems (design and materials).
     He showed a photograph of his home and yard with blue grass turf that had not been watered more
     than once per week during the summer season to demonstrate what can be done to contribute to water
     conservation.

     The Council discussed the proposed updates and asked questions of Mr. Naylor. Council Member
     Green referred to the PK-025 image of park strip design and suggested it looked like a lot of
     maintenance would be required. Mr. Naylor agreed the trees included in the park strip design would
     require maintenance, and said they were included in the design to aesthetically create a canopy effect.

     Council Member Whitelock asked if the irrigation controls on City property were smart enough to not
     water on days with rain. Mr. Naylor explained rain-delay parameters available with the irrigation
     control system used by the City. Responding to a question from Chair McConnehey, Mr. Naylor
     explained reasons for tree health issues in certain hardscaped microclimates in the City. He
     encouraged the City Council to keep their minds open with regard to water conservation. Chair
     McConnehey thanked Mr. Naylor for his efforts and work on the updates.

     B.     New Business
     Ms. Steck briefly described Federal Community Development Block Grant and Cares Act Funding
     issues scheduled to appear on future meeting agendas.

     C.   Unfinished Business
     The Council reviewed a list of unfinished business items from previous meeting agendas.

    Chair Chris McConnehey adjourned the work session at 6:49 pm.

6.      BUSINESS ITEMS
        a. Discussion and possible action to approve Resolution No. 20-37 authorizing the transfer
            of monies from the Enterprise Fund to other City funds
The Council discussed the proposed transfer of funds from Enterprise Funds to other City funds.
Council Member Lamb said he was not in favor of the utility transfer but understood why the utility
transfer was discussed as an option, and said he believed the other options available did not make
sense. He asked for the utility transfer to only occur in FY 2021. Council Member Jacob agreed with
Council Member Lamb and said he philosophically did not think the utility transfer was the right thing
to do. Council Member Pack said he believed the utility transfer was the most viable option for the
situation. He agreed with Council Member Lamb that he would not want the utility transfer to occur
next year.

Council Member Whitelock said she would vote in favor of the utility transfer because the City had a
budget problem and had been balancing the budget off of reserve funds for many years. She
commented that 25% of property in the City was tax exempt and did not contribute to the General
Fund. She agreed that the utility transfer was a tax, but pointed out that the utility transfer would have
a smaller impact on the average home owner than a property tax increase. She acknowledged that
laying off more City employees was an option, but expressed concern with potential employee
City Council Meeting APPROVED Minutes - June 24, 2020                                             Page 5

turnover over time. She said she hoped the utility transfer could be reduced in the future, and said she
favored economic development for long-term budget improvement.

Council Member Green stated that for a multitude of reasons the current City budget was not in good
fiscal shape, and something needed to be done. He said he hoped the utility transfer could be reduced
over time, and said he did not believe raising property taxes in FY 2021 would be the right thing.
Council Member Green emphasized that it was a difficult situation, commented that the Council
Members lived in West Jordan and were affected by decisions they make, and said he had not heard
any citizen comments wanting essential services cut. Council Member Worthen expressed agreement
with comments from Council Members Whitelock and Green.

Council Member Jacob said there was a reason tax exempt properties were tax exempt, and said he did
not agree with taxing all properties through a utility transfer. Council Member Green said he disagreed
and pointed out that even tax exempt properties paid a utility franchise tax. He suggested the proposed
utility transfer would be consistent with other utilities.

MOTION:       Council Member Worthen moved to approve Resolution No. 20-37 authorizing the
              transfer of monies from the Enterprise Fund to other City funds. Council Member
              Whitelock seconded the motion.

Chair McConnehey said on principle he did not like the utility transfer. He said he believed tax exempt
entities were given that status for a reason. He said he wished there were a better policy available. He
commented that the utility transfer would allow everyone to participate in the care of the City, and
added that the utility transfer would have less impact on residents than a property tax increase. Chair
McConnehey emphasized that the language used was dictated by the State.

The vote was recorded as follows:
Council Member Green                   Yes
Council Member Jacob                   No
Council Member Lamb                    Yes
