Eccles Dinosaur Park Annual Report

The City Council has requested to receive information from local foundations and nonprofit organizations that have a
contractual agreement with Ogden City. These presentations help the Council to become more familiar with the objectives and
efforts of the organizations.

Angela Horn, Park Director, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide an overview of the annual report; this
included a description of the Park’s organizational mission and goals, successes and challenges over the past year and any future
plans for the organization. Mark Wayment, Dinosaur Park Board Member, referenced the section of Ms. Horn’s presentation
dealing with proposed increases to admission and membership rates; the Board has always been very attentive to the need to
ensure affordability for participation in events at the facility and he feels confident that the increases reflect changes in the
economy, but that the Park will still be accessible. There was discussion among the Council and Park representatives about
opportunities to offer admission fee waivers or identifying days throughout the year when admission to the Park would be free to
visitors. Additional discussion also centered on fluctuating attendance rates from year to year; significant capital projects at the | )
Park; special events; the contractual arrangement between the City and the Dinosaur Park and how that impacts other contracts
with other entities, such as the Wildlife Rehabilitation Center; and expansion plans for the Park and how that will impact the
Wildlife Rehabilitation Center.

Fiscal Year 2023-2024 Annual Action Plan
When receiving federal funds for economic development and housing programs, the City is required to submit a
Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) to the Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) every five years.
Project Coordinator Fuentes reported the current plan covers the period between Fiscal Year 2021, beginning July 1,
2020, and Fiscal Year 2025, ending June 30, 2025. The ConPlan is part of the City’s application to HUD for Community
Development Block Grants (CDBG) and HOME grant funding. Along with the ConPlan, the City must prepare and submit an
Annual Action Plan each year as part of the annual application for funds. The Annual Action Plan indicates how funding will be
used during the coming year and how the grant funds will further the goals in the Consolidated Plan. It establishes annual goals :
and outcomes for programs and projects funded by the CDBG and other federal grant funds. The FY24 Annual Action Plan is for
the fourth year of the five-year ConPlan period.

the future or possible discontinuation of various programs funded by HUD.
MAKE Ogden Downtown Zoning Ordinance Amendments and Zoning Map
Amendments — continued discussion
The Council received a presentation on the proposed changes at their March 28, 2023 work session meeting. A follow-up
work session is planned for April 4 to address questions that have arisen and to discuss any remaining concerns or issues. The
proposed amendments and rezones are scheduled for public input and Council consideration at the April 11, 2023 regular City
Council meeting. The Council may table the item to April 18, 2023 if there are concerns that they want addressed or if there are
changes requested by the Council.
Chair Choberka inquired as to the notification process for property owners who will be impacted by the proposed zoning
amendments. Planning Manager Brierley noted that all property owners receive personal notification and all residents within the
District will receive notices. He then referenced questions that have been asked regarding the proposed amendments to this point,
including changes to maximum building heights throughout the zoning district; options for addressing concerns about building
heights include:
1. Adopt the Planning Commission recommendation and limit development to east of Adams to 4-stories.
2. Include the Imagine Jefferson block in areas where 6-story buildings are allowed. .
3. Change all of C-9 to allow 6-story buildings.
4. Create a separate zone (C-9 limited?) for these few remaining parcels.
) Additionally, there have been questions regarding non-conforming uses, such as auto repair uses, manufacturing, storage,
_/ — outdoor retail, service stations, and drive-up service windows; electric vehicle charging stations on the Wonder Block; and code
regulations and limitations on the number of drinking establishments allowed in the zoning district.

Council Business
Flooding: The Council discussed eligible events and items that can be funded with a Neighbor Up grant; Council py
member White wondered if funding could be used to purchase and/or fill sandbags in a specific neighborhood and hold an event .
surrounding that activity. Chief Administrative Officer Johnson noted that the City has an adequate number of sandbags and sand,
and he is hopeful that neighborhood groups are not spending money on those items; he added that local emergency response
officials are encouraged to reach out to the City to help coordinate targeted flooding response in their neighborhoods.
Budget Goals and Guidelines: Council Executive Director Eller Smith reported Council staff proposes creating a
Council ad hoc committee to review an updated format of the Goals and Guidelines document and make recommendations
regarding the Council’s goals for Fiscal Year 2023-2024. The recommendations will be presented to the full Council at a future
work session. The Council briefly discussed the proposal and concluded to direct Council leadership to discuss and explore the
concept further, but there was support for the creation of an ad hoc committee.
