                      Utah Legal Requirements:
                        OPMA (Open and Public Meetings Act) – a “quorum” of public body
                      discuss and receive comments from the public.
                      “Quorum” is defined as a membership of public body.
                       “Public Hearing” as a hearing means the members of the public are
                      provided reasonable time to respond. All public hearings are public
                      meetings, but not all public meetings are hearings.

                     Rules of Order:
                      Modified versions of Robert’s Rules of Order. Motions and order of
                     motions, a motion must be completed or disposed of before another
                     motion comes forward. Individuals can make, second, debate, and vote
                     motions. After recognition is made by the chairman of the commission. A
                     motion requires a second to be considered. There are multiple types of
                     motions:
                         1. Main Motion – to introduce items into consideration.
                                a. Substantive Action – used to approve or deny
                                b. Recommendation – used when advising another body such
                                    as City Council.
                                c. Approval of Minutes
                         2. Subsidiary Motion – purpose is to change or affect the main
                            motion.
                                a. Amend – used to change the working of a motion before
                                    voting.
                                b. Postpone Definitely- delay a decision to a specific date
                                c. Postpone Indefinitely- “Kills” the motion without a direct
                                    vote.
                                d. Commit- sends the motion to a committee or staff for more
                                    research
                                e. Previous Question – Closes the debate, requires a 2/3
                                    majority.
                         3. Privileged Motion- Used for urgent or special matters unrelated to
                            pending business.
                                a. Adjourn-Ends the meeting
                                b. Recess- Take a short break
                                c. Question of Privilege – Addresses urgent matters affecting
                                    comfort or rights.

                         4. Incidental Motion – Allows questioning procedure concerning
                            other motions. Must be considered before the motion.
                                a. Point of Order – Used when members believe meeting rules
                                    are being violated.
                                b. Appeal – Challenges the ruling by the chair.
                                c. Division of the Question – Splits the motion into separate
                                    parts to be voted on individually.
                                d. Withdraw a Motion – Allows the original maker of a
                                    motion to retract before voting.
                         5. Motion to bring a question again – Revisits business acted upon
                            already.
                                a. Reconsider – Allows the body to vote on a motion that was
                                    earlier decided in the same meeting. Must be moved on by
                                    someone on the winning side.
                                b. Rescind – Voids a motion passed at an earlier meeting.
                                c. Take from the Table – Brings back a motion that was
                                    previously “laid on the table” for urgent business.
                         6. Planning Specific Actions – Not standard to Robert’s Rules but
                            common in Planning Commission.
                                a. Open/Close Public Hearing, and Motion to accept for the
                                    Record.

                     How to Handle the Unruly:
                         1. Establish Clear Rules of Conduct: Before the meeting or hearing
                            starts. The conduct is laid out.
                         2. Ensure Effective Leadership from the Chair – The chair will
                            maintain the order of the meeting. Enforce the rules, asserting
                            order before escalating tensions.
                         3. Address Disruptions Early – Deal with disruptions immediately.
                         4. Use Recess/Resources Strategically – If tensions are rising, the
                            chair can call for a recess until the public hearing can resume in a
                            calm and professional manner.
                         5. Document Incidents/Engage Law Enforcement if Necessary –
                            Keep record of disruptive behavior, necessary for legal actions.
                     Best Practices:
                         1. Adequate Preparation- Agenda is outlined and available to
                            participants.
                         2. Public Notice/Outreach – Transparency is used with adequate time
                            for public meetings and hearings.
                         3. Accessible & Welcoming Venues – Welcome environment for all.
                         4. Technological Integration – Technology to enhance public
                            meetings and hearings.
                         5. Clear/Respectful Communication – Foster respect and
                            comprehension.
                         6. Time Management – set time limit for each agenda item if
                            necessary.
                         7. Opportunities for Public Input – Provide opportunities for
                            comment submissions in all forms.
                         8. Recordkeeping/Transparency – Keep accurate meeting minutes,
                            with supporting documents.
                         9. Continuous Improvement – Embrace continuous improvement in
                            meeting and hearing practices.
          Mr. Seth Atkinson also presented it to the commission on Robert’s Rules of Order.
          Nephi City is an informal city, but there are benefits to having some formality. Seth
          lined out to the commission why, certain formality indicates to the public that there is
          certain behaviors required. Typically, we do not see a lot of interaction from the
          public until life is disrupted. Public members may or may not have studied the issues,
          and there may be preconceived notions of what the government is doing. They see the

          body in a position of authority, and they come with a lot of emotion. We want to
          create management of those emotions.
            Management and Staff of Nephi City have open doors, and we create that
          communication transparency, and staff are willing to discuss issues and facts. That
          also helps with the formality of the meetings. As for the council, there are a few
          options, one of the things we could do is bring up the podium or set a chair they have
          to come and talk. Rather than the popcorn of the audience, where chaos can happen.
          The Chair needs to direct the meeting. Recognize when you are in that place, you are
          in control of how that will go. Mr. Seth Atkinson then proceeded to run through what
          managed meetings are like when guided by the chair.
            Closing of public comment is when the chair will say that the public comment
          section of the meeting is closed, and then the planning commission will discuss and
          debate if necessary, the issue at hand. Tangents will not be permitted. Be specific in
          statement that this is not a comment period time after the public comment section
          time is passed. The staff are to assist the commission; meetings can be turned back to
          the staff for more information if the chair or commission member chooses. That is an
          effort to keep the temperature of the meeting down.
            IF the meetings go off the rails and if there is a safety issue, then you can
          discontinue the meeting and motion that all items are tabled. Commission can then
          leave the meeting, they are rare, but the rules are here to help and keep the
          temperature down. Emphasize they can come talk to the staff and spend more time
          understanding the issue. If there are an issue or too many questions from an
          individual, then you can move the item to table.

          done commenting.
