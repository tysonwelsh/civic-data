LOVELESS ESTATES PUD SUBDIVISION PLAT B APPROVED:

     Mr. Atkinson presented to  the council  the Loveless Estates PUD Subdivision Plat B final plat which is the second and final phase of the development.  The subdivision is located approximately at 800 to 900 N and 100 to 300 W.  The development is in the R-2 PUD overlay zone and will include 42 of the total 75 lots in the development.  There are fourteen (14) triple townhome banks in this phase of the PUD, each unit will be in separate ownership the same as in Plat A.
     Mr. Atkinson reported that a temporary power easement is needed on the northwest corner of Lot 39 where a temporary power pole will be located for approximately three years, while Nephi City’s power department does some system upgrades on the north of the property, at which point the temporary easement will be released.
    The developer has been informed that they may enter into a reimbursement agreement with the city, so that they may be reimbursed if a future landowner uses their installed infrastructure in the future. The city will collect their reimbursement funds and pay back the developer that portion owed. He also stated that as a PUD, the development is required to record CC&Rs for the property that includes the required upkeep of the mandatory open space areas by a homeowner’s association.
    Councilor Skip Worwood moved to accept the final plat of Loveless Estates PUD Subdivision Plat B.  Councilor Cowan seconded the motion.  The motion passed unanimously. 

POTENTIAL ANNEXATION PROPERTY MOVED TO LEVEL 1 ON THE ANNEXATOIN AREA MAP:

     Mr. Atkinson updated the Council that a concept plan for some property on the northwest side of the city has been received.   As previously discussed, it was determined that the Council would consider changing the annexation area level from a Level 2 to a Level 1 for properties after receiving a concept plan for the property requesting potential annexation. The concept drawings for the property reflect an industrial use which is consistent with the property zoning to the east and south of the property.   Four buildings have been proposed in the plan.   The property is contiguous to the city’s current limits. 
     Councilor Skip Worwood asked about how the council had wanted to have anyone wanting to annex property into the City be required to bring water rights with them.  Mr. Atkinson assured the Council that would happen but a policy for that directive has not been written or adopted yet.   As of now, we would make that requirement through an annexation agreement.  
     Councilor Travis Worwood asked if fee in lieu of water rights would be allowed.  He stated that if we are currently only using 28% of our water rights we own,  then he thought that a fee in lieu would be a good idea to help pay for our water project debt service.  
     Mayor Seely said that he would like to see land that the city annexes in be used as planned in a timely manner.  Many of the previous annexations have not been developed as presented at the time of annexation. 
     Councilor Cowan asked Mr. Atkinson if he would explain the different Levels on the annexation area map.  Mr. Atkinson reviewed the different levels.  
      Councilor Skip Worwood moved to change the property described on the northwest side of the city from a Level 2 to a Level 1 on the annexation area map.  Councilor Callaway seconded the motion.   The motion passed unanimously. 

NORTH RIDGE ESTATES SUBDIVISION PHASE F PROPERTY CHANGED TO LEVEL 1:

     Mr. Atkinson stated that several years ago the City Council approved the Jones Ridge Annexation (Later renamed the North Ridge Subdivision).  At the time there was a rough sketch of a proposed subdivision on the property and the Council, at the time, recommended an annexation that followed parcel lines to achieve a certain number of acres to be annexed.  It was the first annexation request the city had received in many years and the Council was somewhat cautious about the annexation.  Now there have been several subdivision phases that have been submitted and approved by the Council for this area.  In the northeast section of this annexation the road that will eventually become 1620 North has the annexation line running right through the middle of the road.  Staff has spoken to the Council in the past about allowing an additional amount of square footage to be annexed to the north of the road so that the developer could put homes on both sides of the road.  
     A concept plan has been submitted to the city to show proposed homes on the north of the property.  The concept plan also conforms to the city’s desire to maintain the city’s grid system transportation plan and to the North Area Transportation Plan completed earlier.
      Mr. Atkinson explained that because this is more of an “annexation addendum” rather than a true annexation, the developer has requested a waiver of moving through the annexation area levels and move directly to Level 1 where an annexation process can begin.  The staff is supportive of this request since the infrastructure to serve the homes in this annexation addendum has already been mostly installed.  The remaining portion that has not yet been installed would take place with a subdivision request for the last phase of the North Ridge Estates Subdivision. 
     Councilor Travis Worwood moved to make the North Ridge Subdivision Plat F a Level 1 on the annexation area map.   Councilor Skip Worwood seconded the motion.  The motion carried unanimously.

GOVERNOR’S OFFICE OF ECONOMIC OPPORTUNITY GRANT CONTRACT APPROVED:

     Mr. Atkinson informed the council that the City recently received news that it was the recipient of a Rural Communities Opportunity Grant administered by the Utah State Governor’s Office of Economic Opportunity.  Mr. Atkinson had applied for the grant to assist the city with the construction of the 138 kV line project.  Specifically, the grant will help to pay for the new substation next to Substation 3 and the metering yard that will be installed near Nortonville Road.  The grant is for $600,000 and they are willing to pay 90% of the grant up front.  A contract for the grant has been submitted by the state and has been reviewed by our City Attorney.  
     Councilor Travis Worwood moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the grant contract.  Councilor Skip Worwood seconded the motion.  The motion carried on a unanimous vote. 

VICIOUS DOG ORDINANCE 02-06-2024 ADOPTED:

      Mr. Atkinson explained that a local resident has expressed concern over the clarity of our dog ordinance.  After review from the City Attorney and the Police department, it was determined that some things could be made clearer.  The City Attorney drafted some changes to the dog ordinance and sent them to staff members for review.  These changes included a more robust definition of a vicious dog, specific fines for offenses, and procedures for the handling of a dog that violates the ordinance.  The Council reviewed the proposed changes to the city’s dog ordinance at their meeting on January 16th and recommended that the ordinance be brought back to the Council in the next regular meeting to consider it for action. 
     Councilor Worwood expressed his concern about repeat offenders that let their dogs run at large.  He felt like the owner should have three strikes and then their out. City Attorney Wright asked for clarification of what Councilor Worwood meant by out.   The Council considered  many different scenarios of dog issues and eventually agreed to them individually reviewing the current dog at large portion of the Nephi City Code and then come back with suggestions and ideas for revisions to the code.  
     Councilor Travis Worwood moved to adopt Ordinance 02-06-2024.  Councilor Cowan seconded the motion.  The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

JUB APPROVED FOR AWOS ENGINEERING SERVICES: 

   Mr. Atkinson reported that the City staff requested proposals from qualified engineering firms for the replacement of the Airport’s Automated Weather Observing System (AWOS) and for Municipal Advisor (MA) services for when the city issues debt.
      The AWOS system at the airport is outdated and needs to be replaced.  Due to some federal funding that was made available from the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law (BIL) passed by Congress, this project was able to move up the priority list and be funded this year.  City staff requested proposals from qualified engineering firms and put together a staff committee to make a recommendation.  Three proposals were received from JUB, Jviation (Woolpert), and Jones & DeMille.  Each proposal was from a good firm who could do a good job for the city  on this project.  After committee review, it was recommended that JUB be the engineering firm to assist with the replacement of the AWOS system.  They are a qualified firm who has worked with many Utah airports on numerous projects and seemed to fit this particular project very well. 
     Councilor Skip Worwood moved to approve JUB for the AWOS replacement project.  Councilor Callaway seconded the motion.  The motion passed unanimously. 

CREWS & ASSOCIATES APPROVED FOR MUNICIPAL ADVISORY SERVICES:

      Mr. Atkinson informed the Council that for several years the city has worked with Lewis Young Robertson & Burningham (LYRB)to provide municipal advisory services.  Their contract has expired so the City put out a request for firms to submit proposals for these services again.  Municipal advisors assist cities when they issue debt or bonds.  They help to get the best deals for the city and explore many financing options.  A review committee went over proposals from LRB (formerly LYRB), Zions Public Finance, Crews & Associates, and Baker Tilly.  After committee review, Crews & Associates was recommended for the Municipal Advisor role.  They had a lot of experience working with smaller cities and the reviews came back extremely positive.  
     Councilor Travis Worwood moved to approve Crews and Associates to act as Nephi City’s municipal advisors.  Councilor Cowan seconded the motion.  The motion passed unanimously.
