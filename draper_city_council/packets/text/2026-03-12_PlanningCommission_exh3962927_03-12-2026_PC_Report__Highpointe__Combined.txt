BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Site Plan and Parking Deviation for
approximately 1.02 acres located near the southwest corner of Bangerter Parkway and
Highland Drive, at approximately 193 E. Highland Drive (Exhibits B and C). The property is
currently zoned CR. The applicant is requesting that a Site Plan and Parking Deviation are
approved to construct a new office building on an existing pad site. The main part of the
site is developed with a commercial building and associated parking and improvements.

The existing building on the overall site was approved by the Planning Commission in
November 2021. The Highland Drive Office minor subdivision was also approved at that

Parking. The applicant is requesting a parking deviation since minimum required parking
for all existing and proposed uses exceeds the 10% natural deviation allowed by Section 9-
25-090 of the DCMC. The existing building and its current uses require between 227 and
283 parking stalls. Adding a total of 25,806 new square feet of office will require between
93 and 115 more parking stalls. With all uses combined the site would require between 320
and 398 parking stalls. There are 310 parking stalls currently built on the site between the
surface parking and the parking garage.

The proposed addition of one parking stall to the east of the proposed building is offset by
the loss of a stall in the parking garage needed to correct a separate deficiency in the
layout of the ADA stalls within the garage. The applicant will need to provide the applicable
number of ADA parking stalls for the entire site when a building permit for the new
building is reviewed. The parking deviation cannot reduce the required amount of ADA
parking.

A deficiency of 48 parking stalls exists without application of the 10% natural deviation, and
a deficiency of 10 parking stalls would still exist even with application of the natural
deviation. The applicant cannot meet the natural 10% deviation or reduction of stalls and
has submitted a parking study for the Planning Commission to consider a parking deviation
(Exhibit H).

The parking study does outline that based on counts of occupied parking stalls on 12
different days between September 16 and December 5 of last year, the average parking
calculation was 2.22 stalls per 1,000 gross feet of square footage. While this may be the
case, and it is less than what is required by the DCMC, it is only parking counts for 12
random days within a window of 80 days total. The current users of the site may not have

The 2021 site plan for the development was processed with the understanding that the
entire building would be used as general office. That 2021 site plan was approved with
excess parking for this proposed building pad and that was acceptable to staff to have the
excess parking knowing this pad would be developed eventually. However, the owner of
the existing building has allowed uses that have a more intensive parking requirements
than office use and there is now not enough parking for the proposed office building
without approval of a parking deviation.

Staff has concerns with the proposed square footage and future potential uses of the
building. The applicant could reduce the square footage of the proposed building and that
would require less parking.

The parking study notes that almost all the uses would all operate at the same time of the
day. Within the parking study, it states that office space, medical and reception uses have
different peak times. Staff would agree that the reception use would have a different peak

not lend itself to allowing on street parking.

Parking Modification. Chapter 25 of the DCMC makes provisions for the Planning
Commission to authorize a deviation to the strict compliance with the terms of the Code
regarding the calculation of the minimum and maximum number of required parking
spaces. DCMC Section 9-25-090 states that the number of parking spaces may be modified
to provide flexibility beyond the 10% (plus or minus) allowed in DCMC in order to adapt to
specific circumstances, reduce potential environmental impacts, and conserve resources.
The deviation to strict compliance is not justified based upon the parking study submitted
by the applicant. In order to approve a deviation to strict compliance with the parking
requirements of the DCMC, the Planning Commission must make findings based on the
type of use has outlined in DCMC Section 9-25-090(c)(4):

a.       For commercial, retail, office, and mixed use developments, the Planning
         Commission must find that:
     (1)    Adequate parking will be provided;
     (2)    The total number of spaces that would otherwise be required for each individual
            establishment in the development is overly burdensome for the site specific
            factors of the application;
     (3)    Estimated trade offs between businesses which are open when others are
            closed will not overburden the parking provided;
     (4)    There is an adequate availability of approved on street or shared parking for all
            associated uses;
     (5)    Site or use specific conditions or factors do not provide for compliance with the
            parking calculation used or parking requirements outlined in Section 9-25-100,
            Table 9-25-1 of this chapter;
     (6)    Transportation alternatives, including proximity to transit stations, provide
            adequate transportation to offset any reduction in parking;
     (7)    Any potential for future expansion or addition to the development will have or
            will provide adequate parking for that expansion or addition; and
     (8)    ADA compliant parking requirements are not proposed for adjustment.

Geotechnical and Geologic Hazards Review.
1.    All organics, topsoil, existing fill, and other deleterious material should be removed
      from below proposed building areas.
2.    The undocumented fill shall be removed from below proposed foundations, interior
      floor slab areas, and private utilities connected to public utilities.
3.     Permanent cut and fill slopes should not be steeper than 2 horizontal to 1 vertical
       (2H:1V).
4.     Footings for the structures may be supported on undisturbed natural soils, bedrock,
       or compacted structural fill that extends to undisturbed natural soils or bedrock.
5.     Footings may be designed using a maximum allowable bearing pressure of 3,500
       psf if supported on compacted structural fill or natural soils and 5,000 psf if
       supported on bedrock.
6.     Strip and spot footings should have a minimum width of 24 inches.
7.     Footings susceptible to frost should be located at a minimum depth of 30 inches.
8.     Basement foundation walls should be designed using an at-rest equivalent fluid
       weight of 65 pcf for static conditions.
9.     A perimeter foundation drain is required for the basement portion of the structures.
10.    Seismic analysis of the proposed structure at the site should be based on the
       following design spectral response accelerations:
               0.2 sec (short period) SDS = 1.15g, and;
               1.0 sec (long period) SD1 = 0.52g.
               The spectral response acceleration values were factored based on Site Class = C.
11.    A site-specific ground motion hazard analysis may be required at the direction of
       the structural engineer. The City should require a letter from the structural engineer
       indicating if a site-specific ground motion hazard analysis is necessary.
12.    At grade and basement floor slabs should be underlain by 4 inches of free-draining
       gravel. The gravel should be placed on undisturbed natural soils, bedrock, or
       structural fill extending to undisturbed natural soil or bedrock.
13.    No special cement type is required for concrete placed in contact with natural soil.
14.    A qualified geotechnical engineering firm should observe the placement of
       structural fill/backfill and conduct in-place field density tests on the structural
       fill/backfill to confirm moisture content and relative compaction is in accordance
       with the recommendations presented by AGEC.
15.    Before the placement of concrete, a letter from the geotechnical engineer should be
       obtained by the City stipulating that the subgrade for footing and floor slab support
       was prepared in accordance with the geotechnical engineering report and ready for
       the placement of concrete.
16.    Roof runoff devices are to be installed to direct all runoff a minimum of 10 feet away
       from the structure.
17.    Surface drainage should slope away from the structure in all directions.

       INTRODUCTION
       FOCUS Engineering and Surveying, LLC (FOCUS) has been retained to complete a
       Parking Generation Study for the High Pointe development. High Pointe is planning to
       add a new 25,806 SF office building onto Lot 2 of the property. With the addition of the
       office building, the overall number of existing parking stalls will remain the same. The High
       Pointe development is located on the northwest corner of the Bangerter Parkway and
       Highland Drive intersection in Draper, Utah. This parking generation memo presents the
       number of parking stalls needed with the existing 69,687 SF office building which houses
       35,942 SF surgery center/clinic, 22,721 SF Capital Financial, 1,923 SF Endeavor Capital,
       and a 9,101 SF reception center, along with the addition of a 25,806 SF office space
       based on Table 9-25-1 Parking Requirement Formulas in the UT Code of Draper.

       EXISTING CONDITIONS
       Existing Land Uses and Roadways
       The High Pointe development is bordered to the south by Highland Drive, to the east by
       Bangerter Parkway, to the north and west by undeveloped land and existing office
       buildings. Currently on the High Pointe site is the existing office building that is 69,687 SF,
       consisting of a 35,942 SF surgery center/clinic, a 22,721 SF Capital Financial, a 1,923 SF
       Endeavor Capital, and a 9,101 SF reception center. The existing parking surrounding the
       building consists of 310 parking stalls. The site contains two accesses. One onto Highland
       Drive with one lane entering the site and one lane exiting the site. The second access
       onto Bangerter Parkway consists of a right-in right-out access with a single lane in either
       direction.

       Roadways
       Bangerter Parkway: Bangerter Parkway is an arterial roadway that connects Bangerter
       Highway to the north to Traverse Ridge Road to the south. Bangerter Parkway is a two-
       lane road with a shared two-way left turn lane. Bangerter Parkway provides access for
       residential homes, office buildings, and commercial businesses in the area. The speed
       limit along Bangerter Parkway is posted at 35 mph.
       Highland Drive: Highland Drive is also an arterial roadway that runs east and west along
       the south boundary of the proposed development. The posted speed limit is 40 mph. East
       of High Pointe, Highland Drive consists of one lane in each direction. West of High Pointe,
       Highland Drive expands to two lanes in each direction with a shared two-way left turn
       lane. Highland Drive is owned and maintained by Draper City.

       PROPOSED SITE CONDITIONS
       The proposed office building planned for the High Pointe development will be located in
       Lot 2 of the property. The proposed office building will consist of 25,806 SF of office space.
       Also included in this parking analysis is the existing 69,687 SF office building. It should be
       noted the 9,101 SF reception center is primarily used on weekends and evenings, outside
       of regular office hours. The parking projections will take into account both buildings at full
       build-out and also account for time-of-day usage of the parking lot as the peak parking
       demands are different for office space and a reception center.

       CONCLUSION
       Using these three methods to calculate the number parking stalls needed for the High
       Pointe development, the High Pointe development is designed with enough parking stalls
       to accommodate the proposed land uses.
       Based on Table 9-25-1 Parking Requirement Formulas in the UT Code of Draper, the total
       number of parking stalls with each land use fully occupied at the same time is 315 with
       the application of the allowable 10% reduction per city code. Using the above-
       mentioned number for required parking stalls and applying the Weekday Parking
       Demand Percentages per ITE, the peak parking demand time of day is from 11:00 AM to
       12:00 PM and would require 283 parking stalls. This is 27 less parking stalls that the proposed
       site is designed for.
       Looking at the parking demand with how much existing parking is being used, the existing
       site on average is using 2.22 stalls per 1000 gross square feet. This equates to 155 parking
       stalls currently being used. On the highest observed day, 187 parking stalls were being
       used. With the addition of the proposed office building on Lot 2, the total parking
       demand is projected to increase to 259 stalls at peak usage. Even looking at the highest
       observed day, the peak parking demand would be 291 parking stalls.
       The existing site is currently operating with 310 parking stalls between the surface parking
       lot and the parking garage. With the 310 parking stalls currently on the site, there is more
       than adequate parking on the site with the addition of the new 25,806 square foot
       building on Lot 2. Furthermore, the parking study supports any approved use up to five
       parking spaces per 1,000 square feet gross building floor area for Lot 2, as listed in Table
       9-25-1.

            B. Deviations From Strict Compliance: The number of parking spaces required under section
            9-25-060 of this chapter, or the parking calculation requirements shown in section 9-25-100,
            table 9-25-1 of this chapter, may be deviated in accordance with the provisions in this section.
            The purpose of such a deviation is to provide flexibility beyond natural adjustments to this
            chapter in order to adapt to specific circumstances, reduce potential environmental impacts,
            and conserve resources. In cases where parking in amounts other than required herein may be
            appropriate, the planning commission may approve a request for a modification, by way of a
            parking study, to increase or reduce parking requirements based on findings found in
            subsection C of this section. When a deviation from strict compliance is approved by the
            planning commission, parking shall be provided according to the standard approved under that
            deviation with a natural adjustment that only provides for parking closer to the requirement
            being deviated. A deviation from strict compliance as outlined in this section is not necessary for
            a proposed increase in parking for residential uses.
            C. Parking Study: When a parking study is required for a request to deviate from the parking

            of this chapter, a parking study shall be prepared and submitted by the applicant for review.
            Parking studies shall be prepared by a professional engineer licensed to work in the state of
            Utah and reviewed as a part of a site plan application.
