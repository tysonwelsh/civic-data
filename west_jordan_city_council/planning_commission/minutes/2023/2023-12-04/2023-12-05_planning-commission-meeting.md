John Thomas, Next Level Homes, presented the application for a residential subdivision in the R-1-8,

medium density, zone. The project follows the general plan to provide for the future housing needs of

the City. Although the zoning is for 8,000 square foot lots, they exceed the minimum requirement. The
traffic impact study concluded that traffic from these lots will have little impact and suggested that

construction of the homes that will be of a very high quality.

commercial developments. A lot of their history is with the planned community product. For this
project they met with the Design Review Committee, the Council of the Whole, and held

something that tries to balance the rights of property ownership. The concept plan is for 75 acres. They
have been working with staff for 1 4 years and are presenting a development that has a mixture
housing types for people at different times of their life with single-family, townhomes, and carriage
homes. Open space, parks, and trails are provided with walkability. Overall density is 4.9 units per
acre, which falls in the low-density category. They believe that this is the right project at the right time
with the need for housing in Utah. He stated that Governor Cox recently said Utah needs 35,000 more

Kaylen Nichols, Peterson Development, thanked the residents for their attendance. The project has a

provides the flexibility needed to work with the site conditions to make it safe and functional. They
promise in the development agreement to do geotechnical and geological hazard studies before any site
plan approval. Prior to final approval of any face that has cuts or fills above six feet, a construction
quality assurance plan will be submitted to the city for review, and it will be the guiding document to
make sure it is stable and safe for building. A Planned Community zone can make this site a whole site

included in the development agreement. The City Council said that six homes per private lane on the
carriage lots were too many, so that was reduced to four and allowed for more space and parking. She
presented two different layout concepts for the cluster lots that could resolve some of the staff's
concerms with utility placement. The City Council requested that the applicant meet with the residents,
and the comments overall were that they wanted fewer units and larger lots on the northwest side.
Parking on the carriage lots was a concern. The residents generally liked the layout and open space if
the zoning were approved, and they liked that the townhomes and carriage lots were interior to the

with staff on the layout until it is acceptable to everything including utilities. Peterson Development
has developed the Stone Creek and Highlands planned communities in West Jordan, and they like that
they can provide a range of housing to meet the lifestyle and lifecycles of a family. Stone Creek has
senior housing, townhomes, single-family with varying lot sizes and the community thrives. She lives
in that community and has seen some people start in the townhomes and move to single-family and
vice versa. People appreciate being able to stay in the community when their circumstances change.
Other people have moved their parents to the Ranches senior product to be nearby. The code requires a
minimum of 5% or 19 homes to meet the needs of moderate-income households, which allows for
affordability throughout the city and not just in high density areas. A traditional single-family
neighborhood doesn’t usually see open space and trails because that extra space is within the lots
themselves. The open space requirement is 20%. Their concept has 32% open space with six acres in

west, and there is a 10-foot urban trail that fronts porches and front doors. There are eight to nine-foot

the recommendation from staff would either be neutral or positive with conditions.

Tayler Jensen explained the process for the application, which will eventually be heard by the City

come to the Planning Commission for approval. A master development agreement is associated with
the application, but it should be noted that the city code still applies. Staff gave a negative

recommendation because the application did not meet the standards of the code. He referred to the

Based on the analysis contained in the report, staff recommended that the Planning Commission make
a recommendation of denial (negative recommendation) to the City Council for the proposed General
Plan Amendment as the proposal fails to properly satisfy Criteria A, B, C, and E (13-7C-6).

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission make a recommendation of denial
(negative recommendation) to the City Council for the Master Development Agreement/Plan as it is
anticipated to have a negative impact on surrounding development, fails to comply with adopted
ordinances, is not refined for adoption, and doesn’t provide sufficient detail or clarity on how and
when utility deficiencies will be mitigated.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission make a recommendation of denial
(negative recommendation) to the City Council for the proposed Rezone as staff is unable to
identify affirmative determinations for approval Criteria 1, 2, and 4 (13-7D-6 (a)).
