Proposed Resolution 2024-10 adopting the Ogden City Sewer Master Plan
        A memo from the Public Services Department regarding the proposed Sewer Master Plan came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated the Engineering Division has submitted a transmittal for the Council to consider approving the
2024 Sewer Master Plan. The plan was prepared by Sunrise Engineering, who began drafting the plan in 2022. The Planning
Commission held a public hearing on June 5, 2024 and recommended that the Council adopt the plan. The current Sewer Master
Plan was approved in 2013. The Ogden City General Plan (2002) recommends that master plans for parks, trails, and
infrastructure be reviewed and adopted as part of the General Plan process.
          City Engineer Nielsen and Sunrise Engineering Vice President Cliff Linford summarized the memo and used the aid of a
PowerPoint presentation to provide a review of the proposed Sewer Master Plan; the purpose of the Plan is to provide an
understanding of key elements of the sewer system infrastructure; identify required improvements based on capacity or
maintenance needs; analyze the system’s capacity and flows from daily users, inflow, and infiltration; and to define parts of the
system that are out of compliance with current State requirements and identify a prioritized list of projects. For a copy of the
presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Council discussion of the proposed Plan centered on opportunities for flexibility in the project schedule to allow for
sewer improvements to occur in conjunction with larger street/infrastructure projects.
          Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2024-10, entitled:
                    “A resolution of the Ogden City Council adopting the Ogden City Sewer Master Plan.”
          Chair Richey then called for public input regarding the proposal.
          Teresa Bramwell stated that she recently inquired about locating a food-related business in a property she owns in Ogden,
and she was told that the tenant would need to dig into the street and replace the one-inch water line with a two-inch water line
and the five-inch sewer line with a six-inch sewer line. Her building is just 750 square feet in size with one half-bathroom and she
thought the requirements were ridiculous. She is curious about whether similar improvements are required for other property
owners when the City is installing new infrastructure in front of their property; it is her understanding that individual property
owners are responsible for all infrastructure between their home and the meter, and the City is responsible for infrastructure from
the main line to the meter. She asked if that is something that will be addressed as part of the Sewer Master Plan.
          There were no further persons appearing to be heard.
          Chair Richey invited Mr. Nielsen to respond to Ms. Bramwell’s questions. Mr. Nielsen stated that when a new tenant
seeks to locate in an existing building in the City, the permitting staff will evaluate their fixture units and existing condition and
capacity of their water and sewer infrastructure. For a new restaurant establishment, the applicant would be asked to evaluate their
grease input to determine if a grease separator is needed. He is unsure of the specifics of Ms. Bramwell’s property but assumes a
requirement to increase the capacity of the water and sewer laterals would have been consistent with increased water use and
grease introduction from the site. He also noted that when the City is installing new infrastructure in a City roadway, they also
install new laterals to the outside of the curb and gutter in front of a private property; home and business owners are given the
opportunity to connect to these new laterals to avoid future connectivity problems. The City is responsible to maintain a water line
from the main line to the meter, but sewer infrastructure is solely the responsibility of the home or business owner.
          ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER GRAF AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER HYER,
RESOLUTION 2024-10 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL

Public Comments
           Shane Storey addressed recent public concerns surrounding trail access near and within the Mt. Ogden Golf Course. He
stated he is a member of the City’s trails committee and indicated he believes there are opportunities to accommodate both trail
users and the golfers that use the golf course; there is an easement that predates the golf course that runs along the canal road from
23rd Street past Weber State University (WSU) and trail users wish to continue to be able to use the trail. He has sent
correspondence to the Mayor and Council and thanked those that have responded to him and have met with him near the golf
course to gain their own understanding of the conditions. He provided examples of signage that is used in other cities for similar
situations where a trail runs near or onto a golf course and feels that something similar could be used in Ogden for the section of
the trail that interacts with Mt. Ogden Golf Course.
           Brian Van Vliet stated he lives near Lester Park and asked that the Council consider some very important issues before
moving forward with any plans to change the park: first is inclusivity and the fact that parks should be accessible for anyone and
everyone rather than a certain type of user or demographic; preservation of the mature trees at the park; and safety as the top
priority for the park and its surroundings.
           Linda Sanchez thanked the Council for commemorating Constitution Day. She stated that she feels blessed to live in this
nation and especially in Ogden, Utah. She stated she appreciates the elected leaders of Ogden City and their preservation of
freedoms in the community. She also encouraged the Council to be frugal in their use of taxpayer dollars.
           Blaine Richey stated he wished to revisit the petition that was submitted on June 18, 2024 to add a gate to the north end
of Taylor Avenue where it meets Village Drive on the WSU campus. There was some traffic enforcement at the start of the
college’s school year, but it has since waned. Residents have concerns all day and all year round, not just during the fall and
winter semesters. Somehow this issue has been framed as only dealing with speed of the vehicles driving on the road. That is one
issue, but the greater issue is with the volume of traffic on the residential road. The City’s spokesperson has responded to a request
from a local television station and stated that if the street was blocked, certain services would be crippled. He stated that it is
interesting to him that the same argument was not made when the City considered the closure of Birch Avenue; he was told the
gate at the end of Birch Avenue could be opened in the case of an emergency and the same could be true for Taylor Avenue. At
present, when there is an emergency in the area, emergency vehicles use County Hills Drive and Taylor Avenue rather than
driving through the WSU campus. Garbage collection trucks are forced to make a turnaround at the end of Taylor Avenue to
complete their route on the west side of Taylor Avenue. Adding a gate would not impact service provision any more than it did on
Birch Avenue. He stated there is some misinformation regarding the proposed location of the gate; the petition asked for the gate
to be located at the intersection of Taylor Avenue and Birch Avenue. He has driven the routes that motorists would be forced to
take if the gate were installed and has found that commute times would only be increased by 12 seconds. Based upon these points,
he is renewing his request for the gate to be installed.
          Eva Barnett stated she lives at 25th Street and Monroe Boulevard and is the president of the neighborhood group called
“Thrive at Lester Park”. After Council member Graf’s proposal discussed in last week’s work session meeting for funding at
Lester Park – also known as ‘molester park’ – she paid extra attention to the sirens she hears in her neighborhood. Over the course
of five days, she could hear or see sirens in very close proximity to her home on seven occasions. It is the norm for her kids to see
a police vehicle on her street. However, they choose to live where they do because they love the community and believe they are
here to create and shape the community. They believe good people are needed in every neighborhood no matter the average
income and education level. There are hundreds of invested, law-following, friendly residents in the neighborhood; for every
police vehicle they see, they know that someone who cares called for their support. She has gathered and participated in events of
the park regardless of who is sleeping there; she has collaborated with the City to secure funding for a crosswalk for safer
accessibility to the park; and she has helped to secure more trashcans at the park to help improve cleanliness. Soccer goals have
been placed at the park and sports equipment has been donated to the library for check-out for park users. She wants to teach her
kids to use the closest park to them, even though there are sometimes dangerous conditions present. However, she and her
neighbors are tired. They keep gathering because they are tough and invested and tonight, she is asking that the City also be
invested. For the first time, she knows there are funds available for Lester Park; she has waited eight years for this day. Crime has
actually worsened in her neighborhood, which is not the case in other neighborhoods in the City. It is evident that new parks, like
Liberty Park on Monroe Boulevard, can help to improve and strengthen a neighborhood and she wants that within walking
distance of the homes in her neighborhood. This is what she has envisioned and has been working towards; she and many others
have donated time to the development of plans, and she asked that the City trusts this group and proceeds with improving Lester
Park.
          Teresa Bramwell thanked the Council for recognizing Constitution Day. She also thanked many of her neighbors for their
efforts to improve Lester Park and the surrounding areas. She then noted her great-great-great-great grandfather, Thomas
McBride, fought in the Revolutionary War and died in a very interesting way. He became a Mormon later in his life and lived in
Missouri; on October 27, 1838, the Missouri Governor issued an extermination order for the Mormons. Her grandfather was the
Justice of the Peace for the County he lived in, and he owned a home there. When he learned of the order, he did not believe it
would impact him because of his position and his status as a homeowner and taxpayer. However, on October 30, 1838, he and 17
other Mormons in his town were killed by a militia. He was dismembered with his own farm tools and thrown down a well. The
rest of the Mormons left and escaped to Utah. The next generation of his family made it to Utah and are still here today and they
believe in the Constitution and in freedom.
          Jake Bruin stated he wished to offer his support for the paid parking program in downtown Ogden; people coming from
out of town should not be surprised when being asked to pay for parking. He suggested the revenue generated by the program be
used for bicycle and pedestrian improvements in Ogden’s downtown.
          Andrea Bell stated she wished to echo the encouragement that has been offered to the Council to proceed with an
improvement project at Lester Park; she currently chooses to use parks that are not as close to her home as Lester Park because of
the safety concerns, lack of amenities, and illegal activities occurring at the Park. She referenced an apartment project that has
been approved in close proximity to Lester Park and she is hopeful that the people who occupy that complex will make a positive
impact on Ogden in general. She encouraged the Council to consider the many opportunities Lester Park presents in terms of
strengthening the neighborhood.
          Sam Barnett echoed the comments made about the need to improve Lester Park in an effort to unite the surrounding
community and enhance the library and other surrounding uses.
