 3.        Resolution 2021-02 of intent to create a permit area on portions 1625 North and 300 West.
           Carterville Neighborhood. (19-108)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Resolution 2021-02, as currently constituted, has
                          been made by council rule.

Javin Weaver, Planner, presented. On October 29, 2019, the Council directed by resolution that a study
be done of the proposed parking permit area for portions of 1625 North, 300 West, and 380 West in
Carterville Neighborhood. The cost of the permit would be between $50 to $100, which still did not
cover the cost to manage the permit program, according to Mr. Weaver. Staff returned to a Council
Work Meeting on November 10, 2020 with the study and their recommendation. Staff recommended
that a parking permit area not be approved, but that instead the area should continue to be monitored
for other potential parking solutions.

Mr. Weaver shared some facts from his study with the Council, of particular concern was the cost to
administer the program, and it would require additional parking enforcement staff. A portion of the
proposed area had a parking utilization rate of less than 52 percent and there were still plenty of on-
street parking spaces available. Mr. Weaver proposed revising the plan to focus on the area where there
was a higher utilization rate, this was on 1625 North and the northern section of 300 West. He
suggested restricted parking from 11 p.m. to 6 a.m., allowing two permits per dwelling unit with a cost
of $50 to $100. He noted the revised proposal would still require additional enforcement staff.

Councilor Shipley acknowledged additional staffing would be required and wanted to know how many
employees would be needed. Mr. Weaver was not sure of the exact number of employees that would be
required, but he would report back to Council with this information. Mr. Robert Mills, Planner, added
that parking enforcement staff was already at capacity and a new program may require additional staff.

Councilor Handley understood the need for a limited program as described by Mr. Weaver and thought
it might be more manageable with current staffing resources. Mr. Weaver and Mr. Mills agreed that a
more targeted approach was a good way to meet the goals of the neighborhood without straining the
limited staffing resources.

Councilor Handley was in favor of Mr. Weaver’s recommendations.

 4.        Ordinance 2021-02 approving an amended and restated license agreement with Google Fiber
           Utah, LLC. (21-008) (1:12:40)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2021-02, as currently constituted, has been made
                          by council rule.

Daniel Follet, Division Director of Finance, presented. Google Fiber had requested that certain aspects of
the existing license agreement be amended. Google Fiber currently provided high-speed broadband
internet, VoIP (landline phone operating over the internet), and video service. Viewers were moving


Provo City Council Meeting Minutes - January 19, 2021                                                   Page 3 of 7
away from subscription-based television packages and switching to streaming services, this was also
known as “cord cutting.” Google Fiber, like many other providers, was experiencing cord cutting, so in
response they were planning to stop providing video services. The agreement needed to be amended to
reflect this change because Provo City was receiving a franchise fee equal to five percent of the video
services revenue, so the City would lose this revenue. To offset the loss, the agreement was being
amended to include a license fee, which would be two percent of all revenue that is produced by Google
Fiber in Provo. Mr. Follet said this would either be revenue neutral or better for the City.

Councilor Fillmore clarified that customers would be losing their video services, but other streaming
options would still be available through Google Fiber internet, or customers could choose other cable or
satellite providers.

Councilor Harding was excited about this change from a franchise fee to a license fee. He asked if this
model would become the standard for other data providers. Wayne Parker, CAO, said there many other
providers using electric poles and utilities as a conduit to get their services to their customer, so it could
become a new model. Mr. Parker said a license agreement is about renting the City’s right of way or
assets.

Mr. Parker noted that Google Fiber had not made any official announcements about when they would
terminate the video service, at some point they would do that. This was in anticipation of a very
common trend that was assumed to happen eventually.

Chair Sewell opened public comment, there was no response.

Councilor Harding thought this was a much more sustainable model.

Chair Sewell appreciated the partnership with Google Fiber and the ability to offer their service to the
City’s residents. He called for a vote on the implied motion.

       Vote:              The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Harding,
                          Hoban, Sewell, and Shipley in favor.

 5.        Resolution 2021-03 approving an agreement with Utah County regarding funding for the
           Regional Sports Park. (21-016) (1:26:55)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Resolution 2021-03, as currently constituted, had been made
                          by council rule.

Doug Robins, Assistant Director of Parks and Recreation, presented. The Administration negotiated an
agreement with Utah County to donate money to the planned Regional Sports Park. Normally such an
agreement would not require Council approval, but one of the terms is for the City to pay a small
amount of money back to the County each year for marketing purposes.

There had been several local needs identified through the Parks & Recreation planning process. There
was a need for more soccer, football, lacrosse, rugby, and other fields. There had also been an increase
in sports travel tourism. Mr. Robins said it would be ideal to serve our local community in Provo while
capturing some of the travel. The park would have 22 fields which would be able to host major
tournaments and special events.

Mr. Robins spoke about the acquisition of the land, it had been valued at $6 million but was acquired for
just $3.5 million. The City also had a partnership with the Delta Restoration Project to bring in soil to get
the land out of the flood plain. The total construction cost would be $20 million and the project would
be finished in fall of 2024. The park would be located off Lakeview Parkway, near the airport and would
be easily accessible from I-15.

The design concept would allow access from every side of the park to a parking that allowed visitors to
easily access each field. The impact on traffic would be minimal. There would be three playgrounds and
multiple walking paths. This would also help with local sports programming and community needs.

The economic development aspect of this park was also unique; Keith Morey, Economic Development
Director, spoke about this. This was a forward-thinking project, and an economic development tool. All
communities strive to increase tax income, particularly retail, restaurant, entertainment, and hotel.
Provo had a lower disposable income, so bringing in people from other communities would help offset
this. There was no other facility like this in Utah County or Salt Lake County. The Utah youth sports
industry was growing 20 percent annually. One comparable facility was the one in St. George, Utah. The
St. George facility had become a destination sports facility and was generating approximately $38
million per year in economic impact. Mr. Morey said this was a great way to support local business.

Scott Henderson, Parks and Recreation Director, spoke about the tournament demand. (1:38:01)
Tournament organizers were already inquiring about the facility and there was no other place in Provo
and the surrounding areas to host these events.

Mr. Henderson also spoke about the funding plan. The following funding was in place:
     Provo City
           o $6 million Parks and Recreation Capital Improvement Fund
           o $2 million Parks and Recreation Impact Fee Revenue
           o $1 million Parks and Recreation RAP Tax Revenue
     Utah County*
           o $2.5 million TRCC & TRT tax

The City was also planning on the following additional funding sources:
     Provo City
            o $2.5 million Provo City Economic Development Contribution
     State of Utah
            o 2.5 million State Participations
     US Federal
            o $1 million Land and Water Conservation Fund
            o $2.5 million US Legislative Representatives

*The agreement with Utah County for $2.5 million had been renegotiated multiple times. Mr.
Henderson explained Utah County would provide the money through reimbursement grants to Provo. In
exchange, the County would receive consistent rental fees and usage rights. The City agreed the park
would be developed and operational within five years. Provo City would also provide an annual
contribution of $25,000 annually, beginning 2023 towards the County’s visitor and convention center.
Provo City would remain the sole owner of the facility. Mr. Henderson felt this agreement was mutually
beneficial.

 8.        Resolution 2021-RDA-01-19-1 authorizing the Chief Executive Officer to sign an amendment
           to the existing Participation Agreement with Mill Race Partners for the redevelopment of a
           blighted block in Provo. (21-012) (2:00:38)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Resolution 2021-RDA-01-19-1 as currently
                          constituted, has been made by council rule.

David Walter, Redevelopment Agency (RDA) Director, presented. This amendment would allow a new
entity, Mill Race Operating Company, LLC, to take over the previously approved owner participation
agreement with Mill Race Partners.

Mr. Walter described the area; the RDA had purchased a parcel that was previously occupied by IFA and
had assisted them in relocating to their new store on University Avenue. The former IFA property was to
be redeveloped, along with the rest of the block. The RDA partnered with Mr. Justin Earl who had
acquired the remaining balance of the property. The new development would include an office tower,
condominiums for sale, and apartments for lease on the property.

The property was in a Federal Opportunity Zone, Mr. Earl and Mill Race Partners had created Mill Race
Operating Company, LLC which was a subsidiary of, and completely controlled by, Mill Race Partners,
LLC. As was customary for development projects, passive investors are brought in to provide equity
capital needed to develop the parcel in a subsidiary. Additionally, the parcel was located within a
"qualified opportunity zone," and so certain structural accommodations needed to be put in place at the
advice of tax counsel in order to qualify for those benefits and develop out the area in line with the
RDA’s vision. But the qualifications and identity of the Participant had not changed at all given that Mill
Race Partners controlled Mill Race Operating Company.

Councilor Fillmore asked about the pedestrian bridge that had been proposed previously. Mr. Walter
explained the bridge had been proposed to go from the development, over 600 East and the railroad
tracks, landing at the Front Runner Station. Due to redesign, it would only cross over the railroad tracks,
not 600 East. The current plan would not preclude the opportunity to expand the bridge over 600 East in
the future.

Chair Ellsworth opened public comment, there was no response. There was no other discussion from the
RDA, she called for a vote on the implied motion.

       Vote:              The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Harding,
                          Hoban, Sewell, and Shipley in favor.
