1.       A presentation regarding the Sewer Utility Update and Master Plan. (22-018) (0:11:45)

Dave Decker, Public Works Director, presented. He introduced staff members and consultants in
attendance: Gary Calder, Water Division Director; Jimmy McKnight, Public Works Budget
Analyst; Cory Christiansen, Water Works Engineer/consultant on wastewater treatment plant;
Susie Becker, Zions Public Finance; Keith Larson, Bowen Collins; and Dave Torgersen, Provo’s
Wastewater Treatment Plant Manager.

Public Works has been working on an updated sewer master plan. They plan to have the master
plan on an upcoming agenda for the Council to formally adopt. Mr. Decker shared an update on
major wastewater capital improvement projects in the city as well as an update on the City’s

be bringing another action item to the Council to approve the loan by resolution. They also

Mr. Decker gave an overview of the wastewater system, which includes 14 lift stations and over
320 miles of pipe. Provo’s wastewater treatment plant was the second constructed in the state
after Salt Lake, built in 1956 with subsequent updates in 1977. The wastewater system contains
two components: the collection system and the treatment plant; the master plan focuses primarily
on the collection system. In recent years, as Public Works has addressed issues with inflow and
infiltration (I&I), they have seen a slight decrease in the volume of treatment due to the system
being more efficient and having less seepage of groundwater. Mr. Decker gave background
information on the history of the wastewater system to help illustrate the reason for the current
wastewater rates. The sewer master plan will also consider the impacts of population growth on
the wastewater system and how changes in density and other factors interplay with impact fees.

Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked for some clarification about I&I and the downward trend of
treatment volume. Mr. Decker referred to a graph summarizing their findings—despite 2019
being a significant wet year on record, they did not see an increase in flow at the treatment plant.
This seems to indicate to them that they are making some improvements to the system and that it
is more resistant to I&I. Councilor Katrice MacKay said she is regularly asked by residents why
the wastewater treatment plant was not built on the west side to save money on lift stations. Mr.
Decker reviewed the site selection process and the factors that impacted the siting decision. The
two main sites they considered were the existing site and one near the airport—even if they had
moved the treatment plant near the airport, they still would have had to lift the wastewater into
the treatment plant, therefore requiring a lift station at that location. The other key factor was
financial—the site near the airport would have been a completely new treatment plant. By
rebuilding at the existing site, they are making the necessary upgrades but maintaining the
functioning systems that were already in place. There would have also been a large expense
associated with rerouting trunk lines to the airport area. From a financial perspective, it made
more sense to continue with the existing site. Councilor George Handley noted that this decision
came at the same time as discussions about rebuilding the city hall and the fiscally sound option
was a very pragmatic solution.

Mr. Decker then reviewed some major wastewater CIP projects, including:
    Southwest lift station
    Force main
    Gravity flow pipe system (which will eliminate several lift stations/bottleneck points)
Mr. Decker displayed a map which charted out future CIP projects and he encouraged the
Council to accompany him on a tour of some sites including the new Provo Airport terminal, the
Southwest Lift Station, pipe projects (including a 27” pipe being installed underneath the Provo
River), and the wastewater treatment plant.

Moving onto the third topic, Mr. Decker shared background information about the loan the city
received from the State Division of Environmental Quality. This funding was key to acquiring
advanced technology at the treatment plant that would be equipped to address potential future
regulations at the state level. The City still has a gap to close to fund the project; most

2.      A discussion regarding Wastewater Impact Fees. (22-020) (1:25:06)

John Borget, Administrative Services Director, introduced the presentation. Keith Larson,
Bowen Collins, presented. Bowen Collins prepared the collection systems master plan, as well as
the impact fee plan, together with a report by Zions Public Finance on the impact fees. Mr.
Larson reviewed the timeline of previous sewer impact fee activities. He outlined several
changes compared to the previous sewer impact fee calculation, including:
     Growth projections, including an adjustment of density on the west side and
        redevelopment areas of the city to reflect most recent development plans
     Updated flow projections of the sewer system (decreased flow since 2013)
     Updated proportional use of capacity based on the updated flow projections
     Updated list of needed projects and construction costs
All these updates were completed following the same methodology established by the Council’s
Impact Fee Review Committee in 2018.

The majority of the increase is due to construction inflation. The changes in project costs for the
collection and treatment systems totals about $62 million. Mr. Larson showed the earlier impact
fee calculations—of about $509 million in sewer projects, about $34.5 million was attributable to
new growth. Under the new impact fee calculations, the total project cost has changed to $421
million, with about $42 million attributable to new growth. Mr. Larson showed a summary of the
changes to the impact fees, including significant construction inflation, shift in growth from less
                                                                                                  3

expensive excess capacity in existing facilities to more expensive capacity in new facilities
(especially on the west side), and the credit calculation was also updated to consider a new debt
issue. Compared to other Utah municipalities, the old fee was about average. The new fee is in
the upper end of the fees, however many communities are facing these same issues and many
other cities are currently in the process of updating and reassessing their impact fees.

Councilor George Handley asked about the likely impact fees of other cities. Mr. Larson
explained that an impact fee analysis by design is unique to each individual city. He noted,
however that Orem’s SW Service Area is essentially like Orem’s west side. Similar to Provo,
that area is much more expensive to service and would likely have similar results.

Mr. Borget referred to the ongoing debate with impact fees about who pays for the growth. The
traditional thinking is that new developments pay for the impacts of growth. Last time they
updated the impact fees, they indicated they planned to come back on a more regular basis to
revisit fees. Due to construction cost increases, they have a significant increase here, but they
hope that more regular modest increases will be the usual. This change will need formal approval
by the Council. There is a 90-day requirement for when the fees would go into effect and staff
wanted to try to give developers adequate notice. The recommendation is to use the maximum
allowed impact fee. Mr. Larson also indicated that before the impact fee can be adopted, there
must be a public hearing specific to impact fees. Councilor Travis Hoban asked why a city might
decide to absorb the cost. Mr. Borget explained that there is sometimes a lot of pressure from
developers; the City might gain a reputation for being hard to build in. On the other side,
residents might complain about high rate increases that are covering the costs of growth. Impact
fees make a lot of sense, which is why most cities have impact fees in some form.

Councilor Bill Fillmore asked for clarification on the maximum allowed fee. Susie Becker, Zions
Public Finance, clarified that the law requires a rational nexus between fee required and the
service to the new development; that figure is the maximum fee that the law allows. She
indicated that most cities charge the maximum. Council Chair Travis Hoban indicated this would
be placed on a future Council Meeting agenda. Presentation only.

4.      A presentation regarding the FY2023 Budget (Finance and Human Resource
        previews revenues, debt, workforce issues, etc.). (22-016) (2:15:39)

John Borget, Administrative Services Director, presented. He noted that administering ARPA
funds had been a large assignment tasked to Finance. They also provided support to the General
Plan goals with their focus on transparency and reporting, the annual budget, and CIP plan.

Dan Follett, Finance Division Director, reviewed budget information and a balance sheet of the
city’s debt. Provo has an excellent bond rating—this represents the good management of Provo’s
finances from an investment perspective. Mr. Follett reviewed specifics of several of the bonds,
including the recreation center bond for which they did an advanced refunding in 2017, saving
property taxpayers about $2.8 million. The telecom bonds are still outstanding for about four
more years. They regularly track all issuances to take advance of beneficial refunding
opportunities—there are not any at present but they continue to monitor this. In response to a
question about capital leases, Mr. Follett explained they can structure them as tax-exempt leases
and receive a more favorable interest rate. The City owns the property in each case at the end of
the lease and it is a way to structure it under the Internal Revenue Code so that it is tax-exempt.
Mr. Follett is happy to meet with Councilors to review other questions.

Councilor Bill Fillmore asked what stands between Provo and a AAA bond rating. Mr. Follett
said for sales tax revenue, Provo is rated AAA. For other debt issuances, it’s simply the size and

Brandon Larsen, Planner, introduced the presentation. They have identified a consultant for the
general plan statistical survey and they anticipate about 8 weeks to complete the surveying

this was a crucial component for the City to focus on. Councilor Katrice MacKay thought goal
CL2, regarding better integration with the campus community and inform students about city
events and activities, was important—this is frequent feedback she hears from constituents.

Councilor Bill Fillmore said he sent Ms. Salzl some detailed suggestions. He looks forward to
discussing those with her more in the future. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked about CL1(b)
as it seemed a bit vague to her. Ms. Salzl explained that the extended matrix for this item
referenced goals contained in the Conservation and Resiliency Plan. Ms. Ellsworth thought if
everyone supports about 80-90% of the general plan, there is some overlap in what everyone
agrees on and hopefully that represents a good compromise. Councilor David Shipley asked if
they have determined how to put together the survey. Mr. Larsen responded that the main
purpose is to ensure they have heard the voice of the citizenry. Mr. Shipley asked if the citizens

Councilor George Handley wanted to acknowledge the enormous effort which had gone into the

wide variety of voices from across the city. It was a very rigorous process and data was gathered

written the plan. Moving forward with a survey, he thought the plan appendix had helpful
questions that could be used to start. Mr. Handley also noted that while a general plan relates
primarily to land use, it is also a document that helps the community achieve its goals and
diversity and inclusion relates directly to that. He noted that most large businesses, universities,

transparency and representativeness in the city, he felt diversity and inclusion was one way to do
so. On page 70, the implementation matrix for stewardship of the outdoors, it mentions trails.

Valley Trails Alliance. He suggested referencing this by name as a general trail plan would be
helpful. Natural resources are an important attraction for businesses and residents to locate here
