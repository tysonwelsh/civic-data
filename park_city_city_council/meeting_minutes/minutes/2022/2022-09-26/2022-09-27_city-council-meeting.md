II)     SUSTAINABLE TOURISM PLAN UPDATE

1. Sustainable Tourism Plan - Presentation by Jennifer Wesselhoff, President/
CEO Park City Chamber & Visitors Bureau:

Jennifer Wesselhoff reviewed this presentation was the culmination of 18 months of
work. She stated concerns were expressed by both Councils with regard to mitigating
the impacts of tourism, celebrating the economic generation brought by tourism,
celebrating employees, protecting the environment and the quality of life, and looking at
a regional approach to helping businesses do better. This plan added destination
management to the Chamber’s established role of destination marketing. There would
be focus on educating visitors and influencing their behavior through the lens of
protecting the environment. The Sustainable Tourism Plan would address the needs of
visitors, the industry, the community, and the environment. Wesselhoff’s definition of
sustainable tourism was finding a balance with residents, visitors and businesses.

Morgan Mingle, Park City Chamber Bureau Director of Sustainable Tourism, stated
destinations needed to take a holistic and integrative approach. She discussed
destination management, social and economic factors, community and culture, and the
environment. She reviewed the outreach performed with residents, businesses, and
visitors. The stewardship council had focus groups and held two public forums. She
noted the plan components included the methodology, vision, stewardship principles,
and objectives.

Laura Carlson, Park City Chamber Bureau Vice President of Marketing, read the vision
of the plan. Wesselhoff displayed a list of items that were mentioned in the vision
statement, including sense of community, Summit County's brand, visitor economy, and
more. Mingle reviewed the stewardship principles, including local environment and
natural resources, local spirit, sense of place, well-being of the community, benefits of
the visitor economy, the tourism industry goal to lead by example, and the desire to be
bold, creative, and action-focused.

Council Member Wright noted he didn't see any goals outlined in the plan. Wesselhoff
indicated there would be higher level goals that would lead to the bigger objectives. She
wanted to take small steps to start making progress and reviewed some benchmark
areas where they would start.

Council Member Toly stated this was geared to visitors, but it didn't mention residents.
Wesselhoff indicated as they went through the objectives, every objective would touch
locals and visitors in different ways. Since locals and visitors enjoyed the same
activities, the plan would focus on both groups. Council Member Stevens felt the plan
would help locals by leveraging opportunities and mitigating the impacts in a more
robust fashion. Wesselhoff gave the example of tourism traffic being a pinch point for
residents. Part of the Chamber’s role for mitigating that was to educate visitors about
transit and bike share, and work with the business community to ensure they had
shuttle systems in order to get cars off roads and improve the resident and visitor
experience.

Council Member Toly noted day visitors impacted the community differently than
overnight visitors. Council Member Rubell asked how the business community would
commit to the principles. Wesselhoff stated they were at the beginning of the plan, but
there was a green business certification that was being implemented. She also noted
ways businesses could help with transit, employee housing, and employee commuters,
and noted there were many ways for businesses to engage in the process. Tactics like
paid parking and parking reservations embraced the Sustainable Tourism Plan. Mingle
noted the businesses played a big part in shaping the plan. Mayor Pro Tem Doilney
related they wanted to get this plan out as a means to communicate with each other.
There was room to add the more concrete ideas, but a plan was needed in order to get
started and then it would build from there. Wesselhoff stated the idea that the business
community did not carry its fair share was not accurate. They supported employees and
nonprofits.

The plan objectives were reviewed:

Objective One: Cultivate Local Pride and Respect for the Park City/Summit County
Experience. Pat Putt, Summit County Community Development Director, stated this
would mean developing stakeholder ownership for the stewardship of the community.
The stakeholders would raise the consciousness of the people who lived, worked, and
played here, and they would be docents to guests on how to take care of the
community.

Objective Two: Implement Sustainable Transportation, Housing, Water, Energy, and
Waste Management Policies and Initiatives. Carlson stated the current sustainable
programs in place would be supported by this plan. Regional suggestions would be
presented in this objective.

Objective Three: Protect and Manage our Natural Environment to Enable Sustainable
Outdoor Recreation. Carlson indicated the visitors would be made aware of resources
here once they arrived. There was a great nonprofit network and locals were active in
volunteering. This objective would focus on travel experiences that the millennial
generation wanted.

Objective Four: Ensure the Long-Term Resilience of the Park City/Summit County
Economy. Carlson noted an information campaign would be launched related to the
threat of climate change on economic stability and then community resilience plans
would be developed.

Objective Five: Equalize the Economic Benefits and Mitigate Impacts of the Visitor
Economy. Carlson indicated the Chamber's mission and role to address regional
economic diversification would be redesigned. They would create year-round workforce
support programs and transit would be optimized to meet the workforce needs.

Objective Six: Leverage Messaging and Programming to Accelerate Sustainable
Tourism. Carlson stated this messaging had already begun. Visit Park City would be
rebranded to reflect sustainable principles. The paid and earned media would focus on
sustainable visitation and responsible travelers.

Objective Seven: Foster the Development and Management of Summit County's Art and
Culture Tourism Assets. Putt stated the arts communicated with people. This objective
would leverage the arts and culture opportunities within the County. Some of the ideas
required deeper thinking and would require a master plan approach. The business and
arts sectors would partner to advance this priority.

Wesselhoff summarized she would be coming to the next Council meeting requesting
the adoption of the Sustainable Tourism Plan by resolution. Council Member Stevens
stated the issues of the County and Wasatch County were very similar and she asked
what regional efforts were being made. Wesselhoff indicated she communicated with
Wasatch County and they were modeling Summit County with regard to destination
tourism. There was a lot of work to be done, but there was a sense of cooperation and
willingness to work together.

Council Member Gerber heard the terms equity and diversity, and wondered what
efforts were being made to reach out to other demographics, especially the
underserved. Wesselhoff stated in the short-term, she wanted to understand how to
best serve the frontline employees. Council Member Gerber stated oftentimes people
looked to the Community Foundation for help, but everyone looked for others to help.
Council Member Toly thought the small business owners helped too. She asked where
residents could go to get more information, to which Wesselhoff indicated they could go
the VisitParkCity website. It would also include links to partner associations under each
objective.

Council Member Wright stated setting goals would help the Councils see the progress
down the line, and referred to housing and transportation goals specifically. He
indicated both the City and County had carbon footprint goals. He thought the next
steps would be for both Councils to set two-, five- and 10-year goals.

Vice Chair Armstrong stated the Councils had set goals, but they weren't strong in
partnering with the businesses. He thought active messaging was important so visitors
would know they didn't need to rent a car at the airport. He also discussed recycling
efforts and the need for restaurant and lodging partners to play a role in that messaging.
Regarding housing, he felt businesses could create a housing fund, and then leverage
that money for employee housing. He also stated there would be competition for
business when the Mayflower Development went online. He thought those were the
biggest questions in front of the City and County.

Chair Robinson especially liked Objectives Two, Three, and Five, and thought they

III) REGIONAL REMOTE PARK & RIDE DISCUSSION

1. Regional Remote Park & Ride - Presented by Matt Dias, Park City Manager, and
Patrick Putt, Summit County Community Development Director:
Matt Dias stated there was a long history of collaboration between the City and County.
The professional staff from both entities had met and were hung up on what to do with
regard to a pre-Olympics regional intercept strategy. He felt this was a great time to
collaborate on a comprehensive three-, five- and 10-year transportation strategy. He
knew it was central to Park City, but visitors travelled through the County to get to Park
City. He suggested convening as a group with other stakeholders to work on something
collaboratively.

Pat Putt stated it was important that the City and County were aligned on Transportation
goals. The community expected quick results and the City and County had to manage
needs quickly. He knew congestion would never be solved, but it would be managed to
produce a series of effective strategies. Immediate decisions could be considered
before the start of the ski season so the group could move forward. It was agreed at a
staff level that a western intercept portal and an eastern intercept portal were needed.
Ideally, they needed to be at a location before traffic entered the Kimball Junction and
Quinn’s Junction interchanges.

There were many possibilities for a mid- to long-term implementation. They felt studies
were needed. The main focus was the Quinn's Junction area. In the short-term, it came
down to two existing sites: Richardson Flat and the SR-248 Interchange site. Neither
site was perfect, but they could work short-term. He asked which option the Councils
preferred for the short-term. Chair Robinson asked if the Richardson Flat site was the
existing park and ride, to which Putt affirmed. Council Member Stevens asked if this

was an either/or decision, or if both sites could be looked at. Dias stated staff wanted to
be intentional for the short-term in how to focus their work. The short-term project
wouldn't solve all the problems, and he wanted to focus more energy on the mid- and
long-term projects that would have bigger impacts. There was a divergence of views
among the Councils which had staff hung up. Some thought some solution was better
than nothing and others felt the problem could be left alone for the short-term while
focus was put on a future solution that would have a bigger impact. Dias stated the
public wanted government to be intentional, transparent and collaborative on next steps.
If the public had to live with the pain for a while, they wanted to know the decisions were
strategic.

Council Member Stevens asked if personnel resources and traffic mitigation would be
put on hold in favor of a park and ride as the initial step to the longer term solution, or
would the park and ride be the short-term solution that would be cancelled in favor of a
longer term solution. Dias indicated there would always be personnel for traffic
mitigation, law enforcement, etc. This question was for temporary quasi-solutions that
might be foregone in favor of a bigger solution.

Vice Chair Armstrong stated there was a difference of opinion on those sites and neither
option was perfect. He thought Richardson Flat would be used initially. Council Member
Wright stated for this season, it would only be Richardson Flat. Council Member Rubell
asked who the park and ride would serve; visitors or workforce. Council Member Dickey
indicated the driver for the congestion was people coming from the Wasatch Front.

Mayor Pro Tem Doilney stated staff spent the majority of their time since the last joint
meeting trying to make Richardson Flat work. They surmised that visitors would only go
to Richardson Flat once. Resorts wouldn't send the visitors there because they wanted
them to come back. Employers wouldn't send employees because the employees were
asked and they said they wouldn't park there. None of the research showed Richardson
Flat as a viable short-term solution. He favored doing nothing and then focusing on the
bigger solution.

Council Member Rubell asked how many partners committed to using Quinn's Junction.
The resorts wouldn't commit to Quinn's either, so that couldn't be pitched as a better
solution. Vice Chair Armstrong stated the Ecker Hill Park and Ride worked for big
weekends and events. He thought having paid parking at the resorts might put more
parking at the park and rides.

Chair Robinson stated there were no perfect solutions. Richardson Flat would not affect
the regional collaboration for long-term solutions. Regarding Kimball Junction, there
might be a long-term solution, but UDOT would need to get involved. He stated they
could dream of a long-term solution, but in reality there wasn’t much that could be done.
In the meantime, they had something that might help. He knew it wasn't perfect, but it
