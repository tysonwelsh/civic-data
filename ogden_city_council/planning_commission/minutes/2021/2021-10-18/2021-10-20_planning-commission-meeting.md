        1. Discussion, Missing Middle Housing

            Mr. Montgomery stated he had a presentation by Dave Parolek who has written a book on missing
            middle housing. He wanted the Commission to view the video and determine whether there
            should be ordinance revisions to allow these types of units. The speaker noted housing options
            are needed, and introduced specific styles of housing, including cottage courts and stacked four-
            plex buildings. He stated some states are now allowing triplex and fourplex buildings in any
            single-family neighborhood statewide due to the national housing costs and lack of attainable
            housing. He stated these work if the style of building fits with the neighborhood, stating buildings
            should face streets and be in scale with surrounding buildings. He felt these multi-units should
            be limited to two stories in height to fit the character of single-family neighborhoods. He noted
            much of the growth of housing needs are for adults with no children. While current zoning
            regulates building setbacks, he stated the height, width and depth of buildings should also be
            regulated. Parking also is often a barrier.

            Mr. Montgomery asked the Commission to comment, noting some older buildings in the East
            Central Community fit into this category of middle housing. He suggested these would not be
            compatible in every zone, but the overall needs of the community should be identified and areas
            prioritized where middle housing could be located. Commission noted the important part of these
            being compatible with neighborhoods is not necessarily the number of units, but a more
            thoughtful form and scale allows these to be good neighbors. It was felt buildings need to be
            designed as multi-family rather than existing homes being carved into becoming more units,
            making dwellings livable.

            Commissioner Southwick asked about the City-wide downzoning of R-2 property and whether that
            would be rescinded. Mr. Montgomery explained that occurred due to the lack of home ownership
            and was an effort to create neighborhood stability, and has been a benefit to the community.
            Middle housing would likely not affect that action. Discussion continued with the Commission
            identifying areas where cottage housing or smaller scale multi-housing could work. Commission
            agreed that the form and scale is important with building entrances facing the street. It was felt

           developers could make the same amount of money if they were to develop smaller scale projects
           with some form and scale with the neighborhood, rather than using the same architecture and
           design that has been used before. Mr. Montgomery stated the best way to implement these
           ideas is by adding regulations to the Ordinance, indicating while these are not appropriate in all
           areas, there are many communities where they could be made to be compatible with existing
           development. As community plans are updated, goals and policies could identify areas where
           middle housing is appropriate.

           Commission noted the size of new single-family construction should also be in scale with the
           existing neighborhood, stating the Redevelopment Agency often builds homes which are double
           the size of existing homes in the East Central community. Commission felt the concept of
           cottage homes is worth pursuing. Mr. Montgomery noted the Ogden River Project calls out
           cottage housing, but has not yet occurred, with developers skeptical that they would be sellable.
           He noted the initial townhomes also had the same stigma many years ago, and now townhomes
           is the main market in the development world. It was noted Ogden’s boundaries are not going to
           be growing, and some creativity will be required in satisfying the future housing market.
           Commission felt taller buildings should be concentrated in the urban core and near Union Station.
           Some higher buildings could be developed for particular areas such as near the hospital, so long
           as they are well designed. Mr. Montgomery noted many of these types of buildings are already
           allowed by Ordinance, but developers lack the creativity to consider them. He stated there was
           much variety in housing types in the past, but it has been lost over the past several decades.
           Commission agreed consideration should be given to this type of development in both the
           community plans and the language of the Zoning Ordinance.

    There being no additional business before the Commission, the meeting adjourned at 6:55pm.
