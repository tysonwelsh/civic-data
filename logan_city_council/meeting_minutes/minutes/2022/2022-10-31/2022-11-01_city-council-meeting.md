Utah Wellbeing Project – Professor Courtney Flint, USU Natural Resource
Sociology

Professor Flint addressed the Council regarding the results of the Utah Wellbeing Project.

The project goals of the Utah Wellbeing project are to provide Utah city leaders
with information on the well-being and perspectives of residents to inform planning
processes and decision-making; to support comparative research on well-being, and to
bridge efforts by the UT Dept of Health, Get Healthy Utah, UT, Dept of Transportation,
& Huntsman Cancer Institute. (The report can be viewed online at
https://www.usu.edu/utah-wellbeing-project ).

The Logan Wellbeing (Online Qualtrics) Survey concluded that there are 96.6% full time
residents. The Average length of residence is 19.4 years. 25.8% have lived in Logan for 5
years or less. The survey underrepresents those without a college degree, those
unmarried, those who rent, those who are unemployed, those without children, those with
incomes under $25,000, and those age 18-29.

A reminder that the average household income for Logan is $43,056. The median
household income for a similar sized area is $62,354. There is a correlation between
income and well-being.

Utah Watershed Council Act – Andy Rasmussen and Dan Adams – Langdon Group

Andy Rasmussen and Dan Adams from the Langdon Group addressed the Council
regarding the Watershed Act as of present. The Local Watershed Council is in ‘a balance
appropriate for the watershed’ and ‘where feasible’ members shall represent the
following interests: Agriculture, Industry, Indian Tribes, Public Water Suppliers, Water
planning and Research Intuitions, Water Quality, Fish and Wildlife, Water Dependent
Habitat and Environments, Watershed Management, Mutual Irrigation Companies, and
Local Sponsors of Reclamation projects.

A local council may also invite state and federal agencies to designate a liaison to the
local Watershed Council. The local Watershed Council members MUST “reside or work
within or own or control the right to divert water within the watershed.”

Local Watershed Councils Role:

Similar to the Utah Watersheds Council, but at a local level will encourage and facilitate
discussion and collaboration. Facilitate communication and coordination among state and
federal agencies in the implementation of water-related activities. Communicate local
issues, concerns, and priorities to the Utah Watershed Council. Furthermore, the local
Watershed Council will NOT be a replacement for existing organizations NOR
regulatory.

Current Process Update:

Organize documents and policy templates including the council formation and
certification of process drafted.

Bear River will meet with representatives from the Cache Water District. (Staff
acknowledges the tremendous amount of work that has been down with these and other
groups). There are plans to create a Watershed Council for the Great Salt Lake itself.

Next Steps:

Ratify Year One Council onboarding process and schedule. Continue to organize and
convene Bear River, Jordan, and Weber stakeholders. And establish dates and events to
launch local Watershed Council.

Mr. DeSimone stated that on October 27, 2022, the Planning Commission recommended
the Municipal Council approve the Moderate-Income Housing Plan as an amendment to
the Logan City General Plan.

This is a proposal to amend the Logan City General Plan by adopting the 2022 Moderate
Income Housing Plan. The State Legislature mandates that every five years, all cities are
required to adopt a Moderate Income Housing Plan that meets the needs of people of
various income levels living, working, or desiring to live or work in the community;
allows people with various incomes to benefit from and fully participate in all aspects of
neighborhood and community life; and includes an analysis of how the municipality will
provide a realistic opportunity for the development of moderate income housing with the
next five years.

The State requirements were changed in 2022 requiring that municipalities select their
specific Moderate Income Housing Strategies from a list contained in Utah State Statute
10-9a-403(2)(b)(iii) and include implementation plans detailing how the City will follow
through on the provision of Moderate-Income Housing. The Commission’s
recommendation include eight (8) strategies along with a number of strategies
recommended by staff. Planning Commissioners vote (6-0).

What is Moderate Income Housing? It is housing for households with a gross income
equal to or less than 80% of the Area Median Income (AMI).

What is Moderate Income Housing Plan? A plan that provides a reasonable opportunity
to meet the need for additional moderate income housing (10-9a-403(2)(a)(iii)).

Logan City adopted the 2019 Moderate Income Housing Plan, but the State passed HB
(House Bill) 462 in 2022 changing MIH (Moderate Income Housing) requirements. An
updated plan and annual reporting are due on October 1, 2022. (And at present, we are
slightly late in submitting).

HB 462 Highlights:

   •   Clarifies MIH requirements and timing to amend the City's General Plan (MIH is
       an element of the Logan General Plan).
   •   Requires the inclusion of an implementation plan to bring the MIH element to life
       (2019 MIHP did not include this).
   •   Amends the list of strategies to use (2019 MIHP not consistent with the modified
       State strategies).
   •   Outlines the annual reporting requirements (Updated requirements).
   •   Adds priority incentives/restrictions for compliance with the MIH requirement
       (UDOT Transportation Funding potentially at risk).

What is the Priority Consideration in HB 462 and what is at stake if we do not comply?
Transportation Project Funding (TIF & TTIF) and propriety consideration from the
Transportation Consideration. The recommendation is that the 2022 Logan City MIHP
