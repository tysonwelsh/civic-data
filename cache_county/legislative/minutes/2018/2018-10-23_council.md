UNIT OR COMMITTEE REPORTS

2018 Cache County Fair & Rodeo — LaMont Poulsen reported the rodeo was very
successful and thanked the Council, Trish Gibbs, Scott Wilkinson and the Sheriff's
Department for all their help and support. He expressed regret that Brian Locke, who
recently passed, was no longer involved with the rodeo. He enjoyed working with him
over the years. The community is very supportive of the rodeo. Saturday night had five
contestants who are in the top fifteen of finals. There were national finalists every night
and three world champions were here. Council member Worthen asked how many
attended the rodeo. Poulsen said 15,000 or 18,000 and he has met with a bleacher
company to see if some seating can be added. The rodeo brought in a profit and he
credited their good sponsors ($60,000 donated) for helping make that possible. He also
thanked the County Road Department for helping get the big screen installed.

Lane Parker, Fair Manager, said the Fair went well; however, some of the food vendors
were disappointed because there were too many so they didn’t make as much money.
Parker will try to correct that next year. He tried to work closely with all the volunteers
and thanked them. Signage on the fairgrounds needs to be improved. Stephanie
Carlsen did a great job on the computer entry program. She will meet with the company
that does the software to make changes to help everything run smoother.

Council member Worthen thanked Poulsen and Parker on behalf of the Council and
Executive Buttars added his thanks.

PUBLIC HEARING: October 23, 2018 -5:30 p.m.-Apple Goat Acres Rezone-Request for a
rezone of 16.78 acres from the Agricultural (A10) Zone to the Rural (RU5) Zone. Located at
4211 West 2600 North, Benson — Chris Harrild said the Planning Commission recommends
approval, 3-1 and noted that the Planning Commission has considerable angst over these
rezones because there is not a good checklist to work from and they want a better
understanding of where to place these zones. They are uncomfortable making specific to site
only decisions. They would like a stop-gap measure while awaiting the master plan.

Harrild said the existing road on this request does not meet road standards so if and when a
subdivision is applied for, that will have to be addressed. Because of the wetlands only 2-3
parcels will be allowed.

Vice Chairman Ward opened the public hearing and invited public comment.

Jeff Watterson, applicant, said his daughter wants to build a home on the property so his intent for the
property is a single home.

There was no other public comment.

ACTION: Motion by Council member White to close the Public Hearing. Zilles seconded
the motion. The vote was unanimous, 6-0. Erickson absent.

The Council is generally in favor of approving the rezone. Harrild will prepare the documents for
the next Council meeting.

It was decided that Harrild will put together several ideas for guidance on the RUS and RU10
zones for the Council to review.

MEMORANDUM. 23 October 2018

To: County Council
Subject: View of the Valley Design Exception request

A request has been made by Douglas Bassett (Ambience Construction and Development) for a
Design Exception on county road 1600 West, to allow access to the View of the Valley Subdivision,
consisting of three additional lots, without road improvements to the paved portion of 1600 West
(Exhibit A).

The Cache County Manual of Roadway Design and Construction Standards (Road Manual) section
1.8 Authority and Design Exceptions, specifies that:
“Full justification and documentation must be provided explaining the reasoning as to why the
roadway standards cannot be met, why an alternative design or construction method can meet the
intent of the roadway standards, and including any other relevant information.”

The View of the Valley Subdivision has been approved by the Planning Commission with the
condition that the proponent obtains a design exception from the Road Manual for the substandard
sections of the access road (Exhibit B).

The submitted request for Design Exception provides the following reasoning:
1. The existing roadway surface was recently chipped and sealed by the county.
2. The Nautica Subdivision (11 additional lots) gains access from 1600 West and was approved.
3. The cost to the proponent to meet the standards is unbearable for the proponent.

The Development Services staff and Director have completed a review of the Design Exception
request and have identified the following:
1. The Road Manual specifies the following:

a. §2.1-A-3 Local Road, Table 2.2 Roadway Typical Sections: Roads with approximately
40 to 1500 Average Daily Traffic (ADT). This includes roadways that have the capacity
for moderate to low speeds and moderate volumes. This category provides a balance
between through traffic movements and direct access. These facilities move both regional
and local rural traffic with emphasis on local movements.

b. Table 2.2 Roadway Typical Sections: Local roads must meet the minimum standards of a
66-foot-wide right-of-way, two 10-foot-wide paved travel lanes with 2-foot-wide
shoulders (1-foot-wide gravel and 1-foot-wide paved) for a total width of 24 feet.

c. §2.4-A-1-c: Development on inadequate roadways is not allowed, and any substandard
sections of roadway access must be improved to meet the minimum standards specified
in the Road Manual.

d. Table A-8 Typical Cross Section Structural Values: The minimum structural composition
for gravel roads requires 14” depth of granular borrow, 6” depth of road base, and paved
roads required an additional 2.5” depth of asphalt.

e. §2.4-A-4-b: The review of requests for development on existing roadways must occur
through the Design Exception process.
