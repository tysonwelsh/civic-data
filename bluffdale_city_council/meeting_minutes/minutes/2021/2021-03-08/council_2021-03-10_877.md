         a.     Discussion Related to Day Ranch Park and the Rodeo Arena, Staff Presenters,
                Jennifer Robison and Shane Paddock.

Senior City Planner, Jennifer Robison presented the staff report and discussed the amenities that
are coming to the City. She stated that many are excited about the development of the park. Items
that may be of potential benefit to the community were described. A survey was conducted a few
years back with the primary desire expressed being for playgrounds, access to trails, and open
space. There has also been a lot of interest in the Dog Park and other amenities. A cost estimate
was prepared, which was helpful with Vintage Park and other projects. To date, the cost estimate
was $6.5 million to do the entire development. It was also broken down into a phasing schedule.

Mrs. Robison reported that this specific park is just over 18 acres in size. Because there is
separation from the road to the north of the school, there are two different types of uses proposed.
One area was identified as the Dog Park. In 2018 and 2019, the City worked with the school
district who allowed the City to utilize the property at no cost as a Dog Park. An agreement is in

Independence area to the south was also envisioned. This particular park would be built using
impact fees.

Mayor Timothy commented that any amenity that is attractive to teenagers would be important
and believes the focus should be on giving teenagers a place to go. He would like information as
to what the project would cost upfront compared to the cost with inflation if the project were paid
for in phases.

4.     Presentation of the Fire Department Year-End Statistical Report, Staff Presenter,
       Fire Chief Warren James.

Fire Chief, Warren James provided a year-end review for the Council. He reviewed the
organizational chart and reported that they have reduced personnel from 120 to 80. They have two
Battalion Chiefs, and Chief James is currently filling the A Platoon Battalion Chief position. They
are operating using bid shifts, which allows more consistency in scheduling. Since they have
reduced staff, they experience fewer issues filling their shifts consistently. The reduction in
personnel has been positive for the firefighters who are now seeing more consistent shifts. From
a budgetary standpoint, this reduction has reduced the Department's costs for equipment.

Chief James summarized the department' s statistics for the year. They received 603 calls for
emergency service to 911 , regardless of whether Bluffdale rigs responded to those calls. He noted
that Bluffdale provided more mutual aid to their neighboring agencies than they received, so they
have more calls than the calls for service within Bluffdale. Last year they responded to 773 calls,
which is a 4.3% increase over the prior year. Of these calls, 523 were medical calls, which resulted
in transport for 175 patients. The Department responded to 250 fire calls, which was a 16%

increase from the previous year. He attributed the increase to the new Computer Aided Dispatch
("CAD") system where they send units based on location, regardless of agency.

Chief James reported that 472 of the total calls received were within Bluffdale, with the remainder
being mutual aid calls. Station 91 received 410 calls, while Station 92 received 363 calls. He
reported that in 2020, they collected a total of $167,915 from 157 of the ambulance calls. They
collected 89% of the calls they billed, which is an outstanding collection rate. He added that South
Salt Lake collects less than 40% of the calls they bill. He explained that they only collected 50%
of the dollars billed, largely due to Medicare caps on payment for these services.

Wednesday mornings are the busiest day and time in terms of calls. The busiest month of the year
is July. Chief James stated that they responded to more traffic accidents than any other type of
medical call. They also responded to more "sick patient" calls than they typically would, which
he attributed to COVID-19. Chief James also listed the types of fire calls they received. Fire
losses last year totaled approximately $97,000, which includes structures and crops.

Chief James discussed some of the Department's accomplishments from 2020. The biggest
accomplishment involved preparedness and response to COVID-19. He also mentioned that they
received funding to transition to a computer based staffing program which has been very beneficial
for personnel. In addition, shift bids have created more consistent and static crews. They also
upgraded some of their equipment to include the latest version of Self-Contained Breathing
Apparatus ("SCBA"). They have one SCBA mask per person and they are annually fit tested. He
also reported that all department personnel complete a required physical every year. He mentioned
the good work of the two battalion chiefs, Matt Evans and Jarred Roberts. They also have seven
new lieutenants to ensure that department equipment is maintained. All of their front-line vehicles
are now equipped to the paramedic level.

Chief James also stated that they have purchased rescue task force equipment to respond to an
active-shooter-type situation in a school. The Department upgraded its swift water rescue
equipment as well as upgraded expired helmets and equipment. They issued wildland protective
equipment to all personnel and all personnel are now wildland firefighter certified.

In response to an inquiry from Mayor Timothy, Chief James reported that their Type 6 engines are
equipped to the Federal and State levels for wildland response. Bluffdale sent crews to assist with
fires in Oregon and California, which provided a tremendous training experience for those
firefighters .

Chief James also highlighted the new CAD system as well as the major repairs to Engine 91. They
are currently working on the design of a new Engine 91 that will take eight months to build after
the chassis is delivered to the manufacturer in Minnesota. They also upgraded their light fleet.

Mark Hales added that Chief James was appointed this past year, and many of these
accomplishments are the result of his hard work. Chief James thanked staff and the Council for
funding and support. In response to an inquiry from Mayor Timothy, Chief James stated that the

Mr. Paddock continued the discussion on the arena and bleacher upgrades. He stated the VIP

obligation bond approved by the citizens.
