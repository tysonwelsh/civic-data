GPZ-2-2021
Matthew Idema
3724 S 3200 W
General Plan change from large lot residential to low density residential and a zone change
from A to RS
4.3 acres

Matthew Idema has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for four parcels totaling
4.3 acres located at 3724 S 3200 W. The property is currently zoned A (agriculture, minimum lot
size ½ acre) with a General Plan designation of large lot residential (2 to 3 units/acre). The
proposed zone is RS (residential sustainability) and the proposed General Plan designation is low
density residential (3 to 4 units/acre). If this application is approved, it would be the first RS
zone established in the City.
Surrounding zones include R-1-8 (single unit dwelling residential, minimum lot size 8,000
square feet) to the west, R-1-8 and A to the south, R-2-6.5 (two unit dwelling residential,
minimum lot size 6,500 square feet) and R-1-8 to the north, and R-1-8 and A to the east.
Surrounding land uses include single family homes to the south; duplexes and single family
homes to the west and north; and a fourplex, duplexes, and single family homes to the east. The
two north parcels each include a home. The other two parcels are vacant.

As shown in the attached concept plan, the applicant is proposing to demolish the existing homes
and develop a 15-lot subdivision. The proposed, average lot size is 8,702 sq. ft. The RS zone,
which was created last year, allows for 8,000 sq. ft. lots in exchange for a development
agreement commitment to build homes with high energy efficiency and low water use. Included
with this report are the minimum standards for the RS zone. The applicant’s proposed
development agreement incorporates all of the RS zone standards as written in the ordinance.

One of the requirements of the RS zone is: “The development agreement shall include
commitments on the quality of the interior finishes of all homes.” To address this requirement,
the applicant has proposed the following interior features: 5” baseboard and 3” casing; two-tone
paint; cabinets with full-extension drawers and soft close doors; solid surface countertops; choice
of travertine, ceramic tile or hardwood floors in kitchen and entry; carpet with 8 lb. pad; textured
or smooth interior walls with rounded corners and textured ceilings; fiberglass pan for washer
(on framed floor only); plumbing rough-in for future basement bathroom; TJI® silent floor
system; and soft water rough-in.

While the RS zone does not exempt homes from the exterior design treatment standards in
Section 7-11-212, staff recommends that these standards be included in the development
agreement so that it is clear that the standards will apply.

The RS zone also includes a maximum district size of 10 acres. When the RS zone was first
created in the ordinance, the City Council wanted to see how a smaller RS zone development
would work before considering a larger development. This property is well below the 10-acre
cap.

During the study session a concern was raised about the long-term maintenance of two parcels
designated for storm water. The applicant is looking into ways to address this concern. This issue
will likely best be addressed during the subdivision review process assuming this application is
approved.

By way of background, a General Plan/zone change application was made back in 2015 on three
of the four subject parcels. The proposal was for 29 townhomes with RM zoning. The
application was denied.


Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. This application should be approved subject to a development agreement that
      requires:
         a. All of the commitments made by the applicant
         b. The standards in Section 7-11-212

   2. Continuance.

   3. Denial. The RE zone is more appropriate at this location.

GPZ-3-2021
Ken Milne
6087 and 6021 W Parkway Blvd
General plan change from rural residential to medium density residential and zone change
from A to RM
13.8 acres
Ken Milne has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for two parcels totaling 13.8
acres located at 6087 and 6021 W Parkway Blvd. The property is currently zoned A (agriculture,
minimum lot size ½ acre) with a General Plan designation of rural residential (1 to 2 units/acre).
The proposed zone is RM (multiple unit dwelling residential) and the proposed General Plan
designation is medium density residential (7 to 12 units/acre).

Surrounding zones include A-2 (agriculture, minimum lot size 2 acres) to the east, A-1
(agriculture, minimum lot size 1 acre) to the north, A and A-1 to the west and R-1-10 (single unit
dwelling residential, minimum lot size 10,000 sq. ft.) to the south. Surrounding land uses include
single family homes and mostly vacant property to the north owned by Magna Water, single
family homes to the west and south, and vacant property to the east owned by UDOT. According
to the Public Works Department, the UDOT parcel will be transferred to the City and will
eventually be used as park space and storm water detention. Aside from a couple of barns located
near Parkway Blvd, the subject property is vacant.

The applicant is proposing an 84-unit, single level, senior condo development. The proposed
density of the project is 6.1 units/acre. Each unit would have a two-car garage and a driveway
large enough to park two vehicles in. The proposed minimum unit size is 1,500 sq. ft. The
attached concept plan and application materials from the applicant provide details on and support
for the proposed project. For comparison, the table below describes four existing senior condo
developments within the City:

By way of background, the parcel at 6087 W Parkway Blvd was rezoned from A-1 to A back in
2016. A development agreement was approved on this parcel that required homes to comply with
the City’s housing standards.



In reviewing this application, staff suggests that there are two primary questions to answer:

   1. Is this the right location for a senior condo development?

   2. Assuming the location is appropriate, does the quality of the project further City goals?
These two questions are explored below.

Is this the right location for a senior condo development?

The City Council recently amended the criteria to determine whether a property would be
eligible to apply for the RM zone. Prior to this change, the subject property was not eligible to be
rezoned to the RM zone. The recent code change made properties eligible to apply for RM
zoning if they meet the following standards:
     the property is vacant,
     the proposed project on the property is age restricted to seniors,
     the density of the proposed project is no greater than 7 units/acre, and
     the property has access and frontage on a street with a planned right-of-way width of at
        least 80 feet.
The proposed project meets these standards. The property is also conveniently located within
about ¼ mile of shopping and could serve as a transition between the Mountain View Corridor to
the east and single family homes to the west.

While the property is eligible for RM zoning, the City may still deny requests for RM zoning. An
argument could be made to retain the A zone given other agricultural zoning along Parkway
Blvd. An argument could also be made for the RE zone given the neighboring single family
homes.

Does the quality of the project further City goals?

City Code requires requests for the RM zone to be accompanied with a development agreement
proposal that addresses dwelling unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, landscaping and
project amenities. Each of these items is addressed below.

Dwelling Unit Sizes

The proposed units include 2 bedrooms plus a flex space that could function as a third bedroom.
The proposed minimum unit size is 1,500 sq. ft. For dwelling units that are attached to the side of
each other, City Code requires 1,100 sq. ft. for a 2-bedroom unit and 1,400 sq. ft. for a 3-
bedroom unit.

Exterior Materials

For materials, the City’s multi-unit residential design standards state: “All multi-unit residential
Building exteriors shall be brick, stucco, stone, tile, metal panels or fiber cement siding. With the
exception of brick or stone which may be used on the entire Building exterior, no single material
shall comprise more than 60 percent of a Building’s exterior. Where stucco, tile, metal panels or
fiber cement siding are used, at least 20 percent of the Building’s exterior shall be brick or stone.
Wood and vinyl may be used as trim, soffits and/or accent materials only.”
The applicant is proposing a combination of fiber cement siding and stone where the stone would
be at least 25% of the exterior. This means that fiber cement siding would comprise more than
60% of the exterior. This can be addressed by adding another material like stucco, increasing the
amount of stone, or granting an exception through the development agreement.

Architecture

Based on a preliminary review of the senior condo architecture by staff, the proposed units
appear to be deficient in the required 25% fenestration requirement on the fronts, one garage
treatment and the minimum roof pitch of 6:12. After discussing these items with the applicant, he
agreed to amend the plans to reflect these requirements.

When comparing the proposed exteriors with those in the Bingham Point development, it is
staff’s opinion that the Bingham Point buildings include features that make the buildings more
attractive. Staff relayed these features, like more gables or larger gables on the front and more
color variation to the applicant for consideration.

Landscaping

The current ordinance requires 50% open space for condo project with 15% of the site being
usable open space. A code change recently recommended for approval by the Planning
Commission would require 50% landscaping and recreation space. At the time this report was
prepared, the applicant was working on this calculation.

Project Amenities

For a project of this size, the ordinance requires three amenities. The proposed project includes
four amenities from the ordinance: clubhouse, fitness room, sport court, and walking trail.

Interior finishes and amenities include 9’ ceilings throughout with an optional vault in any room,
two-tone paint, and quartz or granite countertops in the kitchen and bathrooms.

In the proposed development agreement standards, the developer is also proposing concrete
address plates for each unit, street lights, wood grained vinyl fencing installed on the perimeter,
and 20 visitor parking spaces.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Continuance. This application should be continued to allow the applicant time to refine
      the renderings and calculate the landscaping and recreation space percentage.
   2. Approval. This General Plan/zone change application should be approved subject to a
      development agreement that incorporates the standards proposed by the applicant with
      the following additions/revisions recommended by staff:
          a. The number of units be capped at 84 as proposed on the concept plan.
          b. As proposed by the applicant, the development will be aged restricted where 80%
              of the units must have at least one resident that is 55 years old or older.
         c. The minimum roof pitch shall be 6:12.
         d. The buildings shall comply with the exterior material requirements in the
             ordinance.
         e. Garage doors shall be offset from each other.
         f. The minimum window percentage on the front elevation shall be 25%.
         g. At least 50% of the total site shall be landscaping or recreation space.
   3. Denial. This application should be denied to preserve the agricultural zoning along
      Parkway Blvd.

SA-3-2021
Anna Caroline Drive Subdivision Amended
2450 South Anna Caroline Drive
M Zone

BACKGROUND

The West Valley City Engineering Division is requesting preliminary approval for the Anna
Caroline Drive Subdivision. This application will amend portions of lot 1, Parcel A, and Anna
Caroline Drive as recorded in the Anna Caroline Drive Subdivision.

ISSUES:

The application is being submitted at the request of the Engineering Division on behalf of the Utah
Department of Transportation. The purpose for the amended subdivision is to revise the alignment
of the built portion of Anna Caroline Drive within the Anna Caroline Drive Subdivision.

The Anna Caroline Drive Subdivision was recorded with the Salt Lake County Recorder’s Office
in 2019. The subdivision is located immediately to the north of the Cabco 5600 West Subdivision
and adjacent to 2400 South. As part of the Anna Caroline Drive subdivision, UDOT constructed
the right-of-way from 2400 South to the north line of the Cabco 5600 West Subdivision.

Shortly after the recordation of the Anna Caroline Drive plat, UDOT began construction of Anna
Caroline Drive within the boundary of their subdivision. While the City was inspecting the
installation of improvements, the Engineering Division noticed that the right-of-way was not in
the correct location. The portion of Anna Caroline Drive constructed by UDOT is approximately
3 feet to the east of where it should be.

The Engineering Division contacted UDOT and the owner of the Cabco 5600 West Subdivision
to discuss the problem. Since the owner of Cabco 5600 West was going to build his portion of
Anna Caroline Drive, a resolution to the problem needed to be worked out sooner than later. The
solution that everyone agreed upon, is to re-align the road slightly to the east in order to make the
connection of this road work. In order to do this the City required that both subdivisions be
amended to reflect the correct location of the improvements.

The subdivision plat identifies the area of the existing Anna Caroline Drive that will be vacated
and identifies a portion of the right-of-way to be dedicated. Thus, the amendment to a portion of
lot 1, Parcel A and Anna Caroline Drive.

STAFF ALTERNATIVES:

        engineering requirements for the amended plat.

2.      Continue the application for reasons determined in the Planning Commission meeting.

201 Commerce Center Subdivision – Phase 5
M Zone
4 Lots & 1 Parcel
32          Acres

BACKGROUND:

Steve Layton, representing, 201 CC Land LLC, is requesting preliminary plat approval for the 5th
and final phase of the 201 Commerce Center Subdivision. This plat also amends Parcel D of the
201 Commerce Center Subdivision Phase 4. The subject property is presently vacant and is located
south of the SR-201 South Frontage Road at approximately 4400 West.

ISSUES:

The proposed subdivision is being requested in order to divide the existing property into 4 lots and
1 parcel. Along with the division of lots and parcel, the subdivision plat will dedicate a new road
to be known as 4400 West and the remaining portion of 2250 South platted as part of Phase 4.

Access to the subdivision will be gained from the SR-201 South Frontage Road, Commerce Center
Drive and from 2250 South. Improvements along the SR-201 South Frontage Road and Commerce
Center Drive will be consistent with other phases of the project. The cross section to be used for
new streets within the subdivision will be a 66-foot right-of-way. A 5-foot parkstrip and 5-foot
sidewalk will be located on one side of the street along with a 14-foot landscaped area. The other
side will have a 15-foot landscaped area. City ordinance requires that the landscaping on each side
of the right-of-way be 20 feet. However, the applicant is proposing a reduction of landscaping on
each side of the road system in order to accommodate larger buildings that are a necessity for this
type of development. For this reason, this application is being processed as a PUD.

In consideration of a reduction in landscaping, the applicant will be providing a 1.7-acre park in
the northwest corner of the subdivision. The park would be an area where employees of the center
could go for a walk, engage in recreation activity, have lunch in the pavilion or simply enjoy being
outside on a park bench. Although the main function of this area will be for those types of uses,
the park will also serve to accommodate storm water needs. The engineering for the storm water
capacity is currently being reviewed. Staff wants to make sure that the park and proposed
amenities take first seat over storm water storage. Final review with City Council will not occur
until staff is satisfied that the park will serve its intended use as an amenity for the project.

Drainage swales used in previous phases along Commerce Center Drive will continue to be used
in this phase. Based on the overall grading and drainage plan, the drainage swale will continue as
originally proposed and approved by the City Engineering Division. The landscaping and
construction of the swales will be reviewed as part of the conditional use.

CA-1-2021
201 Commerce Center – Buildings #1-4
4387 West 2100 South
M Zone (32.45 Acres)

The applicant, Steve Layton with DLM Development, is requesting a conditional use amendment
for four (4) office/warehouse buildings at 4387 West 2100 South. This property is zoned M,
Manufacturing, and the West Valley City General Plan designates this area as Light
Manufacturing. This project is part of the 78 acre 201 Commerce Center project. The subdivision
for this project is being reviewed under PUD-1-2021.

When this project was initially approved in 2008 (C-36-2008) the design included 19 buildings
of various sizes with a total square footage of 1,189,256. At this time the applicant would like to
take what was originally proposed as 7 buildings and construct 4 larger buildings and a 1.7 acre
park area.

The buildings will be constructed to match the existing buildings on site (5, 6 and 7). They will
be tilt up concrete panel construction painted three colors that are used to create a wainscot and
column appearance along all sides of the buildings. The office portions are enhanced with large
windows, relief in the roof line and architectural awning features above the entrances. This
facility is not required to meet the Commercial Design Standards due to the use being limited to
a manufacturing zone.

The general breakdown of the uses includes approximately 20% office uses and 80% warehouse.
The uses will be monitored as building permits and business licenses are submitted so the
parking will be evaluated periodically to ensure that the site is in compliance with the ratio of
uses.

The landscaping required on site is 5% and the applicant has provided 9.7%. All areas that are
considered landscaping must have a minimum of 50% live plant material. As discussed in the
PUD process the applicant has asked for some relief in the roadway cross section and setbacks
along 4400 West and 2250 South. This will help accommodate the larger building footprints. As
part of that request a 1.7 acre park will be constructed. This area will be used for detention but
will also include walking paths, picnic areas and an activity court.
The east edge of the site along Commerce Center Drive includes a grass swale with a 2’ wide
concrete gutter, a shrub row to screen the parking and trees planted every 30. This design was
part of the original approval for the overall center for LEED (Leadership in Energy and
Environmental Design) requirements in the storm water category. The north side has frontage
along 2100 South, which is a High Image Arterial. The setback in that area will be bermed with a
shrub row and trees planted every 30’.

The sidewalk design for this entire phase is still being coordinated with Public Works to ensure
street crossings are safe and that the connectivity makes sense for the proposed ROW cross
sections. This also includes sidewalks along 2100 South.

Signage was reviewed with the previous overall project submittal as well as with the Agreement
for Disposition of Land (ADL) with the City Council. The approval included wall signage for the
buildings, monument style directory signs throughout the site and one pole/development sign on
the 2100 South frontage. All signage shall comply with the requirements set forth in the Sign
Ordinance and require a building permit.

A lighting plan must be submitted that complies with the standards set forth in the West Valley
City Code. Several dumpsters are located throughout this area and they will all be secured within
a 6’ masonry enclosure and additional landscaping has been proposed around these facilities to
help further screen them from view. Any mechanical equipment proposed on site or on the
building shall also be properly screened from view per the standards set forth in the West Valley
City Code.

Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any issues raised at the public hearing and the following
conditions.

1. The buildings and site must be constructed per the approved plans.

2. There must be adequate parking on site for the proposed tenant mix.

3. The landscaping must be completed per the approved plan and in accordance with applicable
   standards in the West Valley City Municipal Code.

4. The right-of-way may be modified per the submitted cross section and the design shall
   include a park as outlined in PUD-1-2021.

5. All signage must comply with the West Valley City Sign Ordinance.

6. All lighting shall be designed per Title 7 and installed in accordance with the approved
   lighting plan.

7. All dumpster locations shall be completely secured with a 6’ masonry enclosure or located
   out of public view per 7-2-118(4).
8. All mechanical equipment on site must be properly screened.

9. Must meet requirements of all effected departments and agencies.


Continuance, to allow for the resolution of any issues raised at the public hearing.
