Discuss Vision 2020 Priorities:
Jed Briggs and Linda Jager presented this item. Matt Dias stated the goal was to
determine priorities as a Council, then budget priorities would be given to those Council
priorities. Briggs reviewed the history of the visioning process from 2009 to present and
noted the priorities of Energy, Social Equity, Transportation, and Housing were
prioritized throughout every visioning process.

Council Member Gerber stated the priorities had evolved. There was value in the five
strategic pillars. It didn't matter how it was phrased, but those priorities needed to be the
focus. Council Member Rubell thought it would be helpful to hear how others wanted to
define the priorities. Council Member Gerber noted when the critical priorities were first
discussed, it focused on the top issues Council wanted to address. The goal was to
clearly explain what the Council's priorities were. Mayor Worel indicated the priorities
were communicated that if Council didn't get these top issues right, there would be dire
consequences.

Budget Outlook Discussion:
Jed Briggs, Penny Frates, and Erik Daenitz, Budget Department, presented this item.
Briggs reviewed the timeline of the budget process. A tentative budget would be
adopted in May. Further budget discussions would take place and tweaks would be
made before the final budget was passed June 23rd. He reviewed the budget cuts made
when COVID-19 impacted the community two years ago. There was a recession plan
and cuts were made based on lost revenue. Last year, budgets were restored to some
departments based on demonstrable need. Briggs explained the Budgeting for
Outcomes (BFO) process. This process ensured that the budget reflected what the City
wanted to accomplish. Staff developed budget requests around the desired outcomes
and projects were prioritized accordingly. Briggs noted general operations were
included in the budget, but the Budget Department looked to Council direction for big
projects.

Mayor Worel asked if Council could see all the budget requests, including the ones that
were not awarded. Briggs affirmed Council could see that information. Dias stated every
request was presented to Council last year and staff would continue to do that. Council
Member Rubell asked if the BFO process was used for 100% of budgeting, or just for
certain requests. Briggs stated BFO was for outcomes the Council was trying to
achieve. He explained the weighting system for BFO. Council Member Toly asked who
sat on the BFO committee, to which Briggs stated it was representatives from different
City departments. He explained each request was scored based on certain
prioritizations. Council Member Rubell asked if items were grouped into mandated items
and elevated priorities. Briggs stated a lot of requests were on a case-by-case basis.

Each program was scored based on criteria and the committee used the scores to

Rubell expressed concern that programs would continue to grow although the City
might be moving in a different direction. Briggs stated the committee was responding to
departments that needed more funding, but they could start cutting programs with low

adjustment allocated additional funds to departments since revenue was above the

Briggs explained budget allocation quartiles. Daenitz reviewed the revenue sources and
their uses. Property and sales tax went to operating expenses. Resort taxes were used
for downtown streets and storm water. Transient Room Tax (TRT) was designated for
the Arts and Culture District. Transit sales tax supported Transportation. Council
Member Gerber asked if TRT funds could be invested until they were used. Briggs
stated they were invested at the State level. He also explained the State allowed cities
to have a budget reserve balance up to 35% of the budget.

Daenitz explained how he projected growth and noted this year had a revenue growth of
12%. He projected a 8% increase in revenue for FY23. Council Member Toly asked if
local indicators were analyzed, to which Daenitz stated he could analyze the behavior of
visitors through cellphone data. Council Member Toly stated Park City Mountain Resort
(PCMR) ticket sales were in the City, but the hotel was in the County, and asked if lift
ticket tax revenue went to the City, to which Daenitz affirmed. Briggs noted that
although revenues were setting records, inflation was a real factor as the City saw
increased expenses as well. Daenitz indicated that with the projections, he estimated a
conservative 8% revenue growth for next year. Council Member Dickey asked if the
lower and upper band projections were shown in the current budget and if the revenues
fell within 95%. Daenitz stated even with the record sales tax revenue numbers, it did
not exceed the 95%.

pay increases at the 90th percentile and he didn't think the 75th percentile was sufficient.

Briggs reviewed the General Fund received more than was spent, and some of that
excess was transferred to the Capital Improvement Fund each year. One alternative
was to reduce that transfer to be able to use those funds on additional projects. Another
revenue strategy was to budget expenses over revenues and dip into the reserve
balance. Council could also determine to move additional money into the reserve
balance to build it up to the 35% maximum allowed by the State.
