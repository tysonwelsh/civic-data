1.1 Update on 3900 S. Reconstruction Project; Katie Kourianos, Horrocks Engineers
Mayor Silvestrini introduced Katie Kourianos from Horrocks Engineers. Ms. Kourianos
explained she was part of the public outreach team working on the 3900 S. Project. The
project limits are between 3900 S. and 2300 E. There will be safety enhancements as well
as curb, gutter, and sidewalk. Specifically, they will be working with Holladay City since
3900 S. is the border between municipalities. The public outreach team started working on
the project that summer. Ms. Kourianos stated they had received a lot of positive
feedback. They intend to add bike lanes and overhead flashing signals at 3100 E. and at
Birch Street to protect pedestrians. Mayor Silvestrini asked why the signal at 3100 E.
would not be a “hawk signal.” Ms. Kourianos said that the traffic flow dictated what type
of signal was to be installed. John Miller, Public Works Director, continued explaining

that the quantity of children crossing the road and traffic dictate which type of crossing is
implemented. However, the overhead flashing signal could be updated to a different signal
in the future. Someone in audience asked how the signal worked. Mr. Miller explained
that drivers must come to a full-stop when the light is solid red; when the light is flashing
red, it acts as a stop-sign. Mr. Miller stated the crossing works this way 24/7. Another
member of the audience stated that the children would then need to be trained. Council
Member Uipi asked how much less pedestrian traffic there was at 3100 E. Mr. Miller
stated that there was a third less pedestrian traffic at 3100 E. The Birch Street crossing was
a major route to Skyline High School. Anna Clare Shepherd stated she believed it was
important to pay attention to the street and make it easier for people to cross safely. Mr.
Miller stated that he will talk to Granite School District to figure out the actual school
boundaries.

Ms. Kourianos explained there would be UTA improvements, and UTA had input on the
project as well. She said drainage improvements would be made. Holladay City would be
putting in new xeriscape on the park strip. Ms. Kourianos showed the projected project
schedule. Currently, the engineers were in the design and right-of-way process. It was
anticipated that the project would be a 6-month construction project with construction
beginning in 2020. Mr. Miller talked about saving money on construction because
construction costs were increasing at 10% a year. Mayor Silvestrini asked about potential
road closures. Ms. Kourianos stated they did not have a specific plan yet., however, they
would try and keep two lanes open at any time. John Miller stated that Horrocks Engineers
was on-board through the whole project to keep the communication to residents whole and
complete. Mr. Miller stated that Horrocks had been doing a great job so far. Ms.
Kourianos said they had received a lot of questions about drive-way openings and they
communicate well with homeowners. The right-of way process was a critical part of the
design phase. They would obtain small leases of property to complete the project and there
would be about 88 right-of-way agreements. There are about 8 parcels where the City
would be buying property to complete the project. Mr. Miller clarified the property
acquisitions would be on corners to put in sidewalk for ADA compliance. Ms. Kourianos
explained the design team was narrowing their search for how many pieces of property
would be bought. Since the right-of-way was so important, they were working to
streamline the process with John Miller and Rita Lund.

Ms. Kourianos said the project team was talking to property owners, helping things move
along, letting residents know they are here for them. Horrocks Engineers sent
approximately 800 mailers to property owners along 3900 S. about a public open house.
The Horrocks team went door-to-door along the corridor and face-to-face conversations to
help ensure people were not surprised, contacting 144 people. One hundred and one
residents came to the open house. Since the open house, there had been a few calls and
emails, about 3-4 a week asking about the schedule and property impacts. Ms. Kourianos
passed out business cards with the project hotline phone number, email, and websites for
the project. Mr. Miller stated that this project was costing Millcreek residents nothing. The
project was being paid for with federal and county grant money. He said that Mayor
Silvestrini did a great job tracking down grants. Mayor Silvestrini said he went out to the
roadway on 3900 S. with John Miller and Wasatch Pavement and the road was in bad
condition.

1.2 Mountair Streetscape Presentation; Steve Gilbert, ArcSitio
Francis Lilly, Planning and Zoning Director, introduced Steve Gilbert to the Council.
Gilbert said making the wall taller in the Mountair Streetscape was a cost issue. Mr. Lilly
said Council Member Jackson had suggested that the wall at the streetscape be continuous
and 5 feet tall in an email earlier that day. Mr. Lilly explained that Council Member
Jackson was responding to the concerns of residents that live near Highland Drive; they
would like a buffer from Highland Drive. Mr. Gilbert explained that he had been working
with the community and City and presented the proposed streetscape at an open house on
October 12, 2019 at Adib’s Rug Gallery. Mr. Gilbert took comments from that open house
and incorporated them into the plan. He said residents were concerned that the lilac strip
provided a buffer and they wished to keep the buffer. Mr. Gilbert stated that they have a
few plans that incorporate a complete wall or partial sections of wall and lilacs. A
continuous brick wall would be very costly. Mr. Gilbert believed that the wall should be 4
feet tall because taller walls are often vandalized with graffiti. Residents were also
concerned about people hiding behind walls, so the four-foot wall helps to alleviate that.
Residents also expressed a desire for safer pedestrian crossing on Highland Drive.

Mr. Gilbert stated they would be adding new streetlights since there was currently only
one in the area. They intended to preserve lilac bushes when they could. Mr. Gilbert
proposed putting parking along Mountair for the local restaurants and landscaping to slow
down traffic. Additionally, residents would like the power lines buried, so he has been
working with other developers and Rocky Mountain Power to bury the lines. Some
individuals had expressed the desire for art pieces that block light and highlight the focal
point at the street at the end. There were potential plans for park benches for people that
might wait for the bus. Mr. Gilbert explained that a water wheel had been fabricated as a
focal point at the welcome sign. The brick sign would pay homage to the Brickyard area.
Mayor Silvestrini asked about the width of the walkway. Mr. Gilbert replied that it would
be 8 feet wide, enough room for people to walk side by side. The whole streetscape would
be 30 feet wide. Mountair Drive would be 22 feet wide to calm traffic but allow parking.
Mayor Silvestrini asked if there would be trees planted. Mr. Gilbert stated there were
currently Sycamore trees there and they would add more. They would also add
Hornbeams to soften the landscaping along the wall. There were plans for a bus pull-off
and bus stop shelter. UTA approval was needed to move the bus sign because the
proposed welcome sign would be where the bus sign was.

Mayor Silvestrini asked if there was feedback from the public on a continuous wall and if
the wall would only be tall at specific focal points. Mr. Gilbert explained that it would be
4-6 feet in height in certain sections and that many residents wanted a complete wall. He
said that a complete wall would be $400,00 and a partial non-continuous wall with plant
scape added in would be $200,000. Mayor Silvestrini stated he would be pleased with
more greenery and the City could add a fence type structure at some point near the
greenery. Council Member Catten asked if it would cost less to use a different material for
the fence and then alternate the material to make the wall continuous but still use
greenery. Mr. Gilbert stated that they could use a different low-maintenance material, but
the cost would end up being similar when using stone. He said a wrought iron fence would
keep people out and the City could put greenery around it. Council Member Catten asked
if a 4-5-foot wall would keep sound out. Mr. Gilbert said it would not, however, a taller

1.3 Proposed Idling Ordinance; Kurt Hansen, Legislative Policy Director
Kurt Hansen, Legislative Policy Director, explained that he used ideas from the
Cottonwood Heights idling ordinance. If individuals idled for more than two minutes, then
they would be subject to a traffic violation. Mayor Silvestrini asked what temperature Salt
Lake City and Cottonwood Heights used for their cutoff on violations. Salt Lake City and
Cottonwood Heights use 90 degrees on the high end and 32 degrees on the low end. John
Brems and Mayor Silvestrini stated perhaps these numbers should change because they
were too high and too low. Mr. Hansen suggested the range should be lowered from 90 to
80 and 32 to 40 degrees. Mayor Silvestrini and Council Member Uipi asked for more
consideration on the temperature range. Mr. Hansen said there would be exception
provisions for emergency vehicles, idling for testing the car, hybrid cars, and K-9 services.
He proposed that there should be a warning for a first offense and no fine and subsequent
offenses would be punishable as a parking violation. Council Member Catten asked who
would enforce the ordinance. Mr. Hansen stated that the police would enforce it. Mayor
Silvestrini said that enforcement would only happen in flagrant cases.

Mr. Hansen stated that he had a list of other proposed ordinances. He asked the Council to
consider his handout that had potential priorities that may need to be addressed as a City
and City Council. Mayor Silvestrini asked about a shopping cart ordinance. Mr. Hansen
stated he had not had new input on that ordinance. Mayor Silvestrini asked Mr. Lilly about
the fence ordinance. Mr. Lilly stated he had a draft of a fence ordinance and the Planning
Commission needed to review and vote on it. Mayor Silvestrini asked if the Community
Councils had seen the fence ordinance. Mr. Lilly said they had, and he would like for them
to see it again. He said Code Compliance had a lot of evidence that Millcreek needed a
fence ordinance. Rita Lund, Communications Director, stated that planner Erin O’Kelley
was working on the accessory dwelling units (ADU) ordinance and multiple surveys on it
had gone out.

1.4 Strategic Planning; Laurie Johnson; HR-Finance Director
Laurie Johnson, HR-Finance Director, said she had been updating the Strategic Plan. The
“engaged community” definition had been updated and proposed strategic objectives had
been updated with three options. The first was, “respond to the needs and concerns of the
community in a professional and respectful manner;” second, “provide ample
opportunities for community volunteerism and participation;” and three, “support an
environment of open communication between the community and stakeholders.” She
asked that the Council review those options. Ms. Johnson added a strategic priority on
“fiscally responsible government” and stated the definition as, “Millcreek supports a
fiscally responsible government through compliance with the Utah Fiscal Procedures Act
along with a transparent budget process, conservative spending and meticulous debt
management.” She stated that the City had been careful with debt and suggested adding
that the City would maintain a fund balance of a certain percent. By law, the City should
keep 5% though the City Council had been more frugal. She said the fund balance was
currently at 21% and could not go above 25%. Mayor Silvestrini stated that other
organizations had goals about what their fund balance should be. Mayor Silvestrini stated
that the Council had a practice of moving money from the fund balance to capital projects,

however, the purpose of the fund balance was to keep money for a rainy day. Mayor
Silvestrini asked that the Council have a goal of 15% in the fund balance. Mayor
Silvestrini said the City should not defer capital projects. John Geilmann, City Manager,
stated that 15% was a good target to keep. The City budget was $26 million which could
be depleted quickly in an emergency. Mayor Silvestrini stated the fund balance could help
a road repair when a storm drain fails to help prevent flooding. Council Member Catten
explained that the Council could set a high bar and it would send the message that the City
had been run a certain way so different councils in the future could continue being fiscally
frugal. Ms. Johnson stated this was a goal and could be changed as needed, however it was
important to keep it on paper. Council Member Uipi stated her support. Mr. Geilmann
stated the bond operators liked to see a written goal. Mayor Silvestrini said that bond
rating agencies liked Millcreek’s frugality.

Ms. Johnson explained there were objectives from the General Plan and that could carry
into the budget process and future strategic plans could list goals and accomplishments to
keep track of progress. She gave the Council a handout of a proposed five-year working
plan. She said that the Council needed to make plans for strategic priorities. Mayor
Silvestrini stated they should talk with John Miller about grant funding, perhaps add the
3900 S. project to the handout since it had been a goal for years. Ms. Johnson said it was a
good way to remember what the City was doing. Mayor Silvestrini stated that in the
future, 1300 E. would need to be resurfaced and the plan could help the City with the
grant-writing process by illustrating it as a long-term goal.

1.5 Mayor and Council’s Discretion on Discussion Topic
Mayor Silvestrini explained that Francis Lilly had a list of priorities and would present
them at the next work meeting. Mayor Silvestrini reported that the resolution on H.B. 411
had received interest from other jurisdictions and Taylorsville was looking at it as well.
Cameron Diehl from the Utah League of Cities and Towns was going to circulate the bill
to other jurisdictions. Mayor Silvestrini said he talked with Moab, Salt Lake City, and
Park City Mayors about working with Rocky Mountain Power on H.B. 411. Park City’s
Mayor had been working with a consultant and Mayor Silvestrini would like to make sure
this was looked at correctly. Mayor Silvestrini stated there would need to be a way to
work with other cities and settling disputes. John Brems, City Attorney, stated that the
rules talked about an interlocal agreement with all the cities and governments who adopt a
H.B. 411 resolution to simplify who Rocky Mountain Power dealt with. Mayor Silvestrini
said that was in the proposed rules. Kurt Hansen stated that Rocky Mountain Power said
there would be one agreement, not customized ones for each city.

Mayor Silvestrini brought up that Unified Police Department (UPD) and Unified Police
Department (UFA) may go to a weighted voting system for participating entities.
Millcreek was the largest voting member in the UPD and UFA. There are jurisdictions that
receive service but do not pay for that service. Mayor Silvestrini stated that two
jurisdictions could call for a weighted vote on budgetary matters. They might vote 50% on
weighted budget and 50% on population. This would give Millcreek protection to the
budget so smaller jurisdictions did not negatively influence Millcreek’s budget.

Mayor Silvestrini said he met with Taylorsville, Holladay, and Midvale Mayors since they
were the largest cities utilizing UPD services. In the last legislative session, the Mayor

the UPD Chief. Mayor Silvestrini explained the Sheriff was an elected official and elected
by all of Salt Lake County and only 340,000 people are served by UPD. The cities served

four cities would like more control and wished to modify the interlocal agreement.

the UPD Board. Mayor Silvestrini stated that the Board was looking for transparency in
the Sheriff's budget and with a transparent budget the cities could make deliberate
decisions. Mayor Silvestrini said this would be beneficial for the people of Salt Lake
