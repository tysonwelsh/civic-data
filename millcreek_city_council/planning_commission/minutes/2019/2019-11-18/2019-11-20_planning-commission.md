1.1 Consideration of CU-19-011, Conditional Use Permit for a 38-unit Multi-Family
Condominium Project Location: 4120 S. Highland Dr. Applicant: Paul Gardner
Planner: Blaine Gehring
Blaine Gehring said the property was currently The Old Meeting House reception center
operated by the applicant. Adjacent uses included commercial and office uses to the north
and east with single family homes to the south and west. The Old Meeting House sat on a
fault line and due to its age and original construction materials, it would cost too much to
bring it up to current codes. The applicant would like to demolish the building and build the
townhomes and condominiums. The 11 townhomes would be 3-bedroom 2-story units and

feet in height for projects that include solar panels with a capacity exceeding 50% of the
power demand. Gehring showed the Commission illustrations of what the monument would

1. The applicant shall provide a project parking as detailed on the site plan.

2. The applicant shall provide the proposed exterior detailing/finishes as provided for in the
application drawings.

3. The development will be limited to the eleven three-bedroom townhomes and twenty-seven 2-
bedroom condominiums as proposed on the plan.

parking area) shall be visible beyond the property line of any off-street parking area as required

14. The applicant shall obtain a complete review for an approved building permit and shall

16. All signs and outdoor light shall be dark sky compliant.

17. Provide a historic plaque and plaza commemorating the Old Meeting House/Winder Chapel

1.2 Consideration of CU-19-013, Conditional Use Permit for a Mixed-Use Project in the
Town Center Overlay Zone Location: 3330 S. Highland Dr. Applicant: Russ Sorensen
Planner: Francis Lilly
Francis Lilly asked the Commission about their evaluation of the information provided in the
staff report and presentation. He said under the Town Center Overlay Zone and base C-2
Zone, the option is provided for additional height up to 74 feet for properties within 500 feet
of the intersections of Highland Drive and 3300 S. and 1300 E. and 3300 S. He said that was
a policy decision made by the City Council when they revised inherited County ordinances.
The allowance was maintained because the General Plan and City Center Master Plan
identified those locations as where the highest intensity of uses would be most tolerated in
Millcreek’s urban fabric. The Planning Commission needed to analyze the building and
determine if the project met the requirements in code for the extra height allowance. Lilly
went over the main characteristics of the project: 176 dwellings; 40,000 square feet of
commercial, including dedicated office and commercial space; arranged in four distinct
buildings, including a stand-alone office building; commercial spaces would be
condominiums, requiring a second conditional use permit at a later date; commercial spaces
were designed with specific Highland Drive businesses in mind; building height along
Highland Drive would be 67 feet, additional height to the west was accommodated by a
change in grade; and the project was designed to comply with the existing Town Center
Overlay Zone and the pending City Center Overlay Zone.
Lilly said the property slopes downhill from Highland Drive at least 13 feet. The existing
property housed the closed Linda’s Furniture business. The property was surrounded by C-2
zoning, with Home Depot’s parking to the west, the old bowling alley to the north, Home
Depot to the south, and smaller scale commercial to the east. The nearest residence was over
250 feet away. Lilly showed the Commission renderings and elevations of the four buildings
in the project that have stepbacks, roof patios/above grade decks, at-grade parking to the
west, and underground parking to the south. He said the project did not include stucco as part
of the material to be used. The buildings would follow grade with the greatest height to the
south. Commissioner LaMar asked about the solid wall on the north building elevation. Lilly
said the Commission could ask the developer about it. Lilly said the applicant was seeking a
parking reduction for shared parking. A parking study was conducted and recommended that
the applicant incorporate travel demand management practices in the proposal. He said travel
demand management was the theory that a developer stipulates to reduced parking demand
based on proximity to transit or a mix of uses, that conditions and standards could be crafted
to require the developer to do certain things to justify the reductions. The highest shared
parking demand would happen on a weekday from 12-7 a.m. and it required 322 parking
stalls. The applicant was proposing at least 353 parking stalls.
Lilly went through the suggested conditions:
e The applicant shall restrict left turns out of the north project access or incorporate another
means of traffic mitigation subject to the approval of the City Engineer which is what the

1.6 Consideration of ZT-19-003, Adoption of the City Center Overlay Zone Planner:
Francis Lilly
Francis Lilly said the Commission first reviewed the City Center Overlay Zone (CCOZ) draft
ordinance in August and it was one part of the policies the City was going to pursue in
relation to the City Center. Millcreek first adopted the General Plan and the City Center
Master Plan. The components that will come after the ordinance include: Highland Drive
corridor improvements, the Mountair streetscape, Villa Vista Avenue development,
Millcreek Common design, parking district/parking structures, residential parking
restrictions, Millcreek Common construction, initial development around Millcreek Common
and along 3300 South and Highland Drive, implementation of a business improvement
district, potential code revisions, and pedestrian improvements. He said there had been
various aspects of community engagement involved on a whole range of efforts for a city
legacy that residents could be proud of. He went through the timeline of engagement for the
ordinance; first draft prepared in May 2019, Planning Commission work meeting on July 29,
2019, open houses conducted in the first week of August 2019, first community council
review in August 2019, Planning Commission public hearing on August 21, 2019,
neighborhood meeting in September 2019, second community council review in November
2019, and the Planning Commission meeting on November 20, 2019.
He said staff has crafted an ordinance that responds to the General Plan and City Center
Master Plan uses. He said the plans address building heights in stories but the ordinance
addresses building heights in feet and the key characteristics of them are a diverse mix of
land use and space for local community events and socialization. The Master Plan had four
guiding principles to guide future development municipal investments, public-private
partnerships, and land use decision. Those are: 1) The City Center should emphasize
walkability and minimize internal traffic and parking demand, 2) The City Center should
include municipal property owned by the City and be designated for public use, 3) The
design of Millcreek’s City Center should represent the City in ways that are distinctive from
other cities and unique to Millcreek; and 4) The City Center should emphasize culture, art,
ideas, and interaction between citizens. Lilly said his goal as a planner was to harness
development into something that the community can like. The City Center has 3 districts,
Neighborhood, Millcreek Center, and City Marketplace, and those districts shape the
building requirements in code. One of those building requirements is building height. The
Building Height Map in the proposed ordinance differs from the one in the City Center
Master Plan. Lilly showed the Commission the maps and explained the differences. The
ordinance map included a maximum height lower than what the Master Plan contemplates
east of Highland Drive, a lower height maximum along the open space, and a greater height
