       B. CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 2026-R-34 ADOPTING CERTIFIED TAX
          RATE FOR MIDVALE CITY
Mariah Hill said as part of the annual budget approval process, the City must either
propose a new property tax rate or adopt the Certified Tax Rate calculated by the Salt
Lake County Auditor. This rate is based on updated 2026 assessed property values in
Midvale and is designed to generate the same amount of revenue as the previous year,
with adjustments for new growth.

For 2026, Midvale City’s Certified Tax Rate for general operations is 0.000908. If the
City chooses to adopt this rate, it must do so by June 22.

City staff recommends that the City Council adopt the 2026 Certified Tax Rate, as
calculated by the County Auditor, for general operations.

In addition, staff is proposing a modest increase to the separate Midvale public safety
property tax, which will be considered independently. Because of this proposed
increase, the City will be required to complete the truth-in-taxation process, which
affects the budget adoption timeline. Rather than adopting the budget concurrently with
the Certified Tax Rate, the final budget will be adopted by September 1 and submitted
to the State Auditor within 30 days.

In the meantime, the City will adopt an interim budget, which will restrict the use of
revenue generated by the public safety property tax increase but will allow the City to
utilize existing funding and maintain operations until the final budget is approved. The
final budget, along with the proposed public safety property tax increase, is anticipated
to be adopted following the truth-in-taxation hearing scheduled for August 11, 2026.

FISCAL IMPACT:
The certified tax rate will generate $3,499,172, which includes $30,527 in new growth.

Mariah Hill said as part of the annual budget approval process, the City must either
propose a new property tax rate or adopt the Certified Tax Rate calculated by the Salt
Lake County Auditor. For 2026, City staff recommends that the City Council adopt the
Certified Tax Rate for general operations as provided by the Auditor (Resolution 2026-
R-34).

In addition, staff is proposing that the City Council increase the public safety property
tax that was levied in fiscal year 2026 specifically for public safety services, as
authorized under Utah State Code §10-6-133.4. The proposed Midvale City Public
Safety Property Tax rate is 0.000266, which is projected to generate approximately
$78,814 in additional property tax revenue. These funds will be allocated to the public
safety special revenue fund and used exclusively for public safety-related expenses.

The largest portion of the City’s public safety budget is dedicated to law enforcement
services provided by the Unified Police Department (UPD). For Fiscal Year 2027, the
cost of these services is projected at $14,571,337. With the proposed increase, the
public safety property tax will cover approximately 7% of the cost of UPD services. The
remaining public safety expenses will be funded through State Homeless Mitigation
Funding (approximately $3.2 million), motor vehicle in-lieu fees (approximately
$130,000), and a transfer from the City’s General Fund (approximately $10.7 million).

Based on the 2026 average residential home value in Midvale of $503,000—with a
taxable value of $276,650—the proposed public safety tax would increase the average
annual property tax bill by approximately $5.53.

Adoption of the proposed Midvale City Public Safety Property Tax rate will initiate the
truth-in-taxation process, which includes adoption of an interim budget and property tax
impact schedule, required public notices, and a public hearing prior to adoption of the
final tax rate and final budget. The public hearing is scheduled for August 11, 2026, at
6:00 p.m. at Midvale City Hall.

FISCAL IMPACT:
The proposed Midvale City Public Safety property tax will generate $78,814 in new
property tax revenue and will fund a portion of the City’s public safety service costs.

       F. CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 2026-R-38 ADOPTING INTERIM FY2027
           BUDGET
Mariah Hill said the process of adopting the Fiscal Year 2027 budget requires several
actions to ensure compliance with state statute. Pursuant to Utah Code §10-6-111(1),
the Budget Officer prepared and presented a tentative budget for each City fund on May
5, 2026, which was adopted by the City Council at that meeting.

The City Council is also considering a proposed property tax rate increase for the
Midvale City Public Safety Property Tax. Because the proposed rate exceeds the
certified tax rate, the City is required to comply with the Truth-in-Taxation provisions
outlined in Utah Code §§59-2-919 and 59-2-924. As part of this process, the City has
prepared a Property Tax Impact Schedule, included it in the interim budget, and made it
available to the public in accordance with state requirements.

State law further requires that, when a taxing entity proposes a property tax increase,

        G. CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 2026-R-39 APPROVING THE TAX IMPACT
           SCHEDULE
Mariah Hill said during the 2026 Utah Legislative Session, HB 236 – Truth-in-Taxation
Amendments was enacted, making several changes to Utah Code §§ 59-2-919 and 59-
2-924, which govern notice, disclosure, and public hearing requirements when a
tentative budget includes a proposed property tax rate increase, including municipal
property tax increases. These provisions are intended to promote transparency and
ensure the public is provided clear information regarding the potential impacts of a
proposed tax rate prior to final budget and tax rate adoption.

Accordingly, the City is required to prepare and present a Property Tax Impact
Schedule throughout the budget process that illustrates the estimated effect of the
proposed tax rate on typical residential and non-residential properties and present and
make it available to the public separate from other budget documents. The City is also
required to adopt the Property Tax Impact Schedule in conjunction with the interim
budget. This schedule is included in the interim budget and is also presented here as a
separate item for adoption by the City Council.

The Property Tax Impact Schedule provides a standardized summary of the anticipated
property tax impact associated with the proposed rate increase and is intended to
promote transparency and public understanding prior to final budget and tax rate
adoption. The schedule has been prepared in accordance with statutory requirements
and will be presented at all meetings where the FY2027 budget is discussed.

Adoption of the Property Tax Impact Schedule does not finalize the property tax rate.
Final consideration and adoption of the property tax rate will occur following the required
public hearing and completion of all applicable notice and review periods.

FISCAL IMPACT:
Adoption of the Property Tax Impact Schedule itself does not have a direct fiscal impact.
The schedule is a disclosure document that illustrates the estimated effect of a
proposed property tax rate increase. Any actual fiscal impact will be determined by the
final property tax rate adopted by the City Council as part of the final budget process.
