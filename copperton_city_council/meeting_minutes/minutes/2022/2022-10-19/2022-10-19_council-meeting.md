                                    Council Working Session

Copperton Code Rewrite

                Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, stated the most important part of the revisions
is Title 19. The goal is to make sure Title 19 is updated to conform to the General Plan and
character areas. It is a two-step process: first rewrite the code and then do the zoning changes.
There will not be many zoning changes for Copperton due to its size. The Greater Salt Lake
Municipal Services District (MSD) staff has taken the lead in drafting the template to change Title
19. They are now taking the draft to the various MSD entities to get their take on how to make
Title 19 fit what their city needs.

                Curtis Woodward, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, stated the
project was broken down into three phases. The first phase was all the zones – commercial and
residential. There are several proposed changes to simplify those chapters. There was
discussion on determining if there are areas to be annexed. The second phase on the project
focuses on design standards; things like parking and landscaping code. Those can be tailored to
the individual community’s needs. The third phase is mostly the administrative chapters.

               Mayor Clayton stated Council Member Stitzer worked on Copperton’s annexation
policy plan and wondered if Copperton will be incorporated into the rewrite.

               Mr. Woodward stated that Kayla Mauldin tailored that part of the plan into the
rewrite.

                Mayor Clayton asked if, in looking at the annexation plan, it would be better to
remove all the areas Copperton thinks it will not need with the possibility of adding them later, or
if it should keep those areas in the plan as a precaution. His overall concern is that other
municipalities vying for the same land might be timelier than Copperton. If there were areas that
did not want to be part of the County any longer, he would want to be prepared.

                Kayla Mauldin, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, stated when she
was packaging the first phase for Copperton, she took out the zones she did not think Mayor
Clayton would want and left in what she felt he would. The zones in phase one are what staff has
recommended. That could be a good starting point. She also mentioned the possibility of setting
the number of planning commissioners at five as that is a recommended best practice. Copperton
currently only has three.

             Council Member Stitzer stated Copperton has failed to fill those positions
numerous times and would not recommend changing the code.

               Nathan Bracken reopened the conversation from the last meeting about using an
administrative law judge (ALJ) as opposed to a land use hearing officer, there could be a pool
that Copperton uses.

              The Council agreed to have a land use hearing officer for variances and if the
decision needs to be appealed, it can go to an ALJ.

Wasatch Front Waste

               Council Member Stitzer introduced Pam Roberts with Wasatch Front Waste and
Recycling District (WFWRD) who reported.

               Pam Roberts, Division Director, WFWRD, reported that WFWRD is proposing
some fee increases due to increasing labor costs and purchase prices. She provided a handout
of the increases. WFWRD wants to prioritize continuing with weekly garbage pickup and seasonal
services. Its second priority is to manage increased costs while maintaining services. The
proposed increase will be about $2.15 per month per home. The annual increase is up from $204
to $234. The last increase was in 2018. WFWRD is dedicated to keeping costs as low as possible.
The budget will be adopted by the Board on Monday, October 24th. A hearing will follow.

                  Paul Korth, Controller, WFWRD, stated the handout does not include stand-alone
and special services. The total budget will be about $24 million in expenses. WFWRD needs to
increase wages to stay competitive. They are having difficulty finding staff. Fuel costs and
maintenance have increased. Disposal and tonnage have not increased as much. He ordered
eight trucks, but the vendor came back and wanted more money for the trucks and still were not
able to fulfill the order. Garbage and Recycling trucks have gone up in cost about $100,000.

            Council Member Stitzer thanked WFWRD for their diligence in keeping costs
down. She feels confident the projected increase will sustain over the next several years.

County Park Transfer

               Nathan Bracken stated the County is finally getting around to transfering the park
to Copperton through an interlocal agreement containing a quitclaim deed. The Salt Lake County
Council has already approved the interlocal agreement (ILA), so any changes would need to go
again before the Council. He has reviewed it as to form and while the form is fine, the ILA does

not contain a legal description. He would recommend waiting to sign until the legal description
can be included. His other point is that it is the County’s standard practice with these conveyances
to include a reversionary clause stating that if the park is ever turned into something different, the
land would be deferred back to the Council. While Copperton does not have plans to develop the
park, for the sake of full disclosure, he wants the Council to know the clause is there. There is a
possibility Copperton might need to develop the land into something not technically “open space”
in the future. His preference would be for the language to state the space is “for a park or open
space for a public purpose”. While reversionary clauses can be problematic, he is not sure this
particular clause is a big deal.

               Mayor Clayton stated he does not like that the clause is there because the land
was never the County’s so they should have no right to it. He agreed that if Copperton should
decide to develop the park, they would have to provide a suitable replacement park, but the land
should not divert to the County. Council Members Stitzer and Severson expressed agreement.

Committee/Board Updates

    Legislative Research Committee

              Nathan Bracken stated there are two bills in the works he wanted the Council to
be aware of. The first was introduced by Representative Bradley Brammer that would require city

does exempt work sessions and certain planning commission meetings. The other bill he felt was
