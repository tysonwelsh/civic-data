Welcome New Commissioner
Chair Sandau welcomed new commissioner, Andrey Akhmedov, who introduced himself.

Discussion of General Plan Update

Mr. Rypien introduced Christy Oostema Brown, of the consultant team with Logan Simpson.
He expressed the importance of the Planning Commission’s role in the general plan, Plan
Ogden. He explained how Zone Ogden and Plan Ogden will work together and reviewed the
phases and timeframes that the work will take place, with the phases being management
and outreach, plan foundation, visioning, draft plan development and plan approval. The
actual development of the General Plan will occur between December 2024 and December
2025. Mr. Rypien expressed that there will be several workshops, questionnaires, and
community outreach opportunities. Discussion continued among the commission regarding
different community outreach methods. Mr. Rypien explained that a general plan guides the
city's growth and Plan Ogden will be planned out to 2050. He indicated that it should address
transportation, sufficient water, and housing.

Commissioner Shinoda expressed concern regarding the software that will be used for the
surveys and to ensure that the data can be segmented. Ms. Oostema Brown responded that
the software to be used has the capability to sort out demographics. Mr. Rypien presented
an example of what the Plan Ogden website will look like, as well as its functionalities,

including tracking and maps showing where participation in surveys is throughout the city.
Commissioner Southwick asked how Plan Ogden incorporates with the existing general
plan. Mr. Rypien explained that the existing general plan will be reviewed to see what was
accomplished from it and each element of it will be updated into the new general plan.

Ms. Oostema Brown introduced the Plan Ogden brand and possible taglines. Discussion
continued about potential challenges that the commission envisions for the city, which
included car optional communities, housing, redevelopment, upkeep of infrastructure, and
more. They also discussed their visions of what Ogden will be known for in 20 years.
Statements included: good education; being the best City in the west to live, work, learn and
play; most economically diverse, vibrancy, and thriving arts and culture. Ms. Oostema Brown
reviewed the next steps and stated that the public engagement plan will be finalized in April.
Commissioner Blaisdell asked where the neighborhood plans would fit into Plan Ogden. Mr.
Rypien indicated that different options are being considered including the possibilities of
reducing the number of areas, having their own section within the plan, or redrawing areas.
Mr. Simpson added that many of the community plans are outdated while many others do
not have a plan established. Mr. Rypien also indicated that other plans, including corridor
plans, will need to be addressed and the general plan process provides an opportunity to
look at the entire city. Conversation continued regarding implementing a safety plan as well
as the financial benefits of the Plan Ogden and being able to utilize it when establishing
zones and uses throughout the city.

Commissioner Akhmedov asked if there are examples available of other cities going through
the same process. Mr. Rypien indicated that he went through a similar process with Layton
City and briefly explained that process and the outcomes of it. Ms. Oostema Brown added
examples of Salt Lake City. Mr. Simpson said that Logan Simpson provided a list of general
plans they had made and that he was very impressed with how easy it was to navigate them.

Ogden Bend Master Plan Amendment

Mr. Brierley presented that the plan area is between Wall Avenue and Washington Boulevard
and 18" and 20" Streets. Referring to the prior Planning Commission meeting, he reviewed
the three main questions the commission wished to discuss which were river access and
protection, ownership, and properties to the north. Mr. Brierley indicated that the plan would
include expanding the trail to the north side of the river, providing trails on both sides. It
would also include bridges and the addition of a route to be able to get back on the trail from
Wall Avenue. He reviewed the flood map, pointing out the floodplain and floodway, and
expressed the importance of the river path for pedestrians, bikes, and maintenance. Mr.
Brierley explained that there are currently no setbacks in the MU zone and in the floodway,
you can’t build if you're going to increase the flood water level in the floodway. Possible
options presented were to require a 10-foot-wide path on both sides of the river, specify that
there is no construction allowed in the floodway, or the addition of five feet to the floodway.
Commissioner Southwick expressed concern to make sure that recommendations get put
into code. Conversation continued among the commissioners about requirements for
providing access to the trail and other building restrictions.


Concerning ownership housing, Mr. Brierley referred to the proposed plan and stated that it
would be possible to make a requirement that the cottage areas be platted for individual
ownership. He reviewed the plan and discussion continued among the commission
regarding the proposal and possible other layouts, particularly green space. Mr. Burnham
explained that the RDA has invested a significant amount of money into the area and is
looking at land to incentivize creating a neighborhood. He reviewed the history of the area
and explained the vision and goals for it. Commissioner Southwick expressed concern about
more apartments in the area and discussion continued regarding cost efficiency of building
single family homes. Mr. Brierley explained that the Commission will be deciding on approval
of the master plan change and the code text amendment, that accompanies it, at their next
regular meeting. Commissioners expressed concern regarding open space, and the financial
cost required to develop the open space, to ensure it does not sit vacant. Mr. Brierley
addressed the properties to the north of the river that are outside of the mixed-use area and
presented options for those lots and the possibility of including those lots in the project
master plan.

Mr. Brierley then reviewed items for the upcoming April meeting of the Planning Commission.

There being no additional business before the Commission, the meeting was adjourned at
7:38pm.
