GPZ-7-2020
Holiday Oil
6443 West 3500 South
General plan change from low density residential to neighborhood commercial and zone
change from R-1-8
0.34 acre

Holiday Oil has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for a 0.34 acre parcel located
at 6443 West 3500 South. The property is currently zoned R-1-8 (single unit dwelling residential,
minimum lot size 8,000 SF) with a General Plan designation of low density residential (3 to 4
units/acre). The proposed zone is C-1 (neighborhood commercial) and the proposed General Plan
designation is neighborhood commercial.

Surrounding zones include R-1-8 to the west and south, A (agriculture, minimum lot size ½ acre)
to the north and C-1 to the east. Surrounding land uses include homes to the west and south, a
church to the north and a convenience store to the east. The subject property includes a home that
was built in 2002.

The applicant is proposing to demolish the existing home and neighboring convenience store and
car wash to make way for a larger convenience store, car wash and fuel canopy. If this General
Plan/zone change is approved, a conditional use permit will be required for the planned
redevelopment.

The City’s Major Street Plan shows the future right-of-way width for this portion of 3500 South
as 106’. The existing right-of-way width is 80’. Section 7-2-111 states:
       “Wherever a Front Yard is required for a Lot facing on a Street for which a Subdivision
       plat has been recorded in the office of the Salt Lake County Recorder, the depth of such
       Front Yard shall be measured from the mapped Street line provided by the Subdivision
       plat. Where a Subdivision plat has not been recorded and no widening is anticipated in
       the Major Street Plan, measurements shall be made from the existing Right-of-way line.
       Where widening is anticipated in the Major Street Plan, measurements shall be made
       from the proposed Right-of-way line.”

Staff recommends a development agreement be entered into on the subject parcel and on the
adjoining parcel to the east to require a minimum landscaped setback of 33’ as measured from
the existing right-of-way line along 3500 South. Staff also recommends that the agreement
require 13’ of right-of-way dedication.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. This application should be approved subject to a development agreement
      requiring a 33’ landscaped setback and 13’ of right-of-way dedication as described in this
      report.

   2. Continuance. This application should be continued for reasons determined during the
      public hearing.

   3. Denial. This application should be denied as the parcel should remain residential.

C-12-2020
Kevin Merrithew
2542 W. Robin Rd.
R-2-8 Zone (.24 acres)

The applicant, Kevin Merrithew, is the property owner at 2542 W. Robin Road and is requesting
conditional use approval for a two-unit dwelling. This property is zoned R-2-8 (two unit
dwelling residential, minimum lot size 8,000 sqft) and the West Valley City General Plan
designates this area as medium density residential. The surrounding zone is R-2-8 on all sides
and the surrounding uses are two family dwellings.

The home at 2542 W. Robin Road was originally constructed in 1966. Zoning records show that
in 1965 this area of the City was un-zoned. In 1970, this property and the surrounding area was
zoned R-2-8. The surrounding two family dwellings were then constructed in 1979.

Mr. Merrithew purchased this property in 2017 and at that time there were two separate
dwellings in the home. One is on the main floor and the other is in the basement. Mr. Merrithew
has indicated there is no internal connection between the two units but the utilities are mostly
shared between the two units. Some research was conducted after the original purchase to verify
if the two units were constructed legally and when they were constructed. According to Mr.
Merrithew the County Archive records indicated this was originally a single unit home. Mr.
Merrithew reached out to the Archives again recently but was unable to get copies of any of
those records at this time. Staff also reached out to Archives and was also unable to get any of
the records and was told that record requests are not able to be conducted in a timely manner due
to their limited Staff and operating hours at this time.

The property assessment was updated in 2018 as part of the property sale and Staff verified that
at that time the Mother-in-law designation was added to the assessment and prior to that it was a
single unit home.
Therefore, since this property is currently zoned R-2-8 and due to the lack of historical
information for the property and records indicating this was legally constructed as a duplex, Mr.
Merrithew is requesting this conditional use.

Since this structure is existing the request is a change of use and does not trigger all of the design
guidelines for new construction that are set forth in the West Valley City Code (7-11-200). But
this change of use does trigger needing to comply with the Building Code regulations, parking
requirements and general site maintenance.

The Chief Building Official, Jerry Thompson, has indicated that a building permit will be
required for this conversion. The home must have complete separation of the units. This would
include separate utilities i.e., furnace, water, electrical, etc.. Each of these utilities are required to
be contained and accessible from within the individual units. Additionally, there should be
separate entrances for each unit, which are currently in place. Finally, there may be additional
Fire-Separation requirements but that will be evaluated once a building permit is submitted and
reviewed.

For parking, 7-9-104 states that a two unit dwelling must have two parking spaces per unit
located outside of the front yard setback (25’). The home currently has a two car garage with no
additional space out of the front yard setback or possibility for access to the year yard for
additional parking. The applicant has proposed possibly removing the attached garage to add
uncovered parking spaces in the rear yard or possibly applying for a variance through the Board
of Adjustment to request additional parking spaces in the front setback.

During the study session of this item different parking requirements and alternatives were
discussed at length. If this were new construction, a two unit dwelling would be required to have
a 3 car garage and then one additional uncovered space out of the front setback (7-11-208).
Section 7-11-209, outlines the guidelines for conversion of parking spaces for existing homes. It
states that if a home was constructed prior to 1998 and they want to convert or remove an
existing garage then that parking needs to be replaced by two parking spaces outside of the front
setback but they do not need to be covered. It was also discussed that since the garage is existing
it would be a reasonable condition of approval to require that a minimum of two of the spaces at
this location remain covered. This could be the existing cover or new covered spaces relocated to
the back of the property as either a garage or carport.

Lastly, the site itself must be maintained. This includes the maintenance of all fencing and
landscaping areas maintained in live condition and free of weeds. Currently it appears that the
backyard has very large weeds and the fencing has not been maintained.

Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any concerns raised at the public hearing as well as the
following:

            1. The approved use for this application is for a two-unit dwelling.
           2. A building permit must be obtained for the change of use to a two-unit dwelling.

           3. There shall be a minimum of 4 parking spaces provided for this use:

                   a. that are located on a hard surface out of the front yard setback per 7-9-104.
                   b. Two of the spaces must be covered and two spaces may be located on hard
                      surface out of the front yard setback.
                           i. Covered is defined as an enclosed garage.
                          ii. Covered is defined as a carport.

           4. The site shall be maintained including but not limited to all fencing and
              landscaped areas.

           5. Must meet the requirements of all affected departments and agencies.

           6. Subject to review upon valid complaint.

Continuance, to allow for the resolutions of any issues raised at the public hearing.
