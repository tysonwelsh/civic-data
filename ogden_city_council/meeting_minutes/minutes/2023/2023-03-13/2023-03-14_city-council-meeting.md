Proposed Ordinance 2023-14 designating the single-family bungalow-style
home and detached garage, located at 136 West 2"4 Street, as an historic
resource

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed historic designation came
before the Council for consideration. The memo stated petitioner Terry Lanier, owner of the house at 136 West 2 Street, located
within the Bingham area, is nominating their house and detached garage to be listed on the Ogden City Register of Historic Places.
The designation would require any significant changes proposed for the building, including demolition, to be approved by the
Landmarks Commission through the approval of a Certificate of Historic Appropriateness. The designation would help ensure the
preservation of the property and would help to ensure that any changes proposed would maintain the character and historicity of
the home and property. The City’s Landmarks Commission has reviewed the proposed designation and has forwarded a
recommendation of approval finding that the building meets both the historical and architectural criteria listed in the City’s code
for local designation.

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
petition; the Fort Bingham area served in the 1850’s as a gathering of nearly 600 settlers and was the largest fort in the area. There
are a few original buildings that remain on the property, including the home and garage at 136 West 2™ Street. He presented
photos of the home, which was built in 1912, and is of the classic bungalow style with no modifications to the house except for
three upper story vinyl windows. The garage faces the Old Pioneer Road; the roof and floor are in disrepair, but the structure is
sound. Doors are original and the brick is the same as the main building. He discussed standards applicable to this type of
application and noted that in this case, the Council can rely upon a finding that the property is associated, in a significant way,
with an important aspect of the cultural, political, or economic heritage of the community. He then provided additional
information regarding the historic significance of the Fort Bingham area and the past occupants of the home. He also discussed the
architectural significance of the home, which embodies the distinctive visible characteristics of an architectural style, period, or a
method of construction. The rear porch was part of the original structure of the house and is made of frame, which was typical of
the time. It has neighborhood significance as an easily identifiable visual feature that contributes to the distinctive quality or |
identity of the neighborhood. Mr. Brierley concluded his presentation by reiterating the Landmarks Commission has found that the
home and detached garage have important historical and architectural significance which should be preserved by adding the home
to the local historic register.

/ Proposed Ordinance 2023-13 amending the Ogden municipal code to
remove newspaper noticing requirements aligning with state notification
laws
A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed amendments to noticing
requirements came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated based on recent state law changes, cities are no longer
required to provide public notice using the newspaper for land use applications. Instead, state law generally requires posting
notices on the City’s website, on the Utah Public Notice website, posting in at least three public places, and, if applicable, mailing
to affected entities as required by both state and City Code. As such, the proposed amendments remove the newspaper noticing
requirements for street name changes, subdivision code section changes, street and alley vacations, zoning ordinance changes and ,
rezones, general plan amendments, and designations to the local historic register.
Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and summarized Senate Bill (SB) 201, which eliminated the
requirements to post notices in newspapers, instituted the requirements to post notices on the Utah Public Notice Website and the
City’s website. He presented a chart illustrating the types of applications for which a newspaper notice is no longer required and
concluded the Planning Commission has recommended that the Council adopt the proposed ordinance amending the municipal
code replacing the requirement for posting notices in newspapers.
Chair Choberka asked if the City will post a notice in the newspaper advertising this ordinance amendment and notifying
the public that certain notices will no longer be published in the newspaper, to which Mr. Brierley answered yes.
Council member White asked if the City could still elect to post notices in the newspaper. Mr. Brierley answered yes;
while notices are no longer required, there is no prohibition against posting a notice in the newspaper. Council member White
inquired as to the amount of money the City will be saving due to this change. Mr. Brierley stated the City pays approximately
$300 per notice, so the savings will be in the thousands of dollars each year. Council member White asked if the City is prohibited
from posting a notice on the website and in the newspaper, to which Mr. Brierley answered no.
Council member Hyer inquired as to the reasoning behind the law changes and if there are any plans for ensuring that
residents without access to the internet are able to view public notices. Mr. Brierley stated that a physical notice will be posted on
the bulletin board at the Municipal Building. Council member White stated she appreciates the efforts to reduce costs but is
concerned about reduced communication with the public. Council member Hyer agreed and asked ifthe Standard-Examiner has
) communicated their current circulation rate in Ogden City. Mr. Brierley stated he has not solicited that information. Council
--~ member Hyer stated that without that information, it is not possible to know how many fewer people will have access to public
notices when they are no longer published in the newspaper. Chair Choberka stated the Council has received communication from
the editor of the Standard-Examiner about their fear of a certain demographic of the community no longer receiving pertinent
information because of a fear of using the internet. The newspaper is also concerned that other municipalities may follow Ogden

City to spend a few thousand dollars to keep public communication intact. \

There are still a number of residents who do get information from the newspaper and it would be a shame to discontinue the
