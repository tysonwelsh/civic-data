BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of Deviations from building façade standards for
a new self-storage facility to be built on approximately 3.88 acres. This property is located
at approximately 15037 S. Minuteman Drive (Exhibit B & C). The property is currently zoned
M1. The site plan was conditionally approved by the Planning Commission on September
14, 2023. Please reference the approval letter in (Exhibit H).

The applicant is requesting approval of Deviations for significant horizontal articulation,
number of primary materials, and height and roofline variations for buildings B, C, D, E, F,
G, H, I, J, and K.

Architectural Modifications: Per DCMC Section 9-22-030 an applicant may request a deviation
from the design standards of section 9-22-040.

The applicant has addressed their deviation request in a letter, found in Exhibit I. Their
letter specifically mentions the specific applicable criteria from 9-22-030(B) which they
believe that they meet as #6, #9, and #10. These are as follows:

        6. The variations are consistent with the purpose of this chapter and any applicable
        master plan or ordinance.

        9. The building design is a specific architectural style, such as contemporary or
        modern, which utilizes façade materials differently then prescribed in subsection 9-
        22-040F of this chapter and is designed by a licensed architect.

        10. The amount of primary materials used for a façade is at least ten percent (10%)
        greater than the minimum required amount.



Staff has reviewed their responses. The purpose statement contained in DCMC Section 9-
22-010 states that the purpose of the chapter, in part, is to facilitate exceptional design in
the built environment and preserve the quality of life within Draper City. The applicant’s
response is that they paid a lot of attention to the intent of the ordinance and design
guidelines and that the project will be visually interesting with coordinated variety
incorporated in its design. With respects to criterion #9 staff agrees that the buildings are
designed by a licensed architect, however the request letter did not provide insight into
what specific architectural style they were designed in. Staff does concur that criterion #10
is met with respect to the proposed plans. The Planning Commission would need to
determine if all three of the criteria are indeed met. The applicant further states the
deviations should be granted because the horizontal articulation and height and roofline
variations are on interior facing walls and not visible to the public. The applicant’s request
also includes additional considerations for granting the deviations, these are ancillary to
the above listed criteria and may also be considered if the minimum three (3) criteria as
listed above have first been determined to have been met. For the request to deviate from
providing a significant horizontal articulation the applicant notes that all overhead doors
used are recessed eight inches (8”) into all buildings, creating a “bay aesthetic” or “paneling”
effect. In the applicant’s response to criterion 9 the applicant states their belief that the use
of different colors of masonry will help to break up the walls of the building and create
visual interest and diversity in the project.

The applicant has requested that all exterior walls of the buildings B, C, D, E, G, H, I, J, and K
be allowed to have facades with only one (1) primary material. The applicant indicates that
the minimum requirement for three (3) colors per elevation will be complied with. The
applicant notes that the interior walls are only visible from the interior of the property.

These interior walls would have the amount of masonry coverage necessary to support the
structure of the building.

The applicant has also included a deviation request for height and roofline variations.
Standards in DCMC subsection 9.22.040(D) require that all buildings should include varied
rooflines to add architectural interest and avoid the appearance of monotonous roofline
expanses. The requirements include providing roofline and parapet variations where there
are long continuous and undisturbed rooflines fifty feet (50’) or longer. This can be done by
creating roofline and parapet variations and using colors and materials for false fronts,
parapets, cornices etc. that extend beyond the roofline to appear cohesive from all views.
The plans reviewed and approved by the Planning Commission were in compliance with
this subsection. The applicant is requesting a deviation to allow for the walls that are
interior to the facility to be exempt from these requirements. The applicant again states
that the noted walls will not be visible to the public. If this deviation is approved staff would
not require that these standards be met on the revised plans the applicant is required to
submit for compliance with the specific conditions of the previous site plan approval by the
planning commission.

Staff notes that building F was not included in the applicants request letter for deviations
from roofline and parapet variation or number of primary materials. Staff has
communicated with the applicant about this apparent oversight and with their concurrence
has included it in the suggested motions for the roofline and parapet variation deviation
and number of primary materials deviation.

RE:       Box-N-Lock Storage Request for Deviation – 15101 Minuteman Drive

We would like to submit a request for deviation from the Draper City ordinance section 9-22-
040 C (Façade Articulation), D (Height and Roofline), and F (Materials) for the exterior walls of
buildings B, C, D, E, G, H, I, J, and K that face the interior of the storage facility.

  C. Horizontal Articulation
          1)       The walls face the interior of the facility and will not be visible to the public
                   outside the property. It should be noted that because the overhead doors
                   are all recessed 8” into all buildings, these walls have a regular “bay
                   aesthetic” which creates a “paneling” effect. These architectural features
                   are identified as ways to create façade articulation in the ordinance.
          2)       We would like to seek a request for deviation to the 5’ x 20’ recess on these
                   same walls since they are not visible to the public.

  D. Height and Roofline
          1)      Since the same walls noted above will not be visible to the public, we would
                  like to seek a request for deviation to the roofline and parapet variation
                  requirements of those walls.

  F. Materials
          1)       We would like to request that all exterior walls of these buildings be allowed
                   to have (1) primary material. We will maintain the (3) colors per elevation
                   as required by F.1. The (3) walls of each building that are exterior to the
                   development will be 100% masonry with a standing seam parapet cap. The
                   interior wall of these buildings that is only visible from the interior of the
                   development would only have the amount of masonry coverage necessary
                   to support the structure of the building.

 The proposed requests for deviation are submitted based on the project meeting the
 following deviation criteria:

          1)       Criteria number 6. The variations are consistent with the purpose of this
                   chapter and all applicable ordinances. Complimentary colors and materials
                  have been applied to all the buildings on the site. There has been a great
                  deal of attention paid to ensure that this project fits the intent of the
                  ordinance and design guidelines that the city has put forth. The project will
                  be visually interesting and has a coordinated variety incorporated in its
                  design.

             2)   Criteria number 9. The building design is in a specific architectural style that
                  utilizes façade materials differently than prescribed in subsection 9-22-
                  040(F). The building design utilizes different colors of masonry to break up
                  the walls of the building. Instead of simply creating a redundant masonry
                  pier system, the colors band across the façade in irregular lengths and
                  spacings to create visual interest and diversity to the project.

             3)   Criteria number 10. The amount of primary materials used for a façade is
                  required to be at least ten percent (10%) greater than the minimum
                  amount. The primary material requirement of 75% of the building façade,
                  not including doors, glass, and roofing materials, is specified in 9-22-040
                  F.3.a. The (3) outward facing walls of each of the single-story buildings are
                  100% masonry and therefore meet the criteria of this deviation.
