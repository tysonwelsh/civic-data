5           Map Amendment Application for 2.05 Acres from I-1 Light Industrial Zone to Heavy
6           Commercial Zone (HC) located at Approximately 955 West 14730 South, Sean Shah,
7           Applicant. (Application 2020-53). Staff Presenter, Jennifer Robison.
8
9    Senior City Planner, Jennifer Robison, presented the staff report and identified the location of the
10   subject property. It is in an older part of the community, has been considered industrial in nature, and
11   has not undergone substantial development. The applicant was seeking to have the subject property
12   rezoned from (I-1) Light Industrial to (HC) Heavy Commercial. Adjacent parcels are currently zoned
13   HC. The Land Use General Plan shows that a wide variety of businesses are envisioned for the area.
14   The Zoning Map shows a differentiation of the uses from the Land Use General Plan, including Heavy
15   Commercial and Light Industrial. The subject property, which is currently zoned I-1, includes some
16   I-1 property to the west. Farther west is land that is zoned Mixed Use. There is a substantial amount
17   of property zoned HC to the north and east. To the south is a new SD-R zone that was created for
18   Independence Village.      The topography creates a buffer between the residential and light
19   industrial/heavy commercial properties.
20
21   The applicants were seeking to have the subject property rezoned to be consistent with the
22   surrounding Heavy Commercial property. They anticipate potential development of flex office space,
23   incubator spaces, warehousing, and employment opportunities for businesses in the area. The
24   difference between the I-1 and HC zones is not substantial; however, there are enough differences to
25   warrant the applicants’ request to have the property rezoned to HC. The recently approved change in
26   setback requirements for HC zones has been a factor in the request to have the subject property
27   rezoned.
28
29   The front setbacks were changed from 30 feet to 15 feet. The side setbacks and the landscaping
30   requirements remained the same. The proposed zone change meets the General Plan because it allows
31   for a continuation of the development pattern that has been established with the adjacent HC property.
32   The proposed rezone would not be harmful to the businesses that currently exist near the subject
33   property because the uses are similar and compatible with each other. Mrs. Robison indicated that

18
19   Mr. Sheeran reported that staff had been working on text amendments to the Home Occupation
20   Ordinance. It had been a difficult ordinance to draft because there were many issues with the current
21   Code. The City does not want to tell people what they can and cannot do, but they also want to protect
22   the neighbors from undue encroachment from the impacts of home occupations. The proposal makes
23   a distinction between Minor and Major Home Occupations. One of the weaknesses of the existing
24   Code is the lack of clarity of the definitions for the terms used.
25
26   The difference between a Minor Home Occupation and Major Home Occupation is the impact of the
27   business. A Minor Home Occupation has virtually no visible impact. Another challenge is to
28   determine the threshold at which a Major Home Occupation becomes Commercial.
29
30   The proposed text amendments establish clear lines so that it is very clear which category of home
31   occupation the applicant falls into. A Minor Home Occupation would allow six trips per day. Parking
32   will also have to occur on the property and not on the street with some exceptions. A Minor Home
33   Occupation does not allow for employees while a Major Home Occupation allows up to one

SUMMARY
The City Council approved the amendment to the City’s General Plan on April 22, 2020 for Howland
Partners with the condition that a subsequent application to rezone the property would be submitted. The
Council requested the applicant provide quality development standards and plans to enhance this area of
the community for the evaluation for the future zoning application.

The applicant worked with Staff and provided the proposed zoning structure and development standards
to enact the R-SL Residential Senior Independent Living Zone (R-SL). In evaluating the creation of a Senior
Living Zone, Staff reviewed many examples of all types of senior residential options. Within a 55+ or senior
living zone, the definition can be very broad and provide for various residential options such as
independent living, assisted living, nursing facilities, continuing care retirement communities,
alzheimer’s/memory care/dementia facilities, or others. Staff determined to focus on the independent
living options only at this time due to the potential complexity for all 55+ senior living situations. Creating a
specific R-SL zone will provide the tools to respond the applicant’s request, but also provide a for the
possibility of other future developments for senior independent living for all areas in the community. In the
future, Staff can address the other types of senior living options, if desired.

11.80.060.010: PURPOSE AND ZONE CHARACTERISTICS:

The R-SL Senior Independent Living Zone is established to provide areas within the City for
housing for residents aged 55 years and older who prefer to reside in independently owned
dwelling units within a larger community of common area, open space and amenities. Rezoning
to the R-SL Zone should be carefully reviewed to ensure compatibility with existing
development and adjacent neighborhoods.

11.80.060.020: PERMITTED AND ACCESSORY USES:

   A. Permitted uses allowed within the R-SL Zone are provided as follows:
         a. Multi-family residential dwellings providing two (2) or more attached units.
         b. Minor home occupations in compliance with BCC xxxx with no employees.

11.80.060.030: MINIMUM PROJECT AREA AND MAXIMUM DENSITY:

Each development project in the R-SL Senior Independent Living Zone shall be located on a
parcel containing not less than four (4) acres.

The density for any senior living project shall be a up to a maximum of 6.50 units per acre for any
R-SL zone.

11.80.060.040: OCCUPANCY QUALIFICATIONS AND RESTRICTIONS:

A. Dwelling units are restricted to a maximum unit size of two (2) bedrooms on the main floor. A
   bonus room or loft is allowed with a second-floor option over a garage.
B. The dwelling units are intended for, and to be owned and occupied by, at least one (1)
   person fifty-five (55) years of age or older per unit.
                                                                                               1
           a. A dwelling unit may be occupied by the surviving member(s) of a household,
              regardless of age, if the qualifying person has passed away, provided the surviving
              member(s) was a resident of the unit at the time of the qualifying person’s death.
           b. A unit may be occupied by the management, medical, or custodial personnel of a
              qualifying resident.
           c. A homeowners’ association (HOA) shall be established for a development to
              ensure compliance with resident restrictions and provide maintenance of

11.80.060.050: PROJECT FRONTAGE:

Senior living projects shall promote an active street frontage on all public streets. The creation
of a second frontage road or courtyard area on the street frontage, in which units may be
oriented toward, can also be an acceptable option for projects located on collector roads.

11.80.060.060: SETBACK AND DRIVEWAY REQUIREMENTS:

The following minimum setback requirements shall apply in the R-SL Independent Living Zone.
Each setback is measured from the property line of the lot or parcel.

A. Front Setback; Unit and Driveway Access: Each building or structure in the R-SL Zone
   adjacent to a public or private street shall have a minimum front setback of fifteen feet (15’)
   measured from the right-of-way, otherwise the front setback shall be a minimum of ten feet
   (10’).

B. Driveway areas shall be a minimum of twenty feet (20’) in length, exclusive of any sidewalk or
   walkways. If units are accessed by an alley or auto court, the width of the alley shall be a
   minimum of twenty-five feet (25’) and shall comply with all fire codes.

C. Side Setback; Corner Lot:

   1. Each building or structure in the R-SL Zone shall have a minimum side setback of ten feet
   (10').

   2. Each corner lot or parcel in the R-SL Zone shall have a minimum setback of twenty feet
   (20') along the street on the side of the lot not being used as the front setback.

D. Rear Setback:

   1. Each building or structure in the R-SL Zone adjacent to a public or private street shall
      have a rear setback of fifteen feet (15’) measured from the right-of-way, otherwise the
      rear setback shall be a minimum of ten feet (10’).
