IV)    PUBLIC HEARING

1. Park City Arts and Culture District – Public Hearing:
Mayor Beerman stated many in the community thought this discussion had been dragging on
and others thought the discussion had been rushed. He noted this was a large-scale project and
had the potential to enrich the course of the City. He indicated the district was being designed
for the benefit of the residents.

David Everitt, Deputy City Manager, reviewed the history leading up to the proposed Arts and
Culture District. Bonanza Park was identified as a location for locals to live and work. In 2017,
Kimball Art Center and Sundance signed letters of intent to buy land and construct buildings.
The City enacted a one percent Transient Room Tax (TRT) for the district, purchased the
Bonanza Park properties, published a feasibility study, and selected an architect. The master
design process proceeded throughout 2018 and 2019. In 2020, the expense estimates were
revised and a finance plan was analyzed.

Stephen Swisher indicated he was brought on the project to help with pre-construction services.
He displayed the project design, which included housing, a food building, a transit area, artist
spaces, and the anchor sites for Kimball Art Center and Sundance. There were also commercial
spaces, underground parking, a plaza, and pedestrian walkways.

Everitt stated the guiding principles to pay for this project included no tax increase, no impact to
existing capital projects, adherence to Council's debt policies, a funding path for future capital
projects, and continued financial strength of the City. He indicated the project would be paid with
the Transient Room Tax and Sales Tax. He broke down the cost by funding source and
indicated the total project cost would be $107.9 million.

Everitt reviewed the indicators that would signify the success of the Arts and Culture District
project: the area would be for the community and not for private development; it would be for
residents; it would be vibrant; it would be a gathering place; it would promote equity and access;
it would strengthen relationships with non-profit arts organizations; it would create affordable
housing; it would enhance walkability; it would be an economic support for the creative
community; and it would have a realistic budget.

Mayor Beerman opened the public hearing. The following gave comments via Zoom:

Kelly Franklin was a local artist and stated she supported the Arts and Culture District. She
thought this district had smaller, casual spaces where artists could collaborate. She liked the
project design and thought the district would be a boon for the City for decades to come.

Hilary Ryder stated the design was great. She thought the affordable housing aspect was
essential to the project, but she was concerned about the cost per unit and thought it was
disproportionately high. She also thought affordable housing should be integrated with market
rate housing and was concerned that this development was not integrated. Mayor Beerman
indicated there would be people with a variety of Area Median Incomes (AMI) living there.

Bill Tidwell, Park Meadows, thanked Council and staff for the time and effort on this project. He
didn't think it was the best use of funds, but it wasn't the worst either. He didn't think the City
should spend taxpayer money to build the district. The City should not own the Arts and Culture
District and should instead sell it to a developer. There would be an opportunity cost, but it could
be a win-win. He thought the expense was too high for the taxpayers. Everitt clarified a GO
bond would not be acquired, but the City would bond for the project and it would be paid with tax
revenues.

Indiscernible Name stated people wanted art from local artists. Currently, there wasn't enough
representation from artists in this town, so she felt it was important to have an Arts and Culture
District. This town had everything and now it needed art and culture.

Tana Toly stated Main Street and its history needed to be preserved. The concern from the
Historic Park City Alliance (HPCA) was that Main Street would be left out when all the City's
money was spent on the Arts and Culture District.

Jake MacIntyre indicated he was not a Park City resident. He was a consultant working with the
Arts Council. He supported the Arts and Culture District and stated the project aligned with the
priorities from the Arts Council’s Project ABC. He knew there were concerns, but an investment
in one thing didn't mean a de-prioritization in something else. He urged Council to keep arts and
culture as a center focus.

Debbie Sebek, Arts Council and business owner, stated art connects us all and builds
community. She supported the district and thought it would be a huge asset to the community.

Emily Loughlin thanked Council and staff for the work on this project. She thought the space
would be so important for the community, school, and residents. She hoped residents would
continue to prioritize creativity.

Craig Dennis represented Prospector Square Property Owners Association and stated the
group supported the project, but had a few concerns. They wanted to make sure there was
connectivity between the district and the Prospector area. He thought with the connectivity, the
district would be well over five acres. He wondered about the impacts on the parking stalls
across the street from the district.

Nan Gray, Park City Professional Artists Association, indicated the district represented doing

Dean Barrett, Prospector Square Property Owners Association, stated he lived in the City for 45

included transportation, housing, and other priorities, on five acres of land. The anchor
occupants were uncertain elements, and they should have been the most certain part of the

Mary Christa Smith supported the Arts and Culture District, the funding plan, and the City
developing the project. She didn't support selling portions of the project to the free market. She
supported 100% affordable housing for this project. This was a public project and it should be
funded with public money for the benefit of the locals. The Arts and Culture District needed to be
called out as an Arts and Culture District. The community supports the arts. Tough decisions
require courage and she encouraged Council to be bold in developing the district.

Norm Schwartz favored having an Arts and Culture District, but it needed to be redesigned and
re-budgeted, so the budget was lower. The project was designed without any eye for budget. It
needed to be value engineered. The affordable housing component was financially ridiculous,
coming to $940 per square foot. He asked what part of the one percent tax was collected and in
the bank. He asked what was planned to take the burden off the taxpayers.

Tim Fellow, Arts Council Board Member, supported the Arts and Culture District. He was in
favor of the walkability portion of the project to Old Town, Iron Horse and Prospector Square
and noted it would expand the arts area along this corridor.

Ed Parigian supported arts and culture and having Sundance and the Kimball Art Center on that
site. He thought everything could be accomplished – the ability to reach 150 units of affordable
housing as well as achieving an arts village.

Anna Moore supported the Arts and Culture District. She was an artist and had lived here for
seven years. She was grateful to the Arts Council for prioritizing art. She hoped she could
continue to live here, but she only earned $32,000 per year. She looked forward to affordable
housing.

Rob Sletten agreed with many of the comments. He was a lodging operator for years and was
pleased they could contribute one percent TRT for projects. He felt funds should come from
other entities as well and suggested work should be done with the State so a real estate transfer
fee could be assessed. He was excited for the project.

Karen Kendall, Park City Professional Artists Association, supported the Arts and Culture
District. She hoped the City would stay true to the vision. She didn't hear a lot of talk about the
art, including the cost to rent the artist spaces. She hoped it would be affordable for artists.

Dave Rockwood lived in Park City for 42 years. He thanked all involved in planning the Arts and
Culture District. He thought there would be great impacts having the Kimball Art Center and
Sundance on the site, and he thought it was fitting for the City to provide the environment for

Everitt answered some questions from the hearing chat window. He noted restrooms would be
planned in the district. The number of food vendors hadn't been decided, but the City would set
a policy for vendors. There would be onsite management and storage would be part of the
design. Regarding the housing cost per unit, many factors went into the cost and there were
many opportunities to value engineer that aspect. The TRT revenue in the bank that was
currently available for the project and what was anticipated going forward was $5.6 million.
Corporate naming was not considered for financing the project to date. He indicated having
housing onsite added vitality to the district. The housing was not designated for artists and
Council would have to make that decision. There was designated short-term artist housing. He
stated the City signed letters of intent with the Kimball Art Center and Sundance and it was
presumed they would acquire their portions of the land. If the project was approved, those sites
would be nailed down quickly.

Mayor Beerman stated there was no plan to move forward without a commitment with the City's
partners.

Mayor Beerman closed public hearing.

Council Member Joyce stated he was the outlier with financing concerns. He thought the project
would eliminate the City's flexibility with capital projects. He thanked the Budget Department for
putting a financial plan together that would be discussed at tomorrow's meeting. Council
Member Worel thanked all for taking the time to comment and send emails. Council Member
Doilney indicated Council was not making a firm decision on this now because they were
thinking it through. The Council and staff tried to make this project and process as transparent
as possible. He encouraged public participation. Council Member Henney stated he responded
to residents' emails and he stated their voices were heard. There was concern that people
couldn't express their true feelings and he stated that was false. Many Council members actively
sought public input and promoted engagement. Council Member Gerber also thanked those that
attended and commented. She emphasized the City would not bond against property taxes, but
there could be a bond against TRT revenue.

V)     ADJOURNMENT

With no further business, the meeting was adjourned.

SHARED DISTRICT GOALS
THE CULTURAL AND ARTS DISTRICT SHOULD:

1.   Accentuate views creating a sense of connection to the natural beauty of the Wasatch Mountains surrounding the site.

2.   Invite local residents, visitors and a diverse audience of all ages into a Community Living Room.

3.   Showcase the presence of art and culture by providing a dynamic, vibrant pedestrian experience that is inviting, unique and culturally relevant.

4.   Provide a public gathering space equipped to hold outdoor events and screenings to serve the entire district.

5.   Provide a gracious experience whether entering by foot, car, bus or bike.

6.   Provide a lively daytime and nighttime experience leveraging the arts year round.

7.   Connect to other districts: Rail Trail, Iron Horse, Main Street, Poison Creek, Prospector and the Sundance Theatre District (Ray Theater).

8.   Anticipate complimentary buildings that work well together and communicate with each other seamlessly.

9.   Allow parking to accommodate public programs and gatherings when not needed for cars.

10. Create new arts and cultural participation and opportunities for Park City residents.

11. Be sustainable with an appropriate mix of earned and contributed income.

12. Showcase and incorporate the City’s energy, transportation and housing goals.

13. Enhance the local and regional identity of Park City as an arts community.

14. Be part of a “complete community” where residents want to live, work and play.

         What does success look like?
     Success began with the purchase of this land to redirect it for
                             community uses
                                   and
    Continues with this public process to refine and evaluate options.

•   Success will be:
     –   A vibrant hub dedicated to community needs.
     –   A place where locals gather, play, create, and find inspiration.
     –   A place with few barriers of entry, promoting equity and access.
