General, CIP, Airport, Parking and Mobility, BDO Operations, Storm Sewer, Medical Services, Fleet and Facilities, and
Miscellaneous Grant Funds. Sources of revenue vary from use of fund balance, use of contingency funds, transfers from other

proposed following appropriations:
               • General Fund - part-time wages, justice court equipment, Union Station projects, transfers to Airport and Fleet
                      and Facilities, and sustainability.
               • CIP Fund - general facilities and 20th Street improvements.
               • Airport Fund - training and terminal upgrades and modifications.
               • Parking and Mobility – contingency for parking plan.
               • BDO Operations – transfer to the CIP and Airport Funds.
               • Storm Sewer – system improvements.
               • Medical Services – equipment purchases (vehicle).
               • Fleet and Facilities – new and replacement vehicles.
               • Misc. Grants – part time wages and grant expenses.

                      “An ordinance of Ogden City amending the budget for the Fiscal Year July 1, 2024 to June 30, 2025 by
                      increasing the anticipated revenues for a gross increase of $2,982,925 from sources as detailed in the body of

Proposed Ordinance 2024-33 rezoning property at 340 Washington
Boulevard (formerly Fire Station #3) from R-1-6 (Single-family Residential),
to C-3 (Regional Commercial)
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed rezone of 340
Washington Boulevard came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Planning Division has proposed to
rezone approximately 7,000 square feet from R-1-6 (Single-family residential) to C-3 (Regional Commercial). The property is the
old Fire Station 3 and is currently owned by the City. The property (0.43 acres) is split by zoning, with the fire station building
being in the C-3 zone and the station parking lot located in the R-1-6 zone. If the proposed rezone is approved the entire property
will be in the C-3 zone.
          Planning Director Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
implications of the proposed rezone. The owner of the car dealership to the south is interested in purchasing the fire station
property for additional vehicle storage. A car dealership is a permitted use in the C-3 zone. The Planning Division feels the
proposal complies with the goals and policies of the General Plan and the Hillcrest/Bonneville Community Plan by making the
zone boundary follow the property lines and by continuing to support the major commercial node near the subject property. A 12-
foot wide cross-access easement is used to access the parking lot from Ogden Avenue. The easement runs between two single
family dwellings and will be eliminated if the property is sold to another party, as it was only meant to be used for fire protection
and emergency services. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Council member Myers referenced the strip of the property that extends to Ogden Avenue and inquired as to the
proposed use of that property in the future. Mr. Brierley stated the easement is owned by the adjoining property owner; he
identified the property on an aerial image of the area and indicated that the strip is used as a shared driveway for the adjoining
owner and the fire station. The shared use will no longer be needed, and the private property owner will have sole authority to use
the property. This led to discussion among the Council and Mr. Brierley regarding how it will be possible for a future owner of the
subject property to use it for a commercial purpose without sufficient access. Mr. Brierley stated that it may be necessary for a
future owner to demolish the existing fire station to create a space for an additional access.
          Council member Hyer asked if staff has any indication that there may be a buyer who will purchase and reuse the
building on the site. Mr. Brierley stated that the property owner to the south has expressed some interest in buying the property,
but there has not been detailed discussion about the reuse of the building or access issues. Council member Hyer stated he is trying
to envision reasonable uses for the property in the future.
           Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-33, entitled:
                   “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the zoning map of Ogden City, as adopted by Section 15-3-3 of
                   the Ogden Municipal Code, to reclassify as Regional Commercial Zone (C-3) property heretofore classified as
                   Single-Family Residential Zone (R-1-6), generally located at 340 Washington Boulevard, and providing that
                   this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final passage.”
          Chair Richey then opened the public hearing.
          Travis Pate discussed the history of the property, namely the acquisition of portions of several other properties that were
formerly used as rear yards for residential properties, in order to increase the size of the City property so that it was suitable for the
fire station. To now change the zoning of the property to commercial with zero restrictions relating to a buffer between
commercial and residential properties is problematic. The City’s General Plan references the preservation of view corridors and
vistas and if he were the owner of one of those adjacent residential properties, he would be very concerned about the vacation of
the abutters’ alleys. He suggested a restriction that would prevent a commercial wall from being built on the property line between
the commercial and residential properties; this could be accomplished by a greater easement than what is required when
commercial properties abut one another. The City must protect private property owners rather than approving blanket rezones that
create a quagmire for them.
          Heath Satow stated she shares some of the Mr. Pate’s concerns; he asked if it makes sense to rezone the property now
before having some indication as to what the site may be used for in the future. He suggested the City market the land for sale and
consider potential uses before approving the zone change in a manner that will allow a commercial use to abut residential uses.
          Linda Sanchez asked if anyone has reached out to the residential property owners to learn if they have any concerns
about this land use adjustment.
          No further persons came forward to be heard.
          Chair Richey invited Mr. Brierley to address the public input regarding this matter.
          Mr. Brierley indicated that the City has met with the property owner who owns the easement between the residential
property and the fire station property; the Planning Commission continued their scheduled public hearing to allow that individual
more time to comment on the matter and their main concern related to the future of the easement and whether the easement will be
preserved. The easement is a written document between the property owner and the City, and the easement will be dissolved
within six months of the zone change. He then noted there are setback requirements for instances when commercial properties
abut residential properties; the uses must be 30 feet apart from one another and fence heights are restricted to six or seven feet,
which will prevent a large commercial wall from being built on the property line.
          Council member Choberka asked how Administration would feel about following Mr. Satow’s recommendation to
market the land for sale before acting on the zone change. Mr. Brierley stated that would be an option, but until the property is
zoned for commercial, no one will propose any type of commercial development on the site. Council member Choberka stated that
the listing marketing the land for sale could indicate the City’s intent to change the zoning to commercial.
          Council member Hyer stated that one issue that must be dealt with is the split zoning of the property, and he feels it is
appropriate to take action to ensure that the entire parcel has the same zoning designation. In this case, it seems practical to him to
change the entire parcel to commercial in nature.
         Vice Chair White asked if the abutting residents were interested in purchasing the sliver of property. Mr. Brierley stated
that was not discussed with the residents.
         ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER HYER,
ORDINANCE 2024-33 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL
MEMBERS BLAIR, GRAF, HYER, MYERS, VICE CHAIR WHITE, AND CHAIR RICHEY. VOTING NO –
COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA.
         Council member Choberka stated that she voted in opposition to the adoption of the ordinance based upon some of the
input provided by residents; she also believes the City could have offered the property for sale before changing the zoning.

Proposed Resolution 2024-14 declaring a need for the County Housing
Authority to exercise its powers in Ogden City
          A memo from Council staff regarding the Weber Housing Authority (WHA) came before the Council for consideration.
The memo stated the Weber Housing Authority is requesting a resolution finding a need for its permanent supportive housing
project at 2325 Madison Avenue. Utah Code Ann. §35A-8-401states that a county housing authority may not undertake a project
within a city’s boundaries unless “the governing body of the city [adopts a resolution] . . . declaring that there is need for the
county authority to exercise its powers within that city.” The project was originally reviewed by the Planning Commission on
December 6, 2023. The Commission determined that the proposal was not consistent with the General Plan. The Commission
found that the project did not qualify as a residential facility for persons with a disability, the proposed number of units was not in
compliance with the R-3EC zone, office space was not allowed in the R-3EC Zone, and the type of project—a group home--was
not consistent with the goals and policies of the East Central Community Plan because of concentration of group homes in the
area. WHA revised its proposal and submitted a revised application clarifying that the facility would be properly licensed in
accordance with state law, that shower facilities would be shared so that each unit did not meet the definition of an individual
dwelling unit, and that the number of units would be reduced from 32 to 25 to comply with density limits outlined in the Ogden
City General plan
          Andi Beadles, Executive Director of Weber Housing Authority, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the
proposed project to be completed by the Authority; WHA is proposing to renovate the former Aspen Care assisted living facility
at 2325 Madison Avenue to provide permanent supportive housing for people with disabilities who are experiencing
homelessness. She discussed the separation between WHA and Weber County, after which she discussed the process by which the
need for a single site facility was identified for this area. She provided the costs identified for renovation of the existing building,
as well as the annual operating budget for the facility. The project is funded entirely through grants and will not require any
assistance from Ogden City. The main goals of the project are to assist households in achieving self-sufficiency. Additionally,
obtaining and maintaining income is a requirement of the program and all participants are required to pay 30 percent of their
income as their portion of rent. Relative to mental health and substance use treatment, WHA will partner with Weber Human
Services to provide treatment options, in addition to three full time case managers and one program manager employed by the
Weber Housing Authority. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Council member Choberka commented on the need for this facility in the community; it will aid the homeless population
in securing housing and working towards independent lifestyles.
          Discussion among the Council and Ms. Beadles centered on the other housing and assistance programs that will
potentially be replaced by this new facility; the budget for the project; other sites that may be viable for this type of project; other
permanent supportive housing projects in Ogden or the surrounding community; and services that residents will have access to
when living in the facility.
          Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2024-14, entitled:
