3. Public Site Plan, for Children’s Justice Center, 1845 Jackson Avenue.

  Mr. Rod Layton, representing Children’s Justice Center located at 2408 Van Buren Avenue, stated the use
  is outgrowing the existing building and has been working with both Ogden City and the Ogden School
  District and has contracted a lease agreement with the School District for this property at 1845 Jackson
  Avenue. The property has been rezoned with a development agreement, tying the zoning to the use
  proposed as well as a specific site plan and building design. He reviewed the proposed building, stating it
  will be limited to 15,000 square feet with a ¾ basement and a residential style appearance. The interior
  will provide an environment inviting and comfortable for children and the use is to protect the safety of
  children. The building will also accommodate Child and Family Services staff as well as a full-time police
  officer. The existing building will then be sold once it is vacated.

  Commissioner Graf asked about the length of the property lease with Ogden School District and
  expressed concern with the use of the building if the Children’s Justice Center were to relocate. Mr.
  Layton answered the lease agreement is for 99 years, and according to the Development Agreement, the
  zoning would revert to the previous R-1-6 zoning if he use were to leave. He anticipated that if the CJC
  were to leave, the building would then be owned by the School District and used for educational purposes.
  Scott Medoza, representing Weber County, commented the County is mandated by the State that a
  Children’s Justice Center be provided, indicating it serves residents of both Weber and Morgan Counties.

  Ms. Patrick stated the Commission is to review any public site plan to assure its compliance with the
  General Plan. Because the zoning of the property was tied to a development agreement, the Commission
  also should consider whether the conditions of that agreement are satisfied. She stated the Children’s
  Justice Center is a private/public partnership serving children up to age 17. The building will
  accommodate meeting rooms, interview spaces, medical examination rooms and training rooms as well

Fridays. The property was recently approved by the Mayor to be subdivided from the main School District
Property, but the School District will retain ownership. She reviewed surrounding land uses, indicating the
building proposed will blend with the surrounding residential area. She indicated the use proposed
complies with the General Plan in that it provides a safe and healthy public building. She stated the

4. Public Hearing, Petition to Rezone property from R-1-6 to R-1-5 at 236 Porter Avenue.

    Mr. Steve Diamond indicated he is requesting this vacant church property be rezoned from R-1-6 to R-
    1-5 in order for lot width and total square footage requirements to be reduced. He has prepared a
    potential single-family lot layout of the property, stating the smallest lot proposed is 5,400 square feet,
    and the project will provide more affordable housing options. He noted properties will be available for
    individual sale and anticipated home size is 700 square feet with sale prices around $350,000.



                                                                                                Page 3 of 11
      Ms. Hale stated a petition to rezone is reviewed for compliance with the General Plan, Community
      Plan and Community Policies. Policies indicate definite edges should be created between types of
      uses, zoning should reflect the prevailing character of the overall district and will not cross lots. The
      proposed zoning change is contained to the church property and existing streets provide an edge to
      the development. The surrounding area is developed as single-family lots, with many under the
      required 6,000 square foot lot area required in the existing R-1-6 zone. She reviewed the standards of
      both the R-1-6 and R-1-5 zones, stating most of the setbacks are identical, while the lot area and lot
      width are reduced in the R-1-5 zone. Direction from the Hillcrest/Bonneville Community Plan is to
      strengthen existing single-family neighborhoods, and supports the request.

      Mr. Diamond indicated his petition also included lots across 3rd Street, indicating he is anticipating
      requesting 3rd Street be vacated as part of this project. Ms. Hale indicated she had not reviewed
      beyond the church property north of 3rd Street. Mr. Simpson indicated Staff would not support the
      zoning to cross 3rd Street as the street becomes the edge between the different zone boundaries and
      the new zoning would isolate these properties. There has been no review or request for the vacation
      of 3rd Street.

   As there were no additional public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to close the
   public hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Safsten and passed unanimously.

      MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to recommend approval of the rezoning of
              property the existing parcels on the north side of 3rd Street only from R-1-6 to R-1-5 in order
              to accommodate single-family housing based on the findings it will provide a definite edge
              to development and buffering between types of uses, will reflect the prevailing character of
              the overall district, will not cut across lots or developments, and is supported by the
              Hillcrest/Bonneville Community Plan. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Southwick
              and passed unanimously with Commissioners Aaberg, Blaisdell, Graf, Safsten, Schade,
              Shale, Shinoda, Southwick and Sandau voting aye.

6. Parking Management Plan, Ogden Business Exchange, 600 Exchange Road.

  Mr. Brierley indicated the Commission had asked to see the parking management plan for the
  development of property surrounding the Business Exchange building. He reviewed the proposed uses
  indicating the existing building would house a restaurant and office space and additional buildings are
  proposed. The size of these uses would require 243 parking spaces, which would require the elimination
  of building space and make the project unfeasible. The proposed site plan provides 56 off-street site
  parking spaces with 19 available along public streets. The applicant is suggesting other options such as
  providing UTA passes for employees, on-site bicycle parking and shared daytime and night-time parking.
  The Commission is to determine whether the parking reduction is necessary to preserve and reuse the
  historic structure, that the plan does not create an undue burden on other uses in the area an provides all
  reasonable efforts to provide parking alternatives for uses proposed as part of the development project.

  He indicated the existing building has been badly deteriorating due to neglect and weather conditions,
  and the City has been seeking a developer who could envision its redevelopment for many years. A plan
  has been reviewed and approved by both the Planning Commission and the Landmarks Commission for
  the building renovation and addition. Approval of the parking management plan and reducing on-site
  parking is the final approval needed to allow the project to continue. Staff feels the proposed parking
  options will create options for users of the building and the proposed renovation and uses will benefit
  both the existing building and the surrounding area. Staff recommends approval of the proposed parking
  plan with the recommendation that if the City receives complaints that cannot be resolved by the owners,
  the Community and Economic Development Director may appoint a representative to review these with
  the option to modify the parking management plan with any appeal to be reviewed by the Planning
  Commission who can also add modifications to the plan.

  Commission felt it is in the best interest of the uses proposed to be a good neighbor to existing uses in
  the area. Question arose relating to the location of UTA stops and whether the use of UTA passes would
  be a benefit to employees. It was indicated while there is a stop close to the site, buses come only one
  time per day and at other times the stop on 24th Street would be the nearest. It also was indicated the
  FrontRunner Station is not far from this area and bicycle access is available which connects the
  FrontRunner to the area. It also was noted there is a bike park nearby and easy access to the existing
  trail system. Commissioner Safsten indicating the building has been vacant for many years and due to
  weather damage and neglect, its renovation is important to the community. Commission asked if existing
  uses had been contacted as the reduced parking could affect their existing parking areas and
  consideration of reduced parking had not been given with their development. Mr. Brierley indicated no
  mailings to surrounding uses had occurred for this project. Commissioner Safsten felt it is in the best
  interest of the owner to make the proposed parking design work and to be a good neighbor to existing
  uses in the area.

   MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to approve the parking reduction and parking
           plan as proposed based on the findings it is necessary to preserve and effectively reuse the
           Stockyard Exchange Building, it does not create an undue burden on other uses in the area

7. CBD Site Plan, Wonderblock Phase 2, Mixed Use Project, 2565 Grant Avenue.

  Mr. Mitch Vance stated this is the second phase of the WonderBlock project, indicating the initial phase
  for the parking structure was approved and construction plans are now being reviewed by City staff. This
  phase contains the bulk of the block, with mixed use with commercial, residential and office space. The
  project is consistent with the Make Ogden Downtown Plan in that it increases residential density in the
  downtown area. He felt this project would be a catalyst for future development in the downtown. This
  phase also includes a 318 stall parking structure and the final phase will be for a hotel facing Historic 25th
  Street.

  Mr. Simpson presented the site plan and renderings of the proposed buildings, indicating the project is to
  be pedestrian friendly. The area is now zoned CBD, but the Commission has recommended the
  implementation of the zoning amendments to match the Make Ogden Plan, which will change the zone to
  C-MU. Uses proposed fit both zoning designations, with residential, commercial and office space s well
  as an additional parking structure. Residential buildings will surround the parking structures and these
  are not visible from the street. The interior of the block creates a pedestrian plaza and the three mixed-
  use buildings will contain 120,000 square feet of Class A office space, 4,800 square feet of retail space to
  include a grocery store and 350 residential units. Buildings are oriented to the street and are brick
  construction with glazing consisting of punch windows. Residential amenities are provided to include a
  pool, fitness area, plazas fire pits, barbeque areas, outdoor seating and pergolas. There is a variety of
  colors with roof and wall variations on the buildings and architectural lines fit with existing development
  in the downtown. He further reviewed the site plan indicating all parcels will need to be combined with
  pedestrian easements identified, and other ordinance requirements satisfied, including any utilities or
  dumpsters must be screened from the public view. He also noted 15-foot sight triangles need to be
  created at the driveways to protect the safety of both pedestrians and vehicles. Staff recommends
  approval subject to the following conditions;
  1. Providing building, utility plan and road cut details;
  2. Providing a financial guarantee for public improvements and landscaping;
  3. Obtaining SWPPP permit;
  4. Placing fire hydrants as determined by the Fire Marshal;
  5. Modifying building and landscape plans to provide a sight triangle view of vehicles and pedestrians at
       sidewalk conflict points and driveways;
  6. Providing details of light fixtures, street lights and photometric coverage;
  7. Consolidating the parcels and dedicating easements along the alley south of 25th Street and the
       internal pedestrian ways; and
  8. Screening all utilities and trash receptacles.

   Chair Sandau asked what measures are taken to assure people do not sleep in the plaza area. Mr. Vance

   Southwick asked if any units would be available for ownership, stating the City should make more

9. Public Hearing, Proposed Zoning Ordinance Amendment, Residential Vacation rentals along BRT
   Route.

   Mr. Wade Bridge stated this is a request to allow up to eight vacation rental properties in an existing
   historic building if it is along the BRT route. Mr. Tanner Leatham stated this request is to allow each
   of dsthe existing eight units in the Bristol Mansion at 25 th & Van Buren to be used as vacation rentals,
   He indicated he and his partner had acquired this building 5-6 years ago and have been working on
   its renovation since that time. At that time there was no working plumbing or electrical service in the
   building and he has worked and obtained historic tax credits to help with its renovation. He reviewed
   the history of the building, indicating it had been built in 1912 by Ralph Bristol as a single-family
   home but has been converted to eight apartments for many years, having been neglected in the
   recent past. At some point it had been divided into condominiums, so each unit could be owned
   separately. He felt due to its location along the BRT route and the beauty of the historic building
   both make it a prime property for vacation rentals. He indicated the existing code limits vacation
   rentals to one per lineal block, and he felt this should be increased for historic properties along the
   BRT route and parking reduced to one per unit. He stated while the intent is for units to be owned
   separately, some may be rented and potential owners may desire to leave during the winter months
   and rent their space for skiers. He stated other options such as a bed and breakfast have been
   discussed, it appears to not be feasible as on-site management is required.

   Mr. Rypien reviewed the petition, indicating it requests to amend the code to allow up to eight
   vacation rentals per lineal block so long as they are condominiums in a historic building along the
   BRT route. He stated there is a transit stop at 25th Street and Van Buren Avenue, and the East Central
   Plan supports transit-oriented development along the BRT route. However, the surrounding

                                                                                             Page 8 of 11
   community is mostly single-family in nature and the creation of vacation rentals eliminates
   opportunities for long-term housing. He stated the existing mansion has a nonconforming certificate
   to allow its continued use as eight dwelling units with five on-site parking spaces. The R-2EC zone
   recognizes the historic development in the community by limiting uses to single-family and duplexes.
   The request to reduce required parking for vacation rentals is not consistent with the ordinance
   regulations, the General Plan or the character of the surrounding community. He stated transit-
   oriented development should be in areas with higher density and not in the middle of an existing
   single-family neighborhood. Staff recommends the Commission forward a recommendation to the
   City Council to not adopt the proposed text amendments for residential vacation rentals as allowing
   more than one per lineal block affects the existing housing stock of the surrounding community, is
   not in the best interest of the community and not consistent with the General Plan.

   Commissioner Southwick noted this property faces Van Buren Avenue and not the BRT route. Mr.
   Bridge responded the side property line is along 25th Street, thus making the property adjacent to the
   BRT. Discussion continued relating to the effect of the historic designation has in relation to a
   vacation rental as most of this community is part of the East Bench National Historic District.
   Commission also felt the use of the BRT route may not be effective as users would likely need to ride
   downtown in order to catch a shuttle to the ski resorts. It was noted condominium units could be
   sold or retained under one ownership but the ongoing use of each of these units is not in the best
   interest of the neighborhood. Commission expressed appreciation to the owner for the renovation
   work completed, but was concerned the request is to amend the code to serve only one property.

As there were no public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Graf to close the public
hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Safsten and passed unanimously.

   Commissioner Southwick commented while he believes this petition highlights that some changes
   need to be made to the vacation rental provisions, he felt it inappropriate that the changes targets an
   individual property, stating any amendments should have a broader range. Chair Sandau commented
   that when first considered, there was much public concern about these becoming too prevalent in
   neighborhoods, and when created, there is a loss of full-time housing stock and expressed concern
   about the effect on the surrounding community if each were a short-term rental rather than being
   owner-occupied long-term housing. She felt allowing more vacation rentals would contribute to the
   high cost of long-term rentals. Commissioner Graf stated he appreciates the efforts to preserve

10. Discussion: Density and Setbacks for Group Dwellings

    Mr. Mike Bastian indicated he owns several properties and when designing their layout, has found
    the existing ordinances reduce potential density when more than one building is allowed, indicating
    separate buildings contribute to the vitality of the community and are often more desirable than a
    single apartment structure and density should be encouraged. On existing property he could
    construct 26 units if in one building, but if separate buildings were desired, he would be limited to 18
    units due to separation and setback standards in the group dwelling section of the Code. He is
    requesting the Commission discuss these standards and has prepared a petition to amend the Code
    to reduce setbacks and allow increased densities for group dwellings.

    Mr. Rypien stated a project is classified as a group dwelling when more than one building is located
    on one lot. He presented examples of types of group dwellings such as garden units and
    townhomes. While Mr. Bastian had submitted a petition to amend the Code, specific language has
    not yet been prepared and as he was not available to meet in person, has requested some input from
    the Commission prior to establishing a public hearing to review the petition. He stated the Ordinance
    would allow 20 per acre if in one building, while limiting units to 11 per acre if there were five
    buildings. He agreed that project density should not be penalized due to the number of buildings.
    The petition is requesting that setbacks and density requirements be modified. In addition, Staff has
    recently suggested planting requirements for trees and shrubs can be excessive for large
    development projects as well as other minor regulations to update the code.

    Commissioner Southwick commented multiple buildings are better for the housing market as they
    can more easily be divided and offered for sale, while a single building becomes cumbersome to
    meet condominium requirements. He suggested projects might be tied to a development agreement
    requiring units to be sold and owner-occupied for the first year after completion. He felt this would
    increase affordable housing for the community, stating home ownership is an important step in
    providing financial independence for residents.

    Mr. Bastian stated while he supports home ownership, each project is unique and depending on the
    property shape and size, dividing parcels or units is not always possible and sometimes not
    financially feasible.

    Commissioner Graf felt it would be more beneficial to do a comprehensive review of the group
    dwelling standards rather than doing them piecemeal. Discussion continued relating to
    discrepancies Staff has noticed in the existing code, as well as new types of developments which
    might fit the code but are not identified. Building materials, glazing, and planting requirements are
    items Staff might consider amending. Commission suggested reduced parking and more waterwise
    plantings should be discussed and that density bonuses should be eliminated, particularly for items
    which should be standard requirements. Commissioners agreed the entire ordinance should be
    reworked.
