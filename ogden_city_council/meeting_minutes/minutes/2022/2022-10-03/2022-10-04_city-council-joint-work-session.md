Land Transfer and Development Agreement and Participation and Incentive
Agreement — Union Stock Exchange Building

Community and Economic Development Director Cooper reviewed the proposed Land Transfer and Development
Agreement (LTDA) and Participation and Reimbursement Agreement for the Union Stock Exchange building; the other party to
the agreements is Q Factor, who proposes to rehabilitate and expand the Building and build a second building on the same site. He
presented photographs of the building in its current state as well as images of other historic buildings that have been renovated that
can serve as ‘precedent’ projects for this project; he explained that in preservation projects, it is ideal to juxtapose historical
architectural features rather than mimic them. This has been done at the Ogden High School preservation project, as well as many
other similar projects in Salt Lake City. He then presented the illustrative site plan for the building; the project includes
construction of an additional building on the site, parking areas between the two buildings, and on-street parking on both
Stockman Way and Exchange Road. He then presented building floor plans for the project; on the north side of the building, the
developer would like to add two levels that would be the same height as the existing building as well as a third level on top of the
building that will only extend half of the length of the building. The reason for that is to use the load point of the existing building
for the new additions to the building. This is the reason for the proposed zone text amendment for the Business Exchange Historic
(BEH) Zone. He presented a rendering to illustrate the view of the building with the additional height when standing on the street
to the south of the site. He highlighted repetition of the windows on the building, which is one of the key defining features of the
building; the developer has tried to make a connection to that feature, while not trying to mimic or replicate it. He then provided
other views of the building from the west and east and also presented renderings of the new building proposed to be built on the
site; the new building also will not mimic the old building but will be designed in a way to give the appearance that it is ‘from the
same family’. The proposal has been reviewed by the Planning Commission and Landmarks Commission; the Landmarks
Commission granted approval, with one opposing vote based upon a concern about the height of the building. He noted that Q
Factor has made application for a Certificate of Historic Appropriateness for the project, which requires compliance with

Board member Hyer then expressed concern about the subjectivity of some of the criteria used to determine whether a
Certificate of Historic Appropriateness should be granted; he feels that the improvements to the building should complement,
rather than juxtapose, the existing historical building style. Mr. Cooper agreed that design beauty is subjective and at least one
Landmarks Commissioner agreed with Board member Hyer’s concerns; however, the developer has assembled a proposal and
application that has received support from the Planning Commission and Landmarks Commission and they are willing to make a
sizeable investment, upwards of $23 million, in the building and site and he feels that the end result will be a wonderful
contribution to the community.

Board member Choberka stated that she loves the design that has been developed; however, she is concerned about
whether the parking areas included in the project are adequate. Mr. Cooper stated that the entire subdivision was designed to allow
shared parking, free on-street parking, and on-site parking.

There was then discussion among the Board and Mr. Cooper regarding other improvements to the site, including curb,
gutter, sidewalk; bike-lanes; pedestrian trails; landscaping; and storm water retention.

Mr. Cooper then noted that the property is 1.81 acres in size and the purchase price is $195,000. The incentive amount
identified in the Participation and Incentive Agreement is $800,000; the incentive will be provided post-performance.

Board member White inquired as to the timeline for the project. Mr. Cooper stated that if the LTDA is approved, the
anticipated completion date is in 2026 in order for Q Factor to be eligible to receive the incentive.

Board Executive Director Eller-Smith stated that Board staff will schedule action on the proposed agreements if there is
no opposition from the Board. The Board briefly discussed the proposal, with Board member Hyer stated that he will likely vote in
opposition to the proposal based upon his concerns about the design of the improvements; however, he is not opposed to an action
item on the matter being scheduled for Board consideration.

Fiscal Year 2022-2023 Budget Amendment — Union Stock Exchange
Building; and

Fiscal Year 2022-2023 Budget Amendment — Union Stock Exchange
Building, Miscellaneous Grant Funding

Comptroller Stout reported this proposed budget amendment is related to the Union Stock Exchange Building project,
which was discussed under the previous agenda item; Administration is proposing to transfer $845,000 from the Ogden City
Capital Improvement project #CD0132 — Stock Exchange Building Purchase, to the Redevelopment Agency. Agency staff
proposes to use the transferred funds to purchase the Stock Exchange Building from the current owner, OBE Vision, per the terms
of the Master Land Transfer and Development Agreement. If approved by the Board, Q Factor will purchase the Exchange
building from the Agency for $200,000. Per the Participation and Incentive Agreement, the Agency would provide $800,000 in
incentives to complete redevelopment of the Exchange Building. The Agency will also provide $40,000 in additional funds to
cover fencing and boarding of the building.

Ms. Stout summarized additional amendments to the City budget; the proposed budget amendment also recognizes
revenues from United Way for police vehicles to support homeless outreach efforts ($88,200), recognizes grant funds from the
Utah Commission on Criminal and Juvenile Justice (UCCJJ) for police equipment ($14,500), and recognizes RAMP EZ Grant
funds for purchase of equipment for the Golden Hours Center ($4,000).

Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reported that Council staff will schedule public hearings for the proposed budget
amendments.

Use of Custodial property — Ogden Police Department

Police Chief Young reported the Ogden Police Department is requesting authorization to sell surrendered/seized firearms
currently in the custody of the Department to 1) reduce inventory and create storage space for incoming items; and 2) dispose of
surrendered/seized firearms in accordance with Utah State Code 24-3-103.5. The request requires the Council to authorize the
appropriation of the property — the firearms — for public use. The firearms will be sold by Hy & Mike’s Pawn and the proceeds
donated to the Weber-Morgan Narcotic Strike Force. The surrendered firearms have not been reported stolen, nor has anyone
attempted to claim them. The seized firearms are from adjudicated cases and cannot legally be returned to the owner. The
Department has contacted prosecutors with the Ogden Justice Court and the 2nd District Court and have authorization to move
forward with the disposition of the property.

Council member White asked if someone buying a gun from a pawn shop must undergo a background check. Chief
Young answered yes. He added that any pawn shop that is eligible to receive this type of inventory from a police department must
be a licensed federal firearms dealer.

There was a brief discussion among the Council and Chief Young about the types of guns in the Department’s possession
and the potential revenue to be generated by the transaction. The Council offered their support for proceeding to take action on the
Chief's proposal. There was also a brief focus on the request for proposal (RFP) process for this project, with Chief Young noting
that the RFP criteria is very strict, but he believes that Hy & Mikes was the only respondent.

Council member Richey referenced firearms that are destroyed rather than sold; he asked if identifying information for
those guns is kept by the City for the purposes of determining if the firearm was used in the commission of a crime. Chief Young
answered yes and detailed the process by which bullets fired by each firearm are catalogued and documented to aid in future or

Council Business

Police Department — Street Racing: Council member Richey reported the Council has received several comments from
residents about street racing activities occurring throughout the City. He asked Chief Young to summarize the Department’s
response to these activities. Chief Young stated he has received many of the same emails the Council has been receiving and he
has created a street racing enforcement task force that is monitoring activities throughout the City. There are some high priority
areas in which enforcement efforts are increased and he will provide an enforcement report to the Council in the future if that
would be beneficial. Council discussion with the Chief and Chief Administrative Officer Johnson centered on opportunities to
locate more cameras throughout the City that could help improve enforcement efforts. Chief Young provided instructions for
residents to report street racing activities they observe.

The group then brainstormed on the idea of creating a racing course somewhere in the county that could be used for street
racing in order to remove the activity from City streets.

Police Department — Staffing: Chief Young then provided the Council with a report of improvements in staffing of his
Department following actions taken by the Council to adjust compensation for City employees; he thanked the Council for their
support and efforts to make wages and benefits more competitive in Ogden City. He noted he will provide more detailed
information in the coming months as the Council begins working on the City’s budget for the next fiscal year.

2023 Legislative Session: Council member White stated that the Utah League of Cities and Towns (ULCT) is preparing
for the upcoming legislative session; key pieces of legislation being monitored include bills regarding affordable housing and
accessory dwelling units.

Fraud Risk Analysis: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reported on the annual fraud risk analysis process, which
is part of the annual audit. Council staff has been considering opportunities for implementing new fraud reporting tools that would
help to increase the City’s audit rating. She noted more details will be reported to the Council in the near future.
