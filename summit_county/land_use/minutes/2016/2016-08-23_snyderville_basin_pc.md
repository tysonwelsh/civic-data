4. Public hearing and possible action regarding proposed amendments to Sections 
10-4-5, Chapter 10, and Chapter 11 of the Development Code clarifying the water 
concurrency requirements for the Snyderville Basin Planning District—Jennifer 
Strader, Senior Planner  
 
Planner Jennifer Strader said in the year 2000, the County Commission adopted a water 
concurrency temporary zoning ordinance.  This imposed temporary zoning regulations 
in the Snyderville Basin that tied development approvals to the availability of water.  
The intent of the ordinance was to ensure that the physical water supply existed rather 
than just the paper water rights before issuing any type of development permits.  
Between 2000 and 2005, various ordinances were adopted that further addressed 
water concurrency.  In 2005, the water concurrency ordinance was added to the 
Development Code.   
 
Planner Strader said the Summit County Board of Health has been working with the 
water suppliers, including Weber Basin Water Conservancy District.  They agree that 
water concurrency should fall under the purview of the Health Department.  Water 
concurrency regulations were implemented in the Health Code in June of this year.   
 
The intent of these Code amendments is to ensure the Development Code is consistent 
with the Health Code and to clarify what is required before approval of a development 
permit.  She said the amendment includes the following changes:

1. The entirety of the Water Concurrency Code (chapter 10) will be removed.                
2. A two-step process will take place during the review of the development.  The 
first step requires the issuance of a willing-to-serve letter and will be received 
prior to any development permit approval.  It is a letter from the water provider 
saying that they are willing to serve the development.  The second step is the 
commitment of service letter.  It is required prior to issuance of a building 
permit.    
 
Since the Staff Report went out, Staff has continued to work with the County Attorney’s 
office to refine the language.  Each Commissioner should have received a copy of the 
amended language (page #6).  This is the most recent document and is what the 
Commission should refer to during the meeting.  The language has been amended to say 
that a willing-to-serve letter is required prior to any type of development permit 
approval, not just a subdivision plat.   
 
Planner Strader said that #1 and #2 under concurrency management have been deleted 
from the Code because the willing-to-serve letter will address those items.  Those items 
addressed available water rights, capacity, water pressure, water volume, and water 
quality.  These will now be addressed under the willing-to-serve letter, which will be for 
all development permit approvals.   
 
The second step in the process requires an applicant to have a commitment of service 
letter from the water company.  This is an irrevocable contractual commitment that is 
issued by the water supplier in consideration for payment of the water supplier’s 
impact fee.  This is where the “wet water” verification comes into play.  These changes 
are consistent with the Board of Health’s concurrency requirements.   
 
Planner Strader said that Staff recommends that the Planning Commission forward a

Attorney Brackin gave the history of concurrency so the Commission can understand 
what is happening.  During the early 2000’s there were a lot of private water companies 
in the Snyderville Basin that ran out of water.  They had water rights, but no actual wet 
water.  Summit County stepped in and started delivering water by the truckloads in the 
Snyderville Basin.  It became abundantly clear that the County needed to be involved 
with water regulations.  The goal was to come up with a water distribution system 
where everybody could get water and that the water wouldn’t run out.  She said this 
was a painful process involving litigation.  The fundamental principle was that all water 
suppliers have actual wet water to deliver to the people they serve.   
 
It became very clear that the State Engineer’s Office had over-issued water rights.  
There were a lot of water rights on paper, but not enough wet water to go around.  
Summit County created the concurrency system, which required that every water 
company perform a yearly test of their water system to determine how much wet water 
they really have.  Based upon that test, the water company determines they have “X” 
amount and that they are currently using “Y” amount.  This means they have “Z” amount 
left over.  This was the banking system that was referred to in the former Chapter 10.      
 
David Hedderly-Smith is the person in charge of the water concurrency program for the 
County.  For a long time, the program was managed through the Engineering 
Department along with Planning and Zoning.  A few years ago, the County determined 
the program would be best managed with the Health Department.  It was not only water

FINDINGS OF FACT 
1. On May 5, 2000 the County adopted a temporary zoning ordinance known as the 
Water Concurrency Ordinance, which imposed temporary zoning regulations in 
the Snyderville Basin Planning District that tied development approvals and the 
issuance of building permits to the availability of water.   
2. The County subsequently adopted Ordinances 400, 415, 415-A, 436, and 525, 
perpetuating the concurrency requirements of the temporary zoning ordinance.   
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission 
August 9, 2016 
Page 17 of 58 
 
3. In 2005, the Water Banking and Concurrency Ordinance was added to the 
Snyderville Basin Development Code (Chapter 10). 
4. The Summit County Board of Health has promulgated new detailed water 
concurrency regulations designed to protect the health of citizens of the County, 
which are intended to replace the County Water Concurrency program that 
currently exists in the Snyderville Basin Development Code.   
5. The proposed amendments will remove the Water Banking and Concurrency 
Ordinance from the Snyderville Basin Development Code.  The Board of Health 
will govern the water concurrency regulations. 
6. In addition to the deletion of the Water Banking and Concurrency Ordinance, 
amendments are also proposed to Section 10-4-5 of the Development Code that 
clarify a Willing-to-Serve Letter is required prior to subdivision plat approval 
and a Commitment of Service Letter is required prior to building permit 
issuance. 
7. Definitions of Willing-to-Serve Letter, Commitment of Service Letter, and Water 
Supplier are proposed to be added to Chapter 11.   
8. Objective A of Chapter 7 (Services and Facilities) of the General Plan states: 
“Coordinate and collaborate with applicable service providers to identify 
acceptable service levels and develop standards for measuring service delivery 
success.”  
9. The proposed amendments are written for the entire Snyderville Basin Planning 
Area. 
10. The purpose of the amendments is to ensure that development permits for new

Mr. Alexander said that proper heliport selection is very important.  It needs to be in a
