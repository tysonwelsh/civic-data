SUMMARY:

During the 2023 General Session, the Utah legislature passed Senate Bill 174
(SB174), amending the process by which cities and counties review and approve
subdivision applications. The intent of SB174 is to establish a more uniform,
streamlined, and predictable subdivision review and approval procedure among
jurisdictions in Utah. Local jurisdictions were given until February 1, 2024 to
adopt updates to their respective subdivision ordinances.

Per SB174, all subdivision reviews must now follow a 2-step administrative
process:

Preliminary Subdivision Plat Review (maximum two review cycles)

e Upon submittal of a complete preliminary subdivision application, city staff
must provide initial review comments within 15 business days.

e The applicant must respond in writing to staff’s initial review comments:

o Ifthe applicant responds within 20 business days of staff's initial review,
city staff will have 15 business days to review and provide comments for
the 2"¢ submittal.

o Ifthe applicant fails to respond within 20 business days of staff's initial
review, city staff will have 35 business days to review and provide
comments for the 2™ submittal.

o A review cycle is not considered complete until the applicant has
adequately addressed all of the redlines identified by the city.

e Upon receipt of the 2™ submittal, the application will be noticed and
scheduled for a public hearing by the Planning Commission. A maximum of
one public hearing may be held.

e The Planning Commission is designated the preliminary subdivision plat land
use authority.

e The city may only add new redlines/comments after the first review cycle in
response to changes made by the applicant or if a correction is necessary to
protect public health or safety, or to enforce state or federal law.

e Ifthe preliminary application complies with adopted local regulations, it shall
be approved and proceed to the second step (final review).

Final Subdivision Plat Review (maximum two review cycles):

e Upon submittal of a complete final subdivision application, city staff must
provide initial review comments within 20 business days of submittal.

e The applicant must respond in writing to staff's initial review comments. The
same 20-day resubmittal deadline applies.

e Ifthe final subdivision application complies with adopted standards, the city
shall approve the final subdivision application.

e While the Director is proposed to be the official “land use authority” for a
final plat, the mayor must still sign the mylar plat prior to recording.

e The final plat is then recorded with Salt Lake County Recorder’s Office

As part of this text amendment, city staff recommends creating a new section
titled, “common review procedures.” This new section consolidates general steps
that are applicable to multiple development application types to streamline and
make relevant information easier to find. The specific review procedures for the
subdivision applications refer back to these common review procedures and note
any modifications or additions. The following steps are included in this section:

e  Pre-application meeting. Early engagement with staff and applicant to
review process and to bring to light any potential concerns that may be
relevant based on the applicant’s request (e.g., infrastructure challenges,
environmental concerns, etc.).

e Application submittal and processing. Establish procedure for reviewing
and accepting applications, as well as timelines for when applications expire.

e Application review. Identify the procedures for reviewing applications,
provide for interdepartmental coordination, commenting on applications, and
preparing recommendations and investigative reports when applicable.

e Neighborhood meeting. This step is intended to allow early public input for
applications that amend city policy (e.g., General Plan Map and Zoning Map
amendments). This is currently a placeholder and will be considered further
during the larger LDC update project.

e Public notice and hearing. Identify the types of public hearings and the
noticing requirements (e.g., published, posted, mailed, website).

e Application decision and action. Establish the general procedures for public
hearings on development decisions, who the decision-making body is and
under what criteria they make their decisions.

e  Post-decision actions and limitations. Describe how decisions are presented
to applicants and the public, and whether decisions expire. This also includes
details on extending expirations or amending previous approvals.

There are several footnotes throughout Exhibit A that highlight relocations, new or
modified provisions, and other information that helps clarify the content. These
footnotes are provided for reference only and will be removed prior to codification
and publication. Other notable updates, beyond those mentioned in this Summary,
include:

e The expiration period for a preliminary plat has been reduced from two years
to one year. The applicant may still request an extension from the Director.

e Plat amendments no longer require a public hearing, unless required by Utah
Code.

e The property line adjustment procedure is updated to align with Utah Code
and current practice.

e New definitions for key terms in the draft.

On December 12, 2023, the Planning Commission forwarded a positive

recommendation to the City Council recommending approval of the amendments,

as articulated in Exhibit A. The Planning Commission recommended two changes
be incorporated:

e Remove the administrative plat procedure from the text amendment and
consider incorporating an administrative plat process with the larger LDC
update project; and

e Require a public notice and public hearing at the Planning Commission
meeting.

Staff recommends approval of Ordinance No. 24-02, based on the findings

WHEREAS, the Taylorsville City Council (the “Council”) met in regular session on
January 3, 2024, to consider, among other things, approving a text amendment to the Taylorsville
Municipal Code affecting several sections of the Taylorsville Municipal Code to implement Utah
Senate Bill 174, related to the subdivision review processes.; and

WHEREAS, pursuant to TAYLORSVILLE MUNICIPAL CODE Section 13.35.050, notice of a
public hearing before the City’s Planning Commission was published on the city’s official website
on November 30, 2023; and

WHEREAS, the notice of a public hearing before the City’s Planning Commission was
also published on the Utah Public Notice Website on November 30, 2023; and

WHEREAS, the City’s Planning Commission held a properly noticed public hearing on
the proposed amendments on December 12, 2023, during which the Planning Commission voted
7-0 to recommend that the Council approve the proposed text amendment; and

WHEREAS, although not required by City ordinances, the City Council heard courtesy
public comment on the proposed amendments during its regularly scheduled meeting on January
3, 2024; and

WHEREAS, after careful consideration, the Council has determined that it is in the best
interest of the health, safety, and welfare of the citizens of Taylorsville to approve the proposed
amendments.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED by the Taylorsville City Council that the
proposed text amendments to several sections of the Taylorsville Municipal Code to implement
Utah Senate Bill 174, related to the subdivision review processes, are hereby approved, as
articulated in Exhibit “A.”

This ordinance, assigned Ordinance No. 24-02, shall take effect as soon as it shall be
published or posted as required by law, deposited, and recorded in the office of the City Recorder,
and accepted as required herein.

13.36.200: "S" DEFINITIONS:

SUBDIVISION IMPROVEMENT PLANS: means the civil engineering plans associated with required
infrastructure and municipally controlled utilities required for a subdivision.

SUBDIVISION ORDINANCE REVIEW: means review by the city to verify that a subdivision land use
application meets the criteria of the city's subdivision ordinances.

SUBDIVISION PLAN REVIEW: means a review of the applicant's subdivision improvement plans an:
other aspects of the subdivision land use application to verify that the application complies with city

ordinances and applicable standards and specifications.
