Recognizing Ogden City Detective Breianna Bakker for being named the
2026 Large Agency Officer of the Year by the Utah Chiefs of Police
Association
          A memo from Council staff regarding recognition of Detective Breianna Bakker for being named the 2026 Large Agency
Office of the Year by the Utah Chiefs of Police Association came before the Council for consideration. Council member Lopez
read the certificate of recognition issued by the Ogden City Council and Mayor Nadolski and presented Detective Bakker with the
certificate.
          Detective Bakker thanked the Mayor and Council for recognizing her; she described cases that led to this recognition.
The cases originated in 1994 and 1997, and a confirmed DNA hit prompted her to investigate a suspect's history, ultimately
connecting him to both cases. She reviewed over 300 cold cases from 1990 to 1998 to identify additional victims and released a
public video requesting others to come forward. Detective Bakker expressed that delivering news of the suspect's apprehension to
the victims, who had waited over 30 years for justice, was the most meaningful part of the investigation.

Proposed Ordinance 2026-4 reflecting a staffing reorganization that
transfers the Code Services Division from the Community and Economic
Development Department to the Fire Department where it will be
restructured as the Community Risk Reduction Team and amending the
Fiscal Year 2025-2026 budget to reallocate funds between the Community
and Economic Development and Fire Departments to reflect this
reorganization
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development and Fire Departments regarding the proposed Code Services
and Community Risk Reduction reorganization came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated City Administration
is proposing a staffing reorganization that transfers the Code Services function from the Community and Economic Development
Department to the Fire Department, where it will be restructured as the Community Risk Reduction (CRR) team. The proposal
includes an ordinance to approve the associated budget transfer between departments and amend the staffing document to reflect
the new organizational structure. This reorganization is intended to improve coordination between code enforcement and public
safety functions. The Administration has indicated the proposal will have no net fiscal impact to the General Fund. The proposal
also includes the addition of one firefighter position funded through the Medical Services Fund, with ongoing costs to be
incorporated into the Fiscal Year (FY) 27 budget.
          Finance Director Sorensen presented the fiscal impact of the proposal; he reiterated the net change to the City's general
fund budget is zero, as the proposal moves existing budget appropriations between departments rather than adding new
expenditures. The reorganization involves removing seven positions from Community and Economic Development’s Code
Services division — one supervisor, five code service officers, and one administrative assistant — and transferring three code
service officer positions and the administrative assistant position to the Fire Department. Two new firefighter positions would be
added in the general fund (budget-neutral through the reallocation), and one additional firefighter position would be added in the
medical fund, covered by existing budget for the remainder of the fiscal year and fully budgeted in FY 2027. Mr. Sorensen noted
that only two or three of the seven current code services positions are filled, and the supervisor position is currently vacant,
meaning no employees would lose their jobs in the transition.
           Fire Chief Slater explained that the reorganization concept originated from a Mayoral directive to identify operational
and budgetary efficiencies. Analysis revealed that Code Services had long operated understaffed, worked in close coordination
with Fire Prevention, and would benefit from integration with the Fire Department's data systems, policy structure, and response
operations. He described the new CRR model as a data-driven, prevention-focused approach that would prioritize life safety
threats — including wildland-urban interface fire risk, vacant property hazards, and repeat violation tracking — over routine
nuisance enforcement. He then outlined how the current two-step citation process (a 14-day fire prevention notice followed by a
15-day code services citation) would be streamlined into a single 15-day process, accelerating resolution of hazards. He also
presented data supporting the addition of two firefighters at Station 3, noting that a four-person engine crew is statistically 25%
faster in overall tasks, 11% faster in getting water on a fire, 27% faster in victim removal, and results in 46% fewer firefighter
injuries. He further noted that Fire Prevention is backlogged with over 3,500 businesses to inspect, and a third Deputy Fire
Marshal position is needed to address that workload.
          Chair Hyer asked about how the City's firefighting capabilities might affect insurance ratings. Chief Slater explained that
Ogden City currently holds an Insurance Services Office (ISO) 2 rating, one level below the highest possible ISO 1 rating. He
noted that achieving an ISO 1 rating would require additional staffing and equipment, as well as certain facility improvements,
which are currently not feasible within this fiscal year or the next. Chief Slater emphasized that while ISO 1 is the top tier, Ogden
City is among very few departments in Utah with an ISO 2 rating, reflecting strong firefighting capabilities.
          Council member Richey disclosed that his son is currently in the Ogden City firefighter hiring pool, which includes
positions being created under this proposal. He recused himself from discussion and the vote on the proposal.

Proposed Resolution 2026-2 approving an interlocal cooperation agreement
with Weber County for the provision of paramedic services
         A memo from the Fire Department regarding the Ogden Fire Interlocal Agreement with Weber County came before the
Council for consideration. The memo stated Ogden City and Weber County propose to renew their interlocal cooperation
agreement for paramedic services for the period January 1, 2026, through December 31, 2030. The agreement continues the
existing service delivery model, under which the Ogden Fire Department provides paramedic staffing and operations, while
Weber County contributes funding and capital equipment, including vehicles.
         Fire Chief Slater summarized the memo and stated the program has been in place since 1986 and is recognized as a gold
standard for paramedic service delivery. Ogden City operates three paramedic units — Medic 1, Medic 3, and Medic 5 — serving
both Ogden residents and broader Weber County as a secondary and tertiary resource. The program is funded through a County-
wide property tax that supports durable equipment, with the interlocal agreement governing annual personnel cost contributions.
He noted that the Department operates 22 County-funded paramedic positions, each covered at approximately 40% of total cost by
Weber County, with the remainder borne by the City. This arrangement provides Ogden with highly experienced paramedics who
respond to 22,000 calls annually — more than three times the call volume of the next busiest department in the county. He
reported that the agreement for 2026 includes a 7% increase in payments, retroactive to January 1, 2026, amounting to a $123,000
increase to the Fire Department's personnel budget. The delay in coming before the Council was attributed to administrative
timing on the County's end. Chief Slater recommended approval of the agreement, citing the program's role in maintaining
consistent training protocols, shared equipment, and seamless interoperability with other county agencies during mass casualty
incidents.
         Chair Hyer introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2026-2, entitled:
                   “A resolution of Ogden City approving an Interlocal Cooperation Agreement between Ogden City and Weber
                   County for the continued provision of paramedic services for the term of January 1, 2026, through December
                   31, 2030.”

Public Comments
          Comments referencing the Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) activities in Ogden City: Joseph Bauman shared
his discomfort with a previous analogy comparing Ogden City actions to WWII death camps, labeling it inappropriate given the
historical context. Thor Doresh clarified that the opposition to the Ogden holding facility did not make such a comparison, but
rather highlighted historical internment issues, emphasizing a need for acknowledgment and regret for past actions.
          James Dixon brought up an assassination case in Utah, believing the accused, Tyler Robinson, is not guilty, and stated his
concerns to various officials, also mentioning his candidacy for President of the United States.
          Heath Satow referenced the action taken regarding the reorganization of Code Services in the City; he highlighted the
issue of abandoned properties in Ogden that pose both safety risks and financial burdens on taxpayers. These properties should not
cost more in public resources than the taxes they generate and suggested exploring best practices from other cities to address the
problem effectively.
          Comments regarding the Utah Renewable Communities program: Laura Lewis raised questions about a solar power
program discussed previously, including measurement of solar power use while connected to the grid, developer roles, customer
relationships, investment returns, maintenance, lifespan, and disposal of solar panels. Kim James expressed personal support for
the Utah Renewable Communities program, recognizing affordability challenges but emphasizing opt-out options and subsidies
for low-income participants, viewing it as a step toward renewable energy. Heath Satow supported solar panel investments,
referring to degradation studies. Annie Dunaway expressed skepticism about trust in the company responsible, based on its
environmental record. Chris Edmonds emphasized the corporate commitment to renewable energy, noting site-specific constraints
in Ogden, while Dan Kylie highlighted the program's expansion of clean energy access to renters. Linda Devos, engaging
remotely, commended the Council's efforts on the community clean energy program, encouraging continued inquiries and sharing
optimism for passage. Jen Bodine urged a favorable vote on the program, citing the economic efficiency of renewable resources
and its particular benefit to renters. Monica Hansen supported the program, highlighting that participants already subscribing to
Blue Sky might see reduced bills, and stressed planned outreach to inform low-income residents about subsidy options.
          Comments regarding the ‘Institute Construction Project’: Alex Johnson, representing the Kingston neighborhood, raised
concerns about the lack of a traffic control plan related to the Institute construction project at Weber State University. He
submitted documentation of his concerns to Council staff in order for those comments to be distributed to the Council. Travis Pate
supported these expressions of inadequate planning and recalled similar issues with another local construction project, urging
proper infrastructure assessment. He also questioned city-owned property code compliance enforcement.
          Council member Lundell addressed questions raised by Ms. Lewis regarding the Utah Renewable Communities program.
He explained that third-party developers build utility-scale solar, and the Utah Community Renewables Agency — which Ogden
may join upon adopting the ordinance — would enter into a power purchase agreement with a selected developer. That power
would then be fed into the Rocky Mountain Power grid under the City's existing utility agreement. He acknowledged that while
the total power generated is quantifiable, the precise routing of individual electrons through the grid is not, and that the offset
model is how the accounting of renewable generation works.
          Council member Richey thanked residents, correspondents, and those who met with him individually regarding the clean
energy program. He acknowledged that the program has been under consideration since the beginning of his first term. While he
supports clean energy choices in principle, he noted that the opt-out structure — rather than opt-in — remains the aspect of the
program he finds most challenging, as he is concerned residents may not notice the change on their bills.
