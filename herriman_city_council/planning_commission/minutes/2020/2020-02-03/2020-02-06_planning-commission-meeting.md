The following are the minutes of the Planning Commission Work Meeting held on Thursday, February 6,
2020 at 6:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Community Center, 5355 W. Herriman Main Street (12090 South),
Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was posted in the Community Center, on
the City’s website, and delivered to members of the Commission and media.

Presiding: Chair Chris Berbert

Commission Members Present: Jackson Ferguson, Heather Garcia, Adam Jacobson, Lorin Palmer, Andy
Powell, Brody Rypien, Andrea Bradford

Commission Members Absent: Joy Kaseke

Staff Present: City Planner Michael Maloy, Assistant City Planner Bryn MacDonald, Planner Nick Whittaker,
Deputy Recorder Wendy Thorpe, HPD Lieutenant Brian Weidmer & HPD Officer Marcus Beckstead



Chair Chris Berbert called the work meeting to order at 6:05 pm.

Assistant City Planner MacDonald informed the Commissioners that staff PowerPoint presentations would be
included in all future packets. Packets would be posted one week before the meeting, to allow Commissioners
time to review the material.

Assistant City Planner MacDonald reviewed potential text changes to the Land Development Code, as
suggested previously by Commissioners. She requested feedback to prioritize them. Commissioner Bradford
suggested that in addition to reviewing residential and multifamily design standards that commercial and retail
be updated as well. Commissioners suggested that single-family residential design standards were too strict,
might increase building costs and should be updated to reflect changing architectural styles. Commissioners
offered that multifamily design standards were in need of more guidelines to improve the appearance and
quality.

City Planner Michael Maloy mentioned a Legislative bill being considered that would curb cities ability to
regulate design standards, and stated he would keep them informed of the progression of the Bill. He
proposed changing from gross to net standards for density calculations. Commissioners requested that the
total number of units be adjusted if schools or churches were added to a development. Assistant City Planner
MacDonald added that current design standards addressed that issue.

Commissioners proposed lowering Planned Community Development Zone on the priority list or completely
removing it. City Planner Maloy stated such a zone would be best for large areas with 100+ acres and may
have high consulting fees to implement. He offered to research the cost of the framework and determine if any
potential development areas would benefit from it.

Open space calculations were vested under the 2008 Ordinance. Commissioners requested clarification as to
whether park strips were included in open space calculations. Commissioners encouraged that parks be located
within a quarter mile of every residential unit.

Commissioners stressed the importance of curb, gutter and sidewalk inclusion in the early phasing of
developments.

Accessory building requirements should be reviewed and additional off street parking for Mother-In-Law
apartments was necessary. Creating an avenue for legal, safe, affordable housing would help the City meet the
State affordable housing mandate.

Commercial zones should be defined with more specific titles such as recreation areas, office professional or
assisted living either with the General Plan or Zoning designation. Big box store trends should be researched
to determine viability. Some cities require them to provide future plans for the locations, should the store
close.

Residential treatment facilities were allowed in all zones, but approval by the Commission was required.
Commissioners proposed underground, hidden parking to encourage the community feel. Safer crossing paths
in parking lots should be required.

Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District (JVWCD) water wise landscaping requirements for contract renewal
would require changes to the City landscaping requirements.

Commercial vehicle parking allowances were reviewed. They must be behind a fence, but multiple commercial
vehicles were not allowed. Potential issues needed to be reviewed by the Code Enforcement Department.



FFKR Susie Petheram reviewed the General Plan Community Survey Fall 2019 results.
Five Findings to remember were:
     A majority of residents report currently living in a single-family home with some variance in yard size,
        and most residents expect to be living in the same type of home in 10 years.
     About half of residents say the type of housing they will need in 10 years is available in Herriman now.
        Of those who say it is not available now, 9/10 would like to see it available in 10 years.
     50% of residents see restaurants as the most desirable gathering place in Herriman. Most residents
        think commercial developments would not fit well in their neighborhood.
     8/10 residents prefer using a car for transportation, but almost half would consider using bus/rail if it
        had a park-and-ride lot or were within a 5-10 minute walk of their house.
     80% of residents express interest in having a farmers’ market in Herriman, and 55% express interest in
        a food festival.
