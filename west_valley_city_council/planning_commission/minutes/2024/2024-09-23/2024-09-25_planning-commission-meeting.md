City staff is proposing a zone text change to modify Section 7-6-301, add Sections 7-10-400P
through 7-10-402, and remove Sections 7-14-100P through 7-14-104. These changes would remove
the Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone and add development standards for properties along Decker
Lake Drive between 3100 South and 3500 South. The reasons for the removal of the Decker Lake
Station Overlay Zone are outlined below.

Reasons to Remove the Overlay Zone

The Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone was first adopted in 1996 as the Commercial Overlay Zone.
At the time, the intended overlay zone area was mostly undeveloped. In the years that followed, the
Maverik Center, hotels, restaurants and other uses were built. Today, the targeted overlay zone area
is largely developed.

The Overlay Zone was originally intended to cover the area between 3100 South and 3500 South and
from I-215 to approximately 2080 West. The Overlay Zone was expanded in 2002 to include
property north of 3100 South and then reduced in 2006 and 2020 to exclude new residential
developments. However, as previously discussed with the Planning Commission, staff recently
learned that the area south of 3100 South was never officially zoned by ordinance as being within
the Overlay Zone. As a result, the Overlay Zone currently affects only 5 parcels north of 3100 South.
These 5 parcels are the subject of a companion application, Z-8-2024, wherein staff is
recommending that the Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone be removed from these parcels.

In addition to removing the Overlay Zone from the subject parcels in Z-8-2024, four of the five
parcels are proposed to be rezoned from the M (Manufacturing) zone to the C-2 (General
Commercial) zone. Doing so replaces many of the use restrictions of the Overlay Zone.

In 2023, the Utah legislature required municipalities with light rail stations to adopt station area
plans for the one-half mile radius from each light rail station. The legislature also required
municipalities to adopt land use regulations to implement each station area plan. The station area
plans and associated land use regulations must be adopted by the end of 2025. The City is currently
working with a consultant team hired by Wasatch Front Regional Council to create station area plans
for the River Trail, Redwood Junction, and Decker Lake light rail stations. Once the plans are
adopted, the City will consider land use regulations to implement these plans. In other words, any
future land use regulations adopted to implement the station area plan for Decker Lake light rail
station area will take the place of the Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone.

Adding Sections 7-10-400P through 7-10-402

There are certain streetscape type standards in the Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone that staff is
recommending moving to Chapter 7-10 of the zoning ordinance. These standards are outlined below.
    1. For Buildings less than 10,000 square feet, no parking shall be allowed between the Street
       and the Building.
    2. A three-foot-tall berm shall be required within the Landscaping along Decker Lake Drive.
    3. On Decker Lake Drive, Bradford Pear trees of a minimum size of two-inch caliper shall be
       planted within the Front Yard Landscaping an average of 30 feet apart. A different Street tree
       may be used to emphasize intersections.
    4. Stamped concrete shall be required for all driveway approaches and where public sidewalks
       cross driveways.
    5. The park strip along Decker Lake Drive shall be six feet wide. The Sidewalk along Decker
       Lake Drive shall be eight feet wide. Sites that do not meet these standards shall be brought
       into compliance when any development occurs to which Section 7-2-117 is applicable.

Request:               A zone text change to modify Section 7-6-301 to allow tattoo establishments
                       as a permitted use in the BRP, MXD, CC, C-1,
                       C-2, C-3, and LI zones and to remove the prohibition on tattoo establishments
                       in all overlay zones.

At the direction of the City Council, City staff is proposing a zone text change to modify Section 7-
6-301 to allow tattoo establishments as a permitted use in the BRP, MXD, CC, C-1,
C-2, C-3, and LI zones and to remove the prohibition on tattoo establishments in all overlay zones.
Currently, tattoo establishments are only allowed in the Manufacturing (M) zone where there is no
overlay zone. Permanent cosmetics are allowed in the Mixed Use (MXD), City Center (CC),
Neighborhood Commercial (C-1), General Commercial (C-2), Transitional Commercial (C-3), and
Manufacturing (M) zones.

The following definitions are helpful background information from Section 7-1-103:
    “Tattoo Establishment” means any location, place, area, Structure, or business used for the
       practice or instruction of Tattooing or Body Piercing, excluding Permanent Cosmetics
       Establishments.
    “Tattoo” means a mark or design made on or under the skin by a process of pricking or
       ingraining an indelible pigment, dye, or ink in the skin, excluding Permanent Cosmetics.
    “Body Piercing” means the act of piercing any part of the body of an individual, excepting
       the ears, for the purpose of allowing the insertion of earrings, jewelry, or similar objects into
       the body.
    “Permanent Cosmetics” means a mark or design made on or under the skin by a process of
       pricking or ingraining an indelible pigment, dye, or ink in the skin for masking discolorations
       or cosmetically enhancing facial features which shall follow the natural line of the feature.
       “Permanent Cosmetics” are limited to eyeliner, eyebrows, and lip coloring procedures.

There are currently two locations in West Valley that are licensed as tattoo establishments: 1743 W
Alexander St and 2346 S Redwood Road. Both locations are zoned Manufacturing (M).

In December of 1997, the City Council adopted Ordinance 97-65 that made tattoo establishments a
conditional use in the C-3 zone and a permitted use in M zone. In March of 2017, the City Council
adopted Ordinance 17-10 that removed tattoo establishments as a conditional use in the C-3 zone. It
is unclear how tattoo establishments were handled prior to December of 1997.

ARK Plaza Auto Service Amendment
1927 West 3600 South
C-2 Zone (1.03 Acres)

The following item was continued from the September 11, 2024 hearing to allow time for the
applicant to address the proposed tenant mix and parking requirements, including the auto service
uses and service bays.

The applicant, Rasoul Gassmepour representing ARK Plaza, is requesting a conditional use
amendment to add auto service uses to the existing multi-tenant commercial/warehouse building
located at 1927 West 3600 South. This property is zoned C-2, General Commercial, and the West
Valley City General Plan designates this area as General Commercial. Surrounding zones include A
(agricultural) and C-2 to the north, A (agricultural) to the west, C-2 to the east, and RM to the south.
Surrounding uses include residential and medical office (Planned Parenthood) to the north, multi-
family residential to the west and south (Homestead Farms) and office/warehouse to the east
(Interwest Brokerage).

This site is Lot 8 of the Plaza 3600 Business Park which was approved by the Planning Commission
in 1998 (C-26-98). Access to the site is gained off of 3600 South, which is a private road. The
original approval specified that each development must be reviewed as a separate conditional use
before the Planning Commission since the tenants were not known at the time of the overall
approval.

This site development was approved by the Planning Commission on March 23, 2022 under C-5-
2022. The building that was constructed contains 11 units. Now that the construction of the project is
complete Mr. Gassmepour is ready for tenants to occupy the building and is requesting to expand the
type uses allowed to include Auto Service. That modification requires an amendment to the original
approval.

CA-7-2024
Higgins Body and Paint Addition
3899 S Redwood Rd
M Zone (4.52 acres)

Gerald Higgins is requesting conditional use approval for the expansion of an automobile service
use. The property is zoned C-2, General Commercial, and the West Valley City General Plan
designates this area as mixed use. The surrounding zoning is C-2 to the north and south, R-1-8 to the
east, and RM to the north. Adjacent land uses include auto sales to the north and south and multi-unit
dwelling residential to the east and north.

The applicant has been operating Higgins Auto Body and Paint on the property since 2016, which is
an automobile service use. The property was originally used for automobile sales and service as the
Henry Day Ford dealership, which City business license records indicate began operating in 1969. In
1993 Henry Day Ford received conditional use approval to build a new auto sales and service
building on the site (ZC 19920033). That is the same building that the applicant is operating out of
today. When Higgins was licensed in 2016 some interior and exterior improvements were made to
the building, but no other site changes occurred. There are existing residential developments on the
north and east sides of the subject parcel that came in after the Henry Day Ford building was
constructed in 1993. The Lakeside Apartment development to the east was approved in 1996 (ZPR


                     West Valley City Planning Commission Public Hearing Minutes

19960003), and the Truong Townhome Development to the north was approved in 2012 (ZPUD
20120002).

Currently, the applicant is proposing to do a 14,500 sq. ft. addition on the back of the roughly 28,000
sq. ft. existing building. The addition will accommodate additional service bays and paint booths.
Since they are proposing to expand their auto service use, and the property is adjacent to residential
uses, a conditional use application is required. The applicant has indicated that the addition will
match the existing building height and building materials. The Fire Department and Building
Inspections Division have concerns related to access behind the building and proximity to the rear
property line. The applicant may need to make changes to the current layout of the building on the
site to meet the requirements of these departments.

The proposed addition triggers a few different requirements in the code. First, there is currently no
masonry wall adjacent to the residential properties. 7-6-303(5)(d) requires that a 6’ tall masonry wall
be installed on an existing/developed commercial site in conjunction of the issuance of a new
Conditional Use Permit. On the north side, the townhome site sits quite a bit higher and there is
already a concrete retaining wall running along most of the property line, with a 6’ vinyl fence on
top of that. Requiring a 6’ masonry wall on the north edge may not serve much of a purpose. To the
east, next to the apartments, there is currently only a chain link fence with slats separating the
properties. A 6’ wall there will provide a better separation between the commercial and the
residential uses.

The large use expansion also triggers the 7-10-103(1) High Image Arterial requirements for
Redwood Road. The applicant’s submittal includes a plan showing the Redwood Road Streetscape
cross section, which will change the frontage to have 15’ of landscaping from the curb, a 10’
sidewalk, and a 5’ landscaped buffer behind that. All other provisions for the landscaping along the
frontage in 7-10-103 also need to be met.

Code section 7-6-303(6) requires that all other landscaping requirements on the site be brought into
compliance when a building expansion increases the floor area by more than 30%. Current
landscaping requirements out of compliance on the site are:

• 15% landscaping for C-2 Zone. This site would only need 13.5% landscaping since the Redwood
Road Streetscape will be installed.

• 10’ of landscaping adjacent to residential properties (north and east).

For the 1992 conditional use application, Henry Day Ford asked for a landscaping reduction from
15% to 4%. There was no landscaping along Redwood Road, and they proposed to add 20’ of
landscaping along the frontage. Staff at the time felt that was sufficient, and the Planning
Commission approved the reduction. Since this is a new application, and the code requirements have
been updated since 1992, the landscaping on the site is being reevaluated.

The overall site is 4.52 acres. 13.5% landscaping for the site comes to 26,580 sq. ft. That would be a
large amount of landscaping to add to an existing site. If 10’ of landscaping is added along the north

about 7% landscaping. Both adjacent residential properties have landscaping and trees lining the
property lines. The Planning Commission may choose to honor the 1992 landscaping reduction or
choose that the landscaping be brought into compliance with current requirements.

The site needs to accommodate the required customer and employee parking for the building
addition and existing building areas and uses outlined in 7-9-104. Also, C-2 zoning does not allow

24 hours. Section 7-7-104 of the code allows for the parking of vehicles outside that are waiting to
be serviced to be left for longer than 24 hours in commercial zones, so long as the vehicles are
screened behind a building, and the vehicle has no visible exterior damage. Hours for the automobile
service use shall only be allowed from 8 a.m. to 7 p.m.

The applicant has not provided plans for any new signage on the property, but any future sign
changes on the property shall comply with Title 11.
