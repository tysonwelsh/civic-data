DISCUSSION – Fraud Risk Assessment



Mr. Nelson stated this is a requirement of the State Auditor’s office that is required to be brought
before the council each year. He reviewed each area and identified where in either policies,
procedures or ordinances that the city has something already in place and is followed in relation
to each one of those items and any areas that the city needs to work on. He indicated there were
three areas they can address that can result in a lower rating which is their ultimate goal. He
continued to discuss those items which are as follows:



1) Have all governing body members’ complete entity specific online training at
training.auditor.utah.gov within four years of term appointment/election day. Mr. Nelson
indicated they will provide the council with the link and the council will need to take that
training and provide the certificate of completion to the City.



2) All employees and elected officials required to annually commit in writing to abide by a
statement of ethical behavior? Mr. Nelson indicated he felt this was something they could
definitely do and over the next fiscal year be looking at ways to have that in place.



3) Does the entity have a formal internal audit function? Mr. Nelson stated this requires the city
to have a certain employee whose only responsibility is to in essence continually audit the
functions of the city and they report directly to city management and or to in our case would be
either the audit committee or the whole council. He stated he does not feel this is a necessary
element and will find that in most cities this is not done. The City usually hires an independent
audit firm to come in and do one of these internal audits on a regular basis.



General discussion ensued regarding the need for some of the regulations and if some of them
are really necessary and would make that big of a difference. Clarification as to other companies
and the city that does training on a regular basis and if nothing else it makes you think about
things a little more. Further discussion continued regarding the definition of a formal audit
function versus the audit functions of the city finance department.



Mr. Nelson stated this assessment started with small Special Service Districts and smaller towns
and cities because they didn't have any idea what made up a good internal control structure. The
State Auditor's office as they worked with those entities created this and it grew from there. He
feels it is a good idea for any board or elected body to at least have an understanding and have
the people who are in charge of finance express to them “yes we are meeting those requirements”
and send the information to the State Auditor’s Office. This is just to make them aware of where
the city is and their intent in the future to address some of these issues.




DISCUSSION – Utah State Retirement Contributions Amendment



Mrs. Rugg stated there are 2 tiers in the Utah State Retirement System. Most new employees are
tier 2; those hired before July 1, 2011 are in tier 1. With USR making changes to enhance the
tier 2 specifically for Public Safety and Fire, it required employees to pick up a portion of the
contribution which was 2.27%. At the time the city had built that difference into the budget in
order for the city to cover those costs instead of the employee. In 2022 the increase to the
employee piece went up to 2.59%. What the city is proposing is that they also pick up that
additional percent and the retirement systems require this be done by Resolution. This addition
has already been placed in the upcoming budget so this is just formalizing that addition.

DISSCUSSION – Parking Proximity Ordinance



Mr. Earl stated he was approached by Council members Millett and Lauret regarding a new
ordinance requiring a parking or residential developments which are developed with attached
residential unit’s not single-family homes. The idea was to consider a policy that requires
parking stalls within a certain distance of the unit that they serve as well as requiring an
assignment of parking stalls for the exclusive use of individual residential units. He felt the
concern was trying to enhance safety for residents of dwellings, with the idea that the less
distance they had to walk particularly late at night the safer they're likely to feel and making it a
little bit easier for those people with mobility issues. They have come up with a draft language
to be included in the city’s general parking ordinance that created two additional proposed
subsections. The first one requires an assignment of essentially two parking stalls for each
residential unit. This would be for their exclusive use and would have some official designation.
The second part is the proximity aspect of it requiring that parking be located within 175 feet of
the unit that it serves. This is merely to get the discussion moving and see if this is something
the council would like to address or not.



 Mr. Clark responded that he has some concerns due to a lot of developments that will center a
green space around the buildings or a clubhouse. He feels that one of the consequences is this
may force developers to surround the buildings with parking lots on all four sides so then you're
going to have your green spaces away from the buildings. He feels you would not be creating an
enjoyable development. They are going to be isolated or they are going to have to cross parking
lots to get to the play areas. Another potential unintended consequence is it takes away a little
bit of flexibility from an apartment manager. An apartment manager that's rented out to one
person you only have one vehicle and if you're requiring two stalls to be assigned to that unit
you're effectively wasting a stall that nobody else can use. An apartment manager could have
flexibility to assign the use of these stalls out on a needed basis depending on the tenants. He
concluded he feels it would work with townhomes but stacked apartments make it more difficult.
There are definitely some pros to it yet there are also some cons.



Mr. Millett stated it was their intent to create security and safety and equal treatment for
apartment dwellers, the same as single family dwellings.



Mr. Macdonald asked if any other communities have something like this. Mr. Earl indicated they
have not looked into other cities regulations regarding this issue.



General discussion ensued regarding possible unintended circumstances, promoting better
planning from developers, nicer looking landscapes and what possibly can and cannot work.



Mr. Spencer suggested the city look into the issue a little further to see if there are unintended
circumstances and keep safety in mind.

ORDINANCE - Amending Chapter 23 of the Orem City Code for Storm Water Utility



Mr. Price indicated Chapter 23 of the Code of Ordinances, which addresses the Storm Water
Utility has been updated to clarify language, address deficiencies and to come into compliance
with new regulations. Basic changes include clarification of some definitions, fixing basic
grammatical errors, adding and updating hyperlinks, removing references to gender as it applies
to titles, changing employee titles, reformatting some areas to consolidate space, and delete some
sections that were unnecessarily repetitive. More extensive changes include adding a new $1,000
bond to ensure property owners continue to comply with storm water regulations after
construction is completed and before landscaping is installed. Requirements were also added to
the Long Term Storm Water Management Plan section to require the developer to complete a
questionnaire during the planning process to assist in the review of plans, and to provide a map
as part of final approval showing locations of site improvements designed to control and treat
storm water. Finally, a retrofitting plan will require redeveloped properties larger than one acre as
well as established properties that adversely impact storm water quality, to come into compliance
with measures to manage and protect storm water.

       ORDINANCE / PUBLIC HEARING – Amending Fiscal Year 2021-2022 Budget



Mr. Nelson stated the Fiscal Year 2021-2022 City of Orem budget has many adjustments that
occur throughout the fiscal year. These adjustments include grants received from federal, state,
and other governmental or private entities or organizations; appropriating reserves in the General
Fund and transferring them to the Capital Projects Fund in order to meet state legal compliance
requirements regarding maximum General Fund balance as well as providing various project
funding; and Senior Citizen Fund partially funding a new bus due to price increases.



Mayor Young opened the public hearing at 6:44 pm.

Hearing no comments, the Mayor closed the public hearing and brought the item back to the
Council for further discussion.



Mrs. Millett moved to approve the Ordinance Amending Fiscal Year 2021-2022 Budget as
proposed. Mr. Macdonald seconded the motion. Those voting aye: David A. Young, David
Spencer, Debby Lauret, Tom Macdonald, Jeff Lambson, Terry Peterson and LaNae Millett. The
motion passed.
