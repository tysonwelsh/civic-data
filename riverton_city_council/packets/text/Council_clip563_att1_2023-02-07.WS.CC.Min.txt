41            Ordinance No. 23-03 - Wright-Parkside Rezone, Proposed Rezone Of
42            Approximately 6.1 Acres, 12995 S. Sand Hollow Drive, from RM-14-SD-EHOV to
43            RM-14-SD, removing the Elderly Housing Overlay, Â Applicant- Wright Homes
44
45   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained this was a public hearing for a
46   Rezone application brought by Wright Homes seeking to rezone an existing development
47   consisting of approximately 6.1-acres located at 12995 South Sand Hollow Drive. The subject
48   property is a triangular parcel located south of 12600 South near 4245 West and is also known
49   as “Parkside Phase 3”. It is located south of the Rocky Mountain Power Corridor and just east
50   of the Welby-Jacob Canal.
51
     Page | 6 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     February 7, 2023




 1   Mr. Lethbridge said the subject property is currently zoned RM-14-SD-EHOV which means
 2   Residential Multi-Family, 14-units per acre. SD is an abbreviation for “Specific Development”
 3   meaning the Rezone Ordinance had additional zoning requirements which are specific to the
 4   property. EHOV is an abbreviation for “Elderly Housing Overlay” which gives some allowances
 5   and some additional requirements related to providing an age-restricted (55 and older) housing
 6   development. The applicant is requesting the Elderly Housing Overlay Zone be removed.
 7
 8   Derek Wright, Wright Homes, explained that they are asking for the elderly housing overlay to
 9   be removed. They are not trying to change the structures or the plat.
10
11   Councilmember Pierucci asked what the analysis is on traffic if they remove the elderly housing
12   overlay. Mr. Wright presented the traffic study to the Council.
13
14   Councilmember Buroker asked if the elderly housing overlay remained, would the developer
15   make changes to what has already been approved.
16
17   Mayor Staggs called for public comments;
18
19   Robert Woods said he was opposed to the removal of the overlay. He was disappointed that
20   Doug Young was able to convince the City Council that the elderly overlay would be okay for
21   high density.
22
23   Sarah Nelson said she lives in the Parkside community where the streets area very narrow.
24   The probability of more traffic is enormous. This development would likely delay the Parkside
25   community HOA. She believes the higher density will drain public resources including schools.
26
27   Justen Smith said he also lives in Parkside and believes it makes sense to remove the elderly
28   housing overlay. He would like the application to be approved.
29
30   Manuel Palacios said he lives in Parkside. He does not have an issue with removing the elderly
31   overlay and would like more families to have the opportunity to move into this community.
32
33   Shirley Van Vliet said she was opposed to removing the elderly housing overlay because the
34   streets are too narrow and there would also be a water supply issue.
35
36   Daniel Rudd said he is in the middle with his feelings about removing or keeping the elderly
37   housing overlay. He said that removing the elderly housing overlay could cause more traffic
38   and would like the City to install speed bumps. However, he is in favor of affordable housing.
39   He would like the developer to invest in the community by adding additional amenities.
40
41   Daniel Horne said he lives west of the zoning area. He believes there is value in leaving the
42   elderly housing overlay and his biggest concern is the number of vehicles.
43
44   Alex Reese said he’s been to several meetings and is glad that the City Council listens to
45   citizens. He believes traffic will increase if the elderly housing overlay is removed.
46
47   Mr. Wright explained that Dutchman Lane will connect to the east according to the City’s master
48   plan. This connection to Dutchman would greatly benefit this community as a whole. He
49   explained this project is over-parked meaning each unit has a two-car garage and a driveway.
50   They implemented a parking ordinance that had to do with these traffic challenges.
51   Mayor Staggs noted there were multiple email comments from residents that were received by
     Page | 7 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   the Council and by staff. These comments are included in the packet.
 2
 3   Mayor Staggs called for additional public comments; there being none, he closed the Public
 4   Hearing.
 5
 6   Mayor Staggs said he doesn’t understand how the plat was approved in 2017 or how buildings
 7   were constructed that are not conducive to elderly housing living. In response, Mr. Lethbridge
 8   said there was a lot of discussion when this was presented for rezone in 2017. The text of the
 9   ordinance that was adopted stated “assisted living congregate care” for this area. This
10   language was not ignored when the site plan came forward. The Planning Commission
11   discussed this and struggled with this language needing to be more specific. These units were
12   approved, allowing for a living area for elderly and their caretakers. In retrospect, staff should
13   have been more specific in the language approved in this ordinance.
14
15   Councilmember Buroker asked about the potential connection to the main road. Mr. Lethbridge
16   said there could potentially be connections however these are private right-of-way areas. This
17   would require agreements with these property owners.
18
19   Councilmember McCay asked if the site plan approval included three-story buildings. Mr.
20   Lethbridge said the site plan approval included building type and height. If there are any
21   changes to the site plan, they would need to ask for an amended site plan approval.
22
23   Councilmember McCay expressed frustration that a three-story building was approved for an
24   elderly housing overlay. She said she would only agree to the removal of the elderly housing
25   overlay if the height of the buildings was decreased.
26
27   Councilmember Wells said he opposed having an elderly overlay on a three-story building and
28   was also frustrated that the site plan was approved with this building height.
29
30   Councilmember McDougal explained that he was part of the group that opposed this
31   development in 2017. When it was approved, the code needed to be more to exclude building a
32   three-story unit. He said it was embarrassing that Councilmembers want to go back on
33   decisions that were made in the past. He believes the environment has changed in the City.
34   Since 2017, zoning plans have been put in place that provides a good plan for the City. There
35   are numerous elderly housing developments, however there is a shortfall in affordable housing
36   and the state is pressuring cities to provide affordable housing. He can see the advantage of
37   pulling the elderly housing overlay and he does not think the Council can go back on a past
38   agreement with the developer.
39
40   Councilmember Pierucci said he lives in a community similar to this area. He shares frustration
41   with the discrepancies in the approved site plan and also shares Councilmember McDougal’s
42   concerns with honoring legal agreements currently in place. He worries about the impact of
43   partially finished townhomes if the elderly housing overlay is not removed.
44
45   Councilmember McCay said she was concerned about the Council’s reputation to the residents
46   because residents in this area bought their homes expecting there would not be three-story
47   buildings behind them. She doesn’t want one party to be a loser in this situation and would like
48   to compromise.
49
50   Councilmember Buroker said she would not support asking a developer to tear something
51   down.
