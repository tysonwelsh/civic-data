PPLICANT’S PRESENTATION 
Jim Charlier said he is the transportation director on the project.  He is here to talk 
about the mobility outcomes of the proposed plan.  They have paid a lot of attention to 
the Kimball Junction Neighborhood Plan and the Snyderville Basin Master Plan.  He said 
that design principles were laid out in these plans: however, the only specific 
qualitative criteria they need to respond to is the intersection levels of service and 
minimum surface parking.   
 
Mr. Charlier said they are thinking in terms of creating a transit ready development.  
Their goal is that people should be able to walk from their homes for most of their 
errands.  The ability to do this is called the “15 minute neighborhood.”  This does not 
include going to work.  He said this can only be achieved if the land uses are mixed.   
 
Mr. Charlier said an ideal neighborhood will have good emergency access and no long 
dead-end roads.  He described the concept of a “walk score.”  In their developments 
they try to reach a walk score of 70 or above.  He said he lives in an area in Denver that 
has walk score of 82.  He has the option of three grocery stores he can walk to, along 
with restaurants, a church, and coffee shops.  When the walk score is above 70, people 
don’t drive nearly as much.  The bottom line for the Kimball Junction Neighborhood 
Plan is to achieve less per-capita driving and affordable living, including housing for the 
“missing middle.”

August 11, 2020

Mr. Charlier said HUD developed a housing/transportation index.  It recognizes that 
households that spend more than 45% of their income on housing and transportation 
are in financial distress.  The ideal is 30% or less.  He said that over 15,000 miles of 
driving is considered less than ideal.   
 
Mr. Charlier said he used Summit County metrics to determine what is happening today 
in the Snyderville Basin.  He plugged in a few existing subdivisions to determine how 
well they meet the desired requirements.  A subdivision to the north had a 
housing/transportation index of 65.  That is well into the trouble range.  It had a walk 
score of zero.  He said he has never seen a walk score of zero.   
 
Mr. Charlier said a condominium development was also reviewed.  The walk score was 
11.  It had an easy walk to transit, but the intersection rating was not good.  Those living 
in this project had a less-than 45% housing-transportation index.  Two other 
neighborhoods were reviewed.  Grocery stores for the developments were over 1 mile 
away.  This is a greater distance than most people will walk to.   
 
Mr. Charlier compared these attributes to the proposed project.  He estimates the walk 
score for this project will be between 80 and 90.  There will be three groceries stores 
within 6/10 of a mile.  The walk to transit will be five minutes or less.  There will be 
four external connections, no dead-end roads, and fairly good intersection density.   
 
 He said they have established traditional neighborhood street patterns, as envisioned 
by the Kimball Junction Neighborhood plan.  Narrow streets will curtail the speed at 
which people drive.  There will be centralized parking.  The parking footprint will be 
reduced dramatically from the previous entitlement.  As they move through the process, 
there will be more work to do with the County.  He said they are aware of, and will 
comply with, the Snyderville Basin parking criteria found in the Code.

Mr. Charlier next addressed the traffic.  The traffic study has been updated.  These 
factors were included in the revised development plan.  He explained how the factors 
were figured in to reach the trip generation statistics.  He said that previously, they 
showed a 40% reduction in peak hour traffic.  Later, Staff requested another method of 
computing the traffic is used.  Using Staff’s recommended method, they show a 30% 
reduction in peak hour traffic.  This is a reduction from the Research Park approval. 
 
Mr. Charlier said they are moving from a development project that generated a lot of 
office trips to residential trips.  Office trips tend to put traffic on the road during the 
peak traffic times, while residential trips are more spread out.  Noon-hour trips 
approximate the morning peaks.  
 
A graphic was shared that compared the differences in the traffic patterns between the 
existing entitlement and their proposal.  This project will generate approximately 30% 
less traffic during the AM peak hours and about 26% less during the PM peak hours.  He 
said because this is a residential area, the daily traffic actually goes up about 50%, but 
this is spread out throughout the day.   
 
Mr. Charlier said if the Tech Center had been built, very few of the employees could 
afford to live here.  Most would commute from the Salt Lake valley.  With the Dakota 
Pacific project, the average trip length is much shorter.  There will be a dramatic change 
in the amount of driving at the household level.  West Kimball Junction will decrease 
from the current 70 miles per day to 39 miles per day, as compared to the Research 
Park uses.   
 
Mr. Charlier said it is certain there will be residential growth within Summit County.  
The Commission should consider where they would like residential growth to take 
place.  He urged the Commission to take a holistic look at what the traffic response will 
be to these subdivisions.

Mr. Charlier said Dakota Pacific will be adding an intersection.  He said that the level of 
service of most intersections in the Snyderville Basin is pretty good, with two 
exceptions.  Those exceptions are the Highway 224/I-80 ramps and Ute Blvd/Highway 
224.  The ramps are currently performing at a “D” grade, which is acceptable by UDOT 
criteria.  The congestion at the ramps is usually caused by the traffic at the Highway 
224/Ute Blvd.    
 
Mr. Charlier showed graphics that demonstrated the level of service in 2028 at these 
intersections.  This graph is without the project.  He said the 224 ramps go from D to E.  
The Ute Blvd. intersection goes from E to F.  He noted that this is a problem even 
without the Dakota Pacific project.  With the proposal, there is a drop in a letter grade at 
the Landmark/Tech Center intersection.  They have developed mitigations that will 
bring this back to a rating of C.   
 
He said that Staff also requested they look at the intersection at Kilby Road by 
Powderwood.  Today, that intersection is at B and C.  With their project it would be at a 
C grade in 2028.  It could still operate at as a stop sign intersection.
