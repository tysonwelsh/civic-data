4.         A resolution authorizing BYU Police to apply to Public Interest Use certain bicycles
           qualifying as lost or mislaid property or property no longer needed for evidence (24-053)
           0:49:46

     Motion:       An implied motion to approve Resolution 2024-33, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Mr. Jones, presented. He explained that state laws dictate how law enforcement agencies must handle
property that is lost, abandoned, or no longer needed as evidence. Typically, such property is auctioned
off, and the proceeds benefit the city. However, there is an exception that allows agencies, with City
Council approval, to donate property for public interest. The Provo Police already donates unclaimed

now requesting the City Council's permission to donate these bicycles to the Provo Bike Collective.

8.         A public hearing regarding the annual disclosure of funds transferred from the utility
           enterprise funds to other funds for the upcoming budget (24-007) 1:08:51

Ms. Zarbock provided details on the public hearing required by state code, which outlines the annual
transfers from utility enterprise funds (energy, water, wastewater, sanitation, and storm drain) to other
city areas. She explained that these transfers fall into five categories. First, there is a 10% transfer from
utility revenues to the general fund, amounting to $12.9 million in the proposed fiscal year 2025 budget.
This serves as a dividend to taxpayers, helping fund essential city services like police and fire, and
keeping property taxes lower. Second, since 2013, 1% of utility revenues has been allocated for road
maintenance, with $1.3 million proposed for the next fiscal year. Third, utility funds cover a portion of
citywide administrative costs, including legal, HR, and IT services, with a proposed $1.7 million for 2025.
Fourth, the utility funds contribute to the Provo 360 software system, which supports citywide
operations. Finally, wastewater funds transfer some money to the water fund to cover services
managed by the water department.

Chair MacKay opened the public comment period. With no public comments, she invited a council
discussion.

Councilor Hoban emphasized that the council has carefully reviewed the budget items and takes their
significance very seriously.

10.        An ordinance amending the Tentative Budget for Provo City for the Fiscal Year beginning
           July 1, 2024 and ending June 30, 2025, and setting a Public Hearing to consider a proposed
           change in the Library Certified Tax Rate (24-007) 1:18:24

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2024-33, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Ms. Zarbock presented. She explained that the council had shown interest in considering a property tax
increase to fund library operations. The ordinance under discussion addresses three key points. First, it
establishes the tentative budget presented by the mayor on May 14 as a temporary budget to cover the
period from July 1 until the final budget is adopted in August, following the truth in taxation process.
Second, the ordinance initiates this process, with the council considering a maximum increase of
$450,000 for the library fund, which was discussed during the last work meeting. Third, the ordinance
would adopt the certified tax rates for general operations and debt service as provided by the county,
with no increases. However, if the council proceeds with the truth in taxation, a different rate for the
library fund would be submitted to the county and communicated to residents ahead of the August
public hearing, scheduled for August 13. She stated the final budget would then be adopted on August
20.

Mr. Jones clarified that there is no current proposal to cut library funding. Instead, the library board has
requested an increase in library funding by raising the specific property tax rate for the library. The
decision on whether to implement this tax increase is not being made tonight. The decision being made
tonight is whether to consider the possibility of this increase in the future. He added that state law
requires that by June 22, the council must either approve the budget with no tax increases or decide to
propose a public hearing in August, where the tax rate could be discussed and potentially adjusted. The
ordinance on the table tonight, if passed, would set the stage for an August public hearing to consider
increasing the library's tax rate. However, the council cannot raise the rate higher than what is decided
tonight. To avoid discussing a tax increase altogether, the council would need to make changes to the
budget ordinance during this meeting. As the ordinance is currently written, it already schedules the
necessary steps for considering the tax rate adjustment in August.
