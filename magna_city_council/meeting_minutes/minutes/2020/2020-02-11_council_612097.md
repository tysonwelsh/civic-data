Ordinance

            THIS BEING THE TIME heretofore set for a public hearing to receive public
comment regarding the following request:

              Paul Ashton reviewed the following amendment to the Magna Metro Township’s
Code of Ordinances:

Ordinance 20-01-02 amending Title 5: “Business Licenses and regulations”, of the Magna
Municipal Code of Ordinances by adding public nuisance, crime-magnet, and general welfare
justifications for revoking a business license and providing greater due process for business
license suspensions and revocations.

               Council Member Prokopis, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to open the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

               Rick Newsmeyer asked if these changes were going to increase the cost of his
business license.

               Council Member Peel stated the changes do not include a fee increase.

                Council Member Pierce asked what was wrong with the due process already
written into the ordinance and if the ordinance identified what a public nuisance was. She worried
this would affect certain types of businesses already in place.

               Mr. Ashton stated a public nuisance is spelled out in statute. The new ordinance
lays out a process for proving that a business is a nuisance or a crime-magnet. It would enable
the Magna Metro Township to suspend or revoke a license, and it would be a tool for law
enforcement. He thought it added more protection for the property owner too.

               Greg Schulz stated the current ordinance is designed to protect the rights of the
business owner first, so it is difficult to revoke a business license even when the business is
violating ordinance or state law.

              Council Member Pierce stated she thought the wording in the ordinance came
across as the Council adding more bureaucracy to what it already does.

                Mr. Ashton stated government has a role to keep the community safe and secure,
but the existing ordinance is it is too vague and leaves too much discretion. If a business starts
to interfere with other people’s rights, the Magna Metro Township should have a right and an
obligation to stop it.

             Council Member Hull asked if this ordinance was already in place in other
communities or municipalities.

                Mr. Schulz stated this ordinance is a rewrite of an ordinance in use in Kearns.

              Council Member Prokopis asked how this came about and whether it was
because police needed more teeth to do their job.

                Mr. Ashton stated this was a recommendation from the land use attorneys.

               Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to close the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

                Council Member Pierce stated she would like to review the ordinance before
voting on it.

               Council Member Prokopis asked if a business could automatically renew its
business license.

                Mr. Schulz stated yes.

              Council Member Prokopis asked who would determine if a business was a public
nuisance, crime-magnet, or had a general welfare issue.

               Mr. Schulz stated the Council would have to demonstrate that existed to an
administrative law judge who would then make a decision. Right now, to revoke a license there
is no administrative process, and some businesses have openly lied on their business license
applications.

               Council Member Peel stated he was in favor of this ordinance change. It cleans
up business licensing and gives it more teeth.

               Council Member Peel moved to approve the ordinance. The motion died due to
lack of a second.

               Council Member Prokopis stated the Council needed to have some ability to
clean up its community. He just wanted more time to look at this and compare the proposed
ordinance with the current ordinance to see what has changed.

               Council Member Hull, seconded by Council Member Prokopis, moved to continue
this discussion to the next meeting. The motion passed 4 to 1, with Council Member Peel
abstaining.

Magna in Motion

                 Kari Duckworth stated Magna in Motion had its first meeting of the year and talked
about focusing on pre-diabetes this year. It is hoping to set up a polling location at one of the
grocery stores to educate people of the difference between pre-diabetes and diabetes, and also
set up a seminar to teach people about pre-diabetes, and hold an 8 to 12 week course with
Exodus. This year’s spring challenge theme is “Focus on you 2020,” which will focus on health
and nutrition versus exercise. It will be an eight-week challenge in an effort to get more
participants. Magna in Motion is also taking on some roles with Magna United to do more with
the school program. It cannot deal directly with kids, but it can try to get parents to change their
lifestyle. Finally, Magna in Motion is taking on the 5k again this year, and she has gotten word
that there will be some funding for the race and that the same person who timed the race last year
will be doing it again this year. She will provide some flyers to hand out as these events get
closer.

Magna Coalition / Community that Cares Program

                Chandler Parkinson, Salt Lake County Youth Services, spoke on behalf of Kellen
Schalter, stating the key leader orientations for the Community that Cares model were completed
in January, and Ms. Schalter is now actively recruiting for the community board. She is also
preparing to attend a conference in Denver in April called the Blueprints Conference. Blueprints

Cares gets to the point of choosing programs to implement in Magna, Ms. Schalter will be able to

Reports from Municipal Service Providers

Unified Police Department (UPD)

                Chief Jason Ackerman reviewed the quarterly report for the end of last year. The
UPD started to more accurately track pooled services, and some of those statistics are in the
report. Past response times, general offenses, how many calls officers handled every month per
officer are broken out into individual communities. The report also includes maps of the types of
crimes and locations so the UPD can look for trends, Violent Crime Unit statistics specific to
Magna, and juvenile crime statistics.

                 Council Member Pierce stated the report does not show drugs, related call outs,
or citations, etc.

               Chief Ackerman stated he could provide the Council with citations, as those were
tracked. Then, the UPD belongs to a Drug Enforcement Administration Task Force and gets
bulletins showing specific drug cases. They are not updated in this quarterly report format, but
he could start providing drug-related investigations, search warrants, and things like that.

The UPD is also getting ready to start its Citizen’s Police Academy. It is an eight-to-eleven-week
course, which meets on Tuesday. Seats will be blocked out for all elected officials to be able to
attend. If someone is unable to make it on a Tuesday, but wants to learn about topics, he asked
that they get in touch with him and he would try to accommodate their schedule.
