 4.        Resolution 2021-25 appropriating $1,250,175 in the Legacy CIP Fund for the acquisition of
           land in Slate Canyon. (21-058)

       Motion:             An implied motion to adopt Resolution 2021-25 as currently constituted, has been made
                           by council rule.

Tara Riddle, Ombudsman and Property Manager, presented. This property consisted of two parcels that
totaled just over 29 acres. Acquisition of this property would help to preserve the foothills from
development. It was noted that the seller of this property approached Provo City. This property was of
interest to the City due to its proximity to the Bonneville Shoreline trail and Slate Canyon Trailhead. It
could also provide future open space with recreation uses.

Several years earlier the owner at the time proposed a development on this land, but it was determined
that about half of the proposed homes were located at an elevation that would be above the City’s
water elevation zone. Staff rejected the application, and the previous owner considered a smaller
development with homes below the water elevation zone, but ultimately, they determined the
improvements needed were too costly and they chose not to proceed. They then sold the property to
the current owner.

In November 2020, the property appraised for $930,000, and in April 2021, the seller had a new
appraisal that came in at $,2,270,000. The more recent appraisal included comparable properties that
were not similar. Ms. Riddle worked with the seller to negotiate an agreed upon purchase price of
$1,250,000.

The property was located in the Critical Hillside Overlay Zone and the intent was to rezone the property
as Open Space and Recreation Zone. To make the seller feel more comfortable with selling the property,
the City would agree to give the seller first right of refusal if the property is ever not used as a park.

Provo City Administration recommended the appropriation of $1,250,175 in the Legacy CIP Fund for the
acquisition of land near Slate Canyon. After the appropriation, the remaining unappropriated amount in
the Legacy CIP Fund would be approximately $429,000.

Vice Chair Shipley opened public comment.

Christina Davis, Provo, was grateful for the City’s bold stance on preserving parks and land. She was
joined by a group from Conserve Utah Valley. She thanked the Councilors for responding to her emails.

Ian Klosoviack, Provo, thought there should be discussions about what had happened to the property
Provo already owned in the area. There had been plans to make a park on that property, but it had not
happened yet due to a lack of funding. He thought there needed to be more public discussion about the
best use for the money.

 6.        An ordinance amending the Zone Map classification of approximately 23.5 acres of real
           property, generally located at 200 N Lakeshore Dr. from Agricultural (A1.10) and (A1.5) to
           Residential (R1.8) and Residential Agricultural (RA) Zone. Fort Utah Neighborhood.
           PLRZ20210049

       Motion:             An implied motion to adopt the ordinance as currently constituted, has been made by
                           council rule.

Brandon Larsen, Planner, presented. The applicant proposed R1.8 zoning for the 23.5-acre subject
property to develop 69 single-family lots and one lot was proposed to be zoned Residential Agricultural
(RA). The subject land had historically been used for agricultural use. The proposal aligned with the
Southwest Area Land Use Map and other planning policies.

There were three identified points of dedication for open space. It was noted that Parks and Recreation
did not want to be responsible for maintain the dedicated trail that was next to the river.

The neighborhood was supportive of the proposal and liked that it was all single-family homes. The
Planning Commission recommended approval and staff was supportive.

Councilor Handley pointed out a parcel to the northwest of the subject property, it had not been
annexed into Provo and he wanted to know if there were any plans to do so. He thought the parcel
would provide excellent open space. Mr. Larsen was unsure whether there were plans to annex this into
Provo but would look into it.

Councilor Fillmore wanted assurance the neighborhood would have access to the park through one of
the dedications.

Vice Chair Shipley invited the developer and neighborhood chair to speak.

The developer, Jason Bennett, spoke about the concept plan. He said they had proposed a fourplex
mansion home, but the neighborhood was completely opposed, even if the developer retained
ownership.

 7.        An ordinance amending the Zone Map classification of approximately .80 acres of real
           property, generally located at 690 E 500 S, from Residential Conservation (RC) to Medium
           Density Residential (MRD) zone. Maeser Neighborhood. (PLRZ20210086)

Brandon Larsen, Planner, presented. The 0.82-acre subject property was in the RC Zone in an area of
mixed uses. There were many residences in the area that were single-family. There was also attached
housing just to the south of the subject property. A large apartment complex was under construction to
the northeast of the property. There were a few small businesses near the property. The property was
approximately one-half block north of the Provo City Cemetery. There were two dwellings and a
detached garage on the land. The property had an abundance of open space that may be currently
under-utilized.

The applicant had requested the RC Zone with a proposal to build townhomes. Townhomes were
considered attached single-family housing. There were many different land uses in the area, including
some townhomes, duplexes, and government subsidized housing. The Planning Commission and staff
recommended approval for 15 units.

Councilor Ellsworth asked what the estimated income would be for someone who would buy one of
these units. Mr. Larsen was unsure, but said the price point would be around $380,000.

Vice Chair Shipley opened public comment.

Sid Allsop, Elk Ridge, was the applicant. He estimated that someone making $50,000 per year could
qualify for an FHA loan for one of these units. He added they had strong support from the Planning
Commission, staff, and the neighborhood.

There were no other comments, Vice Chair Shipley closed public comment.

Councilor Fillmore though this would blend into the neighborhood well. He was supportive.

Councilor Ellsworth appreciated the work of the developer. She was concerned about the density of the
project; it would be about 17-18 units per acre. Ms. Ellsworth also thought south Provo carried the bulk
of affordable and multi-family housing and did not need any more. She wanted something that would
add more value to their area. A lower and less intrusive zone was preferred.

Vice Chair Shipley asked if Councilor Ellsworth would only be satisfied with single-family detached
housing. Councilor Ellsworth explained she just preferred less density and fewer units, she was not
opposed to townhomes. In the last five years, Councilor Ellsworth estimated there had only been one
single family detached project south of 300 South and east of I-15.

 8.        Resolution 2021-27 approving a substantial amendment to the Program Year 2019 Annual
           Action Plan reprogramming CDBG-CV Funds for COVID-19 public health response. (21-050)

       Motion:             An implied motion to adopt Resolution 2021-27 as currently constituted, has been made
                           by council rule.

Dan Gonzales, CDBG and HOME Administrator, presented. In response to the pandemic caused by the
spreading of the Corona Virus COVID-19, the federal government provided an economic stimulus
package through the Coronavirus Aid, Relief and Economic Security Act (CARES Act), made available $5
billion in supplemental CDBG-CV funding to prevent prepare for and respond to the coronavirus.

The funds were administered by the Office of Community Planning and Development of the U.S.
Department of Housing and Urban Development. To use the funding, the City was required to amend
the its PY2019 Annual Action Plan.

A duly noticed Public Comment Period from June 23, to July 6, 2021, was conducted for citizens, partner
agencies, and other interested parties to review and comment on the Plan’s proposed amendment.
Electronic copies of amendment the were available on the City’s website.

Mr. Gonzalez said the project that would receive funding were House of Hope, Meals on Wheels, and
Project Read.

Vice Chair Shipley opened public hearing, there was no response.

Councilor Ellsworth noted these were very worthy programs.

Vice Chair Shipley called for a vote on the implied motion.

       Vote:               The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Harding,
                           Hoban, Sewell, and Shipley in favor.
