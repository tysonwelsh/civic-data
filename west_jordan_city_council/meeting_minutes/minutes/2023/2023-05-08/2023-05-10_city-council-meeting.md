 4. PUBLIC COMMENT
Chair McConnehey opened the public comment period at 6:22 pm

Citizen Comments:
Steve Jones, West Jordan resident, spoke of issues he had observed with homes rented out by the
room, including parking, lack of accountability with only renters in a home, and lack of yard care. Mr.
Jones said the situation on properties near his residence degraded his home and the City. He said
there had been six murders on his street in the last three years, yet he still had not seen what he
considered an adequate number of patrol cars in his area. Mr. Jones said new apartments and high-
density homes pulled police away from existing residential areas. He asked that the City increase the
Police Department.

Bob Halloran, West Jordan resident, asked if the City could take possession of ornamental lighting in
his neighborhood, and spoke of confusion regarding current ownership. Mr. Halloran said he agreed
with Mr. Jones’ comments about safety.

Paul Starkey, West Jordan resident, thanked the Council for what they did for the City on a daily basis.
He expressed concern regarding home occupation standards (City Code 13-11-2b, g, and k), and
existing violations of the Code. He asked that reported Code violations be enforced.

Gregg Sainsbury, West Jordan resident, expressed concern that every home on his street that had
been sold in the last three years had been turned into rental units with multiple families and multiple
vehicles per home (5 out of 11 properties). He said property owners were not resident, yard care
was not done, and there had been drug problems and raids. He spoke of children in the
neighborhood, and said the issues were concerning. Mr. Sainsbury asked for the City’s help.

Aaron Johnson, West Jordan resident, said he wanted to go on record as saying that the City Council
and the School Board were where change happened.

Ryan Wilkinson spoke of recent tragic accidents and grounded military aviation units. He expressed
the opinion that military helicopters flying over West Jordan presented a risk, and questioned why
the Council allowed flights to continue. Mr. Wilkinson suggested that if the South Jordan Regional
Airport ceased operation the next day, the airport’s history would remain intact.

Chair McConnehey closed public comments at 6:42 pm

 6. PUBLIC HEARINGS
       a. Ordinance No. 23-16 regarding a petition from Nhung Cao requesting a rezone of
          0.22 acres located at 1897 West 7800 South from an R-1-8A Zone (single-family
          residential) to a P-O Zone (professional office)
          Assistant Planner Mark Forsythe showed the location of the proposed rezone on a map.
          He said the property currently contained one residential home, which the owner desired
          to eventually turn into a professional beauty salon. Mr. Forsythe provided history of the
          property, and said the existing residential home was the only remaining single-family
          home on the block. He expressed the opinion that the requested rezone made sense.

           Chair McConnehey opened a public hearing at 7:15 pm

           Comments:
           Ryan Wilkinson commented that he did not understand why anyone would want to live
           next to a beauty salon and took exception to calling a hairdresser a professional. Mr.
           Wilkinson voiced opposition to the rezone.

           Andrew Rohass said his wife was the applicant. He said the plans had been very carefully
           thought out, and said he believed the plan would fit the area well.

           Chair McConnehey closed the public hearing at 7:18 pm

           Council Member Green emphasized that the property in question had the only remaining
           single-family home on the street. All other properties had been converted to professional
           office use. Council Member Green expressed the opinion that the nature of 7800 South
           was no longer residential.

           Council Member Whitelock commented that a cosmetologist went to school for training
           and applied for a license from the State of Utah the same as a nurse or a lawyer. She said
           she respected the cosmetology profession.

                      petition from Nhung Cao requesting a rezone of 0.22 acres located at 1897 West
                      7800 South from an R-1-8A Zone (single-family residential) to a P-O Zone

 7. BUSINESS ITEMS
       a. 2023 West Jordan Storm Drain Master Plan Update
          Utilities Manager Greg Davenport presented a 2023 Storm Drain Master Plan update.
          Chair McConnehey noted that the updated Master Plan would be brought back to the
          Council for adoption at a future meeting.

       b. 2023 West Jordan Low Impact Development Handbook
          Mr. Davenport explained that the goal of Low Impact Development (LID) was to keep
          storm water where it fell, with a motto of “slow it down, spread it out, soak it in”. Mr.
          Davenport answered questions from the Council, and showed pages of a 2023 West
          Jordan Low Impact Development Handbook with examples of LID implementation. Mr.
          Davenport requested feedback from the Council. Chair McConnehey noted the handbook
          would be brought back to the Council for adoption at a future meeting.

       c. Resolution No. 23-37 Prohibiting the use of ignitions sources, including fireworks,
          explosive devices, and open flames, in certain areas that historically constitute a
          hazardous environmental condition
          Chief Derek Maxfield reminded the City Council of State guidelines regarding prohibition
          of fireworks. Chief Maxfield recommended the Council adopt the same fireworks
          restriction map adopted in 2022. Responding to a question from Chair McConnehey
          regarding a previous citizen comment, the Chief explained that carving out individual
          agricultural properties in the City for inclusion on the prohibition map would not be
          permissible under State guidelines. Council Member Green suggested putting up signs
          encouraging residents to be good neighbors. Council Member Bloom encouraged
          education efforts regarding restrictions.

           MOTION: Council Member Green moved to APPROVED Resolution No. 23-37 Prohibiting
                   the use of ignitions sources, including fireworks, explosive devices, and open
                   flames, in certain areas that historically constitute a hazardous environmental
                   condition.
                   Council Member Whitelock seconded the motion.
           The vote was recorded as follows:
           Yes: Chris McConnehey, Melissa Worthen, David Pack, Kelvin Green, Pamela Bloom,
           Kayleen Whitelock
           No:
           Absent: Zach Jacob
           The motion passed 6-0.

       d. Overview and discussion of the FY 2024 Mayor’s Tentative Budget.
          Mayor Burton spoke of the FY 2024 Mayor’s Tentative Budget, as well as a supplemental
          report of prioritized department requests not included in the Tentative Budget due to
          funding limitations. Mayor Burton asked that the Council consider including items from
          the supplemental report in the FY 2024 Budget if additional resources were anticipated
          when the County provided property tax information in June. He spoke of the possible
          opportunity to pay off a general obligation bond two years early by dipping into reserves
          if the Council desired.

           Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck presented the FY 2024 Utility Budget, and
           said the few percentage errors in the presentation would be corrected. Ms. Steck said the
           FY 2024 Mayor’s Tentative Budget included proposed increases to the Sewer Utility rate
           (7%), the Solid Waste Utility rate (10%), and the Streetlight Utility rate (4%), for a total
           utility cost increase of $4.22 per month for a typical West Jordan household.

           Solid Waste Utility
           Council Member Green noted that the portion of a dumpster rental cost that would be
           subsidized by the City would increase, and suggested dumpster rental rates should
           increase so the City did not subsidize quite so much.

           Ms. Steck explained changes in the FY 2024 Solid Waste Utility Budget from the prior year,
           and noted that the budget included use of $208,151 from reserves to cover expected
           expenses. She showed a five-year Solid Waste Utility projection with anticipated rate
           increases and continued use of reserves.

           Council Member Jacob arrived at 8:06 pm

           Council Member Whitelock was not comfortable with the projected reduction in Solid
           Waste Utility reserves. Council Member Jacob said he would be interested in building
           reserves up more aggressively and said he would be interested in looking at spending less
           or collecting more. Council Member Green commented on how quickly the City would go
           through reserves following a natural disaster, and expressed the opinion that something
           should be done to change to current projection. Vice Chair Bloom said she agreed with
           the desire to look at ideas. Ms. Steck said she would bring back recommendations.

           Chair McConnehey recognized the importance of reserves but would not be in favor of
           substantially raising the rate increase for citizens in FY 2024 in order to hold more in
           reserves. Council Member Jacob expressed the opinion that Enterprise Funds such as the
           Solid Waste Utility should be self-contained.

           Ms. Steck said the Trans-Jordan green waste program would end on December 31, 2023.
           Residents would still be able to use the cans for green waste, but the green waste would
           be taken to the landfill for the same collection and disposal cost as household waste. Ms.
           Steck said other alternative service solutions were under consideration.

           Ms. Steck reported the City subsidized approximately $207 per dumpster rental with the
           current dumpster rental program. She said 96% of the rentals were first-time
           reservations and 4% were repeat reservations. Ten percent of Solid Waste Utility
           accounts were using the dumpster program, and all accounts were subsidizing the
           program.

           The Council discussed subsidization of the dumpster program, and possible changes to
           the program. Council Member Whitelock believed the program began in West Jordan to
           help keep the City clean. Council Member Worthen suggested focusing on neighborhood
           cleanup programs. Council Member Jacob disagreed that only 10% benefited, since
           neighbors benefited from cleaner properties. The Council and Ms. Steck discussed
           possibilities for a neighborhood cleanup program. Council Member Green suggested
           setting aside a certain amount in the General Fund for neighborhood cleanup projects.
           Chair McConnehey wanted the City to provide a path to a free dumpster, and agreed with
           Council Member Jacob that neighborhoods benefited from cleaner properties.

           Council Member Green expressed the opinion that a free dumpster program belonged in
           the General Fund, not the Solid Waste Utility Fund. Chair McConnehey would be
           comfortable moving the program to the General Fund. Vice Chair Bloom and Council
           Member Whitelock both wanted more information. Council Member Worthen suggested
           incentivizing group rentals and raising the cost of individual rentals. Council Member
           Jacob liked the current program structure, but agreed rates needed to increase to
           compensate for increased costs. He expressed the opinion that the program did not
           belong in the General Fund as long as it was a fee-based rather than a tax-based program.
           Council Member Whitelock suggested the Council consider providing landfill vouchers
           and suggested a blended program. Council Member Green expressed support for a
           blended program.

           A majority of the Council indicated support for raising the first-rental fee to $75.

           Streetlight Utility
           Ms. Steck showed changes to the FY 2024 Streetlight Utility Budget compared to the
           previous year, and a five-year plan including anticipated rate increases. Chair
           McConnehey asked if Council Members would support looking into changing streetlight
           standards to require that both sides of crosswalks had streetlights. Vice Chair Bloom
           expressed support. Staff explained difficulties involved in placing streetlights on both
           sides of crosswalks. Council Member Whitelock said she was concerned about the cost
           but was willing to learn more. Council Member Green expressed interest in further
           information and discussion.

           Water Utility
           Ms. Steck showed a table of water use versus water purchase, and showed changes in the
           FY 2024 Water Utility Budget compared to the previous year. Council Member Green

           budget. Ms. Steck said the cost to purchase one water buffalo trailer was $41,500. Council

           Wastewater Utility
           Ms. Steck explained the request for a 7% Wastewater Utility rate increase in FY 2024, and
           spoke of the possibility of changing to a flat rate. The Council discussed a flat rate versus
           a base charge and volume rate. Council Member Jacob argued in favor of keeping the
           current base charge/volume rate format. Council Member Whitelock spoke in favor of
           moving toward a flat rate in a gentle manner over the course of five or six years. Council
           Member Jacob questioned whether it was fair to charge the same rate to all households
           regardless of the number of residents.

           Chair McConnehey said he was in favor of keeping the current rate format. Council
           Member Jacob said he agreed. Ms. Steck explained the Mayor’s recommendation was a
           7% increase to the base charge and the volume rate. The Budget Subcommittee’s
           recommendation was a 10% increase to the base charge, with no change to the volume
           rate. Council Members Green, Worthen, and Whitelock expressed support for the Budget
           Subcommittee’s recommendation, and Council Members Jacob, Pack, and Bloom
           expressed support for the mayor’s recommendation. Chair McConnehey said he did not
           have strong feelings about the question but was fine supporting the mayor’s
           recommendation for a 10% increase to the base charge and the volume charge.

           Ms. Steck presented changes to the FY 2024 Wastewater Utility Budget compared to the
           previous year. A Master Plan update was included in the FY 2024 Budget, and an impact
           fee study would be presented to the Council sometime soon. Ms. Steck presented a
           Wastewater Utility five-year plan, including anticipated rate increases, and showed a list
           of planned capital projects.

           Storm Water Utility
           Ms. Steck presented FY 2024 Storm Water Utility budget changes compared to the
           previous year, explained an impact fee study would be presented to the Council soon, and
           showed a Storm Water Utility five-year plan, including anticipated rate increases. Council
           Member Green said he wanted to eliminate the Storm Water Enterprise Fund transfer.
           Ms. Steck showed a list of planned Storm Water Utility capital projects, and staff answered
           questions from the Council.
