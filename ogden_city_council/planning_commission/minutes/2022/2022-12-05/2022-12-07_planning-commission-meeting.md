g. Request for Expansion of Nonconforming Use, 2233 Jefferson Avenue.
   Mr. Simpson indicated the property at this address consists of a small single family home at the rear
   with a fourplex facing Jefferson Avenue. While the fourplex was originally built as a single-family
   home, it was converted to four dwelling units prior to zoning being established, and is a legal
   nonconforming use. Due to a recent fire in the fourplex, renovation is now underway, and the owners
   desire to make the units more livable for their tenants and are proposing to add space at both the
   front and rear of the building. The applicant is working with a new architect to assure the elevation
   fits the standards of the zone and is proposing a pitched roof rather than the clipped hip roof now
   existing. He noted there are height restrictions in the zone, and the height of the structure would be
   limited to match the existing height of the tallest building on the lineal block. He clarified his
   recommendation to indicate the height comply with the City’s code and not be higher than any other
   building within the block.
   MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Shinoda to approve the expansion of
               nonconforming use to allow the additions to both the front and rear as proposed based on
               the findings the expansion will not adversely impact surrounding properties, is compatible
               with its surroundings, will conform to site development standards, does not create new
               nonconformities or extend beyond the original lot. Approval is subject to the roof pitch
               being changed to gable so long as the height of the structure is at or less than any existing

j. Request for Encroachment Permit, for WSU Parking Lot, 3659 Birch Avenue.
   Mr. Travis Hogge stated WSU owns property on both sides of Birch Avenue and it is their desire to
   eliminate this access to the campus by constructing a parking lot. He stated entrances from
   Harrison have recently been consolidated and felt eliminating this access would reduce vehicular
   traffic through residential neighborhoods. He stated the encroachment would allow the street to
   remain under Ogden City’s ownership, allowing utilities to remain, and would be gated in order to
   allow egress if needed.
  Mr. Brierley stated the proposed use is for campus parking, but Staff feels there is a need to keep
  pedestrian traffic in this area. He stated the closure of this access would force the use of Harrison as
  access and reduce traffic through this neighborhood. He felt an encroachment is more desirable
  than vacating the street as the option for its future use would still be available if needed as well as
  providing another option for access by emergency vehicles. Staff feels the encroachment is the
  appropriate tool for the use and state the Commission is to determine whether the use proposed is
  an appropriate and serves the public interest.
  Commissioner Schade commented if a pedestrian access is necessary, a walkway should be defined
  rather than only using the parking lot. Commissioner Safsten stated there is no sidewalk on the
  existing portion of Birch and felt it also is desirable along the full length of Birch, but most necessary
  adjacent to the residential uses than the parking lot. Commission also noted while vehicular use
  would be reduced on this block, it would force traffic further through the residential neighborhood to
  the east access from Skyline Drive. It also would create a hardship for students coming from the
  north side of the campus who would then need to drive to Skyline or Harrison to access the campus.
  Mr. Justin Anderson, City Engineer, stated the City continues to upgrade and install sidewalks in
  residential areas, indicating sidewalks have recently been installed to accommodate nearby
  elementary schools. He felt the preferred access to campus should be Harrison and felt the removal
  of this access would reduce congestion on Edvalson, indicating bus service has been eliminated from
  36th Street and now uses Edvalson. He also agreed the encroachment is preferable to the vacation
  of a street as an encroachment permit may be revoked if needed while if the street were to be
  vacated, the City would then have to purchase property if it is later determined the street is needed.
  He also indicated Weber State is in the process of developing a transportation plan for the campus.
  He stated a walkway could be designated along the parking lot, but suggested it not be painted as
  paint creates a slippery surface. He felt an 8’ wide pedestrian walkway would be appropriate. Mr.
  Hogge stated WSU could work with Engineering to provide a walkway.
  MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to recommend approval of the
          encroachment permit based on the findings there is good cause for the encroachment,
          and will not interfere with vehicular or pedestrian travel or utilities. Approval is subject to
          Weber State working with City Engineer to create an adequate pedestrian walkway
          through the parking lot proposed which would line up with any future sidewalk along the
          remaining portion of Birch Avenue. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and
          passed 7-2 with Commissioners Aaberg, Blaisdell, Graf, Safsten, Schade, Shinoda and
          Sandau voting aye and Commissioners Shale and Southwick voting no. Commissioner
          Shale stated she uses this vehiciular access to campus daily and it is the most convenient

3. Final Group Dwelling Approval, for Towns at 24th Street, 550 24th Street.

   Mr. Luke Martin stated this project is for the development of a 26-unit townhome subdivision which had
   previously received preliminary approval by the Commission, and feels it can meet City codes as well as
   conditions of the preliminary approval.

   Mr. Brierley explained the Commission is to determine whether the modifications proposed meet the
   conditions of approval previously identified. He reviewed the project stating the townhomes would
   have interior driveways from a new dedicated Porter Avenue connecting 24th and 23rd Streets. The pitch
   on the roofs now meets the 4:12 ratio and the setbacks are compliant with the new R-9 zone. However,
   the R-9 zone does require an art feature to be established as part of the project which may be
   accommodated as part of an entry feature and may be on either public or private property. While some
   technical issues are outstanding, Staff feels these can be satisfied without any changes to the overall
   layout of the project and recommends final approval be granted.

   Commissioner Graf noted there are several conditions of preliminary approval yet to be satisfied and
   asked if Commission approval is appropriate at this time. Mr. Brierley responded while the Commission
   has the discretion to either approve or wait until these are satisfied, most are technical and relate to
   Engineering standards and can be handled through Staff without any effect to the overall plan.
   Commissioner Shinoda asked about the public art, and how it could be maintained if on private
   property. Mr. Brierley stated if placed on private property, it would be maintained by the HOA. If in the
   public right-of-way, an agreement between the City and the property owner relating to maintenance
   would be appropriate. A third option would be for funs to be donated to an art fund for the
   establishment of art at another location. Commissioner Schade asked how the 1% for art is calculated.
   Mr. Brierley stated it has been determined the cost would be 1% of the cost of the units facing the
   public street rather than the cost of the entire project. Commissioner Shale asked if the project is
   consistent with the moderate-income housing plan being considered. Mr. Brierley stated as it is to be
   owner occupied and creates a higher density near transit, it does achieve the goals of providing
   moderate income housing.

   MOTION:      A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to grant final approval based on the
                findings the proposal adequately addresses conditions of preliminary approval, complies
                with all applicable requirements of the Municipal Code. Approval is subject to Staff
                recommendations being satisfied as follows:

                 6. Necessary SWPP and NOI permits be obtained;
                 7. The final plat for Towns at 24th Subdivision be completed and recorded; and
                 8. Public art be provided to satisfy the regulations of the R-9 zone with value to be 1% of

4. CBD Site Plan, to allow apartments at 2501 Adams Avenue.

  Mr. Trevor Broughton, representing Mr. Vowles stated due to construction costs and site constraints,
  the scope of the project which was previously approved has been changed. The underground parking is
  now limited to one level rather than two, and the penthouse unit has been removed. The project will
  contain units ranging from studio to two-bedroom, and a request is being made to reduce required on-
  site parking from 26 stalls to 14.

  Mr. Simpson stated the request is to amend the previous approval by reducing required parking and
  approve the new building design. While the number of units is reduced and the downtown is more
  pedestrian oriented, the request for .52 stalls per unit could be a hardship for tenants. He stated the
  property is in the newly created R-9 zone which allows one parking space per unit, and felt the owner
  should seek a lease for additional parking within the downtown area to service his tenants. The building
  elevation is much the same as was previously approved, but the art feature is not now shown, and
  should be included to satisfy the regulations of the R-9 zone. Additional commercial space is available
  on the ground floor facing 25th Street, and access to parking will be from Madison Avenue. He stated
  the owner is willing to provide other options for transportation to his tenants such as bicycle storage,
  transit passes or bicycle sharing, but the R-9 regulations indicate one parking space per residential unit
  is required. Staff is suggesting the owner obtain a lease for the remaining parking spaces within the
  downtown area. Staff recommendation is for approval subject to the following conditions:
  1. A mural be incorporated on the west building elevation and art features be developed in the
        streetscape as was previously approved;
  2. The applicant obtain a recorded agreement with a parking lot in the downtown area for 13 parking
        spaces so each unit has access to at least one parking stall;
  3. The site provide secure on-site bicycle storage and/or bicycle share so there is the ability for each
        unit to have access or can keep at least one bicycle; and
  4. All departmental staff comments and conditions of the previous approval which are not altered by
        the amended plan be satisfied.

  Commission expressed support of the revised plan and conditions of approval and asked where bicycle
  storage may be located. Mr. Broughton stated the current thinking is they would be on the ground level
  with the parking lot in order to keep bicycles safe and assure they are not typically taken on the elevator
  to upper units.

  MOTION:      A motion was made by Commissioner Graf to recommend approval based on the findings

               meet the criteria for a parking reduction. Approval is subject to the following conditions:
               1. A mural be incorporated on the west building elevation and art features be developed
                   in the streetscape as was previously approved;
               2. The applicant obtain a recorded agreement with a parking lot in the downtown area for
                   13 parking spaces so each unit has access to at least one parking stall;
               3. The site provide secure on-site bicycle storage and/or bicycle share so there is the

6. Continued Public Haring, to consider Zoning and Text Amendments implementing the Make Ogden
   Downtown Plan.

  Mr. Brierley reminded the Commission the Make Ogden Plan had been developed in an effort to
  encourage investment in the downtown, increase density, jobs and build a better tax base and security
  for the citizens of Ogden. The proposed language and map amendments are to implement that plan as
  code requirements. He stated the Commission has since held a work session and further discussed
  questions and details which had been identified at the previous meeting, and the consultant has made
  some revisions to satisfy concerns of the Planning Commission and public. Staff also has met with the
  owners of Seager Clinic and feel it is a benefit to the community and the City desires their success at
  their present location, has issued a business license for the use and will not oppose any future building
  permits. He stated he is also proposing a few minor changes to the proposed zoning map as property
  on Adams Avenue housing the YCC and Midtown Clinic are now proposed to be zoned CMU as they
  better fit the standards of that zone as well as correcting the boundaries between the C-9 and R-9
  zones.


                                                  ~8~
  Ms. Jessica Garrow presented an overview of the previous meeting, indicating items identified at that
  time have now been further clarified and concerns addressed. She stated while the Make Ogden Plan
  had identified flat rooflines as preferred in the downtown, as the zoning extends into the community,
  residential uses should have the option of either flat or pitched roofs. She stated concern had been
  identified relating to the height of buildings in relation to both Union Station and Historic 25 th Street, and
  while there are currently no height restrictions in the downtown rather than Historic 25th Street,
  transition standards are proposed so buildings will not intrude into these historic areas. She stated the
  Union Station Master Plan addressed only the west side of Wall Avenue limiting height near the Station
  between 46’ and 64’ while allowing increased heights of 76-118’ further to the north and south. She
  stated if standards of the Make Ogden Plan are found to be in conflict with the Union Station Master
  Plan, the standards of the Union Station Plan would be applicable. She also reviewed the 45-degree
  height transition formula and identified the location where taller buildings could be located.

  Ms Marianna Stuck reviewed the placement of balconies in relation to the right-of-way, and standards
  relating to both existing and new construction. She presented images of different types of balconies
  and discussed the benefits of inset balconies.

  Ms. Garrow stated a proposed addition would allow light as an art display as it could be attractive piece
  of the public realm. She suggested a light plan should be approved by the Planning Commission and
  curfew times identified. She also stated shade studies would be required for taller buildings which
  might require some step-back as building height increases.

  Commissioner Safsten asked if the light display would allow video billboards or signs. It was explained
  the intent is not for signage but for decorative lights only. Commissioner Southwick asked if there is
  any restriction relating to the use of balconies for storage, stating these often are areas where bicycles
  are stored and these should not serve as an unshielded storage area.

  Ms. Chelsea Slade and Jerika Mayes, representing Seager Clinic stated their concerns from the
  previous meeting have been addressed as Staff has indicated the nonprofit medical clinic can continue
  at its current location.

  Ms. Mary Roberts, tenant of the Altice Building at 25th & Wall Avenue, expressed concern that the
  proposals for adding buildings along Electric Alley would eliminate parking which already is minimal for
  the uses in the building which houses six businesses with 50 employees. She felt more buildings would
  have a negative impact on existing uses and limit parking to four street-side parking spaces as well as
  negatively impacting the character of Historic 25th Street.

7. Public Hearing, Petition to Rezone, from C-2 to C-3 property at 1123-1127 Wall Avenue.

    Mr. Victor Galindo stated he desires to open a tire shop at this location and is working to meet building
    code requirements. This request is to rezone the property to allow the use.

    Mr. Simpson stated while the C-2 zone allows tire sales, there are specific regulations required which
    cannot be satisfied at this location and the applicant desires to rezone the property to allow tire sales
    and service. He stated adjacent property to the south is zoned C-3 and the proposed rezoning would
    not create an island. He reviewed the Community Policies and the Lynn Community Plan and felt
    edges can be created and the proposed zone would reflect the character of the surrounding area.
    While the Lynn Community Plan does not specifically address this property as it was in use a retail
    bread store. The Plan did, however recommend properties along Wall Avenue be rezoned from
    industrial zones to C-3, which also is consistent with the 12th Street Corridor Plan. Staff recommends
    approval of the rezoning request.

    Commissioner Schade asked if there would be consequences for the rezoning if the building were to
    be removed. Mr. Simpson responded a rear yard variance had been approved to allow an addition
    several years ago, but if the building were to be removed, new construction would be required to meet
    setback requirements as the variance would be lost. He also reviewed the existing access and parking
    on the site, stating adding parking to meet Code requirements may trigger meeting current site
    standards, but is not part of this action but would be considered as the site plan is reviewed.

   Commissioner Graf noted the building has been painted and a sign installed, and asked if the business
   is currently operating. Mr. Simpson responded while the applicant has received a temporary exception
   for the use from the Mayor, the business should not be operating until a site plan is approved. He also
   clarified the Ordinance requires all work to take place inside the building, while tire display outside is
   allowed so long as they are removed and stored inside when the business is closed. Discussion
   continued relating to the existing access points and who has the ability to use the right-of-way to the

   transition between the zoning on 12th Street and the adjacent residential neighborhood. He stated the

   plan for the corner is for multi-family housing and felt the use of a tire store would not be a good
   neighbor.

   Mr. Simpson stated it is suspected the parking for this use must be to the north as there are no

   Housing. He reviewed the definition of moderate housing as 80% of the County’s area median income,
   with those spending more than 30% of their income on housing as being cost-burdened. He stated
