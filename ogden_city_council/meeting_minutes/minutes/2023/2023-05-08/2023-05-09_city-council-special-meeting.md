The Chief Deputy City Recorder presented written notice of said hearing and affidavit of posting on the 2" day of May,

Angel Castillo stated there are so many great things happening in Ogden and those that administer these HUD programs
are doing a great job. However, the City is continuing to do the same thing while expecting a different result. The ‘starter home’
price is much higher than it should be and is not affordable, yet the City is not changing housing models through things like
adjusting zoning or minimum home sizes. Just like last year, this discussion is being held just before the deadline to submit the .
AAP to HUD and there has been little public discussion, no work groups created, and no focus on how to help those looking to
buy their first home. She challenged the Council to work with City staff and Administration to do something different to yield
groundbreaking results next year.

Teresa Bramwell offered suggestions regarding first time home ownership; the State of Utah just adopted Senate Bill
(SB) 240 creating the First Time Homebuyers Assistance Program starting July 1. She discussed the parameters of the program,
loan amounts and interest rates, and payment structure. She noted $50 million has been allocated for this program and it is
available to those buying a home valued at less than $450,000. There is enough money to help 2,500 home buyers in the State. She
is areal estate agent, and she has researched how many homes would qualify for this program and she found just three in Ogden
City listed under $450,000. She noted home ownership is the best way to help the City be a prosperous City and she suggested that
Ogden immediately switch gears and ensure that by July 1 there are many more than three homes valued at less than $450,000 for
sale. Throughout the entire State of Utah, there are less than 340 qualifying homes. This means that over 2,100 new units could be
built this year to qualify for the program and she would like to see Ogden City be home to at least 2,000 of those homes.

Public Comments

, Heath Satow addressed the eviction of the Wildlife Rehabilitation Center from the space at the Dinosaur Park; he

acknowledged this is a matter under the control of the Mayor, but he asked that the Council use their contro] over the budget to
force him to renegotiate the contract or stop the eviction. This is an important issue to Ogden and the public has not been told by
the Mayor this is an emergency; the Center is being evicted in order for a perfectly functional 12,000 square foot building to be
demolished to make room for more parking area. Just because this is legal does not mean it is just or righteous in any way. Plastic
animals are being prioritized over living, suffering wildlife. The parking area will be underused and only truly need a few days out
of every year. He provided a rendering of a parking overlay project that would fit within the area and allow the City to preserve
the building for the Wildlife Rehabilitation Center. Demolition of the building is peak government waste, and he does not
understand the motivation or urgency of the situation.

Teresa Bramwell continued her comments from the public hearing regarding the Annual Action Plan; she suggested the
City make way for 2,000 new homes valued under $450,000 and noted she would love to see these new homes in her own
neighborhood where there is a deficit of owner-occupied housing. She cited past surveys and studies of the neighborhoods
surrounding the area where she lives and owns property, which declared the area as blighted and identified the need to pursue
development that will provide service for residents who live in the area, better jobs, better schools, better parks, and an overall
improved standard of living. She also cited a news piece from Fox 13 where Mayor Caldwell talked about the blighted area and
indicated the City would look for ways to pursue home ownership through private/public partnerships that would result in
community investment. An additional news article from 2016 also discussed the struggle for people to find adequate housing in
Central Ogden and highlighted the Oak Den project; a consultant who was working on that project stated that such a concentration
of poverty is not healthy.

Angel Castillo stated that some rituals are positive and healthy, but she has a ritual with the City Council that occurs each
year when the annual budget is presented to the body, wherein she stands before them pleading for more money for first
responders and increased staffing levels. Every year since 2018 she has stood before the Council to ask them to prioritize public
safety and in tonight’s work session the first responders stood before the Council asking for the same thing. They have been asked
to ‘hang on’ for years and now is the time to prioritize public safety. Everything else in the City is just a ‘decoration’, but public
safety is a vital necessity. She would love to end this ritual and not come before the Council next year asking for the same thing.

Amy Wicks asked the Mayor, City Council, and City staff to consider what will happen if the Wildlife Rehabilitation
Center is not given a reasonable lease extension to give them time to find a new home. The Center treats around 4,000 animals
each year and the next closest treatment facility is located 90 minutes away and operated from a home. There is no other place for
birds and other wildlife in the area to be treated and if the Center is forced to stop taking new patients, there will be detrimental
effects. She stated she feels Mayor Caldwell wants to have this issue finished before he leaves office and seems determined to
leave destruction in his wake, but that is not acceptable. She has not seen a Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) project brief for
demolition of the building that houses the Center, and if there is no publicly allocated funding for the project, there should be no
hurry. There is no harm in allowing the Center to stay in the building for more than just six months; they need sufficient time to
find or build a new facility. She stated that the Mayor has never been personally involved in discussions with the Center and has
only directed his staff to handle it; she suggested he stand up for his decision or give the Center more time.
