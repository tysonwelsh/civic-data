 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
       a. Discussion regarding Landlord/Tenant Utilities
          Tyler Aitken reviewed with the Council how rental properties were currently handled in
          West Jordan. He explained that a Tenant Courtesy Billing Request was available only to
          single-family home rentals, not to HOAs, townhomes, or apartments. Mr. Aitken
          presented pros and cons of the current practice:

          PROS
           • Tenant receives bill in their own name
           • No deposits required of new utility accounts
           • Property owners take responsibility for repairs because they are ultimately
              responsible for paying the bill
           • Very few utility write-offs
           • High collection rate on delinquent balances
          CONS
           • Tenant unable to reserve a dumpster
           • Tenant unable to make service changes
           • Tenant unable to pay online via Invoice Cloud (need a different customer number)

          Vice Chair Bloom rented a HOA townhome, received a courtesy utility bill, and was able
          to pay online. Mr. Aitken responded that it worked in some situations. He said the
          courtesy bill usually had a different customer number from the property owner account.

          To accommodate tenant dumpster rentals (April 1 – October 31 only), Mr. Aitken
          proposed the following:
              •   Reservations in person at Public Works
              •   Pay at time of reservation
              •   Dumpster placed in the street
              •   No need to go through property owner

          Mr. Aitken noted that dumpster rentals would be tracked by property and user and
          monitored for abuse. In order to accommodate tenant online utility payments, Mr. Aitken
          proposed the following:
              •   Set up an online option to ‘pay as a guest’
              •   Anyone can make payments of any amount with a reference to a utility account
                  number
              •   Cost: $25-$50 per month for Cloudstore hosting fee.

          To allow tenants to create independent utility accounts, Mr. Aitken said the following
          would be needed:

              •   Part-time Utility Clerk - $35,000
              •   Part-time Meter Reader - $35,000
              •   Budget for Additional Utility Write-offs - $50,000
              •   Estimated total - $120,000 per year

          Utilities Manager Greg Davenport explained that personnel were still needed for meter
          maintenance with electronically read meters. Mr. Aitken added that meters would need
          to be read within a specific timeframe. Mr. Davenport answered questions about meters
          and the current implementation stage of the tower-read system. Staff presented pros and
          cons of independent tenant accounts:

          PROS
           • Tenant establishes relationship directly with the City for utilities
           • Privacy from landlord knowing their consumption
           • Ability to make changes to utility services

          CONS
           • Property owners no longer responsible for balances
           • Delayed landlord response for water leaks
           • Deposit will be required for new utility account (at least $350)
           • Higher utility write-off on uncollectable balances
           • Additional staff time to create/close accounts and collect meter reads
           • Still need owner approval to put dumpster on the property

          Mr. Aitken said he recommended the City require a deposit of at least $350 for new utility
          accounts to cover three months of services. Council Member Pack said he believed
          requiring a tenant deposit would be treating a tenant different than a property owner.

       c. Discussion and feedback to Staff on Gold Star Memorial in West Jordan Veterans
          Park
          Council Member Green referred to the Sandy City Gold Star Memorial and noted that he
          and Council Member Whitelock were asking for Council feedback regarding the possibility
          of putting a Gold Star Memorial in Veterans Park. The cost to the City would be $60,000,
          plus the concrete pad. He proposed the City pay the initial $60,000, and announce a
          fundraising drive to help offset the cost. The Gold Star Memorial would honor families of
          veterans who paid the ultimate price for the Nation.

          Council Member Jacob said he was very much in favor of allocating funds and suggested a
          design contest. He said he liked the idea of including bricks in the plaza with names of
          families in the City who had lost loved ones in service. Responding to a question from Vice
          Chair Bloom, Council Member Green said the Gold Star Memorial would be in addition to
          the Park Master Plan. Chair McConnehey said it appeared a majority of the Council were
          in favor.

          City Administrator Korban Lee commented that the Veterans Park Master Plan process
          had been started and asked if the Council desired to prioritize the Gold Star Memorial over
          completing the Master Plan. Council Member Green said he wanted to set aside funding
          and reach out to the foundation to start the process sooner than later. Council Member
          Whitelock said she viewed the Gold Star Memorial as an addition to an existing park
          amenity and said she would want to move forward prior to completion of the Veteran
          Park Master Plan.

          Mr. Lee said he recently heard a suggestion to renovate the existing Veteran Memorial and
          asked for clarification. Council Member Whitelock said she did not see the entire plaza
          being redone, just the addition of a flagpole and the Gold Star Memorial.

          Chair McConnehey believed some of the elements may not be conducive to the feeling
          desired at the plaza. He said he would like to see what could be done to bring dignity back
          to the existing memorial, and suggested more discussion was needed. Chair McConnehey
          said he would like to see the permanent incorporation of a Gold Star Memorial, but he was
          unsure how it would fit in with everything else that needed to be done.

          Council Member Green said he agreed with Council Member Whitelock that the Gold Star
          Memorial would be in addition to the existing Veteran Memorial. He said the process
          would take several months, and he would like to begin the process rather than postpone
          it.

       d. Discussion on proposed sewer lift station language for Title 13 Zoning Regulations
          Council Member Green explained the proposal to pass an ordinance with language
          regarding sewer lift stations in West Jordan, in addition to the existing language in City
          engineering standards. Council Member Green asked for Council support in sending the
          drafted language to the Planning Commission to be proactive.

          Greg Davenport said he was not a fan of lift stations because they required constant care.
          When lift stations failed, homes flooded. Mr. Davenport said the City had done everything
          possible to get rid of lift stations. Council Member Green said including the language in an
          ordinance would ensure where liability would fall.

          Council Member Whitelock said she believed the proposal was timely and spoke in favor
          of being prepared. Mayor Burton shared a personal experience, and said he agreed that a
          lift station was not something he wanted in the City.

          Chair McConnehey said the City did not own any lift stations, nor did he want any, but he
          did not think the City should prohibit use of them on private property. He suggested
          updated language should be clear, clean, short, and simple. Council Member Whitelock
          said she preferred more structure regarding what could and could not be done.

          Council Member Green asked if four Council Members were in favor of sending the
          proposed language to staff, followed by the Planning Commission. Vice Chair Bloom said
          she was happy with what was proposed. Council Member Jacob said he would want
          something to vote on. The Council discussed the Council review process.

          Mayor Burton said he had a good feel for what the Council wanted and said he and staff
          would work through it and bring back something more polished for a vote.

       e. Discussion concerning Special Assessment Districts for city mandated amenities
          Vice Chair Bloom felt that some HOAs in the City had not been transparent and were
          gouging on fees. She asked if there was a way for the City to take over certain things to
          make neighborhoods less reliant on HOAs and help West Jordan be more affordable.




Committee of the Whole Minutes             Wednesday, March 15, 2023                            Page 6
          Council Member Green asked if at least four Council Members were in favor of spending
          staff time to look at the possibility of having a special assessment district to maintain
          elements like detention ponds and open space to eliminate HOAs.

          Chair McConnehey was not in favor of creating additional special assessment districts, but
          he wanted to see what opportunities were available for the City to own storm water
          systems and infrastructure that impacted the entire City. Council Member Whitelock
          struggled with HOAs because it was possible for HOA fees to price individuals out of their
          homes. She was not willing to have the City take over HOAs. She pointed out that many
          HOA developments received extra density and other perks even though it did not really
          benefit the residents. Council Member Whitelock did not believe special assessment
          districts were the answer.

          Council Member Pack left at 7:59 pm

          Council Member Worthen expressed opposition to the proposal. She believed it was too
          much of a slippery slope. Vice Chair Bloom wanted to protect residents from escalating
          HOA fees without transparency. Council Member Whitelock suggested working with State
          Legislators.

          Council Member Green asked if the Council would be willing to look at maintenance on
          detention ponds. Responding to a question from Chair McConnehey, Mayor Burton
          confirmed there were some HOAs in the City that existed solely for storm water detention.
          Chair McConnehey spoke of a situation in which an HOA was able to bring a private street
          up to Code, the City adopted the street, and the HOA was able to be dissolved. He said he
          would be in favor of looking at those types of opportunities but would be averse to
          creating special assessment districts.

          Council Member Jacob questioned if it was the best practice to require amenities of
          developers rather than the City putting in parks and letting developers put in tot lots.
          Council Member Green said amenities were only required in PRD and PC Zones, and
          suggested maybe the amenity requirement should be eliminated.

          Vice Chair Bloom said she liked the suggestion to look at storm water and roads and liked
          the suggestion to add amenities to the discussion of PRD and PC Zones. Council Member
          Jacob suggested having the discussion with the Planning Commission. Council Member
          Whitelock said she would be in favor of discussing and having staff look at the storm water
          and road issues, but said she was not sure it would help the intended goal. Council
          Member Green suggested the detention pond discussion would be a good first step, with
          a holistic discussion of HOAs in the future.

          Mayor Burton said he would work on the detention pond issue and HOAs that existed only
          for detention and bring it back to the Council. A majority of the Council indicated support.
