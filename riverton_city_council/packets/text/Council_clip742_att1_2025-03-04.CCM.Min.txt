16            Fire Three Man vs. Four Man Service
17
18   Mayor Staggs discussed fire engine staffing levels, specifically the comparison between three-
19   person and four-person crews. He clarified that the discussion is based on budgetary
20   considerations and not a reflection on service quality. He emphasized the City's strong
21   relationship with the Unified Fire Authority (UFA), expressing appreciation for their
22   professionalism and service.
23
24   Kevin Hicks, City Manager, discussed the need to reach a consensus on the direction for the
25   upcoming fiscal year (2025-26) to allow UFA ample time to adjust if any changes are needed for
26   the next budget cycle.
27
28   Councilmember Buroker expressed appreciation for working with UFA, noting their transparency
29   and willingness to answer questions. She emphasized the importance of engaging the entire
30   Council in the decision regarding three-person versus four-person staffing, as it directly impacts
31   residents. She requested historical context on the transition from three-person to four-person
32   staffing across UFA.
33
34   Fire Chief Dominic Burchett, Unified Fire Authority, explained that in 2001, NFPA established
35   standard 1710, which outlined best practices for fire operations. Between 2001 and 2006, most
36   UFA apparatus operated with four-person crews, but when UFA began its own ambulance
37   service in 2006, staffing was adjusted to three-person crews on heavy apparatus. In 2015-2016,
38   discussions surrounding Draper’s departure from UFA created an opportunity to restructure
39   staffing, leading to a shift of ambulance personnel to heavy apparatus, bringing most stations
40   back to four-person crews by around 2017. Regarding Riverton, staffing decisions were made at
41   the UFSA board level, with Riverton’s stations adopting the four-person model. He noted that
42   staffing levels are determined by each UFA member community and stated that UFSA intends
43   to move all apparatus to four-person staffing, with only two low-call-volume stations still
44   operating with three-person crews.
45
46   Chief Burchett explained the impact of staffing changes on Riverton’s fire services, emphasizing
47   the importance of Station 124 and Station 121 in housing specialty units, including a hazmat
48   company and a heavy rescue company. He noted that due to UFA’s funding model, Riverton
49   currently receives the staffing benefit of 12 firefighters per day for the cost of eight. These
50   specialty units require six personnel per station, as mandated by OSHA and NFPA standards,
51   and are supported by an ambulance. If Riverton were to reduce staffing to three-person crews,
     Page | 3 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     March 4, 2025



 1   these specialty units and an ambulance would be relocated outside the City, resulting in a 33%
 2   reduction in daily staffing. He highlighted that this change would increase out-of-service time, as
 3   three-person crews responding to critical medical calls often lose a paramedic to patient
 4   transport, leaving the heavy apparatus temporarily unavailable. He reiterated that the four-
 5   person model ensures faster response times, greater operational efficiency, and more
 6   personnel available to handle fire and medical emergencies.
 7
 8   Councilmember Pierucci acknowledged the financial realities the City was facing, noting the
 9   current and projected general fund transfers to support fire services. He recognized the
10   importance of both budget considerations and public safety impacts in the Council’s decision-
11   making process. He asked for an estimate of how often the City’s specialty units were deployed
12   outside Riverton for emergency responses.
13
14   In response, Chief Burchett stated that it would be difficult to estimate how often specialty units
15   responded outside Riverton but noted that the heavy rescue and hazmat programs were shared
16   between two stations each. The Riverton stations primarily supported Herriman, Riverton, and
17   Eagle Mountain, while other stations in Midvale and Taylorsville handled regional responses in
18   the northern service area.
19
20   Councilmember Pierucci noted that the City's population and demographics had changed
21   significantly since the days of a three-person crew, citing the development of Mountain View
22   Village and rapid growth on the west side. He questioned whether the financial benefits of a
23   staffing change would outweigh potential impacts on public safety.
24
25   Mr. Hicks clarified that the City does not transfer sales tax revenue for fire services. Instead,
26   funding comes from fire impact fees collected from new developments, which are required by
27   law to pass through the general fund before being transferred to the fire fund. These funds are
28   entirely transferred each year with no remaining balance.
29
30   Regarding the $250,000 set aside for capital outlay each budget cycle, Mr. Hicks explained that
31   while this is a best-practice approach, the actual expenditures vary. Fire stations in the City
32   range in age from approximately 10 to 40 years, necessitating ongoing improvements. Last
33   year, around $160,000 was spent, while this year’s budget includes $210,000 for station
34   improvements. A request for next year’s funding is currently under review, and while some
35   projects may be deferred, an estimated $200,000 in recommended improvements is expected.
36
37   Councilmember McCay reiterated her satisfaction with the service provided by UFA and
38   inquired about the possibility of having one station staffed with four personnel while another
39   operates with three. Chief Burchett responded that, without deeper analysis, the most likely
40   configuration would be keeping the ladder and heavy rescue company at Station 121 while
41   reducing Station 124 to a three-person engine.
42
43   Mayor Staggs noted that Cottonwood Heights has a similar setup, with one four-person and one
44   three-person station, based on call volume. He requested more detailed call data for each
45   station rather than the aggregated annual totals to help assess whether a split configuration
46   would be feasible. The data would help determine if a reduction in staffing at a lower-call-
47   volume station could allow the City to maintain two ambulances while staying within budget
48   constraints.

 4            Fire Three Man vs. Four Man Service
 5
 6   Councilmember Haymond emphasized the importance of planning for the City's future financial
 7   needs rather than focusing on cost-cutting, particularly when it comes to essential services like
 8   public safety. He noted that while rising costs are a concern, reducing funding for critical
 9   services could have severe consequences. Instead of looking for cuts, he suggested focusing
10   on how to sustainably fund the City's growth and ensure necessary services remain adequately
11   supported.
12
13   Mayor Staggs acknowledged learning valuable information during the discussion and
14   emphasized the importance of maintaining public safety as the City grows. While previously
15   considering reducing staffing at some stations, he now opposed the idea but remained open to
16   lowering Station 124’s crew to three if ambulance service remained unaffected. He requested
17   call volume data to assess the impact and sought input from the Unified Fire Authority on
18   whether future growth would require additional ambulances, engines, or other resources.
19
20   Chief Burchett explained that Riverton has experienced a 20% increase in call volume over the
21   past six years and is projected to see the same increase in the next five to six years. In 2024,
22   there were just over 2,100 calls, which is expected to rise to approximately 2,600. To maintain
23   adequate coverage, adding an engine company at Station 120 may be necessary. Riverton, as
24   a contract City rather than part of a service district, would absorb the cost, which is estimated at
25   $3.1 million for a four-person crew. Currently, the City has two ambulances, one at the old
26   station and one at Station 121. While station capacity is sufficient, rising call volumes due to
27   vertical growth may require additional resources in the future.
28
29   Chief Burchett clarified that the current budget with the projected 5.5% increases does not
30   account for the extra cost of an additional apparatus, which would be needed if call volume
31   increases significantly. If a new engine were to be added, the cost would depend on when it is
32   put into service, with prorated charges based on the remaining year. The cost breakdown for
33   UFA's stations includes both staffing costs (just under $2 million for a full four-person station)
34   and regional costs, which would be spread across all stations. The addition of Station 120 would
35   increase the number of stations to 26, spreading the regional costs further. However, adding
36   such an apparatus would likely require a tax increase, unless smaller increases were
37   implemented earlier to build up a fund balance. As of now, staffing levels can manage current
