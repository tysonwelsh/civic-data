Comments from chat: 
 
from Kim Z to everyone:    3:25 PM 
 
Notes for MPD public hearing 1/4/23 from Hi Country II Resident Kim Zacherson 14594 S. Rose Cyn. Rd.: 
 
The staff report said only a few people will be affected by this zoning change.  S.L. Co. code defines 
ephemeral stream as:  "Those channels, swales, gullies, or low areas that do not have flow year-round.” 
Response: If we must have a 50’ setback to ephemeral streams, that affects most of us.  We live in the 
mountains. 
 
One of the claims, horses do not have E. coli in their digestive tract, has been circulating throughout the 
discussion around the Rose Creek impairment. This claim is not consistent with scientific articles published 
on the topic.  
Response: From the CDC: (E. coli) bacteria normally live in the intestines of people and animals. Most E. 
coli is harmless and actually are an important part of a healthy human intestinal tract. We have sent 
numerous proof that horses don’t carry the harmful type of E. coli.  It is mostly from the wildlife, deer, 
turkeys, coyotes, cougars, etc. 
 
Staff report said, some who expressed opposition to the ordinance seemed to be more comfortable with the 
proposed ordinance after having gained a greater understanding that the ordinance will not place 
prohibitions on their animal and other rights to the degree that they had feared.  
Response: This is not true.  There is only one resident who is not opposed to this new ordinance. 
 
The Jordan River E. coli TMDL study by Utah Dept Water Quality suggests that Rose Creek is polluting 
the Jordan River with E. coli.  The highest samples were generally observed in the months of June-October.  
Response: We’ve also sent proof that Rose Creek doesn’t meet the Jordan River and that is diverted to 
crops in Herriman from June – October. 
 
Also in the State  Div Water Quality report: The Rose Creek assessment Unit will need to have 83% 
reductions in E. coli concentrations to meet water quality standards.   
Response:  How is this possible with all of the wildlife living around it?   
 
Limiting our ability to have horses in Hi Country will not have the effect that the state is seeking.
