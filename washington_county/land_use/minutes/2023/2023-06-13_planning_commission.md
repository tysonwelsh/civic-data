Community Development




               Washington County Land Use Authority Meeting
                              June 13, 2023
The Regular Meeting of the Planning Commission of Washington County was called to order by Chairman Kevin
Jones at 1:30 p.m. on June 13, 2023, in the Washington County Commission Chambers and via Zoom, at 111
East Tabernacle Street in St. George, Utah. In attendance; Planning Commissioners Kevin Jones, Mark Owens,
Frank Leuck, Bradley Gaston, Todd Jones, and Brian McMullin. County Staff in attendance were Scott Messel,
Community Development Director; Victoria Hales, Deputy Attorney; Todd Edwards, County Engineer, Leslie
Fonger, Destination Development Manager, Kurt Gardner, County Building Official, Kyle Layton, Building
Inspector, Emerson Rivera, Zoning Compliance Specialist, and Reid Walkenhorst, Planning and Permits
Specialist. Commissioner Brandon Anderson was absent from today’s meeting.


        MEETING OPENING AND WELCOME.

        Chairman Jones opened the meeting at 1:30 p.m. and welcomed everyone.

        PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE.

        Pledge of Allegiance led by Chairman Jones.

        Commissioner Frank Leuck: No Conflict
        Commissioner Brian McMullin: No Conflict
        Commissioner Brandon Anderson: Absent

        Chairman Jones welcomed the public in attendance and reviewed the process of Public Meeting and
        Public Hearing.

        1. REVISIONS TO TITLE 10, INCLUDING DEFINITIONS AND AMENDMENTS TO THE SHORT TERM
        RENTAL (STR) ORDINANCE, TITLE 10-13-23. (Public Hearing - Legislative). .

        Mr. Messel went through examples of Short Term Rentals. He spoke of the unique lodging experiences
        offered and also the importance of finding balance within neighborhood subdivisions to prevent
        conflict between residents and short term rental property owners.

        Ms. Hales went through an overview of the proposed changes. Specifically, she addressed
        misconceptions of what applies to operators who are considered grandfathered in, and new applicants
        operating under the current code. Additional points are as follows.

    •    Line 24 all provisions apply to existing non-conforming, misconceptions exist that existing
         nonconforming short term rentals don’t have to meet the code, they don’t have to comply with
         size limitations or owner occupancy, but all other provisions are required.
    •    Line 32 specifies what short term renters are allowed to rent, specifically described on the
         Short Term Rental license.
    •    Line 42 language changes requested by Assessor’s Office.
    •    Line 47 deals with sewer gas and septic failure. Septic systems are based on the number of
         bedrooms. Four people are allowed per bedroom. Lodging in other areas of the home is not
         permitted.
    •    Line 112 specifies fire districts will inspect units with 10 or more occupants annually.
    •    Lines 138-148 Violation of unpaid fees or fines will result in revocation of Short Term Rental
         License at the discretion of the County.
    •    Lines 211-212 discuss occupancy limits.
    •    Line 216 addresses issues with trash receptacles being left out more than 24hrs.
    •    Line 245 addresses trespass concerns.
    •    Line 249 addresses events, parties, noise. Daytime and nighttime decibel levels.
    •    Line 310 reviews penalty of violations. Violations do not need to proven beyond a reasonable
         doubt. After 3 violations, we will take action against the license.
    •    Drones being flown over backyards to collect evidence or report violations is illegal and is
         considered trespassing.
    •    Tech platforms claim to have banned events, but they do nothing to enforce the ban.

Commissioner McMullin asked what should be done if a drone is seen flying on private property.

Ms. Hales responded that the matter should be reported to local police as a trespass violation.

Commissioner Owens asked Emerson Rivera, Code Enforcement Specialist, to give a brief preview of
his daily activities, background, and what challenges he encounters with enforcing short term rental
violations.

Mr. Rivera stated he works 8:00am to 5:00pm, he has a law enforcement background. He follows up
on a variety of complaints, not only from Short Term Rentals, but all other code violations reported to
Community Development.

Commissioner Owens asked if there was anyone to cover violations over the weekend.

Mr. Rivera stated those complaints are typically covered by law enforcement.

Commissioner Owens acknowledged the difficulty of trying to pursue these complaints after the fact.

Mr. Rivera spoke of a new software program which will allow him to identify unlicensed Short Term
Rental operators so letters can be sent.

Ms. Hales stated the fines are liened against the property, but this isn’t meaningful until the property
is sold, and the title report is cleaned up. For operators who don’t have a license, enforcement is more
difficult, because there is no license to be revoked.

Ms. Hales stated there are criminal enforcement procedures, but those processes take over a year to
process.

Ms. Hales stated the ordinances for the County are more lenient compared to other local jurisdictions.
Some jurisdictions have specific short term rental zones and have prohibited short term rental in
residential zones.

Chairman Jones Opened the Public Hearing.

Chairman Jones reminded those in attendance of the rules for speaking in the Public Hearing.

Mr. Mark Hudgens approached the podium, he is a resident of Sky Ranch. Short term rentals are not
homes, they are commercial entities more like hotels, but not held to the same standard as hotels.
Residents in these neighborhoods are being robbed of the peaceful, quiet, enjoyment or their
properties. Owners of these businesses feel like they are entitled to run the business however they
want, without regard to how they impact the area.

Mr. Bob Nelson Short Term Rental Owner. He owns an existing non-conforming property. He wanted
to emphasize his desire to coexist with the local neighborhood. He asked that we focus more on
behaviors which are causing a negative impact, rather banning specific events. He asked for property
owners to have an opportunity to enforce and respond to unruly guests. He asked if there could be
consideration of major and minor violations.

Mr. Corey Condor owns a short term rental. He asked the local residents to please contact the owners
when guests are being unruly or causing a nuisance. He believes enforcement needs to put onto the
property owners. He also feels the economy benefits from having short term rentals in the area,
especially service providers and as a source of tax revenue.

Ms. Julie Crow is a resident of Sky Ranch. She spoke of her concern with the private resorts in
residential communities. Short Term Rentals are supposed to exist in harmony with the neighborhood
and community and take measures to reduce impact. Protect the overall character of the community.
This is clearly not being enforced.

Mr. Michael Sullivan is the president of the Grassy Meadows Sky Ranch homeowner’s association.
Wanted to reiterate the concern with people on the runway. Because of the unique nature of the
private runway, this creates a unique set of safety concerns. Sky Ranch does not allow short term
rentals, but homes nearby, which are outside of the association boundary are being utilized as short
term rentals. Multiple complaints and instances of trespass have occurred from these homes.

Mr. Rick Beetz has two STR’s within two blocks. They don’t have any signage on the exterior and they
don’t have licenses. He feels the ordinances are merely suggestions which cannot be enforced.

Mr. Slade Hughes supported STR’s as a way families can generate income and support the local
economy. He shared his concern with overregulating, as it would reduce property rights and hurt the
economy..

Mr. Brandon Anderson owns Mollies Landing. Underscores his desire to work with residents and
County. He hopes any complaints will first be addressed to the property owner and allow the property

Ms. Shelia Robinson moved from the city to have peace and quiet and a deeper connection with nature.
She doesn’t feel the decibel levels will work as a control measure. She feels the STR’s should not be
allowed to have outdoor speakers. Even though the STR operators say they have decibel level
monitors, it doesn’t prevent loud music or excessive noise.

Mr. Brent Cohen feels the county should ask the building department why the Short Term Rentals were
allowed and permitted to be built.

Ms. Patty Nation shared her concerns with people on taxiways and runways at the Sky Ranch airport.
She further expressed concerns about property damage and trespassing on HOA property.

Ms. Ricky Helvey owns a short term rental in the Adobe Hills area. She does share information with
guests about off limit areas. She is against having verbiage in the ordinance which prohibits certain

neighbors.

Mr. James Stevens lives near three large STR’s. Stated music and noise can be a problem, but he feels
the decibel requirements are not practical.

Mr. Mark Harms is a resident of Sky Ranch. Adobe Hills is accessed through Sky Ranch, which he

Chairman Jones Closed the Public Hearing.

Commissioner Gaston felt the restrictions on specific activities and decibel levels were too severe. He
also shared his thoughts on neighbors in any community or subdivision sometimes becoming
frustrated with the actions of a neighbor.

Commissioner Jones asked for clarification on what action the Planning Commission would take today.

Chairman Jones said the options were to table a decision, make revisions, or send a positive
recommendation to the County Commission. It would also be possible to schedule a work meeting to
discuss the ordinance line by line and suggest changes based on feedback from today’s hearing.

Mr. Messel indicated the Planning Commission could schedule a work meeting to further discuss the
ordinance and suggest changes.

Commissioner Owens suggested there does need to be changes made to the ordinance presented. He
also expressed concerns about the County’s ability to enforce the ordinance effectively.

Commissioner Leuck suggested we table the discussion and schedule a work meeting.

Mr. Messel stated he agreed there was a need to schedule a work meeting. He also stated the intent of
the ordinance revision is to improve clarity in the ordinance, which would help enforcement efforts.

Commissioner McMullin asked if the work meeting would need to be noticed and if there was enough
time to notice a work meeting before the next Planning Commission meeting.

Chairman Jones suggested a work meeting be scheduled to further discuss proposed changes to the
ordinance on June 27, 2023.

MOTION: Commissioner Owens made a motion to table this meeting to a date certain, July 11,
2023, the next regularly scheduled Planning Commission Meeting. The motion was SECONDED
by Commissioner McMullin. The motion PASSED unanimously.
