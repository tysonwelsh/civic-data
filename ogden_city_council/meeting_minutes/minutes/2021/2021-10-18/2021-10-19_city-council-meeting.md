Proposed Ordinance 2021-48 amending the budget for the Fiscal Year
2021-2022 budget in the amount of $2,484,100 for police salaries; and
Proposed Ordinance 2021-53 adopting salary schedules for sworn public
safety employees within the Police Department
           A memo from the Management Services Department regarding proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2021-2022 budget and salary
amendments relating to police salaries came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Administration is proposing
an amendment to the FY22 Budget to implement Phase 3 of the Evergreen Classification, Compensation, and Benefit study for
sworn police officers. The change will implement full penetration, meaning that officers will be moved to their correct steps
within their respective ranges. Officers will also be eligible to move to the next step on their anniversary date. The Administration
believes these changes are necessary to remain competitive in the current hiring market. The cost of the early Phase 3
implementation is almost $2.5 Million. Funding is proposed to come from sales tax and General Fund fund balance. A separate
related proposal to amend the Range Placement Table for sworn officers increases starting police office pay from $21.39 to
$24.80, changes step progression, and adds additional steps for police officers and master police officer positions.
           Human Resources Manager Judkins reviewed the memo, after which Police Chief Young used the aid of a PowerPoint
presentation to discuss the Utah Police hiring crisis. Chief Young discussed several factors that have contributed to the current
police hiring environment; following Ogden City's actions to adjust wages for police and other employees in the City, Salt Lake
City Police announced a raise in pay. The new pay scale is $26.93 to $39.29 per hour which went into effect on June 27,2021.
Salt Lake Police Department currently has 80- 100 vacancies. Additionally, West Valley announced a raise in pay with the entry
level starting at $27.15 and topping out at$41.90. This was effective the frrst week of July to avoid massive loss ofbodies to Salt
Lake City. West Valley has 25- 30 vacancies. These increases caused a ripple effect throughout the Salt Lake Valley which
spread to Davis and Weber counties. Layton Police Department raised their top end to $41.19; Kaysville moved their top end to
$41.29; Riverdale gave every officer a two (2) step increase, and Weber County gave all employees a $2.00 an hour increase.
This drastic shift in compensation left Ogden Police Department (OPD) approximately $6.00 an hour below entry level and $9.00
an hour below the top of the pay scale compared to other medium and large sized Utah police agencies. The tight employment
market in Utah combined with the national atmosphere regarding policing has led to a shortage of qualified applicants both
nationwide and in Utah. There are more than 600 police officer vacancies across Utah and Utah POST is only able to train about
200 annually at the primary campus and about 200 more at satellite campuses. During the months of January, July, and August of
this year, OPD had reduced the number of vacancies dramatically -19 new/rehires. If the Police Department is unable to change
its pay scale immediately, that trend will shift significantly in the opposite direction. As of September 27, 2021, OPD has a total of
27 vacancies, which include:
               •    10 Unfilled Sworn Vacancies
                •   11 Officers in POST/training
               •    2 Officers on Light Duty Status
               •    4 Officers on Military Leave
           OPD has experienced a significant reduction in new applicants since the pay changes were implemented in other agencies
along the Wasatch Front. Lateral applicants have withdrawn their applications at OPD in favor of Salt Lake City and West Valley.
The Weber Fraternal Order of Police undertook an anonymous survey of OPD officers and found that 22 stated they had applied at
another agency and 45 others were considering applying. OPD had one officer submit their resignation to take employment with
Riverdale. Background investigators have visited in reference to tlrree other officers.
           Deputy Comptroller Sorensen discussed the implementation of the Evergreen Compensation Study; in the Fiscal Year
(FY) 2022 budget, police officers were brought to the closest step and next step, for a fiscal impact of $1,382,775. The total
amount added to FY22 Budget to partially implement the Evergreen Study for police, frre, and general employees is $3,211,800.
Phase 3- or full implementation- of the study is scheduled to occur in FY23; the fiscal impact for police is $711,000, $302,225
for frre, and $1,162,000 for general employees.
           Human Resources Manager Judkins noted that Administration has developed a new pay plan proposal to address the
current crisis. This includes immediately increasing the police salary structure to be competitive with the market; and expansion of
the police officer position steps to reflect the step structure of surrounding agencies - 12 steps. She offered a range of pay
comparison:
                •   Current Police Officer       $21.39/hour- $32.20/hour (adopted July 2021 from Evergreen Study)
                • Proposed Police Officer $24.80/hour- $37.63/hour
               •    Increases to Sergeant, Lieutenant, and Division Commander ranges also included
           The impact of this proposal in the FY22 budget is $2,484,100. The pay increases would be implemented retroactive to
July 1, 2021 and employees will be placed in the next step in their new pay plan.
           Mr. Sorensen stated that the FY23 fiscal impact will be $4,281,650; this includes the amount added in FY22 as well as
the full penetration of the Evergreen Study for police, frre, and general employees.
           Comptroller Stout discussed optional funding sources for the FY22 increases; Administration proposes the use of
increased sales tax and fund balance. It will be necessary to identify funding sources for the FY23 fiscal impact of $4,281,650.
           Chief Young concluded officers employed by Ogden love this community and wantto continue to serve here; they do not
want to be paid more than officers in every other city in the State, but they want their wages to be competitive.

Petition to rezone property at 835-875 West Binford Street from R-1-5 to C-
3/CO or M-1/CO
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a petition to rezone property at 835-
875 West Binford Street from R-1-5 to C-3/CO or M-1/CO came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the
petitioner, Mark Kitchen, is requesting to rezone properties abutting his K-9 kennel business so that he can expand his business
operations. He is requesting to rezone from R-1-5 (single-family) to C-3/CO (regional commercial/conditional overlay) or M-
1/CO (limited manufacturing/conditional overlay). This rezone is directly related to the requested amendmentto the West Ogden
Community Plan, which the Planning Commission recommended denying. This petition requires the community plan to be
amended to allow the C-3 or M-1 zones, but it was found that the C-3 zone did not meet the needs of the petitioner, so the
Planning Commission recommended allowing the C-1 or M-1/CO zone in the community plan, with the condition that all
properties within the block be part of the rezone request. This rezone request does not include all properties within the block and
would thus isolate residential properties. For this reason, the Planning Commission recommended denying the rezone application.
Rezones should follow the direction of Community Plans. If the City Council decides not to amend the West Ogden Community
Plan to allow for either C-3 or M-1 zones, then this rezone request should not be approved, as the rezone would not be consistent
with the plan.
          Planning Manager Montgomery sununarized the memo and noted that this rezone petition is the follow up petition to the
previously acted upon agenda item to amend the West Ogden Community Plan. He noted that the Planning Commission cannot
recommend a zone change that is not consistent with the Community Plan; given that they recommended denial of the Community
Plan amendment, they also recommended denial of the zone change.

THE REZONE OF 835 TO 875 WEST BINFORD TO M-1/CO. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY VICE CHAm WHITE.
