Merideth Reid, of Provo, encouraged the city council to pursue sensible and creative solutions for
Provo's dog issues, and expressed support for reasonable civil infraction penalties. She cautioned against
harsh fines and class B misdemeanor charges, suggesting that other cities have successfully addressed
similar problems with off-leash areas, designated hours, and public education campaigns. Emphasizing
the benefits of providing accessible spaces for dog owners, she urged the city to invest in off-leash areas
and dog parks as a valuable addition to existing recreational opportunities.

Gabby Sabalones, of Provo, addressed the council about off-leash dog concerns, acknowledging the
city's worries about dog waste and safety. She recounted a court case involving her friend Karina, a
single mom studying at UVU, facing a Class B misdemeanor charge and a $150 fine for playing fetch with
her dog in an empty park. Despite seeking leniency, the prosecutor revealed pressure from the
administration to prosecute such cases, resulting in a $120 fine and a one-year probationary period for
Karina. Gabby emphasized the need for better solutions, urging the council to explore creative options
and consider a civil fine structure to avoid harsh penalties.

Crystal Bolan, of Provo, expressed concerns about the strict leash rule in Provo. She discussed the
challenges of walking with a leash, even using a 50-foot leash to allow her dog some freedom. She
emphasized the need for options to provide a better place for dogs to play, considering the well-being of
both dogs and citizens. Crystal mentioned issues with the existing dog park, citing its unpleasant
conditions and the impact it has on her asthma. She urged the council to explore alternatives to seek a
compromise for the benefit of everyone in the city.

With no other public comments, Chair MacKay closed public comment.

State Auditor’s Office and Government Financial Officer Association which include formalizing the audit

4.         An ordinance amending duties regarding internal auditing. (24-010) 0:43:14

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2024-4, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Mr. Harrison presented. He said this ordinance changes the responsibilities of internal auditing from the
Finance Division of Administrative Services to Council. He added that it was recommended by both the
State Auditor’s Office and GFOA that the function be independent of management, which moves it to
the legislative branch of government.

Chair MacKay opened the item for public comment. With none, she closed public comment and asked
for council discussion. With no discussion, she called for a vote on the implied motion.

      Vote:        The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, Handley,
                   Hoban, MacKay, and Whipple in favor.

5.         An ordinance amending Provo City Code regarding the Neighborhood District Program (24-
           013) 0:44:41

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2024-5, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Rachel Breen, Community Relations Analyst, presented. She said the proposed changes were suggested
by the current participants of the neighborhood program after conducting surveys and having
discussions. She shared the first change, which eliminates the maximum number of board members per
district. She added that there is still the requirement to have at least one, but no more than two
residents from each neighborhood to serve on the Executive Board. She stated the second change was
to allow Executive Board Members to serve consecutive terms. She said the third change would
eliminate the language around motions and votes and instead would use opinion polls. She also said
they wanted to change the word communication to endorsement in section (7)(b) as communication
was too broad of a term. She highlighted her favorite change, which was to increase the matching grant
funds from $5,000 to $7,500. She said the last changes were to move the Pleasant View neighborhood
to be in District 1 to align with the council districts, and the University neighborhood to be in District 5
due to the high student population and similar culture that is shared with the downtown area.

Chair MacKay opened the item for public comment. With none, she closed public comment and asked
for council discussion.

Councilor Bogdin shared her excitement for the increase in the grant funding. She shared how difficult it
was to find upgrades for the neighborhoods with only $5,000.

Councilor Garrett said the work that Ms. Breen does with the neighborhood program is highly valued by
the citizens. He thanked her for her service and work.
