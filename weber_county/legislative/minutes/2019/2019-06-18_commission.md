      2. POLICY 16.1 PERSONAL USE OF COUNTY PROPERTY.
            Bryan Baron, Deputy County Attorney, stated that earlier this year the legislature passed HB 163, which has
            the effect of criminalizing personal use of public property as a second or third degree felony. The county
            already had policies that protected public property from being abused by employees but this law introduces the
            criminal element. The county crafted this policy to try and reach a balance between protecting public property
            and also the employees so that they are not charged with a second/third degree felony for something as innocent
            as checking their email during a lunch break. It authorizes occasional/incidental personal use while protecting
            county property from abuse. Chair Jenkins had thought that language stating “as permitted by the supervisor”
            should be added at the end of the first paragraph. Commissioner Harvey had spoken with legal counsel and
            that language would put the onus back on the supervisor where it already is, and he feels comfortable with this
            because it only allows the occasional/incidental use as mentioned by Mr. Baron. Mr. Baron stated that that
            language had been discussed but the concern was that the supervisor would have to approve each and every
            personal use and if not, it could be subject to a felony (i.e., for a personal phone call) due to an oversight.
            Commissioner Harvey moved to approve Policy 16.1, Personal Use of County Property; Commissioner Froerer
            seconded.
            Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Froerer – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye
      3. LOCAL TRANSPORTATION FUNDING AGREEMENT WITH OGDEN CITY TO ESTABLISH TERMS FOR THE 20TH
         STREET (PHASE 2) PROJECT.
            Brooke Stewart, with County Community Development, stated that this project is to improve 0.6 miles of

            originally applied for local transportation funding during the 2016 application cycle and was awarded the full

            additional funding options and notified the county at the beginning of 2019 that the project was awarded Surface
            Transportation Funds from WFRC. The City has committed local funds to complete the project.
         WACOG approved a recommendation on 11/7/2016 to award $99,700 in Corridor Preservation Funds
         programmed for 2018 and $1,709,600 in Sales Tax Funds programmed for 2021. On 12/13/2016 the

         Commissioner Froerer moved to approve the Local Transportation Funding Agreement with Ogden City to

         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Froerer – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye
    4. LOCAL TRANSPORTATION FUNDING AGREEMENT WITH OGDEN CITY TO ESTABLISH TERMS FOR THE 26TH
       STREET PROJECT.
         Brooke Stewart, with County Community Development, stated that this project is to improve 0.42 miles of
         roadway along 26th Street from Wall Avenue to Washington Blvd. The City submitted three applications during
         the 2016 application cycle and was awarded partial funding on those projects. This project and the 21st Street
         project were awarded the full requested amount from Corridor Preservation Funds and 25% of requested Sales
         Tax Funding. Rather than producing two projects that fall short of what Ogden envisioned, in 2018 the City
         formally withdrew the awarded funding for the 21st Street project ($1,512,450) in order to complete 26th Street.
         The City applied for additional funding during the 2018 application cycle to be able to complete it. In 2016 the
         Commission approved WACOG’s recommendation for $62,600 in Corridor Preservation Funds and $1,206,000
         in Sales Tax Funds. In November of 2018 WACOG approved a recommendation to award an additional
         $2,781,050 in Sales Tax Funds and the Commission approved that as part of the 2018 priority list on 12/13/2018.
         This funding agreement includes both of the funding awards from 2016 and 2018 for a total of $62,600 in
         Corridor Preservation Funds programmed for 2017 and $3,987,050 in Sales Tax Funds programmed for 2021.
         Commissioner Harvey moved to approve the Local Transportation Funding Agreement with Ogden City to
         establish terms for the 26th Street project; Commissioner Froerer seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Froerer – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye
    5. RESOLUTION AUTHORIZING THE USE OF FUNDS FOR PROJECTS ON THE PRIORITY LIST APPROVED BY
       WACOG - RESOLUTION 37-2019.
         Brooke Stewart, with County Community Development, stated that this resolution relates to the amendment
         request received from Weber County for the 3300 South corridor preservation project. It was approved for
         recommendation by WACOG at its June meeting. In 2015 the county was approved $1.1 million to preserve
         the corridor along 3300 South from 3300 to 4700 West. The County began survey and design work in the fall
         of 2017 and started acquiring the right-of-way early this spring. The project has encountered some
         unanticipated expenses from rising property values and proximity damages that have subsequently increased
         project costs. County Engineering submitted an amendment request to the transportation subcommittee seeking
         an additional $375,000 dollars in Corridor Preservation Funds to acquire the remaining parcels and WACOG
         approved that recommendation on June 3, 2019.
         Commissioner Harvey moved to adopt Resolution 37-2019 authorizing the use of funds for projects on the
         priority list approved by WACOG; Commissioner Froerer seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Froerer – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye
