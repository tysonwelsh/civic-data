all comments submitted during the given time period would be relayed to the City Council.

Mr. Carter and Development Services Director Jason Lethbridge addressed questions and
comments from the Council Member. Mr. Lethbridge said that Staff believes that the process
explained for public hearings was adequate under the circumstances and provides the

General Plan Timing

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services, explained that the Planning Commission had been
through the open house process for the General Plan and they were ready to move through the
public hearing phase of the process. He said that the Planning Commission was required to
hold public hearings and prepare and recommend a draft General Plan to the City Council;
however, he said, the Planning Commission had expressed a strong desire to be directed by the
City Council. Council Member McDougal said that he had met with some Planning Commission
members and they had expressed a desire to receive feedback from the City Council prior to
their final recommendation.

Lengthy discussion was held and the Council agreed to review the General Plan as it relates to
their districts but to also review the Plan and it relates to the City as a whole. Mayor Staggs said
the consensus of the City Council was to adopt a final General Plan before the end of the Fiscal
Year.

     17.b       Resolution No. 20-27 - Accepting up to 2.35 acres of Property to be dedicated
                to Riverton City as part of the Proposed “River Springs Estates” Subdivision,
                Subject to Final Plat Approval by the Riverton City Planning Commission

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that in April of 2017 the Planning
Commission approved a Preliminary Plat for the proposed River Springs Estates Subdivision
located at 13702 South Lovers Lane. That project included land that had been defined by
Riverton City as a geologic hazard area, specifically being an area of impact should the canal to
the west breach or otherwise cause flooding. Riverton City adopted an ordinance to address
such situations, the Geologic Hazards Ordinance, which allows the City to protect areas of
potential impact, and includes tools the City can use to do so. He said it allows development lots
to be clustered away from areas of potential geologic hazard by keeping the lot count the same
but with smaller lot sizes.

Mr. Lethbridge further explained that the ordinance had been utilized with the proposed River
Springs Estates Subdivision. He said the western portion of the subdivision included 2.35 acres
that are within the defined hazard area. The preliminary plat shows that land dedicated to the
City and kept as open space, with the allowed 21 lots clustered into the remaining area. He said
the result was that many of the lots within the subdivision were below ½ acre in size, which is
consistent with the allowances in the Geologic Hazards Ordinance. However, the ordinance
requires that the City Council formally accept the land dedication of land before the Planning
Commission can approve the Final Plat of the subdivision. He said this issue was before the
Page | 7 Minutes – City Council Meeting
April 7, 2020



Council, not to approve the subdivision itself, but to accept the dedication of the 2.35 acres,
subject to the final approval of the subdivision by the Planning Commission.

Mr. Lethbridge said that Staff had reviewed the submitted material and found it to be compliant
with the requirements of the ordinance. He also said that the proposed resolution included
requirements that the property be fenced where adjacent to the proposed building lots on the
east side.

Mr. Lethbrdige and City Attorney Ryan Carter addressed comments from the Council Members.
