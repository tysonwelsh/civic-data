Proposed Land Transfer and Development Agreement - 550 24th Street
Community and Economic Development Director Cooper provided an overview of the proposed Land Transfer and
\ Development Agreement and Reimbursement Agreement (LTDA) for property located at 550 24" Street. The agreement is with
/ Henry Walker Construction, LLC for the transfer of the 2.7 acres of property for the development of 26 townhome units. The
proposed LTDA would convey the property to Henry Walker Homes, LLC at no cost and the Reimbursement Agreement would
reimburse the developer up to $870,000 for the construction of a new Porter Avenue public street. A combination of
Redevelopment Agency (RDA) funds and Capital Improvement Funds (CIP) are proposed to be used for the reimbursement. Mr.
Cooper then discussed the history of the property, which contains two blighted commercial buildings that were attractants to
frequent vandalism and crime. Based on the proximity to significant landmarks and city amenities, the Administration and City
Council made it a priority to move forward with the following:
e Acquisition and demolition of the blighted buildings and surrounding property
e Facilitate the development of new infill housing that would enhance the surrounding community and provide a
high standard of architecture, quality and livability unique to the east central neighborhood
Chair Choberka asked if the property was acquired using Quality Neighborhoods funding, to which Administrative staff
answered yes.
Mr. Cooper provided the timeline of actions related to the subject property dating from FY2016 to FY2023; the most
recent action was City Council approval of CIP funding for an additional amount toward the street improvements for the project.
While similar infill developments have been successfully undertaken by Ogden City in the past, it was determined that it would be
to the Agency’s advantage to partner with a developer in order to ensure this project’s success. Advantages to forming a
partnership include:
e Economies of scale — Lower costs of construction and development; frees up Agency resources
e Private investment needed to fund the large amount of construction financing
e Capacity and ability to manage the private HOA and common space
e Existing professional marketing of housing product already in place
In 2021, staff solicited proposals for qualified firms (RFQ) to partner with the RDA to assist in the development of the
Agency’s property in accordance with the Quality Neighborhoods Initiative. The RFQ gave clear guidance as to the Agency’s
vision regarding architecture, product type and development timeline. Two respondents submitted proposals, and Henry Walker
Homes was selected as the qualified respondent. There was discussion among the Board and Department staff regarding the
} publication of the RFQ and the notification method that was used to contact the two developers who responded.
_7 Mr. Cooper presented proposed conceptual plans for development of the site, after which he discussed public
improvements and potential reimbursements associated with the project. He proceeded to address questions provided to him about
the project by Board staff; the first question related to the lack of an affordable housing component for the project. He noted that
the site is located within the greatest concentration of low income and subsidized housing in the City. Within a two to three block
a

radius, there are approximately 690 subsidized housing units (not including the additional 178 units to be built at Capitol Square).
Balance is needed to help sustain revitalization and encourage private investment in the downtown and East Central
neighborhoods. The next question related to why the RDA is not selling the property and if the incentive is truly necessary. Mr.
Cooper noted the vision of the project is to create an exceptional housing product and typically, Ogden City would do this
development. However, with the townhome product proposed, partnering with a developer is to the City’s advantage in terms of
construction financing, HOA management, ownership risk, and other factors. The proposed concept originated from staff and was
described in the RFQ; in order to produce this high-quality product, an incentive is necessary, and the chosen partner is willing to
complete the project for a 10 percent gross margin. This is lower than typical private sector yields and the City typically does not
recover land costs and development costs in City projects. The alternative to the proposal is selling the site and letting a developer
build something to the market, which would entail minimum zoning requirements.

Discussion among the Board and staff centered on the terms of the LTDA relating to the transfer of land and any

Board Business:

Developer Selection Process: Board Executive Director Eller-Smith explained the proposed policy for selection of
developer was developed with significant input from Board and Administrative staff, and the City Attorney. Key elements of the
proposed policy are as follows:

e Requires creation and maintenance of a database of potential developers
e Establishes three ways developers may be selected:

o Public Solicitation—Request for Proposals (RFP), Request for Professional Qualifications (RFQ), or
Request for Bid (RFB). Provides that the executive director may waive a public solicitation if it is in
the best interest of the Agency. Also requires notice to the Board when an Exclusive Negotiation
Agreement (ENA) is executed with the developer.

o Sole Source/Direct Negotiation. Potential developers are identified from the database, informal
interviews are held, and a developer is selected. Justification for bypassing the public solicitation
process is required. Also requires notice to the Board when an ENA is executed with the developer.

o Unsolicited Proposal. The executive director reviews an unsolicited proposal and determines if the
proposal is in the best interest of the Agency. Provides justification for recommending the acceptance
of the proposal with any negotiated revisions. Also requires notice to the board when the Agency
enters into an ENA with a party.

Board and Administrative staff facilitated discussion among the Board regarding the implications of the proposed
policy/process; the Board emphasized they would like to amend the proposed policy/process to ensure that any RFP or RFQ
document will include information about the potential for a developer to receive an incentive from the Agency if they are selected
to partner with the Agency on the project. They also indicated a willingness to form smaller groups or a specific focus group to
continue to evaluate the policy/process and proposed needed adjustments before the policy is placed on an agenda for
consideration and possible action.
