Item 1: A resolution approving an agreement between Provo City and Xerces Society for
Invertebrate Conservation to make Provo a member of Bee City USA. (25-014) 0:06:07

Councilor Whipple presented a resolution to approve an agreement between Provo City and the
Xerces Society for Invertebrate Conservation, which would establish Provo as a member of Bee
City USA. She explained that she learned about the Bee City USA program at a conference in
Portland and believed it would align well with Provo's environmental goals. The program
recognizes cities that implement practices supporting pollinators, particularly native bees.
Whipple highlighted that while there are two Bee Campuses in Utah, no cities in the state have
yet received the Bee City designation.

Councilor Whipple outlined several benefits of becoming a Bee City, including encouraging the
growth of native plant species, identifying alternatives to pesticides, creating healthy nesting
habitats for pollinators, and promoting education and awareness regarding pollinator
conservation.

To achieve Bee City designation, the city must:

       Pass a resolution, which has already been drafted and reviewed by legal staff.
       Commit to Bee City membership.
       Pay an annual membership fee of $500, which Councilor Whipple offered to help

       Designate a liaison and establish a committee.
       Create a webpage and install educational signage.
       Host an annual educational event.
       Develop and implement a program to expand pollinator habitats.
       Track and report annual progress on habitat enhancements.

Councilor Whipple noted that Provo already engages in many of the activities required for Bee
City membership. She collaborated with the city's sustainability coordinator to ensure that
meeting these requirements would not impose a significant additional burden on city staff.

Council members raised questions and concerns regarding the resolution, including potential
conflicts with existing yard maintenance codes and enforcement policies, whether the initiative
could lead to an increase in unkempt yards, the necessity of the $500 annual fee, and the amount
of staff time required for implementation.

Following the discussion, Councilor Handley moved to affirm the proposal and schedule the item
for a Night Meeting vote in two weeks, allowing city staff time to submit the application to the
Xerces Society. Councilor Hoban seconded the motion.

The motion passed with a vote of 5-2, with Councilors MacKay and Bogdin opposed.

Item 2: A report regarding ADU Enforcement (December 2024) (25-011) 0:35:50

Scott Johnson presented the monthly update on Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) enforcement.
He reported that in December 2024, three new ADU enforcement cases were opened. Two
notices of violation were issued, and one case was closed as unfounded. Additionally, 11 new
rental license applications for ADUs were received.

       106 total ADU cases were opened.
       53 cases were resolved before reaching the court process.
       16 cases were determined to be unfounded.
       23 cases resulted in notices of violation being issued.
       2 cases proceeded to the legal process.
       40 cases were resolved in total.

Council Discussion and Feedback:

located at 577 East 400 South, from the one-family residential (R1.6) zone to the low
density residential (LDR) zone. Maeser neighborhood. (PLRZ20240234) 0:50:11

Mary Barnes, Planner, presented an ordinance amending the zone map classification of real
property located at 577 East 400 South in the Maeser neighborhood, changing the designation
from the One-Family Residential (R1.6) zone to the Low-Density Residential (LDR) zone. This
presentation followed a previous discussion in December.

The applicant revised his concept plan based on Council feedback, incorporating the following
changes:

       Replacing proposed townhomes with a single-family home in the backyard.
       Removing alley access.
       Including the capability for an accessory apartment in the new home.

Barnes noted that even if the property is rezoned, the project must still go through project plan
approval and subdivision processes.

Council Discussion and Feedback:
Council members inquired about:

       The proposed basement layout for a potential accessory dwelling unit (ADU).
       Landscaping plans, which will be addressed during the project plan approval process.
       Access and parking for the new home.

discussion and potential action.

February 4.

Item 7: A discussion regarding Tiered Water Rates (25-013) 1:39:36

Gary Calder and Keith Larson presented on proposed changes to water rates and tier structures.
Calder highlighted the city's extensive water infrastructure efforts, emphasizing the aging
condition of Provo’s water distribution system. He stressed the necessity of continued investment
in maintenance and capital improvements to prevent failures seen in other regions. Calder also
commended Provo residents for their conservation efforts and explained that the proposed tiered
rate structure aimed to further encourage conservation through simplification.

Key Points Discussed:

       Infrastructure Needs: Provo's water system contains aging components, with significant
        portions exceeding their estimated lifespan, leading to increased breaks and leaks. Larson
        noted that while generally well-maintained, Provo's system experiences slightly higher
        break rates than neighboring cities.
       Current Investment: The city's current annual investment in water infrastructure
        maintenance and replacement is approximately $9 million. Experts recommend
        increasing this amount to between $14 million and $18 million to maintain reliability and
        service quality over time.
       Proposed Rate Increase: A 12% rate increase was proposed, raising the average
        monthly water bill from $48 to $54. Larson explained that the increase would fund
        planned improvements, accommodate inflation, and ensure the continued delivery of
        quality water services.

       Tier Structure Changes: The recommendation includes shifting from a 2-tier to a 3-tier
        system, applicable year-round. The new structure aims to promote conservation, ensure
        affordability for low-usage customers, and align rates more closely with the actual cost of
        providing water service.

Council Discussion and Concerns:
Council members raised concerns about how the changes would impact specific groups:

       Large Families and HOAs: Concerns were expressed about the financial burden on
        larger households and HOAs, where communal landscape watering could lead to higher
        charges.
       High Water Users: Some councilors worried that the highest tier could
        disproportionately impact larger properties or multigenerational households.

Councilor Handley motioned requesting staff to return to the February 11, 2025 night meeting
to adopt the proposed rates and tiers and to consider multifamily and commercial rates.
Councilor Whipple seconded.
