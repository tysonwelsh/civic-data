BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Conditional Use Permit for approximately
5.77 acres located on the east side of Vista Station Blvd., at approximately 483 W. 12300 S.
(Exhibit B & C). The property is currently zoned OS and RA1, and the portion of the property
subject to this application is located in the OS zoned area. The applicant is requesting that
a Conditional Use Permit be approved to allow for the development of a currently vacant
portion of the site as a new wireless telecommunications tower and facility for AT&T.

The Municipal Building Authority of Draper City owns the property and the property is
primarily used as parking for Galena Hills Park (East), and as a materials storage area for
the Parks Department. The City Council, acting as the Municipal Building Authority,
approved of a lease of the subject area to Cingular Wireless PCS, LLC on January 10, 2023.

      screened from view, from public rights of way, on-site parking areas or adjacent

      public rights-of-way.
   4. The proposed accessory building and equipment do not conform to required

   5. The height of the proposed monopole exceeds sixty feet (60’).
   6. The proposed communication and power lines between the accessory equipment
      building and antenna structures are not located underground.

Per DCMC Subsection 9-41-070(C), “Specific fencing and screening plans for each facility shall
be submitted to the city in conjunction with any conditional use permit application. Higher grade
fencing, more aesthetic fence designs, and landscaping may be required for the purposes of
visual screening, architectural compatibility, site compatibility, and other impact mitigation.”

The proposed fencing (Exhibit G) indicates the use of standard chain link fencing topped
with barbed wire. Per the proposed fencing elevations the fence will exceed six feet (6’) in
height, however the applicant has not provided an overall dimensioned height for the
fencing. Of note, the height of the fencing shown in Exhibit G is inconsistent with the
fencing height shown on the elevation drawings in Exhibit H. Fencing in excess of 6’ in
height and up to a maximum of ten feet (10’) in height requires Planning Commission
approval as part of a Conditional Use Permit. The Planning Commission will also need to
determine if higher grade fencing as outlined in DCMC Subsection 9-41-040(C) will be
required, or if the fencing as proposed is acceptable. If the Planning Commission
determines that higher grade fencing is required, a specific condition should be added to
this Conditional Use Permit stating what the requirements and mitigations that will be
required of the applicant in revised drawings. During the initial review of application
materials and correspondence with the application, staff has recommend to the applicant
that they provide higher grade fencing and landscape screening as part of the proposal.
The applicant has elected to continue with the drawings as submitted and proposed in
their request for Conditional Use Permit approval from the Planning Commission.

Where the actual location of the adjacent parking and assumed access right-of-way is not
indicated or identified on the plans, staff is unable to determine an exact setback distance,
however staff can approximate the nearest wheel stop and parking area is located between
fifty feet (50’) and sixty feet (60’) from the nearest corner of the leased area. The rail line
right-of-way is also approximately fifty-four feet (54’) from the lease area. Staff does not
believe that there are any negative impacts created by the proposed locations for the
monopole, equipment building, or other accessory equipment, for which mitigation would
be needed with respects to the proposed setbacks from property lines and other features
of the site or adjacent properties.

Per DCMC Subsection 9-41-070(E)(2), “No monopole with antennas and antenna support
structures shall exceed a height of one hundred feet (100').”

The proposed elevation drawings (Exhibit H) show the antenna support structures on the
monopole approximately five inches (5”) higher than the one-hundred feet (100’) height
limit. The Planning Commission does not have the ability to waive or modify the height
above the one-hundred feet (100’) limit. Staff has included a proposed condition of
approval in this report should the Planning Commission approve the Conditional Use
Permit, that revised drawings be provided to Community Development Department staff as
part of the building permit review process showing compliance with the height limit.

Criteria For Approval.
The criteria for review and potential approval of a conditional use permit request is found
in Subsection 9-5-080(E) of the DCMC. This section depicts the standard of review for such
requests as:

         E. Approval Standards: The standards of this subsection shall apply to the issuance of a
             conditional use permit:
          1. A conditional use permit may be issued for a use to be located within a zone where
             the particular conditional use is allowed by the use regulations of the zone.
          2. Reasonable conditions may be imposed as necessary to substantially mitigate
             reasonably anticipated detrimental effects of the proposed use. These conditions may
             include conditions concerning use, construction, character, location, landscaping,
             screening, parking, hours of operation, and other matters relating to the purposes
             and objectives of this section. Such conditions shall be expressly set forth in the
             motion authorizing the conditional use permit.
          3. If the reasonably anticipated detrimental effects of a proposed conditional use
             cannot be substantially mitigated by the imposition of reasonable conditions to
             achieve compliance with applicable standards, the conditional use may be denied.
          4. The following factors shall be reviewed and considered in determining whether a
             conditional use permit application should be approved, approved with conditions, or
             denied because reasonable conditions cannot be imposed to achieve compliance with
             applicable standards:
               a. The harmony and compliance of the proposed use with the objectives and
                   requirements of the city's general plan and this title;
               b. The suitability of the specific property for the proposed use;
               c. Whether the proposed use or facility may be injurious to potential or existing
                   development in the vicinity;
               d. The economic impact of the proposed facility or use on the surrounding area;
               e. The aesthetic impact of the proposed facility or use on the surrounding area;
               f. The safeguards proposed or provided to ensure adequate utilities, transportation
                   access, drainage, parking, loading space, lighting, screening, landscaping, open
                   space, fire protection, and pedestrian and vehicular circulation;
               g. The safeguards provided or proposed to prevent noxious or offensive emissions
                   such as noise, glare, dust, pollutants and odor from the proposed facility or use;
                   and
               h. The impact of the proposed facility or use on the health, safety, and welfare of the
                   city, the area, and persons owning or leasing property in the area.
          5. The planning commission may request additional information as may be reasonably
             needed to determine whether the requirements of this subsection E can be met.

Additionally, DCMC Subsection 9-41-070(H) contains additional criteria for review and
potential approval of a Conditional Use Permit for a Wireless Telecommunications Facility.

The applicant’s responses to the criteria necessary for approval of a Conditional Use Permit
are included as Exhibit I. Staff notes that in the applicant’s statements do not directly
correlate with the specific criteria for approval listed in ordinance, but do address some
aspects of the ordinance criteria.

In the applicants responses (Exhibit I) the statement is made that all radio equipment and
antennas are to be disguised and screened from public view. Staff finds no evidence of this
being occurring within the submitted plans. Additionally the architecture of the equipment
building is purported to be designed to blend with surrounding landscaping, however there
is no existing landscaping or landscaping being proposed with this request. The current
access drive to the subject site is unpaved, and therefore not completely in compliance
with local regulations as claimed in the applicant’s response. The applicant’s statement also
indicates that the design of the monopole is a stealth design that will not naturally be seen
on the subject property and surrounding community, however this is also not true as the
design is not stealth per the standards of DCMC 9-41-050(B) as the facility design will be
easily seen from adjacent properties, will have a visual impact, and is being referred to the
Planning Commission for decision.

The applicant did not address approval criteria pertaining to reasonably anticipated
detrimental effects, harmony and compliance with the City’s General Plan, suitability of the
specific property for the proposed use, or proposed safeguards.

The applicant did indicate in the response letters that the monopole will allow future co-
location.

Statement of No Variance Required Findings/Burden of Proof

Draper City Municipal Code Subsection 9-5-080(D)(1)(h)
The site for the proposed use is adequate in size and shape.

AT&T is proposing a site design for this project which includes wireless communication antennas
attached to a paint‐to‐match monopole and in accordance with the Draper City Code and Ordinances.
All radio equipment and antennas are to be disguised and screened from public view. The requested
height of the antenna design is the minimum height capable to serve the required target areas as well as
not interfere with the existing network. The height requested in the Conditional Use Permit takes into
account City limitations and requirements for colocation. AT&T uses the most advanced technology to
design the equipment shelter so as to blend the architecture with the surrounding community and
landscaping, thereby minimizing the visual impact of the site.

The proposed location has sufficient access to streets and highways that are adequate in width and
pavement type to carry the quantity and quality of traffic generated by the proposed use.

The proposed project is near the I‐15/SR‐71 intersection and the growing commercial and residential
areas nearby. Galena Hills Park offers many areas with vacant land and ample space away from route
view, which provides a qualified location to serve the required target areas. There are adequate access
methods using the driveways, access and maintenance routes, and public right of way. All the roadways
and access ways to the facility are in compliance with local, state and federal regulations concerning
width and pavement.

The proposed use will not have an adverse effect upon adjacent or abutting properties.

The project is proposed within Open Space zoning and will not have an adverse effect upon adjacent or
abutting properties as it is a stealth design that will naturally not be seen on the subject property and
the surrounding community. The project will provide a public benefit of better wireless
telecommunications and data services to the surrounding neighborhoods and community.

Project Objectives

The current network surrounding the proposed location does not meet AT&T’s performance goals or user
expectations. Increasing service in the area requires the development of a new WCF that can house multiple
antennas and can serve users with a clear view to much of the surrounding area.

To provide consistently reliable service, the WCF requires a clear view (line of sight) to the roads, offices,
buildings, and homes where users live and work. In addition, service must reach all practical user locations,
both indoor and outdoor. A tower height that is generally higher than much of the surrounding tree and
building clutter increases indoor service. This effect is due to the avoidance of obstacles that a wireless signal
must pass through to connect a user.

The network in the area currently provides FirstNet services, and the proposed telecommunications facility is
also planned for FirstNet services deployment.

FirstNet is the only nationwide wireless broadband communications platform dedicated to America’s first
responders and public safety community. This reliable, highly-secure communications platform brings
reliability to public safety agencies and first responders – helping them make faster and better decisions to
keep themselves and the public safe.


Safety Risks

The current network surrounding the proposed facility is not currently meeting AT&T’s performance goals or
user expectations, and its performance will only decrease as time goes on unless the network’s capabilities are
expanded. If the network is not improved, it could reach a point of non-functionality in the future.

Due to the ubiquity of cell phone use, unreliable networks pose a safety risk. The overall reduction in
conventional landline voice service has resulted in the trend toward emergency calls made on wireless devices.
If the network is severely degraded, it can be difficult or impossible for a user to make a call in the case of an
emergency, which poses severe safety risks.

AT&T proposes to improve the network capability in this area of Draper City for both current and future use.
The proposed site adjacent to 12375 S 550 W, Draper, UT is designed to provide effective network service to
the surrounding growing areas. The topography of the proposed location allows a clear view to much of the
surrounding area, and its location places it among population and demand it is intended to serve. The
proposed site will greatly improve AT&T’s network performance and corresponding user experience in the
area.

GENERAL INFORMATION
Site Selection

Customer demand drives the need for new cell sites. Data relating to incomplete and dropped calls is
gathered, drive-tests are conducted, and scientific modeling using sophisticated software is evaluated. Once
the area requiring a new site is identified, a target ring on a map is provided to a real estate professional to
begin a search for a suitable location.

During an initial reconnaissance, properties selected for evaluation for installation of a cell site must be located
in the general vicinity of the ring, possess an appropriate zoning designation, and contain adequate space to
accommodate an antenna structure and supporting radio equipment. The size of this space will vary
depending on the objective of the site. The owners of each prospective location are notified to assess their
interest in partnering with AT&T.

Four key elements are considered in the selection process:

    very specific terms and conditions.

•   Construction: Construction constraints and costs must be reasonable from a business perspective, and the
