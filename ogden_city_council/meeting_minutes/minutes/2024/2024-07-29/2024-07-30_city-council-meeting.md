Joint Proclamation proclaiming August 5, 2024, as “Ogden City Fire
Department Day” in recognition of the Department’s 150th Anniversary
           A joint proclamation proclaiming August 5, 2024, as “Ogden City Fire Department Day” in recognition of the
Department’s 150th Anniversary came before the Council for consideration. Council member Hyer read the joint proclamation,
after which COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED TO APPROVE THE JOINT PROCLAMATION. MOTION WAS
SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER GRAF, ALL VOTING AYE. Council member Choberka was not present when
this vote was taken.
           Chair Richey then presented the joint proclamation to Fire Chief Mathieu. Chief Mathieu thanked the Mayor and Council
for this recognition; it is an honor and privilege to serve the community of Ogden and carry on the rich traditions of the City. He
thanked Ashley Phillips, Fire Captain, for his efforts to coordinate the celebration of the 150th anniversary of the Fire Department
and invited him to address the Council. Mr. Phillips stated that he and many others have worked on this project for the last two
years; he discussed the history of the Fire Department over the last 150 years and stated many of the historical stories of the
Department and its employees have been assembled into a book. He thanked the Mayor and Council, and the many current and
past employees of the Fire Department for their support and efforts to assemble these stories and he presented the Council with a
copy of the book. He stated he is proud to work for the Fire Department and is the fourth generation of his family to do so; he
looks forward to the 150th Anniversary celebration event.

Proposed Parking and Mobility Enterprise Fund. Proposed Ordinance 2024-
23 amending the Municipal Code to revise or adopt provisions pertaining to
enterprise funds
          A memo from the Management Services Department regarding the proposed creation of the Parking and Mobility
Enterprise Fund came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the purpose of the fund is to account for revenues
and expenses associated with development and management of a pay parking system. The management of the new enterprise fund
and staff will be placed in Management Services. The proposal also includes adding existing enterprise funds to the ordinance that
are not currently included. Management of the medical fund will be in the Fire Department and management of the Airport and
Business Depot Ogden (BDO) funds will be in the Community and Economic Development department.
          Deputy Comptroller Jensen summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
proposed ordinance amendments; for a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-23, entitled:
                    “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Section 2-14-13 by
                    adopting a new Subsection C; by amending Subsection 2-4-14.C; by amending Section 2-4-15 by adopting a
                    new Subsection H; by amending Section 2-4-17 by adopting a new Subsection D to revise or adopt provisions
                    pertaining to enterprise funds; by making technical changes; and by providing that this ordinance shall become
                    effective immediately upon posting after final passage.”
          ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER HYER AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS,
ORDINANCE 2024-23 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL
MEMBERS BLAIR, GRAF, HYER, MYERS, VICE CHAIR WHITE, AND CHAIR RICHEY. VOTING NO – NONE.
Council member Choberka was not present when this vote was taken.
          Vice Chair White asked for confirmation that this action does not just create the Parking and Mobility Enterprise Fund,
but it also provides for proper accounting and management of the fund and other enterprise funds in the City. Ms. Jensen stated
that is correct.

Public Comments
          Teresa Bramwell stated that she has had two issues at her home this week involving homeless individuals; these
individuals have been camping on her property and she was able to speak to one of them and find out why he arrived on her front
porch. This is not something that she can fix, but perhaps the City can fix it. She called 9-1-1 to report the homeless encampment
on her front porch, but no one ever came to her property. While she was waiting for emergency responders, the individual who
had been camping on her porch arrived and told her that he had been evicted from his residence; he told her that he had previously
lived in the home where the Family Dollar is now located and he planned to call her to ask if he could place his belongings on her
porch, but he could not find her phone number. She stated that she has ‘no trespassing’ signs along the front of her property, and
she asked him if that deterred him from entering her property and placing items on her porch and he told her no and that he
planned to call her. She told him that he did not have permission to have his belongings on her porch and that he needed to leave
her property, so he proceeded to move to the Family Dollar property. She stated these are the kinds of things she is dealing with as
a property owner, and she thought the City should be aware of it.
