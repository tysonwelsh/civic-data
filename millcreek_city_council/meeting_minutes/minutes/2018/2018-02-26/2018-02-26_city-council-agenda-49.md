1. Discussion of Parleys Interchange EIS; Rebecca Stromness, UDOT

Rebecca Stromness, UDOT Region 2 Project Manager, introduced the Environmental Impact
Study and how Millcreek will participate. UDOT has taken over delegation from the Federal
Highway Administration as the lead agency. There are also cooperating regulatory agencies
involved and the municipalities are the participating agencies. UDOT will ask the cities for
feedback on the project. Stromness highlighted a map of the study area at the mouth of
Parley’s Canyon. The interchange was built in the 1960’s, so the purpose of the study is to
improve the intersections. She highlighted 4 areas of why improvements are needed; safety,
traffic flow, regional mobility, and updated design. The draft project purpose is to improve
the level of service at Parley’s Interchange and improve regional mobility in 2050. Some
alternatives being considered is to: take no action, reconfigure Parley’s Interchange,
reconfigure 3300 S. and 3900 S. interchanges, improve connections, look at transit options,
and a combination of any of the above. The I-80 Major Investment Study was conducted in

2. Discussion on Operations; General Request (i.e. flashing signs, clearing sidewalks,
etc.), Striping & Signing, and Sidewalk Maintenance
Mayor Silvestrini said he has been receiving requests from residents and Councilmembers for
various projects. The City needs to prioritize projects to get things done and properly
budgeted for. Rita Lund said she wants to know how the Council wants to approach the
requests and discretionary spending. Miller has some ideas on how to do this and will be
talking about it in the budget discussion next month. He brought up one solution of dividing
discretionary funding equally to the 4 districts and 1 part to the Mayor for other projects. He
said this year the discretionary spending was significantly reduced. There is an existing
contract with operations to get yearly longitudinal and crosswalk restriping. Street signs are
on an 8-10 year replacement cycle but can be replaced sooner upon a visual inspection. The
signs have a date on the corner of when they were installed. The sidewalk maintenance is to
fix vertical displacements and after a displacement is more than 1 4 inch, it needs to be
replaced. The City ordinance dictates that the resident must replace the sidewalk.

Council Member Uipi asked how the project list was compiled. Miller said when requests
come in, that Lund tracks them on a spreadsheet, they then get ranked by safety and available
funding, and then the best judgement gets used at picking the projects. Some requests have
been transferred from the County. Council Member Uipi wants to look at what work has been
done in the last couple of years so that future projects can be distributed evenly in the
districts. She wants a thoughtful approach to spreading out resources. Miller said some areas
are newer than others, so funding may not be needed in other areas for a while. Council

Member Catten asked if the rankings have changed from when the list came from the
County. Miller said it’s an engineering judgement on how projects get ranked. Lund said
there are some quick fixes, like new light bulbs, but that is maintenance and not operations.
Council Member Uipi asked if the costs are the same for the City as they were for the County

Geilmann said on March 20" at 5 p.m., Laurie Johnson will have a budgeting session for the
Council and train them on fixed and flexible costs. She will have the Council prioritize the
flexible dollars. Council Member Marchant asked how many projects items are on the list
that will be discussed. Miller said a lot. Council Member Uipi wants the list of to-do projects

Council Member Jackson asked about clearing sidewalks of snow. Lund said the prison
crews have been clearing sidewalks. Council Member Jackson asked if snow removal is a
lump sum. Miller said there isn’t a line item in the budget for sidewalk clearing, but there is
flexibility in the snow removal contract. Council Member Marchant would like the staff's
recommendation on priorities and have the Council evaluate the recommendation. Mayor
Silvestrini said the Council will see the recommendation based on the data. Miller asked the
Council to send any requests to Lund. Mayor Silvestrini would like to work with Kevin
Smeltzer with Salt Lake County Operations to come up with a consistent message to give to
residents on snow removal and to have an education component to it with a list of resources
for people for assistance. Council Member Uipi expressed thanks for having the sidewalks
cleared on the south side of 3300 S. Mayor Silvestrini stated that he had noticed some
commercial properties that had not cleared their sidewalks within 72 hours of the storm.
Council Member Jackson asked if clearing business sidewalk property could be a component
of business licensing. Mayor Silvestrini said the City could send letters to businesses. He
wants to help them comply before using punitive enforcement. He thinks the Council should
give direction to staff on how to approach enforcement. The Mayor said problems are usually
with cars parked on the street and that is enforced through UPD, not City code enforcement.
The Mayor would like staff to draft an ordinance on administrative parking regulations that
would allow code enforcement to issue tickets for snow parking, trail parking, and
abandoned vehicles as a civil matter and not criminal. Council Member Uipi suggested
giving ample time of warning to residents for street parking because Millcreek is a new city.
Mayor Silvestrini said code enforcement is complaint-driven. Miller told the Council that if
there is an emergency, please don’t prioritize something, just get it taken care of. The City
can call the emergency operations hotline (385-468-6101).

4. Reports

4.1 Mayor’s Report

Mayor Silvestrini said he has been spending a significant amount of time at the
legislature testifying on behalf of bills. He said S.B. 57 is a bill to increase the penalty
for knowingly killing a police dog. It passed in the House so it will now go to the
Governor. The bill came about because of 2 police dogs that were killed in Millcreek.
Today, there was a Senate committee hearing on the identity theft bill, which
redefines the term device to allow it to apply to any type of tool used to reproduce
fake ids and it criminalizes possession of more than one such tools. It also
criminalizes possession of 100 or more identifications of people. The bill gives the
District Attorney’s Office a tool in prosecuting midlevel identity theft. It passed in the
House and is on its way to the Senate.

He brought up that the Council has had discussion on the closing of the transfer
station in South Salt Lake. He met with Pam Roberts, Director of Wasatch Front
Waste and Recycling District and the Mayor of South Salt Lake City to figure out
alternatives to keep the transfer station open. He said the consequence of it closing
could be an increased fee for garbage pick-up. Council Member Jackson asked about
Ace Recycling, the alternative transfer station. The Mayor said it is an option to use,
but it would cost Millcreek about $700,000 more per year. They are trying to come up
with a solution that does not cost as much. Council Member Marchant said part of the
problem with using Ace is that it is a private facility and it would be better for the
City to be in with a public owned facility. He also said that residents of South Salt
Lake do not pay a garbage collection fee because the transfer station is located there.
Mayor Silvestrini said if the current agreement goes away, they will have to pay too.
South Salt Lake has about 32 years left on the 50-year contract stating that the
