5. Stakeholder Reports
A. Unified Police Department
Detective Dawn Larsen shared her report (Attachment A) with 36 calls for service, 10 of those
had reports written; most were traffic related, three were talking with residents regarding
plowing and putting snow in the roadway. There were a few traffic accidents, fraud cases, and
Officer Wilson caught someone going 100 mph on the straightaway.
B. Unified Fire Authority
Chief Bryan Case reviewed his report (Attachment B) noting call volumes were low. Several
public assists or false alarms, one being burnt food on a stovetop with no fire upon arrival. In
addition, a passerby reported a campfire somewhere along Emigration Canyon Road and was
adamant about remaining anonymous; Medic Engine 119 drove the length of the canyon and
found nothing. A recruit camp just graduated on Jan. 16, with 15 firefighters. Camp 59 starts
February 3 with 36 more recruits. He encouraged the public and council members to check out
and sign up a CPR class on the Unified Fire website at https://unifiedfire.org/classes/cpr-class/
which are offered through the American Heart Association. Youth are invited to sign up for the
classes as well. He announced Chief Larsen retired after many years throughout the county.
He reminded everyone to check their smoke alarms, install carbon monoxide alarms, and have
emergency exit drills even during the winter. With less snow this year they aren’t having the
same issues as last year with vents being blocked, but he reminded everyone to keep their
house number visible from the streets for all first responders. Portable heaters should be kept
at least three feet away from anything that can catch fire, same with water heaters. UFA sent
eight firefighters to help with the LA County fires. They worked 24-hour shifts for two full
weeks. This experience is invaluable since they don’t see those types of situations much here,
and these opportunities give our firefighters great institutional knowledge to bring back with
new skills. He discussed the quarterly report in Attachment B, noting 26% of overall calls were
emergent, with only one fire call. Nothing very serious with EMS calls, a few falls and a lot of
public assists and false alarms with one vehicle versus pedestrian and a rollover call. Vast
majority of responses came from the station here at 119, with one response for the first unit
coming in from Station 106, East Millcreek. Ambulance responses were mostly coming out of
Stations 106 and 101 in Millcreek, with one ambulance response coming out of Holladay
Station 104. He discussed some of the issues being raised with the legislative session, with
several UFA personnel keeping track. Specifically, he wanted to bring the council’s attention to
HB 48 and HB 307, which are related to the wildland urban interface areas, and he briefly
reviewed those proposed bills and how they affect UFA. UFA as of now will be recommending
some language changes to HB 307.
Council Member Harris noted that it is difficult to evaluate these bills and offer advice when
they are constantly changing. They need to be followed closely but that will take work as it
changes with every committee.

Mr. Okobia responded overall the MSD and cities are doing well, bringing in more than they
are spending and keeping a healthy fund balance.
Mayor Smolka noted that the financials for 2023 showed the city at almost $700,000
subsidized, which means money was coming from other members of the MSD and referred the
council to a financial analysis he sent out prior to the meeting (Attachment C).
Deputy Mayor Hawkes asked if that was due to a large construction project at that time.
Mayor Smolka said all the money for the Slope Stabilization project came from bond and grant
money, this is for regular stuff like snowplows.
Council Member Harris noted that Attachment C does show the 2023 numbers having the
bond money listed, but it was miniscule compared to many other municipalities. She then
asked about the large amount in the Public Works Operations section.
Mayor Smolka responded that it is for roads, signs, snowplows, drainage work, etc. Some of it
would be project clean-up that was missed as well.
Tolin Hassell confirmed that Public Works Operations did do a lot of the flood repair work.
Council Member Brems asked to confirm that they were in the red after 2023, but this last
audited fiscal year shows they are in the black.
Mr. Okobia clarified that the 2023 paper everyone was looking at (Attachment C) was an
attempt by Marla Howard to allocate costs to individual cities and towns as an analysis; that is
not a financial statement.
Council Member Harris pointed out that when Magna had the earthquake, they required a
large amount of funds as well, but she believes they were able to recoup some of that through
FEMA and other sources of funding. She asked if Emigration Canyon received any similar
assistance with the flood expenses.
Mr. Hassell explained the MSD did apply for assistance through the State Department of
Emergency Management, but he doesn’t believe anything has been received yet in terms of
responses.
Deputy Mayor Hawkes asked why the MSD Administration was the same across the board, not
proportional.
Mr. Okobia reiterated that Attachment C is only an analysis to help the MSD see where money
is going, it is not a financial statement. This is not a mechanism to charge amounts to one city
over another, it is just used to allocate costs to each municipality.
The council asked to table the discussion of FY 2023 until a later date, after the RFP
potentially, and to move on with the presentation.
