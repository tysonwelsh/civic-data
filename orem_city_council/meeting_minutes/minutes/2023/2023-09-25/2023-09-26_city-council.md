Sewer Code Update: Presenter: Reed Price, Assistant Public Works Director and Giles Demke,
Water Reclamation Section Manager

Mr. Price presented earlier this year and this is a follow up to that presentation. Earlier
there was a stand alone Pretreatment Ordinance passed. Mr Price did a quick summary of the
pretreatment ordinance. This work session will focus on the Water Reclamation Code, which is
Chapter 20 of the city code. The things that will change in the code are general housekeeping,
which is updated definitions and standardized correct terminology. Other items changing are the
removal references to most Pretreatment regulations and it will define sewer requirements for
ADUs. Pretreatment regulations were put in their own section, section 11, this will allow the
EPA jurisdiction over the pretreatment regulations only. This gives Orem flexibility with the rest
of the sewer code. The updated code will prohibit the discharging of “flushable wipes”, provide
requirements for acceptance of a private sewer main to public ownership, and enhanced
regulation for fats, oils and grease.

The next step is to finalize changes, open the code up to public comment for 30 days,
comments from the public will be reviewed and considered, and then the updated code will be
presented at a future City Council meeting. The city council had questions about what is
appropriate to put into the sewer system. The council would like to see efforts made to inform
the public of appropriate sewer practices.

Resolution to Adopt the updated City of Orem Transportation Master Plan and amending
the Orem City General Plan to include the updated City of Orem Transportation Master
Plan in Appendix F, Presenter: Sam Kelly, City Engineer

Mr. Kelly introduced John Dorney, who was the consultant with Horrocks Engineers and

will be Orem’s new full-time Transportation Engineer. Mr. Kelly briefly went over the slideshow
that he presented last week to the city council in the work session. This transportation plan is an
update of the 2015 Transportation Master Plan. Ms Millett asked about the projected growth of
Orem. The projected growth takes into account the undeveloped areas, the trends of population
and the growth in neighboring cities. The state is projecting Orem’s buildout growth to be around
120,000. The census numbers are lower than the state projections.

Mr. Macdonald and Mr. Peterson wanted to know what the differences were between the
city plan and the planning commission recommendations. The planning commission
recommended removing the overpasses/interchanges at 800 South and 1200 North, removing the
widening projects related to the 800 East and 800 West corridors, removing the 1600 North from
the project east of State Street, and the 800 West connection to Campus Drive should remain
closed. The city agrees that 800 East and 1600 North projects should be removed but the other
projects have some merit. Mr. Kelly explained some benefits to the overpass/interchanges. He
also spoke about the narrow area on 800 West that has no bike lanes and is a choke point.

The other point highlighted by the planning commission refers to connectivity.
Connectivity can help with traffic circulation, it has some utility connections benefits and it can
provide different access points in an emergency. The benefit of University Parkway is that they
have connected parking lots and State Street does not. Ms. Millett is wondering if MAG would
fund connectivity on State Street. Mr. Kelly explained that the State has $45 million annually to
put towards active transportation and it would be helpful to use MAG money for State Street.
Ms. Millett reminded the public that Ms. Lauret and Ms. Millett are passionate about the
beautification of State Street. Mr. Clark said that when things get rezoned then the city can use
the transportation plan to make future changes.

Ordinance for Campaign Financial Disclosure Requirement for Elected Officials
Presenter: Gary McGinn, Assistant City Attorney

Mr. McGinn explained the ordinance amends and adds to 2-3-4 of the Orem City code
concerning campaign finance disclosures. He continued that section A requires the candidates to

follow the state code for their disclosure statements. Section B requires an elected official to file
an annual financial disclosure each August Ist to cover any contributions or expenditures that
may have happened since the last campaign financial disclosure. If an elected official fails to file
by the due date then the city recorder can accept the disclosure late with a fine. After 10 days
then it is an infraction with a fine of no more than 750 dollars. Elected officials who hold office
as of today will need to file a financial disclosure report by October 15, 2023.

Ms. Millett suggested moving this year's disclosure to October 10, 2023. Mr. McGinn
explained that the financial report will cover through September 30, 2023. Ms. Millett also
suggested that we change the file date each year to match the fiscal year of June 30th. The other
changes were to change the extension date to 5 days instead of 10 and to make the fine $250 a
week until the financial disclosure is filed.

Ms. Lauret moved to approve an ordinance amending section 2-3-4 Campaign Finance
Disclosure of the Orem City Code to adopt the new transparent regulations as shown in Exhibit
A. Mr. Lambson seconded the motion. Those voting yes: David Young, David Spencer, Debby
Lauret, Terry Peterson, Tom Macdonald, LaNae Millett, and Jeff Lambson. The motion passed.
