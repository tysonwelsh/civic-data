    6. WEBER-MORGAN STRIKE FORCE PETITION TO THE WEBER COUNTY COMMISSION, PURSUANT TO
       UTAH CODE ANN. 24-3-103, FOR THE APPROPRIATION TO THE STRIKE FORCE OF $3,928.05
       CURRENTLY HELD IN THE STRIKE FORCE SEIZURE ACCOUNT

       Christopher Crockett, Deputy County Attorney, explained that the Strike Force participates in drug-related
       arrests and the officers often find currency/other property related to the drug related activities. Through
       due process, they notify innocent claimants. When there is no identified claimant to those funds, the
       seizing agency may apply those funds to a public use after receiving permission from the county legislative
       body. The request under this item is to use the funds for the deposit for a future purchase of a canine,
       which can detect electronic devices, which can aid greatly in prosecution of those who commit child
       pornography/sexual child exploitation crimes, etc.         County Sheriff Terry Thompson addressed
       Commissioner Bell’s questions speaking to the canine’s remarkable abilities.
       Commissioner Bell moved to approve the release of $3,928.05 to the Weber-Morgan Strike Force
       currently held in the Strike Force seizure account; Commissioner Ebert seconded.
       Commissioner Ebert – aye; Commissioner Bell – aye; Chair Gibson – aye
H. PUBLIC COMMENTS: All the public comments related to item G.4.
       Lewis Beck, of Eden, gave some background on the problems. He stated that there is a noise ordinance for
       that lake but it is not enforced. He said that the noise in the summer time is out of control, and the
       offenders concentrate on one location, which the residents call “Cocaine Cove” and law enforcement is
       familiar with it. The actual name is Spring Cove. He noted that most of those present today live near that
       area and hear that noise all summer long, and as the water is higher, the sound reverberates across the
       Valley. There was a recent letter on the reservoir issue by the Mayor of Eden, who is working and could
       not be present. Mr. Beck said that the residents can call the Sheriff’s Office, which owns a boat and is not
       out there, but they can’t do anything. He believes it is because there aren’t sufficient resources. The
       problem is getting worse because the law is not enforced and so residents eventually stop calling the
       Sheriff’s Office. He said that the Sheriff’s Office and the commissioners’ credibility is low and citizens
       feel they have to deal with this on their own. The resident’s frustration is extremely high; this problem has
       been ongoing for many years and something needs to be done. The Sheriff’s Office or the law
       enforcement body needs to be given the opportunity to enforce the law, even if for a short period so that
       the offenders will get the message. Closing the lake is not the right solution; it is failure of enforcement.
       Robert Fuller, of Eden, lives directly west of “Cocaine Cove.” He said that the commissioners may not
       understand the gravity of the situation, and would probably be shocked, at what occurs there. He stated

       noise that rattled the light fixtures on the ceiling of his house. From speaking with Sheriff Deputies he
       understands it is a volatile situation, with the darkness, being on the water, the alcohol, etc., and too
       dangerous to enforce their police powers. Mr. Fuller said that if that cove is closed the offenders would

G. 4.   FIRST READING OF ORDINANCE AMENDING THE OVERNIGHT WATERCRAFT USE ORDINANCE FOR
        PINEVIEW RESERVOIR
        Bryan Baron, Deputy County Attorney, stated that the ordinance is to prohibit watercraft use on the
        reservoir between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. As he understands, noise is a large part of the problem. However,
        there are other problems such as people dumping their toilets at night in the reservoir, leaving their trash,
        having campfires on the beach, and racing in the middle of the night. The sheriff deputies just cannot
        enforce the current ordinance and the Sheriff’s Office asked to amend the ordinance. He noted that Lt.
        Toll, of the County Sheriff’s Office, was present to address questions.
        Chair Gibson said that many of the things heard today indicate a much larger problem that has been getting
        worse for years and the Commission recognizes that the issue needs to be continually looked at and this
        ordinance is a good step/tool. Commissioner Bell brought up two items to address next week—whether
        the county can amend the fee ordinance to charge a fee to every boat that goes in the water to help pay for
        more enforcement on the reservoir, and about people sleeping in their boats in non-slips. Chair Gibson
        said that there should be discussion about people sleeping onboard at paid slips, but it may be a different
        issue than the one on the agenda. Commissioner Ebert noted that there is an overall plan, that
        Commissioner Bell has been studying parking in that area, and they will look at the lake in general as to
        where the best places are to drive a specific use.
        Commissioner Ebert moved to approve the first reading of the ordinance amending the overnight
        watercraft use ordinance for Pineview Reservoir; Commissioner Bell seconded.
        Commissioner Ebert – aye; Commissioner Bell – aye; Chair Gibson – aye
