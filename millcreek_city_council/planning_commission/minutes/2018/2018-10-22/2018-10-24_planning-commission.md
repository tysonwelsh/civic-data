1.1 Consideration of ZT-18-010, Amendments to the Millcreek Code Regulating
Massage Establishments

Millcreek Planning Commission Meeting Minutes 24 October 2018 Page 2 of 14
Francis Lilly said in the meeting last month the Planning Commission discussed changes
to existing codes. He highlighted the changes made since the last meeting; definitions for
“massage establishment” (means any place of business in which massage therapy is
practiced by one or more massage therapists, as defined, licensed, and regulated under the
Massage Therapy Practice Act, under Utah Code Annotated Section 58-47b, each
therapist possessing a Millcreek Business License in good standing) and “office building,
multi-tenant” (means a building occupied by multiple tenants used primarily for the
conduct of business relating to administration, clerical services, consulting, or other
services not related to retail sales) would be added, and adding massage establishments
into the office use list in Code Section 19.04.393. The amendment would establish
massage establishments as a permitted use in the RM, C-1, C-2, and C-3 Zones with the
limitation that they would not be allowed within 200 feet of an arterial/major intersection,
unless the massage establishment is located in a multi-tenant office building. The
amendment would also replace existing footnotes to require each practitioner who is not
an employee of the business license to have a Millcreek business license and the premises
shall not be used for any conduct that violates state code. Arterial intersections would
also be defined in the RM Zone as is it written in the commercial zones.

intersections. Lilly said South Salt Lake limits the amount of massage licenses available

Carlson said if Millcreek already has a lot, then why add more. Lilly said the massage

proximity regulation was becoming an issue. Commissioner Carlson asked why the
changes were not with the business licensing provisions.

2.4 Consideration of ZT-18-011, Amendment to the R-1 zones Allowing Dog
Walking and Boarding as a Conditional Use Applicant: Ellen Greeneisen Planner:
Francis Lilly
Francis Lilly said the applicant has operated a dog walking and boarding business out of
her home. Upon being informed that the use is currently not allowed in the R-1-10 zone,
she petitioned the City Council to amend the R-1 zones to allow for dog walking and
boarding as a conditional use. Staff is concerned about the potential adverse impacts of a
residential dog walking or boarding business on adjacent properties and would support
the proposed amendment only if it can be crafted to mitigate the potential of adverse
impacts. The four community councils requested additional time in evaluating the
potential impacts of dog boarding in residential areas. Millcreek is subject to the Salt
Lake County Animal Code which does not limit the number of cats or dogs that can be
owned by a resident. However, uses such as a sportsman’s kennel, which is defined as “a

Millcreek Planning Commission Meeting Minutes 24 October 2018 Page 10 of 14
kennel for the keeping of three to five dogs which has a valid permit from the department
of animal services and is located on a lot of at least one acre,” is allowed as a conditional
use in the R-1-15, R-1-21, and R-1-43 zones. The City also has a legal definition of
kennel, “the keeping of three or more dogs, at least four months old.” This use is not
called out specifically anywhere in the R-1 zones but is presumably allowed as an
accessory use to an existing residential use. However, this definition is in conflict with
the animal code which imposes no limit on the number of dogs a resident can own,
provided they do not cause a nuisance. The issue is not ownership but business activity.
The home business ordinance excludes raising, breeding, training, housing, keeping or
care of animals from home business regulations, and states that this use is subject to a
conditional or permitted use approval process, provided the use is permitted or
conditionally permitted in the zone. Staff notes that residential dog boarding already
occurs in Millcreek. A brief survey of rover.com, a clearinghouse for residential dog-
sitting businesses, indicated about a dozen dog-sitters doing business in Millcreek.

Lilly read the applicant’s proposed code changes:
“Residential dog boarding” means a home-based operation where an owner and
occupier of a single-family residence temporarily cares for no more than four (4)
household dogs, at least four months old, of other persons and provides the following:
(1) enclosed shelter where the dogs are housed during the night to protect them from
weather conditions and minimize noise, and as appropriate during the day;
(2) fenced yard space and other measures to prevent escape;
(3) daily exercise to ensure the dogs remain healthy, calm and well-behaved;
(4) adequate abatement of noise, including barking;
(5) removal of dog and other waste that complies with the health and safety provisions
of Title 8;
(6) defined dog “drop-off” and “pick-up” times between the hours of 8am and 7pm to
minimize noise and traffic disturbances in the neighborhood; and
(7) procedures to ensure dogs accepted for boarding have received all required
vaccinations.
This use would be permitted or conditional in all residential zones.
Staff initially proposed the following additional or alternative considerations:
1. For residential lots between 6,000 square feet and 10,000 square feet, no more than
two dogs may be boarded at a time.
2. For residential lots 10,000 square feet or larger, no more than four dogs may be
boarded at a time.
3. Dogs must be cared for within the residence. Dogs may not be cared for in an
accessory building or garage.
4. No more than two dogs at any one time may be allowed in residential yard areas.
5. The applicant must obtain a kennel license from Salt Lake County Animal Services.
6. Pick-up and drop-off of dogs must occur between the hours of 7:30 am and 6:30 pm.
7. The applicant must maintain a Millcreek business license in good standing.
8. The applicant must walk each dog at least an hour per day while the dogs are being
boarded.
Lilly said these are not like commercial dog kennels and other states have addressed the
issue differently. Staff solicited and received the following recommendation from the

Millcreek Planning Commission Meeting Minutes 24 October 2018 Page 11 of 14
Humane Society with respect to residential dog boarding: “The Humane Society of Utah
recommends that a person offering pet sitting services be licensed, bonded and
insured. A licensed pet sitting business may be subject to taxation of income by the city.
There is no current law requiring that a pet sitting service is licensed or certified,
however, we have some concerns regarding housing multiple animals on a pet sitter's
property. Temporarily housing dogs with different temperaments, medical and
vaccination history, and requirements for care could potentially result in the spread of
illness, altercations, and potential injury to dogs and humans. For example, if a dog bites
someone, would the dog's owner or the pet sitter be held responsible? Along with concern
for the welfare of animals temporarily supervised or boarded at one location that is not
regulated, we recommend keeping pets in their home for their comfort and safety.

Pets that are comfortable and familiar in their home will have less stress from visitors
checking in to feed, water and provide temporary pet sitting care than they would
experience from being removed from their home and temporarily housed at a new
location. A licensed, bonded and insured pet sitter is recommended to offer drop-in
service to the pet's home instead of removing pets to be housed at the pet sitter's property.
Increased stress caused by a new environment, other unfamiliar animals, and an irregular
schedule may cause behavior and health issues for the pet.”

Lilly said the applicant has a sound business model but there is a potential issue for
adverse impacts on neighboring properties. Staff recommended continuance of this
matter until the community councils can offer their recommendations and Salt Lake
County Animal Services can weigh in on the proposal.
