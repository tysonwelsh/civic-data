                                            BRIEFING SESSION – 6:00 P.M.

1.       Mr. Spung turned the time over to Mr. Tracy Cowdell/City Attorney to bring the Commissioners up to date
         on what to expect during the proposed revocation hearing for the Home Town Suites Hotel scheduled to
         be held in February 2023 and to explain their responsibilities during same.

         1.1       Mr. Cowdell explained the reasoning and issues for the City to prove that the entity is non-
                   compliant with their Conditional Use Permit. The hotel in question has asked for an extension of
                   time. He advised the hearing is scheduled to be heard on Feb 8, 2023, in City Council Chambers
                   before the Planning Commission. There was a hearing regarding this same issue with this hotel
                   previously in 2016 and the City is now asking for their use to be revoked as none of the negative
                   elements have improved. The paperwork for this new hearing has been prepared and given to
                   both participating sides of this issue. There will also be a memorandum containing the standards
                   to be followed and a binder furnished that contains the exhibits. That will be admitted into the
                   record in advance. If the Planning Commission does not feel there is sufficient evidence for
                   conviction, then they should deny it. If they determine the evidence is clear, then revoke the
                   Conditional Use Permit. One thing to remember is if there is a revocation of a Conditional Use
                   Permit for a home, it can still be used as a home but with a hotel, there is nothing else they can
                   do. No action will be taken until all appeals have been heard.            Mr. Cowdell answered the
                   Commissioner’s questions and informed them they would be receiving more information shortly.
                   He reaffirmed that there is a lot at stake with this action not only for the property owners but for
                   the City also and the process may take years to complete.

         1.2       He concluded by saying this may be boring and the Planning Commissioners will wonder why it
                   can’t be moved along more quickly. There just needs to be a trust in the process. It is what they
                   are comfortable with. We have an adversarial system, that Party A gets a lawyer and tells the story
                   in the more favorable light for their situation. Previously attorneys were told to zealously tell their
                   story. Then the other side zealously tells the story in the more favorable light for them. Then the

General Plan Amendment

 3.        1G23 - Public Hearing and Recommendation to the City Council for a General Plan Text Amendment
           to Chapter 8: Moderate Income Housing, to implement updates required by recent State
           Legislation. (Mark McGrath/AICP/Long Range Planner)

3.1      Mr. McGrath presented this item.            Mr. McGrath said that several weeks ago he updated the
         Commission with regard to the General Plan rewrite being undertaken now. Tonight, the discussion will be
         centered around Chapter 8 of that document, which is being accelerated to meet the State Code in a timely
         manner. This is a proposed 5-year plan.

         3.1.1     In 2018, the Utah Legislature adopted a new provision in State Code that required local
                   communities in Utah to address certain issues related to moderate-income housing in their
                   General Plans. Later that same year, the City hired BBC Research to conduct a moderate-income
                   housing study that eventually led to the adoption of the current Moderate Income Housing Plan
                   adopted in 2019. Based on the belief that the statute didn’t go far enough to facilitate real
                   reforms, the Legislature adopted additional provisions in 2022 that included the requirement for

                   specific implementation measures and accompanying benchmarks and timelines. The new statute
                   also includes penalties for communities that do not conform to the new State requirements.

         3.1.2     The most relevant provisions of the new statute concerning the General Plan are Utah Code
                   Annotated (UCA) 13-9a-403(2)(b)(iii) which requires municipalities to implement three or more
                   “moderate income housing strategies” from a menu of 24 items articulated in the Code; and UCA
                   13-9a-403(2)(b)(iv) which requires municipalities with a fixed guideway transit system to adopt a
                   minimum of two additional strategies. Three states on 4700 South on the Mid-Valley Connector
                   Bus Rapid Transit project, scheduled to begin construction in 2023, are considered fixed guideway
                   and therefore necessitate Taylorsville to include the additional strategies.

         3.1.3     Mr. McGrath reviewed the 24 items referenced in the State Code and advised that the following
                   three Strategies chosen are:
                   3.1.3.1 Strategy D – Identify and utilize general fund subsidies or other sources of revenue to
                            waive construction related fees that are otherwise generally imposed by the municipality
                            for the construction or rehabilitation of moderate-income housing. Timeline for
                            completion is 4th Quarter of 2023.
                            Strategy E – Create or allow for, and reduce regulations related to, internal or detached
                            accessor dwelling units in residential zones. Timeline for completion is 2nd Quarter 2024
                            through 3rd Quarter 2024.
                            Strategy F – Zone or rezone for higher density or moderate income residential
                            development in commercial or mixed use zones near major transit investment corridors,
                            commercial centers, or employment centers. Timeline for completion is ongoing.

                   3.1.3.2 Mr. McGrath advised that the City’s 2022 updated plan was approved by HB462, but
                           painted the cities as still not having enough affordable housing. It was noted that most
                           cities must grow “up” rather than “out” because of running out of developable space.
                           That higher densities make better use of properties. Mr. McGrath advised that right now
                           there are differing height requirements dependent on which zone it is in. Right now, the
                           City is looking at developing most aggressively along the projected Bus Rapid Transit
                           routes, which would be ideal for moderate income housing developments. Also, there
                           are a number of shopping centers that are outdated and as they are redeveloped the City
                              needs to make sure it is done in a more forward looking way.           Another issue to re-
                              look at is reducing the amount of parking required for different projects. That is, it is felt
                              the City up to this point has been requiring more parking space than is needed. Correction
                              of that would free up more fee space for development. One more item for consideration
                              is looking at what the City is charging as impact fees to see if there is any room for a
                              possible reduction.

3.2      FINDINGS:
         3.2.1  The Utah Legislature, during the 2022 legislative session, adopted HB462 requiring local
                governments in Utah to modify their Moderate-Income Housing plans to address new provisions
                to the State Code.
         3.2.2  UCA §13-9a-403 (2)(b)(iii) and (iv) of the new statute requires local governments to adopt three or
                more “moderate income housing strategies” from a list identified within the new code.
         3.2.3  Communities with stations on a fixed guideway public transit system are required to adopt a
                minimum of two additional strategies.
         3.2.4  The Mid-Valley Connector Bus Rapid Transit System, scheduled to begin construction in 2023, is a
                “fixed guideway” based on the provisions of the Code.
         3.2.5  The proposed amendments to the Taylorsville General Plan adopt the following strategies from
                §13-9a-403 (2)(b)(iii) and (iv):

3.3      Mr. Spung         added in order to help put the sequencing in order, the station area plans will be created
         by the consultants and reviewed with Staff. That vision will then be to form an amendment to the zoning
         code/land use regulations to allow for the vision to be built (Letter “G”) and then ultimately the City Council
         will have to rezone properties to adopt the rules to implement the vision. At that point, the City just rezones
         people’s properties but it is really up to them if they want to develop it. The City can approve the rezonings
         but until people actually propose and build a project, it is not fully implemented.        He felt what the
         State is saying is that a plan can be created but there actually must be steps taken to implement that plan
         through zoning regulation and rezoning property. Mr. McGrath added that the station area plans will be
         part of the General Plan update and will go through the same process as this. It will come before the
         Planning Commission for a public hearing, then go on to the City Council for final adoption but will be part
         of the official General Plan.
