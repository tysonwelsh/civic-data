**Ordinance: Landscaping and Buffers Chapter Text Amendment Update**   ~ 2:15 p.m.
  20 min.
The Council will receive a briefing about a proposal that would amend various sections of Title 21A of the *Salt Lake City Code* pertaining to the Landscaping and Buffers Chapter. The proposal would allow artificial turf in some required landscaping areas where it was not previously permitted and align with State Code. The new chapter was adopted on April 26, 2023. Since the new regulations have been implemented, some sections have been found to need further clarification and modifications to better understand and utilize the standards. Additionally, changes to the State Code were adopted that limit the applicability of a landscape plan. The Planning Commission forwarded a negative recommendation. Other sections of Title 21A may also be amended as part of this petition.

**FYI – Project Timeline:** (subject to change per Chair direction or Council discussion)
Briefing - Tuesday, June 2, 2026
Set Public Hearing Date -  Tuesday, June 9, 2026
Hold hearing to accept public comment - Tuesday, July 14, 2026 at 7 p.m.
TENTATIVE Council Action - Tuesday, July 21, 2026

**Summary:**

Brian Fullmer introduced the item and noted the Planning Commission forwarded a negative recommendation on the proposed amendments.

Michaela Bell presented the proposed changes, including allowing artificial turf in required front and corner side yards; requiring at least 33% of required landscaping to remain live vegetation; continuing the prohibition of artificial turf in required landscape buffers and park strips; and establishing standards for tree root and vegetation protections, aesthetics, permeability, and turf quality. The presentation also reviewed the advantages and disadvantages of artificial turf, as well as proposed landscaping updates for parking lots.

Council Members, Tammy Hunsaker, Rachel Otto, and Michaela Bell discussed:

* Water conservation benefits associated with artificial turf
* Public concerns regarding impacts on neighboring properties
* Balancing water conservation goals with neighborhood character and environmental considerations
* Whether existing noncompliant artificial turf installations were included in the analysis supporting the proposed amendments
* Enforcement trends and the percentage of artificial turf cases, with most occurring on the west side of the city
* The need for additional public feedback and time to refine the proposed ordinance
* Clarifying ordinance language to distinguish between permitted natural landscape and artificial turf

**Council Request:**

Council Member Wharton requested information on how other cities regulate artificial turf. Michaela Bell said the information would be provided. Tammy Hunsaker added that staff would also provide a City-generated FAQ document.

**TENTATIVE – Informational: Opportunity Zone Program Update**   ~ 3:05 p.m.
  20 min.
The Council will receive a briefing on the key changes to the Opportunity Zone Program. An Opportunity Zone, which is designated by the Governor, is an economically-distressed community where new investments may be eligible for preferential tax treatment, under certain conditions.

**FYI – Project Timeline:** (subject to change per Chair direction or Council discussion)
Briefing - Tuesday, June 2, 2026
Set Public Hearing Date -  n/a
Hold hearing to accept public comment - n/a
TENTATIVE Council Action - n/a

**Summary:**

Lorena Riffo-Jenson presented an overview of the federal Opportunity Zone Program, including recent program changes that focused incentives on long-term investment in low-income communities through local hiring, anti-displacement measures, anti-abuse protections, and enhanced public reporting. Staff noted that recommendations were due to the Governor's Office by June 10, 2026.

Colin Gibbs outlined the nomination process and timeline, including the selection of one census tract from each of seven areas for consideration by the Governor. The presentation also reviewed the evaluation criteria, including opportunities for targeted industry investment, alignment with regional economic development goals, transit access, and community readiness, as well as coordination with regional partners and developer outreach. The proposed priority rankings were Ballpark, Poplar Grove North (including the Power District), Northwest Quadrant, Glendale, Fairpark, Rose Park, and Central City Northwest.

Council Members, Nick Norris, Lorena Riffo-Jenson, and Rachel Otto discussed:

* Opportunity Zone boundaries based on census tracts and previously designated Opportunity Zones
* Tax incentives available to developers, which would not affect City tax revenue
* Strategies to maximize long-term community benefits through stakeholder engagement and targeted investment
* The selection of the recommended Glendale tract based on community feedback and economic development readiness

The Council will consider a motion to enter into closed meeting. A closed meeting described under Utah Code Annotated (UCA) Section §52-4-205 may be held for specific purposes including, but not limited to discussing:

a.     discussion of the character, professional competence, or physical or mental health of an individual.

b.     strategy sessions to discuss collective bargaining.
c.    strategy sessions to discuss pending or reasonably imminent litigation.
d.    strategy sessions to discuss the purchase, exchange, or lease of real property, including any form of a water right or water shares, if public discussion of the transaction would:

(i)    disclose the appraisal or estimated value of the property under consideration, or
(ii)     prevent the public body from completing the transaction on the best possible terms.

e.    strategy sessions to discuss the sale of real property, including any form of a water right or water shares, if:

(i) public discussion of the transaction would:

(A) disclose the appraisal or estimated value of the property under consideration, or
(B) prevent the public body from completing the transaction on the best possible terms.

(ii)     the public body previously gave public notice that the property would be offered for sale, and
(iii)     the terms of the sale are publicly disclosed before the public body approves the sale.

f.    discussion regarding deployment of security personnel, devices, or systems.
g.     investigative proceedings regarding allegations of criminal misconduct.

A closed meeting may also be held for attorney-client matters that are privileged pursuant to Utah Code § 78B-1-137, and for other lawful purposes that satisfy the pertinent requirements of the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act.
