Joint proclamation declaring January 11, 2022, as Bernard Augustine
DeVoto Day in Ogden City

A joint proclamation declaring January 11, 2022, as Bernard Augustine DeVoto Day in Ogden City came before the
Council for consideration. Chair Blair read the joint proclamation, after which CHAIR BLAIR MOVED TO APPROVE THE
JOINT PROCLAMATION. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER HYER, ALL VOTING AYE.

Chair Blair then presented the proclamation to Scott Greenwell, John Sillito, Kathryn MacKay, and Russell Burrows of
the Bernard DeVoto Commemoration Committee. Mr. Greenwell thanked the Mayor and Council for this recognition. He reported
the Bernard DeVoto Commemoration Committee was established to honor the life and legacy of Bernard Augustine DeVoto, the
man hailed by Wallace Stegner as "Utah's most prominent writer." He noted that 2022 marks the 125th anniversary of DeVoto’s
birth (January 11, 1897) and the primary goal of the Commemoration Committee is to raise public awareness of the enormous
contributions DeVoto has made to American letters, to the conservation of western lands and resources, and to the history of the
American West. In the process, the Committee seeks to ensure that DeVoto and his legacy are remembered in his hometown of
Ogden and in his home state of Utah, that he receives the recognition he deserves for his literary and historical achievements, and
that he secures a proper place of honor alongside Ogden’s other notables. He concluded by reporting on upcoming events at
Weber State University intended to introduce participants to Mr. DeVoto’s achievements and his writings.

Fiscal Year 2021 Comprehensive Annual Financial Report

The Fiscal Year (FY) 2021 Comprehensive Annual Financial Report (CAFR) came before the Council for consideration.
The CAFR is prepared by the City Administration each year as part of the fiscal year-end budget process. The CAFR provides an
overall picture of the City’s funds, investments, assets, and debt information over and above the regular annual budget. The City’s
budget provides a plan for revenues and expenditures, while the CAFR provides an overview of the prior year’s financial activity.

City Comptroller Stout recognized the efforts of City staff, especially the staff members of her office, in facilitating the
audit process and development of CAFR. She noted that the document will be posted on the City’s website within 24 hours of this
meeting and indicated she looks forward to continued discussion and review of the content of both the audit and CAFR during the
February work session scheduled for that purpose. She suggested that the Council review the management analysis, which begins
on page 21 of the document and summarizes the key points.

Vice Chair White thanked Ms. Stout and her staff for their efforts and professionalism. Chair Blair and other members of
the Council echoed the expression of gratitude and stated that Ms. Stout is an example of the caliber of staff employed by the City.

Proposed Resolution 2022-1 approving the honorary designation of 675
North and 670 North Streets between Jefferson Avenue and 950 East as
Satnam Singh Drive

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a petition to assign an honorary street
name for a portion of 675 North between Jefferson Avenue and Gramercy Avenue came before the Council for consideration.
The memo stated Jesse Redden is proposing the honorary street name of “Satnam Singh Drive” in honor of Mr. Singh who was
tragically killed in February 2021 in the Super Grocery convenience store he owned and operated. The Planning Division staff
reviewed the proposal and provided analysis to the Planning Commission for its consideration. The proposal was reviewed by the
Planning Commission in November 2021. The Commission was unable to pass a motion to formally recommend approval or
denial of the petition. The Commission’s initial motion to recommend approval of the petition failed to pass. The Commission’s
second motion to recommend denial failed to pass. As per the current and applicable ordinance, the petition was reviewed by the
City Engineer, the City’s Legal department, and the Police and Fire Departments. The City Engineer and Legal department are
recommending denial of the petition as it was felt that the proposal did not meet the standards for a public purpose. The Police and
Fire departments did not express concerns related to the proposed honorary name causing confusion with emergency response.

Jesse Redden first thanked Council Deputy Director Symes for his help in navigating the City’s honorary street name
designation process. He also thanked Planning staff for their presentations regarding the petition. He indicated he moved to Ogden

he has done in the community. He attended the work session two weeks ago to hear the Council’s conversation about the petition

name change rose to the level for which an honorary street name is granted. His argument is that with all the street names that

identified by the Planning Commission. He then indicated he is aware there is a new ordinance being worked on by the City
Council, but he understands that his petition must be considered according to the current ordinance. He thanked the Council for
their discussion of possibly pursuing other opportunities for honoring Mr. Singh, perhaps by renaming a park or other public
facility in the community. He then noted that as he was gathering signatures for the petition, he felt it would be easier and possibly

Proposed Joint Resolution 2022-2 approving the Revitalization and
Economic Focus of the Utah Main Street Program.

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed Utah Main Street
Program (UMSP) came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the UMSP is a State program that is focused on
supporting communities in revitalizing the economy, appearance, and image of downtown commercial districts. The Ogden
Downtown Alliance (ODA) has applied to receive support from the UMSP. To be eligible for funding, the ODA must be
supported by a joint resolution expressing the community’s support for the program.

Business Development Manager Meess summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide
information regarding the administration of the UMSP, which provides a framework and resources to help with downtown
revitalization. The ODA works to increase economic vitality and community vibrancy throughout Ogden’s Central Business
District. These efforts align with the Main Street Approach and the UMSP designated ODA as a “Vibrant Main Street — Tier 2”
community in November of 2021. If the Council and Mayor support the joint resolution, the ODA will be eligible for UMSP
Downtown Enhancement Grant funding.

Council member Choberka asked if there are any competing entities in the community that may be interested in applying
for the funds. Ms. Meess indicated she is not aware of any other competing entities that would be applying for the funds.

Chair Blair invited input from Kim Bowsher of ODA. Ms. Bowsher indicated that there are other competing entities in
other communities, but the ODA is the only entity from Weber County that applied for the funds. She stated she is hopeful the
Council and Mayor will support the program. The funding will allow the ODA to participate at a national level in training and
infrastructure support and she believes it will have a compounding effect in the community and throughout the State.

Chair Blair introduced in writing proposed Joint Resolution 2022-2, entitled:

“A joint resolution of the Ogden City Mayor and the Ogden City Council supporting the revitalization and
economic focus of the Utah Main Street Program.”
