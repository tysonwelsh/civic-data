       Culinary Water Rate Study (Secondary Water Rate Study-December 15) - HDR
       Engineers

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director stated that Council had requested the Culinary
Water Rate Study as one of the strategic priorities for the year. Mr. Hicks, along with Shawn
Koorn from HDR Engineering, presented a summary of the Culinary Water Rate Study, which
was inclusive of all the current enterprise funds. He said the study was divided into two main
parts, culinary and secondary water rates.

Mr. Koorn presented his preliminary study findings. He stated that the purpose of the study was
to provide long term sustainable revenues to meet the operating and capital needs of the
culinary water system, including ongoing operational and maintenance expenses, as well as the
capital needs to maintain, improve, and expand the system as needed.

Councilmembers reviewed the rate study preliminary results as presented and discussions were
held. Discussion was also held regarding the peak assessment received from Jordan Valley
Water Conservancy District; however, the study did not cover the peak assessment but future
discussions would include this component.

         Discuss Possible Moratorium

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, said that there would be a discussion on Fire Impact Fees later in
the meeting. He explained that State law requires that any time an impact fee is adopted, it
cannot be implemented for 90 days.

Mr. Carter explained that, due to the newly created Riverton Fire Service Area (RFSA), the prior
agreement with the Unified Fire Service Area (UFSA) would end on December 31, 2020. He
said that means that there would be a 60 day window where the City would not be allowed to
collect any fire impact fees, which could result in a loss of fees of approximately $10,000 to the
City. He said that one possible solution would be to create a moratorium on building permits for
the next 60 days. He said this may send a wrong signal to developers, in light of trying to get the
economy to work as best as it can during this season of pandemic.

Councilmembers discussed and indicated that they felt that the nominal loss was not as
important as keeping the application process open. Staff will monitor the number of incoming
permits in the next 90 days and keep Council appraised.

         Elderly Housing Overlay

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, stated that Staff was working on the Elderly
Housing overlay with particular emphasis on definitions, including ages, assisted living
situations, housing types, assurances, deed restrictions and HOA limitations, etc. He said that
other areas of focus were zoning, density, and setbacks. Mr. Lethbridge said that Staff
recommended the continued use of the underlying zoning. He said the Elderly Housing overlay
would not carry a density of its own or setbacks; for example, it would be applied to the RM-8
Zone, or the RM-4 Zone.

Mr. Lethbridge asked the City Council if they were interested in incentivizing the Elderly
Housing, which could be done by direct subsidy, or other types of financial assistance to the
developer. He said it could be offered or withdrawn at any time depending on funding. Another
optional incentive would be zoning bonuses.

The City Council discussed the overlay and determined that there was general support for
including density bonus options in the draft ordinance. Mr. Lethbridge indicated staff would do
so, and that the next step would be review and recommendation by the Planning Commission.

Public Hearing(s) / Action Items

         Ordinance No. 20-30 - Enacting a Riverton City Fire Impact Fee, Fire Facility Plan,
         and Related Analysis

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, explained that the Impact Fee was a Riverton
City fee and not a Riverton Fire Service Area Fee. He then introduced Aaron Montgomery,
Public Financial Advisor, from Zions Bank.

The following is a summary of Mr. Montgomery’s presentation:

What is an Impact Fee?
  • One‐time fee charged to new development to offset the capital costs associated with new
    development
      • Can only be collected for the costs of system improvements
      • Fire stations and training facilities
      • Fire vehicles costing > $500,000
      • Must have an Impact Fee Facilities Plan (IFFP) and an Impact Fee Analysis (IFA)

Basic document Requirements
Impact Fee Facilities Plan (IFFP)
       • Existing service levels
       • Proposed service levels • Excess capacity • Demand created by new development •
Consumption of excess capacity • New facilities needed and cost

Impact Fee Analysis (IFA)
       • Proportionate share analysis
       • “Buy‐In” excess capacity component • Construction cost of new facilities
       • Other costs – engineering, financial, fund balances
       • Financing and credits

Facilities and Buy-In to Existing Infrastructure
Riverton has the following three existing fire stations: Station 120, 121 and 124
         • These fire stations have excess capacity of 3,303 square feet
         • There are no plans to build new facilities or plans to make any future purchases of fire
     vehicles over $500,000; therefore, impact fees will be charged to new development as a
     buy‐in to existing infrastructure as development consumes the excess capacity
