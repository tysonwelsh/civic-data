Mr.Donaldson provided Open and Public Meetings Act training. Generally,the Legislature wants
public bodies to have meetings that are open with open deliberations. Meetings can only be closed
for specific reasons. This year,the definition ofa meeting has been modified. The definition used
to clearly state what a meeting was and was not. However, the language that clarified what a
meeting was not has since been removed. He explained that a meeting is defined as the following:

   • A gathering of a public body;
   • With a quorum present (i.e. simple majority);
   • That is convened by the person with authority to convene the public body following the
        process provided by law for convening the public body;
   • For the purpose ofacting as a public body to receive public comment on a relevant matter,
        deliberate about a relevant matter, or take action upon a relevant matter;
   • A meeting can be in person, electronic, or hybrid.

Mr. Donaldson explained that there will not be as many notices required based on the updated
definition ofa meeting. A lot will still need to be noticed, but ribbon ceremonies, holiday parties,
and similar events will not need to be formally noticed moving forward. Mr. Donaldson next
reviewed the definition ofpublic body and read the following language aloud:


   • A public body means an administrative, advisory, executive, or legislative body that:
           o Was created by the Utah Constitution, statute, rule, ordinance, or resolution;
           o Consists oftwo or more persons;
           o Spends, distributes, or is supported by tax money; and
           o Is vested with authority to make decisions about the public's business.


Mr. Donaldson reported that there are specific training and notice requirements. For noticing,
there generally needs to be at least 24 hours' notice provided, unless an emergency meeting needs
to be held. What constitutes an emergency is defined. There is also a requirement that Meeting
Minutes be kept. He explained that the written Meeting Minutes are the official record of the
meeting.

Mr. Donaldson shared information about electronic meetings and explained that this is something
the Council addressed recently. An ordinance was passed that authorized all public bodies in the
City to conduct electronic meetings. There still needs to be an anchor location in most instances
and the same procedures need to be followed during the electronic meeting as in a regular meeting.
The code states that members ofthe public body should be given a specific opportunity to discuss
each matter through a roll call method. For instance, if there is an electronic meeting held, each
member ofthe public body should be called on individually and asked for comment to ensure that
everyone is able to speak.


Individuals constituting a quorum of a public body may not act together outside a meeting in a
deliberate way to pre-determine an action to be taken by the public body at a meeting on a relevant
matter. Mr. Donaldson explained that this means there cannot be coordination or deliberation
outside ofthe open meeting. Sending text messages during a meeting is generally discouraged. If
someone disrupts a meeting, it is possible to remove that individual and there are guidelines for
doing so.


Mr. Donaldson reviewed information about Closed Meetings. He explained that there are certain
reasons that a meeting can be closed, but no local government meetings are required to be closed.
The decision to close a meeting is discretionary. For a Closed Meeting, a quorum must be present
and two thirds of the members present must vote to close the meeting. There must be an open
meeting first, the reasons for a Closed Meeting must be announced, and then a vote can be taken.


Mr. Donaldson reported that a meeting can be closed to discuss any ofthe following:

   • The character, professional competence, or physical or mental health of an individual;
   • Collective bargaining;
   • Pending or reasonably imminent litigation;
   • Security personnel, devices, or systems;
   • An investigative proceeding regarding allegations ofcriminal misconduct;
   • Certain issues related to procurements and trade secrets;

The purpose ofthe Closed Meeting needs to be announced but the details ofthe discussion do not
need to be. There are certain things that cannot be done during a Closed Meeting, such as
approving an ordinance, resolution, rule, regulation, contract, or appointment. No final vote can
be taken on a matter. Mr. Donaldson next shared information with the Council about Meeting
Procedures. The code states that the City Council shall conduct its meetings in a way that is
generally consistent with Robert's Rules ofOrder. It is not necessary for the City be overly ridged
with procedure, but the intention is to generally follow those rules of order. He likes to view this
approach as semi-formal.


Mr. Donaldson recently emailed the Conflict of Interest Statement to Council Members. That

Office needs to know where to find those forms on the website.           Mr. Donaldson reviewed

information about the role ofthe Mayor. The Mayor conducts the meeting and runs the meeting.
With the other public bodies, the Chair conducts and runs the meeting. The Mayor or Chair tries
to focus the discussion and make sure matters that are outside ofthe agenda are not being discussed.
The Code of Conduct talks a lot about civility and not being belligerent, slanderous, or making
disparaging comments. There is also information about public conduct during meetings to ensure
decorum is followed.

the agenda, but the order of the matters heard are at the discretion of the Mayor. An item can be

The steps of a motion were reviewed. Mr. Donaldson explained that the Chair is addressed and
the Chair recognizes the member of the public body. The member proposes a motion, there is a
second followed by a restatement ofthe motion by the Chair,and then a vote is taken on the motion.
As for amendments to a motion, when that occurs, the discussion is confined to the amendment.
After the motion is amended, a vote is taken on the amendment to the motion, and then a vote is
taken on the amended motion. There can be one amendment to a motion to amend. It is appropriate
to share comment after a motion has been made. For instance, there can be comments shared by a
Council Member about whether or not there is support for the motion that is currently before the
City Council.

        8.1     Ordinance 2025-01 - Pronosed Text Amendment to Chanter 3.10 Business
                License Provisions to Allow "Enclosed Mobile Businesses" in Bluffdale and
                Chanter 3.80 Home Occupations to Allow Home-Based Microschools and
                Micro-Education Facilities as Home Occupations as Permitted bv State Law.
                Citv of Bluffdale. Applicant. (StaffPresenter, Caitlvn Tubbs)

Planning Manager,Caitlyn Tubbs,shared Business Licensing Text Amendments with the Council.
The amendments come from the 2023 and 2024 Legislative Session. In 2023, the Legislature
defined enclosed mobile businesses and required cities to recognize Business Licenses for enclosed
mobile businesses that were issued by other jurisdictions. In 2024,the Legislature defined micro-
education entities and home-based microschools. The same accommodation as charter schools

was awarded, which allows them as permitted uses in all zones. Ms. Tubbs shared the definition
of an enclosed mobile business that has been adopted by the State of Utah. This is the definition
that is proposed to be adopted into the City Code. The use does not include food trucks,food carts,
or ice cream trucks.


Micro-education entities serve up to 100 students and home-based microschools serve up to 16
students. Those are permitted uses in all zones. The following additions are proposed:


     • Adopt definitions of enclosed mobile business, home-based microschool, and micro-
         education entity as listed by the State of Utah;
     • Add enclosed mobile businesses to Reciprocal License Recognition section;
     • Allow home-based microschools and micro-education entities as Home Occupations in
         Residential Zones(already allowed in all non-residential zones).

Associate City Planner, Ellen Oakman, shared Temporary Signs Text Amendment information
with the Council. On October 10,2024,there was a City Council Work Session, and on November
6,2024,there was a Plaiming Commission Work Session. Feedback was collected from the Work
Sessions for the proposed ordinance. On December 4, 2024, the Plaiming Commission voted
unanimously to recommend approval. The key takeaways from the Work Sessions are as follows:

       Support businesses;
       Aesthetics matter;

   • Allow inflatables for grand opening periods, baimers, permants, etc.;
   • Allow feather/blade signs as temporary signs;
   • Increase the promotional periods oftemporary signs;
   • Allow a longer duration for vinyl barmers affixed to buildings.

Ms. Oakman reminded the Council that currently, the City allows baimers permitted through a
Temporary Use Permit. Blade/feather signs are prohibited,inflatables are prohibited,and pennants
are prohibited. Ms. Oakman reviewed the proposed amendments, which are as follows:


   • Permitted as Temporary Sign:
           o Two banners(24 square feet) per street frontage for five occurrences;
           o Three feather/blade signs per street frontage.
   • Permitted for Grant Opening Periods Only:
           o Two inflatables permitted during grand openings;
           o Pennants permitted during grand openings.


The proposed ordinance summary was shared. Ms. Oakman noted that it allows vinyl banners on
buildings for 90 days from the date of the Business License. After speaking to some sign
companies, it was determined that the 90 days is a generous amount, as it normally takes between
one to two months at the most to obtain a permanent wall sign. The definition for new sign types
was updated.    Council Member Lord asked if input was received from local businesses.
