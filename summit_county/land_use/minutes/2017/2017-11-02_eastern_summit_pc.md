detail, particularly in the process of initiating the Village Overlay Zone. The Staff Report 
includes a new, clean version of the Village Overlay process and the redlined version. 
Director Putt went over what has been changed and the intent and purpose of the changes. 
The purpose of the Village Overlay process is a way to do land planning on a community-
wide approach. This is different from other tools through the opportunity to create a 
process that brings a lot of stakeholders into the process earlier in the process. This is 
intended as a “bottoms up” approach, with the support of Staff and the expertise of service 
providers to build the land planning solution at a grass roots level up.  
The language of traditional or compact communities was taken out because it seemed 
vague and undefined. Director Putt pointed out other changes that were made. The biggest 
changes were in the establishment process. The initiation will be through an application 
process, the same as any other development application process. The next step will be 
establishing a Village Overlay study area boundary. Staff recommended there not be a 
minimum or maximum area, but be determined through the application and study process. 
The boundary may change throughout the process, but should not change more than ten 
percent.

Willoughby asked who would modify the boundary. Director Putt answered there could be 
a number of reasons for the boundary to be modified, including additional property owners 
wanting to join. It would not be appropriate to have a significant change that the public is

Director Putt went on to discuss the creation of the Village Overlay Plan and Zoning 
Districts and the difference between the Village Overlay and a Specially Planned Area 
(SPA). The Village Overlay process results in new Zoning Districts. The zones will likely be 
created for the particular plan. County Council will appoint a Village Overlay Committee, 
including property owners involved or their agents, a non-quorum of the Planning 
Commission, Staff, and any service providers involved. The goal of this committee would be 
to develop a plan and determine the land uses of the Village Overlay Zone, allowing for a 
mix of uses. The property owners will control the process.

Commissioner Willoughby asked what happens if this process increases the population 
and a new school site is needed. The answer is that becomes part of the civic community 
that must be discussed in the process. Once the Overlay Zone committee is done, it comes 
to the Planning Commission just like any other rezone process. Ultimately, a modification 
would be approved and made to the Zoning Map. There would be an amendment to the 
Development Code, adding any additional new zones that were created. Those zones could 
be modified in the future, if needed. This approach allows larger area land planning for our 
existing communities. Director Putt stated this is not intended for one or two large 
landowners to make a new development. There will be another process for that. 
The Village Overlay Zone could be a stepping stone to community self-determination. 
Nothing is taken away from a landowner. The community could still incorporate at some 
point if they would like and this would bring together the resources for land planning and 
incorporation. 
Staff recommends the Planning Commission forward a positive recommendation to the

Findings of Fact  
1. The goal of Chapter 2 of the Eastern Summit County General Plan is to develop land use 
codes which balance the diversity of desires of Eastern Summit County residents, including 
private property rights.  
2. In furtherance of this goal, §11-1-1 of the Eastern Summit County Code provides that “The 
Eastern Summit County General Plan was developed to ensure that the rural, agricultural and 
small-town character of the eastern portion of the County shall remain, even in the presence of 
growth and change. The intention of the County is to assure the managed, proper and sensitive 
development of land to protect and enhance these desired qualities and the lifestyle that exists.” 
3. On February 18, 2016, the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission forwarded 
amendments to Chapter 3 of the Eastern Summit County Development Code to the County 
Council with a positive recommendation.  
4. As part of their review of Chapter 3, the Commission discussed 3 potential zoning districts 
that were determined to not be ready for review by the County Council; among those zones was 
the Village Overlay (VO).  
5. Public Hearings on the revised Village Overlay (VO) draft were held on October 5 and 
November 2, 2017 Eastern Summit County Planning Commission meetings.  
Conclusions of Law:  
1. The amendment is consistent with the goals, objectives, and policies of the General Plan.  
2. The amendment is consistent with the requirements established in chapter 5 of the Eastern 
Summit County Development Code.  
3. The proposed amendment is not detrimental to public health, safety and welfare.
