2.     Work Session – Economic Development Joint Work Session.

       2.1     Welcome and Dinner.

Mayor Hall welcomed members of the City Council and Planning Commission.

       2.2     2023 Strategic Plan Development Process and Sub-Committee Selection.

City Planner and Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, reported that it had been a few
months since the City Council and Planning Commission had met jointly. The October meeting
was generally focused on planning for the next year. In previous years, there had been a small
sub-committee that did not constitute a quorum. Over the last two years, a few meetings were
conducted with email correspondence and digital participation. This was done to develop the
Strategic Plan. If that was of interest to the City, there could be a small sub-committee with two
City Council Members and two Planning Commissioners. The intention was to review the plan
from last year, look at the status of the items, and determine whether there was a need for follow-
up. Additionally, new items could be added to the plan as necessary.

Mayor Hall asked who the two City Council and Planning Commissioners were. It was noted that
Council Members Kallas and Hales would represent the City Council on the sub-committee and
Commissioners Cragun and Flynn would represent the Planning Commission. Mayor Hall
wondered if any Council Members were interested in serving on the Economic Development Sub-
Committee. Council Member Kallas stated that it is important to have the new Committee
Members determined before the year-end review takes place. He has served on the sub-committee
for the last two years and was happy to continue or allow someone else to participate. Council
Member Crockett was willing to join the Committee. Commissioners Swanson and Griffis were
also interested. A member of Staff would also participate. Mayor Hall believed the meetings
mostly take place over Zoom.

Mayor Hall wondered if there had been a Chair position on the sub-committee. This was denied.
The sub-committee agenda is coordinated by a member of Staff and all Committee Members
participate. Mr. Crowell reported that the first agenda will focus on a review of the plan. It was
determined that the meetings should take place after 5:00 p.m. so that all Committee Members can
attend. Mr. Crowell offered to send out an email to the Committee Members with possible dates
and times. City Manager, Mark Reid, confirmed that there will be two City Council Members,
two Planning Commissioners, and the Mayor in attendance for meetings. As a result, it would not
need to be noticed as an official meeting. Mr. Crowell reiterated that he would send out possible
dates and relevant documents.

       2.3     Discussion of RDA Role, Finance, and Projects.

Mr. Crowell reported that he was asked to discuss the role of the Redevelopment Agency (“RDA”).
It had been two years since there had been a basic overview of the role of the RDA. The State had

taken many different approaches over the years to address redevelopment and Tax Increment
Financing (“TIF”). Bluffdale had the RDA Board in place. In the past, there had been outside
counsel for the RDA. This had been done for years, since the beginning of The Gateway RDA.
The individual used most of the time was Jay Springer. Mr. Crowell explained that members of
the City Council make up the membership of the RDA and the current Chair is Council Member
Kallas. There is a section in State Code related to redevelopment. Every year, the Legislature
typically makes edits and provides additional guidance on how to proceed.

The RDA Board does not have taxing power and requires some staff to handle the work.
Mr. Crowell explained that several people in the room perform various functional roles to assist
the RDA with outstanding business. There are certain project areas where the RDA can focus. He
shared a map of the City. It started with the geographic area where the intent was to do some
redevelopment activity. There was no limitation on size necessarily, however, the larger the area,
the more questions there will be from other taxing entities. The Salt Lake County website
highlighted project areas in Salt Lake County. He noted that the website is interactive.

Mr. Crowell identified The Gateway RDA, which was created to try to incentivize some retail. It
had taken a long time to get started and had not generated a lot of income. He noted that there
were commitments for the financing of infrastructure that were done early on. That debt had been
inherited. The money generated from the site was still paying off that debt. It would not generate
enough to have income outside of paying off the debt. Mr. Crowell reported that there is no money
available in the Gateway RDA. Despite that, there was a lot of activity there currently. The Eastern
Bluffdale EDA was overviewed. Mr. Crowell identified the area on the map and where the Draper
and Bluffdale boundaries are located. The Eastern Bluffdale area has the most potential for
additional project and incentive funding. He noted that the Jordan Narrows EDA is to the south.
He identified that area on the map as well.

Mr. Crowell reported that there had been a major growth of taxable value and a lot of tax increment
was collected. Mr. Reid shared some history with those present. When the area was first set up in
2000 it was for a 25-year period. For 15 of the 25 years, tax increment could be collected. Tax
increment means that all of the taxing entities give their funds above the base year to the City to
develop the area. The biggest contributors were the Jordan School District, Salt Lake County, the
State School Board, and Bluffdale City. Back then, the Gateway only had a few homes. The City
was sued by a developer to try to develop affordable housing. That resulted in The Bluffs
Apartments and Vintage on the Bluffs. As things were built, increased taxes came in, and that tax
increment could be used for additional development. Mr. Reid shared additional history related to
the area and explained that the City used Tax Increment Financing to create an economic
development area.

Economic development project areas are areas where it is possible to utilize funding. Those areas
were established to promote the creation of non-retail jobs. Retail needs to be incidental to the
other attributes. Although the City wanted to facilitate as much retrial as possible, if EDA money
was being used, the focus needed to be on other aspects. For instance, job creation, an increase in

assessed value, and infrastructure. There were discussions regarding the development of 14600
and Porter Rockwell Boulevard. The core is industrial but there were also retail uses. Mr. Crowell
explained that an analysis was done in the area and it was determined that there would be enough
assessed value growth. There had also been conversations related to job creation. Often, when
investments are made, there is a Participation Agreement and performance markers to ensure that
the investment pays off. Due to the investment made, the project was built and included retail
pads. He clarified that the City did not give the money to directly incentivize retail in the area.
That was the distinction.

Mr. Crowell reported that for a project area, the base year and base value were set. The increment
could later be utilized for incentive and development projects for a certain period of time. The
new laws specify that there need to be individual negotiations. Some taxing entities may want five
or 10-year terms or only want to participate at 20%. Previously, when a few years were added to
the length of a term, one of the taxing entities expressed concerns as they had been counting on
that money coming in directly from the Assessor’s Office. Instead, it went to the RDA for a few
more years. It was understandable that there could be concerns in those instances. Mr. Crowell
overviewed the basic redevelopment process, which was as follows:

   •   Step 1: Create a Redevelopment Agency;
   •   Step 2/3: Create a Project Area;
   •   Step 2/3: Attract Development; and
   •   Step 4: Administer Project Area, Projects, and Incentives.

The RDA process was discussed further. Questions were raised regarding whether the intention
was to try to recoup the investment so it could be utilized in other areas of the City. Mr. Crowell
noted that Assistant Services Director, Bruce Kartchner, prepared specific numbers to outline the
financial issues. That information could be reviewed later. Mr. Crowell noted that other RDA
Boards may have a strategy in place so there was always a return on the investment. In Bluffdale,
that money may be spent on roads or other necessary work in the City but may not return directly
back. Ultimately, the funds will likely expire.

Mr. Crowell gave examples of what the RDA could do with Tax Increment Financing. It could be
used to fund infrastructure improvements such as street improvements such as sewer, water, curb,
and gutter; focus on fee waivers and reimbursements; provide business development grants and
loans; property tax rebates and incentives; demolition; environmental cleanup; or land purchase.
He noted that Bluffdale had not yet provided business development grants and loans or property
tax rebates. Mr. Reid discussed one of the most difficult purchases made with the funds. With the
one-lane trestle, there was difficulty with fire and medical protection. The RDA Board approached
the taxing entities and explained that a Fire Station was needed in the area. With the assistance of
the School Board and the Water District, the Fire Station was approved. That was how the Fire
Station was built with tax increment money. The entire area is now protected. Mr. Crowell pointed
out that the current version of the law may not allow for that as it changes frequently. It currently
allows the City to use some for a park but that may not always be the case.

Sample images of redevelopment investments in Bluffdale were shared. Mr. Crowell noted that
the images were not the only investments that had been made. He shared information related to
the laws. As of the 2022 update, the Jordan School District was not interested in new Community
Reinvestment Project Areas and Salt Lake County has specific policies in place. Mr. Crowell
informed those present that a “But For” was needed. This was an important concept and
essentially, means that but for the project area, development would not happen. He discussed past
experiences and explained that there had been an idea that around Porter Rockwell Boulevard and
Redwood Road, toward the river, a new Jordan Crossing CRA could be created. The Council and
RDA passed Resolutions and an Attorney prepared the necessary documents. An application was
submitted to the County but the Jordan Crossing CRA had not moved forward.

Mr. Crowell overviewed some of the agency's responsibilities, which were as follows:

   •   Annual budget;
   •   Annual “November Report,” which was now online via the Governor’s Office;
   •   Monitoring of development and compliance with agreements; and
   •   Agency-specific accounts and internal controls.

A question was raised regarding the prison site in Draper. Mayor Hall pointed out that it is State
land. Mr. Crowell was not aware that there had been any financing or taxing set up for that yet.
He assumed something would be figured out because it is State-owned property and some entities
need to provide services there. He did not know the details as they relate to tax collection. Council
Member Crockett wondered if it would be set up like Olympia Hills, where it would be considered
a Special Taxing District. There was further discussion about the area. Mr. Crowell believed there
may be special agreements, such as Interlocal Agreements.

Mr. Crowell discussed trends and potential future Legislative issues. He reported that retail
incentives are not currently favored. In addition, there may not always be school district
participation. He pointed out that Legislative audits needed to include a “But For.” Mr. Crowell
noted that the laws are constantly changing. As a result, different strategies will need to be
developed in the future. He pointed out that Bluffdale is constrained geographically. If someone
wants something large for a manufacturing use, there is not necessarily space for it in the City.
Creating hundreds of acres of new project areas was unlikely.

Mr. Crowell shared the Bluffdale RDA/EDA Summary Report, which included the Bluffdale
Gateway Redevelopment Project Area, Eastern Bluffdale Economic Development Project Area,
and the Jordan Narrows Economic Development Project Area. For the Gateway RDA, all funds
are contractually committed. For the Eastern Bluffdale EDA, $17 million was listed and for the
Jordan Narrows EDA, there was $15 million listed. Those numbers were part of the June 30, 2022,
Fund Balance. The tax revenue was listed as $6.5 million for Eastern Bluffdale EDA and $2.4
million for the Jordan Narrows EDA. Mr. Kartchner clarified that those numbers were the

Currently, the City has $9 million allocated for low-income housing. There would need to be
continued work with the Council and RDA Board to look into potential locations and partners.
Mr. Crowell spoke to the County and they intended to have a Housing Trust Fund. He would
provide information in that regard. It was possible to make a notable impact with that $9 million.
Those funds are specific to housing and cannot be used to build a road. Mayor Hall reported that
the money could go toward something like The Bluffs or go back to the County for one of their
low-income housing projects. The money does not necessarily have to remain within the project
area but needs to address low-income housing. Mr. Reid noted that a small amount could be put
toward the community garden, which is across from the low-income housing apartments.
Mr. Crowell explained that the County has a lot of resources and he wanted to find out more about
the Housing Trust Fund since there may be some opportunities there.

Council Member Crockett wondered if there was a way to look at all of the options for the low-
income housing money. She noted that Ivory Homes has different projects throughout the State
and some of that housing would be dedicated to workforce housing. It is not low-income housing
but will address outstanding housing needs. Mr. Crowell did not believe that particular scenario
would apply. He reported that there are resources through Salt Lake County and at the State level
to determine how to spend the funds. There could be other options as well but Bluffdale does not
have many Staff resources devoted to the Housing Department. As a result, it made sense to rely
on the assistance of other professionals. The County was a good place to start.

Mayor Hall reported that several mayors serve on a County Board. Those mayors run cities and
towns under a certain population. 15 different mayors will determine where the money is spent
once it is given to the County. She would have to vote on where it would be allocated. A sub-
committee would hear from various applicants and then funding would need to be approved. Cities
with larger populations receive their own funding and need staff to determine how to utilize the
funds. Council Member Kallas inquired about the creation of a project area. He wondered if the
law determines the low-income housing percentage requirements. This was confirmed. A
percentage of what is collected has to be put toward low-income housing. Mr. Kartchner shared
additional information about the process as well as The Bluffs.

Mr. Crowell reported that the City was reaching the cap on the Jordan Narrows EDA. A lot of the
money had been committed. He overviewed some of the commitments that had been made, which
included Day Ranch Park. There was discussion regarding the Eastern Bluffdale EDA. Council
Member Kallas wondered if any of the money could go toward infrastructure such as a Public
Works facility. Mayor Hall did not believe that location was within the Eastern Bluffdale EDA
area.
