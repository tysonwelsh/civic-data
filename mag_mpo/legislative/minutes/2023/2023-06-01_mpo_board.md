Approve the FY2023-2024 Unified Planning Work Program Shawn Eliot, MAG 14:32 The Mountainland MPO Work Program describes the transportation planning work undertaken by Mountainland MPO staff, UDOT staff, and UTA staff within the MPO planning area. The Work Program is an annual budget for the planning activities to be undertaken using federal planning funds. It illustrates to our MPO partners, Federal Highway Administration, Federal Transit Administration, and the general public where the planning resources are planned to be allocated. It describes the planned work, budget, and funding sources. The FY24 Mountainland MPO Work Program has a total budget of $6.9m, $2.4m for staff and admin costs and $4.5m for studies and contracts. Revenue for the program comes from 44% federal funds, 14% state funds, 38% MPO Exchange funds, 3% UTA funds and less than 1% local funds. Staff costs have increased this year from $1.8m to $2.4m or 38%. This increase is due to an underestimation of needed funding last year with the Planning Department restructure. Last year's budget included two new staff positions (which were not filled) and more staff time within the RPO area. Looking over the last two years, the average staff costs have increased 5% per year. Studies and contracts have increased over 55% from last year due to multiple proposed corridor studies, the Safe Streets for All federal grant, the Statewide Grid Study, and an FTA Areas of Persistent Poverty Study. A detailed list of studies and contracts are listed in the Mountainland MPO Work Program. Commissioner Sakievich moved that the MPO Board approve to the 2023 -24 Unified Planning Work Program, contingent that no significant public comments are received that would require and update to the program. Commissioner Evans seconded the motion. Council Member Shane Marshall abstained. The motion passed unanimously. 2023 TransPlan50 Process Development Update LaNiece Davenport, MAG 23:43 With the intent of providing the MPO Board an opportunity to more fully understand the process for developing an RTP Ms. Davenport provided an overview of how the 2023 TransPlan50 was developed and highlighted some of the major differences between the 2019 RTP and the 2023 RTP, namely the financial assumptions used. Additionally, an overview was given of the process including a timeline of key activities and milestones, and how the needs-based project lists and the financially constrained project lists are developed. Namely, any delay places Utah County out of air quality compliance which results in the halt of all regional capacity adding construction projects (no matter funding source) and no new projects can be approved. Adopt the 2023 TransPlan50 and Air Quality Conformity Determination Report Shawn Eliot, MAG 42:32 Every four years, MAG is required to prepare and adopt the Regional Transportation Plan, TransPlan50. MAG staff have worked collaboratively with the municipal, UDOT a nd UTA staff to develop a land use scenario, project lists, and funding estimates. The plan and technical documentation show the regional context of the transportation system today, plans for the future, and a financial plan with revenues, costs, and deficits. The plan meets all federal requirements, including being fiscally constrained and meeting air quality conformity. There are three phases in the plan: Phase 1 2023-2032, Phase 2 2033-2042, Phase 3 2043-2050. MPO TAC and the MPO Board have reviewed the list of projects, revenues and costs and approved them to be in the plan. Total costs in the plan are up 32% since the 2019 plan. A few changes have happened since the financially Constrained Project List was approved by the TAC and MPO Board in March/April, here is a detailed summary : 1) For highway projects: a balance of small additional revenues allowed three projects to be added/moved into phase 1, three into phase 2, and two into phase 3; 2) No changes were made to transit projects; 3) Active Transport ation (AT) projects were adopted as a draft list with the understanding that further refinement was needed, this refinement resulted in the combining of projects, changing limits, and cleaning up errors. MAG staff recommend that the MPO Board consider the TAC recommendation to approve the changes to the highway and active transportation lists shown at the May 22, 2023 TAC meeting; move the Point of the Mountain Connector Freeway project to vision; move the Light rail project to vision; and add the Meadows Crossing project as Vision; and approve the 2023 TransPlan50 and Air Quality Conformity Determination Report. 2:12:59 Commissioner Powers-Gardner moved that the MPO Board adopt the final phased and financially constrained 2023 TransPlan50 Regional Transportation Plan and the Air Quality Conformity Determination Report with: • SR198 and University Parkway updates; • The proposed changes to the Highway and Active Transportation lists as recommended by the MPO TAC; AND • Keep the Point of the Mountain Connector Freeway project and Vineyard Central Corridor Light Rail Project to the airport in phase 3 unfunded and add Meadows Crossing project to phase 3 unfunded. Mayor Westmoreland seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.
