    Marshall N. White Center Facility Report
             The Administration will review the Marshall N. White Center Facility Evaluation conducted by VCBO, which includes:
\                 • Architectural Evaluation;
)                 •    Structural Evaluation;
                  •    Aquatic Evaluation; and
                  • ASCE 31 Structural Evaluation.
             The report was based on a visual inspection of the premises to provide the City with a general understanding of the
    building's condition. No measurements, calculations or analysis were completed to determine actual load carrying capacities, the
    functionality of the mechanical and electrical systems or compliance with accessibility requirements. Additional field work must
    be completed before any renovations can be undertaken. The report indicates that overall, the building has been well maintained.
    However, its design is outdated and does not meet current code requirements.
             Public Services Director Lowder introduced Brent Tippets ofVCBO Architecture, who used the aid of a PowerPoint
    presentation to discuss the facility evaluation conducted by his fmn. He provided the Council with the details of the building site,
    including acreage, square footage, and parking accommodations and indicated that it is easily accessible, located in an established
    spot, has compatibility with the community, and a rich history. The facilitate assessment yielded the following fmdings:
                  • HVAC:
                            o The facility's heating and air conditioning systems have been maintained and upgraded
                                 throughout the life of the building but are in significant need of replacement due to their
                                 age. Upgrading these systems would be costly, but the savings from the increased efficacy
                                 would bring dramatic savings in the future.
                  •    Exterior Envelope:
                            o The exterior walls are under-insulated and are a significant factor in energy loss.
                                 Updating the building envelope would reduce air leakage and increase its insulating
                                 qualities. This investment would be paid back in the future with decreased building
                                 energy expenses.
                  •    Parking:
                            o The facility's parking lot is considerably undersized. Using similar facilities as a guide, the parking lot
                                 should be at least tlu·ee times larger than its current size. Adding the occupant load of the park would
                                 drive the capacity of the lot even higher.
             The gymnasium is a functional space that offers versatility in its use; it is in relatively good condition and has stage

           The pool facility was also part of the assessment; VCBO found a failure of the pool shell. The life expectancy of a pool
of this construction type is approximately 30 to 40 years. The existing swimming pool is 50 years old and may be well past the
expected lifespan. The pool has experienced structural cracking and failure and subsequent testing has revealed sub-standard
concrete strengths, corroded reinforcing, and spalling that are all indicators that it would be very difficult to extend the life of the
pool shell for an additional15 years without replacing much or all of the pool structure. With this and other deficiencies outlined
in the report, it is V CBO' s recommendation that the City strongly consider replacing the swimming pool versus putting additional
funds into repairs and maintenance given that the pool is currently in a state of failure and would continue to have additional
failures.
           Mr.' Tippets then discussed accessibility considerations for the facility; this includes the overall site, front counter,
bathrooms, locker/shower areas, elevator, pool, stage access, railings, clearings, thresholds, and fitness equipment. The facility's
age places it out of compliance with many current accessibility standards. As a pmt of a facility upgrade, site and building
elements would require extensive renovation to comply. In regard to the structural condition and life/safety issues at the facility,
VCBO found that the facilities ability to safely withstand the maximum expected earthquake is a significant safety concern. Many
elements of the facility do not comply with modem life-safety building code such as handrails, egress widths, etc. The gymnasium
is in relatively good shape for its age. However, due to the high demand for meeting space, activities such as basketball are often
pushed aside to use the gym as a gathering space. He concluded that based on this initial review and a review of an earlier seismic
evaluation report that was prepared for the MWC, it appears that many areas of this building have reached or exceeded their initial
service life and will require significant repair, replacement, or upgrading. Since the construction of the building in 1967, the
International Building Code (IBC) has undergone many improvements to enhance the life safety of buildings like this during an
earthquake. Unfortunately, many of the basic code-prescribed improvements or requirements do not exist and they will need to be
added to increase the life safety aspects of this building. Additionally, many of the elements are under-reinforced, over-loaded, or
deteriorating. At a minimum, it is recommended that a full and complete seismic evaluation and structural upgrade be
incorporated as a part of any long-term upgrade, remodel, or addition to this building.
           Council member Choberka inquired as to the total scope of work for the project that has been awarded to VCBO. Mr.
Tippets stated that VCBO is meeting frequently with a steering committee that has been appointed and tasked to work on this
project; the facility analysis has been completed along with a public survey and the results ofthat survey are being tabulated. This
will give VCBO information about desired programming and budget implications of any future projects to be completed at the
facility. A fmal recommendation will be presented to the City Council once each of those independent processes are completed.
Vice Chair White inquired as to the timeline for the fmal recommendation to be available to the Council. Mr. Tippets stated that
he hopes to present the fmal recommendations to the Council in May or June of this year.
           Council member Choberka asked ifVCBO has considered any historical element of the building when performing their
structural analysis. Mr. Tippets stated that VCBO is familiar with the building and understands the need to recognize and preserve
any historical significance or elements of the building; the fmal recommendation will include opportunities for preserving the rich
history of what has been a focal point for Ogden City for many years. Vice Chair White asked if the historical elements to be
preserved are tangible or non-tangible. Mr. Tippets stated there are some tangible historical elements at the facility, such as the
gymnasium floor; however, the history of some programs in the City are tied to the MWC, such as a vital boxing program. Those
are the types of things that can be preserved as the project moves forward. Council member Choberka asked Mr. Tippets ifhe has
been tasked with focusing on the current site of the MWC or other sites in the community. Mr. Tippets state the former is correct;
VCBO is only focusing on the MWC in its present location.
