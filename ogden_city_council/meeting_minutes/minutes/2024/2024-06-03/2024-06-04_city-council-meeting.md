Enterprise Fund Transfers
         A memo from Council staff regarding the proposed Enterprise Fund Transfers came before the Council for consideration.
 The memo stated Utah Code Annotated §10-6-135.5 requires a notice to all users when a city is proposing to transfer funds from
an enterprise fund to another fund for purposes not directly related to services provided by that enterprise fund. The City must also
hold a public hearing on the transfers prior to adoption or the amendment of the City’s annual budget. Ogden has transferred funds
from the City’s water, sanitary sewer, storm sewer, and refuse utility enterprise funds to the general fund for decades. The
requirement to provide notice of the transfers and to hold a separate public hearing is the result of a change to state law that
occurred in 2016. The Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget includes a proposal to transfer funds from the water ($3,224,250), sanitary
sewer ($1,784,575), storm sewer ($768,000) and refuse ($912,000) utility funds to the City’s general fund in lieu of the collection
of franchise and property taxes that non-governmental utilities pay. A public hearing on the proposed transfers is scheduled for
June 18, 2024.
         COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED A PUBLIC HEARING ON THE PROPOSED ENTERPRISE FUND
TRANSFER BE HELD IN THE CITY COUNCIL CHAMBERS ON JUNE 18, 2024, AT 6:00 P.M. MOTION WAS
SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER GRAF, ALL VOTING AYE. Council member Choberka was not present when
this vote was taken.

Proposed Ordinance 2024-16 amending the Ogden Municipal Code to
revise utility rates and billing and to make technical changes
        A memo from the Public Services Department regarding proposed water rate and discount fee amendments came before
the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposed amendments would increase water rates for all users by four percent
per year for the next five years. This would be a set increase over and above the blended inflation adjustment. The Council has
received presentations on the proposed funding for the Canyon waterline and the Council adopted a parameters resolution for
approximately $77 million in federal and state loans. The rate increases are needed to pay the debt service on the loans. The
Council will also receive a presentation on proposed changes to the water and refuse discount program. Currently, Weber County
administers a property tax relief program and provides the list of qualified participants to the City. The City applies a 50 percent
water base rate discount and a $2.50 refuse discount to each utility bill. The proposal is to expand the program to individuals who
are blind, to disabled veterans, and to deployed members of the military and would apply a set monthly discount rate of $15.50 to
the billing account. The discount would increase with the blended inflation adjustment.
          Chair Richey then introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-16, entitled:
                    “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Section 9-1-6 to revise
                    utility rates and billing; to make technical changes; and by providing that this ordinance shall become effective
                    immediately upon posting after final passage.”
          COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED A PUBLIC HEARING ON THE PROPOSAL BE HELD IN THE CITY
COUNCIL CHAMBERS ON JUNE 18, 2024, AT 6:00 P.M. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER

Proposed Ordinance 2024-17 approving the Capital Improvement Plan for
Ogden City for the Fiscal Years 2025-2029
         A memo from the Management Services Department regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2025-2029 Capital
Improvement Plan (CIP) came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the CIP is reviewed and adopted each year
as a planning tool for major capital investments in the City’s physical plant over a rolling five-year period. Each year’s plan is
updated based on the changing needs of the City, funding opportunities, projects completed and other similar factors. During this
year’s budget process, proposed capital projects will be reviewed alongside each department’s proposed budget and in context
with the overall needs and resources available to the City through the budget process.
         Chair Richey then introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-17, entitled:
                  “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, approving the Capital Improvement Plan for Ogden City for the Fiscal

Proposed Ordinance 2024-14 adopting the proposed tentative budget of
Ogden City for Fiscal Year 2024-2025
          A memo from the Council staff regarding the Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 budget came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated the Council will hold a public hearing on the Tentative Budget and staff’s recommendation is for
the Council to continue the public hearing to June 18, 2024; to consider public input and continue discussions regarding the
Council changes. Mayor Nadolski presented his proposed budget on May 7, 2024; the proposed budget totals $90,106,375, which
is a $7.5 million – or 9.11 percent – increase when compared to the FY24 adopted budget.
          Council Deputy Director Symes summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
details of the Tentative Budget, including budget resources, guiding documents, year over year comparisons, personnel changes,
wages and benefits, capital improvement projects, and a proposed property tax increase. For a copy of the presentation in its
entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-14, entitled:
                    “An ordinance of Ogden City Utah, adopting the proposed tentative budget of Ogden City for Fiscal Year 2024-
                    2025.”
          Chair Richey then opened the public hearing.
          Jack Crosland referenced the use of Business Depot Ogden (BDO) lease revenue in excess of what is allowed by
ordinance. He asked if that is a one-time matter or if the increased transfer amount will be ongoing each budget year.
          James Edwards stated he does not feel the Police Department has done its job in terms of protecting the public; he
received a property in 2022 and since then has made numerous calls to the Police Department, but he has only received six case
numbers and the only time he has received assistance is today when he was able to talk to the Police Chief. He stated the City
Council needs to dedicate more funding to law enforcement because the Police Department is understaffed and overworked. He
has been threatened with violence and his car has been vandalized to the point that it contributed to a vehicular accident on
Interstate-15. He has spoken with the Mayor’s office about his desires to make Ogden a better place, but he does not know how
that can occur without adequate law enforcement and improved public safety.
          Sarge Morelli stated she has been working with Ogden City for 10 years regarding some of the matters included in the
tentative budget, including street disrepair/damages; property maintenance; street signage; public safety; traffic enforcement;
inadequate parking in Ogden’s downtown area; and poor leadership of the Police Department.
          There being no further persons appearing to be heard, COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED THE PUBLIC
HEARING BE CONTINUED TO JUNE 18, 2024. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS, ALL
VOTING AYE. Council member Choberka was not present when this vote was taken.
          Chair Richey invited Council staff to address the questions asked during the public hearing. Mr. Symes addressed Mr.
Crosland’s question regarding the use of BDO lease revenue; the City ordinance states that the City must use BDO lease revenue
for prioritized items before using funding on other matters. The ordinance adjustment that has been proposed by the Mayor would
be permanent.
          Chair Richey noted that the other comments that were offered are administrative in nature, and he asked Mayor Nadolski
if he wished to respond to those. Mayor Nadolski offered his support to the Police Chief and his Department; specific to rumors

administration is working on that type of program, and it should be delivered before July 1, 2024. He added he will look into Mr.
Edwards’ concerns.

Public Comments
          Kerry Wayne referred to his past comments regarding the condition of 1200 West on the western border of Business
Depot Ogden (BDO); there has been standing water present for the past eight months and there has been little movement from
Ogden City or The Boyer Company. Ogden City benefits financially from BDO and the residents of Marriott-Slaterville City that
are being impacted by this matter are asking that Ogden City address the problem. The roadway is highly travelled and Marriott-
Slaterville is exploring options for improving it and addressing the drainage issues, but Ogden City should be involved. The
construction on the west side of BDO has killed property values for those along 1200 West and the residents should not have to
beg for any support from Ogden City; the Mayor and City Administration have talked about their values and integrity and that
should be considered when evaluating whether the City should be involved in repairing the drainage issues that were caused by
The Boyer Company.

Mayor Comments
         Mayor Nadolski addressed Mr. Wayne; he has said that he would do everything he could to help address the concerns of
the Marriott-Slaterville residents and he has done that. The information that Mr. Wayne has offered is incomplete or untruthful.
Ogden City cannot find millions of dollars to fix a road in another city. He has said he would support the submittal of an
application for funding from the Weber Area Council of Governments (WACOG) to help address 1200 West. He stated that a
process must be followed in order for any request for funding to be considered and WACOG will visit the area. He does not set
the process or timelines, nor can he require Marriott-Slaterville Administration to complete the funding application in a timely
manner. However, he is doing what he said he would do, and he does have integrity and values.
