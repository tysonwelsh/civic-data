Action/Discussion Items

Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District 2023 Proposed Budget

              Pam Roberts, General Manager, Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District
(WFWRD) presented a PowerPoint presentation reviewing the 2023 budget priorities, that
includes a $2.50 per home per month fee increase, various services provided to residents,
continuous improvements on efficiencies using technology on equipment. Alternate fuels will
continue to be used to keep costs low and it continues to find ways to expand the Seasonal
Container Reservation Program (SCRP) at the lowest cost possible. Hiring and retaining CDL
drivers remains a challenge and effects SCRP. A public hearing for the 2023 budget will be held
on November 14, 2022, at 6:00 pm at 604 West 6960 South.

               Paul Korth, Finance Director, WFWRD, reviewed cost increases since the last fee
increase in 2018 and WFWRD’s cash projects for 2022-2026.

                                          RECITALS

   A. The Magna Metro Township (“Magna City”) is a Municipality pursuant to Utah Code §§

   B. House Bill 96 adopted in April, 2021 requires each Metro Township to designate an

Redevelopment Agency Façade Improvement Grant

               Adrian West, Municipal Economic Development Manager, stated the selection
committee met last week and choose three Magna Main Street businesses as part of the
Redevelopment Agency (RDA) Façade Improvement Grant. The Empress building, Filling
Station, and the Panama Building were all awarded $50,000 each. Jevon Gibb, Economic
Development Director, and himself will meet with the building owners to discuss the scope of work
and next steps and then Stallings Construction. After meeting with both parties, he will connect
Stallings Construction with the building owners and hand the project off.

               Council Member Barney asked why connecting the building owners with Stallings
Construction is important and what the scope of work is.

               Mr. West stated liability with the projects is a concern for Salt Lake County. There
will be a contract with Stallings Construction and another contract with the building owners.
Stallings Construction will contract with the building owners separately to perform the work. There
was not a lot of clarity with the type of projects that were awarded. The building owners will only
be able to complete a couple of projects given the budget. He will work with them to prioritize the
projects, determine what makes sense with everyone involved, while keeping in mind the deadline
and what the budget is. Before Stallings Construction begins working with the building owners,
they want the project scope narrowed down.

              Council Member Barney asked what the success of the project is and will the
program continue.

               Mr. West stated the program will continue for at least a few more years. He is
hoping to increase the allocation that can be contributed.

State Legislative Performance Audit on Tax Increment Financing

                Jevon Gibb, Economic Development Director, stated the State Legislature
released a performance audit that was completed on Redevelopment Agencies (RDA) around
Utah. Three major recommendations that came from the audit are: guidance on managing
unexpended TIF funds once a collection period expires, require local governments to make
financial information publicly available for each project area, and require local governments to

                                                5
DATE            TUESDAY                                   OCTOBER                          11, 2022

conduct a robust justification study known as a “but for” analysis that adequately justifies the use
of TIF funds.

Magna Metro Township currently has four project areas that have not been dissolved and two
active project areas. Preservation Utah was holding $400,000 in a revolving loan fund that was
meant to help businesses recover from the earthquake. Businesses did not take advantage of the
loan. He was able to get that money back and will expend those funds so that there is not a fund
balance sitting in an inactive project area. This will address the unexpended TIF funds
recommendation.

The State has a database that RDAs are required to report financial information to. The Salt Lake
County RDA is in compliance with this recommendation.


The Salt Lake County RDA has not historically, completed a “but for” analysis. As the Executive
Director, he has a fiduciary responsibility to the stakeholders to complete the “but for” analysis to
determine that the amount of taxpayer dollars requested are actually necessary to make a
development feasible.

A bill is being opened at the Legislature to look at TIF reform. He would expect, if not specifically
set forth in the bill, that more audits will be conducted. He would expect the State will audit the
Salt Lake County RDA and that will open the Magna project areas.

               Council Member Pierce asked what the funds from Preservation Utah is going to
be spent on.

               Mr. Gibb stated $150,000 was used for the Façade Improvement Program. He
would like to address the remaining funds at another time.

               Council Member Pierce asked if Arbor Park should have a “but for” analysis
completed.

              Mr. Gibb stated a consulting firm is currently conducting the “but for” analysis for
Arbor Park. He expects to get the report early next week and the report will be presented to the
Council on October 25.

              Council Member Hull asked after the “but for” analysis is completed, does it come
before the Council or the Salt Lake County RDA for approval.

                 Mr. Gibb stated the project will come before the Magna Metro Township Council
first. The final decision will be made by the Salt Lake County RDA Board. In his discussions with
the Salt Lake County RDA Board, there is deference to the Magna Metro Township.

               Council Member Hull asked if there is an option for the Magna Metro Township
to take over the RDA Board.

Meeting Organization, Procedures, and Consent Agenda

               Council Member Hull recommended changing the format of the Council meetings
to include one work meeting and one business meeting a month. There will be no public comment
or reports from the various groups during the work meeting. It will strictly be a meeting to sit down
with various department and individuals to discuss future planning and other items. Currently
items are coming before the Council that need to be passed and the Council is rushing through
them. The Council will then hold their next meeting as a business meeting where it will adopt
resolutions, ordinances, make motions, take public comment, and get updates from various
groups.

                Council Member Pierce stated the Council hears updates from organizations
every two weeks which is not a good use of time on either side. These organizations have full
authority to do what they are supposed to be doing. Individuals are feeling rushed to get through
a presentation and the Council is unable to fully understand the agenda item. The agenda is filled
with a lot of day to day items that does not move the Metro Township forward. There are other
important issues that the Council needs time to discuss and understand.

               Council Member Barney stated he is frustrated with the lack of time to fully digest
and understand items on the agenda. The Council has a fiduciary responsibility to the constituents
and that means he needs to read the packet and be able to understand the issue and its
implications so an informed decision can be made. He supports a meeting format that allows for
rigorous discussion, debate, vetting, and questions at one meeting and then come back to make
decisions at another meeting.

               Council Member Pierce stated if public comment is allowed during the work
meeting, it needs to be related to an agenda item and taken after the item is discussed. Other
