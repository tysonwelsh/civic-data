1.    DISCUSSION REGARDING HOMELESS AND TRANSIENT CAMP ISSUES WITHIN UTAH
      COUNTY, AND POSSIBLE COUNTY ORDINANCE IMPLEMENTATION TO ADDRESS SAID
      ISSUES (Continued from the September 5, 2017 Commission meeting)
(Track A, 15:15)
Utah County Sheriff’s Office Lieutenant Yvette M. Rice, and Sergeant Wayne Keith, came forward to present on
homeless and transient camp issues in the county. Lieutenant Rice explained there has been an increase in transient-
type camps specifically in the canyon areas, and it is causing significant negative environmental. There are also
concerns for citizen safety. Sergeant Keith has been tasked with collecting information, working closely with Utah
County Deputy Attorney Carl Hollan in the last year to canvass the areas and provide an overall picture of crime and
community impact. They are proposing an ordinance to pursue criminal charges (a copy of which can be found
attached to these minutes).

Sergeant Wayne Keith explained the problem has become consistently worse in recent years. He talked about
jurisdiction and the problems in identifying federal and local responsibilities. The county is receiving complaints of
campers setting up for long-term stays, saturating an area with waste before moving on and making it impossible for
residents and visitors to enjoy the space. Sgt. Keith presented the Board with photographs (copies of which can be
found attached to these minutes), noting stolen property is also frequently recovered at these same camp sites. He
mentioned law enforcement empathizes with individuals that are going through hardships, but those persons are not
the majority here: instead it is mainly drug abusers, organized criminals, and others who have decided not to participate
in society. Sgt. Keith pointed out there was even a deceased body that was left unreported for some time.

(Track A, 22:15)
Commissioner Graves noted the proposed ordinance would impact the individuals who are, to paraphrase, ‘down on
their luck.’ When Sergeant Wayne Keith responded most of the people they encounter are involved in drugs and
burglaries, Commissioner Graves pointed out there are existing laws to address those issues. Utah County Sheriff
James O. Tracy added there are also services available for people needing assistance, but those services are not in the
canyon. Sheriff Tracy mentioned the deputies often offer help but are turned down. Sgt. Keith explained how he
carries business cards in his pockets to direct the transients to services, and has personally offered cash and food to
people but it is not feasible to follow if this results in any progress.

(Track A, 27:00)
Commissioner Lee and Sergeant Wayne Keith discussed a federal 14-day and five-mile radius restriction on camping.
Sgt. Keith detailed the unrealistic federal court prosecuting process, noting Provo City has their own ordinance for
addressing these issues and both Spanish Fork and American Fork cities are reviewing similar options. Commissioner
Graves asked if it would help for the county to adopt a code matching the federal code, and Sgt. Keith replied ‘yes.’
They discussed the abandoned camp sites, with Utah County Sheriff James O. Tracy pointing out the rise in transient
camps in Utah County correlates with recent solutions imposed in Salt Lake County for the same problems – the
people are relocating. Sheriff Tracy is concerned parts of Utah County will become like Pioneer Park in Salt Lake
City, where there is a permanent ‘tent city’ rampant in drug use with unsanitary conditions. Commissioner Graves
repeated there are already laws in place to address those issues.

(Track A, 33:25)
Sergeant Wayne Keith talked about being unable to pursue warrants from other jurisdictions, and the impossible task
of ‘catching people in the act’ when participants of illegal activity in the canyons are claiming ignorance of each other.
Utah County Deputy Attorney Carl Hollan confirmed, detailing some of the current laws which only address
individuals but not entire criminal communities. Mr. Hollan talked about Canyon Glen Park in Provo, where there
have been reports of drugs and paraphernalia; criminals are preying on citizens wanting to use the facilities
legitimately. He explained how this ordinance was drafted in comparison to federal and state laws in an effort to
prevent problems without infringing on rights. He is also concerned about county liability. Mr. Hollan summarized
the ordinance would only apply to Utah County property and would specify procedures for removal of unlawful
campsites, and Commissioner Graves countered it would perhaps be easier to simply get rid of all county parks.

(Track A, 41:50)
Commissioner Lee and Carl Hollan talked about semi-permanent structures in the camps. Commissioner Graves and
Mr. Hollan discussed cleanup options, with Commissioner Lee hypothesizing on ways a group could circumvent the
24-hour camping restrictions. Mr. Hollan pointed out removal of property would help minimize those issues, noting
the goal is not to increase citations but to address the problem. Commissioner Graves stated this conversation is about
finding a constitutional and humane way to tell people, “We want you all out of here,” and Mr. Hollan disagreed with
that statement. Mr. Hollan replied county property needs to be maintained within confines that are safe and enjoyable
for everybody.

(Track A, 49:55)
Utah County Sheriff James O. Tracy summarized the purpose of county parks: day use by taxpayers, which is being
prevented by criminal activity and ecological dangers. Sheriff Tracy and Commissioner Graves debated trespassing
and criminal code laws. Commissioner Lee noted he does not want to tie the hands of law enforcement, but also does
not want to hamper citizens. Carl Hollan stated they need to strike a balance and allow officers an enforcement
mechanism without hampering legitimate recreational users; a father camping with his son for a night should not be
penalized. Mr. Hollan noted if nothing is done, the county will be allowing the criminals to victimize citizens.

2.     DISCUSSION REGARDING THE COUNTY’S PARTICIPATION IN THE 2020 LOCAL UPDATE OF
       CENSUS ADDRESSES (LUCA) OPERATION (Continued from the September 5, 2017 Commission
       meeting)
(Track A, 59:45)
Utah County Civil Division Chief Deputy Attorney David Shawcroft came forward with Patrick Wawro, Geographic
Information System (GIS) Manager for Utah County. Mr. Wawro summarized the federal government is requesting
the county verify all residential addresses, municipal boundaries, and other data. While the county has the expertise
to complete the task, there are many additional steps required by the federal government. Mr. Wawro noted the
information is already available on a daily basis through the county, and the cost to the county would be over 500
hours of staff time throughout the upcoming years. Commissioner Graves asked why there are piles of undeliverable
mailed ballots when Mr. Wawro is so confident in the county’s management of records, and Mr. Wawro explained
the resources are already dedicated to those issues – participating in the LUCA Operation would pull staff members
away from those exact daily maintenance routines. Mr. Wawro summarized there is not a great return of investment
for the county in participating in the census.
