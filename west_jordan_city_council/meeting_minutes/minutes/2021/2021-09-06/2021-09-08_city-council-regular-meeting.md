 B.    9000 S UDOT Widening Project
Council Office Director Alan R. Anderson explained UDOT desired to widen 9000 South from
Redwood Road to the east City boundary during the 2023 construction season. He asked the Council
if they were interested in participating with betterments, and what their betterment priorities
would be (e.g., walls, park strips, street lights). Without betterments, the project would involve
sidewalk, curb/gutter, and seven traffic lanes.

Public Works Director Brian Clegg oriented the Council with three possible areas for betterment:
   •   Jordan River – east City boundary to roughly west side of Utah Islamic Center/River Oaks
       Suites
   •   Residential area - roughly west side of Utah Islamic Center/River Oaks Suites to 1300 West
   •   Commercial area – 1300 West to Redwood Road

Staff answered questions from the Council about the planned widening project. Council Member
Lamb said he liked the street lights along 7800 South, and said he was comfortable with spacing
street lights every 200 feet up to 1300 West. Vice Chair Green and Chair Jacob said they agreed.
Council Member McConnehey said he was unsure about street light spacing. The Council discussed
pedestrian activity.

Vice Chair Green said he wanted a safe harbor crosswalk at the corner of 9000 South and 1300 West
for nearby Riverside Elementary. City Administrator Korban Lee responded that safe harbor
crosswalks were difficult when left-turn lanes were involved. Vice Chair Green suggested providing
a crosswalk away from the busy intersection for school children. Mr. Lee suggested inviting input
from the Police Department.

Staff explained that RDA funds were available for betterments in the commercial area from 1300
West to Redwood Road. Mr. Clegg stated he would prefer to not put trees in landscaped park strips.

He recommended low-growing landscaping. Deputy Public Works Director Isaac Astill added the
City was responsible for maintenance of any betterments installed. Chair Jacob said he liked the
center medians along the South Jordan Parkway at the Daybreak entrance. Vice Chair Green said he
agreed visually appealing center islands should be included where possible, depending on cost. Mr.
Anderson said staff would take Council preferences to UDOT and come back with cost estimates for
further discussion.

A majority of the Council indicated a desire to pursue betterments for the 9000 South project. Street
lights were designated as the top priority, followed by landscaped park strips, panel wall near
residential properties, and other betterments (landscaped medians). The Council discussed
improving pedestrian walkways. Council Member McConnehey said he believed improved
pedestrian walkways would set the area up well for redevelopment opportunities in the future.

A member of staff suggested the 9000 South widening project would be an opportunity to improve
the appearance of the entrance to the City. Vice Chair Green suggested an electronic marquee to
advertise City events. City Attorney Rob Wall commented that such an electronic marquee would
be considered an off-premise sign, and if the City could have an off-premise sign, other entities
would also be able to have them. He said law regarding off-site governmental advertising was
unclear. Council Member McConnehey expressed interest in obtaining cost estimates before
determining level of risk.

Mr. Clegg said he would continue to meet with UDOT and bring back information as it was available.
Vice Chair Green asked if there were betterments used by other cities that the Council had not
discussed, and suggested including some type of artwork in median strips. He asked how the 9000
South widening project could be used to help spur redevelopment along 9000 South. Council
Member McConnehey suggested infrastructure upgrades that would help support redevelopment
in the future. Mr. Lee emphasized the opportunity to improve the appearance of the entrance to the
City.

  A.    City Hall Remodel
City Administrator Korban Lee reported staff had been diligently working on the City Hall remodel
project. He reviewed the goals for the project with the Council:

        •   Better public access and greater public use of the building
        •   Make building more flexible and functional for the next 30 years
               o Planning for growth, changes in government services, changes in office use
        •   Improve user experience
               o Improve the image and impression of the City
               o More convenient public access
               o More visible and recognizable entrances for City Departments
        •   Update to ADA compliance and current building codes
               o Improve mechanical and electrical systems
        •   Reduce on-going costs and support revenue opportunities
               o Create spaces that support greater staff efficiencies or can improve revenue
               o More efficient mechanical and electrical systems
               o More efficient use of square footage
        •   Create facilities that help recruit and retain employees
               o Spaces to promote the health, wellness, and welfare of employees
               o Improved security without noticing the security

   complete construction drawings and proceed with bid specs, plan review, and permitting. Mr. Lee

 7.    BUSINESS ITEMS
       b. Jones Ranch Public Infrastructure Agreement
Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck presented proposed Resolution No. 21-042, which
would authorize the Mayor to execute the Jones Ranch Public Infrastructure Agreement. The
agreement would benefit the City in providing a financial plan and the foundation for necessary
public infrastructure to support development in the Jones Ranch area and the southwest quadrant.
The City would have authority and oversight over the design and construction process for Zone 5
water tank #1 and would pay the master developer $600,000 within 30 days of the agreement for
costs related to design services. The City would also make a payment to the master developer in the
City Council Meeting Minutes – September 8, 2021                                                 Page 4

amount of $18 million by December 31, 2025, for construction, land, and easements. The City would
commit with the agreement to construct a Zone 6 water tank by December 31, 2027. Ms. Steck
explained the City would secure with the agreement a future fire station site (U-111 and 8600
South) and a site for Zone 5 water tank #2 (adjacent to Zone 5 water tank #1), with the City
responsible to pay the lesser of $500,000 or the appraised value for each site.

Ms. Steck said she believed the proposed agreement to be in the best interest of the City for return
on investment. She emphasized that the payments from the City were not “reimbursements”
because the full cost to the developer would exceed the cost to the City. A representative of the Jones
Ranch development indicated to Mr. Lee that the applicant would enter into the agreement with
removal of the word “reimbursement”. Ms. Steck provided clarification regarding water impact fees
related to the project. Council Member Lamb said he believed the clarification was important.

MOTION:       Vice Chair Green moved to approve Resolution No. 21-042 authorizing the Mayor
              to execute the Jones Ranch Public Infrastructure Agreement, removing the word
              “reimbursement” from the footer.
              Council Member McConnehey seconded the motion.

MOTION:       Vice Chair Green amended the motion to approve Resolution No. 21-042
              authorizing the Mayor to execute the Jones Ranch Public Infrastructure
              Agreement, inserting the word “infrastructure” in place of the word
              “reimbursement” in the footer.
              Council Member McConnehey seconded the amended motion.
