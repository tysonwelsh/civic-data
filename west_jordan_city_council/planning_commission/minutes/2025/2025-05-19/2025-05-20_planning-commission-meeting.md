2. My Haven Day Support; 1227 West 9000 South; Conditional Use Permit for Adult
Daycare; SC-2 Zone; My Haven Host Home/Wendy Kichiro (applicant) [#34528; parcel
27-02-301-093]
Caroline Kichiro stated that the business provides daycare services during the hours of 8 a.m. to 2 p.m.
for adults with intellectual disabilities. They will begin with three staff members and four clients, who
will be dropped off and picked up at the location. My Haven Day Support provides a place for
socialization, getting out into the community, and learning new skills.
Lisa Kichiro reviewed the application, stating that their business aligns with the goals and policies of
the City of West Jordan by providing community based services for older individuals with disabilities,
and contributes to an inclusive city without impacting the residential functions of the surrounding area.
There are no detrimental effects with the use, which complies with the zoning district in which it is
located. It is a low-intensity day program that produces minimal noise and traffic, with all activities
taking place indoors under qualified supervision. All site plan requirements have been met and no
additional buffering, screening, or landscaping, are required. Clients are transported to and from the
program, so parking is only needed for staff and one transport vehicle. The plan is to start with three
staff and four clients with plans to expand in the future with the SPD occupancy rates. They will
comply with the recommended condition of approval. No heavy equipment, commercial deliveries, or
high traffic volumes are expected. All utilities are sufficient for their use. The fire department reviewed
the plans and had no concerns. Staff will be trained in emergency procedures, and safety protocols will
be in place to ensure the safety of their clients. They feel that the use provides for a critical need.

4. Dollar Tree; 7741 South 5600 West; Preliminary Site Plan; SC-2 Zone; Peterson
Development Co., LLC/Paul Stringham (applicant) [#33930; parcel 20-25-351-016]
Steven Lord, representing the applicant, stated reasons by the proposal complies with West Jordan
ordinances, requirements, and approval criteria. The property is 1.26 acres and will house a single
story building that will be buffered with parking and landscaping. Internal vehicle and pedestrian
connections are provided within the shopping center. The property is zoned and master planned for
medium-scale commercial uses that will help to support the city’s economic development and provide
services to the area. Building design, height, and materials are comparable to the surrounding
commercial structures and meet screening and architectural requirements. Proper drainage,
infrastructure, and traffic connections are provided. Loading areas and screened dumpsters are placed
away from residential and customer areas. Landscaping percentages are met at 15% for the disturbed
area, and parking lot lighting is designed to meet all codes. Vehicle circulation was approved by the
fire marshal, and a new crosswalk connects to the public sidewalk. Forty-three parking spaces were
approved with administrative relief and are sufficient considering there is shared parking within
Highlands Landing Commercial and typical dollar store needs. All existing public services are
adequate to service the new development as confirmed by the Public Utilities department. Mr. Lord
stated that the project will be a valuable community asset, providing needed services.
Mark Forsythe said retail uses are permitted in this location. He pointed out a new residential
subdivision to the east that will provide pedestrian traffic to the site without causing an impact on the
residents. Administrative relief was also given on two landscape islands in the parking lot that were
reduced to six feet due to the existing driveways that constrict the site. Also, the landscape requirement
is calculated on the disturbed area due to the same constrictions. He stated that the Design Review
Committee had requested that the dumpster enclosure gates be solid metal instead of wood paneling,
and that change was made.
