Mayor Stevenson said he has appointed Garrett Wilcox to the position of City Attorney.
As required under Midvale City Ordinance, he was submitting Garrett's appointment to
the City Council for advice and consent.

Garrett has served as Midvale's Deputy City Attorney since 2016. During his six years
working for the City, Garrett has proven to be a versatile, talented, and hardworking
attorney that has been involved in all aspects of municipal government. ln this role he has
provided legal counsel on complex issues, overseen transactions, drafted city policies,
and litigated various types of cases in the Justice Court and in District Court. While
capable of providing legal services in all areas of city government, Garrett has become a
subject matter expert in the areas of procurement, engineering, business licensing, and
construction permitting. Garrett has also shown a particular aptitude for land use law,
which is often one of the most legally challenging areas of municipal government. Garrett
has served as legal counsel to the Midvale City Planning Commission for nearly 3 years
and is the primary attorney working with staff on land use issues. ln addition to his direct
work for Midvale, he has also contributed to the development of state legislation, most
notably and laudably on the issue of small-cell permitting.

Garrett is licensed by the Utah State Bar Association, and received his Bachelors of
Political Science, Masters of Public Administration, and Juris Doctor from Brigham Young
University.

Adam Olsen said House Bill462 was passed during the 2022 legislative session. The bill
requires municipalities with fixed rail guideways (commuter and light rail) to complete

Station Area Plans (SAP's) covering a half mile radius of each fixed rail station. SAP's
may be unique to their jurisdiction and sefting; however, key aspects will include
strategies to increase the availability of affordable housing, promotion of sustainable
environmental conditions, enhancement of access to employment opportunities and
increase of transportation choices and connections.

Midvale has three stations within its boundaries: (1) Bingham Junction, (2) Midvale Fort
Union, and (3) Midvale Center. Staff applied for and received Technical Assistance grant
funding for all three stations and their requisite SAP's. A joint application was submitted
with West Jordan for the Bingham Junction and Historic Gardner (West Jordan) stations.

As such, Midvale is among the first cities to undertake the station area planning as
required by HB 462.

The resolution for consideration involves the Bingham Junction SAP. While the City has
no financial obligation in the study, staff time will be involved. The SAP will ultimately be
approved by the City Council prior to certification by the State.

       A.     DISCUSSUNIFIEDPOLICEDEPARTMENTINTERLOCALAGREEMENT
              AMENDMENT
Matt Dahl said the Unified Police Department (UPD) was established through the
adoption of the Cooperative Agreement by Public Agencies To Create a New Entity, to
Consolidate Law Enforcement Service, and Provide Rules of Governance (lnterlocal
Agreement) by its founding members.

Midvale City became a member of UPD in 2011 and agreed to adhere to the terms and
conditions of the lnterlocal Agreement. The lnterlocal Agreement has been amended
eight times by the UPD Board. There are differing opinions as to whether each
Amendment needs to be further ratified by each member community.

ln August 2022, Midvale delivered a letter to UPD requesting four reforms that sought to

                                              7
Proceedings of City Council Meeting

strengthen cost controls through changes in the budget process, establish a fund balance
policy, review the performance of the organization, and establish a local branding
emphasis. UPD has worked with Midvale and other member communities to address the
proposed reforms. On November 17, 2022, the UPD Board approved the Ninth
Amendment to the lnterlocal Agreement as part of the effort to strengthen cost controls
through changes in UPD's budget process. The Ninth Amendment to the lnterlocal
Agreement includes the following:

1      The UPD administration must submit a preliminary budget increase maximum
       (PBIM) for the upcoming fiscal year to the UPD Board by March.

2      lf UPD proposes an annual budget or budget amendment that exceeds the PBIM
       it will require a minimum 75% weighted vote of the UPD Board to be approved.

Staff feels that this change to the lnterlocal Agreement will:

1      lncrease certainty and support strategic planning in each UPD member
       community's budget process, because large unanticipated increases are less likely
       to occur late in the budget process.

2      lncrease cost controls through improved processes and higher thresholds for
       approval. The member communities of UPD have been requested to approve the
       Ninth Amendment to the lnterlocal Agreement. Staff will be prepared to discuss
       the Amendment during the meeting.

Absent objections by the City Council, the intent is to return on January 3,2023, lo
consider and take action on the Amendment.
