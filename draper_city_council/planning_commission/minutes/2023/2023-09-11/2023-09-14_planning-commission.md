       B.     Public Hearing: Willow Aesthetics and Lash Artistry Home Occupation
              Conditional Use Permit. (Administrative Action).
              On the Request of Brittney Fairbanks Representing The Fairbanks Family Trust for a
              Home Occupation Conditional Use Permit to Conduct a Personal Care Service
              Business from the Home. The Property is located at approximately 1027 East Lone
              Peak Lane in the RA1 (Single-Family Residential 40,000 square-foot minimum area)
              zone. Application USE-070-2023. Staff contact is Tevai Haunga at 801-576-6522 or
              tevai.haunga@draperutah.gov.

City Planner, Tevai Haunga, presented the Staff Report and stated that the request is for a Home
Occupation Conditional Use Permit for Willow Aesthetics and Lash Artistry. The property is located
off of Lone Peak Lane. The land use designation is Residential Low to Medium Density with a current
zoning of RA1. The applicant is requesting approval of a personal care service business to provide
beauty and aesthetic services in the home. The business is located on the first floor of a detached
structure with business hours of 9:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m. Monday through Saturday. Clients will be
scheduled by appointment only and the applicant will be limited to one client per one to two-hour
session. No public comment was received by staff.

Commissioner Nixon asked if business is conducted from an Accessory Dwelling Unit (“ADU”).
Planning Manager, Todd Draper, reported that nothing was on record showing that the second floor
of the structure is approved as an ADU. The applicant, however, specified in her statement that she
lives on the second floor. The building is a legal detached structure. The matter before the
Commission was to consider only the home-based business. The applicant, Brittany Fairbanks, lives
on the property and the homeowners are her parents. The question of whether there is an ADU on
the property is not germane to whether a home-based business can operate on the property by a
resident who resides on the property.

Ms. Fairbanks reported that she is a Master Esthetician and performs personal care services such as
eyelash extensions, facials, and full body waxing.

       C.      Public Hearing: Draper Box N Lock Site Plan (Administrative Action).
               On the Request of Stephen Tripp, for a Site Plan Request for a New Storage Facility
               on 3.88 Acres of Property located at 15037 South Minuteman Drive. Application
               Number SPR-023-2023. Staff contact is Tevai Haunga at 801-576-6522 or
               tevai.haunga@draperutah.gov.

Ms. Haunga presented the Staff Report and stated that the Site Plan Request is for Draper Box N
Lock. The property is located off of Minuteman Drive. The land use designation is Community
Commercial and the zoning is M1. There are two access points off of Minuteman Drive with one to
the north and one to the south of the property. There are a total of seven parking stalls provided,
which complies with the ordinance. Cross Access is required but not shown on the Site Plan. The
proposed plans do not comply with the ordinance requirements for cross access and are included in
the list of specific conditions set forth in the Staff Report. The proposed landscaped area is 21%,
which meets the required 20% set forth in the ordinance. The proposed perimeter landscaping and

screening also meet the ordinance. The proposed parking lot landscaping meets the 5% requirements
for industrial and warehouse sites.

Elevation drawings were presented and showed Building A, which has a maximum height of 45 feet.
The main building complies with all requirements regarding colors and materials as well as vertical
and horizontal articulation. The storage facility will consist of 10 single-story self-storage buildings
with a four-story climate-controlled storage main building that will house the office. Per the applicant
a total of 574 storage units are proposed on the property. The other single-story storage buildings do
not comply with the colors and materials, or vertical or horizontal articulation requirements. Samples
of the floor plans for the various storage buildings were presented. The requirements for the two
primary materials and three colors listed on each façade have not been met. The proposed photometric
plan also does not comply with the ordinance. Some calculations were missing and the maximum
illumination listed must be reduced to comply with the ordinance. The conditions set forth in the
Staff Report reflect the remaining issues that need to be complied with on revised plans. Ms. Haunga
reported that no public comments were received on the application.

In response to a question raised by Chair Fowler, Ms. Haunga stated that a Cross Access Easement is
required to the north. A stub will be required there. There is not a business on the adjacent property
at this time but the lot is able to be developed. It was noted that the use on the adjacent property may
differ from those allowed on the subject property as it is a Commercially zoned property and the
subject property is zoned for Industrial uses.

The applicant, Stephen Tripp, is a Draper resident and was excited about the project. He reported
that his team manages 4,500 storage units across Utah and Idaho. He was pleased to be building one
in Draper. He indicated that many of the five items listed have been corrected in updated plans he
already submitted. He was unsure how to do a stub as proposed since a plastic surgery center is to be
developed next door and will likely not want traffic from the storage facility using the parking lot.
He noted that the Cross Access Easement was also submitted. In addition, much of the metal that did
not meet the required primary material standards had been switched to masonry and block on the
revised plans he submitted in accordance with the desires of the City.

Mr. Tripp clarified that they never applied for color, material, or horizontal articulation deviations
although it was shown on the application. That option was discussed originally but they decided to
conform to the actual requirements. Instead of metal, they have made a change to all block on the
resubmitted plans. He believes his new plans are in conformance with the ordinance requirements.
Chair Fowler commented that there are three variations consisting of a significant façade articulation,
primary materials, and colors.

Mr. Draper, reported that the applicant asked that the request come before the Planning Commission
after the second review, as is, and that the applicants claims regarding the resubmitted plans for a
third review are not substantiated as that review has not occurred. The Planning Commission is
making a determination and decision only on the plans submitted through the second review as that
is what is reflected in the Staff Report.

Mr. Draper clarified that the ordinance specifies that the Planning Commission can approve
deviations. In reviewing tonight’s request along with the second submittal, it was determined that the
façade of some of the single-story buildings do not meet the required major horizontal articulation on
one side. Mr. Draper could not confirm if that had been corrected in the revised plans as reported by
the applicant, as those plans had not been reviewed. Commissioner Squire inquired if approval of the
deviations was necessary if the applicant has made the changes to comply with ordinance. Mr. Draper
reiterated that the applicant’s claims to have corrected the issues with a third submittal after this item
was placed on the meeting agenda were not verified and should not be considered. He noted that if
the Planning Commission desired, they could grant approval for the deviations, and if they were not
needed based on review of the third submittal they would be moot. He noted that metal is a secondary
exterior material, not a primary exterior material. Some of the facades for the single-story buildings
included a substantial amount of metal, and that they do not meet the 75% requirement for primary
materials on each facade.

Mr. Draper reported that there is also an issue with the single-story buildings having only one primary
material consisting of split-faced Concrete Masonry Units (“CMU”). There are two different colors
of CMU proposed but they are still only one material. The City requires a minimum of two materials
that are primary on each façade and three colors. There are only two colors shown on the CMU
façades. The Planning Commission should determine whether the deviations are warranted. If not
granted, the applicant would need to bring them into compliance as part of future review. Chair
Fowler asked if the item should be continued to have time to review the plans submitted.

Mr. Tripp clarified that he is not applying for a deviation. Mr. Barker suggested that the request be
reviewed at face value and it would then be incumbent upon the applicant to comply with the Code
which is reflected in the conditions. Commissioner Ogden explained that the request can be approved
without deviations and the applicant would be required to comply with City requirements to move
forward with the project as long as the final plans reviewed by staff are fully compliant.

Mr. Tripp indicated that colored renderings were provided to the City. The Site Plan shows that the
diagonal buildings are articulated on the inside and outside. Even though no one will see it, they want
the articulation to go in and out to a certain degree. The applicants considered that to be unreasonable
since no one can see it. Mr. Tripp agreed, to do what is required by the City. He stated that they do
not meet the criteria for, or want the deviations. Mr. Tripp claimed they are landscaping 40% of their
property, because the Utah Department of Transportation (“UDOT”) is maintaining ownership of the
entire front, they will not count the indoor calculation. As a result, they do not qualify to count the
extra landscaping. Mr. Tripp commented that they never completed the Deviation Application
because they were informed that they would not qualify. Mr. Tripp stated that common sense and the
Code do not always coincide. Commissioner Squire commented that is what deviations are for. She
encouraged Mr. Tripp to apply for a deviation if he desires one. Mr. Barker commented that
apparently, the applicant does not need or want a deviation.

Chair Fowler opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing was
closed.
