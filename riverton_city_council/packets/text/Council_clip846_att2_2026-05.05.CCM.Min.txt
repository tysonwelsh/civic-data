36          Wing & Walmart Drone Delivery Presentation
37
38   Representatives from Wing and Walmart presented their drone delivery partnership and proposed
39   operations in Riverton. They explained that the program began in the Dallas–Fort Worth area during
40   the COVID-19 pandemic to provide faster, more reliable deliveries and has since expanded to
41   several major metropolitan areas. The presentation included a video demonstrating how Walmart
42   associates load packages onto drones at a parking lot staging area before the drones deliver orders

10            EV Stations
11
12   Kevin Hicks, City Manager, explained that the proposed EV charging station grant project align with
13   several City strategic priorities and help address Riverton’s lack of public charging infrastructure
14   compared to surrounding cities. The proposed project would use approximately $385,000 in federal
15   grant funding with an estimated City contribution of about 8%, or roughly $10,000 to $11,000 if only
16   two stations are installed. He also shared that feedback from other cities with public charging
17   stations has been overwhelmingly positive from both residents and officials.
18
19   Councilmembers discussed differing perspectives regarding the proposed EV charging stations,
20   with several expressing concern about the proper role of government in providing public charging
21   infrastructure and whether taxpayer funds should be used for amenities that could be supplied by
22   private businesses. Staff clarified that the project would go through a standard public procurement
23   process managed by UDOT under federal grant requirements and noted that the City’s actual
24   financial contribution would remain relatively small due to the grant funding. Other Councilmembers
25   acknowledged the concerns but noted the stations could support future City fleet needs, maintain
26   positive regional partnerships, and address Riverton’s lack of publicly owned charging infrastructure
27   compared to surrounding cities.

17   Mayor Buroker called for public comments;
18
19   Jennifer Preece, speaking on behalf of numerous neighbors, stated that 3200 West has become a
20   speeding corridor where drivers frequently travel 40 to 50 miles per hour on a residential street
21   posted at 25 miles per hour, with many motorists also ignoring a nearby stop sign. She questioned
22   the City’s opposition to speed bumps, arguing that traffic calming devices appear effective on other
23   neighborhood streets. She urged the Council to take advantage of planned repaving work on 3200
24   West to incorporate permanent traffic calming measures before a more serious accident occurs.
25
26   Natalie Palmer raised concerns about persistent speeding and reckless driving on 3200 West,
27   stating that the road has become unsafe for families, pedestrians, bicyclists, and children attending
28   nearby church activities. She urged the City to implement permanent traffic calming measures.
29
30   Amy Nicholson described an incident in which an unlicensed door-to-door salesman entered her
31   fenced yard despite warning signs and was confronted by her dogs, leading to police involvement
32   and a lawsuit against her by the City. She expressed frustration that, despite following prior police
33   recommendations regarding signage and containment, she faced legal expenses and stress before
34   the case was ultimately dismissed, and urged the City to strengthen protections and enforcement
35   related to door-to-door solicitors and resident liability.
36
37   Dave Hogge praised City staff and officers for their investigation but raised concerns about
38   breakdowns in communication between the City, legal counsel, and prosecutors that led to
39   confusion and delays in a door-to-door solicitor case involving his daughter. He argued that
40   enforcement of City codes and protections for residents should be clearer and more consistent.
41
42   Dustin Palmer described ongoing safety concerns on 3200 West, stating that its long, uninterrupted
43   stretch has led drivers to treat it like a drag strip, with frequent speeding and stop sign violations. He
44   noted that police enforcement has only provided temporary relief and urged the City to implement
45   permanent traffic calming measures.

47   Summer Joos expressed opposition to ICE involvement in Riverton, arguing that the agency
48   disproportionately targets non-citizens and people of color and raised concerns about broader
