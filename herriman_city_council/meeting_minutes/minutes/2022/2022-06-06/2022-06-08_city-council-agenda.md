    2.1. Herriman Preserve Development Discussion - Blake Thomas, Community Development
         Director
Community Development Director Blake Thomas presented proposal for the Herriman Preserve and
oriented the council to the location of the project which encompassed 970 acres. He oriented the
Council to the location of the project. The developer proposed a development which would include a
total of 1,250 residential units. The current zoning for the property is FR-2.5 which allowed for a
maximum density of 0.4 dwelling units per acre. Much of the property was located within the Hillside
Overlay Zone which held additional restrictions on development for the purpose of protecting the
natural, scenic environment of the southern part of the City.

The developer proposed to dedicate large amounts of open space as part of the development to be set
aside for recreational use and open space preservation. The areas where housing would be developed
could be accessed by two roads where some potential challenges encompassed road grades meeting
engineering standards and providing utility services such as water and sewer.

Director Thomas presented the draft Master Development Agreement (MDA) including a sample lot
layout and indicated the developer was looking for direction regarding the application.

Councilmember Steven Shields liked the simple layout on the Laguna property; however, was torn with
the rest of the development. He relayed his support of the design element concept, preservation, and
the high-end luxury mountain retreat environment. He felt the density felt too high and questioned if

it would be irresponsible to build so many residential homes in an area where there would be a delay
in municipal services. Councilmember Shields reiterated his support for the concept; however, was
not in agreement with the density being proposed. Councilmember Hodges agreed the development
would be tricky and recognized the need to realign the Army Compatible Use Buffer. He relayed
concern about having such an intense use adjacent to the military base. He recognized the cost
challenges of the development and indicated there were amazing aspects in the proposal.

Councilmember Henderson asked if there was a benefit to the community to enter into a master
development agreement. He observed the substantial increase in costs for police, fire, water, and snow
plowing services and suggested those could be paid through a special assessment area. Public Works
Director Justun Edwards relayed a conversation with Draper City officials relating to the higher
snowplowing costs and notated a district had been created which ultimately led to a referendum. City
Manager Cherpeski added the district residents would run operations. Councilmember Henderson
opined if operations worked in this manner, more density would make sense.

Councilmember Hodges asked what the response times for fire and police services would entail. Police
Chief Troy Carr responded it wouldn’t matter the level of density as the proposed development was
disassociated geographically, the limited options would become costly and the only way to get a
quicker response time would be to keep an officer in the area. Police Chief Carr indicated Draper City
had a substantial response time for Suncrest and highlighted they do not keep a vehicle in the area.

Councilmember Henderson relayed concern regarding the support of Camp Williams and the cost of
services. Mayor Palmer asked the Council if there was interest in pursing a master development
agreement. Legal Representative Bruce Baird asked if there would be more of an appetite in pursing a
development agreement if there was a mechanism incorporating reasonable assurances the increased
cost for services would not be borne by the residents at large. Councilmember Henderson agreed.
Councilmember Hodges indicated his concerns surrounded the density, cost, and Camp Williams
buffer. Legal Representative Baird realized the need for future discussions surrounding the density.
Councilmembers Shields and Henderson agreed it needed to be addressed.

    2.3. Vantage Point Open Space Encroachment Discussion - Blake Thomas, Community
         Development Director
Director Thomas presented private landscaping improvements of two existing homes on Vantage Point
Circle which expanded into open space in Rosecrest Plat U. the encroachments included a rock
retaining wall, concrete pad, concrete curbing, in-ground trampoline, sod, trees, and an irrigation
system. One of the homeowners applied for a building permit for an accessory structure which
required an exception on setbacks and building height. This request required approval of a conditional
use permit from the Planning Commission. The Planning Commission expressed concerns when
reviewing the permit and continued the item indefinitely until the rock wall encroachment was
removed and a drainage plan was provided by the homeowner to ensure remediation of the retaining
wall would not impact the placement of the proposed detached building. Due to the requirement from
the Planning Commission, staff looked to Council for direction on how to proceed with the remediation.

Councilmember Hodges opined the wall should be removed and the natural slope restored.
Councilmember Shields shared a philosophy and wondered if remediation would create new drainage
issues. He was reluctant to leave the wall sections over four-feet tall in place without proper
engineering. He thought it would be sufficient to allow the wall to remain and import fill to restore the
natural slope and require the area to be revegetated with a native seed mix. Councilmember Ohrn
stated the wall needed to meet code requirements. The Council agreed.

   2.4. Herriman Main Street Project Scope Discussion - Bryce Terry, Assistant City Engineer
Assistant City Engineer Bryce Terry indicated staff had been working toward design on the Herriman
Main Street project and highlighted the bill passed through the Legislature which granted Herriman
$800,000 for 15 years to mitigate congestion and improve transportation safety.

Assistant City Engineer Terry recalled a previous discussion in which Council directed staff to utilize
the funds on the Herriman Main Street widening project. Staff worked to procure a design consultant
for the project. The cost estimate received was significantly over the allocated funds for the project.
He indicated alternatives were vetted to reduce the cost of the project and presented options for the
Council consideration.
    • Option 1 would be to reduce the width of the roadway from a major collector width to a minor
       collector width of 68-feet. The intersections could widen out with right turn pockets to the full
June 8, 2022 – City Council Minutes                                               Page 5 of 11
       standard major collector width of 80-feet. The power lines would not be able to be relocated
       underground, instead would stay aerial. The park strips would be filled with stamped concrete
       and tree wells.
   •   Option 2 would keep the same cross-section as the original proposal; however, would remove
       landscaping of the park strips and power relocation from the project. This option would allow
       the individual property owners to plant and maintain the park strips which were part of their
       property frontage. This option would require shortening the length of the project. It was
       estimated the project would be completed from 6250 West to Dansie Boulevard or from 7300
       West to 6400 West depending on were the project commenced. An alternative side treatment
       option would be to install a six-foot sidewalk or asphalt path adjacent to the curb and a four-
       foot easement on the back of the walk for street signs, streetlights, fire hydrants, and water
       meters. The side treatment could allow for a small amount of the extension of the project.
   •   Option 3 would maintain all improvements as the original proposal. This would include the
       widening the roadway, installing landscaping in all the park strips, relocating power
       underground, and sidewalk. If all improvements are installed, it was estimated the project could
       be completed from 6250 West to Twisted Oak Drive or from 7300 West to 6600 West
       depending on where the project commenced.

Assistant City Engineer Terry indicated the project could go further if it started at the most westward
point and move east as it would incorporate developer required improvements. Councilmembers
Henderson and Ohrn relayed opposition to reducing the right-of-way to 68-feet as they felt it would
be short sighted. The Council discussed options available to complete the project. Council consensus
determined to move forward with option 1 and look to acquire more right-of-way in the future.

   6.6. Discussion and consideration of an ordinance to adopt the final budget for the fiscal year
        beginning July 1, 2022, and ending June 30, 2024 – Alan Rae, Finance Director
Finance Director Alan Rae informed the Council the tax rate had not yet be set by the Utah State Tax
Commission and recommended approval of the rate based on what they issue. He indicated a separate
ordinance may be required at a future date to set the rate.

Director Rae indicated the budgets consisted of separate entities including the Community
development and Renewal Agency of Herriman City, the Herriman City Safety Enforcement Service
Area and Herriman City. He reminded the Council the Herriman City Fire Service Area budget would
go through the Truth in Taxation process in August and would not be adopted at the current time. The
proposed budget also included the master fee schedule and Director Rae reported most items have
been maintained. The fees have been going through an evaluation process and have been compared to
other jurisdictions. Adjustments had been made as necessary including the water fee increase of two
percent based on the study conducted and approved by the Council.

Councilmember Henderson moved to approve Ordinance No 2022-28 adopting a final budget; making
appropriations for the support of Herriman City for the fiscal year beginning July 1, 2022, and ending
June 30, 2024; revising the 2021-2022 fiscal year budget; approving the Master Fee schedule and
accepting the proposed rate of tax as determined by the Utah State Tax Commission. Councilmember
Hodges seconded the motion.
