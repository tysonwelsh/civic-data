Proposed Resolution 2023-17 amending Redevelopment Agency Bylaws to
reflect state law
         A memo from Board staff came before the Board to consider a resolution amending the Redevelopment Agency (RDA)
bylaws. The memo stated the proposed changes include minor corrections to Code references, a clarification of the duties of the
Board Executive Director, and corrections of typographical errors.
         Chair Choberka introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2023-17, entitled:
                  “A resolution of the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency Board amending the bylaws of the Ogden City
                  Redevelopment Agency.”
         BOARD MEMBER LOPEZ MOVED THE RESOLUTION BE ADOPTED, AND THE CHAIR BE
AUTHORIZED TO SIGN THE SAME. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY VICE CHAIR RICHEY, WITH THE
FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – BOARD MEMBERS BLAIR, HYER, LOPEZ, RICHEY, WHITE,
VICE CHAIR RICHEY, AND CHAIR CHOBERKA. VOTING NO – BOARD MEMBER NADOLSKI.

Proposed Resolution 2023-18 amending Joint Resolution 2013-1 relating to
the acquisition and conveyance of RDA real property
         A memo from Board staff came before the Board to consider a joint resolution amending the Agency’s policy relating to
the acquisition and conveyance of RDA real property. The memo stated Board staff are recommending minor changes to the
current joint resolution governing the acquisition and conveyance of RDA property. Changes include the following:
               • Including legislative history in the recitals.
              •   Clarifying that the Joint Resolution is establishing a consensus formal policy.
              •   Increasing the dollar amount the administration may expend to purchase an option on a property without Board
                  approval from $20,000 to $30,000.
              • Adding a statement that property purchased with Quality Neighborhood Funds must comply with the City’s
                  Infill Housing Guidelines.
         Chair Choberka introduced in writing proposed Joint Resolution 2023-18, entitled:
                  “A Joint Resolution of the Executive Director and the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency Board approving a
                  complete restatement of the Agency’s policies and procedures for the acquisition and conveyance of real
                  property interests.”
         BOARD MEMBER BLAIR MOVED THE RESOLUTION BE ADOPTED, AND THE CHAIR BE
AUTHORIZED TO SIGN THE SAME. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY VICE CHAIR RICHEY, WITH THE
FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – BOARD MEMBERS BLAIR, HYER, LOPEZ, RICHEY, WHITE,
VICE CHAIR RICHEY, AND CHAIR CHOBERKA. VOTING NO – BOARD MEMBER NADOLSKI.
         Board member Nadolski indicated he voted in opposition due to his concern about the lack of a requirement for a formal
appraisal for property being disposed of. He stated this was brought to his attention during tonight’s City Council meeting during
the public comment period. He added another thing that he is concerned about is the language regarding short-term leasing and
this caused him to reflect on the viability of long-term leasing of RDA properties.

Proposed Resolution 2023-16 amending the budget for the Fiscal Year
2024 by increasing the anticipated revenues and appropriations for a gross
increase of $3,638,900, recognizing bond proceeds for improvements to the
Junction parking structures, recognizing proceeds from the sale of property
within the Trackline development and recognizing revenue from the sale of
property associated with the Capital Square project
          A memo from the City Comptroller came before the Board to consider a resolution amending the Fiscal Year (FY) 2023-
2024 budget amendment. The memo stated Administration is proposing several amendments including recognizing bond
proceeds for improvements to the Junction parking structures in the amount of $2,015,400, recognizing proceeds from the sale of
property within the Trackline development in the amount of $865,725, and recognizing revenue from the sale of property
associated with the Capital Square project in the amount of $757,775. The total proposed increase to the RDA budget is
$3,638,900.
          Comptroller Stout summarized the memo and stated in May 2023, the Council approved bond parameters for a sales tax
bond for the Marshall White Community Center and Junction parking structure repairs. The bonds were issued in June 2023 and
final issuance included $2,000,000 for the Junction parking structures. The proceeds are proposed to be included in the Fiscal
Year 2024 RDA budget in the amount of $2,015,400. The funds will be included in the CBD Mall Redevelopment Area. Relative
to the Trackline EDA Lot 11 sale, the proceeds are to be used for landscaping, site signage and landscape furniture in the Ogden
Business Exchange development. Lot 11 is located on the north side of Stockman Way north of the Enable Utah building. The
appropriation for the sale of Lot 11 is $865,725 and will be included in the Trackline EDA district budget. She concluded by
summarizing the Capital Square property sale; as outlined in the Land Transfer and Development Agreement between the RDA
and JF Q25, the sale of property in the amount of $757,775 is proposed to be recognized in the RDA Housing Fund. The RDA
assembled property for the Capital Square development using a loan from DL Evans Bank to finance the purchase of certain
parcels. The proceeds from the sale will be applied toward the balance of the DL Evans loan. After the sale proceeds are applied to
the loan, a balance of $750,000 will remain outstanding with the balance due in November 2024.
          Chair Choberka called for a public hearing on proposed Resolution 2023-16, entitled:
                   “A resolution of the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency amending the budget for the Fiscal Year July 1, 2023
                   to June 30, 2024 by increasing the anticipated revenues for a gross increase of $3,638,900 from sources as
                   detailed in the body of this resolution; and increasing the appropriations for a gross increase of $3,638,900 as
                   detailed in the body of this resolution.”
          The Deputy City Recorder presented written notice of said hearing and affidavit of posting on the 3rd day of August
2023, specifying the time and place of this meeting as the time and place when and where the proposed resolution would be given
a public hearing and be considered for final passage.
          Travis Pate voiced his support for the Junction parking structure repairs and noted he hopes it will maintain access to free
parking at the Junction; the Trackline property sale is also warranted, and he spoke to the manner in which the Trackline area has
been improved. He concluded by addressing the Capital Square transaction, noting he does not think there should ever be a loss
for taxpayers associated with a redevelopment project; the City will be taking a loss by giving out so many incentives with nothing
in return. This action may also concentrate poverty in one area.
          Teresa Bramwell stated that she spoke to the developers of the Capital Square project, and they told her that the project
they are building is intended to be for rent to low-income residents. She informed the developer that they are redeveloping the old
Wheelwright Lumber property, and the intent was to include commercial use on the main level. She asked what the commercial
uses would be and was told that one side would contain a leasing office for the building and the other side would contain a gym
for the residents and perhaps a coffee shop. She stated that she fails to see how anything the developer is building is going to raise
the neighborhood or help address the extreme poverty that exists in the neighborhood. The East Central community has a 31
percent owner occupancy rate, which she is certain is the lowest in the State of Utah. The owner-occupancy rate for all of Weber
County is 72 percent and for the State it is 70 percent. She feels this new project will only make things worse in East Central; she
is not sure how to improve the neighborhood but noted that is the job of the City’s elected officials, not hers. However, she is
trying to help. She assumes the developer of the property paid 60 percent of what the property is worth to purchase it.
          Angel Castillo stated she has concerns about how the City pays developers to build projects in the community; she does
not feel that the City should be paying developers to do things, especially when the RDA already owns the land. The land has been
acquired using resident tax dollars and there are plenty of other developers interested in building in Ogden. She noted she feels
that the practices that have been employed on the Capital Square project are an example of bad business; tax increment financing
(TIF) is only supposed to be used for infrastructure, not when a project does not pencil for a developer, and they are asking the
City to make up the difference. She understands the difficulty with the Capital Square property, but she encouraged the Board to
consider how they are being accountable for the money that belongs to the taxpayer. She agreed with Ms. Bramwell’s assessment
that there are too many rental properties in the East Central neighborhood; she has nothing against renters, and she feels there is a
great need for rental properties, but there is a huge gap in what is available for sale and perhaps the Board could consider projects
that include a requirement for a certain number of units to be for-purchase. She asked the Board to consider how they are using
TIF and paying developers to build a project that they will sell for a profit.
          Heath Satow thanked Community and Economic Development Director Cooper for his comments in the City Council
meeting regarding the Brown Ice Cream building; his primary concern is that the building has sat for such a long period of time
and its condition is worsening. He then stated that he also wants clarification on the final clean-up costs for the Capital Square
project.
          There being no further persons appearing to be heard, BOARD MEMBER HYER MOVED THE PUBLIC
HEARING BE CLOSED. THE MOTION WAS SECONDED BY VICE CHAIR RICHEY, ALL VOTING AYE.
          Board member White stated the Capital Square project is a very large parcel of land and there will be a mix of for sale
product, rentals, low-income units, and market rate apartments. The reason for doing that is to help address the density of poverty.
If the Agency did not own the land, the City would not have any control over those factors, and she feels this is the start of a very
good project. She noted that this property is also located along a public transit line, which is the most appropriate place for this
type of product. Chair Choberka added that the plans also include a grocery store and other retail uses, as well as a good amount
of green space and a playground. She added that people that live in apartment complexes should not be demonized for their choice
of housing option.
          Board member Nadolski addressed Mr. Cooper regarding the Trackline project; the staff transmittal indicates the
proceeds of the sale of Lot 11 will be used for landscaping, site signage, and landscape furniture in the Ogden Business Exchange
development. He asked how that will be administered. Mr. Cooper stated the reason that transaction is taking place is due to the
original Land Transfer and Development Agreement (LTDA) that was initially approved for the project; there were certain things
the developer was obligated to do at the project area, but they have not been able to fulfill those duties. Negotiation of the sale of
Lot 11 includes assigning a portion of the sales proceeds to the Agency to be used to satisfy the requirements of the LTDA. The
developer already had a contractor in place to complete the required improvements and the Agency will continue to work with that
contractor under an adjusted scope of work. Board member Nadolski asked if the entire $865,725 will be spent on site
improvements. Mr. Cooper answered yes; he stated there is a great deal of common space in the Trackline project and the
improvements will be very costly. If the entire amount is not used for that purpose, the surplus will be given back to the developer.
          Mr. Cooper then addressed the clean up costs of the Capital Square property; he clarified that TIF is not being used at this
site and the developers have paid to purchase the land in order to proceed with the development. He does not have the final
remediation costs of the site but can report that at a later date.
          BOARD MEMBER HYER MOVED THE RESOLUTION BE ADOPTED, AND THE CHAIR BE
AUTHORIZED TO SIGN THE SAME. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY BOARD MEMBER BLAIR, WITH THE
FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – BOARD MEMBERS BLAIR, HYER, LOPEZ, WHITE, VICE
CHAIR RICHEY, AND CHAIR CHOBERKA. VOTING NO – BOARD MEMBER NADOLSKI.
          Board member Nadolski stated he supports the success of these projects, but he feels he needs more information to have a
clear understanding of the Capital Square transaction.

Executive Director Comments
        In Executive Director Caldwell’s absence, Chief Administrative Officer Brown indicated there are no additional
comments from Administration.

Board member Comments
         Board member Hyer stated he is very sympathetic to the comments about the need for the City to provide additional ways
to achieve home ownership; home ownership is something that is seriously needed in the community, but there is also a need for
rental properties in the City. He does not want to vilify renters, but he also does not want to vilify those people who own rental
properties.
         Board member Nadolski apologized for changing his vote on a few items at the last minute and for asking so many
questions about the budget amendments; he is not being critical of the proposals or the associated projects, but he feels he needed
more information.
