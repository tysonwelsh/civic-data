VICTIMS ADVOCATE PROGRAM ANNUAL REPORT-RYAN PETERS AND AMY TAYLOR:

    Juab County Attorney, Ryan Peters, and Victims Advocate, Amy Taylor reported to the council on the
work of the victim’s advocate program operated in our area. They presented a summary sheet of the
past two years case numbers for domestic violence, sex abuse, and other crimes and where the cases
originated in the county. Mr. Peters stated that Ms. Taylor follows each case from beginning to end and
is always on call. The two requested continued support from the City. The City has contributed $12,000
annually in the past. The federal grant that supplies most of the funding for the program has been cut
by 60%. Mayor Seely commented that the victims advocate program is very powerful and helpful and
asked what services had been cut due to the cut in federal funding. Ms. Taylor answered that the
emergency contingency, training, and relief-help wages had been cut. They indicated that they have
lobbied our congressional representatives to continue to fund the program. The council agreed to
continue supporting the victim’s advocacy program.

ORDINANCE 02-07-2023-A- REGULATION FOR RV PARKS ADOPTED:

    Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council the history of the RV Park long-term stay dilemma. He stated
that The staff and Council have received complaints over the last several years regarding certain
activities and behaviors at RV Parks that draw police presence and have created safety hazards. To date,
the Council has used a provision in the code that requires users to stay for 30 days or less in an RV Park
to govern and regulate this undesirable behavior. The Council elected to charge RV Parks in the city with
a violation of this section of the city code. During the litigation process, the City Attorney has been
meeting with the RV Park owners to address the concerns from Nephi City residents. Through those
negotiations, a new ordinance governing RV Parks has been drafted that more directly addresses the
concerns emanating from RV Parks.
   The ordinance sets forth the requirements for new RV Parks or RV Parks that may be changing their
size. This includes minimum acreage, hard-surface requirements, recreational space, density, yard
clearances, and amenities. The ordinance reiterates the stipulation for a 30-day rental contract but
allows for an unlimited number of 30 day contracts. However, the contracts must include a provision
that states:
“ that a recreational vehicle is required to leave the RV park if the owners or occupants of the
recreational vehicle are unreasonably disturbing, are engaging in any criminal activity, or fail to comply
with the terms of City Code.”
   In addition, the city will have the ability to revoke a business license from an RV Park with 3 criminal
convictions within a 12-month period. Finally, the ordinance requires each RV Park to schedule 2
inspections per year with the city, with the cost of those inspections being on the business license for
each RV Park.
   The Planning Commission had reviewed this ordinance and recommended it to the Council. There
have been minor changes by the Council and RV Park owners since this recommendation, but the major
substance of the ordinance had not changed.
   Councilor Worwood suggested that when an inspections is completed that the RV Park owner receives
a receipt of inspections along with a receipt being given to Lisa Brough. A discussion ensued about
changing the laundry facility requirement from 1 clothes washer and 1 clothes dryer per 10 RV spaces to
1 per 20 spaces. Kalli Bowers preferred it be changed to 1 to 20. The council agreed to the change. Ms.
Bowers also requested that she be notified when the police are called out to her RV Park. She stated
that it is hard to comply with the code if she isn’t aware of the police activity. It was recommended that
the Code Enforcement Officer Andrea Holm notify the park owners at time of inspection of any police
activity at their parks.
    Councilor Memmott moved to adopt Ordinance 02-07-2023-A: AN ORDINANCE AMENIDNG SECTION
10.15.5 OF THE NEPHI CITY CODE PROVIDING FOR THE REGULATIONS OF RECREATIONAL VEHICLES NAD
RECREATION VEHICLE PARKS WITHIN NEPHI CITY with the change of 1 clothes washer and 1 clothes
dryer per 20 RV Park spaces. Councilor Worwood seconded the motion. The motion carried on a
unanimous vote.

prosecutorial matters. Mr. Atkinson said that we should give it some time to see if our citation numbers

we could negotiate with the county the cost of the clerk and other court costs.
