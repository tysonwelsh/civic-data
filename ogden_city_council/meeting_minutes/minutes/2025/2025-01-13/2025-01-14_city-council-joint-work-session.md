Proposed Rezone – 397-399 Harrisville Road
          Planning Director Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss a proposed rezone of properties at 397-
399 Harrisville Road. MKIB Real Estate, LLC has submitted a petition to rezone 4.9 acres from R-2 to R-2S in hopes of
establishing zoning on the property to allow for a townhome project (between 60 and 80 units) that will be platted for individual
ownership. The subject properties are three parcels located near 400 North and Harrisville Road. There are older single-family
dwellings on two of the parcels, including a home built in 1948 at 399 Harrisville Road, and a home built in 1930 at 397
Harrisville Road. The third parcel is a vacant remnant of the Buddhist church property to the west. The current zoning of the
subject properties is R-2. The R-2 zone is considered a moderate density zone, with allowances for single-family (6,000 square
foot lot size) and two-family dwellings (12,000 square foot lot size). The R-2 properties adjacent to the proposed rezone are the
Ogden Buddhist Church and some single-family properties to the south-east. Other zones abutting the subject properties are R-3
(Multiple-family residential) and C-2/CP-2 (Community Commercial). The property to the south is zoned R-3 and consists of two
multifamily projects known as Bramblewood Apartments and Parkwood Estates. The property across Harrisville Road to the East
is C-2/CP-2 which includes America First Credit Union and Pinebrook Apartments. The intent of the proposed R-2S zone is to
provide moderate density residential development with an emphasis on homeownership of individual dwelling units. The R-2S
zone allows single-family rowhouses with a minimum of 2,500 square feet per dwelling unit. The developer anticipates that the
project will include between 60 and 80 townhome units. The subject property is part of the Lynn Community Plan where single-
family residential is the main land use. The Planning Division transmittal explains that the proposed rezone meets the General
Plan zone change policies and the Lynn Community Plan policies. The Plan suggests that the Planning Commission and City
Council should consider measures to ensure that future development is in harmony with historic sites and features of the area. The
petitioner’s narrative explains that they plan to glean from existing buildings and architecture in the area to design an attractive
community. The Administration has not recommended any historic preservation actions related to this zone change. On November
6, 2024, the Planning Commission considered the proposal, heard public comment, and recommended that the Council approve
the proposal with a condition that a parcel boundary adjustment be completed with the parcel to the west, so the property lines
match the zoning boundary. For a copy of Mr. Brierley’s presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Discussion between Mr. Brierley and the Council centered on the potential density of the project; the number of projects
throughout the City that have been assigned the same zoning as requested by the petitioner; and the process the petitioner must
follow to apply to the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) for access to the property from Harrisville Road.

Proposed Rezone – 2869 Grant Avenue
           Planning Director Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the proposed rezone of property at 2869
Grant Avenue. Race Ostler has submitted a petition to rezone 0.24 acres from CP-3 (Regional Commercial) to R-4 (Multiple-
family Residential), the intent of which is to establish zoning on the property to allow a single-family home or multiple-family
units on the site. The subject property is a vacant parcel located at 2869 Grant Avenue and is zoned CP-3. There are two
commercial developments to the north that are zoned CP-3. There are several single-family properties to the south and west, with
the majority of the block being zoned R-4. The CP-3 zone is typically established on larger parcels of property with access to
major roads. The CP-3 zoning in this area has been established primarily along Washington Boulevard from 28th Street to 36th
Street. In November of 2024 the Council approved a text amendment that prohibited new residential dwelling units in the C-2/CP-
2 and C-3/CP-3 zones. The R-4 zone is a multiple-family residential zone that allows for single-family, two-family, and multiple-
family dwellings. The R-4 zoning designation on the subject property would allow for four condominium units or single-family
row houses, or one-single family dwelling. The subject property is part of the Jefferson Community plan. The land-use map from
the Jefferson Community plan indicates the R-4 zoning is a desirable option for the subject property. The Planning Division
transmittal explains that rezoning the parcel to R-4/CO is consistent with several of the General Plan vision strategies for this area,
including the strategy to integrate new multiple-family and single-family development into the area. On November 6, 2024, the
Planning Commission considered the request and recommended that the Council approve the proposal to rezone to R-4/CO. There
were no public comments about the proposal during the Planning Commission meeting. For a copy of Mr. Brierley’s presentation
in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
           Brief discussion among the Council and Mr. Brierley centered on access to the subject property.

Proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget and Staffing Document
Amendments – Two Additional Positions in Mayor’s Office
         Mayor Nadolski has asked that the Council consider a proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 Budget Amendment that
would fund two new executive level positions in the Mayor’s Office and amend the staffing document to include the additional
positions. Both positions are proposed to be funded at the Assistant Executive Department Director salary range AED190 with a
salary range of $115,282 to $167,159. The proposal is budget neutral for FY25. Funds from other general fund line items
budgeted in various non-departmental and departmental accounts would be transferred to increase the wages line-item in the
Mayor’s budget by a total of $241,400. The Staffing Document would be amended to include two additional positions in the
Mayor’s office.
         Mayor Nadolski and Chief Administrative Officer (CAO) Brown used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide
additional detail and duties to be assigned to the two new positions as follows:
              • Deputy Chief Administrative Officer:
                       o Assist the CAO in developing tactics, policies and initiatives and in administering policies procedures,
                            programs, goals, and objectives.
                       o Represent the city, make presentations, lead and participate in meetings and committees.
                       o Serve as lead on key strategic initiatives, providing general guidance, advice, and administrative
                            policy interpretation to departments.
                       o Assist in preparing work plans, developing and executing special projects, programs, etc., in
                            accordance with city plans, budgets, and policies.
                       o Complete complex research, specialized management studies, and in-depth analyses.
                       o Develop implement, manage and evaluate city programs, policies, guidelines, procedures, and
                            practices.
                       o Coordinate interdepartmental communication and collaboration.
                       o Update the CAO on the status of key issues
              • Chief of Staff
                       o Serve as assistant to the mayor, manage day-to-day administrative activities, and manage the
                            executive assistant.
                       o Work with executive staff and CAO to carry out the mayor’s vision and priorities.
                       o Work with mayor to develop, execute, and manage key community engagement initiatives and
                            projects for both internal and external stakeholders.
                       o Ensure mayor’s office is responsive and engaged with the community.
                       o Organize and attend community events and meetings as a representative of the city.
                       o Plan and manage external relationships for the mayor’s meetings.
                       o Serve as the mayor’s liaison on internal and external meetings, developing partnerships and initiatives
                            as directed.
                       o Initiate, oversee, and complete complex initiatives and projects.
                       o Improve current processes and optimize organization procedures in the mayor’s office.
                       o Draft memos, presentations materials, reports, and other documents
        Mayor Nadolski also provided an explanation of the current operations of the Mayor and CAO’s office and the need for
the new positions in order to support the vision and mission of the City, while also increasing efficiencies City-wide.
        The Council engaged in discussion with the Mayor and Ms. Brown regarding specific projects, initiatives, and
community issues that the Mayor’s Office is focused on that require the addition of the two new positions.

         Chair Richey recessed the work session at 5:12 p.m. and reconvened at 5:16 p.m.

         The Council then heard from Finance Director Sorensen regarding the fiscal impact of the proposal to create the two new
positions in the Mayor’s Office; he presented a spreadsheet detailing the source of funds for the proposal and the impact to various
Departmental budgets to support the appropriation. He concluded the current proposal is to hold a public hearing to consider
adopting an ordinance to support the budget amendments and to fund the two new positions for the remainder of the FY25.
         Discussion among the Council and Administration centered on the recruiting process for the two new positions and the
long-term fiscal implications of the budget amendment. Chair Richey indicated the public hearing for this item will be scheduled
for February 4; he invited Council members to provide additional feedback or share any concerns they have regarding the proposal
through Council staff.


         The meeting adjourned at 5:40 p.m.
