Open Space Definition Zone Text Amendment
          Council Deputy Director Symes explained the Ogden City Planning Division is proposing a change to the zoning
ordinance clarifying the term "open space or open green space" specific to site plan requirements. The terms "open space" and
"landscaping" have been used differently at different times, and at times interchangeably, in the zoning ordinance. The proposal
would clarify that open space is to be used as a term for areas outside of a given setback that are planned for or programmed for
use by residents of the property. This would differ from required landscaped areas, which are areas with living plant materials or
features that include living plant materials.
          Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the text amendment proposal.
According to cunent ordinances, only side and rear yards of a property are defmed as open space; yard areas are typically refened
to as setbacks. Open spaces are planned landscape areas for activity or relaxation and he presented a site plan to illustrate the
presence of open spaces in a commercial development. There are several similar terms used in the ordinance, but they have
different intents:
               •   Setbacks-Required minimum distance from property line to structures;
               • Yards-The open area between building and property line (all yards and setbacks shall be landscaped);
               • Landscaping-The placement of plant material in all areas not occupied by building or parking (This is what
                   is placed in the yards created by the setbacks); and
               •   Open (Green) Space-Areas oflandscaping with specific intent for usage.
          Additionally, there are similar words with different meanings; open space and open green space are both zoning
regulations terms for landscaped areas with specific intent. Open space and green space open space zone are categories ofland use
for undeveloped or name of uses ofland that are outdoor related. In certain zones, there is a general definition of what open space
is - a planned area. These zones include Planned Residential Unit Development (PRUD), group dwellings, 12 th Street Overlay
Zone, mixed-use, and Neighborhood Commercial (NCI and NC2) zones. In other zones, the definition of open space needs to be
updated; cunently in these zones, open space is refened to as a percentage ofland allocated to landscaping: R-3, R-3EC, R-4, R-5,
Professional Institutional (PI), and Central Business District (CBD) zones. It is not necessary to change defmitions for the
commercial and mixed-use zones where open space refers to a percent of a site to be landscaped. He summarized the suggested
amendments to the open space/open green space definition as follows: "A planned outdoor landscaped area designed and installed
or retained and enhanced for uses of the residents of the property for gatherings, activities, or visual enjoyment outside the
required zone setbacks." The Planning Commission voted unanimously to recommend approval of amendments to defmitions and
chapters where open space is used to refer to a percentage of landscaping coverage.
          Council member Hyer stated it seems it would be a daunting task to ensure that all references to open space throughout
the City Code will be addressed and the defmition amended with this one ordinance proposal. He asked Mr. Montgomery ifhe is
confident all instances in the Code have been addressed. Mr. Montgomery answered yes.

Airport Tower Demolition
          Fiscal Year (FY) 2021 Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) Amendment: Airport Manager Garrett used a PowerPoint
presentation to review the proposal to consider a CIP amendment to incorporate Airport ARO 12 - Demolish Old Control Tower
Cab on Terminal Building, a new project into the current plan. This is being requested as a result of structural deficiencies and
safety concerns. The original Ogden Airport Air Traffic Control Tower and Terminal Building were constructed about 70 years
ago. In 1973, a replacement FAA Air Traffic Control Tower was built and commissioned, but the original tower remained in
place. The old tower rests on four supporting legs which extend through the terminal building. The full tower is mostly poured
concrete upright member, cross members, and floor decks. It is extremely heavy for a rather small structure. In 2011 and after
many years oflittle to no maintenance of the old tower, a heavy windstorm broke out one of the tower cab's large glass windows.
The glass shards fell 40 feet to the surface of the membrane roof. This section of roof has dozens of patches to repair the damage.
The airport is now in the process of completing a new roof on the terminal building. Plywood was used to cover the broken pane
of glass. However, over the next couple of years the plywood began to sag, and gaps were exposed. Removal of the cab structure
has been discussed over time and it was considered to be an important, but not itmninent, need. For that reason, a CIP Project brief
had not yet been included in the city's capital itnprovement project book submitted to the City Council. In conjunction with the
Airport Master Plan and updates to the Transportation Element of the City's General Plan, the old airport tower was discussed and
language was submitted to the City Council recognizing that the tower and terminal building might be evaluated in the future for
designation as a historic resource. Ultitnately, that language was not adopted as part of the General Plan. On Tuesday morning,
September 8, 2020, another heavy windstonn struck the Ogden area and several buildings at the Ogden Airport were damaged.
Looking at the old tower from the ground, several sheets of plywood were obviously missing and one of the entry doors was
broken and hanging by one hinge. City facilities employees were called to inspect the damage and after entering the tower cab,
they reported that they believed no one should enter the tower because it was sitnply unsafe. Apparently, the windstonn has tom
open the roof of the tower cab and debris was scattered about the cab area and pieces were hanging down, ready to fall. This
itnmediately raised a concern that parts and pieces were likely to fall from a high elevation and damage the brand-new membrane
roof of the Terminal Building that was in the process of being replaced. The airport then invited a structural engineer to conduct
an evaluation of the cab. A fmal rep mt has not yet been received, but based on prelitninary fmdings, there is a significant concern
that while the winter weather would not likely cause the full concrete structure to fail, a moderate seismic event could severely
damage the tower and could cause it to fail. Virtually all of the supporting heavy poured concrete members have stress cracks that
partially or fully compromise their integrity and strength. Because it cannot be detennined when the next seismic event may occur,
removal of the old tower has risen in priority and the structure needs to be demolished at the soonest opportunity. It is estitnated
that the costs of demolition would be approxitnately $250,000, for the contractor doing the demolition. Additional funds will be
needed to prepare and itnplement the demolition plan and protect the new terminal roof. Mr. Garrett then presented several
photographs of the tower in its present condition to illustrate current conditions.
          The Council briefly discussed the proposal and concluded they concurred with Mr. Garrett's proposal to demolish the
tower at this time due to safety concerns. Council Policy Analyst Mabey noted thatthere is an item on tonight's meeting agenda to
set a public hearing for December 15, 2020 to allow for action on the CIP amendment.

2019 Water Conservation Plan
          Public Utilities Manager Herd explained Utah State Code requires each City or entity that provides culinary water to
more than 500 residents to establish a water conservation plan. The plan must be adopted by the legislative body and is to be
updated every five years. Ogden City's water conservation plan was last updated for 2014 and the proposed plan is the five-year
update as required by state law. He provided a brief overview of the proposed2019 update to the plan, which is broken down into
sections addressing the history of Ogden's water system, population growth projections and continual water conservation, internal
conservation measures, external conservation measures, and water conservation goals.
