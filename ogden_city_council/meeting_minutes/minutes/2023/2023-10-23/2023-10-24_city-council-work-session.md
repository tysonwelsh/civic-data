Administrative Update

Chief Administrative Officer Brown provided the Council with reports on the status of various projects in the
community; she invited Financial Analyst Johns to provide an update on the lockbox project. Mr. Johns reported the City has a
lockbox service through Wells Fargo Bank for utility bill collections. The usage of the lockbox has dropped substantially, and the
City will soon be transitioning to a new bank; starting November 1, the City will begin processing the payments at City Hall. It
will take three to six months for the transition to occur and there will be a great deal of communication sent to residents about
changing the address that they return their utility payments to. He will continue to monitor the lockbox for the next several
months to determine that residents have gotten the message to send their payments to the new address. There was discussion
among the Council and staff regarding all utility bill payment options.

Ms. Brown then provided the Council with an update regarding the Jack in the Box restaurant on Harrison Boulevard;
she believes that customers are getting used to the traffic pattern on-site and the closure of the access to the restaurant from
Harrison Boulevard. She also reported on a planned water service outage over the past weekend for valve replacement and
connections for new development in a certain area of the City; the outage did not occur and has been rescheduled to a future date
and staff is mindful of being expedient with the work that must be performed so that residents experience as little downtime as
possible. There was brief discussion of the public noticing process for these types of utility outages. Ms. Brown then reported on
the installation of electric vehicle (EV) charging stations in the Municipal Building parking lot; the project also includes the
installation of water-wise landscaping. She concluded by providing an update on the building at 144 25" Street, which has been
issued a ‘notice of dangerous building’; communications between the City and contractor are at a standstill. The contractor hired a
third-party inspector to inspect the building and that individual agreed with the issues identified by the City. If the contractor is
unable to bring the building into compliance, it may be that the process proceeds to demolition. There was discussion among the
Council and Ms. Brown regarding the City’s ability to monitor the building 24 hours per day, and the timeframe for the contractor
to comply before the City moves to the next step in the Code Enforcement process.

Proposed Zone Text Amendment — Floodplain Overlay Zone Updates

Assistant City Engineer Nielsen explained City Administration is proposing a series of updates to Chapter 26, Floodplain ( |
Overlay Zone, in order to keep Ogden City compliant with the requirements of the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP)
administered by the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA). FEMA will be updating the flood maps for Weber County
after a recent flood hazard determination. The new maps will be effective November 30, 2023 and the changes to the City’s
ordinance must be completed by then for the City to remain compliant. The changes are based on requirements as set forth in Title
44 of the federal code. The changes generally include minor wording and grammatical corrections, adoption of updated Flood
Insurance Rate Maps (FIRM) by reference, several new and updated definitions, the designation of the City Engineer as the
Floodplain Administrator, and updated procedures for amendments to FIRM maps. The City made similar updates to this section
of code in 2015. Mr. Nielsen presented several maps to orient the Council to the old floodplain delineations and the new
delineations that have already been approved by FEMA; Ogden has been proactive in updating its maps as changes have occurred
in the community.

Discussion among the Council and staff centered on the liable party for any flooding that occurs outside the designated
floodplain area. Mr. Nielsen stated homeowners assume that risk regardless of how the area is mapped. Deputy Public Services
Director Anderson added that if a floodplain area touches a private property, the property owner is required to carry flood
insurance. There was also discussion about areas of the City that experienced flooding over the past year due to the spring runoff
following a heavy 2022-2023 winter and whether any areas not included in floodplains actually experienced flooding. Mr.
Anderson stated the City’s maps were fairly accurate and there was not much flooding in areas not identified as floodplains on the
map. .

Council member White asked what the City is doing to help prevent flooding in Marriott-Slaterville given the shared
boundary between that City and Ogden. Mr. Nielsen stated that the City works with Business Depot Ogden (BDO) management to

ensure proper retention/detention infrastructure to handle storm drainage that naturally increases as a result of development.
Proposed Amendments to Snow Removal Parking Ordinance

Public Services Operations Manager Ramos explained the Public Services Department is requesting amendments to
Ogden Municipal Code §10-5-21 to clarify parking limitations during and directly after snow events. The amendments also clarify
the Mayor’s authority to exempt streets that have inadequate off-street parking as exempt from the ordinance. Proposed
amendments to the ordinance implement the following changes: .

¢ Identifies specific dates that the regulations are in effect—_November 1 to March 15.

e Specifies that a vehicle cannot be parked on a street between 2:00 a.m. and 6:00 a.m. when it is snowing or
within 24 hours after a snowstorm. —

e Removes existing paragraph entitled “Obstructing Snow Removal; Impoundment”.

e Provides that the Mayor may exempt streets that have inadequate off-street parking from enforcement of the
ordinance.

If adopted, the proposed amendments would become effective immediately upon posting after passage.

There was general discussion among the Council and Public Services staff regarding the City’s enforcement practices
relating to illegal parking during a snowstorm and the manner in which the City will communicate the changes to residents of the
City before enforcing the new ordinance. Council member Lopez and Chair Choberka expressed concern regarding the manner in
which the ordinance amendments could impact households with more than two drivers that may not have room for off-street
parking for more than two vehicles. Council members Hyer and White stated that many communities have that same situation, but
they have winter parking regulations that are actively enforced in order to provide safe roads for their residents. Council member
Hyer stated he feels that this type of ordinance is overdue in the Ogden community. Council member Blair agreed, but noted that
communication of the changes is important. Council member Lopez stressed that he feels that this ordinance will have a greater
impact on households with lesser means who do not have the ability to park all of their vehicles off the street during a winter
storm; the proposed policy feels authoritative in nature to him. Mr. Ramos stated that citations will not be issued immediately;
violators will be reported to the Ogden Police Department by snowplow drivers, but an officer will not be able to respond
immediately to issue a citation and it may be that the homeowner will have had the opportunity to move their vehicle before an
officer does respond. He stated that all the City can do is do its best in preserving safety for the entire community. Chair Choberka
thanked staff for their dialogue about this issue, but noted that Administration may not always have the perspective of how people
are impacted by these types of ordinances in their daily lives. She wondered if a less restrictive ordinance could be considered to
address the concerns voiced by herself and Council member Lopez.

Vice Chair Richey stated he would like to see the list of streets that may be exempted from the ordinance by the Mayor.
He added that he feels the City can deal with violations of the ordinance in an educational manner rather than a punitive manner.
Public Services Director Lowder stated that is the approach his staff will take as is always the case when a new regulation is
implemented in the City. ( !

Community and Economic Development Director Cooper explained Administration is requesting the extension of the :
Continental Community Reinvestment Area (CRA) to support the bonds for the WonderBlock project. The Continental CRA plan
and budget was approved initially in January 2019 for a period of 22 years expiring in 2041. In December 2021, the Board
approved the two-year extension of seven project areas including the Continental CRA. No project area budgets changed with the
2021 extensions. The current expiration of the Continental CRA is 2043. Participating entities for the Continental CRA include
Ogden City, Ogden School District, and Weber County. To support the bonds issued for the development of the WonderBlock
project, the Administration is proposing an extension of the Continental CRA. The budget for the project area would remain the

proposal is for the RDA Board to approve interlocal agreements between the RDA and the City, Ogden School District and Weber

and the county would be for a period of six years and for the City would be for a period of 15 years. The extension of the
collection period may be revoked, and the original collection period may be reinstated in the event the WonderBlock project is
sold prior to the expiration of the extended tax increment period and the municipal bonds associated with the WonderBlock
project are paid in full. He noted that Weber County and the Ogden School District have already approved the amended
