Robison next reviewed a layout ofthe actual units. The streets will be private and have two points of

Mrs. Robison next reviewed the proposed plat. The owners plan to retain ownership ofthe property

with townhome units. Therefore, the plat shows building pads only, along with the private drive. If

the 20-foot requirement.

Mrs. Robison next reviewed parking. She stated that this element underwent extensive analysis
because some units will be apartments and others will be townhomes. In addition, there can be no
parking on Heritage Crest Way. The current standard is to designate two parking spaces inside the
garage. Where there is a driveway, there is guest parking at the ratio of one stall per four units. If
there is no driveway, there must be one guest parking space per three units. The 1:3 ratio was met
for all units. There are 60 spaces in garages and 29 provided for the typical townhome units. There
are 32 stacked units proposed with one parking space in the garage for each unit and one space for
each of the 20-foot driveways. The apartments do not get credit for guest parking in the driveway,
so the guest parking must meet the 1:3 ratio. The minimum requirement would be 22 spaces,
however,there will be 42 spaces, which exceeds the City's Parking Standard.

Mrs. Robison reviewed the proposed color palate and building materials. She also reviewed the
Landscaping Plan. She identified the location ofa proposed trail, which will be in the area with steep
slopes. Staff was working with the engineers on the location and design ofthe trail.

In response to Commissioner Swanson's question regarding the slope of the southwest portion,
Mrs. Robison confirmed that it is sloped as well. The sloped area will ultimately be open space.

Commissioner Luker commented on the hammerhead portion of the road for a turnaround for

that she would confer with the engineers.

In response to Commissioner Flynn's question regarding the dots on the Landscaping Plan,
Mrs. Robison explained that some ofit is sloped. The applicant, Casey Forbush, gave his address as
4894 West Mussley Bend Drive and stated that the dots will be concrete. The backyards will be
fenced. Mr. Forbush reiterated that the proposed project is the last in the Independence Project.

       Amendments to the Bluffdale Citv Land Use Ordinance. Title 11. Chapter 11. Special
       Purpose and Overlav Zones, to Create a New Zoning Ordinance Chapter and Map

       Located at Approximatelv 15600 South Porter Rockwell Boulevard. Geneva Rock
       Products. Applicants (Application 2022-24). Staff Presenter. Jennifer Robison.

Mrs. Robison reported that this will be a time-consuming item to discuss fully. The new ordinance
proposal was similar to what has been done with the Bringhurst Station SD-X and, most recently,The
Centrum SD-X. SD-X zones allow for more specific design guidelines and other specific elements

                                                8
                        BLUFFDALE CITY PLANNING COMMISSION

that are proposed for a Special District. A Development Agreement has not been deemed necessary
at this time. However,the timing ofconstruction will likely need to be included. Mrs. Robison noted
that the applicants appeared before the City Council at a Work Session in 2021 to present their concept
in preparation for applying for a Special District Zone (SD-X). The item now under consideration
was the actual application for the Special District.

Mrs. Robison explained that a Mixed Used Special District requires a minimum of20 acres. Even
though the agenda item indicates 19.47 acres, the applicant actually has just over 20 acres, which
meets the 20-acre threshold to apply for a Special District. There is some timing ofreclamation that
still needs to take place. She noted that most of the exhibits to be reviewed will be conceptual in
nature as the starting point. The language in the proposed ordinance, however, will provide adequate
information to vet the proposal appropriately.

Mrs. Robison next reviewed the location ofthe subject property. The Geneva Rock property has been
dubbed "the orphan piece" because it is currently a mining area for sand and gravel. It is west of
Porter Rockwell Boulevard where it veers east. In the General Plan,the decision was made to use the
subject property with the same land use pattern of Mixed Use, which occurred at the Independence at
the Point Project. The subject property also provides potential commercial opportunities for the City.
The current zoning of the subject property is Sand and Gravel SG-1; Banner Hill is near the subject
property.

Mrs. Robison next reviewed the original map for Porter Rockwell Boulevard with the possible
locations for lighted intersections. She identified the current locations oflighted intersections. The
City Council felt that the location ofthe subject property made for a sensible place to have commercial
development by making it Mixed Use because it would be next to a seven-lane highway.

Mrs. Robison identified the locations of potential commercial pads on the subject property. The
outskirts of the property will be used for residential development. Mrs. Robison stated that the
proposed ordinance still needs some fine-tuning to include cross-sections and account for possible
changes in uses, layouts, and traffic.

Mrs. Robison reviewed the general vision of the subject property, which will include up to 65,000
square feet ofcommercial use and up to 50 units. The current proposal shows fewer than the indicated
square footage and number of units. The applicants want some flexibility so that they can move
things around within the established parameters. She summarized that the 20 acres planned for
commercial uses ofjust over 12 acres, residential uses ofa little over three acres, and open space and
landscaping of almost six acres.

Mrs. Robison then discussed the uses being considered for the subject property. The Land Use Table
11.110.140.060 Allowed Uses identifies the uses that will be permitted in the SD-X zone to ensure
that they are truly commercial. The proposal also identifies the residential uses that will be allowed.
The land uses also include definitions so that prospective developers know what they can and cannot
do.

Mrs. Robison next reviewed an exhibit showing the open spaces, which will also include landscaping
standards. No public parks will be required but public trails and connections could be included. Yet
to be determined would be the timing of the project and the responsibility for the trail connecting
points. Mrs. Robison stated that the current trend for open spaces is to include a sitting or activity
area with a variety ofamenity options.

Mrs. Robison reviewed the elevations being suggested and the building materials. The applicants
were proposing a theme that differs from brick for their project. There will be more lap siding, wood
elements, and metal. She presented exhibits of what the buildings will look like. She next reviewed
the residential elevations and design proposals, which will be consistent with the proposed lap siding,
metal, and other building materials. The applicants want to have a cohesive design from the
commercial to the residential development.

The Transportation Network was next reviewed. There will be access from Porter Rockwell
Boulevard, Heritage Crest Way, and through Day Ranch. The Circulation Plan calls for two points
of access on Heritage Crest Way. The accesses were widened per the recommendation of the Fire
Chief.

The applicant, Jared Morgan from GWC Capital, gave his address as 1295 West 1650 South in
Riverton. He stated that GWC Capital is a sister company to Geneva Rock and is the land
development group for the Clyde Family. He presented an exhibit to provide more context regarding
the vision ofthe proposed SD-X zone. He explained that GWC Capital specializes in Master Plans.
Specifically, they specialize in horizontal development but they are venturing into vertical
development as well. He noted that his colleague, Kristy Schellenberg, has been working closely
with Mrs. Robison.


Mr. Morgan reviewed the proposed Master Plan and stated that Geneva Rock owns approximately
1,000 acres at the Point ofthe Mountain that is currently being mined. The Mining Plan aligns with
the Reclamation Plan, which informs the Development Plan. Geneva Rock has created a 30-year
picture ofwhat they plan to bring to fruition in Bluffdale City. When the resources in a pit are mined
to depletion,reclamation takes place. Afterward,development can occur. He noted that development
has taken place in Lehi where the mine was depleted and reclaimed. He reported that 80 ofthe 1,000
acres are currently under development. He reviewed a conceptual rendering that was created for
internal use. He emphasized that it is a living document that is subject to change according to the
economy and the needs ofthe community.

Mr, Morgan reported that property reclamation and development will occur from west to east. He
noted that they have more developable land than is being proposed on the old prison site. Therefore,
he opined that this land would be a premiere development opportunity, even better than the prison
site. The north-south corridor will be a very important thoroughfare and has already been captured
in the City's Transportation Master Plan. He felt that he and his colleagues have been very
collaborative with City Staff in the development of the Master Plan for the Geneva Rock property.
There will be an interchange on 16000 South, which is part of UDOT's long-range plan.

Chair Cragun asked for an explanation of how reclamation takes place. Mr. Morgan explained that
GWC Capital is in a unique position as it can build property backup. The pit will be brought back up
to grade to match Porter Rockwell Boulevard. He described how a silt pond is created, which is
composed ofthe by-product ofthe concrete created on the Draper side of1-15. The pit is filled with
that by-product material and compacted so that it is even with the grade at Porter Rockwell Boulevard.
The ability to fill the pit puts them in a competitive position to develop the land. Many third parties
are involved to ensure that the reclaimed area is safe to build on. Mr. Crowell added that the City has
its own Geotechnical Ordinance, which requires that all inspections and evaluations be verified. The
City Engineer verifies the studies that have taken place. As an example of a development that has

10.    PUBLIC HEARING. CONSIDERATION, AND RECOMMENDATION on Proposed
       text Amendments to the Bluffdale City Land Use Ordinances 11.160.180 (c)(2)
       Supplementary Provisions for Shared Driveways to allow "Access to a Maximum of
       Three Lots or Parcels." Olin VanPerWatt, Applicant (Application 2022-46). Staff
       Presenter. Jennifer Robison.


Mrs. Robison presented the staff report and presented the proposed text amendment as follows:

       2.      The shared driveway shall provide access to a maximum oftwo (2) or three (3) lots
               or parcels.

She reported that the ordinance was originally created in 2004. She presented a slide ofthe location
that precipitated the ordinance. It needed to have criteria, including being off of a public street It
also needed physical boundaries or barriers that did not allow for the creation of a public street.

Mrs. Robison next reviewed an example of a shared driveway that was recently approved. She
explained that shared driveways exist throughout the City, particularly on parcels that are seen as
"infill" pieces in some ofthe larger lot areas. Shared driveways are allowed just in the R-1-87, R-1-
43, R-1-10, and AG-5 zones. Shared driveways must meet certain criteria. The current threshold
allows shared driveways to provide access to two homes. The current proposal was to increase that
threshold to three. Some infill properties in large areas are set more deeply in the property, so shared
driveways become a necessity.

Mrs. Robison confirmed that the proposal was reviewed by the City Engineer and the Fire Marshall.
Both were supportive of the proposal provided the three units meet all required standards. In
particular, the width of25 feet with an all-weather surface, which would include asphalt, concrete, or
a surface that can hold up to a 75,000-pound apparatus must be maintained to allow for emergency
equipment. A fire hydrant must be available nearby.

Mrs. Robison emphasized that the applicant was asking to change the ordinance fi"om two to three
units. She noted that there will be future discussions about potentially expanding the number of
permitted residences on shared driveways because ofthe number ofinfill residential parcels that exist,
now that so much residential development has occurred in Bluffdale.

Chair Cragun asked about the level of compliance currently taking place with no parking allowed on
shared driveways. She had encountered shared driveways that have people parking on them. Mrs.
Robison explained that the City does not enforce that issue because they are on private property, so
it would be up to the property owner to see to the enforcement.

Commissioner Flynn sought clarification on how other towns deal with the transition from private
rights-of-way to public roads. Mrs. Robison stated there are separate standards for shared driveways

and private rights-of-way and private streets. She emphasized that the issue under consideration was

Mr. Crowell stated that when cities get large they want everything to be public for consistency. If a
cul-de-sac has only four lots and is deemed to be a public street, that gives the City much more
responsibility for street maintenance. It is tricky to determine what should be public and what can
reasonably remain private without compromising safety. The primary concem with shared driveways
is to ensure safety. One fear that is commonly expressed is that streets start as private roads and
eventually become public in which case the City is responsible for maintenance. The City Council
has the final say in whether that kind of change takes place. The decision would entail extensive
study beforehand.

Mr. Crowell noted that Conditional Use Permits for developing on private rights-of-way were
something he and his colleagues could study to learn what other municipalities allow.

accessory buildings that take place on private property. He wanted to know ifthere are currently non-
compliance problems with shared driveways. Mrs. Robison stated that the ordinance is specific and
the shared driveways have to apply to a parcel that has already been created and meets the zoning
requirements. Mr. Crowell stated tiiat some non-public roads do not meet the current City Standards
for having more than two home accesses.

Commissioner Loumis opined that the problem was the result of a developer having property and

Chair Cragun noted that another problem was that ifa public road is put in the lot would end up being

because it is not a public street. She noted that some properties have no other option than to use a

putting in a full-width right-of-way, which is currently 52 feet. That width could compromise the
required size of the lot. She stated that the change from two to three homes on a shared driveway
would not pose any safety hazards or have any other negative impacts. Mr. Crowell clarified that
shared driveways, as a policy, do not have to be paved. In addition, the Fire Code does not require
