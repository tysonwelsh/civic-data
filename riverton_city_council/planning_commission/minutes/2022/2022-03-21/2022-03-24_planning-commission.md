       B.     DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT, “OLSON PROPERTY”, RIVERTON CITY
              IS PROPOSING A DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT FOR APPROXIMATELY
              5.5-ACRES OF PROPERTY IN THE RR-22 ZONE AT 13265 SOUTH
              LOVERS LANE. APPLICANT - RIVERTON CITY.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich presented the Staff Report and stated that the request was for
a Development Agreement for the Olson Property. Riverton City proposed the approval of
a Development Agreement that established requirements, standards, and parameters for
the development of approximately 5.5 acres, located at 13265 South Lovers Lane. This was
on the east side of the City. The agreement would allow a subdivision of the property with
lot sizes that are consistent with the current zoning. The property is currently zoned RR-22,
a single-family residential zone with half-acre lots and animal rights. The General Plan
designation for the area was Estate Density Residential. Mr. Prestwich reported that the
RR-22 Zone was compatible with Estate Density Residential. The General Plan Map was
shared with the Commission. East of Redwood Road was Estate Density Residential.

Mr. Prestwich reported that notices were sent out to all property owners within 1,000 feet of
the site. Notice was also posted on the City’s website. Several residents phoned the City
asking for additional information but no written comments were received. Mr. Prestwich
identified the subject property on an aerial map displayed. He made note of several roads
in the area as well as nearby developments. Mr. Prestwich explained that Butterfield Creek
South started further west and came down through the property before going into the Sewer
District property and finding its way into the river. The proposed Concept Development Plan
was shown. It included Phases 1 and 2 as well as seven lots. Some history about the
property was shared with the Commission.

Mr. Prestwich stated that the intention was for the Commission to discuss the request, take
public comment, and continue the item. He noted that this was a Legislative Action Item,
which meant the Planning Commission would make a recommendation to the City Council.
The City Council would make the final decision. Mr. Prestwich asked City Attorney, Ryan
Carter to present the details of the Development Agreement. Mr. Carter noted that the
Development Agreement would work out some of the details related to the contemplated
subdivision. The reason for the agreement was that the developer wanted some assurance
about the development standards for the property moving forward. In the Subdivision Plat
drawing, there was a red diagonal line. This identified the areas included in Phases 1 and 2
of the development. The developer wanted to build on the 1.86 acres of land first. That
section was shown in purple and was included in Phase 1 on the map.

The Development Agreement contemplated that the developer would complete Phase 1
under a certain set of design criteria and Phase 2 be built under a slightly different set of
design criteria. Mr. Carter clarified that the Development Agreement would not allow the
developer to add lots to the property that would otherwise be restricted under the zone.
There was no increase in density by use of the Development Agreement. He felt there was
a fairly conservative lot count compared to what could have been allowed.

Staff requested that the item be continued to a future meeting. One of the reasons for this
had to do with Butterfield Creek South. Mr. Carter explained that there were questions about
whether Butterfield Creek South was at maximum capacity for storm water. In 2006,
Riverton City commissioned a study that looked at Butterfield Creek as a storm water
management system. Several assumptions were made at the time, which made the study
incomplete. As development continued to grow around the creek, this created additional
storm water discharge. The question was whether maximum capacity had been reached.
Staff wanted to see a new study to replace the 2006 study. This would provide the most
comprehensive data and determine the condition of Butterfield Creek South. At the current
time, Staff believed the storm water could be managed adequately without any major
disruptions. However, the study would be able to confirm that.

Mr. Carter recommended that the Planning Commission open the public hearing. The
Commission could then continue the item, as well as the public hearing, to the next Planning
Commission Meeting. Since additional information would be shared at the next meeting, he
did not recommend that the Planning Commission close the public hearing tonight.

Chair Cluff opened the public hearing.

Barbara Dillman lives south of the subject property. Though Staff seemed to be concerned
about the water, she was concerned that additional homes would be added to the street.
There is only one road in and out but there is a subdivision to the north and south. She
wondered how the traffic would be handled. Ms. Dillman hoped that the development would
be done properly and that all traffic concerns would be addressed.

Tammy Larsen noted that Page 7 of the packet included a reference to her. She wanted to
understand what “condemnation of property” referred to. Ms. Larsen expressed concerns
about being referenced in the materials, especially since she only learned about the
document earlier that day. While she appreciated what the developer was trying to do, she
felt he was trying to fit too many homes onto the land. There is not enough access.
Ms. Larsen was not supportive of a stub road on her property. She was disappointed that
no one had reached out to her about this issue but included her name in the document.
Chair Cluff suggested that Ms. Larsen speak with a member of Staff following the Planning
Commission Meeting so her questions could be answered.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing remained open.

Commissioner Cluff moved that the Planning Commission CONTINUE the Olson
Development Agreement matter to the next Planning Commission Meeting.
Commissioner Cannon seconded the motion. The motion passed with the unanimous
consent of the Commission.
