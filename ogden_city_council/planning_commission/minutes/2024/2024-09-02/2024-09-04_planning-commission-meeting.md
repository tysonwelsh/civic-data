Request for Rezone, C-2 to R-4, 150 N 32" St.

Damian Rodriguez, Planning Division, begins with there are two primary considerations for a
rezone petition. First that the rezone petition is consistent with the general plan and second, that the
rezone petition is consistent with the zone change guidelines that they're going to review against the
case. .

The current location and zone are C-2, which is community, commercial. It is moderate density
commercial. The property size is 43,011 square feet total, so it is just shy of one acre, all of which is in
the Jefferson Community. The R-4 zone standards are 6000 square feet for the first unit, then an
additional 1500 square feet for each additional unit. That would give them a maximum of 25 units.
This is identical to what was allowed in the C-2 area, and standards are identical. Unfortunately, there
were no conceptual site plans or any schematics of any kind providing connection with this request.

There is a school and a park within walking distance and apartments and townhomes are allowed, but
multi-family residential zoning was not specified before, and staff would consider a conditional
overlay as a condition to approval tonight. That conditional overlay will limit development of
apartments and townhomes and progress the community's plan and policy of increasing home
ownership. Lastly, staff didn’t find the petition to be contrary to any of the vision strategies of the
general plan and that the rezone is more consistent with the general plan than not. There are multiple
components of consideration.:

a-A definite edge to development and appropriate buffering between types of uses should be
provided. In staff's opinion, the petition rezone improves the situation.

b-Zoning should reflect the prevailing character of the overall district or neighborhood to the largest
system possible, with consideration being given to the use and characteristics of individual
properties, etc. The use and character of the specific site would be conducive to the proposed.
c-Where possible, properties that face each other across a local street should be of the same or
similar zone, etc. The development of the property as an R-4 is similar to and complements the R-1
across the street and is an improvement, in staff's opinion to the current C-2 zoning.

d-The corner of 32" and Lincoln facing west is the subject property (slides shown). Staff finds that
the historic C-2 zoning is a more abrupt and less desirable transition from R-1-5 zoning. So, in terms
of this guideline, staff's finding is that the petition to rezone would serve as an ideal buffer between
commercial and single-family zones.

e-A townhome development, the stated impetus for this rezone position, does reflect a neighborhood
development and the manner in which residents interact with the neighborhood can and likely would
be improved by the stated concept of development. Also, residential zoning of the neighborhood
would remain contiguous, and the reason would not, as far as staff can see, result in a development
pattern that would divide the neighborhood. So, staff finds the petition is consistent, with
consideration.

f- A townhome proposal could help bolster the community and could be a good fit in this location and
in the community. Some community amenities include, an elementary school, a public park,
employment opportunities, some neighborhood commercial zoning and some related neighborhood
services. Most of these things are within a quarter mile walking distance. So, staff finds that a
townhome development would be a great fit for both the site and the community given that a
townhome development does reflect a neighborhood development, and the manner in which residents
interact with the neighborhood can be improved by this concept for development.

g-Avoiding isolating neighborhoods. Staff finds that the petitioned rezone softens the transition of
zones at the subject site and does have the potential to improve any neighborhood isolation effects
that would be or could be caused by land use and zoning consideration.

h- The location of commercial zoning should be based on market patterns and traffic counts, so this
one’s a little more complex. The current and historic use of the property suggests to staff that market
demand just isn't high enough under the current zoning. Traffic counts were also pulled from UDOT,
so staff finds the subject property is a less-than-ideal location for commercial zoning, both real estate,
real estate market, and the traffic count.

i-The commercial zoning should be located on collector and arterial streets. 32nd street is a collector
Street, and abutting properties are a viable option for commercial zoning. Staff finds under the current
zoning designation, development of the subject parcel would take the form of a commercial strip,
rather than viably contributing to any commercial center, and would be an undesirable land use. So,
staff recommends approval of the rezone petition to change the zone from C-2 to R-4 multiple family

residential, subject to the following condition, the property is rezoned to R-4 with a conditional overlay

Ogden Towns, 335 7" St, - A. Final group dwelling

Chair Safsten implied this a two-part item and invited the petitioner Travis Taylor to speak. He
was not in attendance, so he invited the city to present on the item.

Mr. Brierley began with this particular property having been before them two other times. One
was a zone change to R-2S which was approved by the City Council. The second step was a preliminary
group dwelling which was approved with certain conditions. So, this item was to see if they had made
the corrections needed and can get final dwelling approval. It is in the middle of a block and on about
2.07 acres, with one of the requirements being to adjust the property lines so they didn't have any
buildings being built right over the property lines. Another requirement of the R-2S is that they be platted
for individual ownership. The second item after this one was the subdivision to actually create the lots
to address those. The R-2S would allow maximum density, 36 units there. There will be rear loaded, two
car garages with bike and garbage storage in the garages, and some requirements are that electrical
vehicles must be charging in the garage with a simple outlet that will be provided), a 6 ft vinyl screen
fence to be around the perimeter of the project, and there's a requirement for a lighting plan. The plan
provided showed that the individual lighting won't have impacts on the abutting ground, but the
photometric plan didn’t specify what would be considered spilling off into adjoining property. So that
condition hadn't been satisfied yet, but it’s something we can review at a staff level. Planning
Commission approved a driveway setback reduction from 8 ft to 6 ft and a small corner of 3 ft. So, the
revised plan meets that, but they still must get a variance from engineering for the driver approach. One
of the requirements was to have an access easement through the property with East emergency access,
which documentation has been provided for that, it just needs to be recorded. They submitted a
