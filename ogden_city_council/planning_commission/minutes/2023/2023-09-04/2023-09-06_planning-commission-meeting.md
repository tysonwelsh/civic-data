3. Petition to Vacate a portion of 3rd Street between Porter Ave. and Jefferson Ave.
   Mr. Steve Diamond asked Staff to present his proposal.

  Ms. Hale stated the item had been continued from the previous meeting and is a request for a street
  vacation. In approving a vacation, the Commission is to determine whether there is good cause,
  whether it is a public interest, whether consistent with the General Plan. The request would relocate
  the right-of-way but maintain a 66’ right-of-way with a pavement with of 26’. The existing intersection
  with 3rd Street and Jefferson is off-set and the proposal would better align the block at both the east
  and west ends as well as provide safer travel lanes. Because the existing pavement width is less
  than the existing City standards, the reduced width will need to be approved by the Mayor. The
  realignment of the intersection interferes with the existing Lynn Irrigation canal, and a condition of
  approval would be to relocate the headgate 14’ to the south in order to meet the 20’ curb radius.
  Approval of its relocation must be obtained from the irrigation company. She identified the location
  of existing driveways for homes on 3rd Street and stated access will be maintained. The applicant
  then intends to add this property to the large parcel to the north and create a single-family
  subdivision. The street vacation would allow development of 16 residential lots while the existing
  land would allow only 14 homes. Sidewalk will be installed along the property as part of the
  subdivision, with curb and gutter to be installed along the south side of 3 rd Street, As the General
  Plan supports completing sidewalks along local streets as well as improved options for housing,
  Staff finds the proposal consistent with the General Plan. Staff recommends approval, subject to the
  following conditions:
  1. That an agreement with Lynn Irrigation company allowing the petitioner to shift the irrigation
     headwall structure 14 feet south is submitted.
  2. That the curb and gutter will terminate before it reaches the driveway on the southeast corner of
     the Porter Avenue and 3rd Street intersection or the petitioner cuts in a new drive approach for the
     property.
  3. That the applicant submits engineered construction drawings for the for the public improvements
     to receive approval from the Ogden City Engineering Department.
  4. That the applicant submits a financial guarantee to complete all public improvements per city
     standards.
  5. That the plat be modified as follows:
     a. a narrative is added to the plat describing that the vacated portion will be combined with the
         parcel directly to the north,
     b. the 5D development parcel and the 3rd Street Vacated parcel be clearly labeled as a single

4. Conditional Use Permit, to allow solar array farm for Autoliv, 1000 W 3300 S

  Due to a conflict of interest, Commissioner Safsten was excused from the meeting for this agenda
  item.

  Mr. Eric Drake stated Autoliv has committed to become carbon neutral by 2030 and is proposing to
  obtain green energy. They are proposing a vacant 8-acre portion of their property be developed with
  ground level solar array farm.

  Commissioner Shale asked if consideration had been given to installing arrays on the building. Mr.
  Drake responded due to conflict with roof-top air conditioning units as well as the age of the building,
  it was felt the solar units would be better at ground level. He also indicated the soil on the 8-acre
  parcel had previously been contaminated and had recently been cleaned, but has remained vacant.
  He stated the solar units do not create any contamination.

  Ms. Patrick stated this use is identified as a conditional use in the existing M-2 zone, and the
  Commission is to determine it is not detrimental to persons or properties, is compliant with the
  Ordinance, and is compatible with surrounding development and uses. She identified the proposed
  location of the solar units in relation to the existing building and indicated each array will be 8’ in
  height. It will also occupy a portion of the existing parking lot and will require the removal of 141
  parking stalls as well as some trees. Surrounding property is also zoned M-2 with the airport across
  Hinkley Drive zoned A-I. She indicated due to its proximity with the Ogden Municipal Airport, the FAA
  is required to be notified of the proposed use, which has occurred. The use is not detrimental to
  persons or property as it does not emit pollutants into the ground or air, does not encroach onto any
  rivers or streams and will be screened with privacy slats being installed in the fence along
  Pennsylvania Avenue. A glare analysis has been completed for each of the nearby streets and
  structures and determined the array will not cause glare to the roads but could produce glare for up
  to three hours per year for one of the structures, and suggested the array be modified to mitigate that
  potential impact. She stated it is consistent with the General Plan and will not impact views from
  Hinkley Drive as existing trees and buildings create a screen. She stated both the use and design are
  compatible with other uses in the zone, and the use meets the standards of the Zoning Ordinance.
  While the use will eliminate 141 parking spaces, the combined office space, warehouse and
  manufacturing uses would require 951 stalls and the removal would leave 1,042 stalls. She

                                                                                            Page 4 of 10
  recommended existing trees being removed along the south fence be required to be replaced
  elsewhere on the site to meet requirements for landscaping. Staff recommendation is for approval
  subject to the solar array being screened form view on Pennsylvania Avenue, removed trees be
  replaced elsewhere on the site, and potential glare be mitigated according to the glare analysis
  report.

  Commissioner Southwick asked how many trees would be removed as well as the life span of the
  solar units. Ms. Patrick indicate the three trees along the fence line are to be replaced elsewhere on
  the site, but many trees on the vacant property are insignificant and need not be replaced.
  Commissioner Southwick expressed concern that the mature trees would be replaced by younger
  trees and the benefit of having the trees is minimized. Mr. Drake responded the contract is for 25
  years with an option to be extended for another 25 years. The expected life span of the modules is
  30 years. He stated the six trees along the fence line to be replaced would be Linden trees and would
  be scattered throughout the site.

  MOTION:      A motion was made by Commissioner Graf to approve the conditional use permit based
               on the findings the use is in the best interest of the public and in harmony with good
               development, and the site plan and conditional use meet criteria for approval as it will
               not emit pollutants to the ground or air or cause any detrimental effects to the property
               or neighboring properties, does not encroach on rivers or streams and is in keeping with
               surrounding uses and will not impact the character of the area. Approval is subject to
               the solar array area being screened from view along Pennsylvania Avenue, that removed
               trees be replaced elsewhere on the site, and potential glare to Building #4 be mitigated
               according to the glare analysis report. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Shale
               and passed 8-1 with Commissioners Aaberg, Blaisdell, Graf, Shale, Shinoda, Williams
               and Sandau voting aye and Commissioner Southwick voting no. Commissioner
               Southwick explained his vote indicating he felt the existing mature trees should remain
               and felt there is a better use for the land. Commissioner Safsten abstained due to a
               conflict of interest.

  Due to a conflict of interest, Commissioner Shinoda was excused from the meeting at this time.

  Mr. Kyle Green indicated Brenntag is a long-time business in Ogden and has been at this location for
  over 40 years. He stated the intent is to enhance the existing facility as well as help with the
  revitalization of the Exchange Road area.

  Mr. Simpson reviewed the history of the site, stating there have been several upgrades over time.
  This request is for the expansion onto adjacent railroad property which has been leased by Brenntag
  as well as modifying the site to bring all operations up to code and meet Federal regulations. Much
  of the blending, loading and storage is to be relocated to this area which will be further from the river.
  He identified the various buildings on the site on both sides of the Weber River. He identified the
  location of existing bridges and uses in each area, stating access to the property to the east is
  provided by easements which need to be clarified to assure public access is available in the future.
  He stated the pedestrian bridge is also used by forklift operators. Because this also provides access
  to public trails in the area, Staff feels crossing signs be required. While there is some vegetation
  which provides a screen from the public view, there are some areas of outdoor storage and utility
  equipment which must be screened. He stated there is adequate parking on the site for the office
  building, including two ADA stalls, however some ADA accessibility is lacking for other buildings. He

6. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment for uses in C-2 when adjacent to residential uses

  Mr. Rypien stated the Commission had requested Staff to review uses in the C-2 zone which are next
  to residential zones and has held several work sessions discussing potential ordinance information.
  Staff is now prepared with ordinance language to address these concerns. He defined the purpose of
  the C-2/CP-2 zone, stating these provide services to the surrounding community but should not
  conflict with residential neighborhoods. He stated initial consideration had been given to removing
  some high impact uses from the zone, but upon reviewing the maps, it was found nearly all C-2 or
  CP-2 areas are adjacent to single-family residential uses which would likely preclude these uses from
  the zone. Staff has identified several high-impact uses which are currently listed in the C-2/CP-2
  regulations and is suggesting language be added to address impacts of these particular uses while
  maintaining them as either conditional or permitted uses as they currently exist in the code. These

                                                                                            Page 7 of 10
mitigation standards would protect adjacent single-family neighborhoods by increasing distance,
screening, hours of operation, restricting locations of entry and exits, as well as other measures. He
presented maps of the existing C-2/CP-2 zones as well as identifying particular existing uses in the
zones. Staff has prepared potential ordinance language and is recommending approval. He also
noted the Commission had received a correspondence from a resident at Cherrywood asking about
regulations in the 12th Street Corridor zone, and while valid, does not apply to the proposed ordinance
language. He indicated Staff has been in contact with the resident and will follow up with answers to
their questions.

Commissioner Safsten stated the hours of operation listed in the mitigation table should be further
limited, suggesting it be changed from 7am-10pm to 7am-9pm. Commissioner Southwick asked if
the code would apply to existing uses or only to new applications. Mr. Rypien responded these
standards would be applied to all new high-impact uses but would not affect existing uses unless
substantial changes were proposed by the owner. Commissioner Shinoda asked who determines
which uses are high impact, particularly if a use is not listed in the existing code. Mr. Rypien
responded the interpretation of the code is directed to the zoning administrator, which is the Planning
Manager. Discussion continued relating to identified high-impact uses and whether glare or height of
uses might also be a consideration. Commission noted the mitigation table indicates a restriction on
entrances and exits to uses not being allowed within 100’ of a residential zone, indicating some lots
are less than 100’ in width which would preclude the use. Commissioner Williams felt the attraction
of new traffic into the neighborhood creates an impact to residents and suggested it also be
considered when identifying high-impact uses. She felt any traffic study should consider
pedestrians, particularly those with children or pets. Commissioner Safsten stated traffic studies are
typically considering only the volume of traffic and asked if there are particular standards which the
Commission could address during the decision-making. Discussion continued relating to traffic
studies and their benefit and so long as the street can accommodate the proposed new traffic, there
is little discretion the Commission can add. It was suggested standards also might be adopted to
also consider pedestrians or bicyclists.

Mr. Doug Olson stated while he is not an Ogden resident, he owns property in Ogden and is concerned

done to use existing buildings. He felt the commercial property is important near the university as it
provides services to students and residents and is important to the general welfare of the
community.

She expressed concern about homes being converted to office space or daycare centers.
