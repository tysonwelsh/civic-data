14. Presentations / Recognitions / Proclamations

    a. Riverton Police Department Report

Chief Hutson said that April was defined as the month that felt the full impact of policing during
the COVID-19 pandemic. He said that, as expected, their calls for service were at a lower level
than previous months; domestic violence and fraud calls remained consistent with the previous
months, which would be expected with more folks staying home from work and the added stress
of the outbreak. He said that reports of traditional property crimes, such as vehicle burglaries,
shoplifting, and residential burglaries had reduced to the lowest levels since inception. He said
that the traffic accidents and traffic offenses were dramatically reduced, even though there were
theoretically more cars in the community; however, he believed it was due to less through traffic
as a result of fewer people commuting from the surrounding communities.

Chief Hutson reported that they were able to make seven arrests for DUI during the month,
even though they are making far fewer pro-active traffic stops to keep the officers safe. He said
that was a testament to the ability of the officers to focus on the drivers who were most likely
impaired and keeping the streets safe as a result.

Chief Hutson then reviewed the Patrol Activity Report for April 2020 and addressed questions
from the elected officials.

16. Public Hearing(s) / Action Items

    a. Ordinance No. 20-13 - Rezone, RD Development Located at 11845 South Redwood
       Rd, Proposed Rezone to RM-6-SD, Residential Multi-Family with a Maximum of 6
       Units Per Acre, with Specific Development Designations Limiting Density and
       Configuration, RD Development, Applicant

Development Services Director Jason Lethbridge explained that the public hearing was
regarding a proposed rezone of several properties totaling approximately 12.8 acres located on
the east side of Redwood Road at approximately 11845 South. He said the subject properties
were currently a mix of zoning with single family residential (RR-22 and R-3), Professional
Office and Neighborhood Commercial Zones. He said the current General Plan was a mix of
Medium Density Residential and Professional Office. He said that nearby zoning included R-4
and R-3 to the south and R-3 across the canal to the east, and to the north was RR-22 zoning
with larger lots. Across Redwood Road to the west was RM-8 south of 11800 South and RR-22
north of 11800 South.

Mr. Lethbridge said the applicant requested RM-6 SD zoning, which was residential multifamily,
6 units per acre. He said that the “SD” stands for Specific Development, which was a means to
apply special rules and regulations for a development. In the application, he said the SD would
assign areas within the overall development specific lot sizes and unit types.

Mr. Lethbridge explained that the SD designations as original proposed by the applicant were
based on a concept layout. For the purpose of clarity, he said staff divided the project area into
three sections. Under the original application, Area 1 would consist of the southern portion of
the properties. There would be a maximum of 14 lots in that area, with a minimum lot size of
8,000 square feet. Other than lot size, all setbacks and other standards for the area would be
consistent with the R-4 zone. Area 2, which was the northern portion, would be developed
consistent with the detached single-family requirements of the RM-6 zone, and included a
minimum lot size of 5,000 square feet. The lots adjacent to the north and east boundaries of the
area would be a minimum of 6,000 square feet. There would be a maximum of 29 lots in that area.
Area 3 was the smaller parcel just to the south of the existing commercial site on Redwood
Road. The property may be developed consistent with the attached unit standards in the RM-6
zone, with the exception that up to 5 units may be attached. A maximum of 10 units would be
allowed in that area.

Mr. Lethbridge said the Planning Commission forwarded a recommendation with modifications
to the original proposed designations. Specifically, the Commission recommended Area 1 would
be limited to 10,000 square foot lots (1/4 acre). Area 2 would be limited to 8,000 square foot
lots, and Area 3 would remain as shown, with 10 attached multi-family units. The Commission
also recommended that the main road connect through from 11800 South to Laurel Chase
Drive, but that no connection be made to Redwood Road through Area 3. However, the access
point for Area 3 was approved with the commercial project to the north as a shared drive by
UDOT, with the requirement that future development of the Area 3 property expand and improve
that access. Eliminating that requirement for development of Area 3 may conflict with UDOT’s
approval, and leave the existing shared access under sized.

Mr. Lethbridge further explained that the RM-6 zone included a requirement that perimeter
fencing comply with Section 18.155.080 of the Riverton City Code where development is
adjacent to a single-family zone. That section reads as follows:

18.155.80    Fencing between noncompatible zones/uses.
       (1) Noncompatible Zones. A solid core decorative precast concrete or integrally colored
           and textured block, brick, or other masonry fence with a minimum height of six feet
           shall be required between noncompatible zones. Both sides of the fence shall
           receive equal treatment with respect to pattern, color, etc. Hollow, foam core,
           fiberglass/concrete mix, or other alternative fence types are not permitted, except as
           noted below.
       (2) Fencing Height. Fencing shall be a minimum of eight feet in height between
           commercial/industrial zones and residential zoning of any type, and between
           multifamily development and single-family residential zones.

Mr. Lethbridge said that, based on the underlying RM-6 zoning, which was a multi-family zone,
the project would require an eight (8) foot precast fence along the entire perimeter, with the
exception of properties adjacent to the canal and Redwood Road, where six (6) foot would be
allowed. He said the RM-6 zone also included a requirement for open space. Section
18.45.180(11) specified that where individual lots were utilized in a project over 10 acres, which
was the case in the proposed project, a minimum of one (1) acre of open space. He said the
ordinance required that open space be privately maintained, unless otherwise approved. The
conceptual plans show open space that complies with the minimum area requirement, and
specific treatment and configuration would be approved as part of a site plan application.

Mr. Lethbridge said the question of road connections had been a significant issue with the
application. Laurel Chase Drive currently stubs to the southern boundary of the project area,
and the northwest corner of the project area was the intersection of Redwood Road and 11800
South. Much has been discussed on the potential connection of Laurel Chase Drive to that
signalized intersection, and the possible impacts of that connection on the surrounding road
network. H e s a i d t he applicant had presented several conceptual layouts showing alternative
road connections. Traffic impacts were typically reviewed with a rezone, and were an important
consideration; however, the road connection was not specific to the application or to a particular
density. He said the City would need to address the road connection irrespective of the eventual
density of the site. He said that final technical design of a roadway system was typically made with
the site plan/subdivision design for a project. As stated above, the Commission recommended
that Laurel Chase Drive be connected to Redwood Road as a through public right-of-way. He
continued to say that traffic-calming was typically addressed during the site plan/subdivision
process, and the types and placement of traffic calming employed were dependent to some

Mr. Lethbridge said the Planning Commission heard the item on April 23, 2020; at that meeting,
the Commission voted to recommend approval of the proposed rezone with modifications to the

The Council reviewed with staff the lot sizes, density, and land uses as recommended by the

areas was important. Fencing was discussed, and the applicant proposed solid vinyl along the

need for drainage and stormwater storage within the pond area of the property.
