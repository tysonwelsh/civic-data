OPENING CEREMONY:
Jill Anderson, Executive Director for CAPSA offered the opening ceremony and led the
audience in the pledge of allegiance.
Jill Anderson addressed the Council and said the following: As you know, Logan City has
been named one of the safest cities and communities in America and I am grateful to live
in such a wonderful community. Unfortunately, for some residents, the most dangerous
place is their very own home. CAPSA has been working in this community to respond and
serve victims of domestic violence and sexual violence including sex trafficking for the
last 45 years. This year we are celebrating 45 years of service. In the spirit of celebrating
our 45th year, I want to share a little about the founding of our organization, the last five
years, the last year which transformed all our lives.
CAPSA started in 1976 as the Cache Valley Rape Crisis Team in response to several
sexual assaults that occurred on the USU campus. Shortly after they formed the volunteer
team law enforcement, using the team for domestic violence as well. Thad and Jenny Box,
(the Dean of the College of Natural Resources of USU) started sheltering families in their
own home and the program continued to grow based on what survivors were telling us
they need.
The Mission of CAPSA focuses in two areas, Service and Prevention Education. During
the last five years, 568 individuals have been served. Last year 1,658 individuals were
served, and it has tripled in the last five years. During the last five years, education
services have provided 342 presentations to 8,000 participants. And over the last year
700 presentations were provided with more than 14,000 participants. In order to meet the
tripling of requests for help we went from 17 staff to 57 staff members working around
the clock 7-days a week 24-hours a day. We never get to turn out the lights and close the
doors. We have expanded our main building not once, but twice. And built or acquired 21
housing units to ensure survivors can secure the housing they need. The largest homeless
population in our community are those who are homeless due to domestic violence. That

During the last year, Domestic Violence has been called a shadow pandemic. When you
consider tactics, abusers use during normal times, isolation from family and friends,
strict rules of behavior, monitoring and tracking activities and movements, restriction of
resources. While most of us saw these as ways to protect ourselves and others. Abusers

talk about long-Covid but this is also creating long-domestic violence and will impact
these families for years to come.
Tonight in our emergency shelter alone we are housing and serving 18 women, 21
children, and 5 men. For those 18 women, 21 children, and 5 men it means everything. It
meets the most basic need we all have, a safe place to go at the end of the day, a place to
regroup, a place to recharge. And when we can get them safely housed in their own home
it means a safe place for the children to learn and to grow. In fact, in addition to our
emergency shelter, we are currently providing housing to 26 families.
Jill Anderson thanked the City for helping CAPSA with the $120,000 hotel bills over the

PUBLIC HEARING - Budget Adjustment FY 2021-2022 appropriating: $25,000
funds to CAPSA to assist with the cost of running a qualified homeless shelter in
Logan (January to June 2022) - Resolution 21-40 – Mayor Daines

At the July 20, 2021, Council meeting, Mayor Daines addressed the Council and
explained that three years ago the state legislature created a funding source for the law
enforcement costs of homeless shelters primarily for Salt Lake and other larger metro
areas. At that time, the State appropriated City's sales taxes from cities that did not
possess homeless shelters. CAPSA is considered a shelter because they have homeless
victims of domestic abuse. CAPSA has recently expanded its facilities and the laws have
changed slightly permitting the local CAPSA organization to qualify for these funds.
Without local CAPSA services, the City would be sending approximately $100,000 to the
State from the general funds. CAPSA has requested that a portion of the funds that would
have been allocated to the State be allocated to assist them. This is a long-term goal to
address the topic at a local and a state level.

Chairman M. Anderson opened the meeting to a public hearing.

There were no comments and Chairman M. Anderson closed the public hearing.

Anderson to approve Resolution 21-40 as presented. Motion carried unanimously.

Consideration of a proposed resolution approving a Cable Television Franchise
Agreement between Logan City, a Utah Municipal Corporation, and Comcast of
Indiana/Kentucky/Utah – Resolution 21-43 – Kymber Housley, City Attorney

City Attorney Kymber Housley addressed the Council and explained the proposed
resolution. The contract is renewed every five years with Comcast with no changes
except for updates to Federal Statues.

Vice Chair Simmonds inquired if similar franchise contracts exist with other entities.

Mr. Housley explained that similar contracts exist with other entities but under a different
statute or ordinance. Most are done under the telecommunications franchise ordinance.
Cable is specifically excluded from the telecommunications franchise ordinance,
therefore a different ordinance. Telecommunication franchises are generally done at a
staff level, but any franchise outside of telecommunication must be brought before the
Council.

Vice Chair Simmonds asked that with ongoing projects, how can the City know if the
project is legally permitted to do so.

Mr. Housley responded that even if there is an existing franchise agreement with the
entity, the entity is still required to obtain a right-of-way permit. That does not exclude
the entity from violating the terms of the permit and that is dealt with via enforcement.

Mayor Daines referred the question to Paul Lindhardt, Public Works Director for further
explanation.

Mr. Lindhardt agreed with Mr. Housley’s statement. He explained that the simplest
method to determine if there is an existing permit with the entity is to contact the Public
Works administrative assistant to identify if there is an existing permit at that address. If
there is not an existing permit, they enforce the need; and even if there is an existing
permit, they ensure that the entity is working within the parameters of the permit.

Vice Chair Simmonds requested an example of procedures to acquire a permit for a
fiberoptic company.

Mr. Lindhardt replied that the fiberoptic company would need to fill out an application
and submit all the required documentation for a right-of-way permit with the City. He
clarified that any work done in the right-of-way whether a homeowner, contractor, etc. is
required to have a permit. There are various costs associated with the different projects
per permit.

Councilmember Jensen asked if the organization had already begun working prior to the
permit being issued.

Mr. Lindhardt answered that Comcast had yet to commence any project and that the
proposal is a renewal of the franchise agreement.

Vice Chair Simmonds requested clarification on the reference of pole attachment.

Mr. Lindhardt explained that any work added or attaching to a pole must acquire a pole
attachment agreement and permit from the Light and Power Department.

Vice Chair Simmonds inquired if the City-owned all the poles within the City. And if,
and how they are documented.

Mark Montgomery, Light and Power Director responded that the City owns most of poles
except for a few that belong to telecommunication companies already grandfathered in.
The poles are on GIS maps as each pole is identified by an individual number on the pole.

The proposed resolution will be an action item at the August 17, 2021 council meeting.
