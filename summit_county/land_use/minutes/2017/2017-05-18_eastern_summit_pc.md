5.   Continued public hearing and possible action regarding a Conditional Use Permit 
for a proposed commercial kennel; 121 E 3200 N, Kamas, UT; MVE-2; Breanna 
Wright, Applicant. – Sean Lewis, County Planner 
 
Planner Lewis stated that two weeks ago the Planning Commission heard an application for 
this commercial kennel. The applicant two weeks ago was Breanna Wright. Following the 
last Planning Commission meeting, Ms. Wright chose to back out of the real estate

transaction and withdraw her Conditional Use Permit application and transfer that 
application to Ms. Painter, who would like to continue the application in a modified form. 
Ms. Painter would like to represent what she would like to do with rescue dogs: bring dogs 
into the home. The kennel would not really be run like a commercial kennel in the sense 
that people are bringing dogs for a fee. Ms. Painter would like to be clear with the 
neighbors and clear with Animal Control as to what is happening and how many dogs are 
on the property. Ms. Painter would like to continue the Conditional Use Permit in that 
regard. 
Staff did consult with Animal Control regarding how many animals would be appropriate 
for the site. Planner Lewis and Animal Control made a site visit. Pictures were shown. 
Indoor kennel stalls were shown to be quite big. There were also kennels for the dogs to be 
separated. The fence was shown with a galvanized privacy fence on the dog run. There 
should be no concerns with children putting fingers through the fence or the dogs running 
the fence where the dogs would be close to neighbors. During the site visit both Planner 
Lewis and the Animal Control officer pulled in the site in strange vehicles and no dog 
attempted to bark or run the fence the entire time of the site visit. The only dogs on site 
were Ms. Painter’s personal dogs. 
Part of the discussion at the last meeting was about how many dogs would be appropriate 
to have on site at a time. Staff is recommending as a Condition of Approval that the number 
of dogs be limited to a total of twenty during daytime hours, which have been determined 
to be 8am to 8pm and fifteen dogs during the overnight hours of 8pm to 8am. The owner’s

six personal dogs would be included in the number allowed, meaning there can only be 
fourteen extra dogs that currently live on site during business hours. 
Staff has also included a Condition of Approval that noise generated by the dogs be 
regulated by the Noise Ordinance and Summit County Animal Control. Any repeated 
violations of the Noise Ordinance or Animal Control will result in a review of the Eastern 
Summit County Planning Commission. The Health Department has the opportunity to 
inspect grounds to verify proper containment of waste and waste water to comply with 
Title V, which regulates nuisances in that regard. 
Another Condition of Approval is that dogs kept on site in the overnight hours are housed 
indoors. Another Condition is that the applicant install privacy fencing where necessary. 
Where the dog areas are they are screened or far away from the neighbors. Any fencing 
installed will be within one year, at the request of the neighbors. Another Condition of 
Approval is that the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission will review the permit 
after the period of one year to check for compliance and review the permit. 
With the Conditions mentioned and the change in the applicant and limiting the number of 
dogs, Staff is confident that the concerns of the Commission have been addressed. Staff 
recommends approval of the Conditional Use Permit according to the Findings of Fact, 
Conclusions of Law and Conditions of Approval found in the Staff Report. 
Commissioner Sargent asked for clarification concerning the commercial purpose being 
stated in Findings of Fact number 7. Planner Lewis stated that the Use Chart lists the 
requirement of a Conditional Use Permit for a commercial kennel. That is the use being 
approved for the Conditional Use Permit. Even though this is being defined as a commercial

kennel, Ms. Painter does not intend to use it commercially to advertise and bring dogs on 
site. Ms. Painter keeps her personal dogs there and at random times will have foster dogs 
that are being rescued there. This is a temporary home for the dogs to be fed and cared for. 
The kennel is not being treated as a business, but the Use Chart lists it as a commercial 
kennel and that is the name in the Findings of Fact. 
Commissioner Sargent asked what the number of twenty dogs is based on for Staff to 
conclude it would be the right number for limiting the dogs. Planner Lewis answered the 
reasonableness of that was the previous applicant wanted thirty to forty-five dogs. Ms. 
Painter has had up to thirty dogs at a time on-site. The number of twenty is a compromise. 
Staff got the feeling that many of the neighbors thought thirty was too many. This was the 
number between that could be reasonably handled on-site. The facilities that are already 
there can reasonably handle twenty dogs. The number could be higher, but in order to calm 
the fears of the neighbors and draw a line in the sand, the number of twenty was felt to be 
reasonable, while still being able to house the rescue dogs. 
Commissioner Sargent asked in the Conditions of Approval number 2, what does 
repeated mean. Planner Lewis answered it is case by case. Staff wanted it to not be a 
problem with one violation, but seeing the same violation multiple times to cause concern 
to automatically bring it to the Planning Commission. Animal Control has not had a 
complaint from this site for over a year. This is an avenue if violations pile up, it could be 
brought to the Planning Commission and not based on just one possible violation.

Commissioner Wilde believes that noise will be the big issue and has called Animal 
Control numerous times for noise from a neighbor about 1000 feet away. Commissioner 
Wilde was told to make a recording report of the noise for quite a period. Commissioner 
Wilde does not believe the Animal Control noise ordinance is sufficient. Planner Lewis 
referred to Exhibit B in the Staff Report, which includes the Animal Control regulations..

what point is a citation issued. It is after ten days of recording. 
Commissioner Sonntag asked if this was a commercial kennel, what would be the 
limitations on the number of dogs. Planner Lewis answered this is not a commercial kennel 
and there is no limit for kennel use. The standards listed in Exhibit B apply whether the 
kennel is commercial or not. The advantage to having it be a commercial kennel is the 
Conditional Use Permit and the additional regulations associated with it for what is 
otherwise a non-regulated use. The only legal significance of there having been thirty dogs 
in the past is that it gives precedence for there to be that many dogs in the future and to 
show what is reasonable. Commissioner Sonntag asked how many other two-acre lots in 
the county have twenty dogs. Planner Lewis answered none. 
Chair Hanson clarified that if the Conditional Use Permit is approved, it runs with the land, 
including the Conditions of Approval. To change the Conditions of Approval the process 
would be to apply for a Conditional Use Permit amendment. Under current Code there are 
two different types: minor or major amendments. Major amendments include increasing 
square footage or intensity, which could be number of dogs, etc. This could be an 
administrative process via Staff or come before the Planning Commission, depending on to
