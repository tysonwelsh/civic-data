MINUTES     OF   THE   COTTONWOOD     HEIGHTS    CITY  COUNCIL
ADMINISTRATION OF OATH OF OFFICE AND RECEPTION, JANUARY 3, 2022 AT
5:00 P.M. IN THE COTTONWOOD HEIGHTS CITY COUNCIL CHAMBERS
LOCATED AT 2277 EAST BENGAL BOULEVARD

Members Present: Mayor Mike Peterson, Council Member Scott Bracken, Council Member
Christine Mikell, Council Member Douglas Petersen

Staff Present: City Manager Tim Tingey, City Attorney Shane Topham, Police Chief Robby
Russo, Community and Economic Development Director Michael Johnson, Public Works
Director Matt Shipp, Finance and Administrative Services Director Scott Jurges, Records
Culture and Human Resources Director Paula Melgar, Assistant Fire Chief Riley Pilgrim

Excused:         Council Member Tali Bruce

1.      WELCOME – Mayor Mike Peterson

Mayor Mike Peterson welcomed those in attendance and recognized State Legislator Gay Lynn
Bennion, Amber Schill of the Canyons School District, former Council Member Mike Shelton,
and former Mayor Kelvyn Cullimore who were present.

2.      POSTING OF COLORS – Color Guard: Cottonwood Heights Police Department

The Posting of Colors was led by the Cottonwood Heights Police Department Color Guard.

3.      PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE – Council Member J. Scott Bracken

The Pledge of Allegiance was led by Council Member Bracken.

4.      MUSICAL NUMBER – America the Beautiful, Brian Allen and Chantel Nate

Brian Allen and Chantel Nate performed “America the Beautiful”.

5.      REMARKS - Mayor Mike Peterson and City Council Members

Council Member Petersen expressed gratitude to Mayor Peterson for his leadership and
inclusiveness and considered what he has done during his tenure to be immeasurable. He
expressed appreciation to the City Council and those in the City who have rendered service.

Council Member Mikell commented that Mayor Peterson has been a mentor to her and she
appreciated his service over the last four years. She thanked the residents for allowing her to
represent them and looked forward to the future.

Council Member Bracken was excited to interact with new faces and looked forward to moving
forward as a City. He thanked Mayor Peterson for his involvement in the City prior to his
service on the City Council and during his time as Mayor. He considered him to be a
tremendous example of a life dedicated to service to the community. He thanked Council
Member Mikell for her service.

Mayor Peterson expressed gratitude to those online, present, and others for the outpouring of
support. He reported that it has been 50 years since he began working for Salt Lake County
with intercity youth. He worked for the County Recreation Department, The Hansen
Planetarium, County Criminal Justice Services, and recently retired after 30 years. He worked
in the service area for 15 years where he retired a second time. He was next honored to become
a member of the City Council 10 years ago and eventually served as Mayor for four years. His
focus has always been on people. He thanked the residents for electing him and allowing him
to serve.

Mayor Peterson thanked Former Mayor Kelvyn Cullimore and prior Council Members for their
service. He thanked Council Member Bracken for his service and believed his abundance of
institutional knowledge has been extremely helpful. He expressed gratitude to Council Member
Mikell for her passion for the foothills, open spaces, and the balance she brought to the Council.
He thanked Council Member Petersen who is a lifetime resident with deep roots and
commitment.

Mayor Peterson expressed appreciation to Staff for their service and to City Manager, Tim
Tingey, for his dedication and professionalism. He also thanked Finance and Administrative
Services Director, Scott Jurges for allowing him to leave office with a healthy fund balance.
He thanked Community and Economic Development Director, Michael Johnson, and his Staff
for their extensive knowledge. He thanked City Recorder, Paula Melgar, who oversees
numerous aspects of the City. He greatly appreciated Police Chief, Robby Russo for his
dedication to the City as well as the Police Department for providing a police force that is
customized to meet the needs of the City and its ongoing challenges. He highlighted the City’s
partnership with the Unified Fire Authority (“UFA”) and emphasized the importance of the
firefighters and EMTs. He noted that the majority of calls to a residence are not fire-related,
but health-related. He expressed gratitude to Riley Pilgrim and his predecessor Mike Watson
and City Attorney, Shane Topham, for his service.

Mayor Peterson stated that a famous author once stated, “Seek first to understand, then to be
understood.” He believed it was critical that the elected officials listen and gather facts and
information prior to making a decision or taking an action. He stated that it will pay dividends
as they move forward as a City. He offered his best wishes to the newly elected officials and
those remaining and thanked them for their service to the City.

6.      ADMINISTRATION OF OATH OF OFFICE TO NEWLY ELECTED COUNCIL
        MEMBERS – City Recorder, Paula Melgar

        6.1      Remarks – Council Member Shawn E Newell - Cottonwood Heights Council
                 District 3.

City Recorder, Paula Melgar administered the Oath of Office to Shawn E. Newell from
District 3.

Council Member Newell, a 30-year resident, expressed gratitude to those who voted for him to
serve as a Member of the City Council. He stated that the City is an engine and he looked
forward to being a piston in that engine to help create progress. He hoped the entire citizenry
will be a part. He was excited to work with the Cottonwood Heights Police Department.

        6.2      Remarks - Council Member Ellen Birrell – Cottonwood Heights Council
                 District 4.

Ms. Melgar administered the Oath of Office to Ellen Birrell from District 4.

Council Member Birrell thanked those present and was humbled that so many within her
District elected her to this prominent position. She hoped to bring skills, expertise, and good
judgment to her role. She described herself as a mother, a businesswoman, and a 40-year
member of the community. She considered the foothills to be the doorstep to Big and Little
Cottonwood Canyons and Cottonwood Heights as the gateway to those Canyons. She expressed
her commitment to protecting the natural resources as well as the safety and public health of
residents and visitors alike. She pledged to protect the character and unique qualities of
District 4 and ensure that State and Federal agencies, surrounding cities, and Salt Lake County
know where Cottonwood Heights stands.

Council Member Birrell stated that all have experienced disruptions due to COVID-19 and
recognize what is important in the community. She was excited to work toward unifying a City
infrastructure that serves public health and safety, residents, and Staff. She emphasized the
importance of mobility in the City and stated that as traffic increases, they can use all of their
capabilities to ensure that the City is protected.

        6.3      Remarks – Mayor Michael Weichers – Cottonwood Heights Mayor.

Ms. Melgar administered the Oath of Office to Mayor Michael Weichers.

Mayor Weichers was humbled to be the third Mayor of Cottonwood Heights. He recognized
the contributions of Mayors Cullimore and Peterson and respected their integrity, honesty, and
passion for the City. He thanked them for their service and stated he will lean on them over the
next four years. He thanked those who were willing to engage and work on his campaign
including multiple volunteers and City leaders. He pledged his commitment to working

Mayor Peterson thanked those in attendance and adjourned the meeting at 5:33 p.m.
