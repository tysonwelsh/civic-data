43   Council Member Knudsen mentioned           that he had noticed a fair amount of discontent      on social
44   media regarding the recent fee increase          at Wasatch     Front Waste and Recycling        District.
45   Specifically, residents were commenting on what they perceived as better services available in
46   West Valley by a different entity. But it was difficult to know if they were comparing apples to
47   apples.

48

49   Mayor Overson       responded   that the city administration   was currently studying costs regarding
50   services being provided by not only WFWRD but also Unified Fire, Public Works and Animal
51   Control. Results of those studies would take some time, but Council Members were certainly
52   welcome to let people know that the City was looking into it to ensure residents were getting a good
53   value. They were also free to refer interested residents directly to WFWRD if they had specific
54   questions regarding the cost of services.
55

56   Council Member Knudsen said he was pleased with WFWRD' s service but felt that generally
57   speaking, it was a wise practice to study each service periodically to ensure residents were getting
58   the best value for their dollars.
59

60   Council Member Barbieri who served on WFWRD' s Board pointed out that there had been a 40%
61   increase in the entity' s cost of services over the last four years and since different cities provided
62   different services and used different entities and/ or vendors, it was very challenging to provide an
63   accurate city to city comparison. Despite the chatter on Facebook, WFWRD had apparently not
64   been hearing directly from the public. CEO Pam Roberts was happy to speak with anyone who
65   expressed    concern.

66

67   Chair Harker discussed a class she took during the recent Utah League training which was geared
68   towards reminding elected officials that community Facebook pages( those not hosted by the city)
69   were not the place for official city responses to issues. Instead, to refer residents to the official city
70   page or to invite a personal discussion. Council Member Barbieri mentioned that her district had a
71   community council which met regularly and could discuss issues there.
72

73   City Attorney Tracy Cowdell mentioned that there was a need for a closed session that evening
74   following the regular meeting.
