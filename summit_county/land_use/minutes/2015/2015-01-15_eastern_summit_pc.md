WORK SESSION 
  
1. Discussion regarding Chapter 3 of the Eastern Summit Development Code – Ray 
Milliner, Principal Planner  
 
Chair Michael Brown said Community Development Director Patrick Putt and Planning and 
Zoning and Administrator Peter Barnes have a presentation for the Commission.  He

suggested the Commission allow them the time to lay out their entire presentation before they 
ask any questions they may have.   
 
Director Putt distributed a handout of the proposed zones with some specifics of the zones, 
such as the district intent and the lot and site requirements.  He believes the Commission will 
most likely add to and take away from this list, but it gives them a general purpose of the 
zones and the densities they are talking about.     
 
Administrator Barnes said the last time they met, they reviewed a contour map with the steep 
slopes and major roads of the Coalville to Wanship area.  There was a discussion of where 
various zones should be located.  Expanding or reinstating the highway corridor (HC) zone 
was also considered.  This area is now called the AG-1 zone.   
 
Administrator Barnes referred to the maps on the conference table.  He was trying to 
compare the geographical differences between the Kamas Valley and the North Summit 
Valley.  He pointed out some of the difficulties if they choose to expand the AG-1 to 1,000 
feet in both areas.  In the North Summit area, most of the existing agricultural land would be 
eliminated if it is developed.   
 
Administrator Barnes then reviewed the map for the South Summit area if the AG-1 is 
expanded to the proposed 1,000 feet width.  He said this seems to have less of an impact 
because this valley is wider, but the question is what would be the actual impact on the 
ground.  Because of steep slopes, there are areas that may be better suited in the AG-6 zone 
instead of the AG-1.  If they fill the area between Francis and Kamas with one-acre lots, they 
would be joining the two towns together.  The County would be competing with the towns as 
far as density goes.  This type of action would require negotiation with the towns.  He 
pointed out an area that with this level of development, it would essentially be a small town.  
Other possible problems and opportunities were pointed out.   
 
The maps were used to show where the old HC zone was located.  Administrator Barnes said 
other possible widths and perhaps even variable widths could answer some of these issues.  
There may be reasons to set the AG-1 further out on some of the slopes and benches and not 
as much on others; however, they would have to explain to the public why some people had a 
different opportunity than others.  He asked for comments. 
 
Director Putt said one of the things he has been wrestling with is the transition area between 
the valley bottom and the toe of the slope.  For a long time one of the goals of the 
Development Code for both sides of the County has been to keep development off the toes of 
the slope.  He said in a lot of agricultural areas, development is generally put in two different 
locations.  The first is in the streets leading to the town centers.  Development hugs close to 
the road and agriculture is behind.  The other place frequently developed is along the toe of 
the slope.  That leaves the flat bottom area available for agriculture.  Do they want to make 
some consideration of moving development on the flanks of the hillsides?

Administrator Barnes said they may want to keep in mind the AG-6 zone as they look at 
these areas.  He doesn’t think it is a one-size-fits-all, but it is probably not overly complicated 
either.  Commissioner Clyde asked if the difference between the rural residential (RR) zone 
and the AG-1 zone would be mink farms or no mink farms.  Director Putt responded there 
are two distinctions.  AG-1 is one unit for one acre and allows agricultural uses.  RR is one 
unit per acre with a lot size as small as ¾ acre.  Its options for agriculture would be more 
limited.  Commissioner Clyde said they want to make it clear no one is against mink.  It is 
just that there are places where mink ranching is appropriate and places it is not.    
 
Chair Brown introduced two members of the public, Gail Pace and Mike Willoughby.  Mr. 
Willoughby said he hopes there is an opportunity for children and grandchildren to be able to 
come back and live next to mom and dad.  They want to service the needs of the next 
generation that want to live in the valley.  Zoning should at least open the opportunity.   
 
Director Putt said wherever this line gets drawn, they will be using the AG-6 zone to deal 
with the transition from AG-1.  Commissioner Ure said in the Democrat Alley area, the AG-
1 zone couldn’t expand to 1,000 feet.  The hills are too steep and the water table is too high.  
He isn’t looking at this map as development but the potential to do different things on the 
ground.  If someone wanted to put a conservation easement on their property, what would be 
more appealing than to have a lot of density?   
 
Commissioner Clyde said none of them liked the way Indian Hollow came to them, but in the 
end, it is a good form of development and a good land use.  He is very comfortable with 
developing density.  He isn’t comfortable with the one-size-fits-all plan of designating a 
certain number of feet off all the roads in the County.  It addresses the issues of what you do 
with the grandchildren, but it doesn’t address planning.   
 
Commissioner Vernon said his thought is the 1,000 feet will be used to grant the building 
rights, not necessarily fill it up with houses.  A person may have six to eight units of density 
because of the 1,000 feet, but he has a place in the AG-40 where he could put the units.  
Shouldn’t it be his choice to be able to put in a road and put in six houses?  Commissioner 
Clyde said he doesn’t disagree with that other than it is strictly the property owner’s choice.   
   
Administrator Barnes said his concern with what Commissioner Vernon just said is that a big 
developer can come along and buy the land and put 5,000 units down the middle of the 
valley.  Commissioner Ure said they would still have to go through the rezoning process.   
 
Chair Brown asked Administrator Barnes to explain what he means when they say they have 
to save agriculture by means of zoning.  Administrator Barnes said he wouldn’t put it that 
way.  Commissioner Clyde said zoning is what took agriculture out of the Snyderville Basin.  
Chair Brown said it was the person who owned the land who chose to take it out of 
agriculture.  Commissioner Clyde countered that was because he was allowed to do so by 
zoning.  Commissioner Hanson said there are some people who live in this County who don’t 
want to live in Sandy, UT.  There is a balance.

Chair Brown said there are other mechanisms to make agriculture viable.  Commissioner 
Clyde said he thinks that zoning and agriculture preservation are the same discussion.  If you 
zone the land for density, it will receive that density if and when the market allows it to.  
Chair Brown said they are trying to protect someone in a very offensive way.    
 
Commissioner Clyde said this decision really belongs to the County Council.  They should 
define if agricultural, as included in the General Plan, means something.  The Commission 
can make recommendations to Council.  He thinks they need direction if they are ever going 
to get out of these discussions.  Chair Brown said the Council asked the Planning 
Commission to send their recommendations, not ask for theirs.    
 
Mr. Willoughby said he doesn’t think the question is about if they want to preserve 
agricultural land.  The question is how they go about doing it without stepping on the 
property rights of the landowner.  He said his parents were given ground by their parents.  
They built a house and farmed it.  He intended to do the same until development exploded in 
Summit County.  The County began using zoning to control the development process.  His 
land was in the AG-40 zone.  He was unable to build a home and farm because he doesn’t 
own 40 acres.  He thinks Chair Brown is right they need to find a way to allow the children 
who have grown up and want to stay here, the opportunity.   
 
Commissioner Hanson said she is for putting development on the hill.  Commissioner 
Vernon said that is what his concept would allow.  The landowner could take his density and 
do with it what he wants.  The smart farmer would put the density on a non-productive piece 
of property.  
 
Infrastructure needs were discussed.  Chair Brown said infrastructure will force the 
discussion to where it really matters, the County Council.  Eventually this valley will fill up 
with homes.  The question is how it will grow.  Will it be slowly over time or in the hands of 
the developer?  With the developer, the rules will change and it will get filled up twice as 
fast.  He proposes they zone it so the landowner controls the land and can choose their own 
destiny.  The protection would be the infrastructure and the resources.  
 
Commissioner Hanson said development is going to come.  They need to put into place the 
way to manage it the best they can.  She isn’t comfortable going out 1,000 feet with one-unit 
per acre.  She asked what about an AG-2, then an AG-3, up to an AG-10.  Commissioner 
Vernon said what he is proposing would allow the property owners to take the density and 
move it where they want it to be and it is better suited to their needs.  It gets it off the road so 
there aren’t driveways every quarter mile and allows for open space.   
 
Commissioner Clyde said the complicated part is getting there.  To get to a workable plan 
that prevents sprawl can be done, but it will take a long process to get them there.  If they 
allow the landowner to do what they want with their density, they will never get to an “Indian 
Hollow” type of development.  This type of development is complicated.  Commissioner 
Vernon disagreed.  He thought it would be a simple way to go.  Commissioner Clyde said the 
difference between the-owner-can-do-what-they-want kind of project and what-we-want-

you-to-do is an “Indian Hollow” type of development.  Commissioner Vernon said it isn’t the 
American way to tell people what they should do with their property. 
 
Chair Brown said zoning is different than design and layout.  You could push an “Indian 
Hollow” type of development due to factors outside of zoning.  Where they build is dealt 
with under a whole different mechanism.  He thinks if they give people more opportunity, 
they will be willing to discuss different designs and layout.  If there are only a couple of 
units, the landowner won’t be able to consider different designs.   
 
Commissioner Clyde said he thinks the shortest route for them to get to a majority consensus 
would be some iteration of ½ acre, 1 acre, 1 in 6, and 1 in 40.  This would make some kind of 
general planning sense.  It means the further away from the public right of way the property 
is, less service would be needed, there would be fewer cul-de-sacs, and so on.  That is how 
most zoning is done around the Country.  It is called graduated zoning.  He doesn’t think this 
is the best type of zoning, but it seems to be a compromise.    
 
The graduated zoning idea was discussed.  Administrator Barnes said to go from the one-acre 
zone to a 40-acre zone is too big of a jump.  Commissioner Vernon talked about a landowner 
being able to use his density on different pieces of his property.  Commissioner Clyde said 
they don’t want sprawl.  What they want is cluster.  They would need to write a Code to get 
from sprawl to cluster.  High density needs to have regulations.  Commissioner Wharton said 
Commissioner Vernon’s idea is a great concept, but it isn’t what usually happens.  Sprawl is 
what usually happens.    
 
Chair Brown said he thinks the residential zone has people nervous, but they have to 
remember this zone won’t be mapped.  There currently isn’t the infrastructure to support it.  
It would take the government to provide the infrastructure.  Commissioner Clyde said the 
chance of the government doing this isn’t likely, the chances of a big developer is.    
 
Commissioner Wharton said they need to see if they can come to terms on a strategy so they 
don’t banter back and forth forever.  They need to go back where the concept started, which 
was to create a zoning district that matched what is currently on the ground.  They were also 
concerned about the loss of some people’s rights.  Commissioner Clyde said the idea that 
they have a wrong that needs to be corrected is not something he buys into as a Planning 
Member.  That is a political issue.  Unless the Council tells them to correct this wrong, it is 
an idea they should drop.     
 
Commissioner Wharton suggested they should take it step by step.  They could begin by 
having a discussion about restoring the original HC zone back to the 250 foot boundary.  If 
that is agreed upon, they could take the existing HC zone and restore it back to 500 feet.  
That is a happy medium.  It isn’t a full restoration of the old HC zone.  The Commission 
could then look at the different sections of land to see if there are places that 1,000 feet 
would be appropriate.  All of this would be the AG-1 zone.

Commissioner Vernon said that doesn’t accomplish homes being clustered.  Director Putt 
said it could.  Commissioner Clyde said they could write the zoning ordinance so there is a 
benefit going to a clustered subdivision.  Chair Brown said he likes the AG-1 going back 500 
feet with the AG-6 behind it.   
 
The benefits of clustering and how to encourage people to cluster were discussed.  Chair 
Brown said clustering and open space should not be linked together as it has been in the past.  
It created problems.  The entire parcel had to be encumbered.  They should encourage 
clustering if possible, but they shouldn’t have to tie up the rest of the parcel into perpetuity.  
 
Commissioner Clyde said one unit per acre zoning is easy for anyone to build to.  They can 
get the needed infrastructure, but it isn’t pretty.  Commissioner Hanson said she doesn’t want 
to see a subdivision like Black Hawk in the Snyderville Basin come to the eastern side of the 
County.  Commissioner Ure said the Health Department has driven them that way.  They 
used to allow one unit per ½ acre, but that has increased to one unit per acre.  Commissioner 
Clyde said even on small lots, water can be pumped in and sewer out, but it is the Health 
Department that sets the standard.   
 
Commissioner Vernon said if a landowner sets a second tier behind their houses, because of 
the current configuration, they won’t be able to access the back lots.  Chair Brown said that 
would be the landowner’s problem.  If they want to utilize what is behind them they may 
have to knock down a house or people may have to start doing things together, like sharing 
driveways.  It will be up to the landowner to figure it out.  Commissioner Clyde said the 
further you move the density, the more complicated it gets.  The more complicated it gets, 
the longer time it takes.  
 
Chair Brown said he doesn’t see a lot of development occurring quickly because the 
infrastructure isn’t there to support it.  Commissioner Clyde said he sees almost none of this 
being infrastructure dependent.  Water flows to money and sewer flows uphill if there is 
enough cash.   
 
Chair Brown said he prefers the AG-1 zone to be 500 feet for the old HC zone, except 
Democrat Alley.  Otherwise, they should do away with the whole zone.  It is offensive to 
have some roads in and some roads out.  Either you have it or you don’t.  The Commission 
outlined the roads that were affected when the HC zone was altered.   
 
Commissioner Wharton said after the AG-1 zone comes the AG-6 and then AG-40 zones.  
Commissioner Vernon said that forces people to build in those back areas.  Commissioner  
Wharton said there would be nothing to tell people where to build.  A discussion ensued on 
ranchettes, the AG-6, and the AG-40 zones.  
 
Director Putt gave a couple of options for the Commission to think about.  He said the 
municipalities have the annexation declaration areas (ADA).  He thinks it is reasonable that 
municipal-type densities should be located by municipalities.  They should put the ADA 
areas on the maps.  Those areas should be one shade lighter than what the municipality 
allows.  The idea is to create the possibility for the municipalities to extend themselves out

and grow.  It would be a slightly lower density so the County doesn’t run the risk of 
competing.  This idea was briefly talked about. 
 
Director Putt said the other alternative is to go the other way.  They could consider 
maintaining the existing HC zone at 250 feet or perhaps extend it to 500.  Everything outside 
of that is the AG-6.  Let that be the incentive for annexation.   
 
Director Putt said he grew up in rural areas.  The development pattern of units hugging the 
road with other development behind it doesn’t bother him personally or professionally.  The 
problem with linear development, or string towns, drastically diminishes in the event that 
crossroads are created.      
 
Director Putt noted they already have a very linear pattern.  He said they can easily break the 
string and introduce cross sections through the subdivision process.  One idea is to keep the 
HC zone at 250 feet and reduce the minimum lot size down to ½ acre.  The thing about the ½ 
acre is that until you have a public waste water system, you can’t do anything with it.  As 
noted, the Health Department won’t support the ½ acre lot.   
 
The other option is to go to 500 feet and have the minimum lot size at .75 acre.  That would 
still allow for people to move the aggregated units wherever most suitable.  They would 
know there is an opportunity to rezone and opportunities for additional rights of way.  This 
may come up with some designs that are not linear.  He also fears what Commissioner 
Hanson fears.  He doesn’t want to see this be a Black Hawk Subdivision or a Southern 
California.  Chair Brown said the master transportation plan from Wanship to Henefer states 
this road is very adequate, even with heavy development.  Commissioner Clyde said it is the 
opposite if you change to the Kamas Valley.   
 
The transportation master plan for Kamas Valley was reviewed.  Director Putt asked if the 
zoning had not changed and the HC zone remained at 500, what they would see today.  
Commissioner Ure said there wouldn’t have been a lot of change unless they also take in the 
minor/major subdivision.  Commissioner Wharton said he personally would have had five 
less lots.  Commissioner Vernon would have more density and maybe his kids would still 
live here.   
 
Chair Brown asked if both ends of the County have to be looked at independently.  The 
Commission agreed that they do.  Commissioner Clyde said a good example of why this is, is 
the traffic study.  They are absolutely flipped.    
 
Commissioner Hanson made a motion to look at both ends of the County separately.  
Commissioner Ure seconded the motion.  All voted in favor.

Commissioner Wharton asked if they would consider mapping his proposal of graduated
