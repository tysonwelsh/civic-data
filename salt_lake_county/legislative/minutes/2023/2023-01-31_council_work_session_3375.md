2. CITIZEN PUBLIC INPUT
Ms. Katie Balakir , Policy Associate, Save Our Canyons, stated she was concerned 
about the idea of thinning the forest as solution to restore levels of the Great Salt Lake .  
This proposal was made in a recent letter written by Council Member Theodore to 
Governor Spencer Cox, Senate President Stuart Adams, and House Speaker Brad 
Wilson.  Ms. Balakir argued there is no evidence that thinning the forest can restore lake 
levels.  While it is true the forests are overgrown, painting them as culprit to the lake crisis 
is misinformed and dangerous.  She suggested the County give funding to stewardship 
efforts.

- SB 33 –Disaster Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill moves money from a restricted account, making it easier for

Ms. Trevino stated this was brought forward by the Health Department to address the

Mr. Tom Hudachko , Strategic Partnerships and Special Projects Manager, Health

the harm reduction field by clarifying that fentanyl test strips are not considered to be drug 
paraphernalia.

Control Officers Association is concerned it could interfere with local animal control

Ms. Trevino stated over the years, there have been multiple bills addressing problems

put changes in the Code.

Council Member Winder Newton  stated she asked Council Member 
Theodore to present regarding a letter she previously sent to Governor 
Spencer Cox, Senate President Stuart Adams, and House Speaker Brad 
Wilson.  She has been receiving a lot of follow -up questions resulting from 
the letter.
Council Member Theodore  stated she did not anticipate her letter would 
get so much traction so quickly.  Forest health and land management issues 
affect her district.  With her background in biology, these issues are always 
on her mind.  The State has been working on improving watershed 
management, enhancing water conservation, and optimizing agricultural tools .  
These efforts, while good, are costly and might not make the needed impact .   
She has learned from experts and research that healthy forest management is 
the solution.  Many forests are overgrown and unhealthy.  Nonnative noxious 
trees are consuming trillions of gallons of water that would otherwise flow 
downstream to the Great Salt Lake.  Overgrown forests also create a fire 
hazard.  Her data shows that with proper forest management, the Great Salt 
Lake should be well on its way to sustainable water levels in five years.  The 
Council voted on action last year to save the Great Salt Lake, and she wanted 
to offer it the opportunity to join with her in urging the Governor and 
Legislature to explore this option during legislative session. 
Mr. John Bennett , Senior Policy Advisor, Council Office, stated though he 
does not consider himself to be an expert, he did spend seventeen years 
managing the State ’s Open Space Fund.  He reviewed a document entitled 
Fact Sheet - Great Salt Lake.  He discussed how water can be imported and 
exported through produce; alfalfa farming in Utah; different types of trees 
and their effect on the watershed; wildfires and their effect on the watershed; 
conservation of interior water, exterior water, agricultural water; thinning the 
forest to preserve water absorption; and increasing the aspen population.  He 
Page 12 of 17

emphasized it should not be an “either/or” approach, but an “all of the 
above” approach.  
Mayor Wilson  stated she appreciated any Council member ’s interest in 
reaching out independently, but the letter did create confusion for her 
because she did not know about it beforehand.  The Forest Service is in the 
driver’s seat on this issue.  There are established mechanisms in Salt Lake 
County to weigh in on land use in the canyons.  Amending the plan would be 
quite a bit of work.
