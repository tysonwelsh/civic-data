Fiscal Year 2022 Budget Amendment — Use of Contingency Funds and
Water Capital Improvement Plan Amendments

Comptroller Stout reported City Administration is proposing several amendments to the Fiscal Year (FY) 2022 budget.
The changes include the use of contingency money to fund increases to Council wages in accordance with changes approved in
December 2021, increases to Engineering wages to fund a temporary full-time position, funding to cover the Dino Park fund
deficit as explained with the 2021 financial audit, and to provide additional funding to the 4" Street Ball Fields Capital
Improvement Plan (CIP) project and the Lorin Farr Pool improvement CIP project. In addition, the proposed amendments transfer
CIP funding from two Water Utility CIP projects to provide additional funding for the 36” Transmission Line Study project and
filter plant equipment.

Council member Hyer asked if there are plans to convert the temporary position to a permanent position. Ms. Stout stated
it is her understanding that the position is temporary and will only be funded until July; if Administration feels it necessary to
convert the position to permanent, they will need to make that request in the budget.

In response to a question from Council member White, Public Services Director Lowder briefly discussed the scope of
the project at 4" Street Ball Fields in order to accommodate a Triple Crown tournament.

Chair Nadolski asked if the Lorin Farr pool project will help to address long-term aging issues at the facility. Mr. Lowder
stated the seams at the pool have been treated with a pliable epoxy. The project referenced by Ms. Stout will help to improve
safety of the pool by preventing slipping issue. Chair Nadolski inquired as to the long-term prognosis for the pool. Mr. Lowder
stated it is no secret that the pool is old — it was constructed in the 1930s — and the City is trying to keep it operational and in good
repair; this can be costly, but he believes the facility will be viable for at least another five years.

Proposed Ogden Onboard Station Area Plan and Plan Amendment

Deputy Planning Manager Brierley summarized a proposal that the City Council formally adopt the Ogden Onboard
Transit-Oriented Vision and Implementation Strategy, or the Ogden Onboard Plan, and a subsequent amendment updating the
station area plan for the(UTA) Union Station campus and the intermodal hub area. The plan and amendments have been adopted
by the Utah Transit Authority Board of Trustees but have not been formally adopted by Ogden City. The Ogden Onboard Plan is
meant to provide direction on land use changes that can be made along the Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) corridor as a way to catalyze
transit-oriented development in key areas and to maximize the usage of the BRT system. He discussed the goals of the original
Plan and the general concept for the BRT route, after which he noted that the amendments include revisions to the priority station
urban center concept and expansion of the scope of the Intermodal Hub to include the Union Station campus as an integrated
development. He presented an updated concept and density plan for the Ogden Station, after which he concluded the Planning
Commission voted to recommend approval of the Plan amendment.

Council member Hyer asked when the Planning Commission reviewed this application, to which Mr. Brierly answered
February of this year. Council member Hyer stated there was a discussion during the most recent Weber Area Council of
Governments (WACOG) meeting regarding transportation-oriented developments and compliance with House Bill (HB) 462; he
asked if those issues have been evaluated by City staff and UTA. Council Deputy Director Symes indicated there may be some
minor changes to the Plans to ensure compliance with HB 462. Mr. Brierley agreed and noted that UTA has asked for Council
action at this time so that they can proceed with a request for proposal (RFP) for the BRT project.

Proposed Rezone — 1845 Jackson Avenue

Deputy Planning Manager Brierley explained the Weber-Morgan Children’s Justice Center is requesting to rezone a
portion of property at 1845 Jackson Avenue from R-1-5 (Single Family) and O-1 (Open Space) to C-2 (Community Commercial).
The purpose of the rezone is to build a Children’s Justice Center on the property. The Planning Commission recommended
approval of the request, but they recommended adding a conditional overlay to the C-2 zone, which would limit the property to a
professional office for child counseling services, limit the size of the facility, and set design standards consistent with the East
Central District. The Planning Commission also recommends that the building style must look residential.

The Planning Commission recommended approval of the rezone with a vote of 6-1. The commissioner who voting nay
did so because of a concern for lost open space and recreational opportunities caused by the zone change. He also found the
request to not be consistent with the East Central Community Plan. It is important to note that the East Community Plan shows
this site as part of a larger recreational complex, as can be seen with the sports fields to the west. The community plan shows
recreation concept drawings for the school district’s property, and an outdoor aquatic center is shown on this specific piece of
land. The Planning Commission discussed whether or not to continue to plan for the site to be used for open space and recreation,
but they ultimately voted in favor of the zone change.

Along with a favorable recommendation, the Planning Commission is recommending the following conditions for the

rezone:
1. A conditional overlay to limit development to a professional office for child counseling services.
2. The conditional overlay would also:

a. Limit the size of the facility to a maximum of 15,000 square feet;
b. Limit the height of the building to a maximum of 2.5 stories, 35 feet;
C. The main building must look residential in nature and meet the design standards of single-family

dwellings in the East Central District.

Discussion among the Council, Mr. Brierley, and Rod Layton, Executive Director of the Children’s Justice Center (CJC),
centered on the design for the CJC facility; the size of the land upon which the facility will be located; CJC operations and
services provided; safety and security of the facility; ownership of the property and property tax implications of said ownership;
access to the site; and development potential for surrounding properties. The Council concluded to move the application to a
future meeting for action on the zone change.

Proposed Rezone — 149 West 3175 South
Deputy Planning Manager Brierley reported a petition has been filed to rezone property on the northwest corner of 32nd
Street and Wall Avenue from M-2 to C-3 for the purpose of expanding a car dealership. The property is located to the north of a
car dealership, but the dealership cannot extend into the property until it is rezoned to C-3. This lot is part of a larger piece of
property that extends to the southeast, and since only a portion of the property is requested to be rezoned, the property would need
to be subdivided before it can be rezoned and sold. The petitioner is also requesting to rezone a portion of the same property from
CP-3 to M-2 to match the zoning on the southwest end of the property. This property is currently split by two different zones,
which is typically not a good practice. By rezoning portions of the property and subdividing the new C-3 portion, this property
will no longer be split by two different zones, and the car dealership will be able to expand to the north. The Planning Commission
recommended approval and cited consistency with the Jefferson Community Plan and the Wall Avenue Corridor Plan in their
findings. In addition to their favorable recommendation, the Planning Commission recommends the following conditions be met:
1. The petitioner must complete a subdivision plat process to split the property so that the new property line

follows the new zoning boundary line.

Boulevard and Adams Avenue; and
Proposed Rezone — 101 and 131 Adams Avenue

Senior Planner Simpson explained a developer is requesting to vacate a portion of the Ist Street right-of-way to make
room fora large development at 120 Washington Boulevard. The portion requested to be vacated is approximately 15,793 square
feet. This right-of-way was originally preserved to allow a corridor for a future road to be connected between Adams Avenue and
Washington Boulevard, but it was never developed. It is important to note that a portion of Ist Street has already been vacated on
the 101 Adams Avenue property. This is not a complete right-of-way, and it would be unfeasible to develop a connecting road on
this remaining portion. If the right-of-way is vacated, the developer would still need to obtain ownership of the land before it
could be developed. Vacating the right-of-way simply removes the City’s ability to access and develop a road on the property and
clears the way for alternative development to take place on the land. The Planning Commission unanimously recommended
approving the vacation. They felt that there was good cause to vacate, and that no public interest nor person would be harmed by
the action. The Planning Commission also determined that this vacation and the subsequent rezoning of the property would be
consistent with the Hillcrest/Bonneville Community Plan and would aide in the development of a commercial node at 120
Washington Boulevard.

Mr. Simpson then discussed the rezone application; the properties in question are located to the north and east of the old
Wangsgard’s grocery store at 120 Washington Boulevard. Each of the properties has an R-1-6 designation, but the developer is
requesting to rezone a total of approximately .842 acres to C-3 so that they may be incorporated into a larger multi-family/mixed-
use development. Below is a description of the rezone request on each property:

e 101 Adams Avenue: This is a .5-acre vacant lot. The request is to rezone .308-acres of the rear portion of the
lot. This parcel is owned by the petitioner.

e 131 Adams Avenue: This is a .34-acre lot with a home that faces Adams Avenue. The request is to rezone .171
acres of the back yard. This parcel is not owned by the petitioner.

e 1 Street Right-of-Way: The request is to rezone a .363-acre portion of the right-of-way between Washington
Boulevard and Adams Avenue. This is a vacant piece of land. Part of the right-of-way is owned by the
petitioner, and the other part is owned by an adjacent property owner. The right-of-way is owned by the City.

Relative to 101 and 131 Adams Avenue, it is important to note that if the rear portions of these lots are rezoned, then the
rezoned portion would need to be subdivided out and combined with the rest of the C-3 properties. 131 Adams Avenue has a
home on it, but this rezone/subdivision will not affect the home. 101 Adams Avenue is a vacant lot, but there will be sufficient
land to build a single home on the property even after the rezone and subdivision take place. The Planning Commission
recommended approval of the rezone. The Planning Commission also recommended the following conditions:

1. The petitioner obtains approval and completes the street vacation process for I*' Street.

2. The petitioner obtains approval and completes a subdivision plat process creating legal lots for 101 Adams
Avenue and 131 Adams Avenue, joining the vacated portion of the Ist Street right-of-way, 101 Adams Avenue,
and 131 Adams Avenue with other land in the C-3 zone.

3. Minimum R-1-6 setbacks and rear yard coverage standards be maintained for the existing home and garage at
131 Adams Avenue (land serial number 12-058-0016).

4. Minimum 100° lot depths be maintained for the eastern portion of 101 Adams Avenue (land serial number 12-
058-0013) and 131 Adams Avenue (land serial number 12-058-0016).

The Hillcrest/Bonneville Community Plan states the following objective for Washington Boulevard: “Where appropriate,
higher density residential development should be developed by combining the small lot parcels and develop the old strip
commercial and residential sites and buildings”. The Planning Commission found this petition to be supported by the
Hillcrest/Bonneville Community Plan.

General discussion among the Council and Planning staff centered on the ratio of residential and commercial uses on the
subject properties.

Council Deputy Director Symes noted that action on these applications is scheduled for April 19, 2022 if no objections
