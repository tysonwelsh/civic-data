Planner Lisa Halversen reported that the applicant is requesting a Conditional Use Permit
to provide short-term care for disabled individuals in her home. Ms. Jenson reports that she
is a nurse who has provided this type of care for her sister and she believes her skills,
training, and licensing will allow her to provide a needed service to the community. The
applicant requested permission to provide care for no more than three people per day with
the assistance of one nonresident employee who will be either part-time or full-time. Clients
will be dropped off and picked up at the home, but there will be no other traffic.

The 0.29-acre property is located in Summerfield Phase 3 Subdivision and is zoned R-4. All
neighboring properties have similar zoning. Photographs of the home and the parking plan
were presented, indicating a three-car garage and extra-wide driveway. A designated area
of the driveway will be used for client drop-offs and pick-ups. The employee will park off the
street in the driveway.

The Site Plan was reviewed, indicating one bedroom and bathroom solely for business use,
in addition to some rooms like the kitchen and living room that will be shared for business
and personal use. Approximately 25% of the main floor will be solely for business use.

Ms. Halversen noted that because the applicant intends to care for clients overnight, a
Conditional Use Permit is required. The Planning Commission has the authority to grant
those extended hours. Notice was mailed to property owners within 300 feet of the subject
property, and one response was received in support of the application. Staff recommended
approval with conditions as outlined in the Staff Report dated March 27, 2025.

Chair Park asked if there were similar businesses in Riverton or neighboring cities.
Ms. Halversen stated that there are no similar Home Occupations in the City. It is somewhat
similar to a residential group home, which has state licensing requirements but no noticing
requirements, and a daycare or preschool that does have noticing and Planning Commission
approval requirements.

In response to a question from Commissioner Cluff, Ms. Halversen reported that the Utah
Department of Health and Human Services has oversight over the business. They will
perform inspections, and Ms. Jenson will be required to provide proof of compliance prior to
obtaining a Business License from the City.

The applicant, Annjanette Jenson stated that she has been a resident of Riverton for 24
years and a nurse for the same amount of time. Growing up, her family could never go
anywhere together because someone always had to stay with her sister. She has had
guardianship of her sister for the past five years. There are no respite care facilities that can
help on weekends except large care homes and she has never felt comfortable leaving her
sister in that type of facility. She would like to help someone else have time away with their
family, and the business will also allow her to save money to be able to have time away in
the future as well. In response to a question from Chair Park, Ms. Jenson stated that she
has spoken with a lot of people who indicated that they do not have anywhere to leave their
loved ones for brief periods. The State allows a maximum length of stay of 14 days but she
likely would not take someone for that long of a period.

stated that she would have to be very comfortable with the client being in her home with her
family but there are no conditions that she could not manage. There will be an application
and interview process as well as a short initial visit prior to any overnight stays.

Commissioner Matheson stated that it will be a wonderful addition to the community. Most
people have had a loved one who would have benefited from this type of facility. He asked
if she would take severely disabled or disruptive clients. Ms. Jenson stated that she has
been a nurse for 24 years and is very skilled at moving people. Bedridden clients would not
be a concern. Noise or other issues would be evaluated during the application process. If
she were to take more than one overnight client, they would have to be from the same family.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich, presented the Staff Report and indicated that the following
changes had been made in response to recommendations made at the last Planning
Commission meeting:

       1.     Exterior Building materials count increased from four to five.

       2.     Exterior Colors count increased from four to five.

       3.     All references to “City” were changed to “Land Use Authority” per City Attorney.

       4.     The requirement for three feet of wainscot was removed.

       5.     Glass was added as an exterior building material and limited to 30% of any
              building side.

       6.     Architectural metal accents were clarified to not exceed 10% of a building side.

Mr. Prestwich stated that with the above changes, Burger King would have met all
requirements. The House of Vapes could be denied based on two criteria. The Utah
18Collective school would probably meet requirements for exterior building materials and
could likely be approved as-is. The Newman office building could also be approved based
on the new criteria.

In response to a question from Commissioner Matheson, Mr. Prestwich clarified that CMU
stands for Concrete Masonry Unit, which is usually a split-faced concrete block. It is
currently only allowed in a few places in the City, and uncolored or unfinished CMU would
be prohibited by the new standards.

Commissioner Knudsen stated that the American First Credit Union building has more than
30% glass, and the Commissioners agreed that they liked that look. He suggested that the
allowed percentage of glass be increased. He also asked for clarification on the architectural
metal accents. Mr. Prestwich stated that if all limitations on glass were removed, a building
could be 75% glass. The specific percentage allowed was at the discretion of the Planning
Commission and City Council.

In response to a comment by Chair Park, Mr. Prestwich stated that the American First Credit
Union has approximately 30% glass; the building also has 30% wainscot, so the glass does
not go all the way to the ground. He noted that the open feel is very popular on the front
and some sides of buildings. Commissioner Matheson was in favor of increasing the
percentage to 50%.

Commissioner Cluff asked if it was purely a visual preference or if there were other reasons
to limit the amount of glass. Mr. Prestwich stated that the previous ordinance was geared
toward maintaining a turn-of-the-century look, which included tall, narrow windows. He did
not see an issue with increasing the percentage. Commissioner Cluff was comfortable not
including a limit.

Commissioner Keele was in favor of allowing up to 50%.
The Commission agreed that the maximum percentage of glass per side would be increased
to 50%.

Accent metal was discussed next and it was noted that the Commission previously
discussed removing the maximum of 10% per building side. Mr. Prestwich indicated that
metal panels that look like wood, brick, or stone are becoming more popular due to their
ease of shipping and installation, as well as flat, colored metal that is used in place of stucco.
Chair Park stated that cladding a building in metal would make it a primary material, not an
accent. Mr. Prestwich suggested moving the definition of accent metal from the Prohibited
Materials section and clarifying that metal panels made to look like other materials are not
considered accents.

Commissioner Cluff was comfortable allowing 20% accent metal. Commissioner Matheson
asked if it would be appropriate to leave it at the Planning Commission’s discretion.
Commissioner Knudsen agreed with clarifying the definition and liked the idea of leaving the
determination to the Land Use Authority. Commissioner Rushton stated that the percentage
should be clearly defined to allow Staff to better guide developers and architects. After
further discussion, it was decided that accent metal should be limited to 20% per building

rusted or otherwise distressed-look metal.”

              PROPOSING CHANGES TO THE LAND USE AND DEVELOPMENT CODE
              FOR MULTI-FAMILY PARCEL SIZE, FOUND IN SECTIONS 18.45.050,
              18.50.050, 18.53.050, 18.55.050, 18.57.050, AND 18.60.050.

Mr. Prestwich presented the Staff Report and stated that the proposed changes were not
related to an application and there were no time constraints for approval of the amendments.
He reported that the process was similar to rezoning so the Planning Commission would
make a recommendation to the City Council and the Council would make the final decision.

The Zoning and General Plan Maps were reviewed. Each current multi-family housing zone
has a minimum required area. For example, the owner of a three-acre parcel cannot apply
to rezone the property RM-8 because five acres are required. The Planning Commission
and City Council could not even consider whether eight units per acre would be an
appropriate use for the parcel. That made sense in the past when there were many large
parcels available, but recently developers have been unable to build in the areas set aside
for multi-family housing because they have not been able to meet the acreage requirements.

Another issue is that the area requirements are different for each zone, and it is difficult to
keep track of which zone someone needs to apply for and how much acreage is required.
Staff proposes standardizing the acreage requirements across more zones and lowering the
requirement in some zones.

The affected zones are RM-6, RM-8, RM-8-D, RM-14, RM-14-D, and RM-18. At the
Council’s direction, RM-6 was previously changed to only allow detached units, and there
are currently four RM-6 developments in Riverton. It is now a detached, small-lot zone.
Twin homes can be allowed on a limited basis. All other affected zones are for townhomes
and condominiums.

Mr. Prestwich reviewed the current required lot size for the zones as follows:

   •   RM-6: 1.5 acres minimum, 15 acres maximum;
   •   RM-8: 5 acres minimum, 10 acres maximum;
   •   RM-8-D: 2 acres minimum;
   •   RM-14: 5 acres minimum;
   •   RM-14-D: 2 acres minimum; and
   •   RM-18: 10 acres minimum, 20 acres maximum.

Staff recommended removing all maximums and changing all minimums to two acres except
RM-18, which would have a five-acre minimum. There are only a few areas of the City where
an RM-18 project would be allowed even with the reduced minimum, including areas near
2700 West and 13400 South and on 4000 West between 12600 South and 11800 South.
Those areas are currently designated by the General Plan for single-family homes on one-
third and one-quarter acre lots.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the amendments would address concerns expressed by both
the City’s elected officials and the development community, but the specific requirements
could be modified at the Planning Commission’s discretion. Staff believes that two acres is
the proper minimum. People often ask about rezoning three-acre and two-acre parcels for
higher density, but there are no longer any requests for five-acre parcels.

Mr. Prestwich discussed how currently zoning prohibits the development or redevelopment
of areas of the City, including near Redwood Road and 1300 West north of 12600 South.
The General Plan indicates that the area should be developed with multi-family housing of
five to eight units per acre, and the RM-8 zone would be the rational choice for this
designation. However, it is outside of the downtown zone, so a property owner could not
currently apply to rezone to RM-8 unless they had five acres. Part of the area is currently
zoned commercial and part is zoned residential. Even if a developer were to acquire three
separate parcels in the area, it would not meet the five-acre minimum.

Another area near 2700 West is not designated multi-family by the General Plan, but people
have asked about developing the area in the past. It currently has older houses and passive
uses and is an underutilized area. Mr. Prestwich reported that there are also not five acres
available in this area.

Another area that is currently zoned commercial had approval for an 80-foot office building
that was never developed. A developer who expressed interested in developing multi-family
on the commercial property was unable to move forward because they could not meet the
minimum acreage requirement. It would have required him to purchase several adjacent
one-half-acre residential lots with existing homes.

Mr. Prestwich referred to a development in Sandy that the City Council has discussed as a
product that may work in Riverton. It has townhomes with a central driveway, guest parking,
and rear-loading garages. However, the development is on 1.1 acres, so it would not be
allowed even with the proposed changes. Staff recommended decreasing the minimums to
two acres as a first step. If the market is still unable to produce anything with that change,
the Planning Commission and City Council could consider lowering it further in one to two
years.

The public hearing was noticed on the City website and no comments were received.
However, they have had multiple comments from developers and landowners over the years
who were unable to assemble enough property to meet the current minimums.

      D.     “TEMPORARY COMMERCIAL USES,” PLZ-25-5003, RIVERTON CITY IS
             PROPOSING CHANGES TO THE LAND USE AND DEVELOPMENT CODE
             RELATING TO TEMPORARY COMMERCIAL USES, FOUND IN SECTION
             18.135.200.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the proposed changes to Temporary Commercial Uses were
being introduced in response to discussions between Mayor Staggs and the City Council
regarding uses like Christmas tree lots and fireworks stands. Current City Code allows those
uses on commercial properties with restrictions. Because can present challenges in the
allowed areas and compete with existing businesses, they are only allowed as a temporary
uses.

The current Code states that temporary commercial uses may only be conducted on a
commercially-zoned parcel except as described in the Home Occupation Ordinance, which
allows outdoor seasonal retail sales such as Christmas trees, fireworks, pumpkin patches,
haunted houses, and corn mazes only when produced entirely on the site. If someone grows
Christmas trees or pumpkins onsite, for example, that would be an allowed Home
Occupation.
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                               11
March 27, 2025



If the City were to expand temporary commercial uses to residential properties, the following
limitations were proposed:

   •   Limit temporary commercial uses to vacant/undeveloped residential properties.

   •   Limit temporary commercial uses to residential properties which have direct access
       to a major road, as defined in Riverton City Code.

   •   Require a Conditional Use Permit for any temporary commercial use on a residential
       property.

   •   Limit allowed temporary commercial uses on residential properties to uses directly
       associated with holiday sales, namely Christmas tree lots, fireworks stands, and
       pumpkin patches.

Mr. Prestwich indicated that the purpose of the amendment would be to allow traditional
types of holiday sales that were previously conducted on vacant commercial lots. A potential
concern that cannot be addressed in the City Code but should be considered is that
temporary conditions have a way of becoming permanent. Ideas to ensure that the uses do
not become permanent were included in the proposed amendment. The full text of the
Amended Code was then reviewed.

18.135.200 Temporary Commercial Uses

       1.    Temporary commercial uses shall be defined as commercial uses which do
             not exceed 60 days in duration and which do not require permanent structures
             or improvements which are not already established with an approved
             permanent use. Such uses may include but are not limited to, shaved ice
             stands, Christmas tree lots, and fireworks stands. A temporary commercial
             use may only be conducted on a commercially zoned parcel or on
             vacant/undeveloped residential property, except as described in RCC
             18.190.090(16). Temporary commercial uses shall not cause or create a
             nuisance or hazard and shall conform to all requirements of this title. All trash
             will be removed and the property will be restored to a clean condition after the
             temporary commercial use has been terminated.

       2.    Temporary commercial uses can be approved by the Planning Department.
             At the discretion of the planning manager, temporary commercial uses may be
             referred to the Planning Commission as conditional temporary commercial
             uses. In such cases, the applicant will be responsible for the noticing fees. A
             Conditional Use Permit approved by the Planning Commission is required for
             a proposed temporary commercial use on a residential property.

      3.     Temporary commercial uses on residential properties are limited to property
             which has frontage on and direct access to an arterial or major collector road,
             as defined by Riverton City. Temporary commercial uses on residential
             property are limited to uses directly related to holiday sales, namely Christmas
             tree lots, fireworks stands, and pumpkin patch/sales.

      4.     Temporary commercial uses shall obtain and/or provide the following:

             a. A Riverton City Business License for commercial uses.
             b. Property owner’s authorization.

Chair Park asked what areas of the City the amendment would impact. Mr. Prestwich stated

In response to a question from Commissioner Cluff, Mr. Prestwich stated that Staff did not

In response to a question from Commissioner Rushton, Mr. Prestwich confirmed that ice

zones. No changes were proposed to temporary uses in commercial zones.
