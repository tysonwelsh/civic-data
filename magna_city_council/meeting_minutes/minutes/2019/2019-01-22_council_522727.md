Reports from Community Stakeholders

Magna in Motion

               Keri Duckworth stated Magna in Motion is doing an online health challenge
January 8 through March 19. Then, it will be doing a three-month health challenge in the spring,
and will focus on nutrition the first month, activity the second month, and weight loss the third
month. The purpose is to get people thinking about health, not just weight loss, and to reach a
broader group of people. Magna in Motion is also doing a promotion to encourage new people.
If a member brings two new people to the April challenge, they get in for free. Then, if a new
person refers two people, they can participate in May for free.

               Council Member Hull asked if it was too late to start the online challenge.

             Ms. Duckworth stated no. Individuals just need to enter their weight every
Tuesday at magnainmotion@gmail.com.

Unified Fire Authority (UFA)

                Council Member Prokopis stated fire crews responded to a fire at the old barn
on the White property on Christmas Eve, but the fire had destroyed the barn by the time they
arrived. Investigators are still looking into the cause of the fire. Two years ago, the State audited
the UFA and identified 126 deficiencies. All of those findings have now been rectified. Many
internal changes were made with personnel and policy, i.e. accounting practices, transparency.
A capital replacement plan was also implemented, whereby the UFA purchased new vehicles and
equipment. Recently, it hired 29 new individuals who start training February 4th, and promoted
four individuals to captain and one to an engineer.

               Council Member Ferguson stated the UFA’s governance board will be voting on
a hybrid voting model in February. The suggestion is one vote per entity, except when the board
could not come to a consensus on the budget, and that would be a weighted vote by population
based on the state recommended census.

Fee Waiver Request

Application #30478 – Ricky Deloy Jenkins is requesting a fee waiver for a storm drain impact fee
of $20,044.36, a final engineering review fee of $1,066.50, a bond processing fee of $100.00, and
an engineering bond of $17,775.00 for the Jackass Flats Subdivision, located at 2109 South 7600
West.

               Greg Schulz reviewed the request stating the only piece that is waivable is the
storm drain impact fee. The other fees are tied to the engineering bond and are not waivable
under State law. A bond is held until infrastructure improvements are installed. Once they are
installed and accepted by the metro township, the money invested in the bond reverts back to the
property owner. The County’s staff recommended waiving the storm drain impact fee. In 2019,
going forward, that fee will be eliminated from the County’s consolidated fee schedule.

               Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Ferguson, moved to waive
the storm drain impact fee only on Application #30478. The motion passed unanimously.
