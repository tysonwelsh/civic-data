CANYON ENHANCEMENTS (0:19:11)
The Council discussed gravel pits. Mr. Magness noted that one of the best tools is rezoning; the
Council could also explore budgetary options. Councilor Katrice MacKay asked who owned the
gravel pit property. Dixon Holmes, Deputy CAO, explained that there were three gravel pits in
Provo Canyon; the City owned a piece of ground located below the shooting range.

Councilor George Handley raised a potential legislative issue the Council may need to address.
He noted that the Critical Hillside Overlay Zone has a limited disturbance provision. The process
for rehabilitating a gravel pit would likely violate that particular provision of the law; this is
something the Council may need to address in the future. Councilor Bill Fillmore suggested a
feasibility study may be a good way to learn more about the possibilities for the gravel pit
property. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked whether the gravel pits issue was written into the
scope of the foothills and canyons plan. She also asked to add Slate Canyon to this list. Mr.
Magness noted that Slate Canyon was included in the parks and recreation priorities list. Mr.
Shipley felt comfortable deferring this item to the supplementary plans to the general plan, which
would likely include some short-term recommendations. Mr. Handley had questions about the
lifespan of a gravel pit and when it is exhausted. Mr. Fillmore asked Dave Decker, Public Works
Director, if the city still needs the gravel pit. Mr. Decker said the site is not currently used as a
gravel pit, however it is sometimes used to store materials, but it is not really utilized or needed.
Mr. Magness noted the Council could also look at different incentives regarding gravel pits.

Mayor Michelle Kaufusi and Mr. Holmes shared an update on the extension of the Provo River
Trail through Provo Canyon up to Wasatch County.

COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT (0:30:48)
One of the main items in this section is to make sure the neighborhood program functions for all
neighborhoods and citizens. Mr. Magness indicated that revisions to the neighborhood program
were in progress. Another item on the list was a diversity and inclusion committee. Mr. Magness
noted this was included in the draft general plan, but the Council could always act on this sooner
as a legislative item. Councilor David Shipley thought this would be good to do as a legislative
item. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked the Council what they envisioned. She saw it as a
more permanent advisory body that could make recommendations. Councilor George Handley
said there had been lots of concern in summer 2020 about diversity and representation in the
community. Mayor Kaufusi had talked about creating a neighborhood committee of diverse
leaders in the community at one point. Mr. Handley had several ideas for the committee and its
possible objective; it could examine the practices of the city itself as an organization, or it could
have an outward focus on the community. Mr. Handley also noted that research has shown that
decision-making is better when it involves a more diverse group of voices (both in business and
government contexts). Mr. Magness indicated he attended a recent conference and made some
contacts at some different universities which may be helpful resources for ideas that could work
for Provo. He indicated that staff could work on a proposal to bring back to the Council. Mr.
Shipley thought an ad hoc committee might be helpful in evaluating the proposals. Another item
on the list for this priority was creating unity/social capital; Mr. Magness indicated this would
follow from the diversity and inclusion committee and neighborhood program.

ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT (0:40:11)
Councilors shared comments about making the downtown area more pedestrian-friendly. This
was an item which could be addressed by the budget. Dave Decker, Public Works Director,
referenced the recommendations from the Transportation and Mobility Advisory Committee,
which included some temporary adjustments which could serve as a beta-test for more
permanent measures. Councilors were supportive of this effort.

Councilor Bill Fillmore had suggested working with the BYU incubator for startups. Mr.
Fillmore shared some of the opportunities the city could have to attract some of these startup
incubators. Councilor George Handley referenced comments from developers who have
indicated that certain zoning designations would make development projects more feasible.

Regarding westside commercial development, Mr. Magness noted there was $1 million set aside
in the Legacy CIP for recruiting a west side grocer. Councilor Travis Hoban asked if the city was
doing any market analysis; Wayne Parker, CAO, said the city had contracted with a firm to do
some market analysis on the west side and at the mall. This data is helpful to share in proactive
ways with developers, grocers, and retailers. Redevelopment of the Provo Towne Center mall
was important to all the Councilors. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth had a rough draft of an intent
statement emphasizing commercial and retail, true mixed-use, in redevelopment of the mall. Mr.
Fillmore also emphasized the role of incentives and the Redevelopment Agency.

GOVERNMENTAL ADMINISTRATION (1:10:06)
Councilor Shannon Ellsworth shared a scholarship that Orem City does for women staff to
promote opportunities to advance more women to management and leadership positions.
Councilor Katrice MacKay noted that right now, more women graduate from college than men.
Ms. Ellsworth noted that cities provide public services and women’s voices often aren’t
represented even though women pay taxes. There is a tremendous benefits in corporations and
governments when women are parts of conversations.

Another item on this list was keeping Provo competitive in the labor market—similar discussions
are taking place around the state in city government. Councilor Travis Hoban expressed
frustration after an interaction with HR that he did not feel was very helpful in outlining what the
Council could do. The Administration indicated this would be an important conversation to
inform budgeting decisions. Councilors were supportive of these efforts to keep Provo’s
compensation competitive and to prevent loss of talent to other organizations.

Another priority was ensuring fees for services are keeping up with inflation. Councilor David
Shipley thought it was concerning that in spite of city revenues increasing, the general fund
balance was decreasing. He felt that cyber-security and a competitive labor market were really
important initiatives to fund—both of these issues represent risks to the city and how it operates.

Councilor Shannon Ellsworth submitted “use key performance indicators for budget process” as
another priority. She noted the Council has been using the General Plan to analyze the budget,
but she did not feel this was the most relevant or helpful process. She suggested picking several
KPIs for each department that are measurable and matter to constituents, then use them as a tool
for analysis. She thought this would make the budget analysis more germane to the departments.
Councilor Bill Fillmore also supported this idea. Wayne Parker, CAO, indicated the City
Administration would support this as well and could help to collaborate with the Council on it.

Ms. Ellsworth had also submitted a priority to retain a lobbyist. She noted Deputy Mayor Isaac
Paxman did some lobbying during the session and that the Mayor’s office also contracts a
lobbyist. She wondered if the Council and Mayor are aligned to use the same lobbyist, or would
it be beneficial to the Council to retain an independent lobbyist. Mr. Paxman shared details on
the lobbyist, Paul Rogers, who was instrumental in securing state funds for the Provo Airport.
Mr. Parker also noted that the cost of the lobbyist fluctuates but typically the lobbyist works on

HOUSING (1:25:15)
Single-family housing priority issues submitted by Councilors include: increase housing stock,
neighborhood protections, increasing homeownership, decreasing the number of rentals, and
controlling short-term rentals. Councilor Katrice MacKay shared an example of what she meant
by neighborhood protections—in historic neighborhoods like Dixon, homes are often sold and
then sit empty for long periods of time as investors buy up properties in a given area to
redevelop. This creates a predatory situation which limits housing available to first-time or
permanent resident homebuyers. Mr. Magness said he could draft a memo on suggestions for
neighborhood protections. Ms. MacKay also had ideas to share, including some ideas from when
John Curtis was mayor. Councilor Bill Fillmore suggested zoning enforcement also had a role.

Councilor George Handley commented that the city still does not have a housing inventory. Staff
indicated that one staff member in Community and Neighborhood Services was working on this,
but it would take a long time at that rate. Mr. Handley suggested the Council fund a formal
housing audit. He noted many actionable parts of the general plan relied on this data. Councilor
Travis Hoban agreed that this was a keystone task or objective that will then impact so many
other things; he agreed with prioritizing this.

Councilor Rachel Whipple was interested in expanding opportunities for city employees to buy
houses; she noted this could also help with retention. Melia Dayley, Policy Analyst, shared that
several years ago, Representative Steve Waldrip helped run a bill for a pilot program in Weber
County for workforce housing and it was later expanded to include schoolteachers. Mr. Magness
noted there were other similar programs throughout the country.

PARKS AND RECREATION (1:33:08)
The Regional Sports Park is slated to be done in 2026. For the park to be finished by 2024, there
is a gap in funding of $8 million to accomplish it on that timeline. Councilors were interested in
the economic impact of finishing the park early. Wayne Parker, CAO, noted that part of the
challenge was the utility infrastructure in some of the later CIP projects which has a direct
impact on the project. Mr. Parker also wanted to be careful not to equate economic impact with
city revenue—the economic impact is probably most felt by others in the city not the city itself.

The Slate Canyon Recreation Area Plan and Rock Canyon Master Plan were also submitted by
Councilors as priority items. Councilor George Handley indicated that the Rock Canyon Master
Plan was already drafted and identifies several funding needs for its implementation. There is an
old draft plan for Slate Canyon, but it needs revision to the data and recent property purchases.
There is a lot of citizen interest and volunteer potential to implement these ideas. Councilor
Shannon Ellsworth also supported moving plans forward at both canyons. Mr. Magness indicated
this would relate to the Council’s funding decisions.

2.      A discussion on American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds. (22-017) (1:43:22)

John Magness, Interim Council Executive Director, highlighted some ideas for how the Council
could guide the use of these funds. He invited input from the Council regarding the criteria, how
to measure priorities, and any other ways the Council would like to evaluate potential uses of the
funds. Councilor George Handley thought it would be helpful to understand the alternatives and
what the opportunity costs might be of choosing one project versus another. He also suggested
the Administration clearly outline their process for evaluating uses of the funds which would
help the Council understand how certain recommendations were reached. Wayne Parker, CAO,
indicated the Administration was working on developing criteria.

Councilors Shannon Ellsworth and David Shipley gave some context of what the Council ARPA
Committee has been discussing so far—the main point for feedback from the group was what
lenses the Council would use for evaluating projects. Mr. Magness indicated they can also
discuss this as part of the Council’s discussion of budget priorities. There was more discussion
about the vetting process and the challenges inherent to the process. Discussion only.
