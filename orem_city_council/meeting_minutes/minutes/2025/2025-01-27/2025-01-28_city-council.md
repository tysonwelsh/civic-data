Central Utah Water Conservancy District (CUWCD) Incentives Program Update Presentation
Presenter: Chris Tschirki, Public Works Director, Amanda Stack, Water Construction Manager,
and Savannah Peterson, Conservation Program Coordinator

Ms. Stack and Ms. Peterson are here to give an update on the CUWCD incentive
program. Ms. Stack explained that the program has two aspects to its residential and commercial
conversion program. There is a lawn replacement program and an irrigation conversion project

City Council Minutes — January 28, 2025, (pg.1) A complete video of the meeting can be found at www.orem.org/meeting

that people can apply for. Last year, a tree rebate program was added to the program. Today the
purpose of this presentation is to talk about a pilot program that CUWCD is offering. They
would like permission to run a program this spring. This program would not require adopting a
water efficiency program. This program would offer $1.50 per square foot to Orem residents to
remove lawn or replace lawn with water efficient landscaping and $.50 per square foot for
irrigation conversion projects. These would be projects where people swap out their overspray
irrigation for more efficient drip irrigation. There is also a $50 per tree, up to 5 trees, for
residential projects and up to 10 trees for commercial. Ms. Stack explained that in the past Orem
has had great participation in their programs. In 2022, about $55,000 in incentives were given to
Orem residents.

This program is not for an all concrete or no plant project. This program is about water
conservation in an aesthetically pleasing way that is easy to maintain and nice for the
community. This program does not incentivise for zeroscaping, artificial turf, water features, or
impervious hardscapes. The landscape incentive requirements are 50% plant coverage at
maturity, installed drip irrigation system, 3-4” of mulch, and on lawn replacement projects. The
remaining lawn areas must be smaller than 8 feet. Ms. Stack spoke about other programs that the
Orem residents are eligible to participate in. These programs are the smart controller rebate
program, the toilet rebate program, and in person and online classes related to landscaping.

Mayor Young was supportive of this program and complimented the CUWCD for
thinking outside the box. Mr. Tschirki recommended that the City put this program information
out to the residents. He also thanked Mr. Bart Leeflang for attending the meeting. He is the
CUWCD Assistant General Manager.

Proposed Resolution to Allow Alpine School District (ASD) to Sell CTE Property in Orem
Presenter: Aaron McKnight, Assistant City Attorney

Mr. McKnight presented a proposed resolution to allow ASD to sell the CTE (Career and
Technical Education) property located in Orem. There is a program offered that allows high
school students to build two houses every year within the district. These programs are run out of
the Lehi and Timpanogos High Schools. These homes are sold and the proceeds purchase future
property to build upon. Due to the school district split, State law requires that any real property
owned by the old school district be approved. ASD needs the consent of Orem, Pleasant Grove,
Lindon and Vineyard city councils to sell this property. Pleasant Grove, Lindon and Vineyard
have all passed resolutions consenting to the sale. This resolution will be presented in tonight’s
City Council meeting.

Geneva Road Nighttime Construction Exemption Presenter: Gary McGinn, Community
Development Director and Beau Hunter, Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT)

Mr. McGinn explained that this resolution is very similar to the 1600 N nighttime
construction exemption. There is another UDOT construction project, located on Geneva Road,
that is asking for a nighttime construction exemption. Mr. Hunter explained that by working at
night it can minimize traffic impacts for that area and can help get a better price on the project.
The project will be going to bid in the near future. After the bid is accepted then UDOT will have
a better idea of how long the project will take and how much it will cost. The project is on
Geneva Road, south of University Parkway.

Legislative Priorities Discussion Presenter: Keven J. Stratton, Utah State Senator

Senator Stratton gave the City Council a handout titled a Blueprint for 2025. This can be
located online at https://senate.utah.gov/issues/2025-blueprint-for-utah/. This has the priorities
for the State Senators. He spoke about the importance of governing and being a servant to our

people. The handout also shows the recognitions that Utah has received. He highlighted some of

the positives. The priorities for the State Senate are energy, education, water, fiscally responsible
tax cut, innovation, economic prosperity, affordable housing, transportation, public lands, and
quality of life.

Currently, the legislature is working on a base budget that will help guide the legislative
session. Senator Stratton spoke about some legislation that would affect the City of Orem. One is
the Provo Canyon resource management plan. He explained that the resource management plan

is a plan put together with all of the counties to take care of our water and natural resources. He
spoke about the federal money in Utah and he informed the council that the money Utah gives to
the federal government is equal to the money received from the federal government in funding.
He encouraged the City Council to be aware of national policy and how it can affect Utah.

Senator Stratton asked the City Council about their priorities. Mayor Young asked Mr.
Bybee to send Senator Stratton the 2025 areas of focus. Mr. Macdonald asked if the Senator was
still working with water because Utah Lake is a major concern. Senator Stratton quickly touched
on his current assignments. He serves on the water development board and federally on the
natural resources board. His two appropriations are chair of social services and past chair of the
transportation committee. He reported that water is currently 72% of normal, but groundwater is
in the high 70s. Reservoirs statewide are 20% above normal but he emphasized that snow is
needed over the next couple of months. Mr. Macdonald asked Senator Stratton to protect the
funding for UVU. Senator Stratton reported that there are some good voices for UVU in
important places.

Mr. Bybee expressed gratitude to Senator Stratton for helping Orem get some
appropriations to go towards future widening of Center Street between Geneva and I-15. Mr.
Bybee gave Senator Stratton the major priorities for Orem. One is growth management and the
ability to have local leadership manage growth in their cities. Mr. Bybee shared the Orem
housing growth brochure with the senator. Senator Stratton is proud to represent an area that is
getting growth right. He believes that the brochure could be helpful in telling Orem’s story to
others in the legislature.

Ms. Millett has appreciated that Senator Stratton is an advocate for keeping government
close to the people. Senator Stratton said that the government closest to the people is best for the
people. He would like to help Orem tell their good stories. Mr. Spencer is concerned with more
housing being built out and do we have the resources in the area to sustain it.. He would like the
power to determine growth to stay in the cities. Senator Stratton supports not having one plan for
all cities.

Mr. Bybee explained other priorities for Orem which are transportation related. He spoke
about the Lakeview corridor and trails in Orem. The City Council has a desire to improve the
trails in this area. Senator Stratton expressed a desire to help Orem in some of these projects. He
understands that there is a desire to have alternative means of transport. He would like to see
improvements along the trails from Orem to Park City.

Mr. Bybee reported that the City is interested in the bills concerning short term rentals.
He would like to receive state input on how to regulate these types of rentals. Senator Stratton
would like to speak with the council about this. He wants to make sure that the legislation is
being helpful and not inviting unintended consequences.

The final item that Mr. Bybee wanted to discuss the potential legislation related to utility
transportation fees. He would like to be able to look into it and see what makes sense or not for
the City of Orem. Senator Stratton is not familiar with this particular legislation concerning this.
Mr. Bybee will send him talking points about it. Mr. Lambson expressed his gratitude for the

City Council 2025 Areas of Focus Presentation Presenter: Pete Wolfley, Communication
Manager, PIO

Mr. Wolfley gave the City Council members copies of the 2024 accomplishments that
were presented at the City Council Retreat. He explained that the purpose of this presentation is
to let the City Council know that the City is eager and ready to take ownership of the vision and
goals given to them by the City Council. Mr. Wolfley has created notebooks for each of the City
Council that have the goals printed on them.

Mr. Wolfley presented the areas of focus for 2025. These areas of focus are 1) aligning
City Council, City employees and citizens with communication and service opportunities. 2)
Creating safe, livable, connected, and engaged neighborhoods by building community
partnerships, short term rental regulations, preserved neighborhood character, and general plan
updates. 3) Building a thriving and balanced business environment which will involve
connections with businesses and a streamlined business licensing process. 4) Community
focused and effective government which include legislator outreach, UVU dialogue and
promotion of the Orem Community Foundation. 5) Skilled and talented workforce that will
continue to build employee relationships, offer cross training opportunities and career growth
opportunities.

RESOLUTION - Consenting to the Sale of Certain Property Owned by Alpine School
District (ASD) that is located within the Boundary of the New Reorganiz hool Distri
Presenter: Aaron McKnight, Assistant City Attorney

Mr. McKnight informed the City Council that ASD is asking for consent of the City
Council to sell property that is owned by ASD but that belongs in what will be the new school
district. On this property a home was built by students as part of Career and Technical (CTE)
classes. The proceeds of this sale of this home will go back into the CTE program for future
projects. Under state law because of the school district split, ASD needs the consent from all city
councils that belong to the new school district in which the property is found. Lindon, Vineyard
and Pleasant Grove have already given their consent.

Mr. Macdonald moved to approve, by resolution, the consent to the sale of certain
property owned by Alpine School District that is located within the boundary of the new
reorganized School District. Seconded by Mr. Lambson. Those voting yes: Jeff Lambson, David
Young, LaNae Millett, David Spencer, Tom Macdonald, and Chris Killpack. The motion passed.
Jenn Gale is absent.
