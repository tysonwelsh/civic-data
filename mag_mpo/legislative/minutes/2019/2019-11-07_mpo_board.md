MPO Regional Planning Committee November 7, 2019 Page 3 Updated Corridor Preservation Policies and Map – Jim Price, MAG The Utah County Local Option Corridor Preservation Fund (CPF) is administered by MAG in cooperation with Utah County. Statute requires that CPF monies be prioritized and spent according to policies established by the Council of Governments, or in MAG’s case, the Regional Planning Committee. Statute also requires projects be listed in the adopted Regional Transportation Plan (RTP). Past practice has been to adopt the RTP Highways map as the list of prioritized projects. MAG staff recommends the same process with the new TransPlan50. However, statute now allows CPF monies for regionally significant transit projects. The full adoption of the new map also requires a vote by the Utah County Commission in a regular commission meeting. Discussions with both TAC and RPC indicate willingness to include transit projects within the CPF if UTA is required to execute reimbursement agreements with Utah County just as UDOT has been. MAG staff agrees and recommends adoption of both a new Approved Corridors Map and an updated policy document reflecting these changes. Resolution of Support for Utah County to Refinance 2009b Corridor Preservation Bonds - Jim Price, MAG In 2009, Utah County issued $27.7m in Vehicle Registration Fee (VRF) Revenue Bonds to assist the development of Redwood Road, North County Blvd, and other large projects. VRF revenues also fund the Corridor Preservation Program. The 2009 Bonds are currently MPO Regional Planning Committee November 7, 2019 Page 4 outstanding at a net rate of 4.25%. The County sees an opportunity to lower the interest rate, reduce the debt term by 5 years, and create significant savings to the fund by using $6m of the current gross balance of $15m in the Corridor Preservation Program for re- funding the bonds at a lower level and lower interest rate of about 2%. Estimated net savings are ~$6m. After accounting for the $6m drawdown, required debt servicing reserves, and outstanding project obligations, the Corridor Preservation Program would begin the new year with at least $1.75m in usable funds. UDOT may also reimburse the program up to $3.2m in the near term for the Springville 1600 S/I-15 Interchange, for a possible total balance of $4.95m. Continuing VRF revenues add approximately $2.5m annually to the Corridor Preservation Program, with that amount increasing over time as countywide population and vehicle ownership growth rises. Average yearly draws on the Corridor Preservation Program stand at approximately $2.2m – $2.5m; MAG staff anticipate that based on historic demands and forecast revenues; the Corridor Preservation Program would retain adequate funds for foreseeable needs after the bonds are re-funded. The Corridor Preservation Program has been carrying a large, unused balance for some years, and MAG staff do not anticipate this changing soon. Based on the information provided by Utah County and by its bond counsel, MAG staff supports the use of a portion of those reserves to reduce the carried debt and repayment costs of the 2009 VRF Revenue Bonds. Mayor Brunst moved that the Regional Planning Committee pass a resolution of support and approval for the Utah County bond re-funding proposal as presented. Mayor Johnson seconded the motion. Quadrennial Planning Certification of the Provo/Orem Transportation Management Area Findings – Steve Call, FHWA and Ivan Marrero, FHWA FHWA and FTA are required to jointly review and evaluate the MAG MPO transportation planning processes every four years. Steven Call and Kevin Osborn respectively lead this cycle’s review. The purpose of the Planning Certification Review is to formalize the continuing oversight and day-to-day evaluation of the federal planning process. The federal certification review process ensures that the planning requirements federal law are being satisfactorily implemented. The federal certification review process is a valuable opportunity to provide advice and guidance to a Transportation Management Area (TMA), defined as an urbanized area with a population over 200,000 for enhancing the planning process and improving the quality of transportation investment decisions. FHWA showcases some of the concepts from MAG and WFRC as examples. Updates Federal Highway Administration: FHA still operating under continuing resolution until November 21, 2019.
