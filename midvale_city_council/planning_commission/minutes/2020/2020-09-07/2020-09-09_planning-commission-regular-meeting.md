COMMERCIAL (RC) TO MULTIFAMILY RESIDENTIAL — MEDIUM TO

Mr. Jason Binks stated that the applicant, Dale Bennett of Dale Bennett Properties, LLC, requests
a rezone of a 0.21-acre single parcel at 847 W Center Street from Regional Commercial (RC) to
Multifamily Residential Medium to High Density (RM-25). The applicant proposes a conceptual
plan with a duplex that faces west on Le Page Street. A duplex is an allowed use in the proposed
RM-25 zone and would be reviewed by planning Staff through submittal of a building permit if
the rezone is approved.
The property is in a Regional Commercial (RC) zone and is adjacent to four different zones. It
abuts an RC zoned single-family dwelling to the east and an RM-25 single-family dwelling to the
south. To the north, across Center Street, is a multifamily development in the Silver Refinery
Overlay of the Bingham Junction zone. To the west is the Jordan Bluffs zone with several
multifamily residential buildings. The subject property is on a short block with two other parcels,
a single-family home and small business. The properties are all addressed on W Center Street, but
access is along a frontage road named 7725 South, which runs immediately parallel to W Center
Street. The subject site is on the corner of 7725 South and Le Page Street. Adjacent neighborhood
development includes a variety of single family, duplexes, multifamily, restaurants, offices, and |
retail and commercial.
GENERAL PLAN CONSIDERATIONS:
The subject property is located at the overlapping border of two Opportunity Areas identified in
the 2016 Midvale City General Plan. It is within the Main Street Opportunity Area and the
Jordan Bluffs Opportunity Area. The General Plan identifies the following future land use goals
for these Opportunity Areas:
Main Street
1. Preserve and build on the character of Historic Main Street
2. Preserve and support rehabilitation and improvement of nearby stable residential areas
3. Enhance the Main Street and Center Street corridors to create a vibrant, walkable town
center that could become a regional attraction

4. Diversify the land use mix to develop a compact full-service pedestrian scale
neighborhood, particularly in the area of Main Street, Center Street, and Holden Street
5. Provide and implement new bike routes to complete the transportation system
6. Connect Main Street to surrounding neighborhoods and to Bingham Junction and Jordan
Bluffs through pedestrian and bicycle pathways and urban design techniques to create a
cultural center
7. Connect Main Street area with Bingham Junction through the extension of Tuscany View
Road
8. Support redevelopment of existing multifamily structures in the area, particularly on
Tiffany Town Drive and the south end of Ivy Drive, into more functional higher density
housing
9. Support redevelopment of the existing storage units between Allen Street and I-15 in
support of the adjacent residential neighborhood
10. Support redevelopment of the City’s western boundary to blend with and enhance future
redevelopment of the Jordan Bluffs Area
11. Develop design guidelines for new development and alterations on Main Street
The proposed rezone is consistent with the General Plan because it furthers goal #2 and #8 by |
allowing the very small, undeveloped parcel of land to be rehabilitated and improved with higher
density housing through multifamily structures. Midvale’s oldest zoning map from 1983 shows
the subject property was Commercial (C-2), and historic aerial maps reveal that it was not
developed for commercial purposes since then. It was directly adjacent to the Sharon Steel
Superfund Site.
Jordan Bluffs
1. Promote consistency and compatibility with the Bingham Junction project to the north
2. Provide for connectivity to the Main Street neighborhood, either physically (crosswalks)
or by urban design
3. Support redevelopment of the adjacent area to the east to blend and enhance any
redevelopment of the Jordan Bluffs Area
The proposed rezone is consistent with the General Plan because it furthers goal #1 by increasing
the residential density of the subject site, thereby promoting more consistency and compatibility
with the multifamily developments in the Bingham Junction project to the north. The proposed
rezone also supports goal #3 by providing a buffering, or blending, between the high density,
four-story residential structures in Jordan Bluffs to the west and the single-family neighborhoods
to the east.
ORDINANCE CONSIDERATIONS:
Section 17-3-1 of the Midvale City Municipal Code requires the Planning Commission hold a
public hearing and adopt a written recommendation to the City Council, advising the City
Council to approve, disapprove, or modify a request on all zoning map amendments. The
Planning Commission may only recommend approval of a rezone application if it determines, in
written findings, that the proposed rezoning is consistent with the policies and goals of the
general plan and that the applicant has demonstrated the following:
PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION SEPTEMBER 9, 2020 |

1. The proposed rezoning is necessary either to comply with the general plan proposed land
use map or to provide land for a community need that was not anticipated at the time of
adoption of the general plan:

2. The existing zoning was either the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact, or that it
failed to take into account the constraints on development created by the natural
characteristics of the land including, but not limited to, steep slopes, floodplain, unstable
soils, and inadequate drainage; or

3. The land or its surrounding environs has changed or is changing to such a degree that it is
in the public interest to encourage redevelopment of the area or to recognize the changed
character of the area.

In addition to meeting General Plan Considerations, the proposed rezone also satisfies
requirements of Section 17-3-1 by meeting goal #3 above. The area around the subject site has
changed rapidly and extensively. The area has a diverse mix of business and residential
opportunities. The subject property is on a short block zoned Regional Commercial in order to
encourage these properties in the future to develop as commercial opportunities along the busy
primary corridor of Center Street. However, the area along Center Street between Holden Street
and the Jordan River has been primarily developed in the last several years as multifamily
residential.
At 0.21 acres, the subject parcel is small for commercial opportunities and cannot be accessed by
a driveway directly from Center Street. It is locked in by the Jordan Bluffs development on the
south and west and can only be accessed by 7725 South. The City Engineer does not think direct
access from Center Street is feasible because of the slope differential between Center Street and
7725 South and because of multiple utilities in the median. The property is the only remaining
undeveloped parcel in a neighborhood that is primarily residential. The large multifamily
developments along Bingham Junction Boulevard, north and south of Center Street, have
overwhelmingly changed the landscape of the area to a higher density character. The proposed
rezone to Multifamily Residential Medium to High Density (RM-25) would be consistent—and
be compatible—with the emerging character of the area.
Notice of the proposed rezone was mailed to 32 nearby property owners and posted on the
property, online, and in the Salt Lake Tribune and Deseret News. As of September 1, 2020, no
comments have been received.
STAFF RECOMMENDATIONS:
The proposed rezone is consistent with the 2016 General Plan and meets requirements of
Midvale Code Chapter 17-3-1, therefore Staff recommends a positive recommendation to City
Council for approval of the proposed rezone at 847 W Center Street to Multifamily Residential —
Medium to High Density zone (RM-25).

OCCUPIED ACCESSORY STRUCTURE; SF-1/DO ZONE; AMANDA PALMER

Mr. Binks stated that the applicant, Andrea Palmer representing Nichole Falk, requests a
conditional use permit to construct an occupied accessory structure at 7291 S 245 E. The parcel
is an irregularly shaped, 0.40-acre lot at the convex knuckle of a local road. The parcel is zoned
Single Family Residential with Duplex Overlay (SF-1/DO) and is surrounded by other single-
family homes. The back yard has an eastern and southern boundary. A church property abuts the
entire eastern portion, with single-family homes on the south. The Salt Lake Country Accessor
shows a primary dwelling with 1,134 square feet above grade. The applicant shows a total
footprint for the two-story home at 1,673 square feet. [
The proposed single-story accessory structure is 425 square feet of habitable space with a porch
and stairs. It is in the southwest corner of the property, at the rear yard and side yard setback
lines, approximately 35 feet behind the primary dwelling. The porch and doorway of the
accessory dwelling face east toward the church.
| The Midvale City General Plan notes that “a diversified housing stock is integral to any city’s
sustainability [and that] housing choices must be available for all life stages, income levels, and
| lifestyles.” A proposed dwelling structure of this size, in this location, will offer a price point that

is more affordable for all life stages than most housing opportunities in the immediate
neighborhood. Staff finds that the proposed occupied accessory structure fulfills the goals of the
General Plan.

ORDINANCE CONSIDERATIONS:

The SF-1/DO zone allows occupied accessory structures with a conditional use permit, provided
the Planning Commission concludes that the application fully mitigates all identified adverse
impacts and complies with the following standards:

General Review Criteria

1. The application complies with all applicable provisions of this title, State and Federal
Law;

2. The structures associated with the use are compatible with surrounding structures in
terms of use, scale, mass and circulation;

3. The use is not detrimental to the public health, safety and welfare;

4. The use is consistent with the Midvale City General Plan, as amended; :

5. Traffic conditions are not adversely affected by the proposed use including the existence
of or need for dedicated turn lanes, pedestrian access, and capacity of the existing streets;

6. Sufficient utility capacity;

7. Sufficient emergency vehicle access;

8. Location and design of off-street parking as well as compliance with off-street parking
standards provided for in §17-7-13.8:

9. Fencing, screening, and landscaping to separate the use from adjoining uses and mitigate
the potential for conflict in uses:

10. Compatibility of the proposed mass, bulk, design, orientation, and location of the
structures on the site; including compatibility with buildings on adjoining lots and to the
street;

11. Exterior lighting that complies with the lighting standards of the zone and is designed to
minimize conflict and light trespass with surrounding uses; and

12. Within and adjoining the site, impacts on the aquifer, slope retention, flood potential and
appropriateness of the proposed structure to the topography of the site.

Staff has not identified any apparent conflicts with provisions of the zoning ordinance (except for
below), the Midvale City General Plan, or state and federal law. The proposed use is not
detrimental to public health, safety or welfare. No traffic issues have been identified. Utility
service is already available to the property. Emergency vehicle access is not a concern. There are
no lighting requirements for this use. Impact to aquifer, slope, flood, and topography do not
apply.

To increase the compatibility of an accessory dwelling unit in a single-family residential
neighborhood, the City of Midvale requires that the property owner sign a Property Owner
Covenant for an Accessory Structure, Occupied. The covenant states that the “property Owner
shall occupy the primary dwelling unit on the property as long as the accessory structure,
occupied remains on the property.” This will ensure that the property owner is aware of activities
at the accessory dwelling, possible maintenance repairs, and can be easily reached for any code
enforcement issues. Staff has included a Condition of Approval to address this issue.

Per Title 17-7-1.7 all yard and setback areas visible from the street shall be landscaped.
Currently the entire front yard, apart from a couple flower beds, is gravel. The gravel will need to
be replaced “with live materials such as, but not limited to, sod, grass, ground cover, trees,
shrubs, vines and other growing horticultural plant material. In addition, a combination of
xeriscape plantings and designs that may include other decorative surfacing such as bark chips,
crushed stone, mulch materials, decorative concrete or pavers shall also meet landscaping
requirements.” Staff has included a Condition of Approval to address this issue.
Per Title 17-7-1.8, all residential lots shall provide a minimum of two improved off-street
parking spaces for personal automobiles. In addition, accessory dwelling units require one extra
off-street parking space. The subject site has the required three parking spaces for the current and
proposed uses. The primary dwelling has a single car garage and a 12-foot wide driveway that
can fit two vehicles in tandem. There is a triangular gravel area adjacent to the driveway that
appears to supplement on-site parking. This gravel area would probably be used to deconflict
parking for two different families with various vehicle schedules. Midvale code does not allow
parking on gravel surfaces in the front yard. Staff recommends adding a condition of approval
that this gravel area be replaced with landscaping or paved with asphalt or concrete to
accommodate parking. Staff recommends including a Condition of Approval to address this
issue.
There is a perimeter fence along the entire backyard and no proposed changes were submitted.
There is adequate landscaping and mature trees in the back yard. With the fencing, landscaping,
and required 10-foot setbacks for the accessory structure, there will be no conflicts with adjacent
uses. The proposed accessory dwelling has a design aesthetic and architectural composition that
is compatible with other structures on adjacent residential lots. The materials, placement, and
orientation of the accessory structure is complimentary to the neighborhood. The submitted
elevations show energy-efficient facades with smaller window openings and a double door
entryway.
Specific Review Criteria
i. Proximity. The accessory structure must be located no less than six feet from the main
building.

ii. Setbacks. The accessory structure must be located in either the rear or side yard, with no
less than a ten-foot setback from the rear and side lot lines, unless the accessory structure
is located at the front yard setback of an adjacent corner lot, in which case, the side yard
setback to the nearest side lot line must be at least fifteen feet.

iii. Height. The accessory structure height may not exceed twenty-four feet to the midpoint
for a sloped roof and twenty feet to the cornice for a flat roof.
iv. Stories. An accessory structure may range from one to one and one-half stories.

v. Building Area. The maximum area of an occupied accessory structure is the larger of
nine hundred square feet or thirteen percent of the lot area. No more than six hundred
square feet of the accessory structure shall be used for human occupancy.

The proposed accessory structure meets the specific review criteria. It’s 35 feet from the primary

dwelling at the required 10-foot side and rear yard setbacks. It is single story and approximately

10 feet tall to the cornice of the flat roof. The habitable area is 425 square feet.

DISCUSSION:
Commissioner Benson said that at the last meeting there were concerns about the number of units
and its impact on traffic and wanted to know if they are looking at having the same number of
units?
Mr. Murphy stated that the number of units are still the same, 128. There was a traffic study done
at the time of the original proposal that found that there were no concerns about traffic patterns in
this area. There were some comments last time about the intersection on 8000 South and State
Street, but that is not an intersection that the Commission can put the burden to fix on the
developer. There is no evidence to suggest that there will be any traffic issues in the future. The
only possible concern might be the access point off 300 East and if the proposed position is
problematic.
Commissioner Benson asked if all the units have driveways?
Mr. Murphy said that all of the single-family homes will have driveways and there will be 4
parking spaces per unit. The townhomes do not have driveways, but the project will have 2.5 |
parking spaces per unit. There is also public street parking on 300 East and 8000 South which will
provide additional parking areas. This is in his opinion is an adequate amount of parking for this
development.
Commissioner Erickson stated that a lot of the comments from last meeting were in regards to
concerns over parking on 8000 South, which she acknowledges is out of their control. She also
wanted to acknowledge that perception can also be very different than what the traffic study has
shown. There are a lot of factors that played into why they are looking at this plan now.
Mr. Murphy said that the biggest complaint is visibility at the intersection and said that it would
be an issue that needs to be discussed with public works and/or Unified Police. There are state
laws about parking within a certain proximity to an intersection and if those laws are not being
followed than that needs to be addressed. He said that it might be possible to paint some of those
curbs red to reduce the number of occurrences of cars parking in that location. This would all be
independent of the project the Commission is looking at now.

Commissioner Erickson said that she feels like they addressed a lot of the issues that were

Commissioner Snow asked if the traffic study includes the multi family?
Mr. Murphy said it includes the traffic generation counts for the multi family, but it has not been

Commissioner Snow asked if the Commission approves these motions tonight then is that is an
approval for the multi family?
Mr. Murphy said that it is an approval but not a site-specific design approval, so the Commission
can go back and address issues with the site design. This includes access points, landscaping, and
common space design along with any other details that have not yet been addressed.
Commissioner Snow said that if during the single-family home phase there were massive traffic
issues could the Commission come back and say that there can’t be a multi-family project?
Mr. Murphy said that the Commission can’t come back and say that there can’t be a multifamily
project, but they could require mitigation measures are taken to improve traffic issues as long as
there is justification. One of the biggest issues with “traffic” is that the perception of traffic does
not mean that there is necessarily a problem. What the Commission would end up doing is they
can require a new traffic study be done once the single-family homes are mostly developed.
Ultimately, the Commission can’t deny a project due to traffic concerns, but they can require that
they improve the traffic situation by taking on different measures (i.e. a stop light).
Commissioner Snow stated that he is concerned that the Commission could not make changes if
traffic were to become an issue due to the density of the project.
Mr. Murphy mentioned that this current project has lower density than the development on the
opposite side of the street, which has not been problematic besides a few complaints that could not
be substantiated with any evidence.
