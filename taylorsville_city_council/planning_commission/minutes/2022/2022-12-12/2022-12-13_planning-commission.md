6. 1        Mr. Spung presented this item. The City of Taylorsville is requesting a text amendment to several sections
            of the Taylorsville Land Development Code related to home occupations and corresponding definitions. The
            proposed text amendment will address known concerns related to home occupations as well as simplify
            review procedures. City Staff is currently reviewing and updating the Land Development Code, and as part
            of that effort, identified several updates to improve the regulation and licensing of home occupations.
            Rather than waiting to implement those changes with the larger Land Development code update, Staff is
            recommending these updates be considered for adoption now.

6. 2        Staff Analysis: Following are notable changes from current code proposed as part of this text amendment:
            6. 2. 1    Home occupations will be a " permitted use," subject to certain standards, Lane use tables have

                       been updated accordingly.
            6. 2. 2    Content has been reorganized for user- friendliness.
            6. 2. 3    The Director is the decision- making authority and has the ability to elevate reviews to the Planning
                       Commission as necessary.
            6. 2. 4    Class A, Class B, Class C, and Class D home occupation distinctions and subcategories have been

                       replaced with simple terminology and objective standards to address land use impacts.
            6. 2. 5    One residential child day care, preschool, home occupation that provides instruction ( e. g., dance,
                       music, art classes), or home occupation with a business vehicle or trailer, is allowed per lot or
                       parcel.

            6. 2. 6    A residential child day care, preschool, or residential instruction cannot be located within 300 feet
                       of another day care, preschool, or instruction home occupation.
            6. 2. 7    Must be incidental to the primary use and no more than 50 percent of the total floor area of ay
                       dwelling.
            6. 2. 8    Updated and clarified standards related to business vehicles and trailers.
            6. 2. 9    Updated and clarified standards for on- site visits.
            6. 2. 10   Updated the definition of residential child day care to align with State Code.
            6. 2. 11   Child day care, preschool, and residential instruction with five or more children is only allowed in a
                       detached single- family home that fronts on a street with a minimum pavement width of 25 feet
                       that has curb, gutter, and sidewalk.
            6. 2. 12   Child day care is not allowed on a flag lot or a lot with an accessory dwelling unit (ADU).
            6. 2. 13   Home occupations cannot generate more than 24 cumulative vehicle trips per day.
            6. 2. 14   Drop- off and pick- up associated with a home occupation is not allowed in the public right- of-way.

            7. 1. 1    He indicated he has been working with City Staff and City Administration initially regarding the City' s
                       fee schedule. The City is looking to review that in regard to all ordinances and all divisions within

                       the City for various fees.         a      From that spawned a discussion regarding the Good Landlord
                       Program itself.       Therefore, tonight the purpose for Mr. Ziolkowski' s               visit is to share with the

                       Planning Commission the recommendations, which will most likely be taken to the City Council in
                       January to essentially remove the Good Landlord Program in the City.

                       7. 1. 1. 1    He then went into the methodology being considered. Historically the Good Landlord
                                     Program began within the City of Ogden, soon thereafter in the City of West Valley and
                                     then was taken to the Utah Housing Coalition where several other cities joined in the
                                     effort. That basically just outlined various things that landlords are required to do to be
                                     considered good landlords and thereby receiving a reduced fee in connection to obtaining
                                     a business license. That worked well for a while in theory, but one concern expressed by

                                     the City of Taylorsville is it didn' t 12 want to be one of those cities not having the
                                     program and inherit some typically bad landlords that weren' t involved in this program.
                                     There were some benefits gained by having the program due to there being quite a few
                                     requirements that would insure someone being a good landlord if followed. It has also
                                     come with some unintended consequences as a result of the program that none of the

                                     cities foresaw happening.


                       7. 1. 1. 2    One requirement was to obtain certification through taking a training course and then
                                     prove the course was taken via completion certificate in order to qualify for the reduced
                                     fee. There was no real way to check and make sure the landlords were in compliance with
                                     that. In the City there are about 480 participants in the program and staff cannot keep up
                                     to validate that everyone has the certification. Also, in connection to Code Enforcement,

                                     about 30% of the good landlord participants are not in compliance with City Code, meaning
                                     sometimes several inspections must be conducted on individual residences.


                       7. 1. 1. 3    There is a lot of work on the front side for Staff in processing the applications in connection
                                     or comparison to any of the other business licensing taking place. With that, the City is
                                     looking to basically streamline all of the fees associated with rental units and make them
                                     all the same fee. There is the potential that the City could raise the fees in connection to
                                     rental units but rather than that, the City is entertaining a recommendation to the City
                                     Council to leave the fees the same for all current owners of rental units but then work on

            7. 1. 5    Commissioner Quigley         a said it is a two-way street about landlords.        One would be the

                       complaint the neighbors might have and the other would be the tenant having a complaint about
                       their landlord.    His concern about the escalation of fees is he would not want tenants to realize if

                       they complain enough about their landlord it is a way of getting even with them. People can
                       become somewhat vindictive if they see an opportunity open. Another concern of his is the
                       potential of waiving the fee. He felt that because there is still an administrative expense involved,
                       a better idea might be to charge the fee when the original application is made and then when it
                       comes time to renew and there have been no problems, then waive the fee at that time might be a

                       better incentive. He also suggested preparing a handbook to give to each landlord which contains
                       advice on what Utah laws are in this regard, including information on evictions and leases. It would
                       also remind them of their responsibilities to make sure tenants do what they are supposed to do.
                       He added that the Utah Apartments Association has a lot of helpful information that could be
                       included therein.
