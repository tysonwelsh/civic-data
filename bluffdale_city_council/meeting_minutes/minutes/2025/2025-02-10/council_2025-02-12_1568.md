Planning Manager, Caitlyn Tubbs, stated that one area offocus Staff was asked to review is housing
in the City. The statewide housing affordability concerns are felt from the local level all the way to
the state legislature. She then provided definitions ofthe terms frequently used in those discussions.

Affordable housing is defined by the United States Department of Housing and Urban Development
("HUD")as housing that the occupant only needs to spend up to 30% oftheir gross monthly income
on. That 30% should cover the rent or mortgage, property taxes or escrow, insurance, and utilities.
The median income in Salt Lake County is $115,500 per household. Bluffdale's median income is

housing expense is just over $2,400 per month, which equates to a $450,000 home. Ms. Tubbs
reported that it is less and less common to see homes offered at that price point.

The General Plan is required to have a Moderate Income Housing Element. The State mandates that
Bluffdale report on moderate income housing, which is defined as housing that is reserved for
households with a gross income of up to 80% of the area median income. As part of the reporting
requirements, cities must choose three menu items to focus on. Bluffdale focuses on the following:

       E.      Create or allow for and reduce regulations related to internal or detached

               commercial or mixed-use zones near major transit investment corridors,
               commercial centers, or employment centers. Several special district zones have
               come before the Council to request entitlements for higher density housing types that
               are close to Redwood Road or Porter Rockwell Boulevard, where Staff anticipates
               major transportation investments and transit improvements to be made in the future.

for one year after improvements or progress has been made.

Assistant City Engineer, Dan Tracer, presented the Staff Report and noted that the City's Quality

If any of the multiple ingredients in concrete is off in quantity or quality, it can ruin the entire mix.
To mitigate that, the ingredients list is reviewed before the mix gets to the jobsite. Concrete will
always crack, but they try to minimize and ensure that it cracks in the right location. Mr. Tracer
presented a photograph of a crack in concrete and noted that it was repaired by grinding and filling
with an epoxy crack sealer to prevent water from getting into it.

Concrete also needs a sturdy bed. It is great in compression, but not in tension. If it sits on a cliff,
the edge piece is in tension and will crack and fall off. It needs a proper, level base to provide the
necessary strength. Mr. Tracer presented an image of a bed that was prepared with improperly
compacted topsoil that included organic material that should have been removed, and reported that
the concrete would fail immediately. That is mitigated by the City inspectors inspecting the base to
ensure that it is properly compacted and has the strength it needs. Additional quality testing is also
done to verify the results.


Concrete often contains rebar, which does not do well with moisture as it rusts and expands when
wet. Mr. Tracer presented a photograph of severely cracked concrete that resulted from wet rebar.
To mitigate that, the inspectors verify that the rebar is epoxy coated so that it will not rust ifexposed
to moisture.


Poor mixing and curing is another issue. If the workers are continuously troweling or brooming the
concrete, it can cause the ingredients to separate close to the surface. After a few months or weeks,
the top layer of the concrete will begin to spall or fragment. Occasionally contractors must be told
that they cannot pour in the rain as that will also cause spalling.

These issues are addressed by establishing quality control standards through a Quality Assurance
Program. It requires that the contractor provide submittals to verify that all materials are adequate for
the job, conduct independent testing, and provide proofthat the testing was completed by a certified
laboratory.


In response to a question, Mr. Tracer clarified that for City projects, the initial concrete mix is
specified in the bid package. The actual concrete rarely exactly meets those specifications, but it is
always very close. The engineer ofrecord reviews the proposed concrete and approves or rejects that

  • The Utah League of Cities and Towns("ULCT")is tracking over 200 bills and resolutions in

  • A proposed Land Use bill would make substantial changes to the Municipal Land Use
      Management Act. The ULCT supports this bills. The proposed changes came out ofthe work
      of the Land Use Task Force, which is a stakeholder group that includes many city
      representatives, developers, and others. It includes changes related to bonding and timing of
      approvals that may require Code updates after the bill passes.
  • A transportation bill has been proposed that would require cities to identify roads that dead
      end at infrastructure such as canals. As it was originally drafted, it would withhold Class C
      road funds from cities that do not connect those roads. However, it has been substantially
      modified to state that the roads must be identified in plans and costs, potential funding sources,
      and any impediments to construction must be documented.
  • Legislation related to transportation utility fees, broadband fees, and public safety fees.
      Representative Petersen has proposed this as HB 454. As drafted, it would allow Bluffdale to
      continue to have a Public Safety Fee, but it would have to be reauthorized every three years.
       Senator Bramer also has a transportation utility fee bill that might affect this, but it has not
      been released yet so its impact is unclear.
  • A bill related to building official requirements. They want to ensure that building officials are
      better trained related to their responsibilities,so they are adding requirements such as six years
       of experience and testing.
  • There is a bill that would prohibit cities from requiring the addition offluoride to water. The
       ULCT has taken no position on this bill.
  • Legislation regarding critical infrastructure materials that relates to the expansion ofsand and
       gravel operations. Bluffdale's gravel pits do not have room to expand, so this bill will likely
       not affect the City.
  • Other bills related to housing and billboard relocation.
  • There is also an effort to require identification to vote by mail.
