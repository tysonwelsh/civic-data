ITEMS OF SPECIAL INTEREST

Sunshine Terrace Report ~ Bryan Erickson indicated that a year ago he had

presented an idea to the Council on a funding reimbursement program that is approved
by the Federal government — the Upper Payment Limit Program (UPL). This program
helps fill the Medicaid shortfall gap. The County becomes the owner of the Foundation
with the current administrators and staff operating the facility. Cache County currently
supports the Sunshine Terrace Foundation with $65,000.00 a year. Erickson believes
this would be much less or even eliminated with the UPL program. Council members
expressed concerns with liability and turning the operations over to someone other than
the county and a possible unfair competition situation with other caregiver facilities.
Executive Buttars, one of the County Attorneys, and Council members Tidwell, Ward
and Worthen will become a committee to study the UPL proposal.

e Home Repair Program Update — Kim Datwyler, Neighborhood Housing Solutions,
reported they are participating in the Olene Walker Housing loan Fund. Rural residents
making less than 80% of the county’s median income are eligible for low interest loans
for home repairs. In some instances a repair may qualify for no interest. Mobile homes
and trailers are not eligible for the program and it is only for home owners, not renters.
Payment is determined on the income and family size and total household debts.

Datwyler is asking Cache County to help get the word out about the program and
suggested a presence on the County's website. Attorney Swink feels it would be
appropriate for the County to do so and suggested the Public Relations Committee
review the request and make a recommendation to the Council. Council member
White is in favor of the request. Chairman Merrill asked Allen to place the committee’s
recommendation on a future agenda.

Upper Payment Limit Program (UPL)

Presentation to Cache County on August 22, 2017

1. In 2016 | presented a concept that could fill the funding gap we have with Medicaid
_____ reimbursement. This program would enable Sunshine Terrace Foundation to. continue to.

provide long term care services to the Cache County Citizens. This program is titled Upper
Payment Limit or UPL.

a. Shortly after presenting this concept to the County, The State of Utah froze the
application process to establish management criteria of the program. In the Fall of 2016,
they started to accept applications again.

b. February 2017 during the Legislative session, this program was modified once again, and
now has additional criteria that must be met.

c. [did not pursue this program further with the County until these details were solidified.
d. Now, I would like to reengage with the County in exploring this program.

2. 63% of people in nursing facilities rely on Medicaid to finance some or all of their care. 66% of
these individuals will have some form of dementia and/or will need assistance with activities of
daily living.

a. Medicaid underfunds our program on average about $.89 cents for every dollar spent.

i. This represents approximately $38 a day or $700,000 a year. We are dependent
on other programs to help subsidize the Medicaid program.

ii, Programs that we rely on to help bridge the gap are:
i. Tradition Medicare
ii. Medicare HMO (Replacement Plans)
iii. Insurance companies

b. As part of changes in the Affordable Health Care Act, we are now faced with insurance
companies, HMO, and even Medicare choosing certain providers to do business with,
which may not include us. This affects our ability to subsidize the shortfall we have in
Medicaid.

c. Foranumber of months the Medicaid program was being threatened with even more
cuts through the Repeal and Replace. This could have resulted in a reduction of
Medicaid funding of about $525,000 annually. This is something we are keeping a close
eye on. Additional cuts such as this could really compromise the care of the Cache
County citizens that rely on Medicaid for their health care needs.

3. For 69 years, Sunshine Terrace has been a Non-Profit foundation.
a. We appreciate the support we have received from Cache County for these 69 years.

b. We began working with Cache Valley from the onset, when Cache County operated "The
Nibley Home", ultimately leasing it to Sunshine Terrace Foundation.

c. Asa Non-Profit Foundation:

iD We have no Owners, only Stakeholders.

ji. | As a nonprofit corporation we were formed to carry out a public purpose.

iii. Because it is not owned by individuals, it is a public entity that belongs to the
public at large.

4, Today, 67% of nursing home operations in the State of Utah are or will be participating in the
Upper Payment Limit Program (UPL program). They are choosing this option to help them fill the
Medicaid shortfall gap. Most of these facilities are for profit.

a. The UPL program only applies to Licensed Health Care facilities that have a Medicaid
Certification. There are only two in the valley. Others choose not to take Medicaid
residents.

b. Most of these operations participating in the UPL program are owned by Beaver Valley
Hospital, including a competitor here in Logan.

5. How the UPL program works.

a. Medicaid allows non-state owned governmental entities, such as Cache County, to
participate in an Intergovernmental Transfer (IGT) Agreement of private funds that then
draw down federal matching funds.

b. The law allows payment of these extra funds up to the maximum federal rate. This is
known as “Upper Payment Limit Program,” or UPL program.

c. The Utah Medicaid program pays Sunshine Terrace at rates well below the maximum
federal rate.

i. | have been working with John Curless, Bureau Director with Reimbursement
with this program. (He is an USU Aggie, and comes to support the Cache County
Fair every year.)

ii. The estimated UPL gap for Sunshine Terrace is approximately $118 per Medicaid
resident day. This is almost $1,500,000 net revenue.

d. The UPL program is a WIN, WIN, WIN:

i. The people in need of care will see enhanced care, better programs, better
services, and a better environment;

ii. Cache County will see, by participating in the UPL program, a decrease in cost
for the annual subsidies to Sunshine Terrace Foundation and could even make
some money administering the program;

ili. If the skilled nursing facility was owned by Cache County, Sunshine Terrace
would qualify for the UPL program. The UPL program would put additional
dollars into resident care programs. It would also provide opportunities to
improve and update the aging facility. This program substantially benefits the
resident care programs of Sunshine Terrace and the citizens of Cache County.

The Utah State Plan requires that the UPL Gap payments must be used to improve resident care
and the environment. To ensure that UPL funds are used for the benefit of the residents, the
Department of Health will annually review the nursing facility cost profile report in the following
areas:

a. Plant operations and Maintenance

b. Dietary

c. Laundry and Linen

d. Housekeeping

e. Nursing

f. Recreational Activities and Special Services

In the 2017 General Session H.B. 194, Federal Grants Management Amendments, was passed.
This bill now requires legislative approval to create an Intergovernmental Transfer Program

Sunshine Terrace proposes that we form a work group to evaluate this program in more detail
and how it might benefit the entire community.

MEMORANDUM

TO: Cache County Council
FROM: Dianna Schaeffer, Chief Deputy Auditor
DATE: August 22, 2017

Request for Tax Relief Filing Extension —

Every year Cache County processes hundreds of tax relief applications for various indigent abatements, exclusive
use exemptions, military exemptions, blind exemptions, settlements, and deferrals. The application deadline is
September 1° in most cases, but may be extended until December 31" if the county finds good cause.

e Low Income Abatement - State law §59-2-1107 through §59-2-1109 permits counties to abate taxes for
indigent persons subject to proper application, documentation, and deadlines. Allowable extension
§59-2-1109(2)(d).

¢ Blind Exemption - State law §59-2-1106 provides counties authority to abate taxes for the blind subject to
proper application, documentation, and deadlines. Allowable extension §59-2-1106(2)(b)(ii).

e Military Exemption - State law §59-2-1104 and §59-2-1105 allows counties to abate taxes for veterans and
active duty armed forces subject to proper application, documentation, and deadlines. Military applications
are allowed an “automatic” one year extension per UCA §59-2-1105(2)(b)(i).

¢ Non-Profit Use Exemption — State law §59-2-1102 provides counties authority to abate taxes for religious
charitable, and educational organizations. Applications were due by March 1st and have been processed.

° Settlement/Deferral - No deadlines are mandated in §59-2-1347.

After the tax notices are mailed at the end of October, the county will be besieged by citizens in dire need of tax
relief. We must be compliant with the law of the land while maintaining the intent of the law to help those in
need. Therefore, it is in the best interest of the community that the Cache County Council extends the filing
deadline for low income and blind tax relief to November 30, 2017. By extending the filing deadline, Cache
County can continue helping eligible citizens and remain compliant with the law in regards to applications for tax
relief. This would solve the problem of processing impoverished applicants while remaining legally compliant.
