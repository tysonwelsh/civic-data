2.  Continued discussion and possible action for the proposed Plat Amendment for 
Wright Place Lot 2 Subdivision Amended, located at 4045 N. Woodenshoe Lane, Peoa, 
Utaj; Jon Eric Greene, Applicant. – Tiffanie Northrup-Robinson, Senior Planner. 
 
Planner Northrup-Robinson shared the background for this application. On June 17, 
2021 amendments to Lot 2 of the Wright Subdivision were presented to the Planning 
Commission. The subject lot has wetlands and a building pad. The wetland delineation has

been updated and the applicant wants to modify the platting of the wetlands and remove 
building pad. The Planning Commission asked for an updated letter from the Army Corps of 
Engineers concerning the updated wetlands delineation. The letter dated July 8, 2021 has

Findings of Fact:  
1. According to the Summit County tax assessment rolls, GPG Investments LLC is the listed fee 
title owner of Parcel WP-2.  
2. The applicant is Jon-Eric Greene, is the representative of the owner of Parcel WP-2.  
3. WP-2 is approximately 8.23 acres in size.  
4. WP-2 is located at 4045 N. Woodenshoe Lane, Peoa, Utah.  
5. Parcel WP-2 is with the Agriculture 40 (AG-40) Zoning District.  
6. The Wright Place Subdivision was originally recorded in 2004 and consisted of four (4) lots, 
ranging from 1.62 to 9.84 acres.  
7. In 2014, the applicant obtained an updated Wetland delineation that was prepared by Wise 
Earth. That delineation received preliminary approval from the US Army Corp of Engineers in a 
letter dated May 13, 2014.  
8. On March 15, 2021, an application was submitted to amend the platted delineated wetlands as 
they are currently depicted on the original subdivision plat and remove the designated building 
pad for Lot 2 of the Wright Place Subdivision.  
9. Service providers have reviewed the plat for compliance with applicable standards.  
10. On June 17, 2021, the ESCPC conducted a public hearing and discussed the proposed 
amendment. The Commission requested that the applicant provide and updated letter from the 
USACE prior to making a final decision.  
11. On July 8, 2021, the USACE made a preliminary jurisdictional determination based on 
aquatic resources delineation supported by documentation submitted by Wise Earth. The 
preliminary approval is consistent with the revised wetlands indicated on the proposed plat 
amendment.  
12. Staff has reviewed the plat for compliance with applicable Development Code standards.  
 
Conclusions of Law:  
1. There is good cause for the amendment as the proposed amendment to modify the platted 
wetlands to be consistent with the updated US Army Corp of Engineers preliminary jurisdiction 
determination, dated July 8, 2021.  
2. No increases in density would occur because of this amendment.  
 
Conditions of Approval:  
1. All service provider conditions shall be met.

3.  Public hearing and possible action regarding a plat amendment application to 
move the building pad on 6225 El Ridge Drive, Peoa, Summit County, Utah; Parcel 
ERDG-3 (3.76 acres); Scott Simons, Applicant. – Madlyn McDonough, County Planner. 
 
Planner McDonough shared the background for this application. The parcel is located off 
Brown’s Canyon Road and was originally subdivided in 1997. The applicant is proposing to 
move the building pad on Lot 3 to meet the current County front and side setbacks to 
utilize the least steep area of the lot for building.  Additionally, the applicant proposes to 
record an easement recognizing access to an existing driveway and propane tank on a 
neighboring lot that accidentally encroached on Lot 3. There will not be any change in 
access areas. The application meets the Standards of the Code and Staff recommends 
approval according to the Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law, and Conditions of Approval 
found in the Staff Report. 
 
The Public Hearing was opened.

approved, they had a hard time knowing where to do the survey. 
The Public Hearing was closed.

Findings of Fact:  
1. On May 21, 2021, a Plat Amendment application was submitted for a building pad adjustment 
on Lot 3 of the Elk Ridge subdivision.  
2. The lot is located at 6225 North Elk Ridge Road in Peoa, Utah 84061.  
3. The underlying zoning is for this lot is AG/Grazing - 40.

of an institutional use facility at 1755 South Hoytsville Road (Summit County Public 
Works Building); Summit County, Derrick Radke, Representative, Applicant. – Ray 
Milliner, Principal Planner.

Planner Milliner shared the background for this application. The request is for the 
expansion of the existing Public Works building for a salt storage addition, indoor 
equipment storage, and an office remodel to accommodate existing material and 
equipment. The application meets the requirements for approval and Staff recommends 
approval according to the Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law, and Conditions of approval 
found in the Staff Report. 
Commissioner Sargent asked if the new buildings would be similar to the existing 
architecture. Planner Milliner answered in the affirmative. 
Commissioner Peterson asked if there has been any feedback from the neighbors. 
Planner Milliner answered that Staff has not received any public feedback. 
 
The Public Hearing was opened. 
Jamie Barkdull is a neighbor to the Public Works building and shared her confusion of 
where the building is going to be. Planner Milliner explained where the building would be 
located. Derrick Radke stated that she might see the roof of the salt storage building from 
her home. Ms. Barkdull stated that her current view is not very attractive and asked if a 
fence would be put in. Derrick Radke answered that there are plans for clean up of the 
area but not for a fence. The accesses will be the same. 
The Public Hearing was closed. 
Derrick Radke stated that the proposed storage is for existing equipment and there are 
not plans to add equipment. The sun and the weather has beat up the existing equipment. 
There is some junk on the hill that will go into storage as well. There are more employees

Commissioner Benson stated there is a lot of junk being stored and there should be some

Findings of Fact:  
1. The applicant, Summit County, is requesting approval of an expansion of the existing Public 
Works facility at 1755 S. Hoytsville Road.  
2. The property is located on parcel #NS-131-D-X and is 7.78 Acres in size.  
3. The property is in the Agriculture - 5 (AG-5) zone.  
4. The Public Works building is listed as an “institutional use” in the Eastern Summit County 
Development Code.  
5. An institutional use is a conditional use in the AG-5 zone.  
6. Access to the property is from South Hoytsville Road.  
7. The applicant is proposing to add additional enclosed equipment storage to protect the 
equipment from the elements, salt storage to reduce the cost of contractor hauled deicing 
materials, and to address short comings of the existing administrative space.  
8. The uses in the area are a mix of gravel/rock quarry uses, agricultural and residential.  
9. The site is currently in use as a public works building and most of the equipment and materials 
proposed to be stored in the new buildings are currently on site being stored outside.  
10. The administrative expansion will accommodate the needs of existing employees.  
11. There is adequate septic and water for the site.  
12. There are steep slopes in the area, but none of the proposed development will be located on a 
slope greater than 30%.  
13. None of the development will break the ridgeline.  
14. There are no wetlands on the site.

1.  Discussion on Possible Accessory Structure Amendments. – Patrick Putt, 
Community Development Director 
 
Director Putt shared the background for this discussion. This is an initial conversation as a 
follow-up to an application for an accessory building. The Eastern Summit County Code 
allows by right an accessory building under 2,000 square feet, and over depending on the 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
August 19, 2021 
Page 8 of 9 
 
zone. The amendments can be pulled together relatively timely and similar amendments 
are being worked on for Snyderville Basin. Staff would like to know what elements the 
Planning Commission would like explored. 
Commissioner Sargent stated that there needs to be a definition to “incidental to primary 
use.”  
Director Putt stated that there should not be new regulations that are punitive to true 
agricultural operations or to create further burdens on those operations. There are 
differences in barns versus storage or a garage. 
Commissioner Clyde stated there is an application for someone’s toy storage that is 
similar in size to the entire Public Works area. The Code should try to protect the overall 
aesthetic to the community, including size limits, façade variation, setback increases 
related to the size of the building, different standards for lot size and zone, not allowing an 
accessory building unless the primary building has been built, and a limit on the number of 
accessory buildings. Agriculture uses a different track and legitimate agriculture is under 
different rules. Legitimate agriculture should be qualified as green belt for the ag 
exemption. The definitions need to be specific enough. 
Director Putt stated that Staff will start working on the amendments.
