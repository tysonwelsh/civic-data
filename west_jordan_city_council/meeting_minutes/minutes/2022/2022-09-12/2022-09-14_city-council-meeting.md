 1. CALL TO ORDER
Vice Chair Green called the regular meeting to order at 7:00 p.m. He noted that Council Member
Worthen and Mayor Burton were joining remotely, and Council Member Whitelock and Bloom were
excused. He reminded the Council that a motion would require four votes in favor to pass.

Vice Chair Green reported that at the request of the developer, the Bowman’s Arrow rezone had been
pulled from the agenda and encouraged those present who wished to comment on the issue to do so
during the Public Comment period.


 2. PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE
Dave Naylor led participants in the pledge of allegiance.

 3. PUBLIC COMMENT
Vice Chair Green opened the public comment period at 7:05 p.m.

Citizen Comments:
June Christiansen, West Jordan resident, asked whether or not public comments submitted via email
or phone call regarding Bowman’s Arrow would be part of the public record. She could not object
more to the way the proposed development was presented and hoped something better was brought
back.
Jim Dunnigan introduced himself as a Representative of District 39 in the State House of
Representatives and informed the Council that District 39 would become District 36 following
redistricting. He looked forward to representing West Jordan.

City Council Meeting Minutes             Wednesday, September 14, 2022                        Page 1
Ken Wilhite, West Jordan resident, was glad to see Representative Dunnigan at the meeting, and
asked if any other representatives from the west side had showed interest in West Jordan Council
meetings. He believed the issues affected the entire west side of the valley, and expressed the opinion
that people needed representatives to represent them at the State Capitol.
Alexandra Eframo noted her donation for K9 vests and expressed disappointment that K9 Maya had
not been vested before leaving home. She was broken hearted over the unfortunate incident.
Michael Harman, West Jordan resident, expressed concerns of insufficient infrastructure for the
amount of high-density housing currently planned and believed the City was woefully unprepared
for the number of residential units being proposed. He spoke of the responsibility Council had to
ensure the quality of life, safety, security, and convenience for those who already lived in the area.
Responding to a question from Council Member Jacob, Mr. Harman said his opposition would be
reduced somewhat if he perceived sufficient infrastructure was in place.
Anne Forester, West Jordan resident, lives near the planned Bowman’s Arrow development. She had
considered living in Herriman before moving to West Jordan but was concerned about high-density
development occurring. Ms. Forester asked the Council to not “Herriman-ize” West Jordan. She spoke
of decline in quality of life with the increased traffic and crowded schools and wanted to see more of
what was already on the west side of the City rather than high density.
Brian Looney, West Jordan resident, felt the citizens had spoken multiple times regarding the
proposed Bowman’s Arrow development and made their opinions clear. He believed schools were
the biggest problem in his mind and hoped the Council would listen to the residents who had spoken.
Vice Chair Green closed public comments at 7:26 pm.

 4. EXECUTIVE REPORTS TO COUNCIL
       a. Mayor’s Report
          Mayor Dirk Burton thanked Representative Dunnigan for attending the Council meeting.
          He spoke of a ribbon cutting for Porter Rockwell, a recent chalk event, a spelling bee at
          the Senior Center, the “Out of Darkness” suicide walk, and the recent Day of Service. The
          Mayor mentioned upcoming Jordan School District Fall Fair, Sugar Factory Playhouse
          production, Monster Truck Rally, and continued food truck events.

       b. City Administrator’s Report
          Parks Supervisor Brian Clegg updated the Council regarding the 8600 South bridge
          project, out-of-house overlays, and inhouse overlays. Council Member Pack expressed
          appreciation for staff’s responsiveness to citizen concerns. City Administrator Korban Lee
          commented on the amount of road maintenance accomplished by staff that summer.
          Council Member McConnehey complimented Public Works staff for a specific job
          completed just over a month early and under budget.

           Public Services Director Isaac Astill reported on the recent Day of Service and was grateful
           for the volunteerism.

 5. PUBLIC HEARINGS
       a. Bowman’s Arrow Rezone
          At the request of the applicant, the item was pulled from the agenda and not considered
          for action

       b. Ordinance No. 22-32 enacting pet boarding
          City Planner/Zoning Administrator Larry Gardner reported that modifications requested
          by the Council at the June 22, 2022 Council meeting were incorporated into proposed
          amendments to City Code regarding pet boarding.

           Vice Chair Green opened a public hearing at 7:41 pm.
           Comments:
           None
           Vice Chair Green closed the public hearing at 7:41 pm.

           MOTION: Council Member McConnehey moved to APPROVE Ordinance No. 22-
                   32 amending the 2009 West Jordan City Code enacting pet boarding as
                   a definition and as a conditional use in the SC-2 and SC-3 commercial
                   zones and as a conditional use in all manufacturing zones; sections 13-
                   5E-3 and 13-5F-2, and the “kennel” and “pet boarding” definitions in sec.
                   13-2-3. Council Member Worthen seconded the motion.

           The vote was recorded as follows:
           Yes: Chris McConnehey, Zach Jacob, Melissa Worthen, David Pack, Kelvin Green
           No:
           Absent: Pamela Bloom, Kayleen Whitelock
           The motion passed 5-0.

       c. Ordinance No. 22-40 Park Strip Amendments
          Public Services Director Isaac Astill explained the proposed amendment would limit sod

           He thanked staff, especially Mr. Naylor, for work done behind the scenes. Vice Chair Green
           spoke of spending time with City Parks employees at the recent annual corn event. He
           expressed appreciation for their dedication.
