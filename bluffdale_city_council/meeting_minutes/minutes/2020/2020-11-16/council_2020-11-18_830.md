1.       Welcome and Dinner.

Mayor Timothy called the meeting to order at approximately 4:35 p.m. and welcomed those
present. He publicly thanked those who wished him a happy birthday. All Members of the City
Council were present. He also acknowledged the presence of Planning Commission Member,
Debbie Cragun.

2.       2020 Bluffdale Economic Development Strategic Plan Progress Review.

City Planner/Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell presented the Strategic Plan and
Economic Development Strategy. He noted that some of the items are goals that will be an
ongoing priority with no end date. Certain tasks contain dates and are tied to adopting a budget,
such as redevelopment construction projects.

A Transportation Plan was adopted as recently as February 2020 that should be reviewed. For
example, the I-15 interchange is already on the plan. Mayor Timothy indicated that he would
like to see what things they could do as a City with regard to the items on the long-range plan,
such as the I-15 interchange. He would like to see specific tasks that should be accomplished to

reach the goals set in the long-range plan. He suggested the creation of sub-projects that are
necessary to achieving the strategic goals. The sub-projects or sub-goals could be short, medium
or long-range goals designed to achieve the overall goals. He clarified that the discussion of
these issues at tonight’s meeting was designed to give them a head start on future discussions.
Mr. Crowell suggested that it would be more efficient to address specific goals.

City Manager, Mark Reid raised the issue of 14600 South. As staff, they have been working on
that project for 10 years. Initially, it involved the widening of Redwood Road with the idea that
the Utah Department of Transportation (“UDOT”) would push 14600 South through to Redwood
Road, continue it across Frost Court, and merge into 14400 South. Through the years, they have
conducted studies in an effort to come up with ideas to address the problems at the interchange,
which is now at failure. It became a priority when a developer purchased property in the area. It
was a good example of something that gets worked on over time and then becomes a priority as
development occurs.

Mr. Crowell acknowledged that a lot of work remains to be done with regard to 14600 South. He
had been talking with the Utah Transit Authority (“UTA”) for nine years and could not possibly
summarize those conversations in one hour. He stated that there are new people involved with
the project that may result in a new approach to future development.

Additionally, the Point of the Mountain Alternatives Analysis looks at a common ground
alternative. They will know more after a Steering Committee Meeting that will allow them to
engage UTA as they work through the General Plan. He suggested that they could review the
sufficiency of the long-range plan while they work on the General Plan.

Mr. Crowell suggested comparing the maps from UDOT, UTA, the Wasatch Front Regional
Council (“WFRC”), and the City to compare the strategies and influence on future plans. He
noted that some of the line items cross over between zoning, capital allocation, and real estate
acquisition, which involves the budgeting of funds.

Mr. Crowell reported that they have completed the Sign Ordinance, and when the commercial
spaces start becoming occupied, they will see the results of that Ordinance. He commented that
one of the results will be the absence of a proliferation of pole signs along some corridors. He
suggested that they explore whether the General Plan has the potential for a Center. He had
spoken to their consultants about this and suggested that it be explored further.

Mr. Crowell addressed the issue of a Form-Based Code. They could combine their efforts on
zoning across some of the corridors that could either be covered on a project basis or in the
General Plan. They have resources in terms of their consultants as well as public input on some
ideas. He invited those who are well versed in marketing, branding, and imaging to speak to
those items. They renamed one street with a commemorative name and discussed renaming
other streets as well.

Mr. Crowell stated that they should identify an area to prioritize and place the first monument
sign. By next July, which is the beginning of the next fiscal year, they should be in a position to
identify the items they will need to request funding for. He suggested that at the beginning of the
year they revisit the Strategic Plan and look at the priorities to determine what changed from the
previous year. He hoped to have a meeting to go through the flow chart.

In terms of business licensing, significant work had been done to modernize the 1979 Ordinance.
Mr. Crowell expressed his view that this work has been focused on keeping the changes
‘business-friendly’ by streamlining the administration of licenses. They could spend some time
providing information to the Council regarding the allocation of resources for the various
economic development departments to increase efficiency. He was not sure how they would
integrate the Courts into Economic Development. Mayor Timothy explained that they are
looking at instituting an Administrative Court, which would help with revenue.

Mr. Crowell did not believe they have realized the potential of the Redevelopment Agency
(“RDA”) funds. The RDA has resources available and a limited time to obtain the funds.
Mr. Reid looked forward to their RDA discussion at the next City Council Meeting and stated
that there is the potential to do things with a small amount of money. Mr. Reid added that for the
first time ever, they have a relationship with all three Commissioners from UDOT. They were
continuing their efforts with UTA to get them to recognize Bluffdale. The relationships with the
Commissioners should assist Bluffdale in developing a strategy for what they want to accomplish
and implement those steps with UTA.

Mr. Crowell reported that COVID-19 has forced some technologies to accelerate and ridership
and transit should help their relationship with UTA. He expressed the opinion that the common
ground alternative on the Blue Line Study is not bad for Bluffdale because it brings transit stops
closer and has an early opportunity for construction. He recognized that the concern was
whether UTA believes in Bluffdale.

Mr. Crowell included retail and service providers in the Strategic Plan and noted that in other
parts of the State, they are the main economic development factors that are being prioritized.

Mayor Timothy added that the events of 2020 have changed the paths of a number of businesses,
individuals, cities, counties, and governments. As a result, last year’s plan likely needs to be
changed. He posed the question of how they can know what they should be working on with
regard to economic development and determine what will be important for the future. For
example, he previously thought that office space would be a job creator, but now that view is
changed. Many office buildings are now empty and construction on office buildings has been
halted because there is no need for the space. He suggested that they explore what direction to
take.

Mr. Crowell expressed his view that they have not discussed how the plan fits into a fiscally
sustainability policy, which may lead them toward projects that they may not be interested in.

He gave an example of commercial flex businesses, which are being absorbed fairly quickly into
the market. He suggested looking at trends as part of creating a fiscal plan to be included in the
Economic Development Plan. Much of that information will depend on the direction from
elected officials who are in turn getting ideas from their constituents.

Finance Director, Bruce Kartchner reported that over the years he has consulted with different
types of businesses and has learned that successful businesses view flexibility as overly rigid. A
successful business needs to be fluid. He explained that what is key is to make sure that their
decisions have fluidity built into them as the business, commercial, and municipal landscapes
change.

Mr. Crowell noted that the Strategic Plan does not address how housing fits into the Economic
Development Plan. He suggested that be included in the global conversation since housing has
an impact on economic development.

Council Member Kallas commented that the plan lays out what they are doing and is relevant in
considering what is being done. Economic development is not something with an end date and is
an ongoing process. The annual plan measures specific goals that require update and revision.
He specifically addressed Bluffdale Boulevard. The strategy was to have a completed 14600
South Master Plan while the goal was to develop and complete a Master Plan that shows the
future vision of the corridor. On this particular goal, they set an end date of May 1 and should
determine what will be accomplished by that date and how it is progressing. He wanted to know
more about the progress of that particular project. He viewed the Master Plan as a rendering of
what a particular corridor will look like.

Mr. Crowell added that they have renderings. What they need are the specifics of the plan. His
idea was to see if they could utilize Eastern Bluffdale EDA Funds to procure a consultant to help
implement the specifics of the plan. Council Member Kallas commented that shortly before
finalizing the budget, the plan would be updated so that the budget reflects what is in the plan,
however, he was flexible in terms of the timing.

Mr. Crowell noted that he thought about the plan in the context of a cyclist who uses the corridor
and the dangers he is confronted with. With multi-jurisdictional, multi-utility companies, it takes
a great deal to get to the point of developing a specific project plan. He highlighted the example
of the sub-task of retaining a consultant to evaluate the underground utilities.

In response to an inquiry, Mr. Crowell stated that they would be proposing to hire a consultant.
In terms of timing, they could identify and line item out funds in Eastern Bluffdale and begin
procurement immediately. There are firms that provide multi-disciplinary services such as
engineering and utilities, but they also require the services of Planning and Landscape Architects
to contribute to the visual aesthetic. They still have work to do in the corridor in terms of zoning
that can likely be covered in the General Plan.

Mayor Timothy suggested separating the Planning Department from the Economic Development
Department and hiring a Director for each. It was suggested that the division of responsibility
and the increase in staff would help them address the specifics of the plan. Mr. Crowell
disagreed with the notion that the Planning Department needs more resources to focus on
economic development.

Mayor Timothy explained that this is just a discussion of potential options regarding structure.
He stated that they need a comprehensive Economic Development Plan before specific tasks can
be undertaken toward a goal. They need to know what they want the City to look like as a whole
and that specific tasks will be done with the goal of meeting that plan.

It was noted that the Economic Development Plan is included as one of the strategies. Mayor
Timothy posed the question of how the documents presented show how they will achieve the
goals in the Economic Development Plan. He further expressed his view that the Economic
Development Plan does not include the details or include deadlines for specific goals or
prioritization of the specific sub-tasks. He opined that an Economic Development Department
could possibly be more resourced and specialized to allow them to tie all of the elements together
so that when they approve an expenditure of $100,000 for a consultant, they know they are doing
it at the right place and time.

Council Member Kallas expressed his view that they have already had that discussion and
determined that rather than spending one year hiring someone to develop a comprehensive
Economic Development Plan, they created a sub-committee to prepare the plan that will be used.
He expressed his view that the plan presented today is detailed enough. Mayor Timothy did not
suggest that the plan be thrown out but sees it more as a starting point. He wanted to see
something with more detail and more information on priorities.

Council Member Kallas highlighted that part of the presented document that includes reviewing
at least five priority or problematic ordinances. He also highlighted the task of completing an
Education Training Program for the City Council. He explained that the high-level view of the
plan was that they will update and enhance economic development functions and tools within the
City. The strategy to achieve that high-level view was to create an Economic Development
Department and provide necessary resources to achieve their goals. For this year, the specific
goal was that by April 2021, they would review the functions and funding of the department, its
interactions with other city departments, and the level of funding so that it may carry out the
priorities that have been set. He explained that part of this goal would be accomplished through
the budgeting process.

Council Member Kallas applied the same analysis to the high-level plan to implement City
processes and ordinances that are business-friendly. The goal was to complete five tasks by
May 1, 2021, one of which they are addressing tonight.

Mayor Timothy reiterated his view that the document needs to identify more specific tasks
required to achieve their desired goals. A detailed plan upfront will provide a foundation to
direct resources for responding to developers.

Council Member Gaston expressed his appreciation for the excellent work done by Mr. Crowell.
He commented that in the past they have had developers take advantage of the City and in the
last few years, they have set forth exactly what they want in Bluffdale, including design and
signage. They have had more movement in the past two years than Bluffdale has had throughout
its history.

Council Member Gaston added that the work currently being done on 2700 West will accomplish
100 years’ worth of work for Bluffdale. There are only a few areas that can be developed and
they are doing what is right for the citizens. If they add more staff, believed Mr. Crowell could
focus more on economic development. He stated that they have a great team in place and unless
the department feels they need more staff, he believed the department was doing well.

Council Member Gaston further commented that the City is close to achieving its stated design
objectives. He participated in meetings with developers, which were all very positive. The
developers appeared to be excited to know that the City has a vision for development. He was
proud of the work they have done and believed they were moving in the right direction.

Council Member Hales echoed the comments made by Council Member Gaston and expressed
his appreciation to Mr. Crowell for his efforts.

Council Member Kallas directed attention to the plan’s objectives, strategies, and goals. It was
noted that it is an annual document that includes as its first goal to review and adopt new
development standards. They discussed development standards and have a pending ordinance in
place. Council Member Kallas believed they were on track to accomplish this goal.

The next goal was to review and adopt a new sign ordinance, which has been done. Item number
three was to study and review Form-Based Codes. They have had thorough discussion on this
task and it was determined that they would not create Form-Based Codes.

The items addressing zoning on key corridors were being addressed with 2700 West receiving
priority. An update on this item was requested from Mr. Crowell.

Mr. Crowell commented that staff appreciates support for the work they do. They discuss
resources within the department often and they intend to discuss issues with the Council in the
future. They all want the City to succeed and have a team that is uniquely qualified to help them
achieve that.

Mr. Kartchner asked about using EDA money to fund a potential study. He noted that they were
planning to address a budget amendment as part of their December meeting to address

It was agreed that the issue would be included in their budget adjustment with a more detailed
conversation to take place at the next meeting.

Mr. Crowell returned to the issue of 2700 West and stated that the main idea from the General
Plan and some of the Strategic Plan goals was to enhance the site and building design throughout
the community. They have pending plan reviews around Heritage Press, 855 West, and other
properties. They should also think about how these concepts may apply to the property owned
by the City. Specific to 2700 West, he had spoken with everyone who is currently developing
along that corridor and their respective design professionals. They will have to address the
building types with some of the developers who have specific designs in mind. Mr. Crowell
noted that the developers have been informed that they can utilize tilt-ups, but the Council must
be aware that it will still look like a tilt-up, even if they add a lot to it.

Council Member Gaston had seen buildings that have been painted to change out the modern
aesthetic. He commented that the color design standards will set themselves up well for the
future.

Mr. Crowell noted that with the assistance of their design consultants, they have established
details on how to make buildings look consistent with the design concept. On 2700 West, they
want a storefront feel, with fronts on a smaller scale with design enhancements. They consider
all input received and will incorporate it into current developments. The matter was to be
discussed further when the Council revisits the General Plan.

Council Member Aston asked about the definition of “country” or “classic.” Mr. Crowell noted
that they should discuss whether they want a unifying design theme along that corridor. If the
unifying theme is classic, they will need to define what classic means. He was working through
comments to develop something that can be included in the Plan.

Mr. Crowell noted that architects who design tilt-up projects can do amazing things design-wise.
He added that this could potentially create a zoning issue because trying to create a specific
design theme while leaving the zone open can create issues. Some cities establish zones for
different types of building types, such as a business or industrial park. He cited the buffering
between zones as an example of a design concept that could be included in the base ordinance.

Because they are experiencing high growth, they now have to set the tone for the built
environment. Regardless of the plan, developers will raise the issue of costs to comply with the
design requirements. They want to allow architects the flexibility to have creativity in their
designs while making sure that the designs are consistent with the design theme. If they want
