11           1.2      Mayor’s Report
12
13          Mayor Kristie Overson explained that the city had recently hosted a luncheon for local
14   legislators to discuss not only their own priorities but also those of the city. New legislators who
15   would represent a small portion of the city were Mark Wheatley and Ashley Matthews.              This
16   luncheon was an annual tradition which allowed Taylorsville to continue to foster strong
17   relationships with local legislators.
18
19   The legislative session had begun the previous day which was also “Day at the Legislature” for
20   Taylorsville’s Youth Council and Youth Councils of other local cities. There were break-out
21   sessions to learn about designing flags, a self-guided tour around the capitol, and a visit with Rep.
22   Karen Kwan. Additionally, the Youth Council members watched Gov. Cox’ news conference take
23   place in the Rotunda and were able to meet him briefly and snap a photo when he was on his way
24   to that news conference. They then watched the opening of a house session and the legislators
25   debate. The kids were particularly interested to hear the discussion regarding the “test to stay”
26   program taking place in public schools during the pandemic. Mayor Overson expressed her
27   appreciation to Councilmembers Burgess, Harker and Chair Barbieri for participating in the days’
28   events.
29
30   The Mayor then took a moment to highlight Youth Ambassador Emma Powers whose service
31   project this year was a clothing drive to benefit the homeless population. Among other donations,
32   a $5,000 donation from the Crossroads of Taylorsville Shopping Center was given on Ms. Powers’
33   behalf to the Rescue Mission of Salt Lake. That $5,000 qualified for a matching $5,000 grant from
34   a separate donor.
35
36   Mayor Overson stated that police and fire personnel were really taking a hit due to COVID. Other
37   city employees were also being affected and, in some cases, were working from home. She
38   expressed appreciation to the entire staff.

11           3.1      Taylorsville Police Department Report – Chief Brady Cottam
12
13   Chief Brady Cottam introduced the Employee of the Month for September, October, and
14   November 2021.
15
16   The September 2021 Employee of the Month was Det. Cory Lavin. Chief Cottam described the
17   events of one particular evening wherein Det. Lavin ended up arresting an individual for various
18   crimes. That individual was what is known as a restricted person, meaning he could not lawfully
19   be in possession of a firearm. Not only did he have a gun, but it was loaded with just two rounds.
20   It was believed that he had intended to take the life of his ex-girlfriend as well as his own life. By
21   arresting the individual when he did, Chief Cottam had no doubt that Det. Lavin had saved lives
22   that evening.
23
24   The October 2021 Employee of the Month was Sgt. Dan Christensen who was nominated by one
25   of his peers. Last October Sgt. Christensen had come across a man who was living under the
26   Jordan River Trail Bridge. He cultivated a relationship with the gentleman and went above and
27   beyond to help him. To be eligible for housing, the gentleman needed to obtain valid government
28   identification. Sgt. Christensen helped him fill out the application and then accompanied him to
29   the Driver License Division to submit the application and receive an I.D. card. With that I.D. card,
30   Sgt. Christensen was able to help the gentleman obtain housing and accompanied him to see it for
31   the first time.
32
33   Chief Cottam explained that he had nominated Records Manager Donny Gasu as the November
34   2021 Employee of the Month. The Chief explained how very valuable Mr. Gasu was to the
35   department in a variety of capacities. Not only was he willing and able to do whatever was asked
36   of him, but he did it with a consistently positive attitude.
37
38   The Chief then presented the council with a statistical report regarding crime in Taylorsville. His
39   report included information specific to each council district. He and the council discussed the
40   multitude of traffic issues in the city, whether it was speeding or critical traffic accidents. He also

 4          3.2       Bangerter Highway Environmental Study – Katie Kourianos
 5
 6         Katie Kourianos was a public engagement consultant hired by the Utah Department of
 7   Transportation (UDOT). Ms. Kourianos explained that she was present to update the city council
 8   on the environmental study pertaining to the 4700 South Bangerter Highway intersection project
 9   to convert the highway to a true freeway. The primary goal was to improve safety. Secondary to
10   that was a desire to increase mobility and reduce travel time.
11
12   Since beginning the study, various concepts/alternatives were created and reviewed.
13   Neighborhood meetings had been held near the intersection the previous week which were
14   designed for property owners specifically impacted by the project. Meetings for the broader public
15   would be held at a later date. It was hoped that the study would be finalized in March.
16
17   The first decision to be made was to determine which type of interchange would best meet project
18   goals. UDOT sought a solution that would not just work today but would accommodate the traffic
19   needs projected out to 2050.
20
21   Although a Tight Diamond Interchange (TDI) had been considered that option was eventually
22   eliminated as it would not withstand the amount of traffic anticipated to be present in 2050.
23   Instead, the study recommended a SPUI—a Single Point Urban Interchange—which had a greater
24   capacity.
25
26   The next thing to be established was which direction Bangerter should be shifted to make the new
27   interchange work. It had been determined that it would be best to shift the road to the east which
28   had the smallest property impact as well as the lowest cost.
29
30   The vertical alignment of the roadway—whether Bangerter would go over or under 4700 South--
31   had not yet been decided and would be at least partially determined by available funding. An
32   overpass was typically part of the base cost and UDOT historically had worked with local
33   municipalities to share the additional cost if an underpass was preferable. It was Ms. Kourianos’
34   understanding that Taylorsville was not particularly interested in the underpass option although
35   she mentioned that West Valley City was considering it.
36
37   A full road closure of 4700 South in both directions for 9-12 months was anticipated during
38   construction for safety reasons. She wished the city to have as much notice of that as possible.
39
40   The next step on the project before construction could begin was to determine fair market value of
41   certain nearby properties before beginning the acquisition process. Public comments were

36           5.1      Ordinance No. 22-01– An Ordinance of the City of Taylorsville approving text
37                    amendments to Taylorsville Municipal Code Chapter 13.21, Section 13.36.130, and
38                    Section 13.36.200 related to Private Lanes and Shared Driveways – Jim Spung
39
40        Senior Planner Jim Spung explained that current city code required every dwelling unit to
41   have direct access to a public or private street for vehicles. There was no mechanism to allow for
     Taylorsville City Council Minutes
     January 19, 2022
     Page 7



 1   a shared driveway for more than two units as would be the case for townhomes. This application
 2   had previously gone before the city council on May 19, 2021 and the council had voted to table
 3   the item and directed staff to determine how many other properties within the city could potentially
 4   be impacted by the proposed ordinance. Staff research showed that 97 properties could be impacted
 5   by the ordinance that the applicant proposed.
 6
 7   After several internal meetings, both staff and city administration were still concerned about the
 8   following:
 9       • Potential impacts to existing single-family neighborhood character
10       • Ensuring adequate off-street parking to minimize impacts to adjacent neighborhoods
11       • Ensuring sufficient landscape and buffering standards adjacent to driving surfaces and
12          surrounding properties
13
14   Despite a positive recommendation from the planning commission, staff could not recommend
15   approval of Ordinance 22-01. Mr. Spung indicated that alternate approaches might be discussed
16   as part of the larger and more comprehensive update of the Land Development Code which was
17   currently underway.
18
19        Chair Barbieri invited the applicant to address the council. Steve McCutcheon stated that he
20   represented applicant Teresa Thornberg. He explained that the applicant’s proposal was to limit
21   the number of townhomes to eight and the maximum number of units to ten. He also made some
22   recommendations regarding the width of private lanes and explained that two-car enclosed garages
23   be required. He stated that without the ordinance, Ms. Thornberg’s and potentially others’
24   property was unimprovable.
25
26   Chair Barbieri pointed out that the council was interested in discussing the proposed ordinance as
27   it would affect the entire city, not solely Ms. Thornberg’s specific piece of property. She asked
28   that he make any final comments before the council began their discussion.
29
30   Councilmember Harker expressed appreciation to the applicant for the due diligence that had
31   clearly been conducted. However, as much as she wanted to approve how the applicant wished to
32   develop her property, she was concerned about making an ordinance change for a single parcel
33   when it would apply to the entire city and potentially have adverse, unintended consequences.
34   Councilmember Burgess agreed.
35
36   Councilmember Cochran referred to Exhibit D in the presentation showing a maximum of ten units
37   to share a single driveway. He felt it was overly congested.
