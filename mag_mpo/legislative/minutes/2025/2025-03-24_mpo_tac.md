Wasatch Choice Draft Vision (00:03:35) Dan Wayne presented the Wasatch Choice vision process, highlighting their progress in developing a regional planning map. Through extensive meetings with local governments, they've expanded their understanding of future development, focusing on four key strategies: transportation, housing, parks, and city centers. Cody Lutz from Envision Utah explained that they've doubled the number of activity centers compared to previous maps, creating a vision of more dense, walkable, and mixed-use areas. The map is intentionally aspirational, respecting local land use authority while providing a regional perspective. In the coming weeks, Envision Utah will launch a web-based platform allowing cities to review and provide feedback on the draft map, with the goal of refining the vision before seeking MPO TAC recommendation and MPO Board approval. Road Functional Classification Data Update (00:11:39) Matthew Silski presented the road functional classification data update, a once-in-a-decade opportunity for cities to review their road network. This process is crucial for federal funding eligibility under the federal aid program. UDOT and MAG have created a first draft of the updated system, providing an interactive map with helpful layers like city master transportation plans and traﬃc volumes. The map allows cities to recommend changes, such as adjusting a road's functional type, removing redundant roads, or adding new roads. Matthew Silski emphasized that this update focuses on existing and funded future roads, using Federal Highway Administration guidance to classify roads as major or minor collectors and arterials. Cities are encouraged to review the map and provide feedback by April 30, with the goal of maintaining regional consistency while respecting local classification preferences. The process will involve cities reviewing the draft, providing comments, and allowing UDOT and MAG to refine the classification before final approval later in the year. Springville 2600 West (00:28:09) Calvin Clark presented the 2600 West corridor preservation in Springville. The request involves five property owners, with Lakeside Land Partners planning imminent development. The city is requiring the developer to donate right-of-way for a three-lane road, while the corridor preservation request seeks to acquire right-of-way for a future five-lane road. The other landowners are not currently developing, so they will dedicate the remaining right-of-way when their properties are developed. The total request for Springville is $1,660,800. The Page 2 of 3 city representatives clarified that the road would be built with minimal access points and function as a regional traﬃc corridor, with potential future expansion funded through impact fees or additional funding from MAG. Jermy Lapine moved to recommend that the MPO Board approve Springville City’s Corridor Preservation request for $1,660,800 for the presented properties for the 2600 West Roadway. Vern Keeslar seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Saratoga Springs Mountain View Corridor (00:39:48) Calvin Clark presented the second corridor preservation request for Saratoga Springs' Mountain View Corridor, explaining a unique situation involving a nonprofit landowner. Typically, MAG requires a voluntary seller certification, but in this case, the nonprofit cannot sign due to legal restrictions. The property is vacant land with an existing two-lane city road, and Saratoga Springs plans to widen it to five lanes this summer. Saratoga Springs staff emphasized the urgent need to address a highly congested road that will funnel traﬃc from Eagle Mountain and Saratoga Springs. After legal consultation, MAG determined they could make a one-time exception to the voluntary seller certification policy. It was suggested treating this as a case-by-case waiver without changing the overall policy. Arron Willson moved to recommend that the MPO Board waive the Voluntary Seller Certification for and approve the city of Saratoga Springs’ request for $970,000 from the Corridor Preservation Fund. Eric Ellis seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Other Business and Adjournment (00:51:40) Chair Jered Johnson stated the next MPO TAC meeting is scheduled for April 28, 2025.
