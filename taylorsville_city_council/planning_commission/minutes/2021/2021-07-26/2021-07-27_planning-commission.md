           Way. (Jim Spung/AICP/Senior Planner)

3.1       Mr. Spung presented this item via Zoom. The property owner, Susan Lund, is requesting consideration to
          amend Lot 21 of the Taylors Cove Phase 1 Subdivision and Lots A and 9 of the Lund’s Landing Subdivision.
          The applicant is seeking the subdivision amendment to create one additional residential lot at 783 West
          Bayport Way. There is an existing single-family dwelling at 793 West Bayport Way while 775 West Bayport
          Way is undeveloped. Approval of a plat amendment does not authorize construction activity on the site.
          The owner would be responsible for securing the appropriate development permits prior to any

         construction activity The proposed subdivision amendment has been reviewed and is compliant with all
         City subdivision and zoning standards and has been recommended for approval by the Development
         Review Committee and referral agencies.

         3.1.1     Lot A of the Lund’s Landing Subdivision includes several restrictions and limitations, those same
                   limitations are carried forward with this amended plat. These parcels are non-buildable, wetlands,
                   flood plain, and permanent open space.

         3.1.2     Rather than providing on-site stormwater detention on Lots 2 and 3, the applicant has prepared a
                   storm water agreement allowing Lots 2 and 3 to drain onto Parcels A and B respectively. Those
                   stormwater agreements will be signed and recorded concurrently with the amended plat
                   document.

         3.1.3     Since the property boundaries of Lot 21 of the Taylors Cove Phase 1 Subdivision and Lot 9 of the
                   Lund’s Landing Subdivision will be re-aligned, the existing easements along those property lines
                   will be relocated to the new boundaries of the new Lots 1, 2, and 3. The amended plat identifies
                   existing easements that will be vacated and the location of new easements that will be established
                   in their place All parties affected by the easement relocation will sign the plat prior to recordation.

         3.1.4     To ensure Parcels A and B have access to a public street right-of-way, the property owner must
                   secure and legally establish either pedestrian or vehicle access to those properties from a public
                   street, prior to recordation of the amended plat with Salt Lake County. Access to Lot A has
                   historically been via a narrow strip of land immediately east of Lot A that is owned by the Utah
                   Division of Parks and Recreation. The applicant is coordinating with the owner of that parcel to
                   establish and formalize a legal means of access for the owners of Parcels A and B of the amended
                   subdivision.

         3.1.5     The Salt Lake County Recorder’s Office has reviewed the proposed plat amendment and has
                   provided the applicant with comments to correct the legal description language on the plat as well
                   as a few other non-substantive corrections. Staff will work with the applicant to correct these
                   matters prior to recording the plat.

3.2      FINDINGS:
         3.2.1  This application was initiated by Susan Lund.
         3.2.2  775 and 793 West Bayport Way are in the Single-Family Residential (R-1-10) zoning district.
         3.2.3  Parcel A at 777 W Bayport Way is in the Single-Family Residential (R-1-40) zoning district.
         3.2.4  The Future Land Use Map designation for these properties on the City’s General Plan is “Low
                Density Residential”.
         3.2.5  The applicant is requesting to amend Lot 21 of the Taylors Cove Phase 1 Subdivision and Lots A
                and 9 of the Lund’s Landing Subdivision.
         3.2.6  The proposed plat amendment would result in the creation of one additional residential building
                lot at 783 West Bayport Way and one additional non-buildable parcel at 787 West Bayport Way
                (i.e., Parcel A).
         3.2.7  The proposed plat amendment meets all subdivision and zoning standards in the City Code.
         3.2.8  To ensure Parcels A and B have access to a public street right-of-way, the property owner must
                secure and legally establish either pedestrian or vehicle access prior to recordation of the
                amended plat with Salt Lake County.
         3.2.9  The owner is responsible for securing the appropriate development permits prior to any
                construction activity.
         3.2.10 The Taylorsville Planning Commission is the decision-making body related to subdivision
                amendment requests.

                   being looked at this evening and the lot immediately to the west which is currently zoned R-1-40.
                   He wanted to know if that lot too was undevelopable open space. Mr. Spung said that is land that
                   is owned by the State of Utah and he was not familiar with the history but believed that the
                   company that developed the subdivision had, in exchange for having a little bit more density than
                   what the current zoning allowed, essentially set aside that lot as open space but he did not know
                   if it was ever recorded as such. That he would need to do a little bit more research to answer the
                   question of if it is undevelopable.

         3.4.2     Commissioner Wendel           said this property is very interesting with the different topography.
                   She asked if there were any environmental concerns about draining down to that back property,
                   either for erosion or contamination of the Jordan River itself. She wanted to know if that were
                   something within the City’s purview to be concerned with as it potentially looks at allowing these
                   plats to be separated from those lots. Mr. Spung said the answer was no and that is something
                   the City Engineer, Ben White, looked at and said that would not be a problem just because that is
                   currently a flood plain and natural drainage area for the area. It actually is better because it runs
                   through that land to be filtered by all the plants and minerals before it goes into the Jordan River
                   rather than through concrete pipes. That makes it a more preferred scenario for a more natural
                   drainage system.         Commissioner Wendel asked for clarification in light of Plat A and B not
                   being zoned residential and asked if that meant there can’t be RV’s parking on them, no type of
                   storage units allowed or any other buildings. Also clarify the issue of dumping on these sites and
                   allowing storage of old cars and pieces of equipment, etc. Mr. Spung said there are many different
                   answers to that. It depends on the ownership. The intention of separating Parcels A and B is with
                   the hope that the owner of Lot 2 would also purchase Parcel A as part of their property. That
                   because it is their property, he saw no reason why they would not be able to have items such as
                   swing sets or clear an area and have a gazebo, etc. If they wanted to park a recreational vehicle
                   back there on a concrete slab they would need a building permit for the concrete slab. That Parcel
                   A will not have a home on the site, and because of that, there could not be an accessory building
                   there. He added that the City has a very limited say in what people can or cannot do on their
                   property as long as they are in compliance with city ordinances.         The second scenario is if it
                   is under separate ownership, Lot 2 is owned by an individual and Parcel A by somebody else, the
                   owner of Parcel A wouldn’t have those same rights because they don’t have a house there. There
                   are City standards that say, in order to have an accessory use of property there must be a primary
                   use, which is a house, so people couldn’t park an RV back there unless there was a house associated
                   with type of use. They could use it for things such as camping, fishing, gatherings, etc.
                   Commissioner Wendel thanked Mr. Spung, saying that she just wanted it on public record because
                   that differentiation is important to understand with what happens to the property moving
                   forward.
