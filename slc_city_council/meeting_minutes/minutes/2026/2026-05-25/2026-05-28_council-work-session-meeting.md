**Resolution: Intention to Designate The Sugar House Special Assessment Area**   ~ 2:30 p.m.
  30 min.
The Council will receive a briefing about a Resolution of Intention to designate the Sugar House Business District 2027 (SHBD-27), establishing a Special Assessment Area (SAA) in the core commercial area of Sugar House. The proposal would also establish a secondary assessment for specialty lighting and signage for a three-year period.

**FYI – Project Timeline:** (subject to change per Chair direction or Council discussion)
Briefing - Thursday, May 28, 2026
Set Public Hearing Date -  Tuesday, July 14, 2026
Hold hearing to accept public comment - Tuesday, August 18, 2026 at 7 p.m.
TENTATIVE Council Action - Tuesday, June 9, 2026

**Summary:**

Austin Kimmel provided an introduction. Colin Gibbs clarified that staff was requesting adoption of a resolution of intention to designate the Sugar House Business District 2027 Special Assessment Area and welcomed representatives from the Sugar House Chamber and Zions Public Finance.

Liz Ward reviewed the background of the request, the proposed area boundaries, tentative budget assumptions, proposed activities, statutory protest requirements, and the projected timeline, as well noting the proposed Special Assessment Area would be active for three years, with the potential for renewal.

Council Members and staff discussed:

* Comparison to the Downtown Special Assessment Area process, with staff noting the Sugar House area would follow the same process but at a smaller scale
* Clarification that the request was community-driven and that the City’s role was to steward the process
* Support for the proposal as a tool to strengthen the identity, vitality, and long-term economic resilience of the Sugar House Business District

**Fiscal Year 2026-27 Budget: Non-Departmental**   ~ 3:00 p.m.
  30 min.
The Council will receive a briefing about the proposed Non-Departmental budget for Fiscal Year 2026-27, which accounts for transfers to other funds, grants, and other special revenue funds that do not belong to particular City departments.

**FYI – Project Timeline:** (subject to change per Chair direction or Council discussion)
Briefing - Thursday, May 28, 2026
Set Public Hearing Date -  Tuesday, April 21, 2026 and Tuesday, May 19, 2026
Hold hearing to accept public comment - Tuesday, May 19, 2026 and Tuesday, June 2, 2026 at 7 p.m.
TENTATIVE Council Action - TBD

**Summary:**

Austin Kimmel and Mary Beth Thompson introduced the item.

Lisa Hunt presented a high-level overview of the proposed Non-Departmental budget and explained that the budget included transfers to other funds, contracts, grants, debt service, and other items that did not belong to a specific department. Lisa Hunt reviewed the proposed budget totals, Capital Improvement Program transfers, other fund transfers, contractual obligations, contributions and civic support, and police-related funding transfers. Jennifer Bruno clarified that a large portion of the apparent increase in the Police Department budget reflected transfers of existing costs from Non-Departmental to the Police Department.

Council Members, Jennifer Bruno, and staff discussed:

* Clarification regarding removal of one-time conference funding
* Clarification that the Police Department budget increase reflected transferred positions and costs from Non-Departmental, not entirely new spending
* Fire impact fees used to service debt for two fire stations financed through the Local Building Authority
* Concern about delaying Capital Improvement Program (CIP) needs and shifting costs into future years
* Clarification that some CIP funding would be considered later in the process through proposed property tax funding and Truth in Taxation requirements

**Fiscal Year 2026-27 Budget: Department of Public Utilities Follow-Up and Utility Bill 101**   ~ 3:30 p.m.
  30 min.
The Council will receive a follow-up briefing about the Mayor’s recommended budget for the Department of Public Utilities for Fiscal Year 2026-27. The briefing will include a discussion about public utility accounts, a demonstration of how to read utility bills, and answering any general questions.

**FYI – Project Timeline:** (subject to change per Chair direction or Council discussion)
Briefing - Tuesday, May 5, 2026 and Thursday, May 28, 2026
Set Public Hearing Date -  Tuesday, April 21, 2026 and Tuesday, May 19, 2026
Hold hearing to accept public comment - Tuesday, May 19, 2026 and Tuesday, June 2, 2026 at 7 p.m.
TENTATIVE Council Action - TBD

**Summary:**

Jesse Stewart introduced the item.

A video created by the Public Utilities communications team was presented to explain the City’s utility bill, including water, sewer, stormwater, street lighting, waste and recycling, and utility franchise fee charges. Jesse Stewart said the City’s website would feature the video and a rate estimator tool to help customers understand utility bills.

Council Members and staff discussed:

* Whether there was a way to track public use of the rate estimator tool
* Ways to make utility bill information and the estimator easier for customers to understand
* Adding more visual cues to identify bill components such as meter size and average winter consumption
* Connecting customer education to conservation and landscaping water use guidance

**Council Requests:**

Council Member Puy requested staff look into creating a video demonstrating how to use the rate estimator tool. Jesse Stewart said staff could look into that request.

Council Member Petro requested additional education explaining what the bill measurements meant in practical terms and how residents could meaningfully control costs. Jesse Stewart said staff could gather that information and provide more examples.

Council Member Wharton requested staff explore the possibility of a flat sewer rate for apartment units. Jesse Stewart said staff would consider that request.

**Fiscal Year 2026-27 Budget: Fleet Fund**   ~ 4:20 p.m.
  30 min.
The Council will receive a briefing about the proposed Fleet Fund budget for Fiscal Year 2026-27, which provides vehicles, fuel, and vehicle maintenance and repair services for all City departments. Fleet also maintains over 2,700 pieces of equipment.

**FYI – Project Timeline:** (subject to change per Chair direction or Council discussion)
Briefing - Thursday, May 28, 2026
Set Public Hearing Date -  Tuesday, April 21, 2026 and Tuesday, May 19, 2026
Hold hearing to accept public comment - Tuesday, May 19, 2026 and Tuesday, June 2, 2026 at 7 p.m.
TENTATIVE Council Action - TBD

**Summary:**

Sylvia Richards introduced the item.

Jorge Chamorro presented an overview of the Fleet Division’s staffing, services, recent accomplishments, and efficiencies. Julie Crookston then reviewed the proposed Fleet Fund budget, including the maintenance and replacement funds, fuel budget, replacement backlog, and the effect of older vehicles on operations and repair costs. Staff explained that the budget included a reduction in General Fund-supported maintenance funding, meaning some non-cost-effective repairs on older replacement-eligible vehicles would not be completed.

Council Members and staff discussed:

* Replacement-eligible vehicle numbers compared with accident-related losses
* Fuel price forecasting tools and the uncertainty surrounding current projections
* Tools used to project fuel prices and current uncertainty in those projections
* Concern that continued underfunding of replacements would increase deferred maintenance and affect City services

**Tentative Closed Meeting**   -

The Council will consider a motion to enter into closed meeting. A closed meeting described under Utah Code Annotated (UCA) Section §52-4-205 may be held for specific purposes including, but not limited to discussing:

a.     discussion of the character, professional competence, or physical or mental health of an individual.

b.     strategy sessions to discuss collective bargaining.
c.    strategy sessions to discuss pending or reasonably imminent litigation.
d.    strategy sessions to discuss the purchase, exchange, or lease of real property, including any form of a water right or water shares, if public discussion of the transaction would:

(i)    disclose the appraisal or estimated value of the property under consideration, or
(ii)     prevent the public body from completing the transaction on the best possible terms.

e.    strategy sessions to discuss the sale of real property, including any form of a water right or water shares, if:

(i) public discussion of the transaction would:

(A) disclose the appraisal or estimated value of the property under consideration, or
(B) prevent the public body from completing the transaction on the best possible terms.

(ii)     the public body previously gave public notice that the property would be offered for sale, and
(iii)     the terms of the sale are publicly disclosed before the public body approves the sale.

f.    discussion regarding deployment of security personnel, devices, or systems.
g.     investigative proceedings regarding allegations of criminal misconduct.

A closed meeting may also be held for attorney-client matters that are privileged pursuant to Utah Code § 78B-1-137, and for other lawful purposes that satisfy the pertinent requirements of the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act.
