33          Culinary Water Rate Study (Secondary Water Rate Study-December 15) - HDR
34          Engineers
35
36   Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director stated that Council had requested the Culinary
37   Water Rate Study as one of the strategic priorities for the year. Mr. Hicks, along with Shawn
38   Koorn from HDR Engineering, presented a summary of the Culinary Water Rate Study, which
39   was inclusive of all the current enterprise funds. He said the study was divided into two main
40   parts, culinary and secondary water rates.
41
42   Mr. Koorn presented his preliminary study findings. He stated that the purpose of the study was
43   to provide long term sustainable revenues to meet the operating and capital needs of the
44   culinary water system, including ongoing operational and maintenance expenses, as well as the
45   capital needs to maintain, improve, and expand the system as needed.
46
47   Councilmembers reviewed the rate study preliminary results as presented and discussions were
48   held. Discussion was also held regarding the peak assessment received from Jordan Valley
49   Water Conservancy District; however, the study did not cover the peak assessment but future
50   discussions would include this component.

 5            Discuss Possible Moratorium
 6
 7   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, said that there would be a discussion on Fire Impact Fees later in
 8   the meeting. He explained that State law requires that any time an impact fee is adopted, it
 9   cannot be implemented for 90 days.
10
11   Mr. Carter explained that, due to the newly created Riverton Fire Service Area (RFSA), the prior
12   agreement with the Unified Fire Service Area (UFSA) would end on December 31, 2020. He
13   said that means that there would be a 60 day window where the City would not be allowed to
14   collect any fire impact fees, which could result in a loss of fees of approximately $10,000 to the
15   City. He said that one possible solution would be to create a moratorium on building permits for
16   the next 60 days. He said this may send a wrong signal to developers, in light of trying to get the
17   economy to work as best as it can during this season of pandemic.
18
19   Councilmembers discussed and indicated that they felt that the nominal loss was not as
20   important as keeping the application process open. Staff will monitor the number of incoming
21   permits in the next 90 days and keep Council appraised.
22
23            Elderly Housing Overlay
24
25   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, stated that Staff was working on the Elderly
26   Housing overlay with particular emphasis on definitions, including ages, assisted living
27   situations, housing types, assurances, deed restrictions and HOA limitations, etc. He said that
28   other areas of focus were zoning, density, and setbacks. Mr. Lethbridge said that Staff
29   recommended the continued use of the underlying zoning. He said the Elderly Housing overlay
30   would not carry a density of its own or setbacks; for example, it would be applied to the RM-8
31   Zone, or the RM-4 Zone.
32
33   Mr. Lethbridge asked the City Council if they were interested in incentivizing the Elderly
34   Housing, which could be done by direct subsidy, or other types of financial assistance to the
35   developer. He said it could be offered or withdrawn at any time depending on funding. Another
36   optional incentive would be zoning bonuses.
37
38   The City Council discussed the overlay and determined that there was general support for
39   including density bonus options in the draft ordinance. Mr. Lethbridge indicated staff would do
40   so, and that the next step would be review and recommendation by the Planning Commission.

23            Ordinance No. 20-30 - Enacting a Riverton City Fire Impact Fee, Fire Facility Plan,
24            and Related Analysis
25
26   Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, explained that the Impact Fee was a Riverton
27   City fee and not a Riverton Fire Service Area Fee. He then introduced Aaron Montgomery,
28   Public Financial Advisor, from Zions Bank.
29
30   The following is a summary of Mr. Montgomery’s presentation:
31
32   What is an Impact Fee?
33     • One‐time fee charged to new development to offset the capital costs associated with new
34       development
35         • Can only be collected for the costs of system improvements
36         • Fire stations and training facilities
37         • Fire vehicles costing > $500,000
38         • Must have an Impact Fee Facilities Plan (IFFP) and an Impact Fee Analysis (IFA)
39
40   Basic document Requirements
41   Impact Fee Facilities Plan (IFFP)
42          • Existing service levels
43          • Proposed service levels • Excess capacity • Demand created by new development •
44   Consumption of excess capacity • New facilities needed and cost
45
46   Impact Fee Analysis (IFA)
47          • Proportionate share analysis
48          • “Buy‐In” excess capacity component • Construction cost of new facilities
49          • Other costs – engineering, financial, fund balances
50          • Financing and credits
51

 1   Facilities and Buy-In to Existing Infrastructure
 2   Riverton has the following three existing fire stations: Station 120, 121 and 124
 3            • These fire stations have excess capacity of 3,303 square feet
 4            • There are no plans to build new facilities or plans to make any future purchases of fire
 5        vehicles over $500,000; therefore, impact fees will be charged to new development as a
 6        buy‐in to existing infrastructure as development consumes the excess capacity
 7

10   apparatus fees.
