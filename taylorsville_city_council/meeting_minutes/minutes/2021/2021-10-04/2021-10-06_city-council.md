        1.2      Mayor’s Report

Mayor Kristie Overson reported that there had been a ribbon-cutting recently for Summit Vista’s
new Memory Care facility which had been named Meadow Peak. She expressed appreciation to
those who attended the event and stated it was an amazing new facility.

She recently returned from a ChamberWest-sponsored trip to Washington D.C. during which she
had the opportunity to meet with federal delegation members. She and others met with staffers and
key departments impacting Taylorsville businesses. These individuals included Sen. Mike Lee,
Sen. Mitt Romney, Congressmen John Curtis, Burgess Owens, Blake Moore and Chris Stewart,
Dr. Virginia Foxx who was the ranking member of the Education and Labor Committee,
Congressman Byron Donalds of Florida, House Republican Whip Steve Scalise, and Congressman
Hudson, the Republican Conference Secretary.

She initially wasn’t sure if the trip would make a difference, but she ended up feeling that there
had been a huge impact both on the attendees as well as on the federal officials who met with them.
ChamberWest had organized and sponsored the trip and Mayor Overson said she could not
emphasize enough how important the city’s relationship with ChamberWest was.

The mayor updated the city council regarding the Shop Local program. After the first week, 1,224
coupons had been redeemed for an $18,360 value to Taylorsville businesses.

ChamberWest had recently held a press conference regarding the ongoing problem of theft of
catalytic converters. A bill file would be opened and sponsored by both the House and the Senate,
and she looked forward to seeing the result of the cooperative effort.

Taylorsville had recently hosted a monthly COM (Council of Mayors) meeting wherein Mark
McGrath provided a brief presentation regarding the development of Centennial Plaza. It had been
a wonderful opportunity to meet with many mayors around the Salt Lake Valley and discuss shared
interests that various communities faced throughout the valley.

Mayor Overson provided some September statistics regarding staff work throughout the city.
During the previous month there had been 173 business license applications submitted with 188
being issued. Concerning Code Enforcement, 146 new cases had been opened with 144 cases

3.      REPORTS

        3.1      Capital Improvements Report – Ben White

     City Engineer Ben White updated the council regarding various capital projects that were
funded in the current budget.

Centennial Plaza This project was nearly complete although there were some details yet to be
finalized. Art pieces would soon be added and would be rotated with other pieces on an ongoing
basis.

Councilmember Harker inquired about the art. Mayor Overson responded that there would only
be a few pieces placed initially and the collection would increase over time. City logos would be
placed at the entrance of the plaza and additional lighting was still on the way. A formal ribbon
cutting would take place on October 15, 2021.

     City Hall Remodel Mr. White explained that this project appeared to be complete but that
there were some key parts that remained unfinished. He hoped they would be done soon.

     Labrum Park Project Half of this project was made possible by grant funding and there had
been a great deal of effort made by volunteers. He expected the project to be completed in the
Spring which was when planting could begin. Councilmember Harker admitted it was a lot more
work than had been originally anticipated. Mr. White mentioned the park trail that resident Doug
Shupe had brought up during a recent council meeting had been funded and would be complete by
the end of the fiscal year.

CDBG Sidewalk Project There were some CDBG grant funds that needed to be spent and the
project chosen was the curb and gutter on 5000 South which had never been installed. Some ADA
ramps were also put in place and sidewalk was replaced where trip hazards had been identified.

       2700 West Wall Regarding the placement of a wall along the west side of 2700 West, the
money that had been allocated for the project would allow approximately 2/3 of the wall to be
completed on that side of the road, beginning slightly south of 5400 South. Mr. White indicated
that there were some utility challenges and grade issues to deal with. He and others would meet
with residents later in the year to discuss specific issues that would affect specific homes/yards.
He expected to put the project out to bid in January or February. If he did so sooner, the city was
likely to end up paying more for the project.

I-215 Trail Site Plan This was directly under I-215 and the ground was being graded and Mr.
White expected that it would be paved within 2-3 weeks.

Redwood Road Beautification This was a complex project that involved all utilities. The power
lines currently above ground would hopefully be buried by spring. The power lines would still be
visible after that because an entire electrical system would need to be constructed and energized
underground before the equipment above ground could be decommissioned. UDOT had approved
the design and he expected UDOT would have a bid and contractor in place by Thanksgiving or
December. The project involved Rocky Mountain Power, Comcast, and all other utilities would
do more of the work than would UDOT. Councilmember Harker stressed the importance of
keeping the residents informed of impending road closures and changes to traffic patterns.

4700 South & 2600 West Improvements A frontage road was several years in the future but
improvements on 4700 South were expected soon. However, it was important to remember that
the city did not control the property on the north side.

1780 West Concept This was a direct result of the BRT project (Bus Rapid Transit). The city had
committed to having a road from 4700 South to the Salt Lake Community College.

Taylorsville-Bennion Heritage Center/Museum While this wasn’t necessarily a capital project,
it was a city project to hire a consultant and hopefully get this building on the National Historic
Registry. He was attempting to hire a contractor or handy man to handle some current maintenance
issues.

4800 South Bridge The bridge was quite old and had been hit by several vehicles over the years.
It was intended that the parapets on both sides of the bridge would be replaced within the next year
and the new design would replicate the old one quite closely.

Fire Station The old fire station on Redwood Road was undergoing some work for future use as
a city facility.

Carriage Square This was another CDBG project which involved replacing the parking lot and
doing some landscaping. Work was expected to begin in early Spring.

Chair Cochran asked if the property owners were participating in the upgrades as well. Mr. White
responded that each had committed to upgrading their own property, but this was the portion that
the city had agreed to take care of. City Administrator wished to confirm that the parking lot was
going to be reconstructed. Mr. White responded in the affirmative and explained that drainage
would be improved to the extent possible.

Pickleball Courts G. Brown Design has been hired to do a concept and design of “Tank Park” at
3200 West 6200 South to see if it was an appropriate site for pickleball courts. G. Brown was also

Skateboard Park Councilmember Harker asked if any repairs had been made at the skateboard
park. Mr. White responded that some grinding and minimal repairs had taken place but nothing
that he would consider substantial.

Returning to the topic of pickleball courts, Mr. White said that from an engineering standpoint, he
did not recommend attempting to improve the existing court but instead, building new ones.
Councilmember Harker said she still wished to know what it would cost to re-orient the current

its buck by building new courts elsewhere. Mr. White agreed. He also pointed out that a majority
of the council would need to vote in favor of retaining the existing courts at Millrace at which
point there would be a discussion about necessary improvements.

Chair Cochran reminded everyone that the council had allocated a certain amount of funding for
pickleball. Where the courts would be placed was up to city administration. Councilmember
Harker reiterated her belief that there was no reason not to also make the existing court more usable
by reorienting it and adding a fence. Mr. White responded that he would bring a proposal back to
the council.

Councilmember Barbieri mentioned that the lights were out under the bridge on 1300 West near
the duck pond. Councilmember Burgess said there was a similar problem at the monument sign
as you enter the city on 4700 South. Additionally, he indicated that the paint used to cover some
graffiti did not match the original paint. He felt the monument needed some attention to look
better.

        8.3     Council Chair Cochran – Asked that the councilmembers consider whether they
        wished to have 2nd meetings in November and December. There was also a fifth
        Wednesday in December when a Let’s Talk Taylorsville meeting would traditionally take
        place if it were not for the holidays.

        8.4   Councilmember Harker – Wondered if any action had taken place regarding
        making the hours of operation in Taylorsville parks consistent throughout the city.

        Mayor Overson responded that Salt Lake County’s practice was to have their parks open
        from 6am to 10pm. She felt it made sense for Taylorsville to be consistent with the County.
        Councilmembers Harker, Armstrong and Burgess all spoke in favor of maintaining that
        consistency. Chief Cottam pointed out that specific times were easier for officers to
        enforce than were subjective terms such as “dawn” and “dusk.” The subject of changing
        signage throughout the parks was brought forth. The mayor pointed out that in the winter,
        sprinkler systems and restrooms were winterized, and the parks were considered closed.
        That might be something to consider when planning signage.

        Mr. Taylor pointed out that park hours were currently spelled out in city code so a change
        to the hours would require an ordinance. He stated that the administration would bring
        back an ordinance addressing new park hours so that the council could formalize their
        decision.
