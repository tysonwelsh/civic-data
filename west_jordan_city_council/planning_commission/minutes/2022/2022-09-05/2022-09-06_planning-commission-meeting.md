2.
                 Ricochet Canyon Amusement Park; 1132 West 7800 South; Temporary Use Permit ( 150
                 Days); P- C Zone; The Train Shoppe/ Jeff Nichols ( applicant) [# 28213; parcel 21- 26- 352-
                 007]



Jeff Nichols, applicant, said he also owns The Train Shoppe in Gardner Village and is requesting a
temporary use permit for an amusement park.

Mark Forsythe said the temporary amusement park at Gardner Village is located within a fenced area
and includes a roller coaster, two train tracks, a bounce house, and other rides which will operate during
WitchFest. Cost constraints have prevented it from becoming a permanent, year- round business. Mr.
Forsythe reviewed the site layout. There is a small area for storage and maintenance. Temporary use
permits usually require that all equipment be removed after the use is finished. However, as a
compromise,             the conditions will allow for some of the larger rides that are difficult to remove to remain
inoperative on- site as long as the area is well maintained. All of the smaller items will be cleaned up.
As most customers will also attend other WitchFest events, staff does not anticipate the need for
additional parking. Emergency vehicle access will need to remain accessible, and the Fire Marshal will
review the emergency action plan as part of the business license inspection. The building department
will need to inspect the electrical elements on the site.


Staff recommended approval of the Temporary Use Permit for Ricochet Canyon Amusement Park
based on the information                              and   findings in the report and with the conditions     and      requirements   as listed.

Ammon Allen thought that this business would be a fun addition to the area and will work well, but he
had concerns with some conditions of approval. He pointed out condition# 3 that says they shall not
operate for longer than 47 consecutive days, but requirement# 1 lists no more than 150 days, which is
contradictory. Condition # 5 allows the large equipment to remain, which is creating a semi- permanent
feature by way of a temporary use permit. He wondered if the Planning Commission is allowed to make
that exception.   He referred to condition # 9 and wondered   how we will be able to determine     which

Mark Forsythe explained that a temporary use permit approved by the Planning Commission is good for
up to 150 days, but the building code limits a temporary period for six consecutive weeks before it
becomes permanent. Requirement# 1 could be amended to reflect the wording in condition# 3.

Ammon Allen said the staff report pointed out that materials storage is not permitted in this zone, so he
asked for an explanation of how a temporary use permit would allow it.

Mark Forsythe said outdoor storage and operations are usually found in the industrial areas with things
such as fleet vehicles, building materials, soil materials, etc. Storage of that type is prohibited in the P- C
 TSOD) zone. That restriction becomes a little grayer with a temporary use permit.

Ammon Allen appreciated the thought and logic behind the comments, but his concern is that it could
cause a proliferation of the same if others then ask to do it elsewhere. If this happened, then it may
require a code amendment.




Duncan Murray said that a temporary use permit has to be applied for each time. If there is a larger
concern, then they could consider a text amendment to the code in order to control proliferation.

Ammon Allen said it seems to be a temporary use permit for up to 47 days for year- round storage.

Larry Gardner said the temporary use permit is for an amusement park use. The applicant has owned a
business within Gardner Village for two years. He felt it is reasonable to allow for the fenced storage
area. The portable toilets and bounce house will be removed. He didn' t think that it was much different
than the adjacent petting zoo that has storage, which is why the code may need to be reexamined. He
felt that the proposed conditions meet the spirit of the law and are applicable and suitable to protect the
city.


Mark Forsythe explained that the major items that are difficult to move and store such as the roller
coaster, carousel, and train will remain on site as long as they are well maintained, and the smaller
items and equipment would be cleared off.


Jeff Nichols explained that the roller coaster was racked on a trailer when it was delivered, and they
could put it back on the trailer to store. The train ride is ground mounted and is not meant to be
temporary. They would have to cut the track apart to remove it, but they will do whatever the
Commission decides. He said that originally the goal was for a permanent facility, which meant some
extreme costs for things such as restrooms. He approached the owners of Gardner Village to see if they
would participate with any of the improvements, but they weren' t sure if they wanted it as a permanent
use. There was a meeting in June to discuss their options and that is how the proposed plan came
forward as a compromise. He does not intend the temporary use to be long- term, but they want to see
what happens over the next couple of years. If it becomes a long- term use, then he would spend the
money to make it permanent. The application before them is a way to use the equipment that they have
already invested money in. Even if the equipment is dismantled, it will still need to be stored here,
because it is very large.
