2. Financial        Matters
            2.1 Public        Hearing         on Consideration              of a 6% Municipal                 Energy          Sales & Use Tax
         Mayor           Silvestrini     said the council            did not want to raise taxes as taxpayers.                         The council
         had a responsibility              to provide          services     to the community.                He explained            the need for the
         fee increase             and reasons      why      other funding            sources    could     not be utilized.            There     were
         continual          requests      for sidewalks,         traffic     calming,      and street improvements.                     This past year,
         the city was faced with                  a 9% inflation           and a salary        war among           police      agencies.       Unified
         Police       Department          (UPD)       was faced with               the need for a salary           increase.         A neighboring
         city was short 50 police                  officers,     so other area departments                   raised their police           salaries       to
         be able to maintain               existing      officers.      The council        had determined              to provide         the same
         level of police            service      the city current          maintained.         An option        to cut back officers             would
         increase         response       times    and reduce          services.       The city was lean, most employees
         performed           more than one job.             He acknowledged,               for example,            that there was state
        mandated            response       time on building             peri'nit    applications,        so staff was necessary                to limit
        mistakes.           Millcreek      Common           and City        Hall     were being      built     by bonds         and tax increment
         in the area would               pay back the bonds.               The bonds       must be used for what they were
        proposed            for. The interest         rates on the bonds              were about 2%. The city hall lease would
         expire       next year, which            was only       at 1/3 of the market             rate. The city would                 pay three times
         as much          on rent at a new location.                 The police        department        also leases their building.                  The
        street repair          projects       are largely       funded      by grants      and that money              cannot        be used for other
        projects.          The city has restricted             funds,      and the only        flexibility        was with       sales tax dollars.
