Planning Commission Work Session Meeting Agenda
                           19 January 2022
                             21 E. 100 N.
                               6:00 p.m.

Chairman Ann Peterson opened the meeting at 6:00 p.m.

In attendance:
Commissioners: Cory Thomson, Alan Hancock, Karl Brough, Heather Robertson, Ann
Peterson, Fran Petersen
Staff: Seth Atkinson, Shauna Talbot
Public: Donald W. Bell

Opening prayer offered by Ann Peterson.

    1. Concept Zoning Discussion of Annexation Area:
The Commission discussed proposed concept zoning areas in relation to the current annexation
map to suggest types of uses they would like to see developed as annexations are proposed for
entering the city. Ann Peterson had listed several ideas she had labelled on her map including:
       1. A recreation area up by the “Bailey’s property” – near the mouth of the canyon close
           to the golf course. Suggestion of a trailhead could be for multi-purpose non-
           motorized access such as hiking or equestrian that could go all the way up to Reese’s
           flat it could follow the old canal that goes along the canyon this would lend itself as a
           prime recreation area if the golf course were to be extended someday.
       2. Zoning for High-Density Residential south of the current Industrial area the far south
           of the annexation area by the highway.
       3. Extending the current zoning for Industrial in Level 2 and Level 1 of the annexation
           area.
       4. A proposed widening of airport road, with another possible north south road mid-way
           between Main Street and airport road possible in future was discussed
       5. The current level 2 annex area by the train tracks was proposed to develop as a
           combined use, to the south west of town closest to the tracks.
       6. Further west of town in the Level 3 area a possible mixed density housing area that
           may allow for some properties with animal rights especially close by the fairgrounds
           area.
       7. Commercial or combined use along the frontage of Hwy 132 to the west of town by
           the fairgrounds.
       8. Commercial and light industry to the north end of town out the road to Mona
       9. Mixed housing north of town in Level 3 with larger lots possibly allowing animal
           rights there.

The Commission talked about the possible expansion of the golf course and ideas for that
including a possible land swap proposal with DWR. Recreation ideas in the area discussed
included a central barn/pavilion area where food trucks could provide food along the perimeter,

and the covered barn area is where families could go to congregate with their food items from
the various vendors. Fran Petersen said she saw a similar idea on the way from Alpine to
Pleasant Grove, and they saw something in North Carolina this fall that had a pavilion with
tables and seating situated around that was concrete with a stage where the city would have
performances on weekends. There was also a balcony and more seating up on top. This would
allow for eating in the way of food trucks without the full cost of a restaurant, but be able to have
a nice place to congregate as a community.

The idea of allowing areas in town with combined uses that had shops in the bottom and housing
on the top was discussed for more of a walkable community area in our combined use zone.

Locations for different box-chain establishments that would be appropriate areas near the
freeway interchanges was discussed. With shopping areas similar to what you see out of Spanish
Fork Canyon on Highway 6. Close to the current gym was one area that was discussed for large
retail businesses, and a strip mall area.

Shauna Talbot shared that as an informational point the public works director says all
development crossing the railroad tracks will be difficult both because gaining crossings from
the railroad is hard, and the ground becomes very flat to the west of town, so sewer fall will be
more difficult to get the proper fall, so lift stations would become necessary.

Seth Atkinson explained sewer ponds may need to be increased, and could still be a pond rather
than a treatment facility as long as the city stays under a certain size.

The Commission talked about a report of projected growth in Utah and Ann said she would
forward it to the members.

There was a discussion about what does “rural” mean especially since the General Plan refers to
keeping a “rural” feel. Also, there is a limit to what municipalities could govern such as wanting
the west fields to stay, but people have a right to use or develop their property and how to
balance that.

The Commission discussed locations that may allow for more school expansions in the future.

Heather commented that it would be good to know the reasons why people move to Nephi.
Maybe they are not moving here for the rural feel. Maybe they are moving here because that is
the most economical choice. This idea was discussed as to why people are choosing Nephi.
Some probably are for the rural atmosphere and others are moving for other reasons, also to
balance and consider the feelings of current residents, not simply those coming in.

A desire for more large lots were discussed citing areas such as Salem in comparison. Some lots
should be available for animal rights with at least half-acre lots. Alan commented that a half-
acre lot is really not large enough for large size animals such as a cow or horse having been
raised around that. It would be fine for small, or even a couple medium-sized animals such as
chicken, ducks, lambs. Large animals should be possibly closer to 1.5 acre lots.

The Commission talked about how the County would like the city to conceptually zone our
annexation areas, so they know what our future desires are and can have that to work from as
development proposals come to them.

Karl Brough had brought his conceptual zoning plan to show the Commission and it included:
   1. Extending Industrial on the south end of town flanking the highway, and around that
       area.
   2. High-density Residential west of the train tracks, but north of the industrial in the Level 3
       annexation area north to about 1300 south.
   3. Low-density Residential west of the train tracks, and north of the high-density residential
       until Highway 132.
   4. A strip or “pod” area of Highway Commercial along the Hwy 132 corridor extending out
       towards the west and airport road.
   5. A buffer area of High-density Residential around the Highway Commercial.
   6. A buffer area of Medium-density Residential outside of the high-density.
   7. Fading back into Low-density residential outside of the Medium-density Residential
   8. Another mirrored Low-density residential west of the train tracks, but north of Hwy 132.
   9. North of that area some Medium-density residential abutting up to our Industrial area to
       the northwest quadrant of town.
   10. Another strip or “pod” area of Highway commercial along Hwy 28 (Main) at the north
       interchange with concentric circles of density highway commercial →   high-density
       residential →
                    medium-density residential →               low-density residential.
   11. Some Industrial shown north of town headed toward Mona.
   12. Some Light Industrial shown out surrounding the airport.
   13. Highway Commercial shown at the middle I-15 interchange headed east into the canyon
       on Hwy 132.

The Commission discussed maximizing our I-15 exits with commercial and high-density
commuter housing.

They talked about how they like about half a block on Main Street being in a Combined Use
Zone. They talked about encouraging high-density housing along that zone as well as mixed
commercial. Fran said she hasn’t even had to advertise to fill her high-density housing, so there
is a need in the community.

    2. General Plan Update Draft Review
The Commission talked about where we are in the General Plan Review process, they talked
about trying to review it more frequently. Seth Atkinson shared with the Commission that we
hired consultants to review it for now, but we can constantly update it. The Commission
expressed a desire that when we do concept zone the General Plan that we hold future developers
to the General Plan. Seth explained that municipalities can decide whether it is mandatory or
used as a guideline with what we lay out in our General Plan, and Nephi has elected that it be a
mandatory compliance. Conceptually zoning the General Plan should help development
streamline also, because when someone comes in for a rezone the city already knows what it’s
plan for the area is, and rezones and annexations can go quicker and smoother.

Fran Petersen brought up that we either need to increase lot sizes or reduce setbacks because she
is hearing people are having a hard time fitting homes on lots in town. Shauna confirmed that
there was a couple who just this week had to cut off six feet from their building plans in order for
their home to meet the setbacks. The comment was made that our current setbacks is one of the
things consistently felt to make Nephi still feel rural even with smaller lots, it gives space
between neighbors that is beneficial.

The Commission talked about the factors that have been driving costs up for land that included a
greater demand, a lack of supply, and people not willing to sell, because to replace what they
have would cost so much. Economics is what is driving this push to develop lots, and to develop
more density.

The Commission talked about how they like the idea spoken about to create a zone that has
mixed density, where there are a percentage mix of multiple sizes of lots in the same
neighborhood. That allows for a mixed demographic of families, ages, socio-economic
conditions, and is in keeping with the feel of old Nephi that brings the community together in
supporting one another. Also, they talked about how if a developer comes in with mixed density
in phases that each phase needs to be mixed, not segregated one area of small lots, and separated
from one area with large lots.

Community member, Donald Bell, asked since the Commission was discussing housing options
if some type of very low-income housing could be looked at for people who are on social
security or disability income. Fran asked him what type of housing he would envision to address
that need. Would it be studio apartments over businesses? Would he see it more as apartments,
or townhomes? He said he could see it as a studio over a business. That way they could be
within walking distance of needed facilities too like grocery stores. The idea of a tiny home
community was discussed, it was brought up when this idea was presented to the council before
the idea was not embraced. Donald, when asked, said this would be something that could solve
the problem of housing for fixed incomes too. The Commission talked about how low-income
housing of any nature would likely need to be government subsidized, especially in today’s
economy because no developer will voluntarily create this due to the lack of return on
investment. Doing some apartment or high-density housing that was close to an extension of the
State’s Trax system was discussed. Donald suggested that this could maybe be located by the
tracks at the eastern end of the Juab County fairgrounds since there is already Union Pacific
infrastructure right now. There could be commuter parking, and zoning to allow for other useful
vendors nearby. Karl pointed out that he is showing something similar on his concept zoning
map with high-density and commercial, and a master street located somewhere in the area.

Proposed future roads to make sure were shown on the street master plan were discussed
including one that is half-way between Main Street and Airport Road where there is a natural
long, straight stretch of property lines that nearly perfectly line up running north and south. This
would be a great connector bypass to allow for getting off the freeway and accessing the west
side of Nephi, and could even reconnect back up with I-15. That way if someone develops their
land in the future those streets are shown as required that are on the master street plan element of
the General Plan. You could require major intersections at least every two miles. The two right-
of-way sizes for developing streets in Nephi are either ninety-nine (99) feet or sixty (60) feet.

The benefits of our wide road rights-of-way in Nephi were discussed and how it helps even far in
the future to deal with traffic congestion.

Creating a buffer around the airport with light industrial or commercial and keeping residential
somewhat away from that area, and keeping the heavier industrial to the south of town, or on the
road to Mona was discussed.

Heather suggested some low-density housing out there by the Bypass where they already have
half-acre lots and residential housing out there. This would be a good area for animal rights.
Seth explained that having larger lots can lead to inefficiencies in utilities because you are
stringing them out quite far for fewer homes. The comment was made that it would be worth it if
utilities were already out there to service industry, and then the housing develops because the
industry already needed the services, then the lower-density residential would just be piggy-
backing on the already installed infrastructure. It was confirmed that a reimbursement district
can be set up so those who initially install the utilities can be reimbursed later as others hook on
to the system, however, the reimbursement would be at the cost of the utilities installed at the
time, so with inflation that erodes the value of the reimbursement.

Donald asked if there is a possibility of another electric substation out by the airport planned for
the future to cover the use of the airport, light industry, and some housing? Seth explained that
these types of features would be shown on the master utility plan portion of the General Plan,
and that what the Commission is doing now by master planning uses is critical to help the city
determine what needs should be addressed on the master plan.

   3. Combined Use Zone Revision Discussion
The Commission discussed that revisions will be coming forward in the upcoming Planning
Commission meetings to make edits to the CU Zoning code.

   4. PUD/PRD Discussion

The Commission started discussing the potential elimination of the PRD overlay zone during the
regular meeting, as it is somewhat redundant to the PUD. Karl Brough stated that he actually
prefers a lot of the code in the PRD overlay zone to that of the PUD. The suggestion was made to
do away with PUDs. Another suggestion was to eliminate PRDs and edit the PRD ordinance so
it is more useful and palatable. The fact that a developer can meet the 20% open space
requirement by only having setbacks of the buildings was brought up. A new definition of open
space excluding setbacks from the open space definition was suggested by Shauna. The
definition should not just include “space” but true “open space”. What would be allowed in the
open space was discussed and possibly adding that to the definition. Should xeriscape be
allowed to count as open space was brought up where water conservation is what we are told to
promote in our arid region. It was brought up that the state legislature is talking right now on a
proposed bill to not allow cities or HOAs to require any lawn of developers or homeowners.
Seth said his philosophy is that you limit the use of water by charging people for it, and they will
regulate themselves if it is cost prohibitive. Karl said his problem is not enough beautification
with trees especially in the xeriscape. The narrow frontages that have been happening in PUDs
was discussed, and the tiny space available to homeowners in the backyards of homes in current

PUDS was also listed as a concern. Thoughts about allowing parking in the rear yards were
shared. Requiring planting/landscaping plans more detailed when PUDs were submitted was
discussed. More guest parking in the PUD is also needed. It currently only seems to show two
parking spaces per unit. Right now, the standard is talked about as 8 units per acre for PUDs, but
that only equates to 5,445 square feet per unit, and if any open space is provided the lots are just
tiny for ownership in a PUD. The lots in the Gardner Village A desire for greater greenspace in
these developments was discussed.

   5. Miscellaneous Topics
The Commission discussed various ideas for upcoming discussions and possible additional work
meetings.

   6. Adjourn
The Commission adjourned.
