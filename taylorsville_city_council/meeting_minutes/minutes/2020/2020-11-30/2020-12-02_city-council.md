               Ceremonies for January 6, 2021 to be arranged by Councilmember Barbieri)

Chair Harker directed the Pledge of Allegiance and spoke briefly about lessons learned in 2020,
particularly the importance of resiliency, flexibility and adaptability.

The Chair then turned the time over to Mason Harker who played The First Noel and Angels We
Have Heard on High on the city’s grand piano. It was likely to be the last time the instrument
would be played in city hall as it would soon be moved to the new performing arts building.

       1.2    Mayor’s Report

Mayor Kristie Overson expressed her appreciation to Council Coordinator Kris Heineman who
was responsible for the beautiful Christmas décor in the Council Chambers.

The Mayor had recently attended ribbon cuttings for Nothing Bundt Cakes, Lolo Hawaiian BBQ
and Bubbles, Drinks and More. She expressed appreciation to the City Council for funding the
Shop Local program and listed several businesses that had been popular recipients of the Shop
Local bucks.

Chamber West had recently held their fall conference and she, along with the mayors of West
Jordan and West Valley had updated the group on the happenings on the west side of the Salt Lake
Valley. She was grateful for the working relationships Taylorsville had with its neighbors.

There was also a recent workshop via Zoom regarding transportation projects, economic
development, housing and other critical issues which provided an opportunity to look, listen and
learn about what was happening in the southwest quadrant of the valley.

She and economic development director Wayne Harper recently had the opportunity to speak to
Utah Transit Authority’s local advisory Board of Trustees regarding the mid-valley Bus Rapid
Transit (BRT) project. She had learned that the capital projects plan for BRT was approved by
that Board, a project which had literally been in the works for ten years or more.

Wayne Harper recently arranged a tour of the performing arts center. The facility was lovely and
finishing work had begun. The Mayor looked forward to the opening which was most likely to
take place in January.

The sales tax numbers were looking strong, and totals exceeded those from the previous year which
was amazing considering the pandemic. The City would continue to be frugal and was preparing
for mid-year budget adjustments in January.

A microburst storm had ripped through a small portion of District 5 approximately three weeks
before. The City had provided eight dumpsters to help with clean up as well as green waste
containers. Many landfill vouchers had been distributed and the event was a perfect example of
neighbors helping neighbors and strangers helping one another.

Mayor Overson stated that 2020 had clearly been the best of times and also the worst of times.
Despite so much adversity, everyone rallied and provided assistance to one another. Taylorsville
was an amazing community and the Mayor expressed her appreciation to all the residents, the other
elected officials and also the staff.

       1.3     Citizen Comments

Chair Meredith Harker reviewed the Citizen Comment Procedures for the audience. She then
called for any citizen comments.

Taylorsville resident Margaret Harper wished to discuss a pie shaped piece of city-owned property
just south of Taylors Ridge Cove. Two years ago her neighbors had asked most of the residents in
the neighborhood if they could take down a fence in order to build a home. The neighbors agreed
but were told that the fence would be replaced. Instead, unattractive asphalt chips had been placed
where the fence had been, and she did not believe they were compliant with City code. The area
with the asphalt chips was increasingly being used as a roadway. She indicated City staff had been
contacted but rather than consulting with all fourteen households in the area, only two were
contacted. She and her neighbors requested a remedy to the problem.

Greg VanderWerff stated that he also lived on Taylors Ridge Cove and wished to speak on the
same topic. He stated that some in the neighborhood felt the problem was about access. He stated
it was a question of egress. He stated that there was still access to the new home as well as the one
behind it. The lane was the same width that it always had been, but it had been moved with heavy
equipment. He stated that private property owners did not have the right to change city property
without permits and that is what had happened.

There were no additional citizen comments, and Chair Harker closed the citizen comment period.

3.     REPORTS

       3.1     Unified Fire Report – Captain Richard Rich

Unified Fire Authority Captain Richard Rich had changed the report format and encouraged
feedback from the Council. He pointed out a correction to the turnout time for Fire responses. It
was roughly 2 minute 25 second range as opposed to what was indicated in the report. Call volume
remained fairly stable but was beginning to trend upward a bit.

UFA Strategic Plan was being finalized with input from all partner cities, to ensure the plan was
in concert with what the partners wished to see. Captain Rich hoped to have a final draft in
December so that it could be presented to the Board in January.

Councilmember Burgess asked about the change in dispatch response. Captain Rich responded
that data was still being gathered, so it was not yet clear if the call volume was reasonably even
between West Valley and Taylorsville regarding the closest responding unit.

Wildland fire season was finally wrapping up and involved all western states with the sole
exception of Montana. UFA was still sending small incident management teams to some events
and there was a possibility that they would be used for vaccine distribution in the future.

The captain’s promotion list was now in place and would be good for 18 months.

A class of 36 recruits was currently in the background investigation phase and was expected to be
finalized in December.

Councilmember Cochran asked if the number of recruits in the testing and hiring process equaled
the number of retirees. Captain Rich responded that wasn’t quite the case but explained that UFA
was currently over-hiring by eleven in an effort to make up some ground.

Regarding COVID-19, the fire stations were locked down tighter and firefighters were being asked
to wear masks inside the firehouse. There had been a slight increase to the number of personnel
testing positive for the virus, but service delivery was not yet being affected. Contingency plans
were in place in case that changed. Currently there were 36 staff members (10% of the total) who
had tested positive.

         6.1   Resolution No. 20-45 - A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville approving the audit
         report for the Fiscal Year Beginning July 1, 2019 and Ending June 30, 2020 – Scott
         Harrington/Rob Wood

Scott Harrington explained that the books couldn’t be closed exactly on June 30th due to a time lag
between that date and when certain revenues were received by the City. Once the books were
closed, Administration met with the Budget/Audit Committee and then with auditor Rob Wood.
Field work began in October and there was an additional meeting with the Budget/Audit
Committee in November. Spending had been proactively cut but in spite of most economic
forecasts, the City had fared well despite COVID.

Councilmember Cochran asked how Taylorsville compared to what other cities were seeing
revenue-wise. Mr. Harrington responded that it was much the same with other municipalities and
with Salt Lake County, despite tourism taking a big hit. He expressed his appreciation to the
efforts of Stephanie Snow, Shelley McLaughlin and everyone in the City who kept a close eye on
the budget and watched costs closely.

Rob Wood stated that the City had strong fund balances, had appropriate budgetary controls in
place and then went into greater detail regarding the Comprehensive Annual Financial Report
(CAFR). He was giving Taylorsville the highest possible opinion regarding its financial status and
performance for FY19-20.

Councilmember Burgess thanked Mr. Wood and the City Administration for their work on the
audit and for presenting it to the City Council in a clear, understandable manner.

MOTION:        Councilmember Cochran moved to approve Resolution No. 20-45, approving
               the audit report for the fiscal year beginning July 1, 2019 and ending June 30,
               2020 as presented. The motion was seconded by Councilmember Burgess.

might be a good idea to put both into place if there was little expense involved. Mr. Wood pointed
out that Taylorsville need only provide a prominent link to the State’s fraud hotline in order to get

          7.2 Resolution No. 20-46 – A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville approving the Use
         of Force policy for the Taylorsville City Police Department – Jay Ziolkowski

Mr. Ziolkowski explained that the Utah Chiefs of Police Association and the Department of Justice
required that the Taylorsville Police Department have a Use of Force policy in place. It was
important to get it in place early so that the department would be eligible to seek grants sooner
rather than later. The policy contained specific language prohibiting choke holds except with
regards to life safety issues. Mr. Ziolkowski did not anticipate any substantial changes to the
policy as it was currently written.

In response to an inquiry from Councilmember Cochran, Mr. Ziolkowski explained this policy
would guide Taylorsville officers in their pursuits, arrest control measures, etc. Councilmember
Cochran then asked Tracy Cowdell if he had the opportunity to review the proposed policy. The
City Attorney responded that he had “all the confidence in the world” in the policy.

Councilmember Burgess appreciated the explanation of the policy, particularly for the benefit of
the listening public. Mr. Ziolkowski agreed that having such a policy was paramount.
