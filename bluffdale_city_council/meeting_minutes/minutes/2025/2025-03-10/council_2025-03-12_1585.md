         4.1      14600 South Railroad Crossing Project Presentation. (Staff Presentation,
                  Michael Fazio\

City Engineer, Michael Fazio, was excited to showcase the project and introduced HDR Project
Manager, Dave McCune; Construction Manager/General Contracting("CMGC")team members
Cole Frost, Brandon Squire, Mike Moran, and Cory Sergeant; Utah Department of Transportation
("UDOT") Region Two Deputy Director, Lisa Zundel; and HDR Utah Transportation Business
Group Manager, Stephanie Serpico.


Mr.Fazio provided the project overview. The existing crossing is a single lane at the Union Pacific
Railroad bridge with a substandard vertical clearance of 12 feet that requires very slow speeds. It
is unsafe for bicyclists and pedestrians. The project will create a four-lane underpass with 10-foot
multipurpose paths on each side. The tunnel will be 150 feet long.

Renderings of the completed project were presented. Mr. McCune reported that utilities will be
relocated when the project begins. As a condition of approval, Union Pacific Railroad requires
that the existing one-lane underpass be closed. In response to a question from Mayor Hall, he
confirmed that the sidewalk would end abruptly due to a change in the scope of the project. The
road will eventually connect to Redwood Road,but the large expense is getting under the railroad.
To make the project work with available funding,it was paired back to focus on the undercrossing.

The project status was reviewed. Feasibility and environmental studies were completed in 2021
and 2022. Based on public input, it was determined that the preferred alternative was an east-west
connection under the railroad and over the canal. The City has provided over $10 million so far
for the project, and the State has provided $50 million. Mayor Hall stated that the legislature was
very generous in providing the funding, including an additional $30 million that they have
committed. Mr. Fazio reported that the project would not be possible without State support.

In March 2023, the City brought in a contracting team to work through the design phase. The
canal culvert was then constructed. The scope reduction process was completed in early 2024.
The 60% design was finalized in May 2024 and the 100% design was submitted in March 2025.
The next step will be design approval by Union Pacific Railroad, which is expected within four to
five weeks. As soon as approval is obtained they will be ready to move forward. Mr. Fazio
reported that they have had several meetings with Union Pacific and the Utah Transit Authority
("UTA")-    In response to a question from Mayor Hall, Mr. McCune confirmed that they had
already received comments fi-om Union Pacific and provided an updated submittal with additional
information and calculations.

Mr. McCune stated that they had produced a high volume of engineering and collaborative work
on the process, including over 1000 pages of calculations and almost 900 pages of geotechnical
reports, which reflects both the complexity of the project and the need to prove its feasibility to
Union Pacific. Union Pacific has engaged in the process at a higher level than typical. They have
indicated that they are excited about the project and hope to be able to use this method in the future.

Over 100 engineers, support staff, and contractors worked on the following:

        1,500 feet of 14600 South corridor reconstruction;
       New four-lane underpass;
       New canal box culvert (installed);
        Closure of existing underpass;
       900 feet ofretaining walls;
        Relocation of power, water, sewer, and telecommunications infrastructure;
       Property acquisition; and
       Public meetings were also held, and the project team coordinated with UTA,Union Pacific,
        and Salt Lake City Public Utilities.


Design considerations and challenges included substantial additional documentation required by
Union Pacific. They typically do not allow underpasses. As this is also a new technology, several
iterations were required with additional memos and justification. The joint Ralph L. Wadsworth
and Stacey Witbeck CMGC team was invaluable in this process. For example, after a site visit in
Toronto, Union Pacific requested a comparison between this construction method and their
preferred methods and structures, including an hour-by-hour, task-by-task downtime comparison
between the two methods. That would not have been possible without the CMGC team.


Topography was another challenge. The east side ofthe roadway is approximately 50 feet higher
than the west side. Union Pacific typically prefers an overpass, which would have been
approximately ICQ feet higher than the nearby homes. That was not feasible from either a cost or
constructability standpoint, which is what led to them considering an underpass.

Staging was also a consideration and the CMGC team helped them understand how it would be
built. Due to the topographical challenges, the boxes have to be built prior to installation. To do
that, a large pit must be built to build the boxes. That requires land, which presented the challenge
of property acquisition. The City had to acquire the Ridge Rock and DKM Leasing properties, as
well as property on the south side of the roadway. Mr. Fazio reported that they successfully
purchased two properties and are working to acquire the third property.


Mayor Hall reported that the CMGC team also built the Porter Rockwell Boulevard bridge, which
was also a highly technical project.

Mr. Fazio indicated that because they are required to close the existing underpass, they will need
to remove the existing bridges and rebuild the embankment at a substantial unplanned cost to the
City. He then reviewed the project schedule.


Utility relocation will begin within the next month, including water, gas, and power lines. They
expect the project to be completed within 22 months. Project Manager, Cole Frost, stated that the
boxes should be constructed and installed within approximately one year. The crossing will be
closed at that time, and work will continue to the west. It will take approximately 10 additional
months to complete the project once the crossing is closed. Due to the grade differential, the walls
will need to be built up between 20 and 25 feet, and the road cannot safely remain open during that
phase. Mayor Hall noted that there will be no east-west access for the duration of that portion of
the project, so residents will have to use 1-15 or Porter Rockwell Boulevard.

Mr. Fazio reported that they have worked to keep costs as low as possible. The majority of the
costs are for structures and earthwork. They had hoped to reuse the excavated soil, but it is
saturated clays that are not suitable for the embankment. They will, however, be able to reuse
approximately 10 feet of soil. It was noted that they may be able to use the soil for the Jordan
Narrows Road tank project. Mr. Fazio reported that the Verona Method will provide a savings of
over $30 million over the traditional method.

Mr. Raid thanked Communications Specialist, Amanda Luker for all the work she does as her skills

Ms.Luker presented the updated Bluffdale City Style Guide and provided a history ofthe Bluffdale
logo. In the late 1980s, Jeanette Andrews created a shirt design for a celebration that was then
used as the City logo. She later said that if she had known it would be used as a logo, she would
have designed differently. In 2016,in conjunction with efforts to improve economic development,
the City updated its website. As part of that process, the logo was also updated. Residents were
surveyed and focus groups were conducted,and the new logo was selected based on their feedback.

A style guide was also created in 2016. Style guides are used by schools, governments,and private
companies to explain how to use brand elements. They make it easier to maintain consistent brand
identity and recognition over time regardless ofthe way the branding is utilized. In 2019,clarifying
language was added to the guide. Sign options and a color change for the Fire Department were
added in 2020. They were now proposing expansion of the guide to include City-sponsored
groups.



Ms. Luker reported that several groups approached her requesting a new logo, including the
Recreation Department, the Healthy Bluffdale Coalition, and the Youth City Council. She then
worked on ways to keep the design cohesive while allowing different groups the opportunity to be
unique. Working with Redcore, the company that created the original logo, they created various
designs for each group. City department logos will retain the Bluffdale blue, and other groups will
be in different colors to emphasize their uniqueness. The colors were chosen based on colors
traditionally associated with those topics: red for arts, green for the community garden, etc. The
secondary color palette has been updated to include those colors.

If approved, the logos would be implemented over time as new items are purchased or created.
For example,the Parks Department has expressed interest in putting its new logo on vests and the
new department logos would be used on future flyers, banners, and promotional materials.

Council Member Aston expressed approval for the new logos. She asked if the red for the Arts
Advisory Board was a different shade from the Fire Department. Ms. Luker confirmed that they
are different shades. The difference is muted in the digital presentation, but they are distinctly
