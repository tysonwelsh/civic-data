           2.5. Herriman Transit Study Update – Bryce Terry, City Engineer
      City Engineer Terry indicated the City received grant money to conduct the transit study
      within Herriman. Parametrix was the consultant selected for the project. Since January, they
      have been working to study how future transit corridors could be best incorporated in
      Herriman. The corridors included an option for transit to travel out to the Olympia area, and
      another corridor to follow the preserved transit right of way through the Herriman Town
      Center. Data has been gathered for the project to begin modelling different modes of transit
      and how well they would perform in Herriman. The different modes of transit included the
      following:
           • Regular Bus
           • Bus Rapid Transit
           • TRAX – Redline Extension
      Each would be modeled under three land use scenarios: High Growth, Mid-Growth, and Low
      Growth. City engineer Terry presented the results of the scenarios and reported they were
      little misleading to compare directly, as the two modes of Bus and Bus Rapid Transit were
      modeled with a connection to the Draper FrontRunner Station, while the TRAX extension
      was not. Based on these results, it was recommended to run the model with TRAX extending
      all the way to the Draper FrontRunner Station. The consultant was working to model and get
      results back with the connection included. Engineer Terry indicated this item would be
      brought back to the Council when additional details had been secured. Councilmember
      Shields requested ridership being highlighted.

          2.6. Secondary Water Infrastructure Planning – Justun Edwards, Public Works Director
      Public Works Director Justun Edwards stated in March 2023, the Olympia development
      petitioned the city to remove the requirement to install secondary water within their
      development. Staff presented information to the council that identified needed
      improvements, estimated costs, and an estimated timeline for when secondary water may be
      available to the Olympia area. Based on this information, the council approved a Master
      Development Agreement amendment that removed the requirement for secondary water to
      be installed within the Olympia development. The council also directed staff to explore
      options for the future of secondary water and the potential impacts of each on the existing
      culinary and secondary water systems and future water supplies.

      Staff worked closely with Bowen Collins & Associates to explore multiple options at a high
      level to identify potential impacts to existing and future infrastructure. Three scenarios were
      identified as most feasible, and a more detailed analysis of these scenarios was performed to
      better understand impacts to infrastructure and water supply costs. The costs illustrated in
September 27, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                           Page 5 of 9


      the scenarios and tables, are representative of capital system improvements identified in the
      water master plan and do not reflect project level distribution infrastructure cost within
      developments. Project level distribution costs can vary based on the type of development.
      Project level distribution costs typically range from 40-60-percent of capital system costs.

      Director Edwards offered alternatives with each respective scenario.
         • Option 1 (Scenario 2): Proceed with master planning and development regulation in
             accordance with Scenario 2, which is to require secondary water in all new
             developments (within zones 1 through 4), excluding Olympia (baseline scenario).
         • Option 2 (Scenario 3): Proceed with master planning and development regulation per
             Scenario 3, which requires secondary water in all new development (within zones 1
             through 4), excluding Olympia and South Hills Development.
         • Option 3 (Scenario 4): Proceed with master planning and development regulation per
             Scenario 4, which does not require secondary water for any new developments but
             focus on providing source and infrastructure to existing secondary water lines and
             services.
      Director Edwards explained moving forward, there were options open to the Council
      depending on direction.

         •    Option 1 (Scenario 2): Finalize the water master plan and complete the impact fee
              facility plan.
          • Option 2 (Scenario 3): Update the water master plan and complete the impact fee
              facility plan. Amend Wasatch MDA and pertinent city codes.
          • Option 3 (Scenario 4): Update the water master plan and complete the impact fee
              facility plan. Amend MDA’s and pertinent city codes.
      Director Edwards reported that once updates to the plans have been made and capital
      infrastructure has been identified, the water rate study will be completed. The study would
      evaluate the City’s current water right assets to maximize usage.

      Councilmember Ohrn explained there would not be the ability to always guarantee Jordan
      Valley Water would always provide the water necessary. Councilmember Ohrn added water
      usage would most likely change in the future. The Council agreed.

      City Manager Cherpeski referenced a reimbursement agreement within the Olympia
      development where infrastructure would not serve the project, and staff needed to
      understand Council direction to move forward with the project.

      Councilmember Shields indicated it was hard to give clear definitive feedback when he did
      not know the overarching goals of the City, and suggested he would lean towards not
      continuing to expand the secondary water system. Councilmember Ohrn requested to
      continue the discussion to the next meeting to give time to digest the complex information
      provided. The Council agreed.
