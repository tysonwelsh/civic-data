 4. PUBLIC COMMENT
Chair Jacob opened the public comment period at 6:01 pm.

Comments:
Alexandra Eframo tried to speak during public comments via phone, but it seemed she was unable
to hear a response from the Council end of the connection.

Amy Motta, resident of Salt Lake City and volunteer with Bailing Out Benji animal advocacy group,
hoped the selling of puppies in stores in West Jordan would be banned. She spoke of puppy mills,
animal cruelty, and harm to consumers.

Chris McConnehey, West Jordan resident, hoped the agenda item regarding Parker Place would be
resolved during the meeting. He commented that parking on a public street could be addressed at
any time, and suggested the Council move the project forward and watch the parking situation to see

how things went. Mr. McConnehey expressed support for streetlights on both sides of crosswalks
and thanked Council for taking time to discuss the matter. He encouraged support for the lease
agreement with the Historical Society.

Jeanette Drake, West Jordan resident and one of the owners of Drake Family Farms had been to
numerous public meetings since the neighboring development project began. She had many
customers who needed fresh goat milk for health reasons and noted many farm and delivery trucks
frequently requiring access to the farm property. She reported that horse trailers and cement trailers
also needed frequent access to a neighboring property on Drake Lane. Ms. Drake believed there was
a good reason the Senior Housing Overlay was approved for the Parker Place property, and
expressed the opinion that the area could not support increased parking needs. She believed builders
had fought for changes from the beginning. Ms. Drake suggested many of the problems could have
been avoided with a pre-construction meeting with the developer.

Norm Nielsen, West Jordan resident, a bus driver employed by the school district, echoed Mr.
McConnehey’s comments, and suggested some tweaking of traffic signals to relieve congestion.

Ann Marie Barrett, Bingham Creek Library Manager, announced upcoming events and activities at
the library. She announced that the library would undergo a temporary closure beginning February
20 for the installation of new carpet, with plans to reopen in mid-April.

Candida Benson, West Jordan resident, working for Liberty Homes, was incredibly sorry for
frustrations the Drake family had gone through with the development process. She said City staff had
proposed a compromise in terms of parking on Drake Lane that was included in the proposed
Development Agreement for review that evening. She believed leaving the Senior Housing Overlay
(SHO) on the development would create more problems, and felt homes would sell faster, allowing
Liberty to get out of the way of existing residents, without the SHO. Ms. Benson believed removing
the SHO was in the best interest of everyone involved.

Scott Bale, West Jordan resident, asked that lights in the parking lot of the library and senior center
remain on until those establishments closed in the evenings. Mr. Bale spoke of xeriscaping and the
need to conserve water. He suggested availability of an on-demand dumpster for soil disposal in the
community. He voiced appreciation for the City’s online newsletter, and suggested including a
spotlight of community members, as well as an energy, water, or money saving tip from residents.

David Pack, West Jordan resident, agreed with the lease agreement for the Historical Society
included on the agenda. Mr. Pack suggested an amendment to Council Policy and Procedure
suggesting the entire council be involved in scheduling items on the agenda rather than it being the
sole responsibility of the chair. Referring to a previous Council discussion regarding parking needed
for a senior neighborhood versus a single-family neighborhood, Mr. Pack said it was his experience
that older people had more vehicles and therefore needed more parking for visiting family members.
He suggested that lifting the SHO could enhance parking in the area.

Chair Jacob closed public comments at 6:26 pm.

 6. PUBLIC HEARINGS
       a. Resolution No. 24-003 authorizing the Mayor to execute a Lease Agreement for the
          West Jordan Historical Society
          City Administrator Korban Lee presented a proposed Lease Agreement with the
          Historical Society for City-owned land at the north end of Veterans Park, with a potential
          extension through 2034. Council Member Green inquired about the ownership structure,
          noting that the house and barn were owned by a different party than the land. Mr. Lee
          acknowledged that the house predated the City's ownership of the property but was
          unsure of the recorded ownership details.

           Chair Jacob opened a public hearing at 7:06 pm.
           Comments:
           Robby Dalley, acting Historical Society President, appreciated the opportunity for the new
           Lease Agreement. He said the Historical Society experienced a rough 18 months.

           Chair Jacob closed the public hearing at 7:07 pm.

           Council Member Whitelock was in favor of a museum for the City and appreciated the
           work of the Dalley family. She voiced concerns regarding the proposed Lease Agreement,
           including the absence of an insurance certificate, and the need to review the bylaws as
           stated in the Agreement. She had previously understood that the Council would receive
           reports on the number of museum visitors and museum events. She could not vote in
           favor without reviewing information missing from the report, including names of board
           members and hours of museum operation.

           Mr. Lee explained that prior to the Mayor’s execution of the Lease Agreement, the
           insurance certificate would have to be received and reviewed by the City. Council Member
           Bedore agreed that set hours of operation for the museum would be beneficial. City
           Attorney Josh Chandler clarified that the Lease Agreement included indemnification
           language that would absolve the City of liability.

           Council Member Bloom shared concerns about hours of museum operation, expressed
           the opinion that hours needed to post, and asked how the Historical Society would be held
           accountable to the lease. Council Member Green was uncomfortable that the Lease
           included a statement that the museum could be closed from Thanksgiving to the end of
           February, a quarter of the year. He expressed the opinion that the museum should be
           open more often.

           Council Member Shelton believed Council Member Green had a good point. However, he
           pointed out that such requests had dollar signs attached, and the museum operated solely
           on volunteer hours. Council Member Green responded that use of the City-owned land
           was valued at $64,620 per year, and he believed residents should be able to enjoy the
           property with regular hours. Council Member Bedore clarified that he was asking for
           hours of operation to be posted, not that the museum be open more often.




City Council Meeting Minutes              Wednesday, January 24, 2024                          Page 4
           Chair Jacob welcomed further comment from Mr. Dalley. Mr. Dalley reported that the
           Historical Society carried a $1 million insurance policy and had for 24 years. He said
           regular hours were not posted for security reasons, noting the property was broken into
           and vandalized a number of years ago. The hours of operation were posted on the website.
           Mr. Dalley said the average age of Historical Society volunteers was 80, and the board was
           not willing to ask those volunteers to volunteer during winter months. The museum was
           open Tuesdays and Thursdays, typically from noon to 5:00 pm.

           Mr. Dalley said the insurance certificate and list of board members would be easy to
           provide, but the bylaws might take more time to find due to a recent move. He commented
           that the bylaws were reviewed by the City 24 years ago and hadn’t changed since. He
           would do his best to find a copy for review. Mr. Lee confirmed that the Agreement would
           not be executed until the required insurance certificate was provided.

           Council Member Green understood Council Member Whitelock’s concerns, but wondered
           how much the Council would get into the weeds for 501(c)(3)s. Council Member Shelton
           trusted the Mayor to check all the boxes before signing the Lease Agreement, and believed
           the Council should push the matter forward. Council Member Whitelock felt it was her
           responsibility as a Council Member to verify for the residents.

           MOTION: Council Member Whitelock moved to POSTPONE Resolution No. 24-003
                   to February 14 to get a certificate of insurance and list of board members.
                   Council Member Bloom seconded the motion.

           Chair Jacob said he would be comfortable moving the matter forward for administration

           everything in place setting precedent.

       b. Ordinance No. 24-01 amending the 2009 West Jordan Municipal Code Section 13-11-
          2G: Home Occupation Standards: Use Outside Residence; and 13-2-3 “Home
          Occupation” definition
          City Planner/Zoning Administrator Larry Gardner commented that the current Home
          Occupation Ordinance was 24 years old, and while it had served the City well, things had
          changed over 24 years. He explained the current ordinance allowed for only the primary
          dwelling to be used for home occupation, up to 500 square feet. Mr. Gardner explained
          proposed amendments that would allow a home occupation in a garage or accessory
          dwelling. He spoke of the importance of preserving the residential nature of
          neighborhoods. Mr. Gardner reported that the Planning Commission reviewed the



City Council Meeting Minutes              Wednesday, January 24, 2024                          Page 5
           proposed amendments and forwarded a positive recommendation by unanimous vote.
           Staff answered questions from the Council.

           Chair Jacob opened a public hearing at 7:46 pm.

           Comments:
           Brian Ramos, West Jordan resident, shared his experience with a home-based business.
           His wife previously had a home-based business out of her garage, and when they moved
           to another home, they assumed the license would transfer over. They built an ADU on the
           new property for the business, but the license was denied since the business would not
           be operated from the primary residence. Mr. Ramos expressed a hope that Council would
           consider expanding the ordinance, allowing his wife to operate her business out of the
           ADU rather than the primary residence.

           Chair Jacob closed the public hearing at 7:48 pm.

           Council Member Green said he would vote in favor because he believed the changes
           needed to occur but wanted to schedule further discussion at a future meeting to correct
           contradicting language in the code. He suggested increasing the maximum square feet to
           accommodate larger garage sizes. Council Member Bloom expressed support for the
           proposed ordinance and agreed with scheduling further discussion to make things more
           common sense.

           MOTION: Council Member Shelton moved to APPROVE Ordinance No. 24-01
                   amending the 2009 West Jordan Municipal Code Section 13-11-2G:
                   Home Occupation Standards: Use Outside Residence; and 13-2-3 “Home

           MOTION: Council Member Green made a SUBSTITUTE motion to APPROVE
                   Ordinance No. 24-01 amending the 2009 West Jordan Municipal Code

                   February 1, 2024.
                   Chair Jacob seconded the motion.

       d. Ordinance No. 24-05 a petition from the City of West Jordan for Three Station Area
          Plans pursuant to Utah Code 10-9a-403.1 (HB 462, 2022 G.S.) for Bingham Junction
          (Midvale), Historic Gardner, and West Jordan City Center located at 7387 S Bingham
          Junction Boulevard (Midvale), 1127 West 7800 South, and 8021 S Redwood Road,
          respectively



City Council Meeting Minutes              Wednesday, January 24, 2024                         Page 7
           Senior Planner Tayler Jensen introduced Chris Geddes with Design Workshop. Mr. Geddes
           showed the Bingham Junction, Historic Gardner, and City Center Station Areas on a map,
           showing overlap between the areas. He explained the Station Area Plans (SAPs) were
           required by HB 462 (2022) to address transit access, affordable and attainable housing,
           and environmental conditions. The SAPs were intended to create expectations for
           development of Utah Transit Authority (UTA) properties, and establish a vision and guide
           policy decisions for communities without directing development of private property.

           Mr. Geddes described the timeline and community engagement process involved in
           putting together the Station Area Plans. He presented a preferred concept for the Historic
           Gardner SAP, with potential development of a portion of UTA property for mixed-use
           residential, and showed proposed circulation, open space, and land use diagrams.

           Mr. Geddes presented a preferred concept for the City Center SAP, including a festival
           park and restaurant row, with proposed circulation, open space, and land use diagrams.
           He presented ideas for phase one implementation and policy recommendations, and
           suggested build-out would likely occur over 20 years. Responding to a question from
           Chair Jacob, staff explained Council Members Green, Whitelock, and Bloom participated
           on an SAP subcommittee. He commented that Council Members Pack and Jacob attended
           and provided feedback at an open house.

           Chair Jacob opened a public hearing at 8:46 pm.

           Comments:
           None

           Chair Jacob closed the public hearing at 8:46 pm.

           MOTION: Council Member Green moved to APPROVE Ordinance No. 24-05 a
                   petition from the City of West Jordan for Three Station Area Plans
                   pursuant to Utah Code 10-9a-403.1 (HB 462, 2022 G.S.) for Bingham
                   Junction (Midvale), Historic Gardner, and West Jordan City Center
                   located at 7387 S Bingham Junction Boulevard (Midvale), 1127 West
                   7800 South, and 8021 S Redwood Road, respectively.
                   Council Member Bloom seconded the motion.

           Council Member Whitelock thanked the Design Workshop team and staff for the work
           done on the SAPs and thanked the Council for the opportunity to serve on the ad hoc
           committee. She said there were some elements of the proposed SAPs with which she did
           not agree, but believed the proposed ordinance was a way to move forward with the
           partners involved to create something wonderful for the City. Council Member Green
           thanked everyone involved with the project and expressed support despite not agreeing
           with some elements. He commented that UTA had expressed willingness to discuss the
           possibility of a free-fare zone in the Historic Gardner area.

           Council Member Bedore believed the City Center needed a better identity. He wanted to
           make sure the SAPs were not set in stone, and hoped conversations would continue.
           Council Member Bloom said a majority of the Council had expressed a desire at a previous

City Council Meeting Minutes              Wednesday, January 24, 2024                          Page 8
           meeting for the City Center to have something unique that would draw people specifically
           to West Jordan.

           Chair Jacob appreciated the work that had been done, but felt nothing he requested had
           been included, and did not believe the Council as a body had provided input. Chair Jacob
           said the Council had expressed a desire for something bigger and better. He felt
           presenting the ordinance for adoption without consensus of the Council was premature.
           Chair Jacob commented that the deadline for adoption from the State was not until
           December 31, 2025.

           Mr. Jensen said the proposed SAPs would be a baseline to build from. He expressed fear
           that the choice to not adopt that evening might send a message that the City was putting
           on the brakes and pulling back. He suggested that discussion of adding something bigger
           could be included with the RFP process. Mr. Jensen said the proposed SAPs were a result
           of the public process. He spoke of private property involved, and said top-down planning
           seemed liked overreach. Chair Jacob responded that approval of the residents was for
           elected officials to worry about, and expressed the opinion that the proposed plan was
           not big enough.

           Council Member Bloom called the question, which passed by majority consent (5-1).

           The vote was recorded as follows:
           YES:    Pamela Bloom, Kelvin Green, Kayleen Whitelock, Bob Bedore, Kent
                   Shelton
           NO:     Zach Jacob
           ABSENT: Chad Lamb
           The motion passed 5-1.

 7. BUSINESS ITEMS
       a. Review of the Cottages at Parker Place rezone and amended development plan,
          updated from relevant discussions from the Committee of the Whole meeting on
          January 17, 2024
          Assistant Planner Mark Forsythe updated the Council on changes to the proposed
          Amended Development Plan for Cottages at Parker Place. The plan included a proposal to
          prohibit parking on the north side of Drake Lane, and restricted parking on the south side
          of Drake Lane to where the road curved. He reported that the Drake family provided
          feedback that a set timeframe for parking restrictions would not be acceptable,
          considering farm operations.

           MOTION:       Council Member Green moved to APPROVE Ordinance No. 24-06
                         Cottages at Parker Place Rezone, and Ordinance No. 24-07 Cottages at
                         Parker Place Amended Development Plan.
                         Council Member Bloom seconded the motion.

           Council Member Whitelock believed Liberty had been kind to existing residents. She
           suspected that Mrs. Drake was tired and burned out with regard to the project, and hoped

           the situation to be a difficult vote and did not want to set a precedent by removing the
