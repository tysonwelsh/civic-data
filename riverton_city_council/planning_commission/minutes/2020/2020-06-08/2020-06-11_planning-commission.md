  1.     AMENDED SITE PLAN, GREG LARSEN IS SEEKING APPROVAL OF
         AMENDMENTS TO THE APPROVED SITE PLAN FOR COUNTRY LIFE CARE
         CENTER LOCATED AT 13757 SOUTH REDWOOD ROAD.

  Zoning Administrator, Tim Prestwich, presented the staff report and stated that the
  amendments on the agenda for approval are for a pavilion and an oxygen tank on the
  property listed above. The property was identified on a map displayed. The pavilion details
  were outlined.

  The applicant will obtain a Hazmat permit from the Unified Fire Authority (“UFA”) for the
  requested oxygen tank. Currently, individual oxygen tanks are wheeled into each building
  and refilled frequently. The oxygen tank requested will be located in a secured area, which
  will provide a safer environment for the residents and employees. The oxygen tank details
  were described.

  Staff recommended approval of the above item as it meets all City requirements.

  Commissioner Hansen asked if the oxygen piping will be underground or overhead.
  Mr. Prestwich responded it will be underground.

  Chair Cannon asked if the oxygen tank was an oversight in the previous plan. Mr. Prestwich
  responded that this particular care center has received a significant number of residents with
  oxygen needs. In the event of an emergency or disruption of services, the larger tank can
  provide better and longer care for patients.

The applicant, Greg Larsen, reported that the oxygen tank will serve both buildings and the
majority of the patients are on ventilators. Currently, there are no COVID-19 patients,

2.     CONDITIONAL USE, HOWARD SCHMIDT IS REQUESTING A CONDITIONAL USE
       PERMIT FOR A PRIVATE LANE AND FENCE IMPROVEMENTS PROPOSED AT
       13791 SOUTH 3870 WEST.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that this is a two-part conditional use for
a private lane to access a single home as well as for a fencing change that must be approved
by the Planning Commission per the Riverton City ordinance. The property was identified
on a map displayed. At a previous Planning Commission Meeting, the applicant reported
that the home will be required to provide its own access. Subsequently, the applicant
purchased the home and is part owner of Lot 32. He would like to provide access to the
house on a private lane across Lot 32. There is a five-foot gap of property that has been
introduced and the County requires it to be under separate ownership from the house and
Lot 32. Consequently, the conditions of approval were modified to assist the applicant.

Mr. Prestwich reviewed the previous site plan and stated that staff has reviewed the
application. The gate is currently 12 feet wide and the UFA requires 15 feet to access the
property. A house cannot be built on the lot with the current setbacks; however, there is no
plan to build a home. If a home is built, they will move it to meet the Code requirements.

Mr. Prestwich reviewed the ordinance and state that there is access to 13800 South;
however, a continuous rail fence aligns the south property line. If the conditional use is
approved, 3870 West will serve as the access.

The request also includes an amendment to modify the fencing requirements. Mr. Prestwich
reviewed the current fencing and revisions the applicant would like to make. The Code
requirements were read and the conditions of approval were reviewed. The matter was
noticed as a public hearing. One resident called who lives to the south of the subject
property but did not feel they would be impacted.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

Commissioner Lefgren asked if the lot is owned by the person living in the home.
Mr. Prestwich confirmed that the home is owned by a trust and Lot 32 is owned by two LLCs
with a 50/50 partnership. Commissioner Lefgren asked if any of the utilities associated with
the property are to be capped and left dormant until a future time. Mr. Prestwich responded
that staff did not have that information and has requested more detailed plans.

Commissioner James asked how the Commission can circumvent the ordinance when there
is access on 13800 South. City Attorney, Craig Bott, explained that if there is current
accessibility, it is not possible to circumvent the ordinance. Commissioner James stated
that he approved the access from the south, which would allow for a home to be built without
destroying the trees on the property.

Commissioner Rushton asked if the property owner desired to use the existing home as an
Airbnb or a reception center. If so, he asked if the applicant be required to return to the
Planning Commission for a rezone to review the access to the property. Mr. Prestwich
confirmed that an Airbnb would require a Conditional Use Permit but the ordinance does not

4.     GENERAL PLAN, RIVERTON CITY IS PROPOSING AMENDMENTS TO THE
       LAND USE ELEMENT OF THE GENERAL PLAN.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

Mr. Lethbridge presented the staff report and stated that there is a request to review the
amendments by district. A district map was displayed. Each of the districts and the
recommended amendments for the above item were reviewed.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that the property to the rear of Stampin’ Up located at 3600 West
12600 South has been identified as a discussion item to the Commission. The notices that
were sent identified Medium-Density Residential as a potential designation for the area.
Following the noticing and recommendation, there had been further discussion with the
property owner, interested parties in the area, as well as the Commission. Mr. Lethbridge
stated that this has been a difficult parcel of land because of the original zoning and Master
Plan for the area. After receiving feedback from the owner of Stampin’ Up and in response
to rezone applications, the determination was made to retain the Light Industrial designation
for the area. The Medium-Density layer shown on the map displayed was not accurate or
intended to be there.

Mr. Lethbridge reviewed additional changes to the General Plan in specific districts. Stampin
Up’s Attorney, Timothy Anderson, stated that he was involved in the acquisition of the
property and has been the applicant’s attorney for 27 years. The company has not
expanded to the level anticipated; however, it is doing very well. There are potential
opportunities for the property to the rear of the Stampin’ Up building and he desires for it to
be compatible with the community. Mr. Anderson appreciated being involved with the
changes and approval of the Light Industrial designation.

Commissioner Rushton asked if the parcel in the corner that was rezoned is still owned by
Stampin’ Up. Mr. Anderson stated that it is not. Commissioner Rushton thanked
Mr. Anderson for staying true to the vision.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Commissioner Lefgren asked what was decided on the historic designation along Redwood
Road. Commissioner Russell approved of the current designation. Mr. Lethbridge
commented that a study was completed that established general parameters for the Historic
Preservation Area in this location.
