Proposed Municipal Boundary Adjustment — South Ogden City
Planning Manager Brierley explained South Ogden City is requesting a boundary adjustment for a section of Country ,
Hills Drive between Gramercy Avenue and 925 East. The adjustment would shift the boundary from the south side of the Country
Hills Drive right-of-way to the centerline of the right-of-way. The request is the result of South Ogden City’s approval of an
apartment building close to the property line and concerns Ogden City has with liability for issues related to access in and out of
the development.
Council member Hyer asked for clarification that this boundary adjustment will clean up boundary issues for the length
of Country Hills Drive for a substantial length of the road. Mr. Brierley answered yes and presented a map to identify the sections
of the road in which the boundary will be adjusted. He concluded the proposed boundary adjustment was reviewed by the
Planning Commission on May 3, 2023. The Commission recommended approval of the adjustment.
Proposed Municipal Boundary Adjustment — Marriott-Slaterville City
Planning Manager Brierley explained Marriott-Slaterville City, in conjunction with Weber School District, is requesting
a city boundary adjustment to place two Weber School District properties in Marriott-Slaterville City which is in Weber School
District boundaries. Currently, two properties on west 12th Street are owned by Weber School District but are within Ogden
City’s boundaries. The adjustment would realign the school district boundaries with City boundaries for the two properties. He
presented a map to orient the Council to the properties owned by the Weber School District and within the Business Depot Ogden
(BDO) District that are subject to the proposed boundary adjustment. The Planning Commission recommends approval of the
‘proposed boundary adjustment subject to Weber School District abandoning the storm drain line that discharges into the Ogden
_” Nature Center and modifying each City’s boundary according to the proposed boundary adjustment plat.
Chair Choberka asked if Marriott-Slaterville or Ogden City representatives have made contact with the Ogden Nature
Center about this proposal; the Center has had issues with water service in water rights and it would be good to discuss the storm
drain adjustments with the Center.

Proposed Zone Text Amendment — Multi-family Residential Design
Standards in Residential Zones

Planning Manager Brierley explained the Ogden City Planning Division is proposing design standard changes for new
multi-family residential development in residential zones. In 2020, the Planning Division conducted an extensive study
researching design standards for multi-family residential development in commercial zones and significant changes were made as
a result. The 2020 changes were specific to multi-family design standards in commercial zones and did not apply to residential
zones. As a follow-up to those changes, the current proposal is to update the design standards for multi-family residential
development in residential zones. An initial work session was held on October 11, 2022, to review the proposed changes. At that
meeting, Council members requested additional information on options for electric vehicle (EV) parking standards. At the
November 29, 2022, work session, Planning Staff presented options for EV parking for the Council’s consideration. Council
members also expressed interest in input from the City’s Natural Resources and Sustainability Stewardship (Sustainability)
Committee. Mr. Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the Sustainability Committee’s
recommendations; they have researched projections for the number of EVs that will be located in Ogden between the years 2020
and 2030 and they recommend an increase in the number of charging stations required over time based on EV ownership. They
also recommended clarification of the definition of a garage adaptable system. He presented three different options for charging
stations that could be installed throughout the City. Planning staff has presented multiple optional ordinance amendments
regarding EV charging stations; the Council could choose to maintain the current ordinance, which allows for EV charging
stations in residential zones; option two is to reconcile the existing ordinance by addressing EV charging stations in commercial
zones, downtown zones, zones that allow group dwellings, and the R-9 zone. Option three would be to impose a universal
standard for EV charging stations for all zones in the City.

The Council discussed the implications of each of the options and the best choice for the City; this included discussion
about the potential for an increase in the number of electric vehicles in the community in the future and whether the City should
impose requirements for EV charging stations now or at a later date when there are more EVs. There was also discussion of |
whether the private market will assume the responsibility for including EV charging stations in private developments or
commercial businesses, such as gas stations.

Council Deputy Director Symes stated that the original proposal was to amend multi-family design standards and the
proposed ordinance did not include requirements for EV charging stations; the Council can proceed with the changes to the multi-
family development design standards in residential zones without EV charging stations and then revisit the EV charging station
matter as an independent issue at a later date. The Council reached consensus to proceed with the ordinance regarding multi-
family development design standards without the EV charging station component. Chair White dissented and indicated she would
like to develop a universal standard for EV charging stations throughout all zones of the City. Mr. Symes indicated Council staff
will add an action item to the calendar for multi family development design standards and begin working with Planning staff on a
separate ordinance amendment regarding EV charging stations.
