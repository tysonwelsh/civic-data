ITEMS OF SPECIAL INTEREST
o Election Summary Report — Clerk/Auditor Jess Bradfield cited the following

statistics: 55,962 ballots were cast this election. The previous record in 2016 was
47,110 ballots cast with an 85.46% turnout. Turnout is currently 87.67% and trending to
achieve 90% voter turnout. Everyone was offered an opportunity to vote. If a ballot had
not been received, another was immediately sent or the voter was invited to vote at our
Election Day Vote Center. If contacted by a voter with a concern, staff made sure a
solution was found. Bradfield commended his staff for an exceptional job. Some aspects
of elections need updating. The clerk’s office is in need of supplies and equipment for
the future, which he anticipates can be run faster and more efficiently. The greatest
need is space. With the right equipment and space Cache County will be the best in the
state. Bradfield thanked the Council for its ongoing support. Chairman Ward asked if all
the ballots have been counted. Bradfield said, “No, there is 3-4% more to be counted
which is roughly 2,000-3000 ballots.” In reference to how the Event Center worked as a
Vote Center, Bradfield said because of its large space the Event Center was used as a
way to mitigate Corona Virus with more than 5,000 people voting in person. The staff

o Bear River Mental Health (BRMH) Area Plan — Beth Smith indicated official action is

needed by the Council to approve the area plan. Because of its size, there is no hard
copy available, but it is on the Bear River Mental Health website for their review. The
plan ties funding to the different services they provide. The colored text indicates
changes. Council members want to review the online plan before taking action. Smith
gave a short overview of mental health statistics/concerns.

Council member Erickson asked if CARES funding can help with staffing problems.
Smith replied CARES cannot be used for salaries because that is an ongoing expense. .
Executive Buttars asked if the staff shortage is due to a lack of applicants or a lack of
funding. Smith replied they need Masters Level and above and are not seeing any
PhD’s which are needed for psychological testing. Applicants tend to be right out of
school and leave for other positions after receiving licensing training at BRMH.

o Request for Modification of a RAPZ Project — Logan City — Logan Parks and

Recreation Director Russ Akina said a Logan application for RAPZ was part of a match
needed for a state grant. The original number of acres listed for the project has
changed. Akina asked if the RAPZ funds can be prorated as the acreage may be
smaller than first thought. The deadline for the grant money is February 14, 2021 and
Logan City would like something in writing for the state indicating Cache County is still
supportive of the project. Akina added, if not prorated, Logan would like to take leftover
dollars and use them to complete the Trapper Park project. Council members concurred
that if the project amount is less than the original estimate, the residual funds will go
back into the competitive poo! and Logan would have to apply for RAPZ funding for
Trapper Park. The Council also asked for more solid numbers and detail before taking
any action. Akina will secure more details for the Council.

O Ordinance No. 2020-11 —- BGTS Rezone (VO ACTION TAKEN) — Director Harrild

reminded the Council this property is an eating disorder center for young women. They
currently have eight beds and want to expand to twelve; however, because of State and
County Code changes in order to have twelve beds a rezone to commercial is
necessary.

In answer to Council members’ questions, Harrild said the code change was triggered by

( a lawsuit where the Federal code won, so the state and county updated their codes to
reflect the Federal. Harrild remarked Paradise Mayor Vic Little voiced objections to the
rezone at the Public Hearing October 27, 2020.

The issue was tabled until Harrild finds out more about Paradise Town’s concerns and
checks with the County Attorney’s office for a legal review to address Council member
concerns.

Ordinance No. 2020-10
Cache County, Utah

Butcher Shop Rezone

An ordinance request to amend the County Zoning Map by rezoning 2.27 acres of property
from the Agricultural (A10) Zone to the Industrial (I) Zone

Whereas, the “County Land Use Development and Management Act,” Utah Code Ann.

§17-27a-101 et seq., as amended (the “Act”), provides that each county may enact a land

use ordinance and a zoning map establishing regulations for land use and development; and

Whereas, pursuant to the Act, the County’s Planning Commission (the “Planning
Commission”) shall prepare and recommend to the county’s legislative body, following a
public hearing, a proposed land use ordinance and a zoning map, or amendments thereto,
that represent the Planning Commission’s recommendations for zoning the area within the

county; and

Whereas, the Planning Commission caused notice of a public hearing for the rezone to be
advertised at least ten (10) days before the date of the public hearing in The Herald Journal,
a newspaper of general circulation in Cache County; and

Whereas, on October 1, 2020, the Planning Commission held a public hearing, accepted
all comments, and recommended the denial of the proposed amendments to the County
Council for final action; and

Whereas, the Act also provides certain procedures for the county legislative body to
adopt or reject amendments to the land use ordinance and zoning map for the county; and

Whereas, following proper notice, the County Council held a public hearing on October
27, 2020, to consider any comments regarding the proposed rezone. The County Council
accepted all comments; and

Whereas, the Cache County Council has determined that it is both necessary and
appropriate for the County to amend and implement this ordinance.

Now, therefore, the County Legislative Body of Cache County ordains as follows:

1, Statutory Authority
The statutory authority for enacting this ordinance is Utah Code Annotated Sections 17-
27a Part 1 and Part 3, and 17-53 part 2(1953, as amended to date).

2. Adoption of amended Zoning Map .
The County Council hereby amends the County’s Zoning Map to reflect the rezone of the
property affected by this ordinance and hereby adopts the amended Zoning Map with

bd

the amendment identified as Exhibit B, of which a detailed digital or paper copy is
available in the Development Services Department.
Conclusions
A. The location of the subject property is compatible with the purpose of the Industrial
(I) Zone as identified under §17.08.030[F] of the Cache County Code as it:
i, Allows for industrial development in an area with minimal residential
development, which may limit conflict or deleterious effects upon surrounding
properties.

4. Prior ordinances, resolutions, policies, and actions superseded

This ordinance amends and supersedes the Zoning Map of Cache County, and all prior
ordinances, resolutions, policies, and actions of the Cache County Council to the extent
that the provisions of such prior ordinances, resolutions, policies, or actions are in
conflict with this ordinance. In all other respects, such prior ordinances, resolutions,
policies, and actions shall remain in full force and effect.

5. Exhibits

A. Exhibit A: Rezone summary and information
B. Exhibit B: Zoning Map of Cache County showing affected portion.

6. Effective date

This ordinance takes effect on , 2020. Following its passage
but prior to the effective date, a copy of the ordinance shall be deposited with the
County Clerk and a short summary of the ordinance shall be published in a newspaper of
general circulation within the County as required by law.
