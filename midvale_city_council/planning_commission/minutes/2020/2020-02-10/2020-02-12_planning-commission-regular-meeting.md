   2. SUB-21-35-201-004; JORDAN BLUFFS LOT 201 AMENDED SUBDIVISION; 983
      W CENTER ST; PRELIMINARY SUBDIVISION PLAT FOR 3-LOT
      SUBDIVISION; JORDAN BLUFFS SUBAREAS 1-3 ZONE; GARDNER CO
      (APPLICANT)

Mr. Alex Murphy presented that the applicant, Gardner Jordan Bluffs LC, is requesting approval
to subdivide property located 983 West Center Street (7800 South) into three lots to dedicate a
public right-of-way and separate parcels Gardner is required to retain from other developable
parcels in the northern portion of Jordan Bluffs Subareas 1-3 area. The lots created by the
subdivision include Pods D&E (Lot 1), Pods F-H (Lot 2), and Pods I-N (Lot 3) as laid out on the
pod map in Section 17-7-10.12.1 of the zoning ordinance. The subject property is approximately
150 acres in size and is bounded by Center Street (7800 South) on the north, Bingham Junction
Boulevard on the east, the Jordan River on the west, and the cap slope south. Development plans
for the resulting lots will be presented to the Planning Commission for review in a later meeting
as part of the Large-Scale Master Plan process for the zone.

ORDINANCE CONSIDERATIONS:

The proposed subdivision is subject to Midvale City Municipal Code Title 16 (Subdivisions),
and Chapter 17-7-10.12 (Jordan Bluffs Subareas 1-3 Development Zone). Because of the
project’s proximity to the Jordan River and associated special flood hazard area identified by
FEMA, Chapter 8.11 (Flood Damage Prevention) also applies.

Because the proposal includes the dedication of a public road, the large subdivision process laid
out in Section 16.04.040 applies. Staff has found the proposed subdivision generally complies
with the design standard requirements of Title 16 Subdivisions. No lot standards apply for the
zone and the proposed street layouts meet the layout requirements of the ordinance. Redesign of
the planted median in Bingham Junction Boulevard will be required to accommodate the
proposed intersection but can be postponed until the new street is built in conjunction with the
adjacent pod(s). Street tree requirements have been overridden by the development zone and will
be addressed during the site plan review. Public and private infrastructure improvements will be
included as part of individual site development plans. Staff recommends requiring notes on the

PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION FEBRUARY 12TH, 2020
plat indicating street trees, public infrastructure, and reconstruction of the Bingham Junction
Boulevard median will be required at the time of site development.

The subdivision ordinance requires that duty to serve letters from the water and sewer provider
(Midvale City) be provided and courtesy notices be sent regarding utility easements to Dominion
Energy, Rocky Mountain Power, Xfinity, Utopia, and CenturyLink prior to final plat approval.
Staff recommends including this requirement as a condition of approval.

Staff has found the proposed subdivision also complies with the requirements of the
development zone. The zone allows a subdivision plat to identify and dedicate roadways between
pods and to establish park areas prior to submission of the required Large-Scale Master Plan.
Further development on the site will require the applicant follow the Large-Scale Master Plan
review process identified in Chapter 17-7-10.12 and Staff recommends restating this requirement
as a condition of approval.

Chapter 8.11, Flood Damage Prevention, establishes additional requirements for development
within identified special flood hazard areas, also known as floodplains. The applicant has
addressed these requirements by including Base Flood Elevations and the boundary of the 100-
year special flood hazard area on the plat with a note indicating future development within the
special flood hazard area will be required to comply with the development standards of Chapter
8.11.

The subject property currently contains a construction trailer that was placed by the owner last
year. A permit request for that structure was denied and removal of that structure and its
associated improvements is required. Staff recommends requiring its removal prior to recording
of the final plat.

The applicant’s proposal was sent to the City Engineer and Unified Fire Authority for review and
comment. The Fire Marshal and City Engineer are reviewing the plat; their comments will be
provided at the meeting.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION:

Based on compliance with the requirements of the Midvale City Municipal Code demonstrated
in the application or addressed by conditions of approval, Planning Staff recommends the
Planning Commission forward a positive recommendation to the City Council to approve the
preliminary subdivision plat for the Jordan Bluffs Lot 201 Amended Subdivision with the
following findings and conditions of approval:

Findings:

   1. Per Section 17-7-10.12.2(D), a subdivision plat may be recorded to identify and dedicate
      roadways between pods and to establish the boundary of the park before submission of a
      large-scale master planned development application.
   2. With the conditions included in this approval, the proposed subdivision complies with the
      requirements of Title 16; Chapter 17-7-10.12; and Chapter 8.11 of the Midvale City
      Municipal Code.

   3. CUP-22-31-153-003; GEO STAKES; 88 E 8000 S; CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT
      FOR MANUFACTURING USE; CLEAN INDUSTRIAL ZONE; GEO STAKE
      LLC (APPLICANT)

Ms. Jana Ward presented that the applicant, Geo Stake LLC, is requesting a conditional use
permit approval to establish and operate a manufacturing business in an existing 16,255 SF
industrial building located at 88 E 8000S. The building contains multiple tenant spaces, with the
applicant proposing to occupy the furthest south portion of the building in a 4,000 SF tenant
space, with direct access on a non-exclusive right of way on the adjacent property to the west
owned by Harper-Kilgore LLC. The property is in the Clean Industrial zone (CI).

The proposed use is an open floor plan for the manufacturing of wood construction and
surveying stakes. The hours are proposed to be 5:00 AM to 3:00 PM, Monday through Friday.

The subject property is adjacent to other Clean Industrial (CI) zoned properties with multi-family
zoning to the east across the Trax line.

ORDINANCE CONSIDERATIONS:

The Clean Industrial (CI) zone allows manufacturing uses under a conditional use permit,
provided the Planning Commission concludes that the application fully mitigates all identified
adverse impacts and complies with the conditional use general standards:

       1. The application complies with all applicable provisions of this title, State and Federal
          Law;

       2. The structures associated with the use are compatible with surrounding structures in
           terms of use, scale, mass and circulation;
       3. The use is not detrimental to the public health, safety and welfare;
       4. The use is consistent with the Midvale City General Plan, as amended;
       5. Traffic conditions are not adversely affected by the proposed use including the
           existence of or need for dedicated turn lanes, pedestrian access, and capacity of the
           existing streets;
       6. Sufficient utility capacity;
       7. Sufficient emergency vehicle access;
       8. Location and design of off-street parking as well as compliance with off-street
           parking standards provided for in §17-7-13.8;
       9. Fencing, screening, and landscaping to separate the use from adjoining uses and
           mitigate the potential for conflict in uses;
       10. Compatibility of the proposed mass, bulk, design, orientation, and location of the
           structures on the site; including compatibility with buildings on adjoining lots and to

       11. Exterior lighting that complies with the lighting standards of the zone and is designed
           to minimize conflict and light trespass with surrounding uses; and
       12. Within and adjoining the site, impacts on the aquifer, slope retention, and flood
           potential have been fully mitigated and the proposed structure is appropriate to the
           topography of the site.

Staff has not identified any apparent conflicts with provisions of the zoning ordinance or state
and federal law. The business will be conducted within the existing development and tenant
space. Modifications to the site are proposed to be made and the necessary applications have
been made. If any future changes to the site or building are proposed, another review will be
required. To comply with other requirements of the Midvale City Municipal Code, a building
permit for tenant improvements may be required and a business license will need to be obtained.
Staff recommends including these two items as conditions of approval.

No apparent concerns regarding public health, safety, or welfare have been identified.

For manufacturing uses, the minimum parking requirement is 2.5 spaces per 1,000 SF of floor
area. For warehouse uses the minimum parking requirement is 1 space per 1,000 SF. The
applicant has provided a floor plan showing 975 SF of manufacturing space with the rest of the
building intended for a storage. The total parking stalls needed to meet the requirement is seven
(7) stalls. Seven (7) stalls are shown on the site plan, these stalls will need to be stripped, and
staff recommends including this as a condition of approval.

Staff has received an email from a representative of the Harper-Kilgore property owner
indicating the applicant does not have rights to use the Harper-Kilgore property for access, which
conflicts with information provided by the applicant. This is a civil dispute which needs to be
resolved by the property owners involved, not by the City. Before this conditional use permit can
be approved, the applicant will need to obtain a letter from the adjacent property owner (Harper-
Kilgore LLC) allowing the applicant to use the non-exclusive right of way recorded with their
property or a determination from the Court allowing the applicant’s use of the non-exclusive
right of way.

   4. JORDAN BLUFFS POD C CONCEPT PLAN FOR CONDOMINIUM
      DEVELOPMENT; MARK GARZA/HOLMES HOMES

Mr. Alex Murphy presented that the development review process under the Jordan Bluffs
Subareas 1-3 Ordinance requires the Planning Commission have a work session with the
developer for each Pod before any formal development applications are submitted. This is
intended to provide the Commission an opportunity to discuss and provide feedback to the
developer on their proposed concept plan before more detailed plans are prepared and submitted
for formal review. No action is required by the Planning Commission at this time as part of this
work session review.

Mark Garza, representing Holmes Homes, previously completed this process for a townhome
project on Pod C, obtaining a Large-Scale Master Plan (“LSMP”) approval from the Planning
Commission on September 25, 2019. Following that approval, Holmes Homes elected to revise
the plan. Mr. Garza has had initial meetings with staff, including an Initial Contact Meeting and
a Development Review Committee Meeting regarding the revised development proposal on Pod
C and now seeks the Planning Commission’s input.

The developer’s revised concept includes 360 2- and 3-bedroom condominium units in 3-story
buildings instead of the original 169 townhomes. The concept plan includes pedestrian
connections to the existing Jordan River Parkway trail via a trail corridor along the wetlands and
several amenity areas for the residents. Parking is provided in 1- and 2-car garages and in
exterior parking areas along the drive aisles, with some of the exterior spaces being reserved for
guests only.

For this revised proposal, the developer has retained many of the comments from the original
LSMP approval, including the Bingham Junction Boulevard crosswalk, additional trail and
internal pedestrian connections, and thematic elements. Other items the Planning Commission
may wish to discuss include:

   •   Interior development circulation for vehicles, bikes, and pedestrians and how they
       connect to adjacent developments inside and outside of Jordan Bluffs;
   •   Pedestrian connections to the Jordan River Parkway, 700 West, and interior recreation
       amenities;
   •   Amenity types and location;
   •   Building orientation towards streets, private roads, courtyards, walkways;
   •   Architecture;
   •   Parking layout;
   •   Fencing design and location; and
   •   Amenities for proposed trailhead along 700 West.

Following the discussion, the next step in review process is the submittal of a Large-Scale
Master Plan application for Pod C subject to submittal requirements in Section 17-7-10.12.4 of
the Midvale City Municipal Code. Staff has scheduled this public hearing for February 26.

DISCUSSION

The Commission had the following comments/questions:
   • This proposal more than doubles the unit count of the previous proposal in this Pod and
      what that leaves for the upper areas of Jordan Bluffs. How does the Master Developer
      feel about this? Concern with this area becoming overloaded.
   • Would like to see buildings face public streets.
   • Agrees with the need for affordable units and providing for “missing middle” housing

   • Ensure there are pedestrian connections to the public sidewalks, especially along 700
      West.

Mr. Mark Garza, representing Holmes Homes, stated that the affordability of development has
been an issue. They have discussed on many occasions with Gardner ways to resolve this. The
goal is to keep equality and affordability. The hope is that condos will have price points in the $
200,000’s. There is nothing like this offered within the valley. The products will be both 5 and
10-unit, 3 level structures. Units will have 2-3 bedrooms and be between 1200-1400 square feet.
They are over the code requirement by 8 stalls for parking. They will require the HOA to enforce
guest parking accommodations. They are also increasing the amenities packages for this project
to include the outside space improvements provided in the previous plan as well as a clubhouse
facility with computer and fitness centers. Each condo home unit would be single ownership.
Unfortunately, in order to meet the price point, they must increase the density.

The Commission is looking forward to hearing from the developer at the next Planning
Commission meeting.
