2.      Brookeshire Subdivision – Conceptual Plan – 5480 South Highland Drive – (R-2-10
        Zone) Review of Proposed Four-Lot Single-Family Detached Home Residential
        Subdivision, in Accordance with R-2-10 Zone Regulations. File #21-1-13.
Staff Planner, Robert Hobbs, reported that the above matter involves a land revision request, which
is handled administratively. Staff recommended approval subject to the conditions set forth in the
staff report. Mr. Hobbs explained that what is proposed are two parcels of property being re-
subdivided into four building lots. Mr. Hobbs reported that to his knowledge a PUD was not being
sought.

With regard to preliminary-plan finding number six, Mr. Hobbs explained that the discrepancy in
the number of lots is due to the initial review where certain aspects of a service drive are different
than in other jurisdictions. The areas that were counted toward the buildable areas were factored
back in and now comply with City standards. A strip of property on Highland Drive will be
dedicated to the City as part of this plat. An additional portion would be excluded and become
part of the travel way. The entire area depicted is dedicated with the applicant getting credited for
the portion inside the travel way and within the shoulder. He confirmed that access to the homes
will be via a private street.

The applicant, David Morton, thanked staff and stated that they have been great to work with
through the process. He reported that the neighborhood meeting was held on August 12 outside
of City Hall. A handful of individuals were present and all were in favor of the proposal and
desired more information. They were pleased that single-family lots are being proposed even
though he noted that the R-2-10 zone also allows for duplexes. Questions were raised specific to
the lot sizes. Mr. Morton stated that they tried to be transparent and were aware of no major issues.
The uses on the surrounding properties were described.

Commissioner Mackin reported that she visited the site earlier in the day and indicated that the
project to the south includes two ramblers on a private paved street. In response to a question
raised, Mr. Morton stated that at least two of the homes will be two-stories. Because of existing
foliage, no views will be obstructed by increasing to two stories.

Howard Layton asked about fencing and stated that adjoining neighbors are often sensitive to
perimeter fencing, particularly along the driveway. Mr. Morton stated that they plan to install a
solid white vinyl fence along the northern border. He noted that the entrance to the development
was relocated to the north side of the site to avoid potential conflicting turning movements with
the school across the street. The intent would be to provide similar fencing on both sides.

Chair Ricks opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing was
closed.

DISCUSSION ITEMS

4.     Planning Commission Elections – Chair and Vice-Chair – Mid-Year Election Cycle

5.      General Plan – Review of Updated Amendments to Chapter 2: Land Uses.
Community Development Director, Paul Allred suggested the Council have discussion on a draft
update to this part of the Plan based on what former Commission Member, Jim Carter had
prepared. Mr. Allred had no objections to what was proposed. He reminded the commissioners
that what was presented to them last year was not well received by either the commissioners or the
public that spoke up during the review. As a result, Mr. Allred asked Commissioner Carter to
prepare more acceptable language. He stated that a commitment was made to the State of Utah
last year that by December 2020 the City would have a third strategy (ADU’s) and an updated
Chapter 2.

The proposed amendments were reviewed and discussed. Commissioner Lloyd was pleased that
they were taking a closer look at affordable housing. Chair Ricks clarified that they are adding the
Mixed-Use District, which addresses more flexible and/or affordable housing options.

The edits made to Existing Land Use were reviewed and discussed. It was noted that commercial
uses currently comprise only 3% of the existing land use. Chair Ricks commented that citizens
like the idea that the City of Holladay is not just another suburban area but a small town outside
of Salt Lake City. Commissioner Lloyd referred to it as a cohesive community. Mr. Teerlink
stated that the qualities that smaller towns have included fewer pedestrians and densities that make
them more quaint. Commissioner Chris Layton commented that the term “small town” has a
