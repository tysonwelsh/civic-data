A.   REPORT BY KAREN LANG OF A PROPOSED PROPERTY TAX
     INCREASE BY JORDAN VALLEY WATER CONSERVANCY DISTRICT
     FOR WATER SERVICES PURPOSES

       ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT FROM THE PUBLIC AND/OR THE CITY
       COUNCIL REGARDING THE PROPOSED PROPERTY TAX INCREASE BY
       JORDAN VALLEY WATER CONSERVANCY DISTRICT

       Mayor Lang shared that she serves on the Board of Trustees for the Jordan Valley
       Water Conservancy District and announced that the District is proposing a property
       tax increase for the fiscal year 2025–2026. This increase is driven by a growing
       service area and rising water demands, which necessitate additional water supplies
       and infrastructure investments. The planned increase will help fund major capital
       projects over the next five years, including upgrades and expansions to treatment
       plants, construction of new storage reservoirs, development of a new Southwest
       aqueduct segment, and installation of new wells and groundwater infrastructure.

       The proposed tax hike would result in an average annual increase of approximately
       $11 for homeowners and $22 for businesses. Mayor Lang noted that, under Utah
       Code Section 17B-1-1003, the City Council must allow for public input on this
       matter within 40 days of receiving the request.

       Mayor Lang asked if any members of the Public or Council had any questions or
       comments. There were none.

       Mayor Lang noted that a public hearing will also be held by the Jordan Valley
       Water Conservancy District. The hearing is scheduled for August 11 at 6:00 PM
       and will take place at the District’s office, located at 8215 South 1300 West in West
       Jordan. This hearing will provide an opportunity for residents to share feedback on
       the proposed property tax increase and related infrastructure projects.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included

               Surrounding zones include R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential,

               the east, and R-2-8 (Two Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size
               8,000 square feet) and A to the west. Neighboring uses include single family
               homes to the north, east, and south and the Sunnybrook Park Condos and
               Valley Jr. High School to the west. The property includes a home that,
               according to Salt Lake County, was built in 1940, a detached garage, and

       three other outbuildings. The General Plan designation on the property is
       Medium Density Residential (7 to 12 units/acre).

       The applicant is proposing to retain the existing home, demolish the existing
       detached buildings, build a new garage for the existing home, and construct
       a 14-unit, two-story, for-rent townhome development. The proposed density
       of the project including the existing home is 9.8 units/acre. All townhome
       units would face the perimeter of the property, and each unit would include
       a 2-car garage and 3 bedrooms. All garages have driveways that are deep
       enough to park in. The attached concept plan and application materials from
       the applicant provide details on and support for the proposed project.

Mayor Lang opened the Public Hearing.

Karen Thatcher expressed concern about parking, specifically whether there will
be enough parking for visitors without them having to park on the road. She pointed
out that the area includes a bike lane and that several cars are already parking
between Mackay Meadows Place and 3200 West, which makes it difficult to see
when turning out of her street.

Fred Cox shared that, as a former legislator, he still receives feedback from
neighbors. He noted that most of the complaints he's heard over the years involve
single-family homes owned by out-of-state companies that own dozens of
properties. In contrast, he supported the current rental proposal, which involves a
local company and a development agreement that he believes the city has
structured well to ensure long-term quality. He emphasized that the proposed
development could meet the needs of larger families, helping to reduce the
number of people who move away due to inadequate housing sizes. Fred also
mentioned that in his own neighborhood—composed of split-entry, multi-level,
and two-story homes—the proposed townhomes would fit in well with existing
trends. He concluded by stating that this type of housing is something the city
needs to better retain residents.

Ryan Mahoney expressed concern about the current layout design of the proposed
development, specifically noting a lack of parking for visitors. He pointed out that
the parking is concentrated down the center of the property, with only a walkway
around the outer perimeter. He asked the City Council to ensure that the project
complies with all applicable parking and zoning ordinances.

Mike Markham voiced strong opposition to the proposed rezoning and
development of the property in question. He acknowledged that development was
expected eventually but did not anticipate it would involve multi-family dwellings,
MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING JULY 8, 2025

especially since the area is surrounded on all sides by single-family homes. He
expressed concern about the impact of two-story units on neighboring privacy,
especially balconies overlooking adjacent backyards. He also questioned the
consistency and transparency of the communication around the project, noting that
early materials suggested the homes were designed for multiple renters, while later
communications attempted to reframe the project more positively. Mike argued that
the city already has ample rental units and cited multiple examples across West
Valley where rental housing exists or is expanding. Additional concerns included
the lack of firewalls in rentals compared to owner-occupied homes and
discrepancies in project documentation—such as references to Parkway Boulevard,
which he noted is far from the project site. He warned that rezoning could
drastically increase the land value and limit the city's ability to control future
development outcomes.

Orlin Thatcher expressed concerns primarily about parking and privacy related to
the proposed development. He shared a comment from his son noting that their
street, labeled as a "Place" (Pl), now feels more like a "parking lot" due to
overcrowding. He emphasized that parking has already become a problem in the
area. He also strongly echoed Mike Markham’s concern about the impact of two-
story buildings on nearby homes, particularly regarding privacy. As the homeowner
with the largest exposure to the proposed property, Orlin urged the city to require
privacy accommodations—such as fencing—as part of the development approval.
He suggested that either the building height be reduced or privacy fencing be
mandated to preserve the livability of adjacent properties, acknowledging that
while development is inevitable, it must be done with consideration for surrounding
residents.

Mayor Lang closed the Public Hearing.

RESOLUTION 25-92: APPROVE A FEDERAL AID AGREEMENT BETWEEN
THE CITY AND THE UTAH DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION FOR
IMPROVEMENTS TO 3900 SOUTH
Mayor Lang presented proposed resolution 25-92 that would approve a Federal Aid
Agreement Between the City and the Utah Department of Transportation for Improvements to
3900 South.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       West Valley City and Taylorsville have a roadway improvement project planned
       on 3900 South between Redwood Road and the Jordan River. The scope of the
       project includes minor roadway widening, drainage improvements, installation of
       curb & gutter where needed, rotomilling/overlaying the existing pavement where
       possible, reconstructing the road where necessary, and adding 12’ wide shared use
             MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING JULY 8, 2025
                                  -9-

              paths along the corridor to connect to an adjacent Utah Trail Network project to the
              east.

              The current estimate for this project is $12,100,000. Due to the regional benefit of
              this project, specifically for active transportation, the County contributed
              $3,000,000 of County Transportation funds towards the design and construction of
              this project. In addition, the City successfully applied for federal Transportation
              Alternative Program (TAP) funds and Surface Transportation Program (STP)
              Funds and will receive $1,129,817 (TAP) and $2,000,000 (STP) respectively from
              those programs per this FAA. In addition, WVC recently learned that WFRC is
              recommending an additional $2,500,000 in STP funds for the project and UDOT
              has recommended $2,800,000 from the Highway Safety Improvement Program
              (HSIP) which will be addressed in a future FAA modification. The $3,129,817 of
              federal funds identified in this FAA require a local government match of 6.77% or
              $227,275 which will be paid for utilizing the County Transportation Funds.

              Both Taylorsville and West Valley City will continue to look for additional
              opportunities to get this project completely funded. At this point, construction is
              tentatively scheduled for 2027.

       Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang there were no further questions from members of the City
