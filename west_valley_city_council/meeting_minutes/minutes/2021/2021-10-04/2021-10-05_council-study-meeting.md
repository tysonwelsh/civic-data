RESOLUTION 21-176: APPROVE A MEMORANDUM OF AGREEMENT FOR
THE MIDVALLEY BUS RAPID TRANSIT CONNECTOR PROJECT
Steve Pastorik, CED Director, presented proposed Resolution 21-176 that would approve
a Memorandum of Agreement for the Midvalley Bus Rapid Transit Connector Project.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       This resolution authorizes a MOA between the City, Taylorsville, Murray, Wasatch
       Front Regional Council, UDOT, Salt Lake Community College and UTA to
       establish roles and responsibilities for the Midvalley Connector bus rapid transit
       (BRT) project.

       UTA has been working with the aforementioned entities on the Midvalley
       Connector BRT project which will begin in Murray, travel through Taylorsville,
       and end in West Valley at the Fairbourne Station bus hub. This MOA establishes
       roles and responsibilities for this BRT project. The City’s involvement includes
       property for the expanded bus hub and improvements to Lancer Way.


Councilmember Buhler inquired if we are committing to reconstruction of Lancer Way or
a bigger bus hub. Steve Pastorik stated that we are not committing to making
improvements but providing the space. Councilmember Buhler verified that the space
would be where the America First Credit Union use to be located. Wayne Pyle clarified
that it is the north end of where America First Credit Union used to be located and the south
end of city hall parking lot which is currently mostly landscaping. Councilmember Buhler
verified the property is all city owned property and not currently going any further west.
Steve Pastorik answered eventually it will go further west where there is no curb gutter,
etc. affected. Councilman Christensen verified if it wouldn’t affect current BRT? Steve
Pastorik confirmed that it will not. Councilmember Lang inquired if it would connect at
all. Steve Pastorik answered not as this time. Mayor Bigelow inquired if the bus stop at
2700 West bus stop would require modifications. Steve Pastorik answered that it would
not.


The City Council will consider Resolution 21-176 at the Regular Council Meeting
scheduled October 12, 2021 at 6:30 P.M.

B.     CHARTER SCHOOL SAFETY CONCERNS
       Dan Johnson, Public Works Director, stated a couple of weeks ago some concerns
       about safety at charter schools specifically around traffic backing up at drop off and
       pick up times were expressed. The question was raised how the city provides safety
       for these schools. Dan Johnson stated that the challenge that charter schools give
       us is that there is no busing therefore it causes traffic to build up on streets. He
       mentioned that he observed the drop off and pick up times at Monticello for a
       couple of days. He watched parents show up very early to pick up kids. The
       vehicles stopping in the travel lane provides concern. Dan met with the school’s
       Director to discuss solutions. He did note that once the parking lot is opened the
       problem disappears. They are going to try opening up the parking lot earlier. Also
       communicate with the parents whether that be a staggered pick-up time or other
       solution. Dan will continue to work with the school’s Director to come up with the
       best possible resolution.

       Councilmember Lang mentioned that she personally experienced people illegally
       crossing the street in front of her. Dan Johnson mentioned that at Esperanza there
       is plenty of room to do a double stack due to having a lot more space than
       Monticello. Councilmember Buhler inquired where do we go from here. Dan
       Johnson replied that he would be following up with the schools and report back.
       Councilman Christensen stated that he has seen this issue within the Granite School
       District as well.

       The Mayor and Council had no further questions or concerns.
