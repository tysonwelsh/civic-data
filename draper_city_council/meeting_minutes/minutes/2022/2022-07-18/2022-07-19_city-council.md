3.0   Presentation: Proposed Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District Property
      Tax Increase.

3.1   Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District (JVWCD) Trustee Reed Gibby
      expressed appreciation to the Mayor, Council, and City staff for Draper Days
      and Draper Nights events. He said he had been impressed with JVWCD over
      the years for planning and infrastructure.



                                                                     Page 2 of 10
3.2   JVWCD Assistant General Manager Alan Packard explained that a growing
      service area and increasing water demands required additional water supplies
      and infrastructure, costing millions of dollars. He showed a list of planned
      projects and explained the plan to bond for the projects and make bond
      payments with revenue from existing and future customers. The proposed
      7.8% increase in property tax revenue would result in an $8.86 per year
      property tax increase for a $700,000 home. A Truth-in-Taxation hearing was
      scheduled for 6:00 p.m. on August 10, 2022, at 8215 South 1300 West in West
      Jordan City.

      Mr. Packard said he had been impressed with the public response to requests
      to cut back on water use, and said reservoirs were in better shape than they
      otherwise would have been. He encouraged continued careful use of water
      through the balance of the summer.

6.0   Public Hearing: Ordinance #1552, an Ordinance approving the Development
      Agreements for Bangerter Crossing II Office Site Plan and Fairfield Inn & Suite
      Site Plan on 2.9 acres of property located at approximately 13673 South 100
      East and 13678 South 100 East within Draper City.

6.1   Planning Manager Jennifer Jastremsky explained the applicant received site
      plan approvals for the subject properties in 2020, which had since expired. The
      original proposal for the properties would not comply with more recent
      changes to City Code. Ms. Jastremsky said the proposed Development
      Agreement would reinstate the previously approved Site Plans, including
      deviations, without going back through a site plan application and public
      hearing process before proceeding to Land Disturbance Permit and Building
      Permit. The expiration date for the Site Plan approval would be set for July
      2028.

      Ms. Jastremsky showed site plans for the planned Fairfield Inn & Suites and
      showed photographs of the properties. The Planning Commission reviewed
      and forwarded a unanimous positive recommendation.

6.2   Elizabeth Cole, representing the applicant, said the applicant was eager to
      move forward with the hotel project and added the office building would
      hopefully not be far behind.

7.0   Public Hearing: Ordinance #1550 and #1551, an Ordinance amending the
      official Land Use Map of Draper City for approximately 22.37 acres of property
      from Town Center and Residential Low/Medium Density to Residential
      Medium/High Density and an Ordinance amending the official Zoning Map of
      Draper City for approximately 22.37 acres of property from TC (Town Center),
      RA1 (Residential Agricultural, 40,000 square foot lot minimum), and RA2
      (Residential Agricultural, 20,000 square foot lot minimum) zones to R5 (Single
      Family Residential, 8,000 square foot lot minimum) zone and approving a
      Development Agreement, located at approximately 1060 East Pioneer Road
      within Draper City, otherwise known as the Fitzgerald Property Land Use Map
      Amendment, Zoning Map Amendment and Development Agreement.

7.1   Planning Manager Jennifer Jastremsky explained the proposal to rezone
      approximately 22 acres of farmland to residential medium-high density, which
      would allow 4-8 dwelling units per acre. The proposed Development
      Agreement would: limit the project to 62 lots total – 59 lots at 9,000 square
      feet, and 3 lots at 20,000 square feet. The existing Code at the time of
      Development Agreement approval would apply. The property owner would be
      allowed to create a 5-acre parcel without going through a parcel boundary
      adjustment process. Language would be removed that waived claims to
      damages if there were a breach of the agreement.

      Ms. Jastremsky showed a Concept Plan, 3D renderings, and photographs of
      neighborhoods in Draper with similar densities. The Planning Commission

                                                                       Page 5 of 10
      forwarded a positive recommendation for all three applications, with a
      recommended modification to the Development Agreement that the City
      complete the access road across the 5-acre property the City would be
      developing, and that the City complete that road when the subject
      development was done, not when the 5-acres were developed, to provide
      access for the subject development.

7.2   City Manager David Dobbins said one reason for looking at the development
      of the subject property and the 5-acre City-owned property at that time was to
      preempt the need to participate in State’s Station Area Plan requirements.

7.3   Councilmember Green expressed concern that a completed road through the
      5 acres may be in the way if the City decided to pursue some type of global
      development. Councilmember F. Lowry said he agreed, and said it had not
      been his understanding that the City planned to connect the road directly to
      Pioneer Road. It was stated that completing the road through the City-owned
      property was discussed by the Planning Commission, but was not part of the
      current applications under consideration. It was pointed out that the City also
      owned roughly four acres west of the 5-acre property discussed.

7.4   Mayor Walker opened a public hearing.

7.5   Penny Laker, Draper resident, said her neighborhood backed up to the trail on
      the east side of the proposed development. She said her biggest concern was
      that a majority of traffic from the proposed development would use Mansfield
      Way as an access if the City did not complete the access to Pioneer Road. She
      said her neighborhood already had concerns about child safety with current
      traffic speeds on Mansfield Way, without adding additional traffic.

7.6   Mayor Walker closed the public hearing.

7.7   Mayor Walker explained that Station Area Plans included property within a half
      mile of any transit station. The original Station Area Plan process was intended
      to drive high-density housing around transit stations. He said the proposed
      development plan offered the City the opportunity, by acting now, to have
      more control over the City Center. He expressed appreciation to Ivory Homes
      for being willing to sell five acres to the City.

10.0   Action Item: Resolution #22-32, a Resolution of the Draper City Council
       authorizing the filing of an eminent domain action to acquire real property for
       the purpose of widening and improving 300 East Street.

10.1   Engineering/Public Works Director Scott Cooley explained the objective to
       widen the north and south lanes of the 300 East/12300 South intersection to
       add dual left-turn lanes. The project would encroach 3-4 feet onto the Holiday

                                                                         Page 8 of 10
       Oil property. An appraisal was completed to determine just compensation, and
       staff had tried to negotiate in good faith. To date, Holiday Oil had not accepted
       or countered the City’s offer. Mr. Cooley requested the City Council allow staff
       to file an eminent domain action, and said all State Code requirements for use
       of eminent domain had been met. The project had been planned for
       completion in 2022 but was postponed with this matter unresolved.

10.2   Councilmember F. Lowry asked how the City’s acquisition of the property
       would impact the property owner, and asked if any modifications could be
       made to benefit the property owner. Mr. Cooley said the property owner had
       expressed a desire to reconstruct the site. He said it was his understanding the
       property owner had warmed to the situation since a recent Planning
       Commission approved the property owner’s request to deviate from City
       standards. City Attorney Mike Barker said the property owner presented a
       rough concept for the property that would add a canopy to existing pumps,
       and expand and shift the convenience store to the north.

10.3   Councilmember T. Lowery moved to approve                   Resolution    #22-32.
       Councilmember Roberts seconded the motion.

11.0   Action Item: Ordinance #1546, an Ordinance of the Draper City Council
       amending Title 14 of the Draper City Municipal Code regarding speed limits
       and restrictions on use of roads.

11.1   Engineering/Public Works Director Scott Cooley explained proposed
       amendments to Title 14 of the Draper City Municipal Code to establish a 25-
       mile-per-hour speed limit on Traverse Ridge Road between Mike Weir Drive
       and Highland Drive for restricted vehicles. Restricted vehicles would not be
       permitted on Rambling Road, and trucks with three axles or more would be
       prohibited from traveling westbound (downhill) on Traverse Ridge Road

                                                                          Page 9 of 10
       between SunCrest Drive and Mike Weir Drive. Mr. Cooley explained the
       proposed exemptions and said he believed the proposed amendment would
       be easy to understand and easy to enforce. Mr. Cooley answered questions
       from the Mayor and Council.

11.2   Responding to a question from Councilmember Roberts, Chief John Eining
       explained enforcement plans. Staff explained that the Traverse Ridge Road
       side of the mountain had extended lengths of 10% grade. The slope on the
       SunCrest Drive side of the mountain was less steep.

11.3   Councilmember T. Lowery moved to approve                Ordinance   #1546.
       Councilmember Green seconded the motion.
