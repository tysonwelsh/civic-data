         2.1    Presentation and Discussion Regarding the Salt Lake Countv Animal Services
                2022 Annual Report for the Citv of Bluffdale. (Presenter, Carrie Siebert.

Salt Lake County Animal Services Community Liaison Coordinator, Carrie Siebert, began the
yearly presentation by proudly announcing that this is Salt Lake County Animal Services' 10^ year
as a no-kill organization. They will be highlighting the milestone at the upcoming Love Where
You Live event and celebrating throughout the year. They will be giving out 10 awards to their
top supporters that have helped them achieve and maintain that status since 2013, with categories
such as top volunteer, top fosterer, top donor, and top rescuer.

Ms. Siebert offered a brief overview of the services the shelter offers. Some of their services
include items other shelters are unable to offer such as a fully staffed veterinary clinic and robust

emergency operations. They are able to handle all sorts of animals that come into their care. She
shared interesting facts and highlights. The shelter has served over 21,000 poimds of food and
treats to pets. Fosterers spent over 93,000 hours caring for animals in their homes. They offered
humane education presentations to over 5,000 children and adults on subjects including bite
prevention, responsible pet ownership, and what to do when you encounter a stray. Volunteers
spent over 2,500 hours providing care and activities to pets in the shelter. 3,300 pets were sterilized
by their in-house clinic.

Ms. Siebert described some of the shelter's accomplishments through 2022. In honor of Betty
White's passing, a nationwide fundraising event known as Betty White Day was held. Donors
across the nation offered money, supplies, and food to their local animal welfare agencies. Salt
Lake County Animal Services was able to use those funds to remodel their cattery and cat rooms.
The fundraising effort was ongoing.

Salt Lake County Animal Services received a National Association of Counties Award for their
Hounds Around Town volunteer program. This is a nationwide award competing against volunteer
programs for any kind of agency, such as library programs or parks and recreation, and this
program was number one in the nation. This was the second time the recipient ofthis award has
been in Utah. The program trains volunteers and provides them the opportunity to take the dogs
out on trails so they can leave the shelter and get some exercise while providing advertising to
educate anyone who might be interested in adopting them. They also won Best in State for the
14^^^ time in 2022 and again in 2023 making it the 15^ time.

Ms. Siebert shared a summary of the overall Asilomar stats, adjusted for factors beyond their
control such as animals deceased on arrival or owner-requested euthanasia. The full stats are
available upon request. They had an intake ofjust over 6,200 animals with an outcome total of
just over 6,100, yielding a 93% live release rate. 959 animals were sent to rescues and 988 were
sent to foster homes. In Bluffdale specifically, they brought in just over 189 animals of all sorts
of species. Last year, a crab that was running at large in the City of Holladay was brought in. A
blue crab was in a school parking lot. The stats count kittens and puppies separately from cats and
dogs to help determine the programs to focus on such as spaying and neutering.

Most animals in Bluffdale were brought in by Accoimtable Care Organization("ACO")impound,
meaning officers went out and picked them up or brought them in over the counter. They received
412 calls for service. The most common call type was Animal Impound. Animal At Large is
usually a larger number as well as their Citizen Inquiry and Trap-Neuter-Retum("TNR")efforts.
Breaking down calls by priority she expleiined the priority levels. Priority 1 is their hi^est risk
and top priority. They are handled first as they pose the greatest risk to the safety and welfare of
the animal and the public. These include animal attacks or bites in progress, injured animals, or
an agency assist for fire or police departments who may call them in for assistance. Lower-risk
calls are handled accordingly.

Some calls vary in priority depending on circumstances. For example, for an Animal At Large
call, a pack of large dogs in a busy intersection will be a higher priority than a little poodle just
bebopping down the sidewalk. A dog killing another animal would be a higher priority, as the
other animal is at risk, and there is a higher risk ofthe dog redirecting and biting a human.

The United States Department of Agriculture's ("USDA") Animal and Plant Health Inspection
Service("APHIS")generated statistics for the Bluffdale Urban Wildlife Program, which is under
their management. They handled written or phone consultations with the public about 14 times.
It was reported that they trapped and removed 24 raccoons and 29 skunks.

Four officers who went above and beyond last year were honored for their service. Officer
Kathleen Kayo, Sergeant Jennifer Nielson, Officer Don Belnap, and Officer Gerri Cantor were
acknowledged for receiving Officer ofthe Year Awards.

Ms. Siefert reported that two regional projects were currently underway. The first is a regional
animal care campus. A goal for this is to have a regional adoption center/education/event venue,
everything encompassing domestic animal ownership. There is not really anything like this in the
nation. The Coxmty has funded a study using transformational funds. If they move forward with
it, it will feature a library, an interactive education center for children where they can run on a life-
size hamster wheel or climb a cat tree, watch bottle babies being fed, or learn about animal care
jobs, as well as a huge adoption center that will be available for all the agencies in the County.

For the other regional project, they received a mobile unit fi*om the Health Department that was
used during COVID. They are using transformational funds fi*om the Council to retrofit it into a
mobile spay and neuter clinic and adoption unit. The goal of the program is to operate for 18
months during which time they will take it to every city and go to low-income areas. They will
work directly with each city, including Bluffdale, in areas that may need spay and neuter services
the most. They will park in the nei^borhoods, get the animals fixed, and get them right back
home. The goal is to do as many as possible. During COVID,a lot of surgeries were shut down
due to Personal Protective Equipment("PPE")requirements. As a result, they are trying to catch
up on spay and neuters.

The monthly Pet Food Pantry Event began during COVID as many families were struggling
financially and struggling to feed their pets. It was doing well and every month they receive food
and supplies through donations and grants. The service is available to anyone in the community.
They can come to the event and get what they need. Those with specific needs can also be helped
using donations or grant monies.

Ms. Siefert reported that pet licensing is required by law. It allows for rabies vaccinations to be
tracked and is the only way to legally prove ownership. Salt Lake County Animal Services is
always trying to increase its licensing compliance. This last year, they implemented a new type of
license tag. It features a Quick Response("QR") Code and works through Pawfinder.com. Pet

Salt Lake County Animal Services offers free virtual training workshops to the public. It is another
resource for citizens to use when encoimtering a simple behavior issue at home. Training can help
resolve most issues and allow pets to remain in the home. Every month virtual workshops are
offered each month on a variety of topics. The most popular are Building Better Behaviors and
Separation Anxiety. They also feature workshops on Responsible Pet Ownership, Dog

Council Member Crockett had noticed comments on Facebook where people have had issues with
a dog being off-leash. Some are fearful ofdogs and aren't sure what to do. Ms. Siebert stated that
Humane Education is typically taught through the schools but they will make presentations to

Coxmcil Member Crockett asked what age people have to be to volunteer for their Hounds Around
Town program. Ms. Siebert stated that individuals need to be 18 or older. Those under 18 need

Council Member Aston asked why the Council was not given any response times. Ms. Siebert
explained that response times are generated through the Valley Emergency Communications
Center("VECC"). They have updated their procedures and all calls are now coming in as one call
type and one priority. Council Member Aston asked how long it takes Animal Control to get to
Bluffdale. Ms. Siebert stated that they respond to calls throughout the valley so response times
vary. Response times also depend on the priority. The goal for Priority 1 calls is under 30 minutes.
Response times are greater for lower-priority calls.

Council Member Kallas asked how the no-kill classification pertains to situations such as an animal
being brought in with rabies. In the case of confirmed rabies, the animal would be euthanized.
Specific decisions are made in cases of severe medical or behavioral issues.

Ms. Siebert reported that they serve the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, which

Bluffdale, Millcreek, Murray, and the City ofHolladay. Many otherjurisdictions either have their

       2.2     Budget Discussion Relating to Various Budget and Finance Topics. (Staff
               Presenters^ Stephanie Thaver and Bruce Kartchner),

Administrative Services Director, Bruce Kartchner, thanked the Council for passing the Tentative
Budget presented at the last meeting.

Finance Manager, Stephanie Thayer, presented the changes made since the last meeting. Revised
revenue estimates were added to the first section. With regard to expenditures, adjustments were
made to the Salaries, Wages,and Benefits as more information is received from providers. Market
adjustments were made to specifically targeted positions. Ms. Thayer reported that changes were
also made to the Arts Council budget as a result ofinformation received fi*om Arts Council Chair
Laura Gamer.


Ms. Thayer reported on a significant change in Capital Projects, which included the $230,000
purchase of a plow tmck that was moved to the Class C Roads Fund. Mr. Kartchner pointed out
the tmck will be replacing the old orange Utah Department of Transportation ("UDOT") tmck
known as the Pumpkin. Stafffelt it was necessary to upgrade to a newer model.

Changes to the Local Building Authority ("LBA") Fund included the payment for the Public
Works Building and ensuring that the lease payments on the City Hall Building were correct.

Mr. Kartchner pointed out that staff has received additional changes since the budget was last
updated that will be brought back at a future meeting. The majority ofthe changes have offsetting
revenues. There will continue to be changes going forward based on continual review and
revisions to the projections on the revenues and expenses as additional data comes in. Staff also
wants to make sure that the City Council is providing additional information they would like to be
incorporated into the budget.

For budgetary purposes, a new fund has been created to be called the Public Safety Services Fund.
All Fire and Police costs have been moved into that fund. It is a proprietary fund meaning that any
revenue that is put into the fund can only be spent in that fund. They are currently budgeting a
property tax increase in that fund ofaround $498,400 as ofthis revision. It includes two new full-
time firefighter positions per shift, which equates to two people per shift for a total of six new
firefighters. They have projected that they will need to do a market adjustment for the pay raises.
A couple of positions have also been identified that will need to be given promotions, which were
incorporated as well.

Council Member Aston emphasized the importance offull-time combat fire employees, as they do
not currently have any. They have closed one fire station and are having trouble staffing the other.
This is extremely important to maintaining Fire/EMS service.

City Manager, Mark Reid pointed out that during the COVID crisis, a lot of part-time firefighters
who were working full-time elsewhere were called back to their full-time station to work overtime
shifts. He explained that if a firefighter was exposed to COVID it took out his entire shift. All
firefighters working with that individual would be quarantined. A lot of firefighters were pulled
back to work overtime shifts, which created difficulty for Bluffdale since they had no full-time
firefighters of their own. This was the direction the City has been trying to go to get full-time
firefighters who are dedicated to the City ofBluffdale. This was a significant step in terms of cost
and toward the safety of citizens.

Council Member Hales asked if people will want to take those positions and work full-time.
Mr. Reid explained thatjust like with any other position, it will be posted and applicants will apply
and be interviewed. Police Chief, Matt Evans, stated that the positions will first be opened in-
house. Some local firefighters would be willing to leave their departments to join Bluffdale. If
they cannot find six employees, they will search outside the department.

Mr. Reid stated that this helps in an emergency to have firefighters who live close to the station.
Chief Evans added that some of the firefighters have family in Bluffdale and will be more vested
in the City.

Council Member Aston asked for clarification on the shifts offirefighters. ChiefEvans stated that
there are A,B, and C shifts in the department. Two firefighters will be hired for each shift. They
will be at the station for 48 hours. There are three shifts of firefighters that each work 48-hour
shifts. There will be two full-time firefighters at the station at all times.

Mr. Reid pointed out that in the past there were 48 part-time shifts available in the six-day
workweek. When they went firom eight down to six firefighters, they eliminated 12 shifts. Having
two full-time firefighters per shift will eliminate another 12 part-time positions. There will be 24
part-time positions per week available for this group of over 80 part-time firefighters. The hope
was to be able to fill the 24 shifts. Chief Evans stated there should be plenty of competition for
the shifts as they will be reducing fi'om six to four part-time firefighters per day.

Mayor Hall confirmed that the final public hearing and vote on the Budget will take place on
June 14 as the deadline is June 22. Mr. Kartchner stated that it has been noticed. If the property
tax increase is included as part ofthe final budget, there will be the Truth in Taxation Hearing in
August. It is coordinated with the County.

Mr. Kartchner stated that the Public Safety Services Fund will need to be set up prior to July. Staff
was targeting the next City Council Business Meeting in May. If they are not able to get it done
by then it may be postponed to a June meeting. It will be effective July 1.

Mr. Kartchner clarified that the fund will cover all Fire and Police Department expenses,however,
the bulk ofthe money going into the fund will come fi*om the General Fund. Once the funds are

        7.1    Resolution No. 2023-21 - Selecting the CM/GC for Railroad Crossing and
               Authorizing the Citv Manager to Execute Documents. (Staff Presenter,
               Michael FazioX

City Engineer, Michael Fazio reported that there had been an intense process to select a Contractor
for the Railroad Crossing Project. The Resolution before the Council would authorize the City
Manager to conduct negotiations with a Construction Manager and General Contractor
("CM/GC"). The contractor selected during the process was Ralph L. Wadsworth Construction,
with the participation of Stacy & Whitbeck, Inc. Mr. Fazio explained that a box culvert would be
used to cross the railroad tracks in collaboration with Union Pacific Railroad.

Mr. Fazio stated that the box culvert will be placed under the railroad tracks. A lot of work was
taking place to complete the design. As for the contracting method of CM/GC,he explained that
a CM/GC allows the contractor to be selected sooner. The contractor would be on board before
the design process is complete. This means they could contribute to the design and bring their own
ideas and innovation to the project. He felt this was the best option.

It had been more than two months since the Request for Proposals("RFP") was done. There had
been several meetings with contractors to explain the project. Six proposals had been submitted
but one was withdrawn due to health reasons. The five proposals were reviewed by two separate
committees. The proposals included five different tasks, which included the contractor providing
details about minimizing project costs and risks, explaining how they would coordinate the work,
discussing utility coordination, providing information about how they would complete the project
within the desired timefi*ame, and how they would complete the project in budget. There were
also five criteria, which included team capability, project approach, and suggested innovations.
Mr. Fazio explained that in terms of scoring, team capability, and project approach had more
weight than cost proposals. The focus was on how the project would be built.

In terms ofthe selection process, there were two teams. Mr. Fazio reported that he was part ofthe
Technical Team review. Each team member on the Technical Team separately reviewed the
proposals and then there was a meeting to discuss them. At the end of the review process, all
members had essentially the same votes for each of the proposals. The second team was the
Selection Team, which included Mayor Hall, the City Manager, Finance Director, and Public
Works Director. Mr. Fazio shared the scores for the different contractors. One contractor, which
was Ralph L. Wadsworth Construction, stood out and scored the highest. That contractor was
approximately 20 points above any other submitted contractor. The strength ofthe application had
to do with a joint venture with a rail contractor, Stacy, and Whitbeck, Inc. Their experience with
CM/GC and complex transportation projects also made the application stand out. Most of the
bridges on Bangerter Highway were built by Ralph L. Wadsworth Construction who is very
experienced. As for weaknesses,those related to availability with the FrontRunner Project because
they also submitted a proposal with FrontRunner to build the new line. That being said, the
company had the best goals and identified the best mitigation strategies and project phasing details
out of all ofthe submissions. They had a very strong proposal overall.

Based on the team reviews, it was recommended that the City Council approve the Resolution to
allow the City Manager to complete negotiations with Ralph L. Wadsworth Construction.
Mr. Reid shared additional information about the different committees. The Technical Team
looked at all ofthe information and the Selection Team verified that the team had done so in a fair
maimer. It was fairly easy to choose the contractor based on the scoring system. Mr. Reid noted
that this was an incredible project. This is a new process in the United States and will become the
new method of building roads underneath bridges in the future. There would likely be a lot of
national attention. Council Member Kallas noted that CM/GC is a fairly common construction
method and is a great solution to more complicated processes. He thanked everyone for their work
on the selection.
