1. APPROVE (OR DENY) AN ORDINANCE TO AMEND THE OFFICIAL UTAH COUNTY ZONE MAP
FROM THE MINING AND GRAZING (M&G-1) ZONE TO THE GRAZING (G-1) ZONE FOR PROPERTY
LOCATED IN SECTION 33, T5S RIW, SECTIONS 3, 4, 9, 10, 11, 14, 15, 16, 21, 22, 23, 25, 26, 27, 33, 34, 35,
AND 36, T6S RIW, SECTIONS 1, 2, 3, AND 4, T7S R1W, SECTIONS 29 AND 32, T6S RIE, AND SECTIONS
5 AND 6, T7S RIE, LAKE MOUNTAIN/SARATOGA SPRINGS AREA OF UTAH COUNTY (CONTINUED
FROM THE OCTOBER 16, 2018 COMMISSION MEETING)

Bryce Armstrong, Community Development explained the history of this item where Saratoga Springs City requested 16,000+
acres be rezoned in Lake Mountain area from mining and to the grazing zone (G-1). He said the intent of the rezoning was to
prevent future mining operations in certain areas but did not include other areas that currently has existing mining operations.
He explained the Planning Commission recommended denying this agenda item.

David Stroud, Planning Director for the City of Saratoga Springs, described the concerns the City has concerning impacts of
the mining on the City’s infrastructure, roads, future development of the area and quality of life of the residents. He described
how this area is in their annexation declaration overlay. He described how the owners of some of the areas are in annexation
talks with the City. He commented the owners of the gravel pits have the right to develop their properties as they wish. He
and Commissioner Graves discussed owner’s rights.

Todd Cusick, representative of CMC, presented documents in addition to the Waldo letter already presented. He discussed
how a recent Deseret News article showed large dust particles do not get into the lungs and therefore are not a health hazard,
He disputed Mr. Stroud’s allegations. He described how emissions are a bigger hazard. He said if they are hauled further
distances, emissions increase, He continued discussing how 3 parcels that are “vested mining rights” under State of Utah laws.
He said they should be omitted if anything was done on this proposal. Discussion continued how the product is sold in the
area and what is causing the air hazards in the county.

Dustin Hugely spoke about how eliminating gravel pits near Saratoga Springs will not eliminate traffic but will make it worse.
He said he purchased the property in order to sell the aggregate to Saratoga Springs. He said only people effected by the
rezoning of the property are private property owners because the State and Federal Government will still be able to mine. He
said he understood the City’s concerns.

The commissioners discussed how the motion could be stated that would give property owners time to protest the rezoning of
the land and then come back with a request that would have more consensus of the owners.

Bryce explained if this was denied, the applicant would have to start the process over. He explained the process and applicable
fees.

motion was seconded by Commissioner Ivie and carried with the following vote:
