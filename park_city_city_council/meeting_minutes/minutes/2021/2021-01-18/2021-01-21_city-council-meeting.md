WORK SESSION

Park City Arts and Culture District – Follow Up Discussion:
Mayor Beerman stated this discussion would focus on transportation as it related to the
Arts and Culture District, and the next meeting would focus on housing. He also
indicated the Council retreat would be moved to March or April.

David Everitt, Deputy City Manager, stated staff worked on transit for this site and
designated it as a Transit node although he knew Council thought of the site as a
Transit hub. He indicated the district would not be a stop for every bus line, but the
focus of the district would be the destination experience once the passengers arrived.
There were several transit constraints with the parcel. He noted the Fresh Market stop
was a better location for a hub outside of the Old Town Transit Center.

Council Member Henney asked how staff determined the site would be a node instead
of a hub. Sarah Pearce, Deputy City Manager, and Jerry Benson, Transit Consultant,
weighed in on the topic. Pearce stated the project had changed a lot in the past two
years as the team studied what would and wouldn’t work in the district. There was also
more knowledge with regard to SR-248, PEG Development construction at the Park City
Mountain Resort (PCMR) base, bus rapid transit (BRT) on SR-224, the Deer Valley
project, potential aerial connections, etc., so staff realized there needed to be a shift in
the design to accommodate these factors. Benson stated he looked at all these
changes and studied how this would be a hub and work with the access constraints. He
determined having this site as a hub would slow down traffic and transit. He worked with
staff and they determined Transit could provide good service to the site as well as to the
resorts, and the current plans were best for this site. He felt good about this solution
considering the current and future projects in the area.

Council Member Worel asked if there were national or local projections on how long it
would take for people to be comfortable using public transportation after the pandemic.
Benson stated people were concerned about safety, but once they felt comfortable
gathering in groups they would begin using transit. Council Member Doilney stated
Fresh Market was the most used Transit stop, and he liked the nodal plan for the
district. He stated this plan would meet the City's needs now and could be adjusted to
meet its needs in the future.

Council Member Henney asked about ridership trends. Pearce indicated ridership
increased as employees were hired for the resorts this winter. Total ridership was down
50% from last winter, but the routes were reduced as a result of budget cuts, which
somewhat affected that figure. Council Member Henney favored the less costly and
more efficient plan. Council Member Worel asked if a new traffic study would be needed
for the updated plan. Everitt didn't think the bus count would be very different. Benson
stated the bus movements were already captured in the original study.

Council Member Joyce thought the new design was right, but he didn't think anything
was being done to make a fundamental change in traffic. He wondered if Transit should
not be promoted as part of the Arts and Culture District. Everitt stated Transit would be
an integral part of the district. Council Member Joyce stated Transit at the district was
originally promoted as a hub and as a way to make progress in getting people out of
cars. Now, it would not have the proportional impact on traffic. Everitt estimated the
mixed use aspect of the district would promote a 7% drop in traffic. Mayor Beerman
stated this plan was not what Council hoped it would be, but it was car optional and
would meet the City's ideals. The plan would enhance the Transit system without the
major infrastructure that was initially proposed. Council Member Joyce stated the
community's top priority was traffic, and he hoped this district would reflect that priority.
The current design did not tackle the number one priority. Everitt stated that in the end,
this was an arts and culture district first.

Council Member Doilney stated this was not the place to fix everything, but he liked the

Transit system. Council Member Joyce indicated that the project would drain most of

Council Member Doilney was comfortable with the current Transit plan. Council Member
Worel stated the district was intended to be a very vibrant community center, and asked
for a future discussion regarding the capacity that the proposed Transit plan would be
able to handle considering the projected vibrancy. Pearce stated Transit would handle
the district’s occupancy levels and it would have flexibility that the underground proposal
lacked. The Long Range and Short Range Transportation Plans would complement
Transit at the district and would look at the bigger transportation goals.

feedback. One goal was to align with Summit County's Outdoor Lighting ordinance.

Ward stated there would not be amendments to recreation lighting since restrictions
were already in place. There was some concern from the public on lighting at the

Public safety lighting amendments were made to exempt parking area lighting and

the ordinance must conform, including site improvements, reconstruction, expansion,
alteration, or modification of existing structures. Damaged nonconforming outdoor
lighting must be replaced or repaired with compliant lighting. The Planning Commission
proposed that for existing lighting, owners would be required to only replace light bulbs
by December 31, 2024.

The purpose of the proposed code, while incorporating principles from IDA, is not
to become a Dark Sky Community – although it opens the door for future
designation if the community is interested.

Rather, the code updates the 1998 code to incorporate LEDs and current best
practices.

The General Plan recommends a new night sky ordinance and in 2020, the
Planning Commission prioritized this topic.
