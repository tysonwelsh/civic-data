Council Member Crockett asked about the construction costs and noted that the prices of materials
seemed to be lower than before. Mr. Paddock explained that the cost would depend on the bids.
Based on the feedback he had received from contractors so far their projects were lined out for the
next two years. However, the hope was that the bids would be somewhat lower than previously
anticipated. Lumber prices were starting to come down a bit but gas prices were higher, which would
have an impact on the overall construction costs and on the bids received. Administrative Services
Director, Bruce Kartchner noted that residential and commercial construction have different timelines
in terms of supplies. He also stated that the price of concrete for foundations has come down, but
there were still supply chain issues related to steel.

City Manager, Mark Reid pointed out that one of the largest costs would be the financing cost. He
shared a chart with the Council and stated that based on the graph, no money was being saved. Mr.
Kartchner explained that it could potentially change on the front end, but the payments would likely
not change significantly. The interest rates were low at a certain point during the COVID-19
pandemic. The City had been fortunate enough to do its last water project when the rates were lower.
Starting at the beginning of 2022, inflation increased, and interest rates followed.

For this particular project, the City was pursuing a Revenue Bond. That rate was slightly higher than
a General Obligation bond. Mr. Reid explained that a General Obligation Bond was taken to the
citizens to vote on while a Revenue Bond is tied to a specific revenue stream. He noted that it was
risky to ask for a General Obligation Bond for a necessary project because it goes out for a vote.
Many cities had taken their City Hall or Public Works Building out for a General Obligation Bond
where the building would be paid for through an increase in property tax. However, there was a risk
involved in that strategy because residents could vote against a necessary project. That was the reason
the Revenue Bond was selected.

Mr. Kartchner reported that the City would continue to look at the rate. He shared a projection of the
expectations. Revenue Bonds are typically ½ to one point higher than the General Obligation Bond
rates. City Staff would work with advisors and the City Council to determine the best path forward
for the City. Once the bonds are issued, there would be a question about whether the bonds should
be put out at the market rate or if there should be a private placement. There were benefits to both
options. Many felt that the rates were exceptionally high at the moment. As things change over the
next few years there may be an option to retire and replace the bond with a new issue. It might cost
more on the front end but long term it could benefit the City from a financial standpoint. Everything

       2.2     Discussion Relating to Kenneling Text Amendment. (Tracie Kay, Full House Dog
               Training).

City Planner and Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell reported that the proposed item
involved a zone text amendment. Tracie Kay shared a slideshow presentation with the Council titled
“Full House Dog Training.” She wanted to discuss the possibility of expanding the zoning to allow
higher volumes of kenneling. Ms. Kay presented information related to her business, Full House Dog
Training. Over the last six years, the business earned approximately $300,000 in annual revenue.
She employed five other team members. All of that success had occurred while working out of her
third-car garage. It was time for the business to expand and she wanted to find a place that would
allow her to do that. Ms. Kay reported that she is a member of the International Association of Canine
Professionals and is fully licensed and insured.

Prior to her training business, she worked for a local company for 21 years in marketing. For the last
11 years there she did cause marketing and humanitarian relief. Additionally, she started a non-profit
organization. Ms. Kay has a wealth of business experience and wanted to be a community partner.
She reported that there has been a notable shift in the dog ownership culture in recent years. The
majority of dog owners consider their dogs to be family members. Dog owners also tended to provide
the same lifestyle to their animals that they do themselves. Based on a study she conducted, 47% of
households own at least one dog. 62% of households earn over $100,000 per year, which means they
can provide different accommodations and services for their animals. 42% of citizens in the area meet
the target age demographic and 13,607 households already use professional dog services. The
demand is high in the area and there are currently no boarding facilities in operation at the southwest
end of the valley.

Ms. Kay reported that Bluffdale is a wonderful place for the proposed use because it is family-friendly
and an animal culture already exists. Her goal was to provide a premier dog service destination. The
focus will always be on training because that was the core of the business. Training will provide
higher quality interaction for dogs with their families but will also ensure that there are better canine
citizens in the community. Training reduces noise, frustrating behaviors, and creates safety in
neighborhoods. Full House Dog Training will have a strong retail presence as there was a desire to
provide high-end dog food and supplies, dog-related gifts and apparel, and toys. There will also be
an area for a self-service dog wash as well as a daycare, boarding, and grooming. These services will
allow the business to meet the current demand.

Some of the logistics were discussed. Ms. Kay reported that all dogs will be kenneled indoors in
high-quality, easy-to-clean, well-maintained units. The units will be comfortable and provide a great
experience. She shared a sample image with the Council. The dogs will spend the majority of their
days indoors in climate-controlled, well-lit play spaces. Ideally, she wanted there to be some outdoor
space for dogs to enjoy the weather at certain times. It was important to recognize that the dog
attendants are dog trainers who understand how to create structured play. The intention was to keep
noise to a minimum and create a safe environment.

As for sanitation, Full House Dog Training already has a rigorous cleaning process in place. The
same would be true as the business expands. There will also be a specialized ventilation system that
will reduce odor and stagnant air indoors. Ms. Kay discussed waste removal and explained that other
boarding facilities she spoke to recommended a separate dumpster that is not used by other tenants.
The dumpster could be emptied multiple times per week. She informed the Council that the business
currently requires all dogs to be fully vaccinated and that requirement will continue with any new
space the business occupies.

Ms. Kay reported that several surrounding cities allow for high-volume overnight kenneling in
commercial and retail spaces. She shared a list with the Council as well as locations she recently
visited that are located in retail areas. There was a prospective location for the Full House Dog
Training business in the Wright Development on 13856 South 2700 West. Ms. Kay liked the location
because it is close to Bangerter Highway and would allow for easy access. She noted that the traffic
noise would minimize any noise the dog kenneling would contribute. The layout of the commercial
space would also allow there to be a generous distance between other businesses. There was also a
proposed veterinary clinic nearby. She requested to be on one of the ends of the development to
minimize foot traffic and take into consideration the other tenants. Other spaces along 2700 West
were also being considered.

Beyond just being a business in Bluffdale, there was a desire for Full House Dog Training to be part
of the community. Ms. Kay wanted to provide community outreach through dog safety and basic
training programs during community events. She also wanted to host family-friendly dog-related
events and provide potential support to Salt Lake County which services Bluffdale Animal Services.
Shelters are currently overflowing and Full House Dog Training can house some of those dogs
temporarily or assist in finding new families.

Council Member Kallas asked about the prospective property for the business. Ms. Kay clarified that
it had not yet been built. The developers were waiting until there are leases in hand. She almost
signed the lease but wanted to make sure the zoning was in place beforehand. The proposed date for
the development is June 2023. Council Member Crockett noted that the Council was being asked to
do a text amendment. This would not just impact the Full House Dog Training business but others in
the community as well. Ms. Kay believed it would benefit the City to expand the number of dogs that
can be kenneled. Currently, there is a limit of three. That number favored a more residential type of
environment. She was looking to increase that number. With that, there would be several parameters
to put in place. There would likely be restrictions related to space and the distance between the use
and a school or church.

Council Member Kallas supported the proposal within a commercial zone and wanted to see how the
proposal compares to neighboring cities as there will be a desire for Bluffdale to be somewhat similar.
Public safety and nuisance concerns would also need to be addressed before the text amendment is
approved. Council Member Hales asked about the distance from schools and churches and pointed
out that the proposed location is near a school. Ms. Kay explained that the parameter is 500 feet in
other cities. Mr. Crowell was not sure that the distance would be necessary. He noted that “A kennel
is a commercial establishment having three or more dogs or cats for boarding, breeding, buying,
grooming, letting for hire, training for a fee, or selling.” The proposed use would be a kennel and the
table of allowed uses in the Commercial Zone said there were no places for kennels. There is one
place in Gateway Redwood that allows for indoor boarding. He noted that the proposed text
amendment could focus on the GC-1 Zone if a more direct approach was desired.

Council Member Aston wondered why kenneling had not been included previously. Mr. Crowell
explained that the use table went back many years and it was difficult to know why some uses are
allowed and others are not. It may be that no one had asked for kenneling before. Council Member
Aston was supportive of the Full House Dog Training business but wanted to look at the areas zoned
GC-1. Council Member Crockett did not have an issue with the kenneling as long as it is not close to
a residential area. Mr. Crowell pointed out that there could be distance requirements in place if there
were concerns about residential impacts.

Mayor Hall reported that Ms. Kay would be sponsoring the text amendment. She read from 5.30.140
in Regulatory Permits, which specifies that a Class C Kennel needs to be located on a minimum of
five acres of land. Regulations related to kennels were not just located in the business codes. There
may be a lot of work involved to determine all of the areas where there is language related to kennels.
It sounded like there was support for an amendment and the Council felt it made sense for the GC-1
Zone to have kenneling. She asked that the process move forward.

Mr. Reid asked if Ms. Kay intend to breed and sell animals. Ms. Kay did not. She stated that she
breeds animals at her home but would not do so in Bluffdale. Mr. Reid asked for additional
information about assisting Salt Lake County. Ms. Kay explained that she would be open to their
needs. She recognized that at the moment, the animal shelters are full and she wanted to be able to
provide support where possible. She was willing to search the City Code to find places where
kenneling is mentioned. She would also be willing to present ideas from neighboring cities. Mayor
Hall asked that she work with City Staff. She was excited to see the item brought back for
consideration.

7.     Public Hearing Items:

       7.1     Resolution No. 2022-44 – Local Consent for a Full-Service Restaurant Alcohol
               License for ‘Bout Time Pub and Grub, Located at 475 West 14600 South, Tim
               Ryan, Applicant. (Staff Presenter, Grant Crowell).

Mr. Crowell reported that the request was for local consent for a Full-Service Restaurant License for
‘Bout Time Pub and Grub, which will be located at 475 West 14600 South, Unit B. The unit is
currently under construction. When the City updated the alcohol licensing regulations a few years
ago, a procedure was established to obtain local consent. This was the City’s role in the process that
culminated in receiving permits from the Department of Alcoholic Beverage Services. On March 24,
2021, the City Council granted a Bar Establishment License. Since then, the operator determined that
a dual-licensed establishment would better meet the community and business needs in Bluffdale. One
floor of the structure will be a restaurant with the second floor being a bar. Bluffdale City Code 3.60
provided the regulations and application requirements for Local Consent. There were no limitations
on Full-Service Restaurant Licenses.

Council Member Kallas asked for clarification about what the City Council had voted on and
authorized last year. Mr. Crowell explained that at the end of 2019, it was determined that some
updates to the Code were needed. At the beginning of 2020, the Alcoholic Beverage Licensing section
of the Business License Code was updated so it was up to date with the State Code. One of the
provisions the Council allowed was one Bar Establishment License to be issued in the community.
Subsequent to that ordinance, ‘Bout Time Pub and Grub came to the Council to request local consent
for their establishment. This was granted in March 2021. Since the business wanted to have a
restaurant floor and a bar floor, two types of licenses were needed. This was a different approach and
the intention was to serve the market as efficiently as possible.

The applicant, Tim Ryan, stated that the construction process had been slow but the work was near
completion. Some design modifications were made to serve more customers and open the business
to other members of the community. The space was always planned to comprise two stories but it
had been redesigned to provide a 21-and-over bar area upstairs. On the main floor, there would be a
more family-friendly environment. This would make it possible to serve families that want to enjoy
the sports atmosphere. The bar structure was removed from the first floor and the seating capacity
increased. Additionally, liquor displays would be removed from the first-floor design. Customers
could still be served wine, beer, or spirits but they would be poured in a restaurant environment, which

is very different. There would be limited access to the upstairs area, with a host or hostess present to
ensure ID compliance.

Mr. Ryan reported that there was a construction calendar in place. It suggested that there would be a
Certificate of Occupancy around January 6, 2023. The goal was to open shortly after that. Based on
Bar License availability, the ‘Bout Time Pub and Grub might miss the last one for the year, which
would be available in November. The next one would not be available until February. He was not
certain whether the restaurant and bar would open without both licenses or if it would wait an
additional 45 days. Mr. Ryan noted that he had operated dual-license facilities in the past.

Council Member Hales asked if the menu would be the same for both floors. This was confirmed.
There would be a full 50-item menu upstairs and downstairs. An elevator system would bring food
from the kitchen on the main floor to the upstairs area. The restaurant license would require an intent
to dine, which meant there needed to be a food purchase with alcohol. In a restaurant, it is also
possible to provide employment opportunities on the main level for employees who were under 21
years of age. That would be helpful in the current labor market. Council Member Hales asked about
occupancy. Mr. Ryan believed the total capacity would be approximately 180. The main floor would
be all table seating. There would also be a rooftop patio available year-round for patrons who are 21
years of age and older.

Council Member Kallas noted that Local Consent is a procedural process. He wondered if it was an
entitlement that runs with the land or if it is attached to the business itself. Mr. Ryan understood that
local consent would be granted to ‘Bout Time Pub and Grub only. If the business should relocate or
cease to exist, the City could reissue Local Consent to another operator or not. Council Member
Crockett wondered if the increase in tables needed to be addressed. She wanted to know if this would
impact the necessary amount of parking. Mr. Ryan did not believe this needed to be addressed
because the increase is less than 10% of seating. The tenants were also complementary in terms of
use by time of day. The co-tenant in the building would be a coffee shop, which will be busier in the
morning while the restaurant and bar will be busier during the lunch and dinner hours. There would
not be competition for parking.

Mayor Hall opened the public hearing. There were no comments. The public hearing was closed.

South. The motion was seconded by Council Member Hales. Vote on motion: Council Member
Aston-Aye; Council Member Kallas-Aye; Council Member Crockett-Aye; Council Member
Hales-Aye. The motion passed unanimously.
