I.    Welcome and Call to Order – Mayor Dahle.
Mayor Dahle called the meeting to order at approximately 5:45 p.m.

II.    Emergency Management Update.
Julie Harvey, Emergency Management Coordinator informed the Council that the County is
communicating daily at least three different ways and sending out public talking points. The
Emergency Management Team also receives additional official use only documents. The
County is gathering information about jurisdictional impacts on essential functions. She believes
the main impacts are economics and government functions.

Ms. Harvey reported that there was minimal damage from the earthquake. If there is damage, it
needs to be reported to the City by 4/20/2020. She was currently working through the Pandemic
Emergency Operations Plan and the Continuity of Operation Plan. She stated that the best the
City can do is to provide education and a framework for neighbors to help neighbors because
there are not enough government resources. There was a discussion about the “flatten the curve”
process. Council Member Petersen asked about notifying the community on where to gather
during an emergency. Ms. Harvey reported that there is training and outreach to be done and
churches can assist with that. She thought the recent earthquake process went smoothly.

Police Chief, Justin Hoyle reported that things have remained status quo with a few additional
officers on the street. He pointed out that there has been a downtick in calls for service.

Ms. Chamness expressed her gratitude to both Ms. Harvey and Chief Hoyle.

III.    State of the Arts Report – Sheryl Gillilan, Executive Director.
Sheryl Gillilan, Executive Arts Director reported that the Children’s Theater production will be
rescheduled. The Fine Arts show was also being rescheduled to October. The Music Fest was
canceled as well but the first scheduled concert will be July 11 assuming conditions are normal.
If things are not better, social distancing may need to be implemented. There was also
information available about providing refunds if events cannot be attended. There will also be
the option of live streaming concerts if needed. Council Member Gibbons suggested keeping
events scheduled to maintain a feeling of community.

Ms. Gillilan informed the Council that she wanted to recognize the volunteers who assisted last
year with all of the events. The TRC funding for the lighting was received and the lights were

planned to recontact prior donors soon.

The Council and Mayor Dahle commended the program and the work Ms. Gillilan was doing.

IV.    Discussion on Proposed Stormwater Ordinance.
Ms. Chamness introduced the updates to the stormwater ordinances. City Engineer, Jared
Bunch, presented the report and provided background on how the ordinance changes came about.
The ordinance reflects the numerous changes required as part of the MS4 permit as well as
updates to the stormwater management programs.

The current stormwater ordinance was likely drafted from other local jurisdictions. Four out of a
total of 12 chapters were not being edited. The Council asked for a general summary of the
proposed changes.

Mr. Bunch stated that a significant amount of input was made regarding the design criteria for
storm events as well as stating the authority for the MS4. There was additional clarity added
about waste. A question was raised about pool draining. Mr. Bunch responded that pool
drainage is not prohibited under the MS4 as long as the water is not chlorinated. Chlorine in
pools dissipates after about four days. There was discussion about how to regulate the existence
of chlorine and educate the community regarding chlorine. Currently, the City of Holladay’s
Ordinance and the MS4 permit requirements are silent on chlorine restrictions associated with
pool discharge. Mr. Bunch agreed to provide additional information at the next Council meeting.

Mr. Bunch explained that additional clarifications have been made regarding permits and
specifically long-term maintenance agreements with residents. The City of Holladay currently

V.      Discussion on Short-Term Rental Options.
Ms. Chamness asked for direction on the Council wants to proceed. Mayor Dahle stated that of
greatest concern is Short-Term Rentals (“STR”). Community Development Director, Paul
Allred addressed specific STRs and stated that enforcement is difficult because the City has to
clearly show that a property is being rented illegally. The City can only investigate situations
when there is a complaint.

Council Member Gibbons had questions about State legislation regarding STRs. He did not
believe the City was prevented from data mining. His constituents are overwhelmingly opposed
to STRs. The City’s current fines are lower than other surrounding jurisdictions so he suggested
two possible ordinances. He proposed one to license STRs and one to provide for enforcement
fines. There was discussion regarding licensing, fines, and enforcement.

Mr. Allred suggested the Council either consider moving toward prohibiting STRs or licensing
and regulating them. Currently, STRs are not allowed in single-family homes. Council Member
Petersen was not in favor of allowing STRs. Council Member Fotheringham raised concerns
about noise and excess cars caused by STRs.

Mr. Allred presented several slides for discussion and shared strategies from other jurisdictions.

The Council agreed that STRs should not be allowed in neighborhoods. City Attorney, Todd
Godfrey suggested increasing fines and limiting locations where STRs are allowed. Council
Member Gibbons suggested the potential of ideas that would make enforcement easier.

VI.     Plaza Furniture Discussion.
Assistant to the City Manager, Holly Smith reported that staff had been working on acquiring
furniture, including benches that move around the trees, chess tables, that people can be
interactive with. They were also looking to create a cohesive look throughout the Village. Staff
hoped to order the furniture soon. Ms. Chamness asked for direction from the Council as to

VII. City Manager Report – Manager Chamness.
Ms. Chamness discussed refunding the 2011 bonds. She reported that the City has locked a rate
that will result in a savings of $242,000 over the remainder of that term. This was an increase
from a previous estimate of $170,000. She also wanted to acknowledge the efforts of staff over
the last few weeks addressing adjustments due to COVID-19. She acknowledged City Recorder,
Stephanie Carlson in particular for her assistance.

VIII. Council Reports and District Issues.
Council Member Gibbons reported that he met with a neighboring water district about staffing
for weekly pick-ups. The annual pick-up will be modified to have on.ly scheduled drop-offs in
2020. A press release was posted on the City’s website. It was noted that the service is paid for
as part of the regular fees.

Council Member Fotheringham reported on mosquito abatement efforts and state that there has
been no impact on the nature of mosquitos. The committee will continue its work.

Council Member Quinn informed the Council that the district has looked at the cost of
improvements at Morningside but the costs have greatly increased since they were originally
obtained. They do not have the funding currently but plan to move forward once additional
funds are available.

Council Member Petersen had nothing to report. It was mentioned that the City should continue
to review potential improvements near Olympus High School.

Council Member Durham addressed the Tree Committee and reported that the Arbor Day
Observance was canceled, however, they still may have the tree giveaway on June 6. The Utah
League of Cities and Town have been coordinating with Utah cities and helped coordinate with
the Governor. The Legislature will also be calling a Special Session in April and will want input
from cities on whether there are statutory requests for new issues. They also want feedback on
budgetary concerns. Mayor Dahle reported that one concern is small business loans for
businesses and how the City can best help communicate that information and help connect
people with the needed resources. He asked if the City could be engaged in processes pertaining
to funding for small businesses.

Mayor Dahle echoed Ms. Chamness and commended City staff for their efforts. He reminded
those present that social distancing and staying home through April 30 is critical.
