WORK SESSION 
 
Administrator Barnes said when they met last time; they were making adjustments to the 
map.  He has a copy of all the maps the Commission has made.  At the last meeting, there 
was a debate on the proposed map of the Chalk Creek area.  He distributed an excerpt of the 
meeting minutes when the zoning of the Chalk Creek area was proposed.  The minutes shows 
there were three motions were made.  These were centered on the AG-1 zone, the AG-6 
zone, and the AG-20 zone.   
 
Administrator Barnes said the public has gotten confused.  A map was proposed by a 
citizen’s group which showed the AG-6 zone along the Chalk Creek highway; however, this 
zoning was never on a map produced by the Planning Commission.  At one point, the 
Commission did discuss an AG-1 on every County road.  That would have included Chalk 
Creek road to the State line and a map was produced that showed that possibility.

Chair Ure said after listening to the public comments, he would like to look at two options.  
The first is to have the AG-1 zone along the highway up to the point that it would make sense 
to have the zone end.  Behind the AG-1 zone would either be the AG-20 or AG-40.  The 
second option would be to have the AG-6 zone to 500 feet, followed by the AG-20 or AG-
40.   
 
Administrator Barnes said at one time he suggested they keep the Chalk Creek area to the 
AG-40, but the Planning Commission wanted it to be put in the AG-20.  A conversation took 
place about extending the AG-6 along the highway corridor line; however, that idea fell by 
the wayside when they started drawing lines on the map.  There has never been an official 
zoning map with AG-6 in the Chalk Creek area.  Commissioner Henrie said the highway has 
a lot of traffic.  There is the same infrastructure along the road.  There are school bus 
services, emergency services, and fire services along the entire road.  Whatever is zoned 
close to Coalville, should be the zoned for the entire way.   
 
Chair Ure said to him, this is about giving landowners an opportunity to be flexible with their 
land.  If someone wants to use their land for collateral, currently they have to use 40 acres.  If 
it is zoned AG-6, that is all they would have to use.  He thinks smaller zoning will help curb 
growth.  The developers are after the big parcels of ground where they can put in an entire 
subdivision.  If it is only six acres, someone may have a new neighbor, but not an entire 
subdivision.   
 
Commissioner Henrie said he thinks the Commission has not provided the justification for

should be because of a good reason and available infrastructure.  It is not a valid reason to 
zone a certain way simply because it benefits someone.  Commissioner Clyde said he agrees 
with Commissioner Henrie.  He thinks the reason why these conversations have been so
