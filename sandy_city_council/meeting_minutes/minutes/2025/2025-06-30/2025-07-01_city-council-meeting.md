1.         25-208                 Building our Future: A Community Housing Workshop Series, Workshop 1
                                  - Where are we now?
                   Attachments:   July 1 2025 Memo_Housing Workshop Series
                                  Shawn Teigen Utah Foundation Presentation
                                  Steve Waldrip - Governor's Office Presentation
                                  Community Development and Economic Development Dept
                                  Presentation
                                  Council Chair Aaron Dekeyzer welcomed those in attendance at the workshop. The City
                                  Council held the first workshop in a planned series of workshops focused on Housing.

                                  Workshop #1: Building our Future: A Community Housing Workshop Series - Where are
                                  We Now.
                                  The moderator for this evening's housing workshop is Lynn Pace, City Attorney. The
                                  objective of tonight's workshop was to establish a common baseline understanding of our
                                  community's housing landscape, including current needs, projected demands, and the
                                  range of affordability challenges.

                                  Shane Pace, CAO, spoke on the history of the growth period of Sandy City. Sandy had
                                  many years of aggressive housing growth. He spoke about the high property tax rates
                                  levied on Sandy City residents in the past. In the 90's, the city focused on commercial
                                  development. The commercial development eased the property tax burden on Sandy
                                  residents. As the city grew, Sandy City became a Class 1 city. Currently, land costs in
                                  Sandy are very high. There is shortage of affordable housing. The population in our city
                                  has declined because families are locating to other cities with more affordable housing.

                                  Lynn Pace, City Attorney reviewed the format for the workshop and introduced the
                                  presenters. Tonight's workshop is part 1 of a multi-part workshop series. The focus of this
                                  evening's workshop in informational and educational.

                                  Shawn Teigen, President of the Utah Foundation, provided an overview and history of
                                  Utah's housing deficit. He spoke about the need for more affordable housing. In
                                  2018-2020, more homes were built but the housing shortage remained. In a recent
                                  survey, 34% expressed a need for more affordable housing and the impact on the quality
                                  of life. Housing affordability in Utah has been declining for those earning the average
                                  median income and is a top concern for residents. Wasatch Front is expected to require
                                  200,000 housing units each decade. Council discussion and comments followed the
                                  presentation.

                                  Steve Waldrip, Senior Advisor to the Governor for Housing Strategy and Innovation
                                  presented on the housing crisis. Housing construction has decreased across the country
                                  in 2022. He provided an overview of the Governor's initiative for new home and starter
                                  home construction. Home prices are outpacing income in Utah. High demand for housing
                                  is driving these issues. He reviewed the impact of unstable housing. Council questions
                                  and comments occurred throughout the presentation. He spoke about the Legacy Cities
                                  project in Salt Lake and Ogden and the impact of expanding this model around the state.

                                  Following the guest speakers, City staff presented to the Council about the housing
                                  challenges our community is facing.

7.         25-211                 Brew Pubs Calls for Service Update

                   Attachments:   Police Call Data - Brew Pubs Final

                                  Justin Sorenson, Council Office, provided requested information regarding an analysis of
                                  police calls near establishments that serve alcohol. This information was in follow-up to a
                                  proposal by Council Member Zach Robinson in April 2025 to streamline the process and
                                  expand the permitted locations for brew pubs within Sandy City. During this discussion
                                  Council requested the additional information. The analysis examined a 12 month period of
                                  time and collected data from various alcohol service establishments in Sandy and in
                                  neighboring cities. Across all nine establishments, only three alcohol-related calls for
                                  service were reported. There was no clear evidence of increased calls for service and the
                                  data did not show a higher incidence of police activity at establishments that serve
                                  alcohol. Council Member Robinson wanted to address the concerns brought up by the
                                  Council. James Sorensen, Community Development provided additional information.
                                  Council questions and comments followed. This is an information item presented to the
                                  Council.
