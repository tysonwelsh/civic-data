         E. Mayor and Council Reports

Council Member Don Shelton said he has met with the Association of Municipal Councils, a group
of council members from around Salt Lake County, facilitated by staff at Salt Lake County. While
there, they had a legislative update from the Utah League of Cities and Towns. He also attended the
Legislative Policy Committee (LPC) meeting and received a legislative update there. There will be
legislation to limit and/or eliminate incentives that cities can provide, particularly for retail
establishments moving into their city. He had the privilege of attending the Strategic Planning
meeting with everyone and talk about the future of our city. He went to see Once Upon a Mattress
and it was a lot of fun. He led the Western Growth Coalition discussion yesterday and they received
an update from Utah Transit Authority (UTA) on transit. He wasn’t aware, and wanted to share with
everyone else, that the Via Service has been made permanent; the program has been expanded and is
no longer a pilot program. He received an update from Salt Lake County and Wasatch Front
Regional Council (WFRC) on the transportation plans, he encouraged everyone to attend the meeting
on Monday next week.

Council Member Patrick Harris had nothing to share this week.

Council Member Brad Marlor said he and his wife met with Kyle Bowen, South Jordan Cemetery
Sexton, in regards to finding a couple cemetery plots. They had a great experience meeting with him.
He met with a developer on October 11 regarding developing some property near River Park and
he’s waiting to see what happens there. On October 12 he attended the Strategic Planning meeting
with staff, he feels the City is headed in the right direction and he appreciated everyone’s efforts. On
October 13 he had an Architectural Review Committee (ARC) meeting with five agenda items, he is
excited about all the good things happening. On October 14 he attended the Arts Council
presentation of Once Upon a Mattress, it was a lot of fun and he would encourage those who missed
it this year to plan on seeing next year’s production.

Council Member Tamara Zander thanked the staff for the Strategic Planning meeting last week, it
was very informative and she appreciated how proactive they were in helping the council be ahead of
concerns and budget issues with the city. She has learned a lot over her years on the council, but she
really did learn some valuable information at the meeting. Yesterday she had the opportunity to listen
in on the LPC meeting and she was impressed, again, with the leadership of the Utah League of
Cities and Towns, all those working hard to protect the rights of our cities and towns. Yesterday she
had the South Valley Mosquito Abatement Board meeting, they are still being held virtually and will
probably keep that going for a while; nothing major to report there. She had a resident reach out to
her, wanting to get involved in the city; she referred them to our website that lists the councils and
committees they can join but she asked staff to let her know if they know of anything specific that
has an opening to direct this resident to. She shared a story about her daughter leaving the Provo
MTC and using our public transportation to get to the airport, she was so impressed to hear that we
have made such huge improvements to our public transportation that they were able to get from
Provo to Salt Lake so easily.

Mayor Pro Tempore Jason McGuire said he also attended the Strategic Planning meeting and
appreciated all the work put into that. The Arts Council did not have a meeting this month due to the

         H. Public Hearing Item:

             H.1. Zoning Ordinance 2021-09-Z, Rezoning property generally located at 972
             W. ShieldsLane, from the A-5 Zone to the R-1.8 Zone. Applicant Tina Franco.
             (By Director of Planning, Steven Schaefermeyer)

Director of Planning Steven Schaefermeyer reviewed background information from the prepared
presentation (Attachment A).

Mayor Pro Tempore opened the hearing to public comment.

Patrick Eggbert (Resident) thanked everyone for their service and gave a voice of support for
this project. He knows many of the neighbors, landowners and the Mumfords and he plans to
purchase one of these lots; he knows five other families that are also interested in these lots. They
would all be good stewards of this land, and they hope this rezone gets approved.

Mayor ProTempore McGuire closed the hearing to public comment.

Tina Franco (Applicant) has been working very diligently with the city’s Planning and
Engineering Departments to make this possible for the Mumfords; they would really like to get
this done.

Council Member Marlor asked about the hammerhead at the end of the development, specifically
the pros and cons.

City Engineer Brad Klavano said it’s a hammerhead turnaround, it meets the fire code and would

be posted “no parking” to keep it clear so firetrucks, garbage trucks, etc., can turn around there.
Generally they prefer cul-de-sacs, but this is allowed under our code and they do allow them in
certain circumstances where cul-de-sacs are difficult to do.

Mayor ProTempore McGuire reopened the hearing to public comment.

Glade Mumford (Applicant) said it has been quite a challenge to get to this point so far, they
hope that the council is willing to look at the plan with open eyes and be able to help them
accomplish this development they are planning for so they can have more funds for retirement.

Mayor Pro Tempore McGuire closed the public comment.

Council Member Shelton asked, after looking at the concept map, if the entrance to the trail
parking lot was across the road.

Director Schaefermeyer said the property next to their property isn’t theirs, their home is just west
of that. The subject property and subsequent land to the north is what will become part of this
subdivision. There are challenging grade issues in the area. In the past they wondered if this was
even a possibility, so this has taken quite a bit of effort to determine that it was possible to put a
road through there.

Director Klavano said it would have been ideal to line up the roads, but with the different property
owners and similar grade issues it is not optimal but for this to only be for seven homes and with
the parking lot that doesn’t have a lot of traffic either I don’t see it being an issue.

Council Member Marlor asked if there was a walkway from the current cul-de-sac on the plan to
the trail.

Director Schaefermeyer said yes, all the developments along the trail have been requiring those
connections.

Council Member Zander asked if the neighbors to the north of this proposed hammerhead have
voiced concerns about this development.

Director Schaefermeyer said this is the ideal option for them. Part of the history for this is that
there was an easement for that development and coming through that cul-de-sac is not ideal for
those neighbors. When the subdivision was recorded there was an access easement as part of that,
there are several issues with using that easement at this point and that’s eventually why they
looked to Shields Lane as an alternative for access.

Council Member Harris said this is in his district. These are bigger lots than the surrounding
neighbors and he sees no reason not to support this.

Council Member Harris motioned to approve Zoning Ordinance 2021-09-Z, Rezone of
property from A-5 zone to R-1.8 zone. Council Member Marlor seconded the motion; Roll
Call vote was 5-0, unanimous in favor.
