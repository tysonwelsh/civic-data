    DTS study and transportation project funding

              Kevyn Smeltzer, Director, Salt Lake County Public Works Operations, presented
a PowerPoint presentation regarding White City Metro Township’s Overall Condition Index (OCI)
evaluation. The Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD) instructed Salt Lake County
Public Works and Engineering to hire a company that does data collection of pavement on every
road in the MSD. A van that is equipped with cameras and other technologies gather information
about the pavement it is driving on. That data is analyzed and generates an OCI for each road.

                Brian Hartsell, Associate General Manager, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services
District (MSD), stated as part of the 2022 MSD budget, $3.5 million was approved for road and
maintenance projects. The Council will need to come up with a priority list of projects; once the
list is completed, it will be combined with other metro townships priority lists and a funding
estimate will be attached to the projects. Some of those projects will be completed in 2022 and
others will be completed in the following years.

White City Metro Township has received approximately $340,000 in ARPA funding and will most
likely receive another tranche of the same amount. The Department of Treasury issued its final
rule on the criteria to spend ARPA funds. One option recipient’s have, is selecting a standard
allowance, up to $10 million. That means, entities can use ARPA funds for any legal government
purpose under the general revenue loss provision and provides flexibility on how the ARPA funds
are used. Since ARPA funds are federal funds there is a question if entities must use federal
procurement and labor requirements if the funds are used for capital projects, he is waiting on the
answer. Until the answer is received, he recommends that any capital project over $100,000
follow the federal grant project requirements. ARPA funds will need to be spent by December 31,
2024.

    Engineering projects

                Shane Ellis, Transportation Engineering Manager, Salt Lake County Engineering
and Flood Control, stated Salt Lake County Parks and Recreation has taken all the funds out of
the canal trail project. If the Council wants to continue with the project, it will need to find funds
elsewhere.

Sandy City did not approve a HAWK crossing, so those funds were allocated to other projects.
The 730 East sidewalk project between Sego Lily and Tulip was previously funded but costs went
up, $60,000 was allocated to this project from the HAWK crossing funds. The bid process will
start in the middle of next week and it will take a few weeks to complete.

Ski Connect received $116,000 from the HAWK crossing funds and the plan review was done
today.

               Mayor Flint asked if matching funds for Ski Connect could be obtained and
allocated to accommodate ADA requirements.

               Mr. Ellis stated grants are not being applied for right now because it is outside of
the grant cycle.

                Mayor Flint stated there are grants available right now, she has recently written
letters to obtain grants.

               Mr. Ellis stated he will put in a request for any upcoming grants.

               Mayor Flint stated whatever funds that are not used for these projects need to be

project is that Sego Lily will be readdressed to get more community buy in.

City project. Residents were already surveyed, and the design has been decided upon.

              Paul Ashton stated when the engineers are hired, they will need to speak to the
Council and discuss the design.

             Mr. Ellis stated he has not seen a final cross section that the Council is saying
everyone agreed to. He will see what he can find on it.

             Council Member Price stated she worked on the design with Madeline Galang-
Francisco who previously worked for Salt Lake County Engineering.

               Mayor Flint stated the design was brought to the meeting and presented to the
Council. She instructed Mr. Ellis to meet with Ms. Galang-Francisco to obtain that information.

               Mr. Ellis asked if someone went house to house surveying residents.

                Mayor Flint stated residents on Sego Lily were interviewed and surveyed and an
architectural rending has been created.

                Council Member Price stated the information from the initial survey was thrown
out. The Council decided it wanted real sidewalks, real gutters, and parking on both sides of the
street. They also want a stripped bike lane, the center turn lane would remain, and the homeowner
yards would be unaffected. The design was to help narrow the road and slow speeding cars down.

    Big Bear Park improvement and funding

                Mayor Flint stated she received a call today informing her that the funds are not
available for the Big Bear Park portion of the canal trail project. Sandy City has pulled out of the
project because they do not have any money. Someday the trail will be completed, and it can
become a destination that people want to come to. Sandy City will need to eventually fix a portion
of the canal. Let’s enjoy the park and use the remaining money to complete other projects like Ski
Connect.

    Walk White City open house results

               Erin O’Kelley, Long Range Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District,
presented handouts to the Council reviewing the three phases of the Walk White City project
schedule. A public event workshop will be held between March and April, in the meantime, the
steering committee will meet to discuss the goals of the project. They will formulate ideas that
pertain to those goals like; safety, parks, school access, catering to all users, and enhance sense
of place. She presented the Walk White City project website, www.walkwhitecity.org.

               The Business meeting was closed and the Public meeting was opened.

Unified Fire Authority

               Assistant Chief Riley Pilgrim reviewed call for services for the month of January.
The budget process has begun and will be finalized in June or July. There has been an increase

Unified Police Department (UPD)

                Detective Jeff Fenton reviewed calls for service for January with domestic and
fraud cases being high. January was hard on the precinct with the number of COVID cases on
the rise. The stop sign was taken out at the three-way intersection near the school crosswalk, Salt
Lake County did a traffic study and determined it was not needed.

                Chief Randy Thomas stated on January 26, 2022, an assisted care facility in
Midvale City, that housed vulnerable adults, was shut down. A complaint was received a year ago
and code enforcement tried to work with the care facility, but the owner did not fix the issues and
it was ultimately deemed inhabitable. UPD teamed up with Utah Transit Authority (UTA) to provide
buses to allow the residents a place to sit and keep warm and Unified Fire Authority (UFA) to help
with the hazmat issues. Detective Jeff Fenton was the police component for the hazmat cleanup.
The residents were cleaned up, interviewed, and the UPD Metro Mental Health Unit helped get
the individuals to various facilities.

Former White City Council Member, Cody Cutler, was interviewed and accepted to the Citizen
Advisory Board (CAB) and will represent White City Metro Township.

Calls come in everyday regarding stolen vehicles; do not leave vehicles warming up unattended
or valuables in them. Criminals use stolen vehicles in police pursuits, robberies, hit and runs, and
various other illegal activities.

               Council Member Little stated the ordinance that sets the policy on contribution
states the request needs to be in writing.

month along with a resolution formally allocating those funds.

policy to request funds, and there are three different deadlines for the same request, the process
needs to be clarified. A written request was submitted in October or November 2021.

Township budget process.

amount for the Council to use but did not set it specifically for the White City Community Council.

will not delay Southeast Township Days planning, then it should be alright to wait another month.

make it clear. A public hearing will also need to be set to allow the public to comment.

approval of funding to the White City Community Council until next month. The motion passed
unanimously.

               Council Member Price, seconded by Council Member Perry, moved to set a public
hearing for March 3, 2021, to receive comments on amendments made to the White City Metro
Township Ordinance regarding contributions to the White City Community Council. The motion

South Salt Lake Valley Mosquito Abatement District

                 Council Member Price stated she was elected as Chair. The Board discussed
district goals for the year. The salary line item in the budget was raised for seasonal employees

Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services Provider (MSD)

                 Mayor Flint stated the Board approved a budget adjustment for Sego Lily to Tulip
and 9400 Ski Connect sidewalk projects from the HAWK crossing budgeted funds. The auditing
laws for code of ethics, conflict of interest, and use of public property were reviewed. A UDOT
