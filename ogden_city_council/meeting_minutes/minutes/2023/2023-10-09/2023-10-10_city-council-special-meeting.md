A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed zone text amendments
regarding use and development standards in the M-1 and M-2 zones came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated
Administration is proposing several amendments to the allowable uses and development standards of certain uses in the M-1 and
M-2 zones. The amendments would do the following:

e Prohibit new junk and salvage yards, rebuildable car lots, recycling collection centers, and limit the area
vehicles could be stored in new auto repair shops.
e Limit new entertainment and fitness facilities, except motor vehicle related uses.
e Allow manufactured home sales displays and RV storage lots with offices. .
e Limit new personal storage facilities.
e Modify setbacks, height, and lot coverage standards.
e Establish design standards for new industrial buildings.
e Establish landscape buffer standards between industrial and residential uses.

The proposal focuses almost entirely on the M-1 (Limited Manufacturing) and M-2 (Manufacturing and Industrial) zones
and would not generally be applicable to the City’s other manufacturing zones, which include the MRD and DDR zones at BDO,
the OCIP zone in west Ogden, or the BEI and BEH zones within the Trackline Economic Development Project Area in west
Ogden. Administration posted a notice of pending ordinance on April 20, 2023 which limited the new applications for most
permitted and conditional uses in the M-1 and M-2 zones, prohibited new junk and salvage yards in the M-2 zone and prohibited

} new recycling and collection centers and towing and impound lots in the M-1 and M-2 zones. The pending ordinance restrictions
“expire on October 17, 2023.

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the
discussions of the proposal that have taken place to date, which have led Planning staff to separate the broader proposed text
amendments into three separate proposals/ordinances. Mr. Brierley noted that the proposals that have been made to date are based
upon strategic directive number one from the Ogden Community-Based Strategic Plan, which states “I want to live in a
community where there are well-paying employment opportunities that allow me to provide for my family.” The general purpose
of the proposed amendments was to maximize the value output of Ogden’s remaining industrial land, promote businesses that add
jobs for citizens and economic output for the entire community, and provide balance. Mr. Brierley presented an illustration to
communicate the economic impact of Utah’s manufacturing industry in 2018; the indirect impacts of the manufacturing industry
are far greater than the direct impacts according to the Kem C. Gardner Policy Institute analysis. Mr. Brierley then presented the
list of permitted and conditional uses allowed in the M-1 and M-2 zones and compared that with the proposed permitted and
conditional uses allowed; used and rebuildable car lots, junk or salvage yards, recycling collection centers, adult theaters, and
indoor athletic facilities would be removed from the list and limits would be placed on motor vehicle repair, towing and impound
lots, and personal storage uses. He offered a graph illustrating the employment per acre for manufacturing uses, compared to
warehousing, wholesale trade, junk and salvage yards, and personal storage uses. Employment rates are far higher for the first
three than the latter two. The average annual wage for manufacturing jobs is also approximately $20,000 higher than the other
listed jobs. The Planning Commission has recommended adoption of the proposed amendments and ordinances have been drafted
to give the Council the opportunity to vote on junk and salvage yards, personal storage, and athletic facilities separate from
storage and towing and impound auto repair uses and the proposed adjustments to design standards. He offered optional motion ,
language for Council consideration of adoption of the proposed ordinance.
