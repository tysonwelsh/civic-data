Washington County Land Use Authority Meeting
                              September 08, 2020

The regular meeting of the Washington County Planning Commission was conducted on Tuesday,
September 08, 2020 from the Commission Chambers in the Washington County Administration
Building, 197 East Tabernacle, St. George, Utah. Also, via electronic means through Zoom. Chairman
Kevin Jones opened the meeting at 1:52 p.m. He led the Pledge of Allegiance.

Chairman Jones apologized for the late start to the meeting. He explained how the meeting would be
conducted. He reminded the participants that although this is a public meeting, it would only be open
for public comments when so declared.

Commissioners present: Brandon Anderson, Dan Briggs (Alternate acting as a voting member), Shayne
Durrant, Slade Hughes, Reed Scow (Alternate), Doug Solstad, Mark Owens.

Excused: Jon Walter

County Staff present: Scott Messel, Community Development Director; Natalie Nelson, Deputy
Attorney; Doreen Bowers-Irons, Administrative Assistant; Cheyenne Bentley, Deputy Clerk at host
location. Todd Edwards, Engineer; Kurt Gardner, Building Official; Kim Hafen, County Clerk.


1.   PRESENTATION OF THE HABITAT CONSERVATION PLAN.

Mr. Cameron Rognan gave a presentation on the Habitat Conservation Plan program. He explained the
beginning and reasoning of the program’s initiation. He spoke of the Desert Tortoise protection and
areas they are located in within the Reserve. He pointed out that the renewal of the program is process.

The commission asked a few questions related to the tortoises, the Reserve and activities within it,
which Mr. Rognan answered.


2.    CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT. Review a conditional use permit request for a public utility
waterline project. The project will include construction of a new pump station and 12’ culinary water
pipeline within Cottonwood Springs Road to the Ledges development. The applicant is St. George City
Public Utilities/Todd Olsen of Bowen Collins & Associates, agent.

Mr. Messel provided a power point presentation that included the route map of the project. He explained
that St. George City Water, a public utility, is seeking approval to construct a new pump station on
their property at the existing City Creek Water Tank site. The property is located on Cottonwood
Springs Road within the unincorporated area of the county. The proposed project includes running
a new 12-inch culinary water transmission pipeline from the tank to the existing tank near the
Ledges Subdivision. The proposed construction will be within the existing Cottonwood Springs
Road. Public Utilities are a conditional use in the OSC-20 (Open Space Conservation 20 acre
minimum lot size) and OST-20 (Open Space Transition 20 acre minimum lot size) zones. This
project will provide culinary water to the Ledges Subdivision.

3.   PUBLIC HEARING/ZONE CHANGE. Review and hold a public hearing for a zone change
request of Parcel 3003-B-NS from the A-20 (Agricultural 20 acre minimum lot size) zone to the A-10
(Agricultural 10 acre minimum lot size) zone. The parcel is generally located at 10 East 1000 North in
New Harmony. The applicants are Roger and Stephanie Hughes.

Mr. Messel provided a power point presentation of the project that included a map of the location. He
reported the applicants would like to build a new single-family dwelling on Parcel 3003-B-NS, which
is located at approximately 10 East 1000 North in New Harmony. The 10-acre parcel does not meet the
existing A-20 (Agricultural 20 acre minimum lot size) zoning designation. The applicants are requesting
to rezone the property to the A-10 (Agricultural 10 acre minimum lot size) zoning designation to bring
the property into compliance in order to construct their home.

Recommendation:
Staff reviewed the proposal and recommends that the Planning Commission hold the public hearing.
Then, if they are comfortable with the rezone moving forward, may recommend to the County
Commission to approve the zone change request, based on the following findings:

4.    PUBLIC HEARING/ORDINANCE AMENDMENTS. Review, discuss, and hold a public
hearing for amendments to the Washington County Ordinances: Title 10, Chapter 9: PD Planned
Development.

Mr. Messel handed out the map of the areas of the proposed PD (Planned Development) Ordinance
amendment. He explained the ordinance amendments in detail noting that a PD zone change for Rural
Recreation Grounds and Facilities could not be approved in those areas.

Commissioner Hughes asserted that what we would like to do is take the private property rights of these
particular areas out of the zone so that we are taking the rights to do a planned development in these
areas.

Commissioner Durrant asked what the basis of the change was. Mr. Messel answered there have been
concerns from residents in these areas of how their community will develop.

Commissioner Hughes began speaking in Russian. He emphasized his disagreement with the
amendment, in that we have a PD zone to allow flexibility in our zoning for developers to bring projects
forward in a way that we could supposedly work with it. Two, we have not had a PD zone actually be
approved since we made this zone. It appeared to him that this is just a feint attempt to say we don’t
want anything to be built in Washington County but instead of coming out and saying that, we are going
pass some convoluted extra ordinance that just buries it in paperwork.

Commissioner Durrant asked, so that it is on public record, who recommended this change.
Commissioner Solstad added who came up with the map. Mr. Messel pointed out that the map came
from the General Plan of the communities. Mr. Messel was pressed as to who came up with the
amendment.

Commissioner Owens spoke up stating instead of asking questions that puts Mr. Messel in a bad
situation. Commissioner Durrant interjected and expressed to Mr. Messel that he was not trying to put
him in a bad situation, he just thought it important.

Ms. Nelson explained that the Rural Recreation Grounds and Facilities should be truly rural in nature.
If you look at the map and the areas, you’ll notice they are little communities rather than a super rural
out in the middle of nowhere.

Commissioner Hughes argued it is still a planned development. Ms. Nelson answered it was a planned
development but the thought was that it should be truly rural and not in a community where it affects
the community it is in. They are trying to stay within the General Plan.

Commissioner Solstad commented that the Kolob area represented on the map is large. He believed that
most of the Kolob area would be a good place for a rural recreation facility planned development.

Commissioner Owens remarked that outside of Commissioner Hughes, none of the commissioners lived
in the areas outlined in the map, though there may be some appreciation or understanding of the
community’s concerns. He disclosed he was not 100 percent in favor of the amendments, but understood
the impact rural recreation grounds and facilities have inside of a community. That type of development
would not be what the residents of those communities want and not in harmony with the General Plan.
This is the first time we have seen the map and have questions. However, we need to be cognitive of
the intent. We would do a lot more good in refining it than looking at it as a final document.

Commissioner Solstad argued that this is a planned development zone, which means nothing could
happen without approval.

The commission discussed the ordinance amendment in more detail. Commissioner Hughes was
adamant about infringing on private property rights.

Brian Thiriot via Zoom asked what this means to existing applications already in process, specifically
his project, Pine Valley Legacy.

Mr. Messel stated it would not affect those currently in process. He reported Mr. Thiriot submitted a
rural recreation ground and facilities PD project, Pine Valley Legacy, plan before the amendment was
considered. Therefore, his project will continue under the old plan.

5.   CONCEPT REVIEW OF A PLANNED DEVELOPMENT PROJECT. Presentation of a
concept plan for a planned development project on Parcel 7126-1-NW, which is the Monte Holm Rural
Recreation Ground Facility. The applicant is Monte Holm.

Mr. Messel provided a background on the Holmstead project. He reported Mr. Holm is looking to make
major changes to the project and it was determined a Planned Development Project would be the best
route for the project to take. He stated the applicant would welcome any feedback the commission would
provide. Questions in relation to the project were asked and answered. He introduced Mr. Monte Holm
and turned the floor over to him.

Monte Holm thanked the commission for their time. He pointed the project location as 1000 E.
Holmstead Dr. near Brookside and adjacent to Baker Dam. He explained the locations of the currents
and possible future buildings. He reviewed the long term proposed plans for future projects that would
include additional homes, cabinettes, RV sites, and several other amenities. He would be requesting a
zone change of the property to a PD (Planned Development) zone in order to accommodate the project.

The project was discussed at length that included questions and answers on roads and access, the dam,
fire protection, the water and wastewater systems. Mr. Holm introduced Steve Kamlowsky to explain
the wastewater system. Mr. Holm will bring the project plan back at a future date to move forward.
