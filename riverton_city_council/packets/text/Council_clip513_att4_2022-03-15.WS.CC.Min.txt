 2         EntryPoint Fiber Network Presentation
 3
 4   Mayor Staggs explained that Phase 2 of the broadband project is to put out a request for a
 5   proposal. Entry Point has finished Phase 1 and will present the design.
 6
 7   Bruce Patterson, Solutions Services Director for EntryPoint, and Tyler Riblett explained and
 8   displayed the design for broadband connections throughout the City. The design is included
 9   with the packet.
10
11   Councilmember McDougal asked Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, what was
12   currently budgeted for Phase 2 of the broadband project. Mr. Hicks replied that $1.2 million is
13   currently budgeted. Councilmember McDougal said he would like to order all of the supplies
14   before the project begins because he is concerned about the project stalling due to supply
15   issues.
16
17   Mr. Riblett said that supply issues exist and the City could purchase supplies before the project
18   begins.
19
20   Councilmember Buroker asked about the upgrade on Bangerter and 13400 South and whether
21   this would affect the broadband project. Mr. Patterson said they will coordinate with UDOT and
22   doesn’t believe this upgrade will affect the broadband project.
23
24   Councilmember Wells asked if there is UDOT conduit on Redwood Road and whether the City
25   would be using this conduit for broadband. Mr. Patterson explained that there is UDOT conduit
26   on Redwood Road, but this conduit does not have microduct. The City will need to place
27   microduct in three new ducts which would then be placed in the large duct owned by UDOT.
28
29   Councilmember Stewart asked whether fire stations would be connected in the broadband
30   project. Mayor Staggs said that fire stations were not included in this project and Mr. Patterson
31   confirmed that the fire stations were in the original scope of work but were removed.

40   Mayor Staggs commented that Google fiber is working in Riverton and all residents should be
41   extended service by 2023. He said the legislative session wrapped up on March 4. House Bill
42   242 mandates secondary meters be installed on all secondary water connections. Riverton City
43   has installed one-third of all City connections. The state of Utah is contributing $200 million
44   dollars to fund these meters and they will match 70% of the cost for the next two years. The
45   City will utilize ARP funds and match with state funds to finish the secondary meter project.

47   over/underpass. The City lobbyist is working to secure funding to pay the additional money that

32            2022 Legislative Update
33
34   Mayor Staggs reported that Rob Jolley, lobbyist, was very impressed with legislators at this
35   session especially since they were able to finance the 13400 South and Bangerter project by
36   moving funds into the TIFF fund. In 2011 to 2013, there was a 21-acre parcel that Riverton City
37   gave to UDOT to build out Mountain View Corridor and the exchange was to be a continuous
38   flow interchange at 13400 South and Bangerter which was received. However, there was a
39   question of whether the City received the full value for this land. Mr. Jolley is planning in April to
40   have a conversation with Senator Harper about the final cost for 13400 South to go fully
41   submerged. House Bill 242 was approved and the City will receive a 70% match for secondary
42   water meters. The City should be one of the first ones to receive the money that has been
43   appropriated.
44
45   Councilmember Stewart clarified that the State is acknowledging the 21 acres as a donation
46   from Riverton City. David Brickey, City Manager, said that Mr. Jolley did say that the State
47   acknowledged this donation and he is willing to come and answer questions.

11            Discussion on Lovers Lane
12
13   Councilmember Wells explained that Lovers Lane from 13600 South to the S curve has been a
14   rural dirt road but will eventually be paved. He is proposing that going south the road would be
15   a one-way road until the S curve. However, drivers heading south would be able turn around at
16   the springs. He believes this will keep the rural feel of this area.
17
18   Mr. Brickey said the Council is the roadway authority for the City. He said that the Council could
19   invite the public to make comments or the Council could make this tough decision and move
20   forward.
21
22   Councilmember Buroker commented that developing this narrow road into anything other than a
23   one-way road would be very expensive. Mr. Brickey agreed and said that he would maintain
24   this as a Class D road because the residents like the rural feel of this road.
25
26   Councilmembers, Mayor and staff had further discussion. After the discussion, all
27   Councilmembers were supportive of making this road a one-way road.
