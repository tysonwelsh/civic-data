Proposed ordinance 2024-2 changing the name of 1500 West on a cul-de-
sac south of Midland Drive to 3030 South
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed street renaming action
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Ogden City Engineering requests a street name change for 1500
West, near Midland Drive, to be designated as “3030 South”. The purpose of the request is to make the street numbering more
consistent with the established grid along Midland Drive. In January of 2024, the Planning Commission recommended that the
Council approve the petition.
          Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and stated Midland Drive runs diagonally, northeast to southwest
across the grid, through the Airport Industrial Park District. All properties addressed on Midland Drive are numbered via their
respective north-south location on the Weber County Grid (e.g., 2984 South Midland Drive). Likewise, streets that intersect
perpendicularly with Midland Drive, and have a general east-west orientation, are numbered via their north-south point of
intersection (e.g., 3250 South Street). The subject segment of 1500 West was created in 2015 with the approval and recordation of
the Midland Industrial Subdivision Plat. The subject does not connect with any other occurrence of 1500 West in the Weber
County Grid and is inconsistent with the general north/south orientation of the other occurrences of that street.
          Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-2, entitled:
                    “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, changing the name of 1500 West on a cul-de-sac south of Midland drive to
                    3030 South; and providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final
                    passage.”
          Chair Richey then opened the public hearing.
          There being no persons appearing to be heard, COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED THE PUBLIC HEARING BE
CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS, ALL VOTING AYE.
          ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER HYER AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER BLAIR,
ORDINANCE 2024-2 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL
MEMBERS BLAIR, CHOBERKA, GRAF, HYER, MYERS, VICE CHAIR WHITE, AND CHAIR RICHEY. VOTING
NO – NONE.

Proposed ordinance 2024-5 amending the zoning map of Ogden City by
reclassifying property at 335 7th Street as Two-Family and Small Lot
Residential Zone (R-2S)
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed rezone of property at 335
7th Street came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated PS Real Estate Investments, LLC has submitted a petition
to rezone six parcels, totaling 2.10 acres located at 335 7th Street, from R-3 (multiple family) and C-2 (community commercial) to
R-2S (two-family and small lot residential). The developer’s intent is to establish zoning on the property that allows for owner
occupied townhomes.
          Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and stated in July of 2023, the R-2S zone was adopted into the Ogden
City Municipal Code with the intent of providing moderate density residential development with an emphasis on homeownership
of individual dwelling units. The existing R-3 and C-2 zoning on the property could result in a maximum potential density of 45
dwelling units (two large apartment buildings) under one ownership. The applicant, rather than seeking to maximize density, is
proposing townhomes that can be owner-occupied, which requires the entire project to be under one zoning. If the rezoning to R-
2S is approved, the developer has a possible density of 36 townhome/rowhouse units, including an existing duplex that fronts off
7th Street. The Planning Commission has made a recommendation to adopt the proposed rezoning ordinance based on the finding
that the proposal is consistent with the General Plan and the Lynn Community Plan.
          Council member Hyer stated it looks as though the access to the subject property is from Grant Avenue and he asked if
that would change upon development of the site. Mr. Brierley stated the access will actually be from 7th Street and just one access
to the development is required. He added that the actual project concept has not yet been submitted.
          Chair Richey invited input from the applicant.
          Travis Taylor stated that he has developed in many communities and has found that increasing regulations lead to
unintended consequences and higher costs for the potential home buyer; however, he has been pleasantly surprised to see Ogden’s
approach to revamping the group dwelling text and adopting the R-2S zone. He is excited to proceed with this project and he
believes it has the support of City staff. He has enjoyed working with the Planning staff of the City.
           Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-5, entitled:
                    “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the zoning map of Ogden City, as adopted by Section 15-3-3 of
                    the Ogden Municipal Code, to reclassify as two-family and small lot residential zone (R-2s) property heretofore
                    classified as multiple-family residential zone (R-3) and community commercial zone (C-2), generally located at
                    335 7th Street, and providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final
                    passage.”
          Chair Richey then called for public input regarding the proposal.
          Angel Castillo stated that she is a fan of this zoning designation and the opportunities it can create in the community in
terms of home ownership. Additionally, she would like for the City to consider smaller individual housing units when the market
bears that type of development. She asked that the Council also discuss restrictive land use covenants requiring a percentage of the
units be made available for sale or occupancy by low-income residents.
          Linda Sanchez thanked Mr. Brierley for the manner in which he presented this application to the Council and to the
public. She added she is also in favor of this project and home-ownership opportunities.
          Teresa Bramwell stated she also loves the R-2S zoning designation; she had envisioned something different than what
she has seen. This subject project is an assemblage of six mid-block lots, and she asked how those lots came to be. Her concern is
that the developer was able to find six unusable lots, purchase them, and rezone them to create great value for himself.
          No additional persons came forward to be heard.
          Chair Richey asked Mr. Brierley to address Ms. Bramwell’s question regarding the history of the subject property. Mr.
Brierly stated that when Ogden was originally platted, it included huge blocks with large lots for agricultural and animal keeping
purposes. Many residents built their homes on the edge of the lots and the rest of the property eventually became unused and sold
to other property owners. There are other areas of the City where there are mid-block parcels that are undeveloped. Chair Richey
stated that during the last discussion of this application, the Council acknowledged that development of the property may not be
feasible if the owner were building just one home, but spreading the development costs across several units, makes the project
doable.
          Council member Graf stated that he believes the R-2S zone is a great benefit for the City and developers; additionally,
restricting the group dwelling ordinance will also help to accomplish various housing goals identified for the City. Vice Chair
White added that it is important to note that the developer could be building many more units under the current zoning of the
property, but she appreciates he has chosen the R-2S zone in order to complement existing development of the area.
          ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER HYER AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER GRAF,
ORDINANCE 2024-5 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL
MEMBERS BLAIR, CHOBERKA, GRAF, HYER, MYERS, VICE CHAIR WHITE, AND CHAIR RICHEY. VOTING
NO – NONE.

Communication Plan for Proposed Partial Street Vacation at Cahoon Street
between F Avenue and G Avenue
          A memo from Council staff explained a proposal was presented to the Council regarding a request to vacate a portion of
Cahoon Street between F and G Avenue. At the Council’s February 20, 2024, meeting, the Council voted to extend consideration
of the proposed street vacation, to allow the petitioner to meet with Ogden City Engineering to see if there is an option that can
work for both sides. As per the Council’s Rules and Norms, the item was extended. An extension, unlike a tabling, does not
require a date certain for when the item will be brought back for consideration. The Council’s rules and norms do require that
Council staff provide an update and present a communication plan for the item at the Council’s next regular meeting.
          Council Policy Analyst Burton summarized the memo and reported on a meeting between the petitioner and City
Engineering and Planning staff; Engineering has communicated that they cannot support the project given that the Utah
Department of Transportations (UDOTs) plans for the Interstate-15 (I-15) interchange are not finalized. However, there is a
meeting with UDOT later in March and they may be more familiar with the project plans after that meeting. Council staff is
suggesting an additional work session meeting following the UDOT meeting to discuss any new information regarding the
interchange project. Following the work session meeting, Council staff proposes placing the petition on the April 9. 2024, meeting
agenda for continued discussion and action. Notices of that meeting will be sent to affected property owners.

Council member Comments
          Council member Hyer addressed the rain barrel program; he acknowledged that utilization of rain barrels increases
awareness of the need to conserve water. However, the cost expended on rain barrels to save water is very high compared to smart
sprinkler controllers. He has encouraged the City’s Sustainability Coordinator to look into opportunities to utilize sustainability
funding to make sprinkler controllers available to residents who are interested. Residents can also utilize water monitoring devices
to determine their usage and the information gathered by the devices can help individuals to make better use of their resources.
          Chair Richey also addressed the rain barrel program; other communities that have participated in the program over the
past several years have viewed it as a catalyzing program for other conservation activities. There may not be a huge cost saving as
a result of the program, but there are many benefits to the storm drainage system as well.
          Council member Hyer stated that he would be interested in receiving data regarding the amount of rainwater collected by
the rain barrels.
          Council member Graf referenced an upcoming training opportunity at Weber State University regarding the benefits of
walkable communities. He then addressed Ms. Bramwell’s comments about the development at the corner of Quincy Avenue and
25th Street; it was his understanding that the project would be low-income housing, but he wondered if the City has any control
over that use or if it was driven by the developer. Council member Choberka noted it is her understanding that the developer was
able to access government subsidized funding sources by making a certain percentage of the housing units available to low-
income residents. Planning staff provided input regarding low-income housing funding programs and the restrictions developers
must heed in order to access that funding. The City does not have a great deal of control over whether a developer builds low-
income housing; if the zoning designation assigned to a property allows for multi-family development, the developer can build
low-income multi-family housing if they so choose.
          Vice Chair White referenced the project highlighted by Ms. Bramwell and noted that includes several other elements
besides the apartment building, such as retail, for-sale housing products, and open space. She hopes that Ms. Bramwell will look
into the entire project scope before making a determination about the benefit the project can provide to the community.
          Council member Blair applauded Administration and the Diversity Commission for the work they are doing in the
community; he appreciated them highlighting the females in the community that are doing such great work.
