   Sanitation Presentation

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, presented a Sanitation Comparison explaining
Strategic Priority #8, which is to promote fiscally responsible governance. He said the priority
could be accomplished by long-range planning and by establishing appropriate fee levels.

Mr. Hicks explained that on June 16, 2020 the City Council requested a high-level analysis on
various options regarding the sanitation fund, including an option for costs to self-provide. He
also said that direction given from the Auditor was to either make sure the Sanitation fund was
correctly treated as an enterprise fund (by eliminating the subsidy) or to no longer treat it as an
enterprise fund.

As part of their Review Process, Mr. Hicks explained that he had met with department heads to
project what departmental needs might be over the next ten years such as projects, staffing, etc.
He explained that, based on the FY 2020 budget, sales tax revenue had been updated and
projected based on current experience and the past five years’ trend. He reviewed the long-
range plan process, along with the sales tax budget and projections to be conservative, but not
to conservative.

Mr. Hicks then presented and reviewed the following four options in no particular order of
prioritization:

    •    Option 1: Maintain subsidy at original ($850k-$875k)
    •    Option 2: Increase rates incrementally until self-sustaining
    •    Option 3: Maintain subsidy at current rates
    •    Option 4: Zions Bank Rate Study
    •    Option 5: Increase rates to be self-sustaining while, self-providing sanitation

Mr. Hicks explained that the current Waste Management contract would expire June 30, 2023
and landfill rate increases were projected for all options. He said that in the process of putting
together long-range plans for all funds, there were many more additions anticipated in the plans
but were not considered when doing the analysis.

Councilmember Stewart clarified that, under the current assumptions, it would be cheaper to
self-provide than to continue the contract with Waste Management.

Lengthy discussion occurred regarding self-providing sanitation service.

Councilmember Buroker said that, when looking at the options, it could be challenging
determining “What is the right decision for Riverton City?” She then explained that she felt the
right decision for Riverton was “whatever brings the greater good as a community.”

Mayor Staggs said that his approach while serving as a Councilmember and currently as Mayor,
putting together a Mayor’s budget, was not just the subsidy, but the greater good should
translate into keeping fees as low as possible and not potentially looking for new projects or for
an opportunity to grow government. He said this issue would be discussed further at the
upcoming Strategic Planning Meeting.

    Riverton Police Department Report

Chief Don Hutson reported that September 2020 was highlighted by more widespread virus
outbreak in the community as the schools were impacted and some of the city employees tested
positive for the virus. He said this prompted a renewed focus on safety protocols and
procedures to mitigate the potential spread of the virus throughout their ranks. To date, he said
they had not experienced a positive test amongst their officers and they were keeping their
mitigation strategies in place for the foreseeable future.

Chief Hutson said they were still experiencing an above average number of vehicle burglaries in
the city and they were focusing their patrol efforts toward those areas where vulnerable vehicles
were located. He said they were also continuing their outreach efforts to educate the citizens to
lock their vehicles and not keep valuable items in them. Additionally, he said they conducted
more speed enforcement operations during the month in response to several complaints from
citizens regarding speeding in their neighborhoods. He then reported that the police department
also conducted their annual firearms training and their members spent an entire day on the
range qualifying and familiarizing themselves with all of their weapons systems. Chief Hutson
then reviewed the Incident Report for September 2020.

Chief Hutson then reported that October 2020 brought the first cold temperatures of the year
and a slight increase in the number of stolen vehicles (11) within the city, believing they had an
active ring of car thieves working in the community. He said they were able to collect
surveillance video that showed the same group of suspects involved in several vehicle theft
cases and they were able to identify one of the suspects; and, as they were trying to determine
where the suspect was living, he was arrested by another police agency driving a stolen vehicle.
He said the suspect was an ex-convict and was on parole from Idaho, so they were working with
the District Attorney to ensure he is sent back to Idaho to account for his criminal actions.

Chief Hutson reported that they conducted active impaired driving enforcement, which yielded
17 arrests for impaired driving, involving both drugs and alcohol. He said they also manned a
booth at the annual Halloween Bash, which provided an opportunity to interact with the
community in a fun and relaxed atmosphere. Although the COVID pandemic required them to
implement safety protocols and limit some of the activities, he said it was a great event and they
were honored to be a part of it. Chief Hutson then reviewed the Incident Report for October
2020.

Chief Hutson also reported that the Riverton Police Department recently completed
comprehensive Autism Awareness Training.

    Ordinance No. 20-29 - Approving a Rezone of Approximately 9.92 Acres located at
    1300 West Withers Lane - Newman Construction, Applicant

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that Newman Construction
submitted an application for a rezone of multiple parcels that add up to approximately 9.92
acres located on 1300 West between Silcox Drive (12700 South) and Withers Lane (12800
South). He said the property is currently zoned RR-22, which is a single-family zone with animal
rights. He said the area has a variety of zoning designations; the golf-course to the south is
zoned Parks-Open Space (P-OS), as is the rodeo arena and City Park to the west. To the north
is Commercial Gateway for the small office buildings; to the southwest is more RR-22 zoning,
and to the east is The Vistas at Riverbend, a 65-unit development zoned RM-8, which is a multi-
family zone allowing up to 8 units per acre. He said the General Plan for the subject properties,
as set in the recent General Plan update, is Medium High Density Residential and Mixed Use,
which carries with it a 5 to 8 Dwelling Unit per acre designation. The Medium High Density
Designation corresponds to the City’s RM-6 and RM-8 Zones, so the requested RM-8 zone
matches the General Plan.

Mr. Lethbridge said that on October 8, 2020 the Riverton City Planning Commission held a
public hearing to review the proposed application from Newman Construction. The Planning
Commission voted to recommend approval of the proposed rezone and recommended the
following motion: I move the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 20-29 - Rezoning the subject
properties located near Withers Lane and 1300 West to RM-8-D-SD (RM-8-Downtown-Specific
Designation) with the following provisions:
Page | 7 Minutes – City Council Meeting
November 17, 2020



    1. Single-family detached units only.
    2. Single-story units only.
    3. No driveway or street access onto 1300 West.

Mr. Lethbridge said that, in addition to the Planning Commission review, the applicant held a
neighborhood meeting to discuss the proposed zoning. Based on that feedback, the developer
adjusted his concept plan to address the neighbors’ concerns. Taking the concerns expressed
by the public, along with feedback from the Planning Commission, Staff worked with the
applicant on an SD designation. He said that Staff recommended the property be rezoned to
RM-8-D-SD, which is Residential-Multifamily, 8 Dwelling units per acre maximum, in the
Downtown, with the following Specific Development provisions:
              1. Single-family detached units only.
              2. Single-story units only.
              3. No driveway or street access onto 1300 West.
Mr. Lethbridge said this would result in a development pattern very similar to the adjacent Vistas
community, which consists of detached rambler units. The other designation would prohibit
driveway access directly onto 1300 West, which he said would help reduce traffic on that
roadway, as well as creating a better buffer. He said the SD designations would run with the
land, meaning, even if this developer chooses not to pursue the project following the rezone, the
restrictions would remain in place. Following the Planning Commission’s recommendation, he
said that staff had received no further comments in opposition to the application and that Staff
concurs with the Planning Commission’s recommendation for approval as described above.

Discussion was held regarding roadways and parking and Councilmember Wells reported that

shown on the concept plan.

    Ordinance No. 20-26 - Riverton City is proposing changes to Sections 17.15.030(2)
    and 18.215.020(10) allowing payment in lieu of dedication of water shares for new
    development

Trace Robinson, Public Works Director, said that the public hearing was for a Zoning Text
Change proposed by Riverton City. He then explained that for many years the City required
dedication of secondary water shares from residential and commercial developments. He said
the proposed change would allow the City to accept a payment of a fee in lieu of the dedication
so that the City could purchase water shares from which ever canal that would balance the
Page | 8 Minutes – City Council Meeting
November 17, 2020



City’s secondary water system supply and demand. Presently, and for many years, he
explained that the City had accepted water shares from any of the four canals running through
the city. The location of the canals and the city pumps create pressure zones. Water can be
transferred from an upper zone (on the west side of the city) to a lower zone (on the east side)
by gravity, but he said that moving water from east to west was more difficult. He said the
proposed Zone Text Change would allow the City to accept a fee in lieu of water share
dedication, so the City could then purchase shares to meet specific needs and system design
objectives. He said that Staff recommended the Planning Commission forward a
recommendation to approve the proposed changes.

Mr. Lethbridge said that on October 8, 2020, the Planning Commission held a public hearing to
review the proposed text amendment; and, following the hearing, the Commission voted to
recommend approval of the text amendment.

In response to Councilmember Stewart, City Attorney Ryan Carter said the City would always
take water shares unless they were at capacity and then the City would take a fee in lieu, which
would be based upon an engineer’s assessment as to how the demand was in that particular
zone, so it is very zone specific. He said that if it was already decided that there was the
capacity to meet all of the water needs in a particular zone, a fee in lieu would be accepted that
would be based upon the market value of the share at the time of dedication. That money could
be used to purchase water shares in other zones where the water was needed. He further
explained that when a fee in lieu was accepted, it would be specific to the zone where the
development was occurring.

Mayor Staggs opened a Public Hearing and called for public comment; there being none, he
declared the Public Hearing closed.

Councilmember Stewart MOVED that the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 20-26 -
Approving changes to Sections 17.15.030(2) and 18.215.020(10) allowing payment in lieu
of dedication of water shares for new development and adding language to the annual
review or more frequently as determined by City Staff or the Council. Councilmember
McCay SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion; there being
none, he called for a roll call vote and the vote was as follows: Councilmembers Buroker-Yes,
McCay-Yes, McDougal-Yes, Stewart-Yes, and Wells-Yes. The motion passed unanimously.

    Ordinance No. 20-28 - Riverton City is proposing changes to Chapter 18.135 regarding
    the required distance separating agricultural animals from other land uses

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, said that the public hearing for a Zoning Text
Change proposed by Riverton City. He explained that the Zone Text Change was designed to
eliminate an ambiguity and potential ordinance conflict in the existing code. He said the conflict
was identified when the Legal Department was asked to investigate a complaint about animals
in an RR-22 zone. The RR-22 Zone comes with animal rights and includes requirements for
where animals may be kept in relation to residential structures. General Regulations 18.135.090
Animals and Fowl RR-22 Zone 18.25.110 (2) Farm Animals: No agricultural animals shall be
kept or maintained within 75 feet of any dwelling nor shall any such animal be kept or
maintained within 40 feet of any zone district boundary that prohibits such use. No coop or
shelter for keeping of agricultural fowl shall be constructed or maintained within 75 feet of a
dwelling nor 40 feet of a zone district boundary which prohibits such use. Keeping of Farm
Animals: No agricultural animals shall be kept within 40 feet of any residential dwelling. No
structure for the keeping of agricultural animals or fowl shall be constructed or maintained within
40 feet of a residential dwelling. He said that Staff recommended adjusting the ordinance to
make all set-backs a consistent 40-ft from a residential dwelling to agriculture animals or animal
structures.

Mr. Lethbridge said that on September 10, 2020, the Planning Commission held a public
hearing to review the proposed text change; and, at that meeting, the Commission voted to
recommend approval of the text amendment.
