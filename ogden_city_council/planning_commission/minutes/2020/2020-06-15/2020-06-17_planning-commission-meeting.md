   1. Discussion, Pending Ordinance: Apartments and Group Dwellings in Commercial zones.

       Mr. Spencer stated Staff wanted to initiate a discussion relating to multi-family projects in
       commercial zones so appropriate language can be developed to address concerns. He stated
       Staff has had some preliminary discussions and is doing some research relating to these. Some
       questions have arisen as to what makes a particular project more livable than others, what
       projects retain their value over time, and what creates a better quality of life for its tenants.
       Commission felt connectivity to the neighborhood and surrounding amenities such as parks,
       schools, entertainment and shopping is important. Commission felt usable green space and a
       connection to nature is important, as well as connections between the living area and on-site
       green or play areas and parking. Commission discussed the new project to the west of Winco
       on 12th Street, noting it appears there is little green space. While this facility is near the grocery
       store, it appears to be isolated from other facilities. It was suggested while the BDO or Ogden
       Nature Center are nearby, they don’t appear to be accessible from this community. Commission
       was concerned with projects that appear to only be served with a parking lot and a dumpster,
       suggesting walking paths and green space are important. It was stated while many show deck
       space, the decks are too small to be usable and often become storage space.

       Commission stated projects are often presented to the Commission as owner occupied and then
       become rentals, noting owner occupancy creates better stability to the community, the units and
       are better cared for. It was noted there are studies that indicate students living in owner-
       occupied units do better in school, and these create a better economic benefit to the community
       as well as to nearby retail uses. It was indicated while there is a need for rental communities,
       there should be incentives to create owner occupancy, noting the particular location may affect
       its desirability as a long-term residential investment. Commission felt incentives to purchase

units include the location of transit, parks and schools, in relation to the housing community, as

Commission then discussed the sustainability of projects, noting architecture often makes a
difference It was indicated the Ogden River Project requires a variety in architectural styles,
allowing the developer to choose from several options, which has created a more interesting
project. In contrast, many of the City initiated projects have been lacking in both variety and
desirability of architectural styles for both single-family and multi-family projects.

Discussion continued relating to the use of bonus points in group dwelling and PRUD projects.
It was noted bonus points are offered for items which should be requirements such as
xeriscaping, covered parking, architecture and access. It was suggested the proximity of parking
in relation to the unit location is also important. Commissioners felt there should be some
benefit over time for the use of bonus density, stating these are often only of benefit to the
developer in the cost of the project, but over time are not a benefit to the project or the
surrounding community. Again, Commission felt variety of design and the appearance over time
is more important than the cost of developing a project. Some projects have a timeless design
while others have such as peculiar design that the time it was built is easily identifiable.

Commission felt is important for developers to know the rules and what is expected of them as
a project is presented. Mr. Spencer stated the City has a weekly Development Review Team
meeting where applicants are encourage to meet with Staff to receive comment relating to not
only ordinance requirements, but standards of other departments, including Building Services
and Engineering, stating many details of development are worked out between Staff and the
developer prior to it appearing on a Planning Commission agenda. Commission discussed
whether there is benefit for the creation of an Architectural Review Committee. Commission felt
the Ordinance could contain appropriate standards, and felt such a committee would only create
another step in the process with little benefit to the community. It was felt items which would
benefit projects are play space, gathering areas and connectivity to other communities and
nearby facilities. It was felt items such as a development theme, finish materials, architectural
variety and grassy play areas create a better project, and while some may cost more for the
developer, they create a better community in the long run. Commission felt the ordinance should
establish architectural criteria, creating a base line of what is expected, with options for bonus
points only if these are built upon. Commission felt the integrity of the building and its physical
appearance has a stronger community benefit than items such as a pool.

Commission then discussed the placement of multi-family projects, stating some commercial
areas might be desirable while others are not. It was indicted there are some inequities between
commercial and residential areas, including increased noise and traffic as well as Ordinance
inequalities. It was quasi-commercial areas may be identified as being more suited for
residential and multi-family tenants might want to be closer to amenities such as transit while
those who own might prefer more open space and a more quiet location. Additionally, there may
be areas in the City where multi-family uses are not appropriate and might be excluded by
Ordinance language. Commission noted while there are some residential developments near

industrial areas, these are typically not desirable as these areas are more congested with traffic.
It is also noted there are often problems when development of multi-family projects empty onto
an already busy street, yet busy streets provide better access to public transit and other services.
Some projects are more desirable for families while others are marketed to single residents.
Commission felt it is important that a sense of community be created, and some areas of the
City already are lacking for services such as a drug store, grocery store or parkland.

Mr. Spencer presented ordinance language relating to apartments in St. George, stating the
number of amenities is based on the number of units, and developers are given a list of potential
amenities and are able to choose what they desire. Commission felt amenities are better
described as enrichments, and may be either indoor or outdoor. If a playground is provided, it
should be centrally located and not at the edge of the project. The current situation is that
Planners can identify preferences, but if there is no ordinance requirement, they cannot make
these requirements. It was suggested if projects are closer to a City park, on-site green space
may be reduced. Commission favored the concept of the number of amenities being based on
either the number of units or other criteria specified by the proposed ordinance. It also was
suggested with the Utah climate that any project more than four units should be required to have
covered parking. It was stated if minimal standards are established, enrichment concepts could
be identified as an option for density bonus points, noting not all amenities would have the same
value. Commission felt minimal standards should include covered parking, usable green space,
walking paths conducive to strollers etc., on-site storage and usable active play space.
Commission also felt the standards should be the same for projects in either the residential or
commercial zone, noting the current regulations favor the development of commercial as multi-
family. On-site storage should be adequate to handle seasonal decorations as well as a minimal
area for toys and equipment of tenants. Commission felt the appearance of facilities in
commercial zones often appear as stark and uninviting when compared to those in residential
areas.

Relating to student housing, Commission felt different standards might be more appropriate,
such as gathering areas, high-speed internet, game rooms, lounge areas and big-screen
television in a central location. Outside green area may be appropriate to be reduced for student
housing.

Commission then discussed affordable housing in relation to low-income housing, stating while
certain amenities may increase both the cost of development and the cost to tenants in terms
of HOA fees, it is important that housing be affordable to either a single-income or two-income
family, and care should be taken to attract residents, particularly students, to Ogden rather than
the outlying communities. Units should be livable without being cost prohibitive. It was
suggested the most affordable living option is an accessory dwelling unit. While Ogden has more
low-income housing than other communities in Weber County, these often are sub-standard, and
tenant may be willing to pay a higher price if there is a better perception of safety or better
amenities are provided.

Commission also discussed the option of reduced unit size, stating these may limit not only who
can live at a given location, but their long-term living arrangements, but also might increase the

   Mr. Spencer asked if the Commission had recognized other inconsistencies between projects in
   commercial and residential zones besides the amount of required landscaping. Finish material,
   architectural design and all other requirements should be consistent independent of the zone
   and any inconsistencies eliminated. Commission expressed there should be no incentives for
   development in commercial zones.

2. Discussion, East Central Community Plan update

   Mr. Spencer stated East Central is the next community plan for an update, and while not the
   oldest, there are some ongoing projects in that area that need to be implemented into the Plan,
   and direction from the community is important as these proceed. These include 9-Rails Arts
   District, the BRT travel line through the community, with the potential development of transit-
   oriented projects in the area. Staff also is suggesting the prohibition of ADU’s be lifted from
   the single-family residential area of East Central. Given the existing situation where public
   meetings are not allowed, Staff is asking if other methods of public outreach might be
   acceptable. He asked if the format used for Southeast Ogden was felt to work well and asked
   for other ideas for the development of the plan.

   Chair Southwick stated Zoom has a feature which allows a large meeting to be held and then
   smaller break-out sessions, but unaware as to whether the City has purchased this version of
   Zoom. It was noted the future sessions will be a webinar format in an effort to allow only those
   that are invited and not be hacked. Commissioner Graf asked if there are statistics available as
   to whether internet services are available in this community. There is concern due to the
   number of citizens who are renters rather than property owners may affect citizen
   participation. Mr. Spencer reviewed the typical plan format has been to conduct an initial town
   meeting where citizens are presented with information about their community, then steering
   committees are formed to discuss in detail the elements of the plan, and then these are then
   presented at a community open house where citizens are again invited to comment.

   Commission favored the website as used in Southeast Ogden, and suggested a series of
   videos be prepared for that website to educate the public about particular items. It also was
   suggested there may be Facebook or other social media groups within the community which
   could be utilized to help spread the word. There also may be other organized groups of
   citizens such as those active in the Trolley District. It was noted an on-line meeting may be
   better attended than a physical meeting as parents are unable to leave young children.
   Commissioner Blaisdell felt the introductory meeting is essential to educate citizens as to the
   purposes of a community plan and how it will affect their neighborhoods. Relating to the
   Southeast Ogden Plan process, Commission suggested the public comment session in the first
   meeting be eliminated, as it seemed to become a gripe session.

   Commission suggested there may be information available at APA’s website relating to the
   effectiveness of digital town meeting participation. While they felt an in-person meeting is
   desirable, it may not be an option.
