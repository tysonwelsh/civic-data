Proposed Executive Municipal Officer Compensation. Scheduling a public
hearing to allow public input regarding proposed Executive Municipal Officer
Compensation
        A memo from Council staff regarding executive municipal officer compensation came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated during the 2024 legislative session, the Utah State Legislature adopted S.B.91 requiring that a
municipal governing body proposing compensation increases for executive municipal officers provide notice of and hold a public
hearing on the proposed increase. The proposed Fiscal Year 2025-2026 Budget includes a proposed COLA of 2.97 percent salary
increase for all public safety and general city employees, which includes executive municipal officers. General employees and
executive officers are eligible for a merit increase of 1.5 percent.
         VICE CHAIR HYER MOVED A PUBLIC HEARING ON THE PROPOSAL BE HELD IN THE CITY
COUNCIL CHAMBERS ON JUNE 17, 2025, AT 6:00 P.M. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER
CHOBERKA, ALL VOTING AYE.

Proposed Ordinance 2025-16 approving the Capital Improvement Plan for
the Fiscal Years 2026-2030
          A memo from the Management Services Department regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2026-2030 Capital
Improvement Plan (CIP) came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Capital Improvement Plan is reviewed
and adopted each year as a planning tool for major capital investments in the City’s physical plant over a rolling five-year period.
Each year’s plan is updated based on the changing needs of the City, funding opportunities, projects completed and other similar
factors. During this year’s budget process, proposed capital projects will be reviewed alongside each department’s proposed
budget and in context with the overall needs and resources available to the City through the budget process. The current proposal
is to have the Council review the proposed Fiscal Year 2026-2030 Capital Improvement Plan, make any changes the Council sees
as necessary, and approve the plan along with the final FY2026 annual budget.
          Chair White then introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2025-16, entitled:
                  “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, approving the Capital Improvement Plan for Ogden City for the Fiscal
                  years 2026-2030, and providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final
                  passage.”

Proposed Ordinance 2025-14 adopting the proposed Tentative Budget of
Ogden City for Fiscal Year 2025-2026
         A memo from Council staff regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2025-2026 Tentative Budget came before the
Council for consideration. The memo stated after holding a public hearing, the City Council will consider adopting the proposed
2025-2026 Tentative Budget. Mayor Nadolski presented his proposed 2025-2026 budget to the Council on May 6, 2025. The
Council has reviewed the proposed budget and has received presentations from all City departments. There are no changes
proposed for the tentative budget; however, there are anticipated changes that will be incorporated into the final budget. The final
budget is anticipated to be considered on June 17, 2025. Tonight’s action for the Council is to adopt the tentative budget and set a
public hearing on the final budget for June 17, 2025.
         Council Assistant Executive Director Symes summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
provide Council staff’s analysis of the proposed FY 2025-2026 budget; his presentation identified publicly accessible resources
that can aid residents in navigating and understanding the budget, provided a comparison of the proposed budget with the FY
2024-2025 adopted budget, stressed no tax increase is proposed as part of the Mayor’s budget and there have been no significant
changes to financial principles or budget goals and guidelines, summarized proposed personnel changes, identified Capital
Improvement Plan (CIP) projects included in the budget, and concluded staff recommends adoption of the proposed budget as the
tentative budget and setting a public hearing for June 17 to consider final adoption of the budget. For a copy of the presentation in
its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
           Chair White introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2025-14, entitled:
                    “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, adopting the proposed tentative budget of Ogden City for Fiscal Year
                    2025-2026.”
           Chair White then opened the public hearing.
           Laura Lewis discussed proposed compensation increases for City employees; she referred to documents that were
published several weeks ago regarding employee compensation and those documents indicated that there are many different types
of pay increases certain employees can receive and, in some cases, employees could potentially receive up to an eight percent
increase. She asked if that is correct. She then asked if employees must meet certain standards or achieve certain goals in order to
receive a merit increase.
           Joseph Bauman stated he attended a meeting last year to discuss the proposed Enterprise Fund transfers and during that
meeting he made comments about the past history of the Ogden Canyon waterline project and why it was not done before now;
the City is paying nearly double the project cost as would have been paid if the project were done 20 years ago. He specifically
asked the Mayor about the Enterprise Fund transfers and the amount of money that is being generated and he believes that if the
City utilized that money to retire bond debt for the waterline project, the bond could be retired much earlier and save the taxpayers
money. He was told that idea would be investigated and would like to know if Administration has made any changes to their plans
in terms of the Enterprise Fund transfer. He then referenced the travel section of the City Council’s budget; from 2025 to 2026 the
budget increased by $80,000 and he would like to know the cause for that increase. He added that page 102 of the budget
document identifies an allocation for professional and technical services at the Marshall White Center in the amount of $237,825
and he inquired as to significant increase when compared to past budgets. He concluded by asking for an explanation of the line
item titled ‘haircut’ in the Marshall White Center budget.
           There being no further persons appearing to be heard, COUNCIL MEMBER RICHEY MOVED THE PUBLIC
HEARING BE CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER GRAF, ALL VOTING AYE.
           Chair White addressed Ms. Lewis’s questions regarding employee compensation; non-sworn employees are eligible for a
1.5 percent merit increase, while sworn public safety employees are eligible for a 4 percent merit increase. All employees will
receive a 2.97 percent cost of living adjustment on top of their merit increases. Mayor Nadolski added that merit increases are
based upon an employee’s performance.
           Chair White then addressed Mr. Bauman’s comments; residents will soon be receiving a notice of the public hearing
scheduled to consider the Enterprise Fund transfers and it will detail the amount of money being transferred from each fund. She
referenced his question about the Marshall White Center budget, noting that all line items for the Center will likely be much
higher than in last year’s budget because the Center has been closed for the past year. The FY 2025-2026 budget is to operate the
Center now that it has been reopened. She asked Council or Administrative staff to answer Mr. Bauman’s question about the
‘haircut’ line item. Finance Director Sorensen stated that there are haircut districts in the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) budget
and the tax increment collected was used to repay debt for the Central Business District (CBD). The districts have expired and
there is a large decrease in the amount that was formerly transferred from the haircut districts to the CBD debt. He indicated he is
happy to visit with Mr. Bauman after the meeting to ensure he answers his questions.
           ON A MOTION BY VICE CHAIR HYER AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER GRAF, ORDINANCE
2025-14 WAS ADOPTED AND A PUBLIC HEARING SET FOR JUNE 17, 2025 AT 6:00 P.M., UPON THE
FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL MEMBERS BLAIR, CHOBERKA, GRAF, MYERS,
RICHEY, VICE CHAIR HYER, AND CHAIR WHITE. VOTING NO – NONE.
           Vice Chair Hyer thanked Administration for their efforts to increase transparency and teamwork on the budget over the
course of the past several months; providing more information and engaging in dialogue much earlier in the year has made the
process much easier and less contentious.

Proposed Ordinance 2025-18 amending Section 11-6-3 of the Municipal
Code to modify provisions related to hazardous environmental conditions, to
make technical changes, and amend the index
         A memo from the Fire Department regarding proposed hazardous environmental conditions amendments came before the
Council for consideration. The memo stated Fire Department Administration proposes to amend Section 11-6-3 of the Municipal
Code to establish a color-coding system to alert the public to the varying levels of risk of fire ignition in hazardous areas and
impose restrictions based on the identified risk level.
         Fire Marshal Brown summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the proposed
code amendments as follows:
              • Adds new definition for “hazardous areas” and removes definitions that are being replaced. Hazardous areas are
                  defined as:
                       o Mountainous, brush covered, forest-covered, or dry grass-covered areas, including wooded areas
                           along the Ogden and Weber River Parkway.
                       o Areas within 200 feet of waterways, trails and canyons or similar areas.
                       o      Wildland urban interface areas where structures meet or intermingle with undeveloped wildland
                              including all areas east of Harrison Boulevard from the north city boundary to the south city boundary
                         o A limited area outside these defined hazardous areas to facilitate a readily identifiable closed area.
               • Simplifies definition of “ignition source”.
               • Removes current prohibition on use of ignition source.
               • Adds new section establishing Fire Restriction Levels to allow the fire marshal to determine and declare the fire
                    restriction level for hazardous areas to reflect the risk of fire ignition in those areas based on the currently
                    existing environmental conditions:
                         o Yellow – Elevated Conditions. Prohibits aerial fireworks and any ignition source in non-landscaped
                              areas, areas that are not continuously attended, or without a means of extinguishment. Allows small
                              open-flame fire pits, outdoor appliances (grills, etc.), and ground level fireworks and sparklers.
                         o Orange – Critical Conditions. Prohibits any open flames, all fireworks, and any ignition source in non-
                              landscaped areas, areas that are not continuously attended, or areas without a means of
                              extinguishment. Allows use of fire pits and outdoor appliances if used in areas that are landscaped,
                              continuously attended, and equipped with a means of extinguishment.
                         o Red – Severe Conditions. Prohibits use of all ignition sources in designated hazardous areas.
               • Makes technical changes to noticing requirements to align with state statute.
          Mr. Brown concluded he and Fire Department Administration are requesting the adoption of this ordinance prior to the
2025 fire season.
          Chair White asked if the status quo will be green, which means there are no restrictions. Mr. Brown answered yes, and he
referred to the text above to explain what the yellow, orange, and red categories should signal to the public. He also discussed how
notification of a fire restriction level change will occur.
          Council member Graf asked if there may ever be an instance where the entire City will be assigned red level in terms of
restrictions. Mr. Brown stated that would be very rare, but there have been City-wide restrictions in the past based upon drought
levels and significant risk. Council member Graf stated he attended a meeting of the Utah League of Cities and Towns (ULCT)
recently and there was some discussion about House Bill (HB) 48, which was also discussed during the May 20 work session
when this proposal was first presented to the Council. He stated he learned that property owners in areas identified as Wildland-
Urban will be assessed a fee or a tax increase that is intended to finance the Wildland-Urban Interface Prevention, Preparedness,
and Mitigation Fund. Property owners can mitigate those fees by inviting the Fire Department to visit their property and perform
an assessment of wildland fuel, but the bill does not address ignition sources. He is very concerned about the shortcomings of the
legislation, namely the fact that it does not even address fireworks as an ignition source, even though 90 percent of wildland fires
are caused by humans and fireworks are in the top five ignition sources. He suggested City Administration and residents lobby
their State representatives for a law change because the City cannot prohibit fireworks unless allowed to do so by the State. He
stated he is concerned about fire events similar to those that have occurred in other states happening in Ogden City during
firework season. Mr. Brown thanked Council member Graf for his input and noted that the fees assessed as a result of HB 48 are
determined by the State and the City has no direct control over that matter.
          Chair White introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2025-18, entitled:
                    “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Section 11-6-3 to
                    modify provisions related to hazardous environmental conditions; to make technical changes; to amend the
                    index; and by providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final
                    passage.”
          ON A MOTION BY VICE CHAIR HYER AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER BLAIR, ORDINANCE
2025-18 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL MEMBERS
BLAIR, CHOBERKA, GRAF, MYERS, RICHEY, VICE CHAIR HYER, AND CHAIR WHITE. VOTING NO – NONE.
          Council member Choberka stated she shares Council member Graf’s concerns about fireworks as a wildland fire ignition
source and added that fireworks are often lit during times of bad air quality, which only makes that problem worse.

Public Comments
          Joseph Bauman referred to his earlier comments made during the public hearing regarding the tentative budget; his
question regarding the ‘haircut’ item was in reference to a budget note on page 242 of the budget entitled ‘haircut allocation to rec
center’. He stated he does not understand why a line item would be called a ‘haircut’ if it were referring to a reduction of some
source of funding. The dollar amount of the line item is $459,000 and he would like to have that addressed. He then noted that he
understands that the Marshall White Center only recently re-opened, but there still should have been some type of budget for
professional and technical services in Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025, but there was not.
          Laura Lewis referenced past discussions about the Mt. Ogden Golf Course and the presence of a public trail on the
course; she understands that the Mayor will make the final decision on the matter, and she stated she feels it is a very bad idea to
make the golf course a mixed-use facility. She spent some time at the golf course over the weekend and in talking with golfers she
learned new information about a golfer’s liability. She always assumed that if a golfer hit someone or hit a building with their golf
ball, the golf course would cover it; however, that is not always true, and the golfer is the first person approached to cover the
costs of any damage or injuries caused by their activities. If the City continues to allow a public trail crossing on the golf course,
the City is putting all golfers at risk of increased liability. She then stated that another person who lives near the golf course is
concerned about homeless people moving into the area; there are already people living in the foothills and increasing public
accessibility to the area will also increase the homeless population. She acknowledged a significant number of signatures of
people who want the public trail crossing on the golf course to be preserved, but she is opposed to that arrangement and asked the
Council to consider how to handle increased traffic, parking, and security of the area.
         Heath Satow stated that while he disagrees with Ms. Lewis’s position on the golf course, he appreciates her taking the
time and effort to visit the golf course and speak with people who are familiar with the situation. He then stated that he appreciates
Council member Graf’s comments about fireworks and urging caution relative to fire conditions in the coming months.
         Chair White referenced Mr. Bauman’s comments and stated that the explanation that Mr. Sorensen offered earlier in the
meeting regarding the ‘haircut’ line item was an accurate explanation; the rec center is actually the Solomon Center in the Central
Business District (CBD), and not the Marshall White Center as she believes Mr. Bauman is assuming.

Mayor Comments
          Mayor Nadolski thanked Mr. Sorensen for his willingness to visit with Mr. Bauman to answer his questions regarding the
tentative budget. He then acknowledged the efforts of City staff during his recent absence; he was gone when the Marshall White
Center was reopened, and he thanked City staff for making that event possible and completing the project for the community. He
also referenced Council member Graf’s comments about fireworks and stated he shares many of his concerns; based upon his past
private employment, he has a very good understanding of wildfire risks and ignition sources, and he also lives in the wildland-
urban area of the City. Ogden’s conditions are unique, and he will continue to advocate for more local control of fire restrictions
for Ogden. He also referenced Ms. Lewis’s comments about Mt. Ogden Golf Course and stated he shares her concerns about risk
to golfers and the liability they assume when they engage in the activity; the City owes it to golfers to not put them at increased
risk or putting others at risk by allowing a public trail crossing on the golf course. He shares the concerns about increased
homelessness in the area as well. He continues to engage with the group that has been concerned about the issue and there will be
a very robust public engagement process before a final decision is made on the matter. Fencing along the golf course is being
repaired, but a gate has not been installed and will not be installed until a final decision is made. He reiterated his gratitude to Fire
Marshal Brown and Deputy Chief Willis for their service to the City and wished them best in their future endeavors.

Council member Comments
         Chair White stated tonight she is wearing a jacket for Pride Month; she stated pride matters to her. Someone recently
asked her if an entire month should be dedicated to the matter and why the initiative is being pushed on others. She offered an
analogy regarding why Pride Month is important; many people have deep pride in the school they attended or the teams they
belonged to. That pride is what creates a sense of belonging and that is what Pride Month creates for the LGBTQ community; this
community is not asking for preferential treatment or favors, but they are asking for fairness, safety, and unity from a place of
love. She knows that not everyone sees the issue the same as her and she respects their opinions, but it is important to treat one
another with kindness, decency, and compassion. She did some research on the LGBTQ community today and found 11.8 percent
of youth today identify as not heterosexual; in Utah, 50 percent of LGBTQ youth have considered suicide and 13 percent of them
have actually attempted suicide. These are not just statistics, but real kids and many of them are in the Ogden community and she
feels community leaders have the responsibility to stand for belonging and inclusion. This should not be a political issue, but it
should be about people. Pride month is a chance to tell everyone they belong no matter who they love.
