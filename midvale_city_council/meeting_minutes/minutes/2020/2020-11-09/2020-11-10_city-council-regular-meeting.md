Nate Rockwood discussed the FAR Standards Floor Area Ratio.
Far Standards Floor Area Ratio
¢ FAR= Floor Area - Lot Area
* The ratio is constant for a zone. A floor area ratio of 1.0 means that floor area may
equal lot area. FAR 5.0 means that the floor area may be up to five times as large
as the lot area; and FAR 0.5 that it may be no more than half the lot area.
* An FAR is set to work with other controls, such as setbacks, height, etc. and is
used to regulate volume or density.
* lf used correctly a FAR will allow more flexibility within a buildable envelop for
architects and developers.
* Has traditionally been used in urban areas for office and high-density housing.
¢ While the concept has been used since the 1950s, it is still considered a new and
creative way of making zoning ordinances more flexible.
* More recently used to incentivize additional density in residential zones while
maintaining building envelope limits on lots.
¢ tis a way to allow an area/zone to slowly transform over time.
¢ Los Angeles — Minneapolis — Portland
¢ Itcan become overly complicated with multiple controls and regulations.

Kyle Maurer discussed the Utility Billing Estimate Update.
Overview
* City has had long-term issues with premature meter failure (approximately 3,628
meters affected).
* Meters should have 20-year life; some failing at year 12.
* February 2020 — City Council approves 3-year contract for replacement of failed
meters (requiring approximately $200K per year).
* March 2020 — Xpress Bill Pay website down during peak times; autopayments not
run for 1 cycle.
* September 2020 — City had utility billing error where 94% of accounts west of State
Street were estimated.
* Numerous posts on social media/calls about amount of bills.
* What has the City done to address meter failure issue? Is this enough?
* How can the City prevent large-scale billing errors moving forward?
* What is an acceptable number of meter estimates? How can we achieve that goal?
* How can the City educate residents on their utility bills?
How Reads are Collected
* Utility billing exports a list of meters to read through the utility billing system
(Caselle).
* This export file is then imported into the meter reading software (from the meter
manufacturer, Neptune).
* As the Meter Reading Technician drives down the street (15 mph max) (or
approaches the meter), reads are collected.
* Reads are transmitted through radio frequency.

Transmitters are attached to the meter’s register, and data is transmitted through radio
frequency to a data collector (meter reading software). Read is sent every 15 seconds.
* Once collected by the data collector (meter reading software), a file is created to
be imported into the utility billing system (Caselle).
* Utility billing system imports this file into the utility billing system.
Challenges Collecting Reads
¢ Radio frequency systems are considered “line of sight” systems.
* Signal can be blocked by structural elements
* Trees, buildings (lathe/plaster), telephone poles, chain link fences, ete.
¢ Water Meter Technician must be close enough to collect reads.
* Meter signal gets weaker over time as register ages.
* Issues with private HOA’s changing gate codes.
* May not be close enough to collect a read.
* Register or battery failure.
- If register fails due to battery, unable to collect a read from the meter.

B. CONSIDER ORDINANCE NO. 2020-00-19 MODIFYING PARKING
STANDARDS IN THE STATE STREET (SSC) ZONE
Alex Murphy said in 2001, following the City’s annexation of the Fort Union area, the City’s
zoning ordinance was rewritten to fold the City and County zoning ordinances together.
As part of this effort, the State Street (SSC) Zone was created and given the following
goals:
A. Facilitate transition of the State Street corridor from underdeveloped commercial
parcels to a more vibrant mix of commercial and residential uses;
B. Provide an identifiable entry corridor for the city;
C. Assure and enhance the investment of State Street property owners;
D. Promote economic development;
E. Protect surrounding neighborhoods from the potential adverse effects of more
intense land uses; and
F. Require thematic landscaping to provide a distinctive visual quality to the area.
Consistent with these goals, the SSC Zone was written to encourage mixed-use
development by removing procedural barriers to mixed-use development. However, the
Zone does not currently establish a minimum parking requirement for the residential
component of a mixed-use project, a standard seen in almost all the City’s other zones.
In combination with the relaxation of other standards in the Zone, this could lead to
projects that provide inadequate parking and significant impacts on the public street
network and adjacent properties/neighborhoods. These impacts would be inconsistent
with the goal of the Zone to protect surrounding neighborhoods from adverse impacts.
To temporarily address the issue, the City Council adopted Ordinance No. 2020-0-08
(attached), establishing temporary parking regulations for mixed-use developments in the
SSC zone. Under this temporary ordinance, mixed-use developments are required to
provide parking for the residential component using the same rate as the RM-12 and RM-

by the board member’s governing body.
