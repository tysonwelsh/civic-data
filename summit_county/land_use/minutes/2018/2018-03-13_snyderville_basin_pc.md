1. Presentation of the 2018 major transportation projects in the Snyderville Basin-
Derrick Radke, Public Works Director   
 
Public Works Director, Derrick Radke, said he would like to share the transportation 
projects planned for the Snyderville Basin for the upcoming year.  He introduced Gary 
Horton who is the consultant working with the projects.   
 
Director Radke said the County Council adopted a strategic goal and mantra of: 
“A transportation system that connects people to jobs, services and communities 
while limiting congestion, utilizing solutions that do not involve adding more 
pavement to the road network just for the sake of increasing capacity.  Additional 
lanes and pavement and intersection improvements must involve some form of 
transit improvement or other alternative modes of transportation. “  
 
Director Radke said as they go through the different projects, they will see the 
connection.  The Jeremy round-about project has been needed for years.  Funding is 
coming together for the project.  Two other projects have been identified by Staff 
and the County Council as projects that could be implemented early due to the 
transportation sales tax funding.

2. Discussion regarding amendments to the Development Code to allow food trucks 
in certain areas of the Snyderville Basin- Ray Milliner, Principal Planner  
Planner Milliner reminded the Commission they looked at this in November.  Staff has 
made some changes to the language based on the feedback that was given at that 
meeting.  He is requesting input on the refined verbiage.  He added that the State 
legislature recently passed a bill that gives direction to municipalities and counties 
regarding what can and cannot be regulated with food trucks.  The ordinance is divided 
between food trucks and food courts.   
 
FOOD TRUCKS 
Planner Milliner said a State regulation does not allow a food truck to be located within 
100 feet of a brick and mortar restaurant.  The State regulation says that food trucks 
cannot be prohibited from zones where restaurants and delis are allowed.  He 
described other items connected with business licenses.   
 
Planner Milliner reviewed some of the language changes for the County Ordinance.  One 
was at the request of the Health Department.  For sanitation purposes, they would like 
to prohibit food trucks from parking within 100 feet of a restroom facility.  Chair Peck 
said it seems they would want to have bathrooms accessible.  Commissioner Stevens 
asked what would constitute a restroom facility.  Would that only include a honey-pot?  
Someone parked outside of a store like Michaels which has a restroom, should not be a 
problem.  Planner Milliner said he would talk with the Health Department about this. 
 
Planner Milliner said there was a language change that was made at the Commission’s 
request.  Although a food truck could not set up in a residential neighborhood, it could 
be hired to provide food for a party.  Another proposed regulation is a food truck could 
not be in one location for more than 12 hours within a 24 hour period.  There would be 
no overnight parking.  Other regulations were about signage, garbage, and recycling.

3. Discussion regarding amendments to the Snyderville Basin Development Code for 
Accessory Dwelling Units- Ray Milliner, Principal Planner   
 
Planner Milliner said this agenda item is to begin work on an amendment that will 
regulate accessory dwelling units in the Snyderville Basin.  The language proposes these 
are approved as rentals for 30 days or more.  The language before the Commission is a 
very rough draft of proposed Code Amendments.   
 
Staff is proposing the deletion of the existing language about agricultural employee 
dwelling units.  These are generally used for ranch hands.  The Snyderville Basin 
doesn’t have a lot of large ranches anymore.  The need no longer seems to exist; 
therefore, it is suggested this be removed from the Code.   
 
Chair Peck asked if Summit County has the ability to curtail nightly rentals.  Director 
Putt said that nightly rentals are permitted on both the west and east sides of the

Planner Milliner said a purpose statement has been added because it is important for 
the public to understand why this is being done.  A prime reason the Commission is 
allowing accessory units is because it is a quick and easy way to provide affordable 
housing.  If someone adds an accessory dwelling to an existing home it cuts down on 
traffic without increasing the built environment.
