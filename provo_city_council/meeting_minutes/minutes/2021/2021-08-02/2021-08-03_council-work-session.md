Administrator, presented. Mr. Hansen shared several updates from the hospital. Misinformation
has been a considerable challenge over the last 18 months, so they are grateful for the
opportunity to speak to the Council and community members today. Mr. Hansen recognized the
many amazing healthcare workers in Utah who have faced unprecedented circumstances over the
course of the pandemic. Mr. Hansen shared COVID-19 admissions and mortality data from 2020
(prior to the availability of vaccines). They saw a decrease in admissions as vaccination became
more prevalent in the community, but in recent months they have seen an increase in admissions

whether there were specific age groups or demographics among which the Council help promote
vaccination. Mr. Hansen referred this question to Dr. Ford, but he indicated that they are seeing
younger demographics in the ICU, such as patients in their 20s, 30s, and 40s. They have seen
higher populations of the Latinx population admitted to the hospital and who are not vaccinated.
The Latinx community and younger demographics would be helped by additional messaging.

Dr. Larry Ford, infectious disease specialist, presented. He is a member of the Provo community
and is concerned about how our communities are struggling with the pandemic. To date,
COVID-19 has resulted in over 613,000 deaths and over 35 million cases nationwide. The delta
variant is a different virus in its characteristics and comprises over 90% of Utah cases. It is a
worse virus due to its increased viral load, and therefore increased transmissibility. Carriers are
contagious sooner, and there is greater illness severity and mortality. The data is still emerging,
but thus far shows much younger people who are much sicker than hospital caseloads earlier in
the pandemic. Three vaccines are available: two MRNA (Pfizer and Moderna) and the single-
dose viral vector Johnson & Johnson. The vaccines are well-established as safe and effective.
Intermountain has had more than 94% of admissions of individuals who were unvaccinated.
Serious side effects of vaccines are very rare. Dr. Ford briefly reviewed several potential severe
side effects of the different vaccines. The rare side effects are actual potential side effects of the
COVID-19 infection. The very low risk of potential side effects from the vaccine is much lower
than the potential risk of side effects from natural infection. Vaccination always has a better side
effect profile than the actual infection. Dr. Ford reviewed some of the rampant misinformation
(or “non-evidentiary sources of information”) which has been perpetuated about the vaccines.
Dr. Ford reiterated the core message that vaccines are the answer. He would welcome the City’s
encouragement and support of vaccinations among the City, businesses, and education sectors.

Councilor Bill Fillmore thanked Dr. Ford for his presentation. He asked about the duration of
immunity for the vaccines. Dr. Ford indicated that right now, there is no short-term planning for
boosters but they may be needed if variants continue to evolve. Mr. Fillmore asked how
confident they are with the three vaccines and their ability to guard against variants. Dr. Ford
indicated that the vaccines continue to be tested with new variants. Mr. Fillmore asked about

3.      A presentation discussing a proposed ordinance for transferring private ownership
        of streets and utilities to public ownership of streets and utilities. (21-087) (1:11:35)

David Day, Development Engineering Coordinator, presented. This item arose from an HOA in
the City who had expressed interest in transferring private roads and utilities to Provo City for
the City to own and maintain. Provo City does not have a process in place to dictate how this
should proceed, so Public Works has prepared an ordinance draft to address this type of request.
A draft of the ordinance has been circulated to the Council for their review. Mr. Day explained
the process they have suggested for how such applications would be considered in the future.

Councilor David Harding noted the proposal was a change from the current policy for the City
not to take on roads privately developed as part of a subdivision plat process. He suggested that
in cases of a compelling public interest, the City should be proactive about making those
conversions. In situations where it is either neutral or against the public interest, he hoped that if
it were against the public interest, that the City would not pursue it. Councilor Shannon
Ellsworth asked how often a planned road did not meet the City’s design standards. Mr. Day
noted that there used to be a loophole in the code, but the City changed the public and private
roads standard in the 1980s so that all roads were built to the same standards.

Brian Jones, City Attorney, shared that the drafted ordinance was based on the process employed
by Salt Lake City. In response to a question from Mr. Jones, Mr. Harding indicated that a revised
draft of the proposal resolved that outstanding question. Councilor David Sewell thought this
situation was in many cases a matter of public health and safety. If an HOA has not maintained a
fire hydrant, that could create a problematic situation. Where there are private and public lines
that are interconnected, this could also create problematic situations; if the lack of maintenance
of private water or sewer lines on the part of an HOA caused impacts to the members of the
HOA and residents beyond its boundaries, that would be problematic. Mr. Sewell suggested that
in such cases, the City should consider converting such infrastructure to City ownership.

Councilor David Shipley reiterated Ms. Ellsworth’s comment about whether the code motivates
developers to create private roads rather than public ones. Mr. Shipley was concerned if the
current setup motivated an end product that resulted in future issues for the City when an HOA
desired conversion of the infrastructure to public ownership. Mr. Day indicated that in gated
communities, this was sometimes an issue, however the roads in such developments were built to
the same standard as other subdivisions. As Mr. Sewell mentioned, some of the things that make
the City the most nervous are having a private water or sewer line tied to the city system. In
response to Mr. Shipley’s and Mr. Harding’s question about developers skirting the rules or
standards, Mr. Day indicated that part of the criteria for evaluating a transfer proposal would be
to determine whether the infrastructure meets the City’s standards—was it built to the same
quality, what is the condition of the facilities, has the infrastructure been maintained, and what
would the City be taking over? Councilors were generally supportive of the concept so long as
appropriate safeguards were in place. Mr. Day hoped the Council could share their feedback.
This proposal would proceed as an ordinance text amendment through the normal process for
review by the Planning Commission and subsequently the Council. Presentation only.

4.      A presentation regarding City programs that are funded through CDBG and
        HOME funds. (21-050) (1:41:54)

Cindy Sweeten, Housing Division Administrator, and Dan Gonzalez, CDBG Administrator,
presented. Community Development Block Grants, or CDBG, is a federally funded program
which provides resources to address a wide range of housing and community development needs.
CDBG funds must be specifically used to benefit low- and moderate-income areas. Ms. Sweeten
outlined details of how CDBG dollars have been used in Provo. She highlighted several
programs in Provo and information about the programs, including:
     Egress Window program (in place since 2016)
     Emergency Home Repair Program (in place for over 30 years)
     Neighborhood Revitalization Program
     Commercial Façade Investment Program (in place for over 35 years)

Ms. Sweeten also shared details about the HOME Investment Partnership Program. The HOME
program frequently partners with other housing organizations in Provo and the broader
community. Provo’s main HOME program is the Home Purchase Plus Program. Councilor
David Shipley asked how that would work; Ms. Sweeten indicated that would be shown as a lien
on the house when the owner sold the home. The owner would repay the full amount of that loan
after moving out of the house. Own In Provo is another program with a similar purpose for home
purchases with higher incomes than the HPP program. Mr. Shipley thanked Ms. Sweeten for the
information. He wondered how many loans they granted for the Own In Provo program and
whether it was still an impactful program in spite of the smaller loan amounts. Ms. Sweeten said
they recently closed on a loan for an individual who made just $5000 over the HPP income limit;
the homeowner was very grateful to receive the loan.

Mr. Gonzalez shared details about the small business loans that were part of the CARES Act
funds disbursed by the City. The City has $566,776 for small business loans which could provide
up to 37 businesses with assistance. As of a few weeks ago, they had not issued any of those
loans. The requirements from the CARES Act funds can be complicated. Similar to CDBG, it
must assist low- or moderate-income individuals, the intent is to create jobs, and there are further
requirements, including businesses needing to create a DUNS number and register on the federal
award system management site. There are also other requirements that have to be fulfilled. Other
funding is also available. Mr. Gonzalez has been in contact with other funding sources, including
Utah County and Mountainland Association of Governments. MAG’s program had much lesser
requirements; 276 businesses in Provo benefitted from MAG’s program by receiving a loan. Mr.
Shipley asked where small businesses can find information if they are interested in considering
getting a loan from Provo City. Mr. Gonzalez explained that it was not posted in a specific place
but the information is available for someone who calls and asks. Councilor David Harding
wondered if any of the funds could be used for some of the initiatives to promote vaccination.
Mr. Gonzalez indicated that it would probably qualify. Presentation only.
