Study Session

1.0   Discussion: Overnight Winter Parking Regulations

1.1   Scott Cooley, Engineering/Public Works Director explained that before the last
      set of changes made in spring of 2020, the City’s ordinance prohibited parking
      on the streets from November 1st through April 15th between certain hours of
      the day. The following language was determined: “No person shall park or
      leave a vehicle or trailer on any highway while snow is on the street from
      November 1st through April 30th.” Mr. Cooley said this was consistent with
      other codes, including the State’s.

      Mr. Cooley said staff tried not to restrict parking in residential areas as much
      as possible, though there were exceptions. For example, residential areas
      near trailheads or schools had some restrictions.

2.0   Discussion: Special Event Ordinance

2.1   Rhett Ogden, Parks and Recreation Director, stated that some changes to the
      City’s Special Event Ordinance were prompted in light of COVID. This was an
      opportunity for the Council to share its thoughts with staff. Trail usage had
      increased and the staff was mainly looking at limiting use around the canyon
      and surrounding areas. Exclusions would include public streets, reservable
      public parks, and paved trails.

      Councilmember T. Lowery complimented staff on managing a race over the
      weekend, acknowledging the complexity of the task. Councilmember F. Lowry
      asked if the route for the race was in the restricted area presented by Mr.
      Ogden, to which Mr. Ogden answered affirmatively. The City could continue
      holding its events on City property; however, private events would be
      restricted.

      David Dobbins, City Manager, said staff had discussed differentiating between
      users. The easiest way to do this was to say “only City events.” Exceptions,

      how many events this language would be eliminating. Mr. Ogden said
      approximately eight to 10 per year in a non-pandemic world.

      Councilmember Green stated that he thought the use of the subject areas

3.0   Council/Manager Reports

3.1   Mr. Dobbins mentioned the playground at Draper Park. Staff budgeted $1.5
      million: $750,000 from the park impact fee fund, $750,000 from the General
      Fund, and $550,000 preliminary funds from Salt Lake County. The current
      budget for the playground is $1.3 million; therefore, the City could potentially
      complete this project using the funds from Salt Lake County and the City’s park
      impact fees. Mr. Dobbins provided a tentative outline of what equipment the
      park would entail.

      Councilmember F. Lowry asked what kind of liability was there on rope
      pyramids. Mr. Dobbins said staff would consult the City’s risk management
      group on how to protect itself from possible claims. Staff and Council then
      discussed possible locations for new pickleball courts as well.

7.0   Action Item: Resolution #21-53, approving an agreement to provide Draper
      Irrigation Company/Water Pro with its reuse water to use for secondary water
      purposes.

7.1   Mr. Cooley stated that this item was previously presented to the Council for its
      consideration. Since that time, the City’s attorneys had been working with
      Water Pro’s attorneys to determine a contract that would accomplish the
      purpose outlined above. Mr. Cooley highlighted several components of that
      contract.

7.2   Councilmember T. Lowery said she appreciated the opportunity to reuse this
      water. She asked what would happen as they sent less and less water down
      to the Great Salt Lake. Mr. Cooley explained that for the most part, this would
      help facilitate water quality improvement. It would replace existing water
      coming straight out of the canal with this treated water from Water Pro;
      therefore, the canal water would continue to flow to the Great Salt Lake. In
      the case of Galena Park, new water would be taken but it would also conserve
      culinary water that was currently being used.

      Councilmember F. Lowry stated that if the City were to construct its water
      reuse facility, the City would be limited in terms of what water it could reuse.
