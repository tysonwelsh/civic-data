1.      Call to Order:
Mayor Dahle called the meeting to order at 6:02 p.m. He read the Order on Public Meetings of
the City Council for public meetings of electronic means, and briefly explained the intent of the
retreat as (1) review past priorities and goals, (2) review financials, and (3) set priorities and
goals for the upcoming year, including a continuation of the Council’s discussion of a potential
property tax increase.

2.     Review Progress on FY2020-21 Goals:
 City Manager Gina Chamness shared a document with the current year goals, edited to indicate
 completed or items no longer considered to move forward and organized by priority areas. City
 Manager Chamness reviewed the highlights of each section.

        a. Economic Development– explore opportunities for further redevelopment: Both
        establishing a redevelopment area for the Holladay Crossroads and forming a real estate
        committee were removed. Ms. Chamness noted the recently passed American Rescue
        Plan (APR) Act, and the possibility of earmarks returning. The evaluation of economic
        development conditions remains a priority, and to move forward, funding for the study is
        needed in the budget. The sale of the Cotton Bottom and subsequent reopening was noted
        as a successfully completed project.
        b. City Organization and Administration: The 911 outreach is complete. The City
        hired an emergency manager; relocation of the hired individual now requires the selection
        of a new candidate, likely in June. The pandemic delayed the implementation of events,
        like the proposed lecture series. Significant progress was made on fulfilling the MS4
        permit requirements.
        c. Develop a Sustainable Funding Model for the City: The Council-appointed Citizen
        Advisory Group has fulfilled their charge to evaluate the City’s financial condition,
        unmet, project needs, and funding tools to recommend a sustainable financial plan. That
        plan included a recommendation for the implementation of a storm water utility fee,
        which went through a Council public process in Fall 2020 and subsequently approved by
        the Council.

3.      Review FY 2020-21 Financial Picture: City Manager Chamness shared a Wasatch
Front Regional Council (WFRC) tool that provides sales tax data for 2019 v. 2020 by quarter for
comparison for each jurisdiction. She noted the stunning growth in non-store retail category (on-
line sales) over that past year. This growth makes future revenue projection difficult.
Additionally, the amount of general fund balance legally permitted by units of local government
has increased from 25% to 35% as a result of recent legislation passed in the 2021 State of Utah
Legislative Session. Holladay’s balance is currently about 31%, largely due to the influx of the
on-line sales tax. There is approximately $5 million in General Fund Balance, and $5.8 million in
Capital Fund Balance. The Citizen Advisory Group’s recommended sustainable financial plan
considered spending $3 million dollars of fund balance in year 1, and $2-3 million in year 2. The
increase in fund balance will be updated in the financial model to calculate any changes to a
potential property tax increase. The City is also awaiting more guidance on the American Rescue
Plan funding.

4.      Determine FY 2021-22 Budget Assumptions and Goals: City Manager Gina Chamness
shared a document with the proposed priorities for FY 2021-22 goals, organized by priority
areas. City Manager Chamness reviewed the highlights of each section.

        a. Economic Development: The upcoming year will focus on completing a study to
        evaluate economic development potential in the community and explore funding
        opportunities provided by CARES and American Rescue Plan (APR). The funding may
        provide an opportunity to expand broadband in the estates and Canyon Cove
        neighborhoods.
        b. City Organization and Administration: The Holladay Chamber of Commerce has
        approached the City to consider a new model – a Holladay Business Council, and the
        City will continue to work with the Chamber to explore this option. City staff will also
        assess the organizational structure and staffing in key areas, including Administration and
        Community Development. The implementation of credit card as payment option will also
        extend to business licensing and room/park rentals. The City will also complete the
        implementation of electronic plans submission. Lastly, staff will continue working on
        ordinance changes to comply with statutory changes made by State Legislature.
        c. Develop a Sustainable Funding Model for the City: To implement the new storm
        water utility fee, staffing recommendations are currently being developed for FY 2021-
        22. The model assumes addition of FTEs, including engineering function and technician
        to fully implement program. Other priorities include the implementation of a potential

5. Ongoing Property Tax Discussion: The Council reviewed the recommendation of the
Citizen Advisory Group. Discussion ensued. Council Members weighed using additional fund
balance to lower the potential property tax increase amount and wanting to have a proposal they
can defend and support while still meeting the unmet needs. They discussed the merits and
challenges of phasing the increase or proceeding with a larger, upfront increase. Council
Members expressed a strong desire to hear from more residents. Messaging will be critical.
Community may misunderstand that the funding from the American Rescue Plan (APR) Act will
provide funding to meet unmet needs. The reality is that ARP funds are very limited in use, and
can’t be used on roadway projects, as an example. There was general consensus to infuse more
fund balance into the model, consider additional data, and review potential options before
deciding on a preliminary proposal to share with the public.

6. Adjourn: There being no further business, Council Member Paul Fotheringham moved to
adjourn, Council Member Drew Quinn seconded the motion. The Council voted in the
affirmative and the meeting adjourned at 8:48 p.m.
