Public Comment 0:08:04

Chair MacKay read the public comment preamble and opened the public comment period.

Andrew Thompson, a Provo resident of 43 years, addressed the council regarding the urgent need for a
full-time employee to oversee ADA (Americans with Disabilities Act) matters in the city. While he
acknowledged that Provo has better accessibility than other areas in the county, he emphasized that
significant improvements are still needed. He highlighted issues such as UTA buses not being accessible,
utility poles on sidewalks, and many businesses and restaurants having barriers for wheelchair users.
Thompson expressed frustration over the city's spending on art installations, such as the $73,000
allocated for signs at the Rec Center and on University Avenue, suggesting that taxpayer money would
be better spent on accessibility needs. He specifically mentioned accessibility challenges at Rock Canyon
and Pioneer Park, calling for action to address these concerns.

Bruce Fillmore, a resident of American Fork and owner of condominium in Provo, shared his concerns
with the council regarding a city policy limiting the number of residents per unit. He explained that in
1993, when he purchased the condominium, he was allowed to have four people living there. However,
about 10 years later, the city reduced the limit to three people per unit due to parking concerns.
Fillmore feels this is unfair, as the rule is based on the number of cars rather than the number of
residents, and not everyone owns a car. He suggested that this policy be reconsidered, especially since
housing remains a significant issue in Provo.

for their efforts and shared positive feedback from visitors. He mentioned that Provo has hosted large

Action Agenda

1.         An ordinance amending the Zone Map classification of approx. 1.53 acres of real property,
           generally located at 484 N 2760 W, from the Agricultural (A1.5) zone to the One-Family
           Residential (R1.8) Zone. Lakeview South Neighborhood. (PLRZ20240184) 0:14:27

     Motion:       An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2024-44, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Mary Barnes, Planner, presented. She explained that the rezone request involves a property with a
portion zoned for agricultural use. The owners are seeking to rezone it to R1-8, a residential zoning
commonly found in the surrounding area. This change would allow them to subdivide the property into
two lots instead of keeping it as a single lot.

Chair MacKay asked if they chose to do something else, would it be required to come back to council for
approval.

Ms. Barnes clarified that if the rezone is approved, any development on the property must comply with
the R1-8 zoning requirements, just like all other properties in the neighborhood. She emphasized that
the rezoned lots would be used for single-family homes, ensuring consistency with the surrounding area.

Chair MacKay opened the public comment period. With no comments, and no other discussion from
council, she called for a vote.

     Vote:         The motion passed 7:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, Handley, Hoban,
                   MacKay, and Whipple in favor.
