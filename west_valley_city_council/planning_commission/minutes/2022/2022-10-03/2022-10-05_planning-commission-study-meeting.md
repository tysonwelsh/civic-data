GENERAL PLAN CHANGE APPLICATIONS
GP-2-2022 Petition by WEST VALLEY CITY to amend the City’s General Plan by updating the
          Major Street Plan to 1) extend Lester Street (5700 West) as a 66’ right-of-way north of
          2400 South, 2) add 7200 West as an 80’ right-of-way between 2100 South and SR-201,
          and 3) extend 5900 West as a 66’ right-of-way between Beagley Road (2540 South)
          and Parkway Blvd. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-
          ut.gov)


Discussion:   Steve explained that our Public Works Department is requesting an update to the Major
              Street Plan. He stated that he would discuss the three changes proposed and the
              reasoning behind each change.

              Steve presented the current Major Street Plan and explained that it is used to identify
              the alignments and right-of-way widths of the City’s major streets. Steve mentioned
              that the Major Street Plan is also used as a guide to determine the right-of-way needed
              as development occurs. He then presented the Major Street Plan showing the location
              of the proposed changes. Next, Steve reviewed the concept plan for extending Lester
              Street to 5600 West.

              Steve showed a map he obtained from the Wasatch Front Regional Council’s website.
              He explained that the Wasatch Front Regional Council is an association of governments
              that plan transportation improvements on a regional level. Steve said there are plans for
              7200 West to extend up to I-80 and beyond. Therefore, adding 7200 West as an 80’
              right-of-way would keep the road consistent with future plans to extend 7200 West.
              Steve noted that he was concerned that truck traffic would have the opportunity to
              travel down Parkway Boulevard. The Public Works Department pointed out that
              extending 5900 West would create a road connection that would provide a more direct
              route to the Mountain View Corridor. Therefore, deterring the truck traffic from
              Parkway Boulevard.

              Commissioner Wood asked Steve if traffic would only be able to travel southbound
              onto the Mountain View Corridor, where there isn’t a northbound connection. Steve
              said that was correct and noted that there aren’t any ramps on the north side of Parkway
              Boulevard.

CONDITIONAL USE APPLICATIONS
CA-4-2022 Petition by VALLEY COLLISSION requesting conditional use approval for outside
          storage. The property is located at 2738 S Constitution Blvd on 1.74 acres and is zoned
          M (Manufacturing). (Staff – Brock Anderson at 801-963-3361 or
          brock.anderson@wvc-ut.gov). Continued from the September 14, 2022 public
          hearing.

Discussion:   Brock Anderson explained that the applicant requested to continue this application from
              the September 14, 2022, public hearing. He stated that the primary concern was staff
              and customer parking, so the applicant wanted time to work on that issue. Per the
              applicants' request, Brock visited the site on Monday, and the applicant stated this was
              their busiest day of the week. Brock noted that the parking was cleaned up a bit from
              what was shown in the original photos.

              Brock reviewed the parking requirements according to our code. He stated that they
              would need sixty-four parking stalls to meet those requirements. Brock pointed out that
              this business does not offer the same services as a traditional auto service shop and
              would not require the same amount of parking. He explained that there isn't a lot of
              customer traffic, and the customers visiting the business stay for a short time. Brock
              mentioned that they have two thousand square feet of office space utilized by four
              employees. He noted that the applicant could operate his business with less office
              space. Therefore, the applicant proposes a total of thirty-five parking stalls, with
              thirteen of those stalls reserved for employees. Brock said that the applicant provided
              their parking generation manual that gives their breakdown of required parking, and he
              will provide that information in the staff report.
              Brock noted that there had been concern regarding the on-street parking in front of the
              subject property. He explained that a staff member from our Economic Development
              Department visited the site when this business was closed, and there was a significant
              amount of on-street parking. Brock said it is safe to assume those cars are for the
              mobile-home park across the street.
