Planning Manager, Jonathan Teerlink, presented the Conditional Use Permit request for the
McQuinn Accessory Building. He reported that accessory buildings are a permitted use in all
residential zones. In 2007, the City Council created a new matrix for maximum accessory building
footprint sizes. In an effort to understand all potential impacts for an accessory building, a footprint
maximum and setback were established based on lot size. Anything larger than the maximum
footprint would be considered by the Planning Commission as a Conditional Use request. The
belief was that there could be negative impacts to a larger detached accessory building. In most
cases, they are situated in a rear yard behind the home. In this case, an accessory building is
proposed to be built within the same setbacks as the home with the use being a guest house. The
maximum permitted footprint is 1,200 square feet.

Because the applicant does not have one acre or more of property he is limited to a 1,200 square-
foot accessory building. The ordinance, however, allows him to request a conditional use through
the Planning Commission to exceed the 1,200 square feet. The proposed footprint for the
accessory building is 2,000 square feet. Mr. Teerlink stated that guest houses on lots that are one

PUBLIC HEARING
1.     Conditional Use Permit – McQuinn Accessory Building – 2163 East Pheasant Way –
       R-1-43.
Chair Bradshaw acknowledged a conflict of interest with the above request and recused herself
from the discussion and vote. Vice-Chair Chris Layton assumed the Chair.

Planning Manager, Jonathan Teerlink presented the staff report and stated that the application
involves a conditional use request from the property owner, Tony McQuinn. The section of the
ordinance being considered is a conditional approval for an accessory building to exceed normally
permitted footprint sizes. A pre-determined threshold for footprints for accessory buildings was
established by the City Council in 2007. If a property owner wishes to exceed that size, the request
needs to be forwarded to the Planning Commission for consideration and approval.

Mr. Teerlink explained that the difference between a Conditional Use Permit application and a
variance is that Conditional Use and Approval Permits are allowed uses in any zone.
Circumstances may, however, present themselves in a way that not all mitigating or regulatory
standards can be written in the zoning ordinance. Therefore, it is up to the Planning Commission
to determine what those situations are and apply standards to mitigate the adverse situations. A
variance involves a specific application that can be applied as relief from any standard in the zoning
ordinance. The application is presented as a quasi-judicial application that is heard by the Hearing
Officer. Those applications involve situations that are not self-imposed or economic in nature and
are elements that are established by State law and carried through district courts.

The request tonight is a conditional use request to exceed the minimum permitted footprint size of
1,200 square feet. The applicant is requesting that it be increased to 2,000 square feet, which is
the threshold level of one-acre parcels. The lot is just under one acre in size and is, therefore,
limited to an accessory building footprint of 1,200 square feet. The property is in the R-1-43 zone,
which is a one-acre zone, however, there are few one-acre parcels. In the immediate vicinity, the
majority of the lots are less than one acre in size. The Commission Members were provided with
the comments received via email.

The applicant, Tony McQuinn, reported that one year ago they purchased the property at 2163
Pheasant Way with the intention of building a large family retirement home there. He was pleased
to find a lovely, private community that is centrally located and with bus access that will provide
added independence for their special needs daughter. They are the third recent owner. The first
owner demolished the home and landscaping despite concern from the neighbors. The second
owner developed house plans but ultimately decided that it would be easier to build in another
location.

Mr. McQuinn stated that at the neighborhood meeting held on December 12, a lot of anger was
expressed for the previous property owners but very few substantive comments were received

       1.      The use of the structure is residential and shall follow Holladay’s land use
               regulations. Specifically, it may not support a commercial business, long-term
               or short-term rental, commercial storage, or leasing activities.

       2.      The accumulative total of accessory buildings may not exceed a footprint of 2,000
               square feet or an overall height of 35 vertical feet.

       3.      Architectural treatments are required as the permit submitted drawings.

       4.      The conditional use shall run with the land and not the applicant.

       5.      The conditional use is subject to inspection upon complaint of non-compliance

       6.      The applicant shall apply for and obtain an approved Building Permit for both

WORK SESSION
3.      Community Development Department – Review of 2021 Ordinance Amendment
        Schedule.
Mr. Teerlink reported that at the next meeting there will be a continuation of the public hearing on
the Waterway Protection Ordinance. Previously, when a house, shed, or structure was built within
a certain distance from a waterway, the Planning Commission had to review and consider the
approval standards. Following other municipalities in the County with similar waterways, he
believed it behooves the City of Holladay to create expected standards that residents can clearly
understand. A set of criteria were added to that end. It is a lengthy piece of legislation that will
take time to review, however, it will be very clear in terms of the intent, standards, and what is
expected from the Planning Commission and City Council.

Mr. Teerlink stated that several pieces of Code will follow and update the ordinance to be in
compliance with State legislative review from the 2018 and 2019 Legislative Sessions.

Mr. Teerlink stated that there has been a great deal of change in the Planning Department over the
past few months. In addition to hiring a New Community and Economic Development Director,
the City has a New Building Official. Greg Kennard previously worked for Forsgren Engineering
and has been hired as the first City of Holladay Building Official.

The Commission Members expressed gratitude for Mr. Teerlink and his role as the New Planning
Manager.
