DISCUSSION — Surveying the Public

Mr. Downs introduced Kevin Lyons from FlashVote, and stated the city has reached out to the
company, being interested in contracting with them to use their method of quick-turnaround

surveying.

Mr. Lyons introduced himself and gave the council a brief description of how FlashVote works.
He stated they first reach out to as many residents as possible, whether this be by mail, email,
text, etc. and build a userbase by asking them to sign up for their platform. Once signed up,

DISCUSSION — Rental Licensing Program

Mr. Downs gave a recap on past decisions the council has made regarding this topic. He stated in
December 2019, the Council voted to 1) Require those with a rental property to have a rental
license, and 2) Require that utilities for all residential properties stay in the name of the property
owner. Mr. Downs stated these were passed to improve neighborhoods and reduce the number of
utility accounts going to collections each month. Mr. Downs continued presented the council
with a list of neighborhoods in Orem and their goals for each of them. These goals mainly
consisted of wanting to better enforce ordinances with absentee landlords. He then went over
some unique situations concerning rental licensing, and how the city delt with them.

Mr. Spencer stated how he didn’t like the fact that you have to pay a sewer hookup fee for a
home, and an attached accessory dwelling unit. This caused general discussion regarding
differentiation between different kinds of accessory dwelling units and what kinds of fee they are
charged.

Mayor Young expressed he would like to discuss these individual issues more in-depth at a later
date.

1, PD-30, PD-37, PD-39, and PD-40 zones. And, amending portions of Section 22-8-8

Mr. Bench stated at the request of the City Council, Development Services proposes to rezone all
of the properties in the State Street District zones back to the zones that applied prior to June 6,
2018, when the first State Street District was adopted. The proposed zones are mostly
commercial zones, but also include residential and planned development (PD) zones. The

proposed zoning maps, which are included as part of the staff report, show the zoning
classifications that the subject properties will go back to.

In addition to the zoning map changes, staff proposes several amendments to portions of Section
22-8-8 (Zone Development Standards) pertaining to standards along State Street. These changes
include:

1.

Height. Limiting the maximum height in the C2 zone from 60 feet to 48 feet. The maximum
height for structures which are setback less than 100 feet from a residential zone would be 35
feet. Non-habitable structures (chimneys, ventilators, antennas, etc.) would be allowed to
extend a maximum of 10 feet above the maximum building height with the requirement that
such structures occupy no more than 30% of the uppermost floor area.

Setbacks. The existing requirement of an 8-foot separated sidewalk with a 15-foot planter
strip will remain as currently required. A setback of 15 feet from the back of sidewalk will
now be required. This 15 foot setback area must be landscaped. The maximum setback
requirement will be eliminated. A diagram illustrating the application of these requirements
is attached to the staff report.

Minimum Building Height. The minimum building height requirement will be eliminated.
References to State Street Districts. All references to the State Street Districts will be
eliminated.

Other Miscellaneous Requirements. The proposed amendments eliminate certain other
requirements in the State Street Corridor including building access requirements, spaces open
to the public, and the small building addition exception.

He concluded his presentation by stating the Planning Commission has recommended this

occur throughout the fiscal year. These adjustments include grants received from Federal, State,
and other governmental or private entities or organizations; appropriating excess FY 2021 funds
in order to expedite funding for public safety digital radio replacements in order to take
advantage of current pricing; appropriating excess FY 2021 funds to purchase the necessary
police vehicles and officer equipment and supplies needed for four additional police officers to

had any questions or concerns with these amendments

Mr. Earl stated the Council has spoken in the past about the possibilities of doing a Resolution
regarding HB 232, the bill which creates the Utah Lake Authority. He stated at the time of the
original conversations, the city had large concerns about the bill. Since then, a substitute bill has
been introduced that has remedied most of their concerns. Mr. Earl concluded by saying that if
the council still wanted to do a resolution, they should identify and focus on the remaining issues
they have with the bill.

Mr. Peterson stated he is not in favor of doing a resolution at this time.

Mayor Young expressed that when the bill first came out, they were not in favor, but now that it
has been updated more, he supports the way it is moving. He concluded by stating he is in favor
of improving the lake in an environmental aspect, but not necessarily in a developmental way.
Dave Spencer moved to continue this item. Terry Peterson seconded the motion. Those

voting aye: David A. Young, David Spencer, Jeff Lambson, Terry Peterson and LaNae Millet.
The motion passed.
