3. Final Conditional Use Permit, for Meadowbrook Apartments, 912 Wall Avenue
  Ms. Alisha Hammett, representing the Weber Housing Authority, stated that they were

  Taking advantage of the new parking ratio for affordable housing, they were able to modify
  the design of the building and have an appropriate parking lot for the facility.
  Mr. Simpson stated that the project had been before the commission in May of 2022 and had
  been approved with several conditions. One of which, was the requirement to come back
  before the Commission once concerns regarding bringing the site into compliance with zoning
  requirements were addressed. The applicant is now seeking final approval of the site plan
  and building plan review and all the conditions, of the previously approved Conditional Use
  Permit, have been met. Mr. Simpson noted that the property is in the R-4 zone and is 1.7

4. Conditional Use Permit, to allow conversion of existing church building to a therapeutic office
   at 3350 Harrison Boulevard
  The applicant, Mr. Selk, stated that he is requesting to reuse the existing church building and
  convert it into a therapy office. The status of the project is awaiting approval of the conditional
  use permit, at which time they can finalize financing and close on the property.

  Ms. Girardo explained that the request is for a conditional use permit to reuse a church as a
  professional office for mental health services, which is allowed in the Ogden Municipal Code.
  The building is a non-residential building, in a residential R-1-10 zone, located within the
Mount Ogden Community Plan area. The property is surrounded by residential homes on
three sides and abuts a commercial property on the west side, with similar uses adjacent to
the property. She indicated that the property is made up of two parcels, which combined size
is 1.93 acres. The existing church is on one parcel and the parsonage, built in 1936, is on the
other. The two parcels would need to be combined by an un-platted parcel combination. Ms.
Girardo reviewed the proposed floorplan for the professional office and indicated that the
parsonage will undergo interior renovations and be used as a long-term rental. No exterior
changes will be made to either the church or the parsonage. The use will be intensified as the
hours are proposed to be Monday through Saturday 8am to 8pm. She stated that managing
traffic flow would help mitigate the increased intensity. Staff is proposing that the 33rd Street
access be used as the entrance and the Harrison Boulevard access be used as the exit, with
traffic flow indicated by signs and painted arrows. Reviewing other details about the property,
Ms. Girardo noted that there is mature landscaping but additional landscaping along the east
and south sides will be required for screening from noise and lights. She reviewed parking
requirements and stated the parking lot will need to be reconfigured to meet wide enough
driving isles. Raised curbing, and if needed, Landscaping islands, would also be required. If
the applicant decides to use a dumpster, a 6’ screening fence made of chain link with slats or
vinyl would be required.

Commissioner Graf expressed concern regarding the two parcels and if the parsonage would
be encumbered if the owner were to sell only it. Ms. Girardo explained that the properties
would need to be combined because the parsonage is landlocked. Mr. Simpson indicated that
the owner can either combine the parcels, as recommended by Ms. Girardo, or develop the
parcels into a private subdivision. Which would likely have significantly more modifications.
Mr. Selk commented that they are fine with doing the parcel combination. Discussion among
the commissioners continued regarding traffic flow options.

Ms. Angel Castillo, Ogden resident and former Planning Commissioner, stated that she
recommended that the Commission approve this conditional use permit as recommended by
staff. Mental health is a huge challenge and people are waiting up to nine months to get an
appointment with a therapist. She indicated that this project is necessary and has huge social
capital advantages for our society, as she understood it, the therapy office will be working with
disadvantaged populations as well.

Mr. Scott Lythgoe, resident on 33rd Street, indicated that there are important considerations
that the Planning Commission needs to consider. The first being the proposed property is in
the middle of a residential neighborhood, which is currently zoned residential for a reason.
The Mount Ogden City Plan’s purpose has always been to keep the area residential.
Secondly, when a residential area is changed and a conditional use permit is issued, it dilutes
the plan. Expressing that the proposal should not be taken lightly, Mr. Lythgoe stated that
nobody went to neighborhood and asked the residents what they felt the impact would be on
the neighborhood or the traffic plan. He indicated that a building within a commercial area
would be a more appropriate location. He expressed concern about there not being enough
parking for some of the other existing buildings on 33rd Street so people park on the street.
The proposed use will also add significantly to the traffic plan and create even more issues.
Mr. Lythgoe concluded that if the conditional use permit is approved, the access, both to and
from, should be from Harrison Boulevard.

Ms. Jill Calton, resident at 1262 34th Street, stated that her property is adjacent to the furthest
east border of the proposed plot. Stating that having the traffic and noise intensity increased
worries her. She stated that 33rd Street is not a well-designed street for the increase in traffic
and the bus line on Harrison makes that street also difficult to navigate. She also showed
frustration that the residents in the neighborhood were not approached prior to the meeting.
Ms. Calton expressed concern of having a long-term rental in an area with really no access.

Mr. Jason Guise, resident at 1229 33rd Street, stated that having access to the property at
33rd Street should not even be an option, unless it was widened. He expressed concern about
the garbage cans being moved along the street. Stating that he has young children, the
addition of the proposed project will greatly affect the way his family lives on their property
and a different location should be chosen.

Mr. Jon Christensen, resident at 1243 33rd Street, stated that he lives just east of the
property’s access from 33rd Street, which is where his driveway is located. He expressed
concern about the traffic on 33rd Street and stated that the entrance will need to be from
Harrison Boulevard. Stating that he has grandkids, Mr. Christensen expressed that he did not
want mental health, and what comes with it, in his backyard. He recommended finding a
different location and expressed concern about the proposed hours.

Mr. Guy Williams, Civil Engineer for the applicant, confirmed that the property does have two
legal access points, one on 33rd Street and the other on Harrison Boulevard. He also stated
that there will only be six offices in the building. Because therapy sessions last one hour there
will only be six vehicles coming and going every hour, averaging one car every ten minutes,
which is not an impact.

Ms. Lacey Hurd, resident at 1259 33rd Street, stated that the property is in her backyard,
where there is not fencing. She stated that she had worked in mental health with Weber
Human Services and knows what mental health brings and does not want it in her backyard.
She also expressed concern regarding her sewer lines and other utilities that are along the
property line. She questioned if a wall would be put up, what it would look like and who would
be responsible to maintain it. Ms. Hurd stated that the alleyway is dirt and people already
drive too fast up there. She expressed concern about the current traffic and what this use
would add to it. Also concerning to her was if construction would be done with the project and
if there would be digging in her yard.

Mr. Hurd, resident at 1259 33rd Street, stated that his water access is on the opposite side of
his property line and will need to always have access to it. He indicated that it is unrealistic to
walk the garbage cans 700 ft to the street. Mr. Hurd stated that he has kids and dogs and
does not want people who are transitioning from prison, have PTSD, flashbacks, coming off
meth, or have schizophrenia in his backyard. He specified that if the proposal is for a
transition home, he really does not want it and expressed frustration that the neighbors were
not approached prior to the meeting. He agreed that there are other locations that would be
more appropriate for this use.

Commissioner Graf asked for clarification if the request is for an in-house residential facility or
for outpatient treatment. Ms. Girardo confirmed that the use is for a sit-down professional
office and no rehab or transitional housing will occur. Discussion continued among the
Commission regarding the access to 33rd Street. Mr. Simpson explained that conditional uses
have the potential to create impact to surrounding areas, which is the purpose of inviting the
neighbors, he stated that under Utah State Law a conditional use permit cannot be denied
unless it can be determined that impacts cannot be mitigated. He also explained that the City
does not go knock on doors, but rather mails notice to property owners within 300’ of the
property under review, per Utah State Code.

Commissioner Saftsten proposed the question of if concerns would be mitigated if the 33rd
Street access was not an option to be utilized as an entrance or exit. Mr. Tanner stated that
because the property would be owned by the applicant that it would be difficult to tell them
they can’t use it. The question then arose of what else the property could be used as. Ms.
Girardo stated that it could be used as residential homes or other conditional uses as listed in
the code. Mr. Simpson confirmed that potentially up to 8 single family homes could occupy
the property. Discussion continued among the commissioners about access to the property.
Mr. Brierley stated that a condition could be set that the applicant does not use 33rd Street to
mitigate impact via a barrier or fence placed at the end of Mr. Christensen’s right-of-way. The
applicant would still be able to use the private road to walk down, take garbage cans to the
street or for emergency use, just not for the business.

Mr. Selk stated that this proposal is for his wife to operate a therapy office with her six or
seven employees. He clarified that the project is for a strictly outpatient facility, for people who
are going through a rough time and just need someone to listen to them and help them with
some coping mechanisms. Mr. Selk stated that the parcel will have all off-street parking. The
church is only about 2000 square feet, and they hope to get 6 offices in the building, which
will not generate much traffic. He indicated that the hours of operation could be addressed.
Expressing gratitude for the comments being shared by the neighbors, Mr. Selk stated that he
lives two blocks away and the building is a perfect location for his wife’s practice.

Mr. Hurd addressed the Commission again inquiring about what he is going to be looking at if
the conditional use is approved. Chair Sandau stated that nothing with the building will be
changing other than the use. Asking if there will be a fence or wall installed, Mr. Hurd
expressed concern for the impact that will be caused to his and the neighboring properties.
He stated that he would rather have children playing in his backyard than drug addicts, sex
offenders or people with mental illness. Commissioner Graf stated that the comments made
by Mr. Hurd were offensive and unnecessary. Mr. Tanner reiterated that the petitioner said
the mental therapy is for anybody, and does not necessarily mean criminals and sex
offenders, which is typically dealt with by the Department of Corrections, not by a therapist.

Chair Sandau requested clarification of where the proposed fence would be located. Ms.
Girardo stated that it would be screening for a dumpster and reviewed the location of current

5. Public Facility Site Plan, for Marshall White Community Center Replacement Building.

  The petitioner, Mr. Brent Tippets, reviewed the significance of the Marshall White Center and
  its rich heritage within the Ogden Community and the State of Utah. He stated the position of
  the new building will be further north than the existing Marshall White Center, which will
  provide well-lit parking in the front of the building, along 28th Street. The preservation of the
  park to the east was also a key element to the design of the new building. Mr. Tippets
  reviewed the floor plan of the building, noting each of the areas and their uses. He indicated
  that the existing facility is deteriorated, and a structural analysis was done on the building,
  finding that it would have been more costly to try to resurrect the existing building than to build
  a new one.

  Mr. Simpson stated that the Marshall White Center is one of the parks that is close to
  downtown urban core and plays a central role in our community. The new facility will be more
  than double the footprint of the existing facility, which will provide adequate space for existing
  amenities and new activities in the recreation center. The park itself is zoned open space, O-
  1, and is nearly six acres, being made up of multiple parcels, which will need to be combined
  into one lot to meet building code and setback requirements. Mr. Simpson stated that the
  architect has worked closely with staff, including police and fire, to find ways to improve
  visibility and safety on the site. The input received was to have the parking be more visible,
  allowing the police to better monitor the area, as well as making it more inviting. A small area
  on the north end of the property, behind the building, will be used as employee parking and
  function as a delivery and utility area, with the dumpster also being in this area. For security,
  the proposal is for this entire area to be enclosed with a wrought iron fence.

  Mr. Simpson indicated that the proposal has three separate connections from the street,
  providing good accessibility. The parking lot from 28th Street is well defined and has islands
  and buffering to identify pedestrians and incorporates a drop off area in the front of the
  building. He stated the basketball courts on the rear of the building will be removed, but the
  courts on the east, donated by the Utah Jazz, will be preserved. Staff recommends approval
  with the following conditions: that the dumpster enclosure be six-foot tall masonry walls and
  solid metal gates that are compatible with the building; obtaining and satisfying all department
  staff comments in the site plan and permit review processes; and, the combination of all
  parcels associated with the facility and park through a city approved process.

  Commissioner Safsten inquired if the facility is fee based and if an economic analysis had
  been completed to conclude if the facility will pay for itself. Mr. Simpson stated that the center
  will charge fees to use the facility, but outdoor activities will remain free of charge. Mr. Bridge,
  Recreation Manager, stated that the fees will not cover the cost of the building and it will be
  subsidized by the city. Commissioner Shale asked if the bowery and picnic tables on the east
  side of the building will remain. Mr. Tippets indicated that the bowery will be relocated to a
  different park. Conversation continued among the Commissioners about the use of the pool
  and if it should have more lanes added to be used as a competition pool. Mr. Tippets stated
  that competition pools typically lose money and would need 8-10 lanes.

  Ms. Angel Castillo, Ogden resident, former Planning Commissioner, and member of the
  executive committee of the NAACP, stated that the proposed development to rebuild the
  Marshall White Center is not big enough. She recapped a previous Planning Commission
  meeting where citizens expressed their desire for a swimming pool in the new facility. She
  stated that it could be economically sustainable if the city were to double down on the
  tournament space around the facility. Ms. Castillo stated that there isn’t enough money going
  into the proposed center and requested for the Planning Commission to make a
  recommendation to the City Council that they hold one more public hearing before they vote.

  MOTION:      A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to approve the Public Facility Site
               Plan, for the Marshall White Community Center Replacement Building, located at
               222 28th Street, finding that it fits with the General Plan, which calls for recreation
               facilities for the public, with the following conditions:
                    1. that the dumpster enclosure be six-foot tall masonry walls and solid metal
                         gates that are compatible with the building;
                    2. obtaining and satisfying all department staff comments in the site plan
                         and permit review processes; and
                    3. the combination of all parcels associated with the facility and park
                         through a city approved process.
               Motion was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously with
               Commissioners Aaberg, Blaisdell, Graf, Safsten, Schade, Shale, Shinoda,
               Southwick and Sandau voting aye.

Review of Meeting
Referring to the Conditional Use Permit for reuse of the church earlier in the meeting, Mr.
Tanner reminded the Commission that it was not a public hearing, but it was good that they were
able to hear what the issues were to see if they could be mitigated. Referring to state code, he
reminded the Commission that it does not require elimination of anticipated detrimental effects,
just mitigation to approve a conditional use permit. If mitigation is possible then the conditional
use must be approved.
