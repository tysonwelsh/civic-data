2. Presentations and Reports
2.1 Fraud Risk Assessment Model- Dean Lundell
    Dean Lundell, Finance Director, said that the State issued a check list that allows cities to
    assess their fraud risk based on a point system. He stated that segregation of duties with cash
    handling is always very important, and that the city is in compliance there. The risk model
    currently shows the city in a moderate to high risk rate. There are some things the city could
    implement to improve that score. One would be to create a fraud hotline that would allow
    employees and the public to call or email someone in the City to report their concern. Also,
    some cities have an audit committee that would be involved in the audit process. He said that
    some cities have talked about having a full-time auditor or sharing an auditor. Mayor Johnson
    stated that there is an employee code of ethics that was created years ago that could increase
    their score. He suggested they bring that up again for review.

2.2 COVID-19 operation recommendations briefing- Chief Craft
    Chief Craft stated that under the EMS Act they are required to operate under a physician’s
    office, which for Lehi City is Dr. Singleton at Mountain Pointe Medical. Chief Craft said their
    EMS operates under his guidelines and license. Dr. Singleton also has oversite of
    recertifications and medical procedures. Also, in turn the Fire Department reaches out to him
    for recommendations and information on how to help our employees stay safe during the
    pandemic. Chief Craft explained how some of the operation procedures for the employees have
    changed to limit their exposure.

3.7. Consideration of Resolution #2020-15 approving an Agreement to Create a Fiber Network
Feasibility Study between Magellan Advisors and Lehi City.
    Councilor Condie wanted to be clear of the intent and what the feasibility study will tell us and
    what it will accomplish.

    Mayor Johnson stated that they received 5 responses to an RFP for this. Mr. Walker stated that
    they wanted a third party to analyze if the City should proceed with a model like UTOPIA or
    if it would be better for the City to offer fiber on its own. Both options are compelling, and
    both are multimillion-dollar operations, so it’s a good investment to have a neutral third party
    recommend what would be best for Lehi. He said that UTOPIA did submit a bid that was at no
    cost, but he believed there was too much of a conflict of interest with them doing the study
    because they have something to gain in the outcome. Councilor Albrecht inquired if Magellan
    would have a financial interest in the outcome of this study. Mr. Walker stated that they are
    neutral and have no interest in running this type of operation for Lehi.

    Laura Lewis is the city’s financial advisor on this project and has years of experience with
    fiber network financing. She stated that she analyzed the proposals and was impressed with
    Magellan’s expertise. Mr. Walker stated that he knows the elected officials have pressure from
    the public to move quickly on this, but he stated that it’s really important to be methodical and
    careful with this type of investment. Councilor Albrecht stated that she also feels it’s important
    to have a neutral third-party recommendation from someone with expertise in this type of
    operation. Mayor Johnson stated that having this study and recommendation should reduce the
    City’s risk.

    Motion:     Councilor Southwick moved to approve Resolution #2020-15, an Agreement to
                Create a Fiber Network Feasibility Study between Magellan Advisors and Lehi
                City. Councilor Hancock seconded the motion.

    compliments what Thanksgiving Point is offering so to have them together would be a great
    opportunity for Lehi. He understands there are concerns with traffic and an incomplete
    amended Area Plan, but felt they have been able to mitigate those concerns with this
    development agreement. He also stated that a lot of people dropping off their children will
    already be working in the area, so he feels the traffic impact would be minimal.

    quality education for young children and are excited to partner with Thanksgiving Point and

    Councilor Southwick inquired about when the new Area Plan will be complete. Mr. Washburn

Agreement with Magellan Advisors, Consent Agenda Item #3.7

My name is Cory Shelton, I'm an employee of Verisk/Xactware and Lehi City resident. The
following statements are my own and not the opinion of my employer or others.
I'm writing this comment regarding Resolution #2020-1:
I will outline the critical points of my comments in a summary below:
1.     The proposed contract between Magellan Advisors and Lehi City is wasteful spending
and delays the potential implementation of critical fiber internet as part of commercial and
residential infrastructure.
2.    COVID-19 and related work from home and social distancing efforts have given rise to
an immediate need for Fiber Internet options in Lehi.
3.     Action planning proposal.
1. The proposed contract between Magellan Advisors and Lehi City.
As stated in the above summary section, I believe the proposed contract between Magellan
Advisors and Lehi City is an unnecessary and disruptive step towards the adoption and
implementation of a fiber network in Lehi. After reviewing the Scope of Service outlined in
"Exibit A" I would argue that several of the core findings of the Magellan study are either
unnecessary, already in the public domain, or are the responsibility of a potential fiber network
vendor.
For example, the following questions are asked in the scope of service
A. Understanding the infrastructure and market. a. What is a fiber network and how is it used?
B. What is the federal regulatory process for a fiber network?
G. State Requirements
The critical issue with the questions asked in the SoS is that we already have regional examples
of successful implementations of fiber networks. I would argue that the findings of such a study
are less relevant than actual real-world feedback and historical implementation data of fiber
networks in the State of Utah. In terms of State and Federal regulatory requirements, it appears
almost inconsequential in the decision-making process. We know that neither the State or
Federal government has a legal restriction prohibiting Lehi from installing a fiber network, and
other cities have already done so within those legal and regulatory frameworks. I understand
that both bodies of government may have certain regulations that need to be considered
during implementation, but vendors such as Utopia Fiber already have a proven ability to
comply with such regulations during installation.
Finally, I would argue that given the growth of tech-related employment in Lehi over the past
decade we are a better-suited community than other municipalities who have already
successfully implemented fiber-optic networks in terms of both demographics and demand.
2. COVID-19 and related work from home and social distancing efforts have given rise to an
immediate need for Fiber Internet options in Lehi.
Covid-19 has exposed an extraordinary weakness in our infrastructure. I would argue that
reliable access to high-speed internet access rivals other critical public services. Not because we
need to stream more Netflix or YouTube videos, but because internet connectivity is now the
most critical employment resource for a significant portion of Lehi's working population.
When we hear stories of the internet "Buckling" under the stress of higher demand for data, it's
not the global infrastructure under duress. Global network trunks are capable of supporting
network traffic several times higher than we see today. However last-leg service providers such
as Comcast are totally unprepared for the demand provided by their customers due to the
inherent nature of legacy cable and DLS network systems. Comcast and other providers have
had ample opportunity to upgrade both infrastructure and service offerings but due to a near-
monopoly that they've enjoyed for years, they have not.
3. Action planning proposal.
As it's often less than helpful to criticize the current strategy without proposing a solution, I
recommend we implement the following action plan.
1.     Lehi City Council should request a fiber network installation proposal from Utopia Fiber.
2.     Upon reception of the installation proposal, Lehi City should disseminate the potential
impacts of a fiber network installation to the public. This should include critical items like target
adoption percentages to avoid low-adoption costs and potential taxpayer-funded penalties for
missing adoption penalties.
3.     Lehi City should enable a citizen-driven campaign using social media and other
resources to gauge the potential adoption of a fiber network in Lehi.
4.    The results should be shared with the city council and the public to inform a vote on the
proposal from Utopia Fiber.
5.     Pending the results of the vote, the City should take action on the will of the citizens.
This may include efforts to obtain more information as the Magellan proposal affords or a
complete rejection of a fiber network.
