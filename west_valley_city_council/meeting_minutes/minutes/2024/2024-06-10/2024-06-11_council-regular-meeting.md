COMMENT PERIOD
A.  PUBLIC COMMENTS
    Scott Bohn expressed his frustration about speeding on his street. The latest speed
    test results showed that 85% of cars were traveling at 27 mph, slightly over the
    speed limit. He noted some improvements over time but shared that neighbors had
    a similar negative reaction to the report. Scott explained that the city used an 85th
    percentile report from the Department of Transportation to set speed limits, which
    didn't accurately reflect the number of speeders. The report had discrepancies,
    including incorrect addresses and date ranges, and failed to account for 176 to 244
    cars exceeding the speed limit daily, with 40 cars traveling over 30 mph. He found
    several errors in the report, making it unreliable. He recounted an incident from a
    week prior, where a speeding car caused a girl on a bike to crash into a parked car,
    illustrating the dangers of speeding. Scott pleaded with city officials to change the
    Public Works department's reporting method, emphasizing that current reports only
    showed average speeds and not individual speeders.

       Jim Vesock first thanked the Mayor and city council for attending Spring Fest,
       which had a great turnout. He acknowledged issues with vendor parking that
       would be fixed for next year to provide more guest parking. Jim mentioned the
       My Hometown Youth Council hold monthly meetings, often in churches. When
       those aren’t available, the city has graciously provided the multi-use building,
       thanks to Craig's help. He appreciated the city's support. Jim then discussed the
       success of a six-week skateboarding program at the skate park, organized by two
       community resource centers and other groups. Jim shared feedback from kids
       using the skate park with their bikes who indicated that they avoided the new bike
       pad because falling on the asphalt caused injuries. He also pointed out that the
       bike track had two large holes, posing a hazard for kids on scooters, and
       suggested it should be an easy fix for maintenance. He noted the skate park had
       weeds growing taller than him, indicating neglect, and suggested it needed better
       maintenance. Jim expressed disappointment that the city did not hold a Memorial
       Day event, unlike neighboring cities.

 Morgan Golightly thanked everyone for their support in keeping West Valley
 Performing Arts open, highlighting their collective achievements in problem-
 solving and collaboration over the past month. As a freelance set designer and
 scenic painter, Morgan emphasized the importance of accessible theater in the
 community, noting that theaters tell unique stories and share culture with
 audiences. Morgan shared her personal connection to the theater, describing it as a
 supportive family and a vital space for creating connections. She recounted her
 childhood dream of designing sets at this theater and working in various theaters
 across Utah. The theater provided a safe haven during a rough childhood and
 later, as Morgan left abusive situations. The community and employment at the
 theater greatly aided her recovery, supported her career, and helped her as a single
 parent. Expressing gratitude for the opportunity to work at the theater, Morgan
 stressed the theater's role in saving lives and supporting dreams. She looked
 forward to continuing to serve the community and thanked everyone for their
 ongoing support in making a positive impact on people's lives.

 Liz Christensen thanked everyone for their support in ensuring a future for West
 Valley Performing Arts. As a Davis County resident and regular audience
 member, Liz emphasized that she drives past six other theater companies to attend
 shows at West Valley, which she finds uniquely valuable. Liz shared insights
 from her mother, a consultant, who highlighted that most industries merely
 redistribute wealth, whereas sectors like agriculture, mining, and manufacturing
 create new wealth. Liz likened the support for West Valley Performing Arts to an
 "artistic mining and manufacturing operation." This theater, according to Liz, taps
 into community talent that other venues overlook and produces high-quality
 shows, thereby increasing the artistic, cultural, social, and reputational capital of
 the community. Liz stressed the importance of entertainment to city life,
 highlighting the theater’s unique role in nurturing and combining talent along the
 Wasatch Front. She argued that not all wealth is monetary; artistic and cultural
 investments yield significant human returns. Liz concluded by urging the city to
 make a consistent, ongoing commitment to West Valley Performing Arts. She
 suggested a 1% subsidy in perpetuity as a means to support the city's mission of
 fostering a diverse, creative, and vibrant community, providing tangible evidence
 of this commitment.

RESOLUTION 24-97: APPROVE AMENDMENT NO. 1 TO AN AGREEMENT
WITH THE DEPARTMENT OF WORKFORCE SERVICES
Mayor Lang presented proposed Resolution 24-97 that would approve Amendment No. 1 to
an Agreement With the Department of Workforce Services

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       A temporary overflow homeless shelter managed by Switchpoint opened in
       November of 2023 and was anticipated to close on April 30, 2024. However, the
       State has decided to keep the shelter open until at least the end of September of this
       year with the possibility of the shelter being open until April 30, 2025.

       Since the shelter opened, the City has seen a significant increase in the demand for
       public safety services. As a result, both the Police and Fire Departments are staffing
       overtime shifts to meet the increased demand for services.

       The Utah Department of Workforce Services offers cities with homeless shelters
       within their borders mitigation funding to help offset the increased costs resulting
       from the additional public safety service demands from the shelter and surrounding
       areas. The original contract amount for the mitigation funding was $408,776.26.
       This amendment would add $345,622.59 of additional mitigation funds to the
       contract to be used by September 30, 2024.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and she called for a motion.

       Councilmember Nordfelt moved to approve Resolution 24-97.

       Councilmember Harmon seconded the motion.

RESOLUTION 24-98: APPROVE THE PURCHASE OF A MOBILE
EMERGENCY OPERATIONS CENTER TRAILER
Mayor Lang presented proposed Resolution 24-98 that would approve the Purchase of a
Mobile Emergency Operations Center Trailer

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       In 2020 West Valley City was affected by an earthquake. At that time the fixed
       emergency operations center in city hall was rendered unusable. We at this time
       had a few smaller units which we patched together to run the city operations. In
       2022 we applied for a grant through the Federal Emergency Management Agency,
       which we were granted 1,066,000.00 for a new mobile emergency operations
       center.

       We went out to gather information on a unit which would be suitable for a city
       our size and found that EVI trailers would be able to manufacture the unit.
       Attached are drawings and outlines for the unit we are going to purchase.

       The agreement attached is from Siddons-Martin Emergency Group, who is the
       dealer for the unit and will be the local representative we will be working through.

       The delivery time for this unit will be about 19-20 months.

RESOLUTION 24-99: AWARD A CONTRACT TO STAPP CONSTRUCTION
FOR CONSTRUCTION OF THE WEST VIEW PARK EXPANSION
Mayor Lang presented proposed Resolution 24-99 that would award a Contract to Stapp
Construction for Construction of the West View Park Expansion.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       West View Park Expansion is 2.54 acres of land located at 4059 S 6000 W. It is
       currently a mostly vacant lot. This land is situated between West View Park and
       the Mountain View Corridor Sports Field. This land used to have two homes that
       were purchased by the city for the combination of these two areas, both homes have
       been removed. This project will construct phase one of a two phase park. It will
       include a challenge course, parking lot and landscaping. Utah Outdoor Recreation
       Grant monies are also being utilized to help with the installation of the Challenge
       Course which was approved by City Council last year. We will also be reimbursed
       from Public Works for the street improvements in the amount of $68,629.00, so the
       total used from impact fees will be $601,371.00.

       Bids for construction were solicited and four bidders responded:
       Stapp Construction                    $670,000.00
       Wasatch West Contracting              $849,738.00
       S & L Inc                             $988,020.00

       Stapp Construction was selected as the lowest responsible bidder. Construction will
       begin as soon as possible.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and she called for a motion.

RESOLUTION 24-100: AUTHORIZE THE MAYOR TO SIGN A LOCAL
GOVERNMENT UNDERSTANDING AND AGREEMENT FOR CONSULTING
SERVICES FOR THREE STATION AREA PLANS
Mayor Lang presented proposed Resolution 24-100 that would authorize the Mayor to Sign
a Local Government Understanding and Agreement for Consulting Services for Three Station
Area Plans.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The State of Utah requires West Valley City to have a Station Area Plan for the
       one-half mile radius area surrounding each light rail station. For reference, the
       State’s Station Area Plan requirements are found in Section 10-9a-403.1 of Utah
       Code. These plans are intended to promote the availability and affordability of
       housing, promote sustainable environmental conditions, enhance access to
       opportunities and increase transportation choices and connections.

       Funding for these plans has been made available by the Governor’s Office of
       Economic Opportunity. These funds have been allocated to Wasatch Front
       Regional Council (WFRC) to assist communities who are in need of preparing these
       plans. To comply with State Law, West Valley City applied for and received
       funding from WFRC for the three Station Area Plans noted above.

       To assist West Valley City and WFRC with these Station Area Plans, WFRC
       engaged a consultant selected by West Valley City. WSP USA Inc. (WSP) has
       been selected to provide assistance to complete the work required in the time frame
       allotted.
