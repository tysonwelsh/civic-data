**Resolution: Addendum No.10 to the Interlocal Agreement with the Utah Transit Authority (UTA) for Transit Master Plan Frequent Bus Service Routes Implementation**   ~ 1:00 p.m.
  20 min.
The Council will receive a briefing on a resolution that would authorize the Mayor to enter into the proposed addendum No.10 to the Interlocal Agreement with UTA to implement 2026-27 Frequent Service Network (FSN) Routes. Frequent service is a goal for buses to arrive at least every 15 minutes. This agreement covers the routes on 200 South, 900 South, 2100 South, and 1000 North. The interlocal agreement signed in 2018 is for twenty years, with a goal of full implementation of the FSN Routes as described in the City’s Transit Master Plan.

**FYI – Project Timeline:** (subject to change per Chair direction or Council discussion)
Briefing - Tuesday, June 9, 2026
Set Public Hearing Date -  Tuesday, April 21, 2026 and Tuesday, May 19, 2026
Hold hearing to accept public comment - Tuesday, May 19, 2026 and Tuesday, June 2, 2026 at 7 p.m.
TENTATIVE Council Action - TBD

**Summary:**

Kira Luke introduced the item.

**Nichol Bourdeaux** (UTA Vice President of External Relations) and Lynn Jacobs presented information highlighting City sponsored services through the interlocal agreement, addenda history (2018 to present), overview of addendum No. 10, and community impacts including ridership data, and elements of affordability and equity.

Council Members, Nichol Bourdeaux, and Lynn Jacobs discussed how routes were selected for the FSN network, expanding the FSN network through State and surrounding city partnerships, and sharing the outcomes and benefits of this partnership with other jurisdictions to expand participation opportunities.

**Summary:**

Jennifer Bruno reviewed the following items for Council Members:

* New state law required the Council to identify the specific purposes funded by any property tax increase and include those items in the Property Tax Impact Schedule
* Hypothetical options for modifying or reducing the proposed property tax increase, noting that new growth property tax revenue figures would not be finalized until June 12, 2026, and advised that the updated figures would be provided before the Council considered budget adoption
* Key Expense Changes spreadsheet and outlined how funding adjustments could be made before adoption of the budget

Council Members agreed to meet on Thursday, June 11, 2026, to discuss potential budget reductions. Council Member Puy encouraged Council Members to research proposed reductions, determine whether funding should come from the property tax increase or the General Fund, and prepare recommendations for the meeting.

Council Members and Jennifer Bruno discussed this year’s unique separation of reviewing budget line items for both the General Fund and property tax increase, whereas next year it would be combined. Discussion also included strategies for building Council consensus before final budget adoption, potential budget items to restore, add, or keep reduced, and removal of the Fleet Fuel item from further consideration.

Mary Beth Thompson advised that a 1% budget reduction would equal approximately $5 million and clarified that the upcoming fund balance availability report would not include revenue projections.

**Straw Poll:**

Support for reducing the Council Office’s Operations Budget by 5%. All Council Members present were in favor.

**Council District Four Vacancy Applicant Interviews**   ~ 3:20 p.m.
  TBD
The Council will interview applicants for the vacant Council District Four seat. Each applicant will have up to five minutes to speak to the Council on topics of their choice.

**Summary:**

Council Member Puy explained the District Four interview and selection process.

***Applicant Interviews***

The following applicants were interviewed:
Austin Taylor, Kaytlynn Scott, Lilah Rosenfield, Jeffrey Wilbur, Brenda Scheer, Javier Chavez Jr., Clayton Scrivner, Pamela Silberman, Jennifer Napier-Pearce, Gavin Serr, George Hofmann, Tom Merrill, Thomas Christiansen, Benjamin Draleau, Julian Jurkoic, Kallin Glauser, Nicholas Pensari, Nathan Carlin, Scott Lyttle, and Jennifer Starley.

Council Member Puy thanked all applicants for the time and effort invested in their applications, encouraged continued community involvement in whatever capacity was most meaningful, and encouraged interested individuals to consider running for public office.

***First Round of Voting***

Council Members cast verbal votes as follows:
Council Member Wharton – Jennifer Napier-Pearce
Council Member Petro – Javier Chavez
Council Member Carlsen – Jennifer Napier-Pearce
Council Member Young – Scott Lyttle
Council Member Dugan – Jennifer Napier-Pearce
Council Member Puy – Scott Lyttle

Keith Reynolds read the tally of candidates voted on; Jennifer Napier-Pearce received three votes, Scott Lyttle received two votes, and Javier Chavez received one vote.

**Council Remarks:**

Council Member Wharton thanked the applicants, stated it was a difficult decision filling a vacancy on the Council, and encouraged further community public service.

Council Member Young commented on the unique nature of the opportunity and expressed optimism and encouragement regarding the number of individuals interested in serving on the Council.

Council Member Puy spoke to the impressive applications and presentations received and highlighted several individuals who made big impressions on him, and encouraged continued involvement in the city.

***Applicant Question***

Council Member Puy reviewed the process for round two of voting that included a question for the applicants to answer. Council Members then discussed the options of questions in which to pose to applicants. Council Member Puy chose one of six questions randomly for the applicants to answer.

Jennifer Napier-Pearce was the first to respond to the question and spoke on common ground, seeking various perspectives from other Council Members who lived and related within other districts, and mutual trust.

Scott Lyttle was the second to respond and spoke on prioritizing collaboration, the background work as Council Members to come to a consensus, working with stakeholders, and coming from a thoughful place.

***Second Round of Voting***

Council Members cast verbal votes as follows:
Council Member Wharton – Jennifer Napier-Pearce
Council Member Petro – Scott Lyttle
Council Member Carlsen – Jennifer Napier-Pearce
Council Member Young – Scott Lyttle
Council Member Dugan – Jennifer Napier-Pearce
Council Member Puy – Scott Lyttle

Council Member Puy explained that, due to the tie vote, the selection would be determined by a coin toss.

***Coin Toss***

Keith Reynolds invited the candidates to inspect the coin, designated heads for Jennifer Napier-Pearce and tails for Scott Lyttle, and conducted the coin toss. The coin landed on heads, and Jennifer Napier-Pearce was selected as the District 4 Council Member.

**Final Council Remarks:**

Council Member Carlsen offered gratitude to the applicants who interviewed.

Council Member Dugan spoke on the applicants’ passion for serving the public.

**Tentative Closed Meeting**   -

The Council will consider a motion to enter into closed meeting. A closed meeting described under Utah Code Annotated (UCA) Section §52-4-205 may be held for specific purposes including, but not limited to discussing:

a.     discussion of the character, professional competence, or physical or mental health of an individual.

b.     strategy sessions to discuss collective bargaining.
c.    strategy sessions to discuss pending or reasonably imminent litigation.
d.    strategy sessions to discuss the purchase, exchange, or lease of real property, including any form of a water right or water shares, if public discussion of the transaction would:

(i)    disclose the appraisal or estimated value of the property under consideration, or
(ii)     prevent the public body from completing the transaction on the best possible terms.

e.    strategy sessions to discuss the sale of real property, including any form of a water right or water shares, if:

(i) public discussion of the transaction would:

(A) disclose the appraisal or estimated value of the property under consideration, or
(B) prevent the public body from completing the transaction on the best possible terms.

(ii)     the public body previously gave public notice that the property would be offered for sale, and
(iii)     the terms of the sale are publicly disclosed before the public body approves the sale.

f.    discussion regarding deployment of security personnel, devices, or systems.
g.     investigative proceedings regarding allegations of criminal misconduct.

A closed meeting may also be held for attorney-client matters that are privileged pursuant to Utah Code § 78B-1-137, and for other lawful purposes that satisfy the pertinent requirements of the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act.
