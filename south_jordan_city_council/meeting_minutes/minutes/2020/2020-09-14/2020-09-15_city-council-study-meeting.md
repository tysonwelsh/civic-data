The City Council reviewed tonight’s regular meeting agenda. Mayor Pro Tempore Shelton noted

Council Member Zander asked if they have received feedback from the neighbors on the Jones
property rezone? Planning Director Schaefermeyer said yes, those comments have been
forwarded to the City Council. He said there are comments relating to design and some
comments concerned about the increase of 5 lots. There are differing opinions if there should be

brought forward are the result of much discussion with the City Council.

Mr. Schaefermeyer said regarding K, tonight’s amendments are technical, but it may lead to
some policy issues. He said they have a survey that Salt Lake County did regarding ALU’s in
communities across the state. He will forward that to the City Council after the meeting. He said
the Legislature is pushing communities to allow ALU’s. He said they are not addressing the
more controversial issues at this time.

Council Member Zander asked what will happen if the text amendment is approved? Mr.
Schaefermeyer said they are not changing where the ALU’s are allowed or how big they can be.
He said they are clarifying questions about if you can have a second kitchen in the house. They
are trying to clarify when an area becomes an ALU and when it is not.

The City Council discussed item L. on the agenda. Mayor Pro Tempore Shelton asked what is
the plan to take care of the storm water? Mr. Schaefermeyer said it is much cheaper to retain
water in an open basin. He said it is the value of the land that becomes the tipping point where it
makes sense to put in underground tanks in the parking lot, rather than having wasted space in a
commercial area.

Engineering Director Klavano concurred. He said it is typical to put underground tanks in the
parking lot. He noted that it is a detention gallery, rather than retention.

         E. Discussion Item: Peterson Development – Rezoning Overlay Zone. (By Peterson
            Development)

Barrett Peterson, Peterson Development, introduced himself and Jeff Seaman. He noted that
Scott Howell, their government affairs consultant, was also present. He said they have a couple
of new proposals. He reviewed a prepared presentation (Attachment A). He reviewed the traffic
report and gave a brief background on the history of the property. He said currently, they have a
right on their property to have up to 20,000 sq. ft. of commercial space. In talking with the
neighbors, the concerns they heard were about traffic and safety.

Mr. Seaman reviewed the 2 proposals. He noted that the plans have changed since they met with
the neighbors. This new proposal has more parking than is required by the city. The neighbors
are concerned about privacy. As a result, they downgraded the units on the south. The end caps
are planned to be ramblers and the middle units will be 2 stories.

He reviewed the second concept, which they are calling Parley’s condo. It includes underground
parking and does away with the concern that people store personal items in the garage and park
on the street. He said the proposal is for high quality units and would add value to this property.
He noted the green space planned for the condo residents. He said it is just a footprint and
concept at this point; it is not fully designed.

Council Member McGuire asked if the condos would be age restricted? Mr. Peterson said no, he
does not believe so. They have done other age restricted projects in the city.

Mr. Peterson said they have talked about doing 10 percent of the units as workforce housing
units.

They discussed the city’s parking requirements. The parking requirements are the same for
townhomes or condominiums. Mr. Schaefermeyer said it is 2.5 parking spaces per 3 bedroom
units. There is guest parking also required.

Mayor Pro Tempore Shelton asked about the price point for the units? Mr. Peterson said the
townhomes would be in the $350,000-$400,000 range. The condominiums would be in the
$350,000-$375,000 range.

Mr. Schaefermeyer said guest parking would be 1 stall for every 4 units. Mr. Peterson said for
the townhomes, they are well above the city’s parking requirements.

It was noted on the townhome proposal, there are 37 off road parking stalls, not including
parking on the commercial property. Council Member Zander said the developers have increased
the parking. With their proposal, every townhome has room for 1 guest plus. She asked when the
commercial development is closed at night, can the residents park on the north side? Mr.
Peterson said they are open to that. Council Member Zander said in the proposals, she likes the
parking that is more safe, secure, and private. She also appreciates that the proposed parking is
not on the road.

                                                 History
In 2002 Albertson’s purchased 6.68 acres on the southwest corner of 10400 South and Bangerter Highway with
the intention of having a long-term presence within a neighborhood shopping and gas center. Albertson’s left the
Utah market in 2009 and the building sat vacant for many years until Vasa Fitness took occupancy. The
prolonged vacancy sucked any momentum and synergy out of the center and as a result the junior anchor pads to
the east and west of the Vasa property have failed to gain any traction. The property has been marketed
aggressively for more than two decades, with the possibility of retail at this location growing increasingly
unlikely. The expansion of Bangerter Highway has eliminated the east pad and provides an opportunity to for a
mixed use project on the west pad.

The 30 townhome units would include two different floor plans in various configurations ranging from 2,125-

RE: Harvest Pointe - West Rushton Meadows Trip Memo – South Jordan, UT

The following is a trip generation memo comparing various land use options for the Harvest Pointe West
Rushton Meadows area of South Jordan, UT. UDOT is building an interchange at 10400 South (South
Jordan Parkway) / Bangerter Highway which will impact the available land use possible. This memo
evaluates three options. The original land use zoning and two possible alternative land uses.

Option #1: 20,000 sf of retail
Option #2: 25 townhomes and 6,000 sf of office condos
Option #3: 39 condos and 0 sf office


Because of the interchange, 9 single family homes will be eliminated as traffic from the area. Those trip
numbers are also identified in the trip generation to quantify the net traffic change to the area.

Figure 1 identifies the site location. Figure 2 shows the 9 homes to be eliminated and the developable land.

What this indicates is that the original entitled retail / commercial land use for the area could generate an
estimated 755 daily trips with 19 projected AM peak and 76 PM peak trips. If the land use changes to
townhomes and office (Option 2), then the daily and peak hours trips are estimated to be less than Option 1
trips (18 AM Peak, 21 PM peak and 241 daily trips). If the land use becomes residential only (Option 3),
then the trips are 18 AM, 22 PM and 285 daily trips. Since residential and office space generate less traffic
than retail traffic, the change in land use to less retail, residential or office from retail has a substantial
reduction in traffic generation.

Note that the existing 9 residential homes being eliminated would generate 7 AM (35%), 9 PM (12%) and
85 daily (11%) of the Option 1 trips and would be eliminated from the current traffic situation.

Harvest Pointe Drive is a collector street that leads to 10400 South and a signalized intersection. The street

on the park side but no parking on the commercial side of the street. A two-lane collector has a daily
capacity of approximately 13,500 daily trips. Depending on what option is selected will determine the
capacity utilized by the proposed development.
