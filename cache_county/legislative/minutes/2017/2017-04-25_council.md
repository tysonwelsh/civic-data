6 Flood Damage Report — Director Runhaar will start the paperwork for funding on the
May 11, 2017 FEMA visit and noted the February 7 to 27, 2017 time period is
considered a “first event” by FEMA so applications will be for that event’s damages. A
“second event” may occur with the spring runoff and additional applications to FEMA will
be necessary.

There are two funding options — disaster relief (restore everything as originally built) and
mitigation (upgrade) — to apply for. Runhaar listed the following resources:

© FEMA & FHWA (Federal Highway Administration-applying for Emergency Response
money on classified roads) — public infrastructure only

© NRCS - public infrastructure, irrigation companies, some farm assistance - two
programs-Emergency Watershed Protection & Emergency Conservation Program

0 FEMA Disaster Relief — retroactive to start of event

>” FEMA Mitigation, NRCS, FHWA - expenses eligible after signed agreement

Access issues are dealt with first, then safety issues and threats to other infrastructure.
There will be long-term closures or reduced access in some areas. Runhaar warned the
Council that contract work will be required because the county can’t do it all.

Runhaar said they are now prepping for Round 2 — the snow pack runoff which is usually
more predictable. The county website has links for runoff problems/preparations.

04-25-2017

Runhaar anticipates that FEMA’s nearest date of funding return may be late June or
early July.

Runhaar announced that the Bureau of Reclamation will replace the Hyrum Dam

problems at the spillway and this will correct those problems. It is proposed the project
will take three years and there will be full road closure during that time.

In response to Council member Worthen’s question, Runhaar said farmers seeking help
with farm road damages should contact the NRCS field office in North Logan.

WHEREAS, pursuant to the Utah Industrial Facilities and Development Act,
Title 11, Chapter 17, Utah Code Annotated 1953, as amended (the “Act”), Cache County,
Utah (the “Issuer”), is authorized to issue its revenue bonds to finance the costs of any
“project” as defined in the Act to the end that the Issuer may be able to promote the
general welfare within the State of Utah; and

WHEREAS, the Act provides that a municipality or county may issue revenue
bonds for the purpose of using substantially all of the proceeds thereof to pay or to
reimburse a company for the costs of the acquisition and construction of the facilities of a
project and that title to or in such facilities may at all times remain in the company and in
such case the bonds of the municipality or county shall be secured by a pledge of one or
more notes, debentures, bonds, or other secured or unsecured debt obligations of the
company; and

WHEREAS, Issuer is authorized and empowered under the laws of the State,
including the Act, to issue revenue bonds and to enter into loan agreements, contracts,
