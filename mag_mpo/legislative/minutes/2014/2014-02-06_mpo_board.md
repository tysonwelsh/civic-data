Introductions Public Comment No Comments Seat Belt Safety - Teri Newell, UDOT Region 3 Director UDOT has added Zero Fatalities to its strategic goals. Failing to wear a seat belt was the primary factor in most fatal Utah crashers in 2013. 71 of the 219 fatalities are attributed to improper restraint, more than any other factor. With improved engineering, like the use of cable barriers, rumble strips and crash cushions has contributed to the reducing vehicles crossing the median and leaving the roadway. Over 90% of Utah crashes are due to driver behavior: driving drowsy, distracted driving, driving aggressive, driving impaired, and driving unbuckled. People not wearing a seat belt are 30% more likely to be ejected from a vehicle during a crash. 3 out 4 ejected during crash die from their injuries. In a Mountainland MPO Regional Planning February 6, 2014 Page 3 crash unbuckled passengers can become projectiles and increase the risk of hurting or killing others in the car by 40% (Insurance Institute for Highway Safety). Buckling up seat belts is something we can all live with. We can download the slideshow and videos from the Mountainland website (www.mountainland.org) or a DVD copy from Nan. Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) Modification Request - Shawn Eliot The TIP lists federally funded transportation projects and programs for the fiscal years 2014-2017, and is updated every four years, projects can so be amended into the TIP. Provo City is requesting $7 million in additional funds for Provo Westside Connector Road, for I-15 University Ave Interchange to the Provo Airport. Design work between I-15 and Provo 500 West requires a full 4 lane configuration due to retaining walls and wetland impacts. Provo City has asked to cancel and transfer the $2.5 million from the Provo ACS project that is currently in the TIP to the Westside Connector Road. The additional $4.5 million funds needed would come from the county sales tax fund. The MPO Technical Advisory Committee recommends the current TIP project, Provo ACS, be canceled and that the $2.5million transferred to the Provo Westside Connector project. They also recommend that $4.5m of county sales tax funds be approved for this project. No impacts to currently programmed projects would occur. Staff also recommends approval of Provo's request. Commissioner Gary Anderson moved to approve $4.5m of county sales tax funds for the Provo Westside Connector project, I-15 University Ave Interchange to Provo Airport, knowing that the county commission has the final decision on these funds, in order to complete committed improvements in the area. Also, cancel the Provo ACS project funds currently in the TIP and transfer the $2.5million the Provo Westside Connector project. Commissioner Larry Ellertson seconded the motion. The motion carried unanimously. Enterprise CarSharing - Jamie Clark, Enterprise CarShare Account Manger CarShare is a membership-based car sharing program with vehicles available at various locations as alternative method of transportation for people who walk, bike, take transit, or need an additional vehicle. There are four locations where you can pick up a vehicle in Utah County: Lehi FrontRunner Station, Orem FrontRunner Station, Provo FrontRunner Station, and Utah Valley University. Members make reservations online and you can reserve a vehicle for an hour, day, or even overnight. You use your membership card to unlock the vehicle and the keys are already inside. Return the vehicle back the dedicated parking spot where you found it, leave the keys and your rental is complete. Membership includes:  Access to vehicles in convenient, designated parking spots  Online access to vehicle reservations Mountainland MPO Regional Planning February 6, 2014 Page 4  Low rates for hurly, daily, or overnight usage  Vehicles available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week UDOT Active Transportation Policy & Trail Usage Patterns - Jim Price UDOT adopted an Active Transportation Policy that lays out the future direction the department will take concerning bicycle and pedestrian infrastructure in state rights-of-way. Active Transportation will be considered in funding, design, construction, operation, and maintenance on state facilities. Currently we have counters on the Provo River Parkway, College Connector, and Murdock Trails to assess the level of use of the trails. The counters use infrared heat to count individuals. Provo River Parkway Trail @ Orem: in the year 2013, May, June, July, and August monthly user totals were the highest with averages over 40,000 users each month with Saturday the highest day of the week. College Connector Trail @ Orem: counters were put in place in August 2013, in September there were 10,000 users. Murdock Canal Trail @ Pleasant Grove: counter were in place in June 2013, June, July, and August averaged over 30,000 users, again with Saturday being the popular day. UTA: No Comments Other Business a. BRT funding update: Mayor Curtis asked Mayor Gygi and Chad Eccles to conduct the discussion and excused himself from any part of the discussion. Chad stated that the prior Provo Council approved the Option 4 BRT route, that is recommended from the Environmental Assessment (EA). The Regional Planning Committee approved Option 4 and agreed to match the Federal Transit Administration's (FTA) New Start Federal Funds with $75 million local MPO funds. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning February 6, 2014 Page 5 Now with a new Provo City Council sworn in there are several members and some residents opposed to the EA's proposed BRT alignment. Another option, called Option 0 has been suggested. Chad and the consultants have been meeting with each council member to discuss the reasons that Option 4 is the EA's preferred route. Option 4 outperforms Option 0 in regards to ridership, costs, and other impacts and Option 0 would costs approximately $10 million more. Funding through FTA is very competitive and the project must demonstrate the highest number of riders and the lowest cost to be competitive nationally. UTA believes Option 4 meets the FTA requirements. As potential regional investors in the construction of the BRT at $75 million, the committee instructed the staff, to prepare a resolution that supports the preferred alternative as identified in the Environment Assessment, for Vice Chair Mayor Gygi's, signature. Staff will prepare a draft resolution and email a copy to members for their comments. b. The next meeting is scheduled for March 6, 2014.
