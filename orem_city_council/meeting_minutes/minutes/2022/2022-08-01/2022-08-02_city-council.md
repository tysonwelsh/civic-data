PERSONAL APPEARANCES
Richard Brunst, former Orem City Mayor, commented that a previous Council was in a similar situation in March of 2004 as they are currently in today. The Council listened to a feasibility report study on a project, the report stated that the revenues would exceed the cost. He then explained that in reality what happened was the cost exceeded the revenues to the tune that the City of Orem found itself in ninety million dollars of debt that they are still paying off at three million dollars per year and will be paying on up until 2040. He continued to state that in 2008 the City of Orem and others experienced a recession and the problem they experienced was the costs were much higher than they were led to believe. He explained that with thirty-three million dollars, they went into debt and property taxes went up by twenty percent.   He indicated the environment in our community and in our nation last year was such that our inflation rate was 2.5%, gross domestic product was positive for four consecutive quarters and things were well. Today life is vastly different and the inflation rate is almost 10%. Mr. Brunst concluded stating we have had two quarters of gross domestic product that is negative.  We are in a recession, and he would ask the people to vote no because this is not the right time, the costs are too high, the environment is too strenuous, and he believes that they should not move ahead.
Evan Cox stated he is also speaking on behalf of his father David Cox and read his statement:. “ I taught elementary school at Orchard, Lehi, and Sego Lily Elementaries and have grandchildren attending Orem schools and Lehi so I have a vested interest in what happens for both sides.  I have stage 4 cancer and with only a few months left to live I have been asked ‘why are you expending your limited energy and time left, to advocate for an Orem School District?’ My response:  How can I face the fore-fathers of this great nation or my pioneer ancestors in the next life if I cannot stand for creating an Orem School District.  They both broke away from powerful nations to start their own.  They did not let fear govern them.  Local citizens formed schools to educate their children, before this country no group of people had done this.  Public education was created by those who laid the foundation of this nation.  And when the pioneers came to Utah, they built schools, in their poverty, before any other building.  They believed in the freedom to choose the right way for themselves, as individuals and as communities. They made this nation, settled this intermountain area, and developed it into what we are today. There is a tremendous amount of research that shows how beneficial more modestly sized districts are, which I have compiled on my website at SmallerSchools.org. Orem will have almost fifteen thousand students. That size will put them in the largest five percent of school districts in the nation, bigger than most in Utah. The other Districts can do it, too, so Orem can. Since the last time this was considered in 2006, Orem taxpayers have paid millions in taxes and have seen pennies in return. He feels the purpose of the feasibility study was to determine if it was  financially feasible. The answer is a resounding yes. Despite best efforts from teachers and administrators, who care very much, Alpine is too big to care adequately for all the students.  We cannot wait for Alpine to split. Evidence says they will not as they have actively opposed this in the past.  The Mayor and City Council have the responsibility to care for Orem and its citizens, the study was a great star; now follow it up and let the citizens of Orem vote on it.  I call on you to allow Orem citizens the chance to determine their own future.”
 Cissy Rasmussen, an Orem resident, volunteer in schools, and founder of the group Stronger Together said she stated in February she opposed the idea of an Orem School District because of proof from studies and data that shows that it is too expensive. She sensed that it would be incredibly divisive, and it has been. She believes that it is a risky decision to let the voters decide, because that would mean the Council is telling the people of Orem that they accept a scenario of increased taxes, future bonds that will also increase taxes, diminished services, and an uncertain future. She feels by putting this on the ballot the Council is willing to accept the worst case scenario: tax increases between 30-40% just to maintain current levels of services and not including any startup costs or improvements.  By putting this on the ballot the Council is saying  they approve of spending even more money on a plan that didn’t measure resident interest first. She indicated the city spent thirty thousand dollars for a company that never conducted a feasibility study before, then approved fifty thousand dollars for a public relations firm, and at least a hundred and twenty thousand dollars for an appointed attorney whose main job has been to work on this. She believes it is an irresponsible use of taxpayer money and she is dismayed by the energy, time, and money that has already gone into this while other city issues are neglected. She expressed  she is heartbroken for the teachers who are overwhelmed by the stress of the issue and is frustrated because this appears to be nothing more than a chance to get back, get even  with the district for past grievances. Ms. Rasmussen concluded by stating that using the schools as a pawn in this game is disrespectful to Orem parents, school employees, PTAs, low-income residents, seniors on fixed incomes, and young families who are too busy to pay attention to city issues.  She believes the Council wants what's best for Orem but this isn’t it. She requested that the Council vote no.
Blyden Loutensock indicated he is a former teacher from southern California teaching secondary education. He said that he has taught in three school districts, two of them were smaller districts. With his varied experience he said he has the distinct advantage of knowing the pros and cons of a smaller district versus a larger district and why a smaller district in our city is the better way to go. He said that in his experience, larger districts y had less control and smaller districts had more control.     He feels whether or not Orem should create their own school district is a decision that the citizens of Orem should decide, not Alpine School District and citizens of other surrounding communities. He stated that of the tax dollars paid by Orem that are allocated for education, 100% of those funds, not 17%, should stay in Orem for our schools and for our children. Mr. Loutensock concluded by asking why hasn’t ASD fixed the schools in Orem that have been deemed as not being earthquake safe?  That’s an ongoing issue that hasn’t been solved and yet we are building other schools.  We the citizens of Orem need and want our own school district.  Vote to put this on the ballot so we the citizens of Orem can decide what’s best for our children.
Becky Buckley, Orem resident, stated she is entering her sixteenth year as a teacher in the Alpine School District and has been involved with many school programs. She would like the Council to think of what is best for the students. She thinks that Alpine School District supports students and teachers by:
·         Giving technology grants to narrow the digital divide and believes it is important for children to learn how to use technology effectively and appropriately to become responsible and contributing members of society.
·         They currently have professional learning community coaches who work with teachers and individual students to improve instruction and learning particularly with literacy and math.
·         They have given district-wide opportunities to collaborate with other teachers, school counselors, psychologists and social workers to help struggling students and teachers receive the support they need to thrive.
·         They have subject specific professional development partnerships with BYU to educate and integrate new teachers.
·          They have professional learning communities where teachers look at the student’s data and best practices weekly to improve learning.
She stated that these things take years to replicate and funding to develop. She feels the children, teachers, and community are still recovering from the pandemic and that pulling these services and others out from under them will cripple their education further. She inquired how an Orem district can guarantee these services that already exist without significantly raising taxes. Miss Buckley concluded by stating that taxes will go up and services will go down and teachers will leave. She asked the Council to vote no. 
Kathy Minster, an Orem resident, stated thirty-eight years ago there was a problem of overcrowding in her child’s elementary school. She and some of the other parents were very concerned and so they went to Alpine School District and voiced their concerns. She stated the kindness shown to her at that time was so wonderful. Fast-forward thirty-five years and another issue came up in her school and so she went to the Alpine School Board. She felt she was treated poorly and the opposing side was treated wonderfully. Their representative from Orem did not look up from her paper to look in her eyes as she talked to the school board. S Ms. Minster concluded by stating she is concerned because the board members have so many cities and feels they cannot handle them all.
 Paul Thompson, an Orem resident, stated that he is opposed voting in November to create an Orem-only school district. The study completed by the DEC consultants is flawed and neither one of the consultants has had direct experience with Utah school finance. He feels the report deserves a review of a third-party of unbiased professionals who are experts in Utah school finance. In the meeting on July 28th on the Orem feasibility study, one of the consultants said there are six single city school districts in Utah. What he didn’t say is that two of those districts have decided in the past that they were too small and tried to combine with another district -  Logan and Ogden. In both cases the attempts to combine were rejected by voters of the county districts with whom they were trying to combine. He stated once you become a single city district, it’s extremely hard, if not impossible to correct the mistake. He stated the Salt Lake City district is a single city district. In the 1980s they had four high schools. Enrollment declined so much that the district concluded they could no longer support four high schools and after a painful process they decided to close down South High, and it was a painful procedure and the residents of the high school area have been upset with the superintendent at the school board ever since. He believes that Orem has a similar problem, that if they create an Orem only school district, they will have three high schools in a city that can only support two. He indicated Provo School District has more high school students in their district than Orem and they have two high schools. Mr. Thompson concluded by explaining that Orem has three high schools and seven hundred and fifty of the students in those high schools are from other cities. He encouraged the Council to vote no.

Karie Sigua, an Orem resident, stated she has served in the PTA and at her children’s school for over twenty years. She stated a few months ago she shared her experiences about an issue at Mountain View High School and the process that she went through that led to a federal investigation by the U.S. Department of Education Office of Civil Rights (OCR). She explained that in that statement she made a request to the City Council to consider putting a vote on the November ballot, regardless of the feasibility study results, to provide a safe place for residents to voice their desires without fear of retaliation. She indicated currently two of the six ongoing OCR investigations with Alpine School District are for retaliation. She feels her parent group has faced many types of retaliation since filing a complaint with Alpine School District and OCR two years ago. She stated that the decision the Council will be making is to give residents their voice and their right to vote, and that it doesn’t matter who is for or against the split.She asked the council to make history be the first city council to give constituents the opportunity to exercise their right to vote for Orem’s future in a safe environment without fear of retaliation.  Ms. Sigua concluded that she believes that if the Council were to make the decision to not let the people of Orem vote that it would be taking away their rights..   
Laurie Forsyth, an Orem resident, stated she is opposed to leaving the Alpine School District. She  questioned why this decision was such a rush.  When she pressed officials they indicated that they favor leaving the school district before an education bond is issued so Orem wouldn’t have to pay a share of that debt. She understands why some would favor that but she is concerned that the best way to describe the outcome would be out of the frying pan and into the fire. She feels the consultants have done their best to make estimates and projections about unknowns and have been optimistic that financial and other challenges resulting from a division could be solved through shared service agreements, but she feels predictions are difficult and we do not need to rely on them. 
Brad Daw expressed his love and appreciation for meetings and points of view on both sides of the debate. He indicated they did not have all of the services but they had a great district and received an amazing education. He stated education is the most local thing that we have and concluded by commenting he wants what is best for his child and he believes in his heart and in his core that the best government is the government closest to the people. 
Erin Markel, an Orem resident, stated she is a mother of five children who attend Orem schools. She expressed she is a hair stylist by profession and in her line of work she listens to people. She said that she has been listening to the previous meetings and she has been listening to what her neighbors and some teachers have been saying and she has formed an opinion that Orem should not form its own district. She feels they will lose many amazing teachers. She indicated her kids have had an enormous benefit from fantastic teachers and administration at all of these schools. She feels that an Orem-only district would be unstable. She believes they need a concrete plan about how they can ensure all children’s needs are being met. Ms. Markel concluded stating she does not understand the point of forming an Orem only district if Orem cannot provide for the needs of Orem kids and Orem teachers.
 
Jody Hassen, an Orem resident, stated when she started in the Alpine School District her son was in kindergarten. She said she just heard from ASD that class sizes will be rising and feels it means students who don’t meet the status quo will not have their needs met.  This causes her to believe the Alpine School District is too big and cannot help those unique students who are either above or below and can only help the status quo students. Teachers are stressed and have so much to do and are not feeling supported from their district. She indicated a teacher wrote to her and indicated she is scared to speak out against their co-workers and has not felt supported by Alpine School District. The teacher indicated they felt overworked and ignored by Alpine School District, and the students are just numbers.  This teacher felt ASD is too big, too wasteful and teachers are feeling scared by the lies they are hearing.  She then cited the Geneva roof as an example. ASD decided to replace the roof and then they decided to demolish the school. She feels ASD is too big and asked for the constitutional right to vote.
 Sara Hacken introduced herself as a school board representative for the east side of Orem. She stated she represents thirteen schools and eighteen thousand people voted for her as school board representative. She has visited every school many times and she is here to tell the Council Alpine School District gives support to those schools that only a large district can give. She provided an example of the high Schools in Orem and said they are the three smallest high schools in Alpine School District. They are so small that they do not generate enough funds from the state to fully fund a program. She indicated this coming year Orem High is getting six extra teachers. She believes that they would need to cut those teachers if they had an Orem-only school district and she compared it to losing an entire math or English department. She voiced her concern that it would also cut programs and do damage to the football team or arts and music programs. The same tough decisions would need to be made at other Orem high schools. She feels a smaller school district will not have as many options. She stated that last year even during COVID the graduation rate at Mountain View High School was 92%, Orem High School was 94.7% and Timpanogos High School was 91%. Ms. Hacken concluded by stating these are great schools but they need great support and she does not feel they will get it from an Orem school district.

Mr. Carlisle  concluded by saying that City Councils have looked at this issue before and he wanted the opportunity to get it out there and let the people of Orem know about it.

Helena Kleinlein, Orem resident, stated there was so much contention over Orem paying DEC $30 thousand dollars for the feasibility when Alpine paid Y2 Analytics $60 thousand to evaluate the likelihood of passing a $600 million dollar bond. She indicated there are many facts Y2 reported to the school board that she felt were pertinent.  She stated the study showed that American Fork and Pleasant Grove are likely not to pass the bond regardless of their convincing language, but the study showed that one demographic area values a well-educated populace and they would decide the outcome of the bond, Orem. She mentioned that Y2 added that the only threat to bond failure was some enthusiastic distraction from the bond, like Orem offering a split to their voters on the same ballot as the bond. Y2 recognizes that if ASD loses Orem bond support, the entire bond will fail. She stated this explains why Ada Wilson and Sara Hacken felt a sense of urgency to organize a PIC.  Ada said, “Normally a school board member would not be interfering or advocating in municipal affairs, but this is different.”  Ms Kleinlein asked how is it different? Because it is over half a billion dollars for infrastructure that will not benefit Orem.  Ms Kleinlein concluded by stating the United States of America is a constitutional republic and governing by survey is exactly what is expected of our elected officials.  She urged the Mayor and City Council to let the residents decide.
