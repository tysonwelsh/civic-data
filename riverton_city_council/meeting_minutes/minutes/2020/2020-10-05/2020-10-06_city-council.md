   a. Proposed Ordinance regarding Freedom to Assemble

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, proposed a new ordinance regarding public gatherings related to
protesting. He said the ordinance includes the purposes, definitions, time, place and manner of
free speech activities, expected appeals, applications, First Amendment guidelines and targeted
residential demonstrations/ picketing.

Mr. Carter stated that the government allows cities to regulate the “Time, manner and place” of
these types of gatherings, allowing residents to maintain their first amendment rights within the
bounds of public safety concerns, and without property damage or other negative impacts.

Mayor Staggs clarified that the proposed ordinance would be presented at a future date to the
Council for approval, and that it was not a land use item and would not be presented to the
Planning Commission. Mayor also requested that the term “peaceable” be injected in the
ordinance.

    b. Discussion of Maximum Allowed Square Footage for Accessory Structures

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director explained that Council had recently had
discussions regarding the method of measuring for allowing accessory structure permits. In this
proposal Mr. Lethbridge explained that the current method of measurement used to determine
the size of these structures was based on the percentage of the rear yard size (approximately
25%). The proposed measurement method changes would be to calculate a percentage of the
total lot size. He said that other cities use between 8 and 12% as their standard. He said that
Staff recommended adjusting the measurement allowed for accessory structures to be
calculated at 10% of the lot size.

A brief discussion took place and Councilmembers felt 10% was a good number to present to
the Planning Commission.

    c. Ranked Choice Voting

Councilmembers Tawnee McCay and Tish Buroker introduced Kory Holdaway, Utah Rank
Choice (RCV) Voting. Mr. Holdaway explained RCV is a voting program cities can opt in if they
choose. Since 2018 two Utah cities have opted to use RCV, Vineyard and Payson.

Mr. Holdaway stated that he feels that RCV was gaining favor and was successful in the Utah
County cities that used it in the 2019 Election. He said the key benefit is cost savings because
the need for a primary election can be eliminated. He explained other cost savings such as the
elimination of two campaign seasons, which he said benefits the candidates and they
appreciate the savings as well as the citizens. Additionally he stated that the type of debates
that occur in a RCV election are more collegiate. There was an example used of two opposing
candidates for the same position in an RCV run election who stated, “Hey if I am not your
number 1 choice let me be your number 2 choice.” He said another benefit of RCV was the
ability to get to a majority.

15. Discussion/Action Items

    a. Resolution No. 20-62 - Approving a Riverton Fire Service Area to Fund First
       Responder Services Within Riverton City

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, and Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, explained that
Riverton City has completed a lengthy process to create the Riverton Fire Service Area. The
City Council adopted Resolution No. 20-31 that formally commenced the process to begin the
creation of the Riverton Fire Service Area, or “RFSA” on May 5, 2020.

Mr. Carter further explained that the City Council held a public hearing on June 16, 2020 which
allowed the public to ask questions and obtain further information regarding the issues
contained in resolution No.17-42, pursuant to See: Utah Code Ann. §17B-1-210. This hearing
also commenced the time for persons to file protests against creation of the district if they so
desired. The 60-day deadline to file protests (following the August 15 public hearing) lapsed on
August 15, 2020. Riverton City received no protests from the public by this date, nor has the
City received any protests afterward.

Mr. Carter said that in order to perfect creation of the Riverton Fire Service Area, one of the
remaining steps was to file an appropriate local entity plat with the State of Utah, Office of the
Lieutenant Governor, which was ordinarily required to occur within ten days of filing a resolution
approving creation of the district. However, said districts, once approved commonly run into
problems with filing said plat within the ten day deadline. Mr. Carter said that Staff has done
what it can to the date of this memorandum to assure a smooth filing process but we don’t yet
have the verification we need from Salt Lake County to be fully assured. He said that, in a
“worst case scenario,” an entity which cannot file a plat within the 10 day filing period simply
needs to adopt another approval resolution to reset the filing period, and the appropriate plat
would be accepted by the Lt. Governor’s Office at a later date.

Councilmembers made a few technical and grammatical changes. Mr. Carter said he would
make the changes and prepare the final draft for the next City Council Meeting.

Councilmember Stewart MOVED that the City Council adopt Resolution No. 20-62 -
Approving the Creation of the Riverton Fire Service Area to Fund First Responder
Services within Riverton City, noting the adjustments to the statements as discussed.
Councilmember Wells SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the
motion; there being none, he called for a roll call vote and the vote was as follows:
Councilmembers Buroker-Yes, McCay-Yes, McDougal-Yes, Stewart-Yes, and Wells-Yes. The
motion passed unanimously.
