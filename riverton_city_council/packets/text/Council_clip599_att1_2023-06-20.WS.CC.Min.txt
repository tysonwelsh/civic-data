 3            Resolution No. 23-47 – Adopting the Strategic Priorities
 4
 5   Josh Lee, Communications Director, said that changes were made to the Strategic Priorities
 6   document in February 2023 but it was never formally adopted by the Council.
 7
 8   Mayor Pro Tem McDougal asked if it was necessary to formally adopt the Strategic Priorities
 9   document. In response, Ryan Carter, City Attorney, said that resolutions are the official means
10   through which the Council expresses its position or makes official statements on matters related
11   to Utah law. He emphasized that resolutions require a majority vote of the Council to be adopted
12   and the document should have been formally brought back to the Council for ratification. He
13   highlighted the need for open and transparent communication between staff and the Council,
14   ensuring that the final product aligns with the collective understanding and direction of the
15   Council.
16
17   Mayor Pro Tem McDougal asked if the Strategic Priorities document was part of the budget.
18   Mr. Carter said it is a separate document from the budget but should be included on the website
19   on the same page as the budget.
20
21   Mayor Pro Tem McDougal summarized that the purpose of the Strategic Priorities document
22   was for the Council, with input from staff, to establish the direction the City will take. The
23   document should be adopted by resolution, but can be changed if a majority of the Council
24   desires to bring up a particular subject. He asked Mr. Lee to review the changes that were made
25   as of February 2023 and said future adjustments can be made as needed, and a follow-up
26   strategic priority meeting will be scheduled for January.
27
28   Mr. Lee said that the proposed updates to the strategic priorities document were as follows:
29
30       Objective 2.2, bullet 1: The city has a verbal commitment from the landowners, and a review
31       of the bridge's architectural design is being conducted. This can be considered as in
32       progress, and an update will be included.
33
34       Objective 2.2, bullet 5: This item should be marked as "on radar" or "in progress." Similar
35       updates will be made to other items that are not being regularly updated.
36
37       Objective 3.2, bullet 2: More information is needed for this item, and it will be updated
38       accordingly.
39
40       Objective 3.2: Regarding the reverse in business participation in groups and events, it is
41       recommended to revise the wording to ensure that city policies encourage Riverton
42       business participation and representation in events. This broadens the scope and
43       encourages involvement.
44
45   Councilmember McCay said it is important to incorporate the changes and updates that were
46   discussed during the sessions held earlier this year. This ensures that the document accurately
47   reflects the work and decisions made by the Council.
48
49   Councilmember Pierucci suggested the language should be broadened to more
50   comprehensively encourage, prioritize, and engage Riverton businesses. This broader
51   approach would involve actively promoting and supporting Riverton businesses as part of a
     Page | 5 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     June 20, 2023




 1   comprehensive economic development plan. The focus would extend beyond simply
 2   communicating about Riverton city events and opportunities for engagement, and instead seek
 3   to actively involve and support local businesses.
 4
 5   Mr. Carter suggested getting the transcripts from previous meetings to capture all of the
 6   changes that were made.
 7
 8   Mayor Pro Tem McDougal agreed and said once Mr. Carter has compiled the information and
 9   provided it to the Council, they can review the document and propose verbiage changes as
10   needed. It would be beneficial to schedule a work session to thoroughly discuss and finalize the
11   Strategic Priorities document, ensuring that all necessary updates and revisions are
12   incorporated. Once the document is finalized, it can be formally approved by the Council. This
13   approach allows for collaborative input and ensures that the document accurately reflects the
14   Council's intentions and priorities.
15
16   Mayor Pro Tem McDougal motioned to table the adoption of the Strategic Priorities document.
17   Councilmember Buroker seconded this motion. Mayor Pro Tem McDougal asked for discussion
18   to the Motion.

20   Councilmember McCay stated that the Strategic Priorities had already been discussed in
21   previous work sessions, which amounted to around four or five sessions. She questioned the
22   need to allocate additional work session time for the same topic. She proposed a change and
23   addition to one objective in the Strategic Priorities document. She emphasized the importance
24   of discussing and agreeing upon the Strategic Priorities and conducting a vote without further
25   delay. She suggested that the previous work sessions had already covered the necessary
26   groundwork for this decision.

33   2023.

35   Councilmember McKay expressed a desire to make changes to the wording of bullets in

41   Mr. Carter discussed the process and principles applied when issuing an RFP for food trucks
42   and businesses for Town Days. He explained that the RFP included a preference for local
43   businesses, and once the deadline was reached and the booths were filled, priority was given to
44   a variety of different types of foods and local businesses. He said the Council acknowledged
45   that not all businesses on the waiting list would likely be able to secure a booth due to high
46   competition. With this in mind, the staff proposed notifying local businesses in advance of future
47   RFPs to ensure they have an opportunity to participate. At the previous meeting, the Council
48   also examined a visual layout of the event, highlighting the capacity and power supply
49   limitations for booths. He noted that increasing the number of booths could potentially impact
50   the quality of the event due to power constraints and noise from generators. He emphasized the
51   need to consider the implications and limitations before making any changes to the layout or

23            Resolution No. 23-48 – Adopting the Non-Reciprocal Utilities Transactions for
24            Fiscal Year 2023-2024
25
26   Mr. Hicks acknowledged that the wording in the annual notice required by the state auditor's
27   office for the non-reciprocal utilities may be complex. However, he has chosen to keep the
28   wording consistent as it has been approved by auditors in the past. The purpose of the notice is
29   to clarify that the City is not transferring cash between funds, but rather accounting for revenue
30   within each fund separately. This accounting transaction is necessary to ensure compliance with
31   state auditing requirements.
32
33   Mayor Pro Tem McDougal further explained that the dollar amounts assigned to services in the
34   annual notice are not actual transfers of money but rather a means of transparently showing
35   residents the costs associated with those services. This practice allows residents to understand
36   the specific dollar amounts allocated to items such as water usage or secondary services.
37
