City Council would recess after the consent agenda to convene a closed session to discuss the
character or professional competence of individuals.

performing arts building. He had heard from both the Cultural Diversity Committee as well as the
community councils. Mayor Overson responded that the administration would be meeting with

        Mayor Overson conducted the Pledge of Allegiance before offering a reverence.

          1.2        Mayor’s Report

      Mayor Overson pointed out that the supply of donated PPE kits had been shared with six
schools so far. Donations were still being coordinated with other schools. In addition to schools,
the kits had been distributed to the YMCA, Taylorsville Food Pantry, Taylorsville Senior Center,
Taylorsville Museum and Plymouth View Senior Center.

The Mayor was aware that the committees were anxious to get to work in 2021 and to begin
meeting in person once again. She thanked those volunteers and the councilmembers who sat on
the committees in an advisory capacity. It was still unclear how long the construction of the
performing arts center and the Centennial Plaza would take. She would keep the Council apprised
and recommended that they coordinate committee activities with Council Coordinator, Kris
Heineman.

     John Hiskey had been advocating vigorously for Taylorsville. He was very excited to watch
HB 244 (Transportation funding) and see the result. Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) were a
hot topic and there were various bills that if passed, would diminish local control. The city’s four
legislators had been in almost daily communication with City Administration and Mayor Overson
appreciated that greatly. She also expressed appreciation to Mr. Hiskey and his fine work and
asked if the Council would be interested in hearing directly from Mr. Hiskey at the next meeting.
They indicated they would.

Communications Manager Kim Horiuchi had created a new slide show that was being pushed out
to various social media platforms. It highlighted the city’s many challenging circumstances and
accomplishments of 2020.

     Mayor Overson expressed appreciation to the Unified Fire Authority, members of which were
present at the meeting. The heavy rescue team housed in Station #117 had recently been involved
in the rescue of a woman who had crashed and been trapped in her vehicle in Big Cottonwood
Canyon. The city was very proud of the team, which specialized in structural collapse and confined
space rescue, among other things. The station housed not only firefighters but also Ladder #17,

Rescue #17, Engine #17, and the Fire Prevention Division. She appreciated the great partnership
that the City had with UFA.

Taylorsville Police continued to move forward with preparations to begin operations on July 1.
The City was currently hiring a lieutenant and would soon post the positions of Records Manager
and Office Manager. Jay Ziolkowski would present an update to the Council in two weeks. She

            1.2.1 Unified Police Officer Recognition
Chief Dwayne Anjewierden invited Sgt. Melody Cutler to present information regarding an
incident that UPD Officer Jesse Allen had recently been involved in. Sgt. Cutler explained that
although Officer Allen did not think what he had done was a big deal, it was. She then explained
that both Officer Allen and Murray Police Sgt. Jason Kunz were stopped at a traffic light recently
when they noticed a vehicle begin to drift into the intersection despite a red light. They both
realized that the driver, a Taylorsville High School student, appeared to be having a medical
problem and was unconscious as her vehicle was rolling into rush hour traffic. They attempted to
position their vehicles so that they could bump her vehicle and get it to stop but their attempt was
unsuccessful. Ultimately, Officer Allen pulled in front of her vehicle and allowed it to impact his
truck, thereby stopping the victim’s vehicle from rolling further into traffic. They were then able
to render aid and get the driver the medical help she needed.

Officer Allen stepped to the podium and explained that he and Sgt Kunz just happened to be at the
right place at the right time. He was glad that Miss Canepari was doing well and that everything
worked out.

          3.1        Unified Police Department Report –Acting Precinct Chief Dwayne Anjewierden

      Acting UPD Taylorsville Precinct Chief Dwayne Anjewierden explained that Officer Allen
strongly disliked being referred to as a hero, so his fellow officers had chosen to refer to him as a
superstar.

Chief Anjewierden then went on to report statistical data from the first quarter of FY20-21.
Overall, crime had trended downward quite a bit during the quarantine period earlier in the
pandemic, although domestic disturbances had increased. He presented data specific to each
council district as well as traffic accident and citation data for all of Taylorsville. The number of
traffic citations issued was reduced, and warnings were increased not only for social distancing
purposes but also in an attempt to alleviate the burden on the justice court which, as with all other
courts, faced significant operational challenges during the pandemic.
