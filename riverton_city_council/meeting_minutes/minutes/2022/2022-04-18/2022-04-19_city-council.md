       Legislative Update

Rob Jolley, RRJ Consulting, LLC, reported on the legislative session.

                focus on water conservation. The City can receive a 70% match for secondary
                water meters and the application process is open. Mayor Staggs reported that
                Riverton City had already made an application for this money.
               HB 282 – Prohibits cities from prohibiting water-wise landscape.

                    HB 151 – This bill initially made an effort to prohibit retail incentives. It passed
                     with language that said retail incentives are allowed if there is a housing
                     component in the development with 10% of low-income housing.
                    HB 462 – Housing Affordability Amendments. The legislature wanted to mandate
                     zoning at transportation nodes and require each city to provide low-income
                     housing. This bill passed with language that creates a new criteria for cities to
                     adopt in their general plan. It requires two additional criteria to receive
                     transportation improvement funds and included a provision for accessory
                     dwelling units.

Mr. Jolley stated that 13400 South and Bangerter plans should be finalized in the next couple of
months.

Councilmember Wells said there is a disconnect between what developers are willing to do and
the housing stock needed in the state for affordable housing. Mr. Jolley explained that HB 462
focuses on transit cities that will need to adopt stationary plans.

Mayor Staggs said the requirement is area mean income (AMI) at 70-80%. Ryan Carter, City
Attorney, responded that the City had met this requirement. He explained that once the City
has done all it can to allow for the density, there is nothing more by way of a mandate that they
can do to fund it. The rising cost of construction materials has made it difficult for developers to
build affordable housing.

Mayor Staggs thanked Mr. Jolley for his work throughout the legislative session.

Councilmember McDougal asked about restrictions on auto malls and car sales. Mr. Jolley said
that no adjustments have been made to these restrictions for a couple of years and thinks there
is a small area along Mountain View where car dealerships could be built. Mayor Staggs
responded that used car dealerships can be placed anywhere, however, the Franchise Advisory
Board must approve new car dealerships. Mayor Staggs asked Mr. Jolley to follow up on this
issue.

Mayor Staggs said that HB2 has $25 million appropriated for capital improvement projects and
water projects. He asked Mr. Hicks and staff to see if any projects in the five-year plan would
qualify. Mr. Jolley responded that there were $1.4 billion worth of applications in the first round.
Mr. Jolley said that a better source to look at would be the economic opportunity funds.

Mayor Staggs said he is waiting to see what Riverton will need to pay for the betterment of
13400 South and Bangerter.

reported that Utah Lake currently sits at 60.6% compared to 78% last year at this same time.
She said the below compromise level will be reached much sooner than last year.

Ms. Olson explained that the City has already been pumping water and residents are currently
using this water. Mayor Staggs asked Councilmembers if the City should consider turning off

Public Hearings

          Ordinance No. 22-13 – Perry Rezone, Approximately 3.79-Acres at 4623 West
          12600 South, From SP-Commercial to Commercial Gateway. Applicant-Michael
          Perry.

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained the public hearing was for a
Rezone application brought by the applicant for 3.79-acres at 4623 West 12600 South. The
subject property is a vacant parcel on the southwest corner of 12600 South and 4570 West with
about 600-ft of frontage on 12600 South. 12600 South is the Riverton-Herriman City Boundary
in this area. To the north of the subject property is Maverik convenience store and about 10-
acres of vacant commercial land. To west is the Big-O tire store. To the south is Riverton City
Park and Jordan Valley School District Property.

Mr. Lethbridge said the property is currently zoned “SP-Commercial” which means Specific Plan
Commercial. It was zoned commercial on May 16, 2000 as part of the Western Springs
Specific Plan which adopted a Special Zoning Designation for about 182-acres along Riverton
City’s west side. The Western Springs Specific Plan included unique zoning and land-use
regulations for both residential and commercial uses. The residential portions of Western
Springs were completed before 2017. The lone commercial project, Big-O Tires, was built in
2016. The applicant is requesting this rezone to Commercial Gateway to change the land
uses available on the vacant commercial property. The Western Springs Plan anticipated future
changes to the plan or properties might be necessary and made provisions and allowances for
such changes.

The General Plan designation for the area is Community Commercial. Zoning designations that
are appropriate for the General Plan Designation are Commercial Gateway (C-G), Commercial
Neighborhood (C-N) and Commercial professional Office (C-PO). The applicant is requesting a
rezone to the C-G zone,

Councilmember Stewart asked what the concerns were when this was previously brought
before the Council. Mr. Lethbridge said they wanted commercial regional zoning which was
inappropriate to the property.

Councilmember McCay asked if Mr. Lethbridge knew the plans were for this area.              Mr.
Lethbridge said that they have plans for a multi-tenant retail building.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.

Councilmember Buroker MOVED that the Riverton City Council adopt Ordinance No. 22-13
– Perry Rezone, Approximately 3.79-Acres at 4623 West 12600 South, From SP-

Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion. There being none, he called for a roll-call
