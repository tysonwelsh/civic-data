> UDOT SR-30 Environmental Study — Mike Pepper introduced Rod Terry who gave

a quick update on the study noting that a project to add passing lanes on SR-30 just
went out to bid and some lighting will be added to the intersection at Cornish.

Pepper presented the Study Overview emphasizing it is NOT an environmental study
and does not include any realignment changes. The Overview included physical,
operational and environmental considerations and outreach to stakeholders such as
local governments, law enforcement, special interest groups, PacifiCorp, the trucking
industry and Utah State University.

Executive Buttars, Council member Zilles and Vice Chair Erickson questioned
whether the satisfaction with current capacity will apply several years down the road.
How far ahead are we looking? Pepper replied projections to 2040 were looked at.

Vice Chair Erickson asked what the timeline for future steps is. Pepper said that
after the study is completed a schedule will be drafted. Construction is probably 2-3
years out.

e Nautica Subdivision — Director Runhaar reported this subdivision applied for a

« Discussion — County Gravel Roads-County O! ‘ions for Maintenance
and/or Extension of Services — Council member Zilles asked if the county can
allow an individual to build a road at his/her own expense and agree to maintain
the road. Or could an individual pay more on his/her property taxes to maintain the
toad? Zilles asked this to determine if it would be a way to allow an individual to
build where there is not a currently serviced county road.

Director Runhaar stated there is no vehicle or channel for the county to receive
those additional monies. A Special Improvement District (SID) is the only vehicle
to accomplish Zilles’ proposal. A SID requires all surrounding property owners’
approval and participation and a board of supervisors as well as separate
accounting procedures.

Attorney Edwards indicated if the road is not maintained by the individual, the
county is responsible for any injury to life or property that occurs.

Council member Potter recommended contacting UAC to find out if other counties
have faced this situation and how they have dealt with it.

. Discussion — Open Public Comment at County Council Meetings — Chairman
Merrill asked for Council members’ opinions on allowing public comment at each

Council meeting. Merrill proposed a time at the beginning of the meeting to allow
public comment on any subject. No definitive answers could be given and the
individual would be referred to the appropriate Council member or county
department. Merrill proposed a six-month trial period.

Council members expressed concerns that too much time taken at the beginning of
the meeting would disrupt the agenda items, particularly public hearings which are
scheduled for a specific time. The Council also noted that their emails and phone
numbers are public knowledge and any citizen can contact them about their
concerns.

Cache County Council
03-29-2016

Council member Potter stated public comment was tried in other public
offices/councils he has participated in and were not successful. Potter asks
citizens who have concerns to give them to him in writing or by email so he can
have something in hand to take to the appropriate persons to address the issue.
If the public voices a concern in a public meeting and the Council cannot respond
specifically to that issue, the individual will go away frustrated.

Generally, the Council was not in favor of the public comment period.

CHILD ABUSE PREVENTION MONTH
PROCLAMATION

CACHE COUNTY’S GREATEST ASSET IS OUR CHILDREN,
NOW AND FOR THE FUTURE.

WHEREAS; ALL CHILDREN deserve to grow up in a safe and nurturing
environment to ensure they reach their full potential.

WHEREAS; CHILD ABUSE is a serious and growing problem affecting millions of
our nation’s children and thousands of children in Utah annually; and,

WHEREAS; CHILD ABUSE respects no racial, religious, class or geographic

WHEREAS: IT IS IMPORTANT for all citizens of Cache County to become more
aware of child abuse and the critical need for prevention within their respective
neighborhoods and community; and,

WHEREAS; DECREASING the occurrence of child abuse relies upon the efforts of
every individual in order to make a positive, substantial impact upon the children of today,
who will become the leaders of tomorrow;

THEREFORE; |, » do
hereby proclaim April 2016 as Child Abuse Prevention Month. I support child abuse
prevention efforts and education, and I encourage all citizens to actively help protect our
children and work to create strong families within this community.
