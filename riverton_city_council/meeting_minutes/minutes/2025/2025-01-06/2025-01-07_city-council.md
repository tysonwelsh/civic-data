Mayor/Council Reports

Councilmember Buroker provided updates on several matters, including a nearly finalized City
policy allowing residents to place memorial plaques on existing City benches for $1,000, with
placement locations predetermined. She also reported ongoing discussions within Unified Fire
regarding fee increases, transitioning from three-person to four-person crews, and clarifying
county contributions. Additionally, she attended a policy meeting, where a project on training
public officials in post-disaster emergency preparedness was discussed, with plans to arrange a
future work session to share the information.

Councilmember Pierucci reported on his role on the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District
Board, noting recent boundary changes that no longer include representation for Bluffdale. He
informed residents that the EPA is reassessing safety levels of fluoride in water, a topic
discussed at both the district board meetings and city level.

Councilmember McCay – No Report.

Councilmember McDougal announced that the Trans-Jordan annual all-day meeting has been
rescheduled to February 6. The meeting will cover updates on the opening of the Sandy transfer
station and the construction of a new transfer station in preparation for transitioning from the
current Trans-Jordan landfill to the new site in approximately six years.

Councilmember Haymond reported attending a Utah League of Cities and Towns training
focused on Redevelopment Agencies (RDA) and their applications. He highlighted the City's
effective use of RDA funding in previous projects, such as the one across the street, and
emphasized the potential benefits RDAs can offer to the City for future initiatives.

Mayor Staggs announced the City’s annual Strategic Planning Meeting, scheduled for Saturday,
which is open to the public and expected to run from 8 a.m. to 2 or 3 p.m. He explained the
importance of the City’s strategic plan, a four-year initiative developed with input from a biennial
resident survey. He highlighted financial benefits achieved since the City began self-providing
law enforcement in 2018, saving approximately $21 million in property taxes by exiting the
countywide taxing district. The City has also maintained some of the lowest utility fees in the
valley, supported by conscientious planning and strong sales tax revenue, particularly from
developments like Mountain View Village.

Additionally, Mayor Staggs issued a statement reaffirming the City's commitment to upholding
the law in cooperation with county, state, and federal partners. He emphasized that the City is
not a sanctuary city and will prosecute criminal activity regardless of an individual's immigration
status, working closely with federal agencies to ensure enforcement.

Unified Fire Authority Presentation

Chief Wade Watkins presented an overview of Riverton City's 2024 EMS and fire response
data, noting 2,168 incidents, a 7.49% increase from previous years, with half classified as
priority one calls requiring urgent advanced life support (ALS). The top address for 911
responses, including care facilities and senior living centers, received 90 calls, with the top 20
addresses accounting for at least 10 each. He also addressed 441 business inspections,
describing their benefits, including situational awareness, verifying Knox box keys, and ensuring
compliance with the International Fire Code.

Additionally, Chief Watkins recounted a November 9 garage fire where two firefighters sustained
injuries due to gasoline, outlining the department’s incident review process, which includes open
communication via blue and green sheets to evaluate performance and identify improvements.

         Ordinance No. 25-02 – Sycamore Glen South Rezone, Rezone of approximately
         1.04-acres located at 118551 South Redwood Road from RR-22 (Residential ½
         acre) to RM-14-SD

Mr. Lethbridge presented the rezone application for a property located south of the 11800 South
and Redwood Road intersection, currently zoned RR-22. The surrounding area includes the
Sycamore Glen development to the north and east, which has a mixed residential designation
(SD). To the south, the property is commercially zoned but remains undeveloped, while the
property to the north is vacant but approved for 10 townhomes.

The general plan for the property designates it for professional office use, though this direction
has not been followed in the area's primary development. The applicant, involved in the
Sycamore Glen development, proposed rezoning the property to RM-14, with a Special
Development (SD) overlay, which limits the property to a maximum of 10 units, mirroring the
adjacent development. The SD designation includes a stipulation that the parcel size cannot be
reduced below one acre, with no more than five contiguous units permitted.

The Planning Commission recommended approval with the SD designations, including a
condition that shared access points with the adjacent commercial property be coordinated and
compliant with existing easements. This condition ensures that the shared access is properly
addressed in the development process. Staff recommended adding this condition to the zoning
approval, which the Planning Commission had already considered.

Dave Morton, Applicant, expressed gratitude for the support he received throughout the
Sycamore Glen project, which began approximately four years ago. He highlighted efforts to
listen to neighborhood concerns and assemble four or five parcels while avoiding duplication of
services, particularly regarding detention ponds. He emphasized that this collaboration between
public services and private industry created a positive outcome and expressed pride in the
Sycamore Glen development and upcoming improvements to the detention pond, which will
benefit the local residents.

         Ordinance No. 25-03 – Pelaez Rezone, Amending the General Plan Designation
         ad Zoning for Approximately 0.96-acres located at 3516 West 12600 South from
         R-1 Residential Zone to C-G (Commercial Gateway) Zone

Mr. Lethbridge presented the proposed rezone for the property located at 3516 West 12600
South, which is just under one acre in size. The property is currently zoned R-1 (single-family
residential with a one-acre minimum lot size) and is part of a block with similar zoning. It lies
north of 12600 South, east of 3600 West, and adjacent to a mix of regional commercial,
professional office, and residential zones. The applicant is requesting a rezone to Commercial
Gateway. The area’s general plan designates the property for estate-density residential use,
while nearby properties to the south are commercial gateway and professional office, and to the
west are regional commercial.

Mr. Lethbridge noted that City ordinances allow home-based businesses only within owner-
occupied homes and with strict limitations on remodeling, employees, and business operations
to preserve the residential character. He also addressed concerns about the perception of
predetermined outcomes, emphasizing that no outcomes are guaranteed and that all decisions
depend on public hearings and final Council votes. The Planning Commission previously held a
public hearing on this application and forwarded a recommendation for denial.

Rob Freeman, the property owner and representative of First Choice Heating and Air, clarified
that while the applicant listed is their contractor, the property and business are owned by him.
The purpose of the rezone is to allow the business to operate on the property. He outlined his
intentions for the site: to use the existing home as an office and meeting space for customers
and to construct a storage facility at the rear of the property for equipment and materials such
as furnaces and air conditioners. He also addressed concerns about traffic, emphasizing that
their operations would generate minimal traffic.

Councilmember McCay asked how the home would be remodeled. In response, Mr. Freeman
compared his vision to the Apex Insurance property nearby, which was remodeled into a
functional and attractive business space. He stated his intention to similarly renovate the house
on the property, improving its appearance, updating the landscaping, and addressing existing
issues like drainage.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments;

Marvin Hagen, owner of Hagen Collision, said his primary concern was not the proposed zoning
change but the possibility of further road widening or additional property being taken from
homeowners, which he believes could harm businesses in the area.

Lawrence Sorge expressed concerns about the gradual reduction of R1 zoning (one-acre
residential lots) in Riverton. Referring to the zoning map, he pointed out that there are only a
few light green (R1) zones left in Riverton, with most located near the golf course on the east
side of town. He urged the Council to preserve these areas and avoid eroding the character of
Riverton's neighborhoods.

Troy Anderson read a prepared statement emphasizing his love for Riverton and the rural
character of his neighborhood, which has been R1 (one-acre residential lots) with specific
CC&R restrictions for over 50 years. He stressed that the zoning change is not aligned with the
City's general plan and would negatively impact property values and residents' rights. He urged
the Council to preserve the general plan and deny the rezone application, referencing the strong
opposition expressed by neighbors in attendance, by proxy, and through submitted letters.

Wendy Onstott expressed her opposition to the proposed zoning change and emphasized
concerns about preserving the long-standing, peaceful nature of the neighborhood. She voiced
frustration with the idea of gradually "chipping away" at the existing R1 zoning, pointing out that
similar attempts have occurred previously and cautioning against setting a precedent for further
zoning changes.

Marcus Wayman described the peaceful nature of the subdivision, encouraging others to visit,
ride a bike through the area, and experience its unique atmosphere away from the busy main
roads. He emphasized the importance of preserving the neighborhood's character, stating, "God
help us all."

Daniel Pay, the Applicant, shared his perspective as a small business owner and general
contractor, explaining that his role is to help make his clients' dreams come true. He noted the
rural feel of the area and the intention to keep the property green with minimal concrete,
contrasting the commercial properties to the west. He reassured the Council that the goal was
to create an aesthetically pleasing space that the community could appreciate.

Mayor Staggs called for additional public comments; there being none, he closed the Public
Hearing.

Councilmember Pierucci asked if the developer would be required to build a masonry wall if the
zone change was approved. Mr. Lethbridge said the ordinance mandates that in situations
where incompatible uses are adjacent, such as commercial next to single-family residential, a
solid masonry fence is required around the project area. In the current case, if the zoning is
approved to commercial, the requirement for solid masonry fencing on three sides of the
property would remain in place.

Councilmember Buroker expressed concern about lighting when commercial property is
adjacent to residential. Mr. Lethbridge explained that the updated code includes restrictions on
lighting for commercial developments located near single-family residential areas. Specifically,
for any lighting facing residential properties—whether to the north, east, or west—there are time
limits on when those lights can be on. Although the amendment initially addressed holiday

lighting, the restrictions apply to all lighting facing residential areas, limiting its duration
throughout the day.

Councilmember McCay explained her vote in favor of the proposed rezone by emphasizing the
minimal impact on traffic, citing data showing that the property is located on a road with 30,000
cars per day, while nearby homes contribute a very small portion of that traffic. She also
highlighted the importance of respecting personal property rights, noting that neighbors should
not have a say in how someone uses their property as long as it does not negatively impact
others. She also pointed out the economic benefits of the rezone, as it would help increase the
City’s sales tax base, and noted that Riverton has a history of supporting businesses.
Ultimately, she believed that approving the rezone now, rather than waiting for a general plan
update, was the best course of action to support the City’s growth and respect property rights.

Councilmember Haymond expressed concerns about the potential issues of spot zoning,
particularly the impact it could have on the integrity of Riverton’s general plan. He cautioned that
allowing spot zoning could lead to fractured development, creating isolated pockets of
businesses and making the City’s growth more disjointed. This could lead to complications with
traffic flow, property access, and future planning. Ultimately, he advocated for addressing
development from a broader perspective rather than making piecemeal changes that could have
unintended consequences.

Councilmember McDougal began by thanking the community for their continued involvement in
Riverton’s development and emphasized the importance of maintaining the City’s integrity
through a well-established general plan and zoning. He stressed that zoning provides a
framework that allows for organized, coherent growth, contrasting it with the hodgepodge
development seen in other cities that lack a clear structure. He pointed out that when residents
invest in Riverton, they do so with the expectation of living in areas designated for specific types
of development, such as residential or commercial. Changing zoning from residential to
commercial could disrupt this balance, diminish property rights, and lead to inconsistent
development that could harm the community's overall appeal and property values. He
concluded by reaffirming the importance of respecting Riverton’s master plan, supporting the
residents who have already invested in the City, and ensuring that future development aligns
with the City’s vision for the next 5-10 years.

Councilmember Pierucci acknowledged the distinction between properties set back from 12600
South and those directly facing it, noting the unique challenges of living along a road designated
as a state highway with heavy traffic. While agreeing with the value of adhering to the general
plan, he argued that the Council should not delay action on something likely to be addressed in
the upcoming general plan review, particularly when a willing investor is prepared to contribute
to the City’s economic growth. He highlighted the City's efforts to support businesses while
maintaining protections for residents, including requirements for masonry fencing and lighting
regulations to mitigate the impact of commercial developments on adjacent residential areas.
He stated his intent to vote in favor of the proposal.

Councilmember Buroker acknowledged the challenges of living in homes directly facing 12600
South, suggesting that expecting those homes to remain residential long-term might be
unrealistic. She expressed a preference for waiting to address these changes during the general
plan review rather than proceeding now and said she would vote against the proposal.

Councilmember McDougal prefaced his motion by highlighting key distinctions between a recent
general plan change on Redwood Road and the current proposal. He explained that the

Redwood Road change maintained residential use, merely altering density, which acted as a
buffer between different residential zones. In contrast, the current proposal represented a
significant shift from residential to commercial use, which he argued would disrupt the
established character and feel of the neighborhood. He expressed concern about treating the
general plan update as a predetermined process favoring commercial development, without
adequate consultation with residents.

Councilmember McDougal MOVED that the City Council DENY Ordinance No. 25-03 –
Pelaez Rezone, Amending the General Plan Designation ad Zoning for Approximately
0.96-acres located at 3516 West 12600 South from R-1 Residential Zone to C-G
(Commercial Gateway) Zone. Councilmember Buroker SECONDED the motion. Mayor
Staggs called for discussion on the motion.

Councilmember Pierucci clarified his stance on the general plan, emphasizing that he was not
asserting a predetermined outcome. He referred to the Planning Commission's prior
recommendation, where the majority supported transitioning high-traffic corridors to
commercial use, including the area in question. He argued that delaying the decision for six
months only to likely reach the same conclusion would be inefficient. He emphasized the role
of elected representatives in making decisions based not only on resident input but also on
broader information and citywide considerations. He expressed optimism about upcoming
conversations with residents in each district, emphasizing how this input will shape future
discussions on the general plan while acknowledging the need for other forms of learning and
deliberation.

Mayor Staggs called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: Buroker-yes, Haymond-yes,
McCay-no, McDougal-yes and Pierucci-no. The motion passed with a 3 to 2 vote.
