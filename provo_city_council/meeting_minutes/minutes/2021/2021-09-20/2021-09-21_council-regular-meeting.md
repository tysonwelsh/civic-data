 58   Cliff Strachan, Council Executive Director, presented. He gave a brief overview of a Percent for
 59   Public Arts Program (PFA) used by more than 350 cities and state governments. Using a PFA, a
 60   percentage of capital improvement project budgets were set aside for the integration of public
 61   arts into public facilities. A PFA created a sense of place, captured the eye and mind of
 62   someone passing by public spaces, and created a city vitality. Utah State designated one
 63   percent (1%) of construction costs to be added to a project for site specific art. Other cities in
 64   Utah have created a similar program.
 65
 66   In terms of the proposed appropriation, this would fit with a PFA by adding money to the
 67   original project to provide for public art in or near the new city building. The appropriation of
 68   $400,000 would serve as back-up funding for art if the city building project was not finished
 69   within budget. This would allow the proposed water feature to be installed now without having
 70   to tear up the plaza later. The funds would come out of the general fund.
 71
 72   Brent Tippets, VCBO Architecture, was invited to present. He shared some of the elements that
 73   led to the selection of the proposed water feature.
 74        It was unique and impactful – there was nothing like it in the western United States.
 75        It was interactive and not static.
 76        It provided a benefit to the building, both from the interior and the exterior.
 77        It was an impactful gateway entrance to the city from the west.
 78        It related to the scale of the new city building.
 79        It reflected a forward looking ideal for the city.
 80
 81   The city building project was on schedule and on budget. The steering committee felt this art
 82   piece was the right selection for the project. Funds were expected to come from contingency,
 83   but with eight months left, they decided not to tie up contingency funds until the project was
 84   completed. However, it was not practical to wait on approving the water feature because the
 85   plaza concrete was scheduled to be poured and completed in two months. We must move
 86   forward now in order to avoid extra costs tearing up the concrete.
 87
 88   In response to a question from Vice-Chair Shipley, Mr. Tippets said that members of the
 89   steering committee included Mayor Kaufusi, Wayne Parker (CAO), Isaac Paxman (deputy
 90   mayor), Police Chief Ferguson, Fire Chief Miguel, Dick Blackham (facilities manager), and Scott
 91   Henderson (project manager). Similar to the same process with state projects, there was no
 92   input from the public.
 93
 94   Mr. Tippets explained that the water feature had the ability to create any image, shape, letters,
 95   or words in the water as it fell vertically. A computer controlled very small jets of water that
 96   would shoot down in a wall of water. There was also a LED lighting feature that gave the ability
 97   to have color in the display. There was no desire to have it become an announcement or
 98   political display. It would represent art alone. The water feature was recyclable. The only
 99   water lost would be through evaporation. It was designed to prevent any kind of substantial
100   spillage.

102   Councilor Harding asked if the placement of the water feature would inhibit the function of the
103   plaza as a gathering place. Mr. Tippets replied that there had always been an art piece planned
104   for the plaza. The proposed water feature would not take up any more room than art
105   previously considered so there would still be space in the plaza that could be used for public
106   gatherings. The water feature could be a calming influence in that type of setting.
107
108   The technology for the water feature came out of Germany. The installation, maintenance, and
109   operation would be contracted with a local company.
110
111   Vice-Chair Shipley invited public comment. There was no response to the request. He invited
112   council discussion.
113
114   Councilor Harding said he was supportive of public art, regardless of their decision that night.
115   He felt the total cost of $600,000 for the water feature was reasonable for a project of this
116   scope. Having to make the decision that night put the council in a bind. In reference to water
117   consumption, it was estimated the feature would lose up to ten (10) gallons per week, which
118   was reasonable. There had been public input about using the $600,000 to commission local
119   artists to create art for this space. His wish to support local artists made this decision a difficult
120   one. He asked for someone from the administration to give reasoning for supporting the
121   proposed water feature.
122
123   Mr. Parker stated that having a significant piece of public art had always been part of the
124   equation. After looking at various options for a noticeable, interactive piece of art, the steering
125   committee chose the water feature. They liked the flexibility of visual art that could be
126   depicted on the water wall. It could include many different artists, not just one or two. The
127   construction contingency exceeded the $600,000 needed for this project. However, with
128   several months left, they did not want to cut something out of the building that would affect
129   the function of city operations.
130
131   He said the committee did not want to eliminate the water feature from the project so they
132   looked to identify another funding source outside the project budget. The proposed resolution
133   just guaranteed there would be enough money and, hopefully, they would not have to use the
134   appropriation. They were at a point where the city had to guarantee the funding now to
135   include the water feature in the project.
136
137   Councilor Handley felt that supporting public art, and especially local artists, was an important
138   conversation to have. He said we needed to celebrate, promote, and facilitate local artists and
139   poets’ contributions to the community. The new water feature would allow us to do that. He
140   felt this was the right space, opportunity, and location for this type of display.
141
142   Mr. Tippets replied that this water feature was versatile enough that it would allow us to
143   display local art, poetry, etc. The city could designate one person to manage the context,
144   making sure that it was controlled.

       5.       Resolution 2021-34 appropriating $12,855 in the Economic Development Division in
                the General Fund for a sales tax increment post performance payment to Parkway
                Village. (21-097) (1:20:43)
186
             Motion:       An implied motion to approve Resolution 2021-34, as currently constituted,
                           has been made by council rule.
187
188   Keith Moray, Development Services Assistant Director, presented. The proposed resolution
189   would appropriate $12,855 in the Economic Development fund to make a sales tax increment
190   post performance payment to Parkway Village. Sales tax increment performance pay was a
191   way to help businesses/developers make improvements that would help increase business.
192   The developer has to be accountable for those funds.
193
194   In response to a question from Vice-Chair Shipley, Mr. Moray said this was not an agreement
195   through the Redevelopment Agency. The city identified projects that were a little dated and,
196   instead of letting deterioration continue, we collaborated with the developers to make
197   improvements. A portion of sales tax increment was used to make those improvements. The
198   sales tax increment we pay was just a portion of the amount they invested on improvements.
199
200   Mr. Jones clarified that post performance payments were not paid until they could show they
201   were performing. It gave us a chance to hold them accountable. If they were not performing,
202   we do not need to pay. Fiscal year tax results are not readily available when the budget was set
203   so we need to make this appropriation after we determine the amount of sales tax increment.
204
205   Vice-Chair Shipley invited public comment. There was no response.
206
207   With no council discussion, he called for a vote on the implied motion.
