         3.       Public hearing to receive input on all matters relating to a petition for the creation of Mount Ogden
                  Public Infrastructure Districts No. 1, No. 2, and No. 3 (three separate public infrastructure districts)
                  which are located in the Snowbasin area of unincorporated Weber County.

                  Chris Crockett (Lead Civil Attorney): In 2019, Senate Bill 228 passed Public Infrastructure District
                  (PID) Act. PIDs are a process for developers to create a separate legal entity similar to water
                  districts, mosquito abatement districts, etc. PIDs exist specifically in order to fund infrastructure that
                  provides services similar to those provided under the County’s Municipal Services Fund. That
                  infrastructure then is transferred to an entity that will operate those services going forward. In order
                  to create a PID, you have to have 100% consent of property owners and any voters within the PID
                  boundaries. The debt is paid through property taxes and bonds levied by the PID. Debt issued by
                  PID is not a liability of the county or state. Residents living outside the PID boundaries are not
                  responsible for the costs of funding that infrastructure within the PID. We have worked closely with
                  our bond council, financial advisor, and the Commission to develop a policy to govern the process.
                  We require a petition signed by 100% of the owners within the District along with a application with
                  maps, detailed description of what services will be provided, what types of infrastructure will be
                  financed, long term projections, and identification of benefits to the county and nearby residents will
                  receive. That will all be reviewed, which costs are paid by the applicant. Only large projects with
                  expected public infrastructure costs over $10M will be considered. Once the application is reviewed,
                  the application is shared with the Commission and receive approval to start the negotiation process
                  of a governing document under which the PID will operate. For these three PIDs, we have spent a
                  year getting these documents prepared, having gone through many, many drafts. This governing
                  document collectively covers all three PIDs. These PIDs will solely fund infrastructure and will be
                  transferred to other existing Districts. Applicant must adhere to all federal, state, and county laws for
