4.      APPROVE PROPERTY TAX ACTIONS CONTAINED IN RECOMMENDATION LETTER NO. 2015-5
Of the four action items contained within Recommendation Letter No. 2015-5, two parcels were registered to a couple
present in the audience. Richard and Jan West approached the bench to address those items. Mr. West explained he and
his wife are the trust managers; the executor has requested settlement or deferral of the delinquent property taxes due
to the death of the owner in November 2014. The executor was not aware of the delinquent taxes until February 2015,
and has been trying to handle the estate since that time. The property is currently slated for auction during the annual
May Tax Sale.

Commissioner Ellertson noted the county does not auction property unless taxes have remained unpaid for five years.
Richard West explained the property taxes were paid, but for some reason the personal property taxes were neglected.
Commissioner Lee pointed out that county staff sent multiple letters notifying management of the situation, and Jan West
repeated the owner passed away - the son is attempting to reconcile affairs. Mr. West stated it is unknown why previous
notices went unanswered, and Ms. West mentioned it has not been an operating business since 2010. Utah County Civil
Division Chief Deputy Attorney David H. Shawcroft confirmed that any action taken by the Board today would not be
subject to appeal. Mr. Shawcroft also mentioned the notices would have been sent to an Arizona address per property
records, and there was some discussion regarding the registered names on the trusts.

Richard West requested a settlement to pay the balance due in installments, or perhaps for the auction to be delayed.
Commissioner Ellertson clarified the total due would include taxes, penalty and interest. When David Shawcroft noted
payment could be accepted right until the day of the May Tax Sale, Utah County Clerk/Auditor Bryan E. Thompson
invited Richard and Jan West to meet with his office to discuss their options. Mr. Shawcroft explained that avenue is
still available to Mr. and Ms. West if the Board approves the recommendation letter.

1.     APPROVE, MODIFY AND APPROVE, OR REJECT THE KENYON FARLEY AGRICULTURE
       PROTECTION AREA 1 APPLICATION WITH THE PROPOSED MODIFICATIONS LOCATED IN
       SECTION 30, T9S, R2E, SLB&M, IN THE SPRING LAKE AREA (Continued from the March 24, 2015
       Commission meeting)
Peggy Kelsey of the Utah County Community Development Office summarized the application submitted by Kenyon
Farley and discussed at the previous meeting of the Board for an Agriculture Protection Area which may or may not
include exemptions requested by Payson City and the Utah County Public Works Department. Mr. Farley was not
present in the audience, and Commissioner Ellertson mentioned visiting the property since the last discussion. Ms.
Kelsey reminded the Board that the Utah County Agriculture Protection Advisory Board had suggested approving the
application as submitted, without the requested government exclusions. The Utah County Planning Commission
recommended approval with the exclusion of the requested right-of-ways.

When Commissioner Ellertson noted he may have a solution for all parties involved and asked about the timeline of
events, Commissioner Graves reminded him that a decision must be made today in order to allow county staff adequate
time to file paperwork by an April 17, 2015 deadline. Ms. Kelsey and Utah County Civil Division Chief Deputy
Attorney David H. Shawcroft confirmed the deadline, noting that approval could default to that of the original application
with no modifications if action is not taken by the Board.

Commissioner Graves asked about the necessity of a 96-foot road and also an additional 40-foot easement for a potential
power line, and Mark Sorenson responded on behalf of Payson City. Mr. Sorenson explained the city could work with
the existing right-of-way if approved, and Commissioner Graves asked what the motivation would be to overlap or
minimize area if both the road and easement are approved. Mr. Sorenson responded it would take further planning and
discussion, but it would be to everyone’s benefit for the power line to be as close to the road as possible. David
Shawcroft commented on financial incentive, and Commissioner Ellertson noted the road and easement could be shared
between Payson and Utah County. Mr. Sorenson confirmed consultants have been hired to determine property ownership
along the path of the power line in order to acquire land as soon as possible and begin construction.

Commissioner Lee asked whether Payson City would still be willing to negotiate with the Farley property owners if the
Board were to approve the agriculture protection area without excluding the requested exemptions. Mark Sorenson
stated it would not halt plans but the city would have to reconsider costs; each property needing to be removed from
agricultural protection would need to cycle through the Boards and Commissions again individually. Peggy Kelsey
clarified that it would not return to the Utah County Board of Commissioners if the property owners agreed to the offers
from Payson City.

Commissioner Graves commented on restrictions of protected land, and David Shawcroft clarified that the requested
exemptions would not be defined as protected if excluded from the agriculture protection area by action of the Board.
Mark Sorenson indicated a preference for negotiating with the property owners rather than resorting to eminent domain
claims, and Commissioner Lee expressed a desire to err on the side of the property owner. Mr. Sorenson spoke of
existing right-of-ways and Commissioner Ellertson acknowledged the property owners’ concerns about nuisance
complaints; it is difficult to determine if noise is originating from within or outside of an agriculture protection area when
sections are carved from it. Commissioner Ellertson stated he is in favor of agriculture protection unless it is treated as
a means to forego any compromise between private and public interests. Commissioner Lee countered that the property
owners should be allowed an advantage for beginning negotiations, and Commissioner Ellertson repeated that both sides
of the argument must be considered.

Mark Sorenson addressed long-term Utah County plans, mentioning combined efforts between the Strawberry Electric
Service District and Payson City. He and Commissioner Ellertson reviewed geography and individual services, with Mr.
Sorensen mentioning the City’s intent to combine services wherever possible. There was some discussion regarding the
current crops, and the Board discussed the height of fruit trees compared to electric poles for successful farming. When
Peggy Kelsey confirmed Kenyon Farley acquired the land in 2013, Commissioner Graves pointed out the master plan
was already in place at that time - this plan includes the road which would need to be excluded from the proposed
agriculture protection area.

Commissioner Lee summarized both sides, stating the property owner is seeking the best protection available while the
government entity is indicating there are no other readily available options. He questioned again why there is an
assumption that negotiations cannot be made, and Mark Sorenson clarified that Payson City chose this route because it
is part of the Master Plan. Negotiations can still take place but there are many example of agriculture protection zones
costing millions of dollars because property has to be circumvented. Commissioner Lee argued there are also examples
of compromise. Commissioner Ellertson reiterated his concern that the agriculture protection area could be considered
a mechanism to block the road and power line, stating elected officials are accused of wrongdoing when planning for
the future yet are also blamed if they fail to do so.

When Mark Sorenson expressed skepticism for good faith negotiations, Commissioner Lee noted both sides are likely
wary. They discussed timelines and Commissioner Graves mentioned the struggle of balance in protecting the public
while also holding property owners responsible for their own actions. Commissioner Ellertson commented on needing
more time, and discussed deadlines with Peggy Kelsey and Utah County Recorder Jeff Smith. Commissioner Graves
expressed reluctance in any further delay while Commissioner Ellertson continued to discuss legal process with David
Shawcroft.

Commissioner Graves stated he is ready to move forward, noting Kenyon Farley acquired the land in 2013 and should
have been aware of the Master Plan already in place at that time. Mark Sorenson explained letters were sent to the public
in October 2014 and Peggy Kelsey confirmed the agriculture protection area application was filed in December 2014.
Commissioner Graves suggested taking measures to protect all sides, and David Shawcroft explained that excluded areas
could be added back into agriculture protection at a later date. When Commissioner Lee asked whether the process could
work in reverse: grant agriculture protection for the entire area and remove the road and easement later, Mr. Shawcroft
responded it would be much more difficult to remove property from agriculture protection rather than add to it.

Peter Quittner of the Utah County Sheriff’s Office has been actively working on the process to create a county-wide
emergency notification system for approximately two years. Mr. Quittner explained that Everbridge, Inc. has been
selected as the best option for the cities in Utah County, as well as the county overall. There is an advantage for cities
currently serviced by Everbridge: they will receive additional discounts by contracting through the county. There will,
however, be extra cost to cities that have no noticing system in place or are currently working with a different company.
The system is needed to warn residents of terrorist attacks, natural disasters, and other traumatic events. The annual price
of $115,000 will serve 255,000 households and there is no use limit. It can also be used internally between county and
city employees and departments as well as the citizens.

Peter Quittner confirmed for Utah County Clerk/Auditor Bryan E. Thompson that $115,000 is the total cost - the cities
will be reimbursing the county for their shares. Commissioner Graves noted not all cities have an agreement in place
with the county, but those entities will not be able to use the system until the contracts are signed. Mr. Thompson
questioned why the funds were not included in the budget if this contract has been anticipated for two years, and
Commissioner Ellertson mentioned the amount should be included in a restricted fund. Commissioner Lee declared his
appreciation for the work that has been put into bringing this project forward, but also his nervousness to move forward
without memorandums of understanding already in place. He warned against pushing approval of items to a deadline
which causes a rushed decision in faith that the money will be “found;” funds do not magically appear, and the county
is left to rely on luck that the city councils will approve agreements for payment.

Commissioner Lee asked whether there is a free trial period to entice cities to participate, and Peter Quittner stated that
decision would be left to the Board. Some cities do have specific questions and issues to address, but the overall
discussion has been “how can Utah County familiarize the cities with the system so that they want to use it?”
Commissioner Ellertson commented on system use and Commissioner Lee reiterated his concerns regarding payment
once the expense has been approved. Peter Quittner stated he would continue working diligently as this has been a group
effort; there are meetings with county emergency managers, police chiefs, fire chiefs, and other such personnel.
Commissioner Ellertson stated they each carry responsibility in the success of this venture.

Wally Perschon of the Utah County Sheriff’s Office commented on the system of support from the commissioners, and
Commissioner Graves volunteered to take the lead in follow-up discussions with the cities. The system will maximize
efficiency and there is too much liability in not moving forward. Mr. Perschon indicated the cities would follow the
example set by Utah County, and Commissioner Ellertson stated there are more entities prepared to commit than the
Board may think. Mr. Perschon mentioned the group discount again, and Peter Quittner added that some cities would
not be able to participate in such a system without the county contract. Commissioner Ellertson noted today’s deadline.

Utah County Deputy Attorney Paul Jones introduced Utah County Arts Board President Jeanne Gomm. Commissioner
Ellertson summarized the discussion which took place at the last Commission meeting regarding changes to the co-
sponsorship agreement. He mentioned that an updated budget had not been provided as requested, and Ms. Gomm
approached the bench to provide each commissioner with the document.

Jeanne Gomm thanked the commissioners for funding and supporting the Utah County Arts Board. She explained they
have been operating since 1956 to promote and generate excitement for art in the community. Ms. Gomm spoke of the
art displays in the Utah County Health and Justice Building, and participation in the Downtown Provo gallery stroll every
first Friday of the month. The Arts Board hopes to eventually have an Arts Festival in Utah County. Ms. Gomm
displayed a copy of the Utah County Artists Sourcebook, a resource in which artists can list their contact information
for fifteen dollars per entry. She spoke of the volunteers and a desire for a secured gallery where three-dimensional art
would be safe and welcome. Ms. Gomm explained that funds provide balloon animals, food, puppetry and other items
for events. She reviewed the monthly budget of composed amounts since 2007, asking the commissioners to help
maintain their standard. They discussed revenue, entry fees, and awards for shows held in May and October each year.

Commissioner Graves commented on tourism promotion through the Utah County Arts Board. He expressed
appreciation for their work, but also noted that a nearby museum attracts 47,000 visitors annually without any financial
support from the county. Jeanne Gomm countered that the same museum had provided an estimated cost of $24,000 to
provide the same services for the county which the Arts Board currently performs for free. She reminded the
commissioners that the museum has paid staff; the Arts Board is volunteer-based. Commissioner Lee reviewed the
figures of the proposed budget, and Ms. Gomm explained why internet and publicity fees are needed. Ms. Gomm
pointed out that not everyone has a computer, and people still rely on printed material.

Commissioner Lee expressed hopes for the Utah County Arts Board to have less financial dependence on the county and
encouraged Jeanne Gomm and her volunteers to seek contributions from the community. Utah County Clerk/Auditor
Bryan E. Thompson also commented on the budget, mentioning that revenue was not included and reviewing more
numbers with Ms. Gomm. Commissioners Lee and Graves explained that they were seeking more detail to help them
understand the Arts Board budget needs, but the information provided does not seem complete.

Joseph Stanton is the Utah County Arts Board Treasurer. Mr. Stanton has been ill and unable to gather the detail sought
by the commissioners, but acknowledged that “artists and accountants see things differently;” he is hoping to resolve the
gap in communication between the two extremes. Jeanne Gomm and Mr. Stanton continued to reconcile the figures and
attempt to quantify provided services with the commissioners, asking them to be generous in helping make and keep Utah
County beautiful.
