Remarks from state and county leaders was the first item on the agenda:

Brian Steed, Executive Director, Utah Department of Natural Resources —

Steed discussed the challenges of predicting water reliability, such as population growth and
drought. Washington County has been one of the fastest growing areas in the nation and in the
state. He provided the following potential solutions to ensure water reliability in the future:

e New water development, including acquisition of existing local water rights, new storage
on the Virgin River System and importing water from outside the Virgin River System
Conservation — without this we will have difficulty meeting supply needs
Water reuse/recycling is expensive, but it is being done in in different parts of the
country. Regional efforts yield more success than independent local projects.

Tim Davis, Director, Utah Division of Drinking Water

The Division of Drinking Water helps all Utahans have access to safe drinking water by
calculating the average and peak daily demand throughout the year to determine the necessary
source sizing standard for water use connections.

The legislature has adopted timelines that require all drinking water systems to have sufficient
capacity to meet these standards. Water providers can work with the Division of Water Rights to
collect that data (three years) based on actual usage. If they have sufficient capacity, the state can
approve water system extensions. If they fail to have sufficient capacity, the state will not
approve water system extensions. It’s important to get this right. The division will be hosting
workshops for water system operators on what data they need to collect.
Three cities in Washington County — St. George, Washington and Ivins - have already calculated
its source sizing standard.

Teresa Wilhelmsen, State Engineer for Utah, Director of Utah Division of Water Rights
Teresa explained the history of her office and their duties and offered the following information:

e Reuse projects — must get approval under existing water rights to do a reuse project and it
is limited to depletion amount of the water rights.

¢ State of Utah is divided into hydrologic basins, and they manage each of those basins.
They have seven regional offices across the state. The local office for this area is in Cedar
City.

e Area 81, the Virgin River, is closed to new appropriations. All water has been
appropriated. For new development, you must file a change application on the existing
water right. Can find current policy for Area 81 on the division’s website.

¢ New diversions and uses must be accomplished by change applications based on valid
existing water rights.

e Distribution and measurement of water is the division’s responsibility. There are three
state administered surface water distribution systems in this area: Santa Clara River,
Virgin River and East Fork of the Virgin River. Each system regularly experiences
shortages of water.

¢ General stream adjudication is a court process and binds all parties or users within the
area, The division has been working on adjudication in this area for a long period of time.
The result of general stream adjudication will be court decrees.

e There are two areas open to small amounts of water for homes in the Beaver Wash
drainage and Canaan Gap.

Candace Hasenyager, Director, Utah Division of Water Resource

The division’s mission is to plan, conserve, develop and protect Utah’s water resources.

The two challenges are drought and population growth. Washington County is projected to grow
to a half a million people in the next few decades.

One of best ways to meet water shortages is through conservation. The end users’ choices
regarding water use are very important.

Water conservation tools include :
¢ integrate land use and water planning
install secondary water meters
water loss accounting
transparent billing
statewide water conservation campaigns

The division is currently working through an Integrated Land Use and Water Planning — phase 1
completed, working on phase 2.
Statewide Water Conservation efforts summarized on the Slow the Flow and Utah Water Savers
websites.

Water and growth challenges don’t appear to be decreasing anytime in the future and it’s more
important than ever that we work together, collaborate and communicate more.

Commissioner Adam Snow, Washington County

Most of us know the importance of water. The story of water in the west is about adaptation and
the story is the same here in Southern Utah. The very first thing Erastus Snow and other early
settlors did was look for water. They laid out a water plan where they could find water.

We still rely on many of the sources of water they used. It is because of their foresight, efforts,
and pioneering spirit that we are here today. We need to work together on water issues to provide
for our children and grandchildren and use that same pioneering spirit.
