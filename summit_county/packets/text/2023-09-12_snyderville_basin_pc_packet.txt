Background: 
 
The Canyons Specially Planned Area (SPA) was originally approved in 1998.  This approval 
involved 6,388 acres  and five (5) different property owners.  With that approval there were 
entitlements granted for some of the developments  at the Canyons known as Ski 98.  This 
included developments such as the Grand Summit and Sundial Lodge, the Colony and the 
Silverado.   It was anticipated that there would be a comprehensive amendment to this plan that 
would expand the entitlements and property involved. 
  
In November of 1999, that amendment to the SPA was completed.  After numerous public 
meetings and hearings,  the SPA was extended to include approximately 7,768 acres and 37 
different property owners to establish the current Development Agreement (DA).  The guiding 
principles for the DA were the Global Principles: 
 
• Comfortable Carrying Capacity 
• Allowable Density 
• Unit Configurations & Occupancy 
• Resort Support and Mountain Recreation 
• Viewshed 
• Environment 
• Employee Housing 
• Economic Base 
• Transportation 
• Highway 224 & Resort Entry 
• Benchmark Assessments 
• Development Design Character 
• Master Community and Resort Facility, Amenity, Recreation, Cultural Arts, and 
Marketing Plan 
• Community Integration 
• Infrastructure Maintenance and Management 
• Construction Mitigation and Management 
• Development Pattern 
• Development Phasing 
 
The key components to the Development Agreement (DA ) were to establish development
 
planning areas, create a detailed Land Use & Zoning Chart to allocate maximum heights, 
maximum square footages, uses for each parcel, create Comprehensive Design Guidelines, set 
benchmarks for a Canyons Transportation Master, the Canyons Workforce Housing Plan, Trials 
Master Plan, Natural Resources Management Plan and Open Space and Viewshed Protection 
Plan, to name a few.

There are twelve (12) different development areas within the SPA.  Each area has unique planning 
standards based on location for development and includes allowable uses, density  and

Due to the location and the phasing requirements in the DA, the mid-mountain development has 
not begun and cannot begin until certain thresholds have been met.   As part of the  long-range

Per their application  statement, TCFC has vetted Red Pine traffic -related matters with County 
personnel and the Fire Marshal, including access for service vehicles and emergency services. 
From those discussions, it has become clear that if the "general" limitation under Section 3.15 
were to be applied in such a manner as to strictly prohibit all vehicular access not only for the 
general public but for residents and guests in particular, such a strict prohibition would eliminate 
a crucial means of evacuation that must be available in the ev ent of a wildfire or other life -
threatening emergency. By letter of March 17, 2023 (copy attached), the Fire Marshal has 
concluded that (i) the use of a gondola is not a sufficient means of egress for occupants of the 
development and does not meet the requirements of the Park City Fire District or the 
International Fire Code, and likewise, (ii) the use of shuttles is not a sufficient option for 
emergency egress. The Fire Marshall has further concluded that given the constraints of the 
development area, personal automobiles need to be available, as a combination of shuttles and 
lifts or a gondola would not provide a successful means of emergency evacuation required for 
the numbers and locations of people on-mountain.

would allow vehicular traffic only for residents and guests via a gate-controlled non-public access 
road. Access for emergency and resort service vehicles would, of course, still be allowed. This 
approach would serve the dual County goals of (i) mitigating traffic and (ii) meeting life -safety 
demands by providing the only practical means of emergency evacuation.

OVERVIEW OF APPLICATION FOR DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT AMENDMENT 
As part of TCFC's long-range planning for its Red Pine project (entitled under the Canyons 
SPA DA), TCFC is undertaking to proactively address traffic-related items associated with that 
future project. This Application relates to Section 3.15 of the DA, which reads: 
As required in Section 3.3.S{a) of this Amended Agreement, a remote parking and 
management plan, to include check-in, concierge service and transportation 
between parking and accommodation, must be included in the Transportation 
Plan. This aspect of the Plan shall be adopted and implemented in conjunction 
with hotel/lodging unit development in Red Pine Village, Tombstone, and Red Pine 
Lake Development Areas. In general, automobile access to these areas, will be 
prohibited. Access will be provided and maintained for service vehicles 
and emergency services. (underscore added) 
Of particular note, the highlighted vehicular limitation is "In general," not absolute, and access 
related to emergency services must be provided. 
TCFC has vetted Red Pine traffic-related matters with County personnel and the Fire 
Marshal, including access for service vehicles and emergency services. From those discussions, 
it has become clear that if the "general" limitation under Section 3.15 were to be applied in such 
a manner as to strictly prohibit all vehicular access not only for the general public but for residents 
and guests in particular, such a strict prohibition would eliminate a crucial means of evacuation 
that must be available in the event of a wildfire or other life-threatening emergency. By letter of 
March 17, 2023 (copy attached), the Fire Marshal has concluded that (i) the use of a gondola is 
not a sufficient means of egress for occupants of the development and does not meet the 
requirements of the Park City Fire District or the International Fire Code, and likewise, (ii) the use 
of shuttles is not a sufficient option for emergency egress. The Fire Marshall has further 
concluded that given the constraints of the development area, personal automobiles need to be 
available, as a combination of shuttles and lifts or a gondola would not provide a successful 
means of emergency evacuation required for the numbers and locations of people on-mountain. 
TCFC believes this major life-safety issue can be properly addressed and reconciled in 
harmony with the general traffic limitation through the insertion of a clarifying phrase in Section 
3.15 that would allow vehicular traffic only for residents and guests via a gate-controlled non­
public access road. Access for emergency and resort service vehicles would, of course, still be 
allowed. This approach would serve the dual County goals of (i) mitigating traffic and (ii) meeting 
life-safety demands by providing the only practical means of emergency evacuation. 
Attached is a March 30, 2023 Traffic Study Update from Hales Engineering that is specific 
to Red Pine. This focused Study Update concludes that the only potential additional traffic 
mitigation measure associated with future Red Pine development would be at the High Mountain 
Road / Escala Court intersection. This Study Update also notes that the 2018 Transportation 
Master Plan Study, which was utilized in connection with the 2018 SPA amendment, took into 
account projected traffic data associated with the Red Pine development (including vehicular 
traffic for residents and guests) and substantiated that existing traffic infrastructure and 
improvements have already been sized to accommodate Red Pine-related traffic impacts.

Chapter 6.1 
Moderate Income Housing Element 
INTRODUCTION 
Rather than being monolithic, moderate-income housing is a multifaceted challenge for 
Summit County. It is tied closely to a variety of Snyderville Basin challenges including 
extremely high median housing costs; traffic congestion and legacy transportation 
issues; availability of sufficient local laborers; business challenges due to lack of 
access to labor; an increasingly non-diverse community; a shrinking number of full- 
time residents with long-term community and generational ties; increasing 
environmental degradation due to increasing development pressures; concerns about 
sustainability of air, land, and water natural resources; and the critical need to manage 
and limit rapid growth in a County that seeks to preserve open spaces, agricultural and 
cultural heritage (collectively, “Critical Concerns”). Accordingly, creating standards and 
strategies concerning moderate-income housing in this Chapter would be incomplete, 
ineffective, and potentially harmful to the vision and goals of this General Plan as well 
as the County Council’s vision, values, and strategic goals without carefully evaluating 
the impacts of any such standards and strategies on the Critical Concerns to 
determine if they will aggravate or mitigate and provide solutions to those Critical 
Concerns. All such standards and strategies and the Critical Concerns should 
continually be evaluated given the dynamic nature of growth in and on the borders of 
the County. 
The Summit County Manager, relevant staff as determined by the County Manager, 
and the County Council should regularly assess the County’s moderate-income 
housing needs and seek to articulate where such housing is most appropriate and for 
whom the need is greatest at any given moment always taking into careful 
consideration the Critical Concerns (“Moderate-Income Housing Assessment”). A 
priority of any such assessment should seek to identify if housing challenges are 
creating significant labor shortages in critical areas such as law enforcement, 
emergency medical services, education, doctors and nurses and other health care 
professionals, and local government workforce. In addition, the Moderate-Income 
Housing Assessment should examine the needs of long-term resident seniors seeking 
to age in place in the County, vulnerable populations, as well as the County’s tourism 
industry’s workforce. With respect to tourism workforce, any strategies developed in 
this Chapter 6.1 should seek to require major employers to bear some of the burden of 
providing employees with housing opportunities as well as reasonable wages that take 
into account the high housing costs in resort counties much like the difference in 
wages paid in rural Utah compared to San Francisco, California. 
External influences also need to be part of the Moderate-Income Housing Assessment, 
such as whether nightly rentals are removing long-term housing from the available 
housing capacity with a result of hollowing out formerly well-established 
neighborhoods, particularly outside of resort cores. Such assessment should consider

Moderate income housing is defined as: “Housing occupied or reserved 
for occupancy by households with a gross household income equal to 
or less than 80% of the median gross income for households of the 
same size in the county in which the housing is located.” However, 
given that the median gross income for the County is substantially 
higher than the national average, consideration should be given to 
requiring moderate-income housing that is targeted to lower median 
gross income depending upon the intended solutions sought as 
identified in the Moderate-Income Housing Assessment. 
GOAL: Provide moderate-income housing opportunities that address the 
moderate-income housing needs identified in the Moderate-Income 
Housing Assessment, as updated in accordance with the Introduction of 
this Chapter. Due to already aggressive market rate housing growth 
pressures, it is essential that moderate-income housing does not come 
as a trade-off for additional market rate housing that may ultimately 
increase the moderate-income housing deficit due to the additional

The Summit County Community Development Department has received an application for a 
Conditional Use Permit for a Rocky Mountain Power transmission line upgrade between Kimball 
Junction (Powderwood Apartments) and Summit Park (Salt Lake County-Summit County 
Boundary). The applicant is requesting to upgrade ~4.4 miles of an existing Rocky Mountain

existing and future electric power needs for Summit County.
