 7.       Ordinance 2021-38 amending Provo City Code regarding Floodplain Development
          Standards. Citywide Application. (PLOTA20210362) (0:41:59)

       Motion:       An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2021-38, as currently constituted, has
                     been made by council rule.

Melissa McNally, Provo City Planner, presented. She said the change in buildable area
elevations within the floodplain would offer future development in the floodplain more
protection against potential flood events. It would give more leeway to developments along the
Provo River in areas outside the floodplain. Future improvements to levees were taken into
consideration for development closer to the Provo River.

Ms. McNally noted in Section 15.05.180(3)(c)(i) the elevation of the lowest habitable floor
would remain at one (1) foot instead of changing it to three (3) feet above the base flood
elevation.

Guidelines for encroachments into the required 100-foot setback would be allowed, subject to the
following requirements:
     Provide access to the river at intervals not exceeding 400 linear feet of river frontage.
     Provide a minimum 40-foot maintenance easement in favor of Provo City. The easement
       shall be measured from the top of the bank extending a minimum of 40 feet into the
       parcel.
     In areas where the riverbank acts as a levee or where the Special Flood Hazard Area
       extends beyond the high bank of the river, a minimum 100-foot setback shall be
       maintained. A reduced setback may be approved where improvements to such levee are
       constructed in a manner consistent with FEMA regulations.

Mr. Jones explained that he had prepared a draft ordinance that changed the three (3) foot lowest
habitable floor elevation back to one (1) foot. He recommended that, after public comment,
councilors make a motion to substitute the published ordinance with the updated version of the
ordinance before voting on the implied motion.

Chair Sewell invited public comment. There was no response to the request.

       Motion:       Councilor Harding made a motion to substitute the old ordinance with the new

ordinance.

 10.      Ordinance 2021-41 enacting On-Street Parking Management Regulations. Citywide
          Application. (21-028) (1:17:50)

       Motion:       An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2021-41, as currently constituted, has
                     been made by council rule.

Chair Sewell explained that this ordinance proposed creating Chapter 9.90 - an on-street parking
management tool. If passed, it would allow the City to use timed paid parking in certain areas of
the City. Creation of Chapter 9.91 (designating a parking permit program area) was not being
discussed that night. He announced that a town hall meeting would be held on Thursday, 18
November, for citizens to present their views and concerns about a possible parking area in the
Joaquin neighborhood.

Javin Weaver, Provo City Planner, presented. The Council Parking Committee had been
meeting for the past three (3) years and one of the main discussion points was how to manage
on-street parking. The committee felt the proposed On-Street Parking Management (OSPM)
program could be used citywide to accomplish many of their goals. Those goals included
reducing parking frustrations, parking predictability for residents, encouraging efficient use of
off-street parking, being financially self-supporting, and improving the quality of life. The
proposed ordinance language was solely discussing the framework of how to manage this
program.

Staff posted the proposed ordinance on Open City Hall from October 8, 2021 to October 31
2021. Mr. Weaver reviewed the results of the survey with councilors (summary attached to
permanent minutes). He noted that most respondents thought that the proposed ordinance
designated a paid parking program for the Joaquin neighborhood and responded accordingly.
Some of the main comments included:
     People think it will cost to park everywhere in a proposed OSPM area.
     There was not enough off-street parking, especially at residential buildings.
     The City should build a parking garage – construction of a 100-spot parking garage
        would cost about $2 million.

Mr. Weaver explained that the ordinance allowed the parking manager to implement paid
parking or parking with a permit on streets within each OSPM area. It did not have to be the
entire area. Many people had illegally subdivided their residential homes without providing for
adequate parking. This created more on-street parking problems. The cost to build a 100-acre
parking garage would be about $2 million.

The cost for implementing an OSPM program was difficult to determine due to many unknown
variables.
     Size of the area.
     Location of the OSPM program area.
     Additional staffing needed to enforce program.
     Equipment necessary for program - signage, meters, etc.
     Cost to be demand based.

Provo City Council Meeting Minutes - November 16, 2021                                    Page 8 of 14
        The program must be revenue neutral.

Councilor Handley said the councilors had received a petition from students opposing the
creation of Chapters 9.90 and 9.91. They said it would be a financial burden to students. He
asked Mr. Weaver for clarification. Mr. Weaver explained that Chapter 9.90 would not create a
financial burden. This was just the framework (or tools) for the program.

Councilor Ellsworth said she heard from a citizen that expressed concern about residents abusing
the system by requesting an OSPM (Section 9.90.050(3)). Mr. Weaver said the Parking Manager
would notify the Council within thirty (30) days of receiving an application for an OSPM. They
would return with a report and recommendation no more than ninety (90) days after that. The
Council would then determine if they wanted to proceed to create an OSPM area or deny it if
they had any concerns.

In response to questions and concerns from Councilors, Mr. Weaver made the following
comments:
     The Council would approve all proposed parking permit areas.
     We enforce time restrictions in downtown Provo now.
     This program would eliminate visitor parking permits in areas that have a permit
        program. Visitors would pay an hourly rate.
     We should adopt this now rather than wait for a parking manager to be hired. The tools
        would already be available for the new manager.
     The Council would approve parking fees, based on the recommendation of the parking
        manager.

Chair Sewell invited public comment.

Celeste Kennard, Joaquin Neighborhood Chair, said parking had been a problem for more than a
decade. Public transportation, such as UVX and UBER, made getting around a little easier. She
felt it was time for a change to the parking in the neighborhood. On-street parking should be
looked at as amenity. She understood it was a financial burden but, over the long-term, this
would make an impact in lowering housing costs.

Tim Metler, Springville, said they managed 1,000 beds in Provo. Knowing the students’
budgets, he questioned whether they could afford paying extra for parking. It seemed they were
giving a lot of authority to the parking manager to determine what areas should be metered, and
the costs associated with the program. He said the city already received many complaints about
predatory towing. This would increase the amount of complaints and put pressure on the system.
That also needs to be taken into account when looking at the costs.

Suzanne Metler, Springville, managed property in Provo. If the Council did not intend to have
paid parking throughout the City, it would be wise to put this aside until they decide if they
actually need to hire a parking manager. Paid parking would not reduce the amount of cars
because students needed cars for jobs. She expressed concern about having a parking manager
randomly decide where to put a permit program and how much to charge.

There were no more public comments. Chair Sewell invited council discussion.
