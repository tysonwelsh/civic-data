WORK SESSION
Chair Jan Bradshaw called the meeting to order at 5:33 p.m. The agenda items were reviewed and
discussed.

Planning Manager, Jonathan Teerlink introduced the Zone Map Amendment and stated that the
applicants have been waiting to apply for some time. It involves a legislative action requiring the
Planning Commission to make a recommendation to the City Council who will make the final
decision. There are approval standards in the zoning ordinance that guide the Planning
Commission on rezone applications and the General Plan. The subject property has frontage on
2300 East and 4500 South both of which fall under the specific zone district for the Medium-
Density Two-Family Residential Transitional (“MDR-T”) Zone. In 2016, when the General Plan
rewrite was proposed and the City Council reviewed and approved it, a significant portion of 4500
South and 2300 East were considered transitional and specified duplexes. The applicant has relied
on that and the General Plan being the guiding document for long-range decisions and proposed
the R-2-8 Zone. R-2-8, R-2-10, and R-1-8 are the three zones that are recommended for the
MDR-T.

Mr. Teerlink reported that a home is on each of the six properties and they all wrap around Salt
Lake City Public Utilities’ well site. That area is slated by Salt Lake City Public Utilities as being
a future pump station or well site for water service. He clarified that the rezone boundary will not
include that corner. Mr. Teerlink suggested that the Planning Commission discussion not address
site plan development elements and focus only on the rezone request. He indicated that the R-2-8
zone requires duplexes only. The yield for the area would be approximately eight twin homes or
16 total units at a maximum height of 32 feet. If approved, staff had spoken to the applicant about
pursuing a possible Planned Unit Development (“PUD”), which would allow for flexibility in
terms of location and the setbacks are not as restrictive.

Chair Bradshaw asked about the number of units for R-2-10 versus the R-2-8 Zone. Mr. Teerlink
reported that an R-2-10 zone would allow for approximately six twin homes or 12 units. Chair
Bradshaw indicated that there has been a lot of negative feedback received about the proposal.

In response to a question raised by Commissioner Banks, Mr. Teerlink explained that when they
create a General Plan District, it does not necessarily follow property lines. They went back 200
feet to show a corridor from the middle of 2300 East going north to south and east to west. It is
up to the interpretation of the Planning Commission and the City Council with regard to how to
apply the district. Discussions were had about whether the MDR-T category was most appropriate.
Mr. Teerlink explained that a higher percentage of the lot fits into the MDR-T category and the lot
gained access from 2300 East.

Commissioner Chris Layton asked about the potential for the lot to become a PUD and whether
there would be an opportunity to be more aggressive than the standard within the proposed zone.
Mr. Teerlink explained that there are two things the Planning Commission cannot amend or adjust
in a PUD. One is height and the other is use. A question was raised as to whether rentals would
be allowed with a PUD. Mr. Teerlink stated that rentals are allowed within PUDs. In this case,
however, the intent was for the units to be for sale units. He stated that Covenants, Codes, and
Restrictions (“CC&Rs”) and Homeowners Associations (“HOAs”) can be much more restrictive
than a municipality’s regulations or zoning laws.

Senior City Planner, Robert Hobbs presented the Ordinance Amendment and explained that when
the Planning Commission sent a favorable recommendation to the City Council for the Holladay
Crossroads (“HCR”) Zone standards, there was a grouping of Code statements that authorized and
required the placement of landscaping within the hardscaped areas in a more specific manner than
allowed for in the current Landscape Code. Part of that was to require tree planters in parking lots
with plantings therein. The Council expressed a desire to get input from the Tree Committee on
the matter. They met and memorialized the conversation and Mr. Hobbs recrafted the Code that
was impacted by what they wanted to do. The hope was that it could be added to the HCR
language. Ultimately, the language regarding landscaping was removed from the Code draft and
submitted as a separate Code amendment proposal to the HCR zone. A minor change was also
proposed to Chapter 13.77. Ultimately, they defaulted back to end caps every 10 spaces, planters
that are the size and shape of a parking stall, and a Class 2 tree. They would not be placed in front
of building entryways for access and including them with ADA needs.

Commissioner Banks asked for information on what is included in an end cap. Mr. Hobbs stated
that they are used as an interrupt where there are enough stalls or at the ends. They are placed at
the end of a parking bank with a planter on either side. There would be parking on the side of the
planter with a service drive on the opposite side.

Commissioner Chris Layton moved to adjourn the Work Session. Commissioner Lloyd
seconded the motion. The motion passed with the unanimous consent of the Commission.

The Work Session adjourned at 5:58 p.m.

2.      Zone Map Amendment – R-1-10 to R-2-8 – 4425 South thru 4449 South 2300 East
        and 2327 East thru 2359 East 4500 South. Consideration and Recommendation of a
        Request by Jake Christensen to Amend the City of Holladay’s Zoning Map for 1.59
        Acres of Property from a Currently Designated Zone, R-1-10 (one home per 10,000
        square feet), to R-2-8 (duplex per 8,000 square feet). Application to be Considered as
        Per Procedures as Referenced in Holladay Ordinance §13.07.030.
Planning Manager, Jonathan Teerlink, presented the staff report and stated that the above matter
is a legislative decision involving a request to amend the zoning map. He explained that zoning
maps are set by a legislative act by the City Council. Any change requires a recommendation from
the Planning Commission. Guiding documents help the Planning Commission in its decision on
rezone applications. One is a long-range General Plan. The General Plan notations for the area
are MDR-T. That district runs east to west along 4500 South and north to south along 2300 East.
The subject property falls within that long-range General Plan envisioning district.

The applicant, Jake Christensen, shared a presentation and reported that Atlas Ventures is the
sponsor of the project. They are a real estate investment and development company with holdings
in the western United Stated of Class A multi-family high-end homes, condominiums, and
townhomes. Mr. Christensen introduced Quinn Millet, who formally served on the Board of
Trustees at Utah State University. Mr. Millet has owned a residential appraisal company for 15
years and is the co-sponsor of the project.

Mr. Millet thanked the Planning Commission and staff for their time. There were three things he
wanted to outline in relation to the subject property:

     •   What the property is;
     •   What the property is not; and
     •   Why the Zone Map Amendment makes sense.

Mr. Millet stated that the subject property is 1.59 acres in size and includes six parcels. An aerial
map was shared with the Planning Commission and Mr. Millet outlined the surrounding zones.
He addressed a previous question about the development to the west of the subject property. Non-
conforming multi-family would be compatible with an R-2-8 Zone. Mr. Millet discussed the
traffic in the area and stated that there are two main roads consisting of 2300 East and 4500 South.
2300 East has an average trip count of 12,500 vehicles per day while 4500 South has an average
trip count of 14,500 vehicles per day. Comparisons were shared with Highland Drive and the
Holladay Village. This corner has double the traffic of the Holladay Village. Mr. Millet outlined
the existing conditions. He reported that each of the homes is approximately 80 years old and
were built in 1939, 1940, 1942, 1948, and 1956.

Mr. Millet discussed what the subject property is not. He reported that they had spoken with
several neighbors and learned about some of the concerns. He clarified that the subject property
is not for rent and they anticipate a for sale twin-home product. It is not an affordable housing
complex and there is an anticipated price point that is a bit higher than the Salt Lake County
average. It is not an apartment complex, no additional height is being requested, and the setbacks
will be more than the Code requires.

The closest subdivision to the subject property is Briarwood Circle. Mr. Millet outlined several
mitigating factors of the project as it relates to Briarwood Circle. For instance, there is a mature
canopy that extends along the entire property line. They are committed to maintaining the tree
line to continue to provide beauty, shade, and privacy. Another mitigating factor for Briarwood
Circle is the fact that there is no vehicle access directly onto Briarwood Circle. The topography
was also discussed. The survey for the property found that it slopes approximately 20 feet from
east to west. The visual height of any homes built on the property would be decreased.

Mr. Christensen discussed why the Zone Map Amendment makes sense. He reported that the
subject property is in the 4500 South overlay, which is a recommended MDR-T and is intended to
accommodate small lot single-family and two-family with slightly higher densities than the Low-
Density Residential-Stable (“LDR-S”) Zone. The LDR-S Zone is consistent with the Briarwood
Circle neighborhood.

Mr. Christensen added that the current zone is at the maximum density of six units. He discussed
the current traffic conditions and stated that a potential site plan for the subject property will have
one access from 2300 East and a private subdivision road to service the garages. Everyone will
have access through the one access point on 2300 East, which has a median turn lane. The
anticipated increase in traffic is estimated to be under 100 daily trips per day, which will easily be
absorbed by 2300 East.

There is a corner on the south that has been earmarked for a future Salt Lake City Public Utilities
water pump and the rezone boundary will not include that corner. Mr. Christensen discussed the
difference between the R-2-10 Zone and the R-2-8 Zone and noted that it was not significant.
However, he felt that the R-2-8 Zone is more appropriate for the subject property. He added that
they are committed to creating a sense of community and to providing community amenities. They
are committed to a tree buffer along 4500 South, maintaining the perimeter tree canopy to the north

3.      Ordinance Amendment – Holladay Crossroads Zone Landscaping Standards
        (13.77.050 and 060). Consideration and Recommendation of Updates to Existing
        Landscaping Standards to Include New Requirements for New Development and
        Parking Lots within the Newly Established Holladay Crossroads Zone.
Mr. Hobbs presented the Ordinance Amendment and reported that the Planning Commission
previously sent a favorable recommendation to the City Council to approve the HCR Zone. The
City Council looked at the landscaping portion of the HCR Zone and determined that they wanted
to invite input from the Tree Committee. They met and memorialized the conversation and
Mr. Hobbs incorporated the suggested changes.

The nature of the proposed changes were intended to take the landscaping section out of the HCR
Zone and add it to Section 13.77. The proposed changes on pages 3, 4, and 5 relate to parking lot
areas. Ultimately, the Tree Committee defaulted back to end caps every 10 spaces, planters that
are the size and shape of a parking stall, and a Class 2 tree. They would not be placed in front of
building entryways for access and would be included with ADA needs. Rather than including a
specific list of trees to use, the Tree Committee referred back to the already adopted tree selection
guide. These changes would be incorporated with new construction or substantial reconstruction
or redevelopment of a property.

Commissioner Chris Layton felt that the changes were clear, concise, and easy to follow. He was
very impressed with what had been done. It would help beautify the parking area without making
snow removal difficult. The Planning Commission thanked Mr. Hobbs for his work.

Chair Bradshaw opened the public hearing.

Ryan Steele gave his address as 4876 South Colony Drive and stated that the landscaping
requirements are reasonable. He believed the Planning Commission, Tree Commission, and staff
gave them good thought. However, he worried that the efforts may be for nothing and discussed
the allowed coverage of the site. The two main parcels could potentially become massive building
