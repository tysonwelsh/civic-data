Recognizing Ogden–Weber Community Action Partnership’s 60 years of
elevating the individuals, families, and communities of Weber County by
encouraging self–sufficiency through their provided services and
collaboration
          A memo from Council staff regarding recognition of Ogden-Weber Community Action Partnership (OWCAP) in honor
of its 60th anniversary of service to Ogden and Weber County came before the Council for consideration. Council member
Choberka read the certificate of recognition issued by the Ogden City Council and Mayor Nadolski and presented representatives
of OWCAP with the certificate.
          OWCAP Executive Director Christine Ipsen thanked the Mayor and Council for this recognition; she noted OWCAP has
been serving the community for 60 years with the commitment of helping people to change their lives. OWCAP is dedicated to
serving for another 60 years, or until poverty in the community is eradicated. There will be a community open house on Friday,
March 28, 2025, and all are invited to attend.
         Councilmembers relayed their own personal experiences with OWCAP and expressed their gratitude to OWCAP for
being a great partner to Ogden City.

Proposed Sales Tax Agreement – Forest Service Building. A sales tax
agreement with the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency and in connection
therewith, the issuance of a City Obligation and any potential economic
impact that the project to be financed with the proceeds of bonds to be
issued by the Redevelopment Agency may have on the private sector
          A memo from the Management Services Department regarding a proposed Sales Tax Agreement came before the
Council for consideration. The memo stated on December 10, 2024, the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency Board approved
several actions to authorize the purchase of the U.S. Forest Service Building located at 507 25th Street. The Redevelopment
Agency Board approved a resolution approving the Redevelopment Agency Executive Director to purchase the Forest Service
building for $3.6 million, a resolution declaring the intent of the Redevelopment Agency to reimburse the Redevelopment Agency
for expenditures incurred in connection with the building, and a budget amendment appropriating $600,000 for earnest money and
maintenance costs associated with the purchase of the building. The Agency put the earnest money down as required by the
purchase contract in December 2024. On February 18, 2025, the Council adopted a resolution pledging sales tax and setting
parameters for the issuance of bonds for the purchase of the Forest Service Building. The resolution pledged sales tax as a
backstop to the Redevelopment Agency tax increment financing for the purchase of the Forest Service building. Also included in
the action was setting a public hearing on the bond parameters for March 18, 2025.
          Nate Robertson of Lewis Robertson Burningham (LRB) summarized the memo and reported that this item is a
continuation of the parameters resolution that was adopted by the Board last month; pledging sales tax revenue to secure the bond
requires the Board to hold a public hearing. He provided a brief overview of the remaining steps in the process to issue the bond
and proceed with the purchase of the Forest Service building.
          Chair White then opened the public hearing.
          There being no persons appearing to be heard, COUNCIL MEMBER RICHEY MOVED THE PUBLIC HEARING
BE CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY VICE CHAIR HYER, ALL VOTING AYE.
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reported that no action is required tonight; the bond parameters resolution was
approved by the Council on February 18, 2025.
Public Comments
          Steve Jones reported the “Save the Union Station” group has resumed their activities and is holding weekly meetings;
their current focus is on how the Union Station impacts 25th Street, downtown Ogden, and the community as a whole and he
wanted to be sure the Council and Administration know they are invited to attend those meetings. Some of the ideas that have
been proposed as part of the revitalization of the Union Station campus are great, but others are concerning to the group, and he
advised the City to continue to research and review all options and provide opportunities for public involvement in the planning
stages of the project.
          Laura Lewis addressed the condition of the water in her home; it was very dirty last week but is much clearer this week
and she is very happy about that. She wished to compliment City staff on how they handled what was a very difficult situation.
Every person she talked to was very helpful and patient. She then referenced the discussion during tonight’s work session
regarding wellbeing in the community; her feelings align closely with the findings of the survey, especially pertaining to the loss
of green space. She is also concerned about building heights for development projects and that new buildings could block views
and shield sunlight. She then discussed the property along the Ogden River that was formerly considered to be green belt in
nature; it was agricultural in nature, but that has shifted and commercial development and high-density residential is now allowed
along the River. People walking along the trail next to the River used to feel connected to nature, and now they feel like they are
walking down a tunnel between all the apartment buildings. Those types of things need to be carefully thought of when projects
are being planned.
          Teresa Bramwell raised some issues that are happening in Ogden as a result of some of the changes that have been made
in the federal government that have resulted in the loss of federal jobs. She has seen many rumors about the potential for the
federal buildings located in Ogden to be vacated and even sold. Ogden, for many decades, has relied upon federal jobs and money
and she has watched the community deteriorate in that same time. She would love for Ogden to focus more on small businesses,
or anything that is not being funded by the federal government. If the City government focuses more on its own people, the City
will improve.

Mayor Comments
          Mayor Nadolski thanked Ms. Lewis for recognizing City staff; the water issues she mentioned were very difficult and he
was pleased by how the staff handled the public feedback they received. He noted he spends a great deal of time at the Municipal
Building and knows there are many great people who work for the City. They are not perfect, but they work hard and will always
own when they make a mistake. He also referenced the wellbeing survey that was discussed during the work session and indicated
that it made him feel the City is focused largely on the right things but has more work to do. Everyone is interested in making the
community better and helping it thrive. He then discussed the years’ worth of efforts to revitalize the Ogden River; he
acknowledged concerns about development along the River but noted that the development has been planned in a way to preserve
the riparian area, but also to provide meaningful access to the River for the public.

Council member Comments
         Council member Myers reported that for the last four years he has had the privilege of serving as an Executive Officer for
the Ogden-Weber Chamber of Commerce and he disagreed with Ms. Bramwell about the lack of business growth in the
community; the opposite is true as the Chamber has noticed significant business growth in Ogden and surrounding areas and the
majority of that growth is due to entrepreneurs starting new businesses in the community. In the next few weeks, Council
members will be participating in ribbon cutting events for at least four new businesses in Weber County and approximately 50 to
70 new businesses are opened in the community on an annual basis. He is pleased with that growth as Ogden City is the hub of
what is happening in Weber County. Small businesses bring in a small tax base and large businesses generate even more, but all of
them are important because they bring people to the community.
         Chair White reported that during tonight's work session meeting, the Council visited with an employee from the City’s
Information Technology (IT) Division; during last week’s meeting she commented that she had not received an email that a
constituent claimed to have sent, but later she found that email had been filtered into a spam folder. The Council received advice
on how to adjust their filters on their City email accounts, and she wished to be transparent and follow-up on the comments she
previously made. Vice Chair Hyer stated he has experienced the same thing as Chair White and noted that he will be diligent in
reviewing his email account to ensure that his constituents have access to him. Chair White then noted that she was out of town
for a week in February and when she returned, she discovered she had a small water leak in her house; she reached out to City
staff and received support from a water conservation employee, and it was great to know that service is available to residents.
