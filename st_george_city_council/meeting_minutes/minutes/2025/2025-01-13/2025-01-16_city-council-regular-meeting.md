TEMPORARY LAND USE REGULATION AMENDMENT/ORDINANCE:
Consider approval of Ordinance No. 2025-006 for a temporary land use regulation amending §9-13-4 of the St. George City Code prohibiting off premise signs from being located along State Route 7 (SR-7) also known as Southern Parkway.

BACKGROUND and RECOMMENDATION: The City Council held a  meeting on January 9, 2025 and had a discussion item regarding off premise signs (billboards). At the meeting, the City Council desired to have City staff bring a temporary zoning ordinance to them that would prohibit off-premise signs from being located along State Route 7 (SR-7) also known as Southern Parkway. Title 9-13-1 (Signs and Displays) of the Code states, "It is determined to be in the best interest of the health, safety and welfare of the citizens of the city to regulate advertising in order to eliminate potential hazards to motorists and pedestrians; encourage signs which, by their good design, are integrated with and harmonious to the buildings and sites which they occupy; encourage sign legibility through the elimination of excessive and confusing sign displays, thus reducing driver inattention; preserve and improve the appearance of the city as a place in which to live and to work and create an attraction to nonresidents to come to visit or trade." The temporary land use ordinance is specifically intended to further this purpose.

HILLSIDE DEVELOPMENT PERMIT:
Consider approval of a Hillside Development Permit for the construction of an interchange near mile post 7 on SR-18 and a section of roadway to the west where existing slopes exceed 20%.  (Case No. 2024-HS-013)

BACKGROUND and RECOMMENDATION: In 2006, a master planned community called "The Trails" was approved by the City Council. Shortly thereafter, construction of an interchange and overpass began but was never completed.  On October 23, 2024, the application went before the Hillside Review Committee. They recommended approval of the application, stating that the overall slope where the road is proposed is less than 40% with some localized outcroppings. The hillside committee commented that the development should look at the large hill slope across the wash to aesthetically match the slopes by using a mixture of hydroseed and rocks, not just a bare slope, and to optimize the cut slope. The proposed interchange would be an underpass. No buildings or lots are proposed at this time. The Hillside Review Board (HSRB) and Planning Commission (PC) both held public meetings on the request allow development on slopes greater than 20%. The HSRB recommended approval of the roadway and determined that certain areas shown as 40%+ slopes were localized rock outcroppings. As part of the HSRB motion, they stated that the slopes adjacent to the proposed roadway should not be left bare but should aesthetically match the surrounding area by using hydroseed and rocks, and that the roadway should optimize the  cut slope. The Planning Commission made a motion recommending approval of the hillside permit as recommended by the HSRB. The motion was unanimous with a 7-0 vote.

Consider approval of Resolution No. 2025-003R approving and entering into an Interlocal Cooperation Agreement with Washington County regarding election services.
