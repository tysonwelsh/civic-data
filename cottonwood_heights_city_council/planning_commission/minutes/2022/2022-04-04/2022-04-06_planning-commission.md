36   Chair Mills introduced Project ADU-22-002, a public hearing to receive comments on a request
37   from Brian Allen for a Conditional Use Permit for a Detached Accessory Dwelling Unit
38   (“ADU”) located at 2217 East Cottonwood Cove Lane in the Rural Residential (RR-1-43) Zone.
39
40   Senior City Planner, Andy Hulka, reported that Project ADU-22-002 is the first Accessory
41   Dwelling Unit (“ADU”) application for review by the Planning Commission. ADUs became
42   legal in Cottonwood Heights with the passage of Ordinance Number 368, an ordinance enacting
43   and codifying Chapter 19.75 (“Accessory Dwelling Units”) of the Cottonwood Heights Code of
44   Ordinances. The ordinance was approved by the City Council in September 2021 in compliance
45   with House Bill 82 (HB 82 – Single-family Housing Modifications) during the 2021 General
46   Session of the Utah State Legislature. It required municipalities to allow internal ADUs in



     Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 04/06/2022                                 1
 1   single-family residential zones. The City Council decided to also allow detached ADUs as a
 2   conditional use when adopting the City’s ADU ordinance.              Before the adoption of
 3   Ordinance 368, ADUs were prohibited in Cottonwood Heights. However, guesthouses have been
 4   allowed if compliant with the bulk and massing requirements for accessory buildings and with
 5   the condition that rental of the guesthouse was prohibited (19.76.030.A). He confirmed that a
 6   permit to expand the existing guesthouse on the subject property was approved by the City in
 7   2019.
 8
 9   A map of the subject property was displayed. Mr. Hulka defined an ADU as a permanent
10   residential dwelling unit intended for one additional family located in a separate dwelling unit on
11   the same lot as a single-family dwelling unit. The difference between an ADU and a guesthouse
12   is that the rental, lease, or use of a guesthouse as a permanent residence for a second family on
13   the premises is prohibited. The conversion of a guesthouse to an ADU would allow for rental of
14   30 days or longer. Detached ADUs shall be subject to the following additional development
15   standards:
16
17      •   Any detached ADU shall be a permanent structure. Trailers, mobile homes, and other
18          portable structures shall not be permitted as detached ADUs. The City’s Building
19          Official shall determine whether a structure is permanent.
20      •   Any detached ADU shall be subject to all primary structure setback standards for the
21          zone in which it is located.
22      •   Any detached ADU shall meet all accessory building standards for height, lot coverage,
23          rear yard coverage, size, and any other applicable standards for the zone in which it is
24          located.
25      •   Any detached ADU on a flag lot shall meet primary structure flag lot setback standards
26          for the zone in which it is located.
27      •   Conversion of existing accessory buildings to detached ADUs is only permitted if the
28          structure meets or is modified to meet all current City standards and all applicable
29          provisions of this chapter.
30

34          1.2     (Project ZTA-19-001) Sensitive Lands Evaluation and Development
35                  Standards (“SLEDS”) Ordinance.
36
37   Assistant City Planner/Sustainability Analyst, Samantha DeSeelhorst, presented Project ZTA-19-
38   001 Sensitive Lands Evaluation and Development Standards (“SLEDS”) Ordinance. The
39   riparian standards were reviewed. She stated that within the existing SLEDS Ordinance, riparian
40   protection guidelines are referenced but the chapter that is supposed to contain them does not
41   exist. Most guidelines implement a buffered area system dividing areas adjacent to a waterway
42   into three segments. She noted that buffering measurements are consistent with live ordinances
43   being more restricted. The matter was reviewed by the Planning Commission in February 2022
44   at which time feedback was provided. It was requested that requirements from other agencies,
45   measurement protocols, Staff review processes, and all system mapping analyses be further
46   reviewed. Changes within the current draft were detailed. Ms. DeSeelhorst reported that they


     Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 04/06/2022                                      2
 1   intend to coordinate with the Federal Emergency Management Agency (“FEMA”) to require a
 2   similar feedback process as it can impact the flood plain.
 3
 4   Ms. DeSeelhorst stated that measurement protocols were updated and require measurements be
 5   considered from the top of the bank. The clarification also included that Staff review process
 6   should flow through the large area Development Review Committee (“DRC”) to provide more
 7   flexibility and language allowing for third-party experts if needed. Staff coordinated with the
 8   GIS department to prepare an estimate of structures impacted by the buffer areas. Geologic
 9   hazard areas were reviewed. Ms. DeSeelhorst confirmed that she will provide clarity regarding
10   the threshold for requiring the standards. It was anticipated that public comment will be received
11   during a May 2022 meeting. The Commission was favorable to understanding primary versus
12   secondary structures.
13
14   Tim Thompson from GeoStrata Engineering reported that he is a Professional Geologist and has
15   been consulting with the City since its incorporation. He was involved with the original SLEDS
16   Ordinance and has been assisting with the current update.
17
18   Mr. Hulka reported that the current draft includes multiple changes and addressed the
19   Commission’s concern with the 45-day requirement, steep slopes, preparation of a newsletter
20   article, and an extended Work Session. A redline draft was included in the Staff Report
21   highlighting the substantive changes. The appendix now references major sections of the Utah
22   Geological Survey (“UGS”) guidelines including mapping information, fault studies, slope
23   stability, landslides, debris flow, and rockfall studies. They pulled from conservative standards
24   with the requirement that a Geologist or Engineer provide stamped certification to verify that
25   safety standards and hazards have been addressed. They also are required to provide liability
26   insurance of up to $2 million.
27
28   Mr. Thompson stated that while the UGS makes great recommendations, they do not practice in
29   the field and their ideas may be ahead of the curve of the actual practice. He explained that
30   providing a warranty, guarantee, or certification would void his insurance coverage. It is illegal
31   in the State of Utah to provide a warranty as a licensed professional. The stamp of certification
32   is provided to establish that everything has been completed to ensure compliance. He noted that
33   the requirement to provide a certain level of insurance has not been determined in the State and
34   limited liability may be based on each project that remains negotiable. It was his opinion that the
35   unintended consequence of liability costs is that many top consultants often turn away smaller
36   projects. The remaining consultants charge an exorbitant amount for the same work due to a
37   lack of competition. It is their standard of practice to negotiate the amount of liability to be
38   covered for a particular project. The negotiation process was reviewed.
39
40   Ms. DeSeelhorst stated that Staff has noted that this topic will require continued discussion and
41   refinement.
42
43   Chair Mills stated that the public comment period will be forthcoming. He believed that
44   determining who is responsible for both private and commercial is extremely critical in terms of
45   the City’s liability.

 1   Slope issues were next reviewed. Mr. Hulka stated that the new ordinance specifies that no
 2   development, including structures and retaining walls, is permitted on slope areas above 30%.
 3   Exceptions were described. They intended to allow smaller slopes around local locations that
 4   may impact adjacent properties. Concerns were addressed while still allowing limited
 5   disturbances on properties of one acre or less. He confirmed that there is an appeal process that
 6   applies to any language in the chapter.
 7
 8   Mr. Thompson noted that as a consultant, he does not practice in Cottonwood Heights. He
 9   expressed concern with trying to rectify two distinct portions of the ordinance that allow cuts and
10   fills of up to 2:1 or 50% slopes. He stated that a natural slope of 30% cannot be touched.
11   Bridging the issues to create a more cohesive ordinance was desired. It was his opinion that
12   most communities strive to protect their viewsheds. This is done by restricting how high they
13   are allowed to go on a hillside by limiting the slope. He believed that they are more of a
14   viewshed issue and should be addressed separately under another City Code. Modification of
15   slopes was discussed. He encouraged discernment between slope stability issues and suggested
16   that the quality of community issues be dealt with separately.
17
18   The avalanche appendix was next discussed. Mr. Hulka stated that the model ordinance was
19   referenced that detailed elevations of 5,000 feet with adequate snow supply to produce
20   avalanches, which include slopes greater than 47%. An elevation map was displayed.
21
22   Mr. Hulka referenced structural fill and stated that compaction criteria are required beneath
23   structures with required percentages. He was notified that several changes were incorrect and
24   offered reassurance that updated language will be provided to ensure accuracy in the final
25   version.
26
27   Changes to the ordinance’s Architectural Design will include the removal of the Planning
28   Commission’s role to ensure consistency with current architectural review standards. Mr. Hulka
29   explained that in addition to the City’s normal design guidelines, additional sensitive area
30   requirements will be included and clarified.
31
32   The appeals process was next discussed. The original Code provided standards with the last
33   draft being revised to specify that instead of relying solely on the Appeals Hearing Officer, the
34   City will assemble a panel of experts to hear appeals on technical disputes regarding sensitive
35   lands issues. Mr. Hulka confirmed that the two have been merged and clarified that the City’s
36   Appeals Hearing Officer is the appeal authority for disputes, however, the City may assemble a
37   panel to assist with technical disputes. Details regarding the appeals process were discussed. It
38   was confirmed that if a decision becomes complicated and includes complex technical geologic
39   requirements, the City may assemble an Appeals Board.
40
41   Mr. Hulka reported that the revised language specifies that the review shall not exceed 45 days.
42   The intent of putting a number to the review eliminates an argument over the previous language
43   that stated that the City will act diligently in its review. Time frame options were discussed.

 1   Senior City Planner, Andy Hulka, presented the Staff Report and stated that the proposed one-
 2   acre property is located at 2217 East Cottonwood Cove Lane. A zoning map of the property was
 3   displayed. The RR-1-43 zone is a one-acre minimum lot size residential zone surrounded in part
 4   by residential zones. He defined an ADU as a permanent residential dwelling unit meant for one
 5   additional family located in a separate dwelling unit on the same lot as a single-family dwelling
 6   unit, either within the same building as the single-family dwelling unit or in a detached accessory
 7   building. Mobile homes or other portable structures do not qualify as an ADU. Additionally, an
 8   ADU may not be located within a mobile home or other portable structure. ADUs became legal
 9   in Cottonwood Heights with the passage of Ordinance Number 368, an ordinance enacting and
10   codifying Chapter 19.75 (“Accessory Dwelling Units”) of the Cottonwood Heights Code of
11   Ordinances. It was approved by the City Council in September 2021.
12
13   Mr. Hulka reported that the ordinance was adopted in compliance with House Bill 82 (HB 82 –
14   Single-Family Housing Modifications) during the 2021 General Session of the Utah State
15   Legislature, which required municipalities to allow internal ADUs in single-family residential
16   zones. The City Council decided to also allow detached ADUs as a conditional use when
17   adopting the City’s ADU ordinance. Prior to adoption of Ordinance 368, ADUs were prohibited
18   in Cottonwood Heights. However, guesthouses have been allowed if they comply with the bulk
19   and massing requirements for accessory buildings and with the condition that rental of the
20   guesthouse is prohibited (19.76.030.A). A permit to expand the existing guesthouse on the
21   subject property was approved by the City in 2019.
22
23   Mr. Hulka explained that the renting or leasing of the guesthouse is prohibited as are separate
24   utility meters. All other bulk and massing requirements must be met. The existing building on
25   the proposed lot was reviewed in 2019 according to the standards allowing the issuance of the
26   Building Permit. A visual depiction of the proposed site plan was displayed. Mr. Hulka
27   explained that the parking requirement for detached ADUs is two additional spaces that do not
28   block the required parking for the primary dwelling. Required setbacks are to be a minimum of
29   10 feet and must include a buffer zone. Staff recommended approval with conditions set forth in
30   the Staff Report.
31

33   enough room to provide three cars with adequate egress into the rear of the property. There is
34   also a garage with space for three more vehicles.
