The following are the minutes of the Herriman Planning Commission meeting held on Thursday, January
20, 2022 at 6:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Council Chambers, 5355 West Herriman Main Street, Herriman,
Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was posted in the City Hall, on the City’s website,
and delivered to members of the Council, media, and interested citizens.

Presiding: Vice Chair Andy Powell

Commissioners Present: Brody Rypien, Jackson Ferguson, Heather Garcia, Andrea Bradford, Darryl Fenn,
and Forest Sickles

Commissioners Excused: Chris Berbert, Adam Jacobson

Staff Present: City Planner Michael Maloy, Communications Specialist Destiny Skinner, Deputy Recorder
Wendy Thorpe, Planning Manager Clint Spencer, Planner I Talia Wright, Engineer Josh Petersen, Planner II
Sheldon Howa, Community Development Director Blake Thomas, Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas


Vice Chair Andy Powell called the meeting to order at 6:03 p.m.


City Planner Michael Maloy informed the Commission that, in the work meeting, Council discussed the
appointment of Heather Garcia to the Planning Commission, term limits, and the addition of more Commission
alternates. They also discussed potential changes to the accessory structures draft chapter. The Bowler rezone
for property at 13100 South and 5600 West was approved with the zoning conditions as recommended by the
Commission.

Commissioner Rypien arrived at 6:05
Planning Manager Spencer explained the distribution of units for items 3.1 and 3.2. He reviewed the comments
received for items 3.3, 3.4, and 3.5, which had already been forwarded to the Commission, and briefly overviewed
the conceptual plan for the crescent shaped piece. He provided a history of the auto mall development and the
density for the neighboring areas.

Commissioner Bradford arrived at 6:19 p.m.

Commissioners discussed the potential zoning conditions, buffering, potential density, and ideal uses for the
separate properties. Contaminated soil history, treatment options and challenges were discussed. Allowable
contamination levels, remediation and locations were reviewed.



Planner Howa reviewed the proposed amendments to the digital sign ordinance. Ambient light measurement
and sign distances from roadways were discussed. Commissioners expressed concern with allowing them in
some city areas and debated allowable distance from residential communities.

The Commission adjourned the work meeting by consensus at 6:49 p.m.


Vice Chair Andy Powell called the meeting to order at 7:00 p.m.


Joe Rummel led the audience in the Pledge of Allegiance.


Full Quorum Present.


No conflicts of interest were reported.

Planning Manager Spencer presented the recommendation to reconfigure pods 35 and 39 of the South Hills
Master Plan. The applicant requested to change from ground level garden style apartments to a five-story over
podium parking design. The proposed amendment would shift all residential units to pod 35 and designate the
remainder of pods as commercial uses. As a Legislative item, the Commission was making a recommendation
to City Council. The engineering department reviewed the proposal and had no concerns.

Applicant Alex Dahlstrom approached the podium and stated they looked forward to beginning the project.

Planning Manager Clint Spencer explained the general land use amendment. All comments received before 3pm
today were forwarded to the Commissioners. He identified the western property location and contaminated soil
relocation details. The proposal met the general plan requirement to provide life cycle housing, recommended
25 feet rear yard setbacks and maximum building height of 35 feet. A change from commercial to high density
housing was requested for the eastern property section.
Commissioners confirmed maximum allowable building heights. Planning Manager Spencer confirmed
maximum height was 35 feet in the C-1 zone, and 45 feet in the C-2 zone. Details were still being determined
for the Master Development Agreement. Potential heights could be determined by the MDA.

Applicant Larry Myler approached the podium and offered to answer questions. He provided a general overview
and explained the history of the properties.

Vice Chair Powell explained the City Council would make the final decision.

Vice Chair Powell opened the public hearing.

Jared Preusz, Spoke representing a group that lived near the crescent piece of land. He and his neighbors would
prefer homes with the density lowered to closely match their community, with a maximum height of two stories.
If lower density residential was not possible they would prefer commercial development. They were in favor of
responsible development for this prime commercial location.

Marica Danes Did not want apartments. She’s a teacher and the new developments have added way more kids
than the current schools can handle. Teachers talk about this a lot and their jobs were greatly affected by it.

Joe Rummel Acknowledged Herriman needed more housing, as he has spent the last year looking for a house.
Olympia Hills will add more housing, so this area should remain commercial to keep business in Herriman.

David Blake stated he was against the plan because Herriman City acquired 15 acres when 12600 South was
extended. Any needed improvements should be paid for by the City. It should not be changed to residential, as
the City already had enough high-density residential housing. The City needed carefully planned commercial and
should not make money from the deal.

Fern Cloward Stated she was concerned the reputation of the city could change and make Herriman a place
her grandkids would not want to live. The high-density housing has made them less likely to want to live here
because they want space. It would be best for the future for this area to remain commercial.

Ron Koller His main concern was losing commercial sites by changing them to residential. He did not want to
see more three-story apartments and condos. He had additional concern with the odd shaped commercial piece
being sold to a developer as it was prime commercial property and the city needed sales tax. Herriman residents
should have options for spending money in Herriman instead of Riverton, and two more fire stations were
needed. The commercial areas should remain commercial.

Andrew Lawrence Spoke on behalf of a group which preferred residential in the triangle piece, but they had
problems with the proposed plan. More studies were needed on the contaminated soil and the remediation plans
should be available for public review and completed before zoning changes. Three story buildings were too
high, and no buildings should be higher than two stories. House Bill 82 removed accessory dwelling restrictions,
which could increase basement and mother-in-law additions. He thought that could be used as a loophole to
allow taller buildings. He thought it was dangerous for first time home buyers to qualify for expensive new home
loans with conditions requiring tenants. The state recommendations should not be overruled by Master
Development Agreements. The addition of too many cars would cause further traffic and pedestrian issues near
Bastian elementary school. He thought there could be a conflict of interest and the auto mall should be a separate
item. The Commission should recommend City Council do what was best for the land, regardless of the auto
mall outcome.

Kenneth Olsen Stated he was the owner of the 7.5-acre rectangular parcel. Viable commercial needed visibility,
location, and access. The corner was better for commercial than his property. His property used to be zoned
multifamily and he spent lots of money removing lead and arsenic from the soil to meet residential standards.
After which, it was changed to commercial. If the corner was rezoned multifamily then his property should be
also. Connectivity was needed between Main Street and 12600 South.

Philip Bates His biggest concern with the odd shaped 15-acre section was 12600 South would be the main
travel lane for the Olympia development. His parents lived on Bangerter where property was lost to eminent
domain. If 12600 South were widened property would be lost. The developer was there for quick cash and not
thinking ahead long term.

Austin Maxwell He was pleased to see lots of educated people speak on behalf of the citizens. He asked if the
property would be subsidized section eight housing. He was concerned to have many apartment complexes near
each other. People living off the government would move to Herriman and decrease property values.
Commercial was needed to provide more jobs.

Brenda Mortenson She was concerned with the developer saying dirt from the triangle portion was needed to
fill in the auto mall property. The developer has said if he can’t get dirt the city would lose the auto mall and he
would build a tall building on top of the dirt next to residential. She did not appreciate the fear tactics. The city
really needed more commercial.

Vice Chair Powell closed the public hearing.

Applicant Larry Myler offered to answer questions raised by tonight’s comments. Commissioner Bradford asked
why he preferred residential instead of commercial. He responded they were interested in providing commercial,
which was why he was trying to add the auto mall along with about 14 retail pads. It was the goal of the developer
to make a viable project, which would be more likely with a mix of uses. They were brought in at the behest of
the city and have spent lots of money assembling the proposal. He certainly didn’t say if they don’t get this, they
would build forty-five feet high on top of a mound. What he said was it was zoned C-2 currently, as the soil was
too contaminated for residential, which did have a 45-foot maximum height. He had no plan to do that, but it
could happen on the site. This developer would bring more commercial than any other developer. The auto mall
would be a big tax generator. He emphasized they would continue working with the neighborhood and
accommodate their wishes when possible. Commissioner Garcia asked if they have had the soil tested. Applicant
Myler responded they have, and a soil movement plan was in development. It would be presented to the City
and made available to citizens upon completion. They intend for it to be a very safe and open process. As it sits,
the toxicity level precluded residential, but not commercial development. The dirt was not necessary in the
fifteen-acre auto mall area, and he apologized for the misunderstanding. However, it could be moved there to
reduce the multi-million dollar bid to move it further away. That specific area was better suited to residential,
and most traffic would be from MVC directly, and not through the existing neighborhoods.

Commissioner Rypien asked if the fifteen-acre site remained commercial and no dirt needed to be moved, would
dirt be added to the auto mall site from elsewhere? Applicant Myler responded some dirt would be brought to
the property to level the site. Commissioner Ferguson asked if discussion had taken place between the developer
and Mr. Olsen regarding the needs of both commercial developments. Applicant Myler responded he spoke
with Mr. Olsen about joining the properties but had not come to terms. They discussed the addition of roads
connecting the properties to main street to increase access points. Commissioner Powell addressed anticipated
expansion of 12600 would not require eminent domain home removal as adequate setbacks were in place to
accommodate additional growth, per the transportation master plan projections. He emphasized consideration
of financial aspects were considered by City Council, and not an aspect of the Commission consideration
process. Commissioner Garcia expressed concern with the proposal to move contaminated soil from the
crescent area to the nearby location. Contaminated soil needed immediate action regardless of the anticipated
use of the property. It should be properly cleaned up now. Vice Chair Powell was in favor of moving it to the
commercial location and capping it with cement car lots. Commissioner Fenn preferred to consider each lot of
land separately, as they were three separate properties. He felt his duty was to protect the city and watch out for
the public. More information was needed before he could support a change to the general plan. Commissioner
Sickles expressed appreciation to the residents for expressing their concerns. It was important for the growth of
the city that all current commercial properties remain commercial or mixed use. Commissioner Ferguson
expressed the opinion the separate properties were intertwined and should be planned together. It was difficult
to consider this proposal without knowing the intention for the Olsen property. If this section were changed to
residential, the Olsen property would request the same, and commercial would be lost. Commissioner Rypien
emphasized ideal commercial property locations were discussed at length with the general plan process. The
addition of new large residential developments would make Herriman Boulevard more commercially viable. Plan
for the City’s future needs. Commissioner Bradford was reluctant to give up viable commercial properties.
Commissioner Garcia agreed losing commercial areas would be detrimental to the city. Commissioner Ferguson
reminded the Commission that MVC would eventually be a full freeway with access roads and lots of traffic.
Community Development Director Thomas stated staff was working through the transportation master plan
update with the addition of the Olympia development. Thirty-five thousand to forty thousand vehicles per day
were projected on that road for 2050. Such volumes were planned with current commercial use on these
properties. Traffic projection models anticipated seven lanes of traffic and two fire code access points. The
Commissioners discussed options for action and possible motions.
