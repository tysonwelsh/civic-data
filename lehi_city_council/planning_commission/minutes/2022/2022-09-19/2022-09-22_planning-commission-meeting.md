2. Regular Agenda

2.1) Staff Report on research into parking requirements for attached dwelling units.
Planning intern, Trevor Allen, gave a Staff report on research into parking requirements for the
attached dwelling units. He provided information and parking studies on local parking
requirements that were taken from surrounding cities.

The Commissioners thanked Trevor for his research. The Planning Department was happy with
the findings and would be implementing some of the changes.

2.4) Public hearing and recommendation of Jeffrey Merchant’s request for review of the All
Seasons Assisted Living General Plan amendment on 1.52-acres of property located at 322
& 344 North 2375 West changing the designation from VLDRA (very low-density residential
agriculture) to NC (neighborhood commercial).
Jacob presented information on the agenda item.

Kim Struthers clarified the code was amended due to General Plan changes and because the State
Code changed how Cities could regulate Group Homes. Commissioner Everett stated this was not
identified as Neighborhood Commercial when the General Plan was updated.

The applicant, Jeff Merchant, was present. He clarified a portico sharing a driveway and being
vertical on the lot. He had a surveyor come out and draw up plans for the property and it did not
meet the requirements to be a residential lot. He would like to have the ability to build the structure
on the opposite side of the property, similar to the building adjacent to the vacant lot. He does not
for-see more traffic issues with a new building being built. He feels he is a good neighbor, and his
current business has not created issues. This building would replicate the building they own on the
same property. The existing building is approximately 8,700 square feet. He would like to use the
land for what it was originally approved for.

Commissioner Nielsen opened the public hearing. Commissioner Nielsen acknowledged there was
one online comment from Diedre Clark that was opposed with the amendment.

Diedre Clark stated she is opposed to the amendment. She provided information on LDR areas
from the City. She feels the current zoning is essential to the residents in keeping the area safe.
The traffic is busy, and the kids don't cross where they should and there are not crossing guards
for the middle school age kids. She knows the residents of the assisted living center are not a threat,
but the employees, citizens, maintenance vehicles, etc. are all contributors to traffic. Business
traffic will also be increasing to the area. The concept to the Beehive Home was to provide Senior
Living Center to the neighborhood, which she supports, but changing the zone to fit the facility is
not conducive to fit the neighborhood. She wants to keep the zoning laws as they were intended
when they purchased their homes with the idea that they were in a residential area.

Tyler Bateman stated he has concerns with this zone change. The Beehive Home is currently
empty. He is concerned about expansion of the current home. He is concerned about the selling of
the Beehive Home. He does not find it in the best interest of the residents as purchasing the Beehive
Home has been an issue. The proposal has expired, and Jeff, the applicant, purchased the property
after the zone changed. The structure that was proposed was entirely different than what was built.
Tyler stated the access off of 2300 West is affecting 300 North where there are already traffic
issues. Once the zone is changes, he is concerned about his rights. He feels it is a good buffer to
allow a non-conforming use for Beehive Homes but allowing a new zone change will create issues
if it passes as Neighborhood Commercial and will bring in unwarranted issues.

RaeLynn Bateman wanted to reiterate what Tyler said. She is not concerned as much with the
zoning change, but once the change is made, businesses move, and her biggest concern is what
might move into the building in the future. When she looked up neighborhood commercial, the
hours were highly concerning. The property is surrounded by all residential and a park, and it could
potentially have customers coming and going all hours and could have an impact on the residents.
She has small children who attend the schools on 2300 West, and the traffic is already an issue
with visibility. There is also a lot of parking along the road. She has had to ask people to move
from her driveway so she could have access. Initially, with the first proposal, they were not going
to add more parking. The Beehive Building is currently empty, and she believes this is just a
marketing strategy. With a zone change, she feels this will open potential issues.

Doug Clark stated when the previous plans were considered they were a big part of the process.
Lehi City is a lot more permissive than other Cities, and he is happy to hear a lot has changed
because it guarantees safety in his neighborhood. He was happy to hear parking has been restricted.

Lane Adamson stated he is opposed to the zone change. He is happy ambulances haven't been
around lately because Beehive currently is not housing anyone.

Rex Wilkin stated he is concerned the traffic in the neighborhood, and a four way stop has been
installed. There are a lot of new homes that have been built that has contributed to the traffic. He
has no problem with the Beehive home but is opposed to the zone change. The last three years
there has been a significant increase in traffic.

(Inaudible) Hassleback stated the area is not zoned for the area and the traffic is horrific. She had
concerns that the company wouldn’t be sold to a company that Jeff owns. She wanted to know
what why the residents of Beehive Home were asked to leave, and if the new building would be
an All Seasons home (locked down home). She would like some clarification with the plans.

Tyler Bateman wanted to say that the Beehive Home is not an issue and would like to see it stay.
He wanted to clarify that as it is no one has complained, and it seems logical to leave it as it's use
and as a commercial building.

Commissioner Nielsen closed public hearing.

The applicant stated they have never been a source of parking or traffic issues, with a few
exceptions of holiday parties. They are looking at a general plan change, not a zone change, as it
is currently being utilized. He is just asking to be given the opportunity to have a discussion if it
is changes on the general plan later. He doesn't like the idea that the term Neighborhood
Commercial is being used. He wanted to respond to Ms. Hassleback. The name was changed to
All Seasons. It was closed explicitly due to staffing issues. They have maintained their Health Care
License and have had offers to buy the building from other Health Care professionals. He
understands traffic concerns and feels it is intercommunity traffic. Safety is not a huge concern
with traffic from their buildings.

Commissioner Everett asked when the Living Assistant building will be back open. Mr.
Merchant stated they have seen a slow shift to opening back up. COVID 19 staffing has been a
huge issue and he is hoping to be up and running by mid-January 2023. Commissioner Jackson
asked how many other facilities Mr. Merchant has. Mr. Merchant state he has one in West
Jordan, and one is Cedar City.

Kim Struthers stated the applicant could request a variance for a residential lot, or a flag lot, but
they would be separate application processes. He would still be able to apply for a group home,
but it would be a conditional use. It would be a reasonable request with a variance, and a hearing
officer would most likely take that into consideration. Commissioner Everett asked about a lot line
adjustment instead of going to NC designation. Kim stated lots could be combined, but once a non-
conforming use has been a year, the application expires. Commissioner Newall asked if they were
able to build a home if there would be square footage and height requirements and brought up
property rights and legal issues and other options from the applicant. Commissioner Jackson likes
the idea of a development agreement where there is some control of what business goes in the NC.
Commissioner Everett is not inclined to be in favor of NC, but to find another option for the
community. Commissioner Ellis doesn’t know that NC is unreasonable, but doesn’t feel that it is
the answer, yet. He thinks the ability to do a development is an option. Kim stated it would have
to be used as a home, and could be used as a youth home, with up to eight residents. Commissioner
Nielsen stated this seems like it could create legal issues.

        Motion: Commissioner Carlson moved to forward a negative recommendation of Jeffrey
        Merchant’s request for review of the All Seasons Assisted Living General Plan
        amendment on 1.52-acres of property located at 322 & 344 North 2375 West
        changing the designation from VLDRA (very low-density residential agriculture) to
        NC (neighborhood commercial), revising it to be in compliance with the State Code;
        findings that the proposed GPA is not in conformance or consistent. Noting that staff
        evaluate other uses to the North property. Commissioner Everett seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Ellis, no; Commissioner Nielsen, no; Commissioner Jackson;
        Commissioner Newall, no; Commissioner Eyre, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes;
        Commissioner Carlson, yes. The motion failed.

        Motion: Commissioner Ellis moved to send a positive recommendation of Jeffrey
        Merchant’s request for review of the All Seasons Assisted Living General Plan
        amendment on 1.52-acres of property located at 322 & 344 North 2375 West
        changing the designation from VLDRA (very low-density residential agriculture) to
        NC (neighborhood commercial); including all DRC comments and findings stated in
        the report, providing Staff a list of possible alternatives of changing the General Plan
        Amendment, group home, changing possible Neighborhood Commercial, boundary line,
        and a development agreement. Commissioner Jackson seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Ellis, yes; Commissioner Nielsen, yes; Commissioner Jackson, yes;
        Commissioner Newall, yes; Commissioner Eyre, no; Commissioner Everett, no;
        Commissioner Carlson, no. The motion passed 4 - 3 with a positive recommendation.
