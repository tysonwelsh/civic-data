The purpose of the work session was to receive a presentation regarding the Marshall White Center project, including an
update on the pre-construction process and preliminary constructing financing options; discuss Council business; and hear Council
comments.

Marshall White Center Project

Public Services Deputy Director/City Engineer Justin Anderson thanked the Marshall White Center Advisory (MWC)
Committee for their efforts as part of this project, the public for their involvement, and members of City staff for their
involvement in the project to date. He then noted that tonight’s presentation outline includes an overview of the project design by
Brent Tippets, VCBO Architects; a review of the bid process by himself; a funding review by Comptroller Stout; discussion of
bonding from Laura Lewis, the City’s bond counsel; and the proposed construction schedule and program adjustments by
Recreation Manager Edd Bridge.

Mr. Tippets used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide exterior and interior design renderings for the MWC
facility; he identified community spaces and discussed the programming opportunities of each space, a teaching kitchen, artroom
with kiln, indoor pool and associated changing spaces/locker rooms, space for the Head Start program, childcare area,
exercise/fitness studio, boxing ring and trailing area, and administrative offices.

Council member Hyer stated Mr. Tippets referred to the kitchen as a teaching kitchen, but he asked if it is a functional ©
kitchen as well. Mr. Tippets stated that it does have a cooking surface and pantry, but it is not classified as a fully commercial
kitchen, and it is important to use the correct terminology when describing the space. Council member Hyer asked if the kitchen

: can be used as a service kitchen for large events, to which Mr. Tippets answered yes; however, the intent is not for food to be
cooked and served from the kitchen. Council member Hyer asked about the artificial turf area and asked if it will be similar to
football field turf surfaces. Mr. Tippet stated that the turf will include rubber or sand as filler material, which is common in
duplicating natural turf surfaces.

Chair Choberka asked if there have been any major changes to the design since the Council’s last review. Mr. Tippets
answered no.

Council member Hyer asked if there are any programmed spaces in the basement of the facility. Mr. Tippets stated that
the building will be twice the size of the existing facility and it will be more flexible; there is nothing below ground level and all
construction will be above grade. (

Ms. Stout acknowledged the gap between the project cost and available funding and noted Administration is still working
on fundraising for the project; however, if those efforts are not successful it may be necessary to increase the bond amount. The
annual bond payment for the $17 million bond would be approximately $1.6 million per year for 30 years and the repayment
source would be Business Depot Ogden (BDO) Lease Revenue. :

There was discussion about fundraising efforts and whether there is any flexibility in the bid; Mr. Anderson noted the bid
has been qualified and pricing will be held for 60 days. It will be necessary to award and execute the contract in order to guarantee
the bid amount. Bids were opened on April 21 and the contract must be awarded within 60 days from that date.

Council member Nadolski asked if the turf area in the facility will include drop-down batting cages, to which Mr. Bridge
answered yes.

Bond Counsel, Laura Lewis, provided a review of the schedule for moving forward with bonding for the project; closing
could occur by June 21, 2023. The parameters resolution for the bond will have a maximum par amount of $31 million for the
MWC. Administration has also identified a need to improve the parking structure at The Junction and it makes sense to include
the funding for that transaction in the bond resolution, with a total bond amount of $2 million. She discussed the debt service

} structure for the bond, which is a sales tax revenue bond rather than a Municipal Building Authority (MBA) bond.

“ Council Executive Director Eller-Smith inquired as to why bond counsel has not recommended an MBA bond. Ms.
Lewis stated that it was an option, but the interest rate for such a bond would be higher due to the way the bond would be secured.
It made more sense to pursue a sales tax bond in order to keep interest rates as low as possible.

Discussion centered on the use of BDO Lease Revenue for debt service of the bond and options for early repayment of
the bond; Council member Hyer stated he is becoming increasingly concerned about the use of BDO Lease Revenue for expenses
that are not one-time in nature. He asked Council leadership to carefully review any proposed ordinance amendment relating to
the use of BDO Lease Revenue and pursue strongly worded statements in the ordinance to communicate that BDO Lease Revenue
should be used for one-time expenses, but an exception is being made for this project. Ms. Stout agreed and stated Administration
can work with Council staff on that issue. :

Mr. Bridge then summarized the proposed construction schedule and program adjustments; he reiterated City staff
proposes that the contract be awarded to BH, Inc by June 20, 2023. He noted the anticipated closure date for the existing MWC
facility is June 1 with demolition and construction to begin the first week of July 2023. The programs that are currently being held
at the MWC facility — boxing, dance, cheer, and tumbling — will be relocated to the Ben Lomond High School facility. Music, arts
and crafts, Lego, chess, and ballet classes will be relocated to the main branch of the Weber County Library. Adult arts classes
will be relocated to the Golden Hours Center. Additionally, mighty mite basketball will be relocated to Highland Jr. High; mighty
mite soccer will be relocated to the School District Gymnasium; speed and agility classes will be relocated to Liberty Park; and
cooking classes will be relocated to Kitchen Kneads.

There was discussion about the disposition of the equipment and materials at the current MWC facility; the construction
timeline; the need for additional gym space for other recreation programs that have been hosted at the MWC in the past; and plans
for an open house for the public to say goodbye to the current facility as well as a groundbreaking ceremony for construction of
the new facility.

Chief Administrative Officer Johnson commented on the efforts of City staff and the MWC Committee and thanked
everyone for their commitment to the project. He then invited Monica Kapp, Fleet and Facilities Manager, to discuss the proposed
improvements to the Junction Parking Structure. Ms. Kapp noted there are ongoing structural improvement needs at the facility; a
structural study was conducted in 2020 and BHB Engineering recommended structural improvements totaling $2 million. She

___ presented photos taken during the study to highlight some of the issues at the structure. The Garland Company/CDC Restoration
.’ completed repairs and resurfacing on the ramp of the structure and waterproofing at various locations. The total cost of these
. repairs was $869,896. There are still critical repairs to be completed; costs for the north structure are estimated at $775,556 and
costs for the south structure are $814,797. With increases in pricing, it is estimated that at least another $1,000,000 will be needed

to complete the remaining repairs. These repairs would be on both structures and can be phased over multiple years.
a

Council member White asked if this will reduce the bond amount for the other parking structures to be constructed in the
City. Ms. Stout stated that this amount will actually be included with the bond for the MWC facility; however, the operation and \ }
maintenance costs for The Junction parking structure will eventually be transferred to the larger parking network structure in the
City, but the repairs are needed right away and cannot be delayed until future bonding is completed for parking structures at The
Wonder Block.

Council member Blair asked if it is wise to include the $2 million in the bond, which means the costs will be repaid over
a 30-year period with high interest costs. He asked if there are other options for funding the project without bonding. Ms. Stout
stated Administration can review the budget to determine if there is funding available, but she would not recommend using BDO
Lease Revenue for the project. She can evaluate the cost of interest associated with adding $2 million to the bond in order to help
the Council make an educated decision. Council member Hyer suggested the project meets the definition of a Capital
Improvement Plan (CIP) project and there may be CIP funding available for it. This led to discussion about the timing of the
project and the source of repayment of the debt; Mr. Johnson noted the problems are significant enough to dictate that the project
be done now rather than waiting for budget development for a CIP project for the work. He added the Redevelopment Agency
(RDA) owns the parking structure and RDA funding will be used to repay the debt. Ms. Lewis discussed the flexibility in
structuring bond repayment using multiple funding sources.

Ms. Eller-Smith then reviewed the schedule of actions needed by the Council to proceed with awarding the construction
contract and issuing bonds for the project; the parameters resolution can be adopted on May 2 with a public hearing scheduled for
May 23 on the bond.

Chair Choberka thanked the MWC Committee for attending tonight’s meeting; she also thanked City staff, Mr. Tippets,
and Ms. Lewis for attending and providing valuable information tonight.
