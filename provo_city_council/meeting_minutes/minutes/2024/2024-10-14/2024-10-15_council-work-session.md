    1.      Water Rate Recommendations (24-045) (0:10:26)
Presentation:
The item was presented by Gordon Haight, Public Works Director, and Jim McKnight, Public
Business Manager. They provided an update on the financial performance of the water department
and discussed adjustments to proposed water rate increases. Over the past decade, the department
generated $193 million in revenue. They revised their initial recommendation for a 15% rate increase,
proposing a lower rate of 12%. The presentation highlighted that 83% of revenue comes from utility
rates, though they aim to diversify income sources through grants, impact fees, and connection fees.

Discussion:
    Councilor Rachel Whipple inquired whether the city was leasing water.
           o Gary Calder responded that the city is not currently leasing any water, though it has
               leased water to Sundance in the past, pending state approval.
    Councilor Rachel Whipple asked whether new water legislation passed last year benefits the
       city or only farmers.
           o Gary Calder clarified that it benefits farmers and advised the city to utilize its existing
               water rights and follow the new water plan.

    2.      Provo River Delta (24-085) (0:58:07)

Presentation:
The Director of Utah Reclamation Litigation & Conservation Commission, Michael Mills,
presented a summary of the Utah Lake Delta Restoration Project. He provided an overview of the
project’s history, highlighting recent progress and inviting the Council to upcoming events on
Friday and Saturday. He displayed an aerial photo of the project and discussed how heavy snowfall
over the past two years has significantly replenished the area. The project aims to develop the delta
into a recreational zone, with plans for trails, canoe launch areas, and wildlife viewing towers.

Although the project is nearing completion, there is still work to be done on the original channel,
including adding water, improving recreation facilities, and creating a park. Additionally, Mills
emphasized the need for a boundary line agreement to manage storm drainage infrastructure that
crosses property lines. The agreement would grant easements for maintenance and management,
including a temporary easement for ditch piping. The Council will need to take action since this is
considered a land disposal.

Mills also discussed the outreach efforts for the project, including organizing field trips, working
with volunteer groups, and involving high school students in restoration activities.

Discussion:

        Councilor Travis Hoban: What is the long-term plan for dealing with stagnant water, as
         the bubblers don’t seem effective?
             o Michael Mills: The work is not yet complete. Two dams will be installed to help
                raise the water level. Some trees that fell caused issues, and the Council needs to
                decide the future direction.
        Councilor Hoban: Are the dams temporary or permanent?
             o Michael Mills: The smaller dam will be permanent to keep water levels stable. The
                delta will fill when Utah Lake rises and release water when the lake levels drop.
        Councilor Hoban: Will the water become stagnant?
             o Michael Mills: The delta always flows slowly, and aerators will help maintain that
                flow. A pump will eventually be installed to assist with water movement.
        Councilor Rachel Whipple: Are you ensuring that the water quality meets standards?
             o Michael Mills: Yes, the water quality is monitored to meet state requirements.
        Councilor Becky Bogdin: Does the water have a bad smell?
             o Michael Mills: It smells like natural water and does not have a putrid odor.
                Although the water appears green, it is not due to harmful algal blooms.
        Councilor Gary Garrett: Is there a need for mosquito abatement?
             o Michael Mills: They are prepared and have already responded with a three-day
                abatement process.
        Councilor George Handley: Expressed satisfaction that the project is encouraging birds to
         move away from the airport and nest closer to the delta.

No action was taken at this meeting, but the boundary line agreement will require future Council
approval.

    4.      A resolution of the Redevelopment Agency of Provo City designating a Survey Area
            for the Proposed City Center Community Reinvestment Project Area (24-082)
            (1:25:46)


Presentation:
Keith Morey, Assistant Director of Development Services, led the discussion on designating a
survey area for the proposed City Center Community Reinvestment Project Area. He emphasized
that there was no immediate rush on approving the resolution and recommended that the largest
map be used. This map encompasses the area north of Center Street, from University Avenue to 400
South and between 1st West and 4th West. Keith explained that the map serves as a public
notification indicating future development plans for the designated area. The first step is to establish
the boundary, followed by drafting a resolution. Afterward, the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) can
approve the resolution, and public notification can proceed.

Discussion:

        Board Member Becky Bogdin asked if this project would help improve the traffic flow
         from 1st North to Center Street.
             o Keith Morey confirmed that it could be addressed as part of the RDA's projects.
        Board Member Becky Bogdin shared ideas for potential projects within the designated
         area, and Keith acknowledged them, advising her to specify locations in future discussions.

Motion:
Board Chair Rachel Whipple made a motion to move forward with the map, ensuring that the full
right of way on the north side of 100 North and the west side of 100 West is included. Board
Member Becky Bogdin seconded the motion.

Vote:

        Yes: Board Members Garrett, Bogdin, Whipple, Hoban, Handley
         Result: The motion passed unanimously (5-0).
