2. Presentations and Reports
2.1 Dry Creek Recreation Area Discussion
The Council discussed the potential recreation development of Dry Creek Reservoir. Staff noted
that the dam was originally built for flood control, debris management, and water storage and has
proven to be successful in that endeavor. Council members and city staff discussed the
feasibility, costs, and challenges of developing the large area for recreation, including issues like
restrooms, maintenance, and staffing. Other issues discussed included concerns with maintaining
the water level and usability. Potential funding sources were also discussed, including a county
grant and contributions from other entities, but concerns remain about ongoing maintenance
costs and staffing requirements. The Council was interested in obtaining more detailed cost
breakdowns and exploring collaborative solutions, with the Mayor proposing a comprehensive
spreadsheet of costs and options to aid in decision-making. Alternative sites like Mill Pond were
also considered for recreational development.

2.2 Fiber Network Update
Shaye Ruitenbeek provided an update on the fiber optic build-out process, including pre-
registration rates and subscriptions. Areas for signups are happening every two weeks. The
Council and Mrs. Ruitenbeek discussed the speed of internet service in different areas and
addressed concerns about construction timelines and communication with residents.

2.3 Financial Report
Dean Lundell, Finance Director, provided an update on sales tax revenues, highlighting a
positive trend despite some fluctuations. He also discusses development revenues, noting a
decrease from previous years but expecting projects to come online soon. An estimated $3
million in unspent funds will roll into the general fund balance. The Council discussed the
upcoming budget and fund balance policy.

2.4 Environmental Sustainability Update
Todd Munger, Susan Wilson, and Angie Parkin with the Environmental Sustainability
Committee gave an update to the Council. They noted that citizens can check out radon testing
kits from the library, and the city is promoting water conservation through water barrels and
waterwise awards. They also invited the Council to participate in an event celebrating the 900
acres of open space preservation on Traverse Mountain.

4. Consideration of Ordinance #30-2024 approval of the North Lake Commercial Park
   General Plan Amendment on 2.99 acres located at approximately 1250 South 1100 West,
   changing the land use designation from Commercial to Heavy Commercial (C-H).
   (Tabled at the April 23, 2024 meeting.)

    Jared Stoddard was representing the applicant. He said that the concept is to build a multi-use
    flex space.

    Kim Struthers discussed different potential uses if the council decides to make an amendment.

    Mayor Johnson asked for public comments.

    Jessica Toffler read a letter on behalf of Sandra and Kenneth Jensen, who are homebound.
    They felt there was already unbearable noise in that area and that 1100 West was at full
    capacity. Personally, she does not feel it is fair to rezone the area. She had considered the
    zoning when purchasing her home and believes it would be unfair to change it now. She also
    read a letter on behalf of Michelle Girdle, who expressed concerns about the already bright
    lights and congestion in the area.

    Randy Mellor stated that he feels that there is no heavy commercial presence in the area, which
    is concerning for the neighborhood.

    Casey Glade noted that the city is not a good neighbor and pointed out issues with current
    zoning in the area. He acknowledged the entitlement to commercial use but believes adding
    heavy commercial would overwhelm the local infrastructure. He recommends keeping it
    commercial.

    Trent Toffler reiterated previous points, expressing concerns that approval could lead to
    negative outcomes.

    Larry Hadfield stated that he does not believe the area meets the requirements for heavy
    commercial. He thinks it should remain as it was originally designated.

    Tyson Hodges expressed concerns about the future implications of heavy commercial use. He
    strongly recommends adhering to the existing planning and zoning regulations and also urged
    the city to improve the area occupied by Strata.

    Mandy Jensen spoke on behalf of Kenny and Sandra Jensen. She noted that their house is
    always dusty from the nearby property. They prefer it remains commercial and urged
    consideration for neighbors if it is changed to heavy commercial.

    The Council discussed concerns with the heavy commercial zoning in this area. There was also
    discussion about a development agreement to specify use and requirements, and address
    concerns. Councilor Newall noted that buyers should be aware of zoning when purchasing land
    and that developers should choose land that aligns with their intended use. Buffering
    requirements were discussed and stated disapproval of heavy commercial zoning.

    The applicant returned to discuss potential fits for the space and expressed a desire to do
    something beneficial for the community, ultimately deciding to withdraw the application.
