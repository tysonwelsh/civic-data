443   "Thus, we conclude that to give meaning to the statutory right of appeal, neighboring landowners
444   must receive actual or constructive notice of the issuance of a building permit in order for the
445   ten-day appeal period to begin."
446   "Once a party has actual or constructive notice that a permit has been issued, the party is
447   chargeable with knowledge of the information contained in the permit and the permit application.
448   Accordingly, if the facts that form the basis for the party's appeal can be ascertained by a review
449   of the permit application, the party is charged with knowledge of those facts once he or she has
450   actual or constructive notice of the permit's issuance. If, on the other hand, the permit
451   application does not contain the facts, and the party is not charged with knowledge of the facts,
452   then the appeal period does not begin until the party receives knowledge of those facts from
453   some other source." (emphasis added).



454   [Appellant] contends that the appeal period should not begin until an affected party receives
455   actual notice of the alleged violation. Under the [Appellant's] proposed rule, the appeal period
456   began when the buildings on the Subject Property reached a height that alerted them to a possible
457   height violation.
458   "These competing interests are not properly weighed under a rule that the appeal period does not
459   begin until there is actual notice of the alleged violation. Such a rule weighs too heavily in favor
460   of the neighboring landowners and gives the permit holder no finality or assurance. For example,
461   assume a developer submits a building permit application. During the municipality's review of
462   the application, a question arises regarding the proper application of a zoning ordinance. The
463   municipality reviews the ordinance and interprets it in a way that allows the structure to be built
464   as proposed in the application. Accordingly, a building permit is issued. The developer, who may
465   not even be aware that a question arose during the permit review process, then expends
466   substantial sums of money constructing the building.
467   As construction is nearing completion, a neighboring landowner notices that the building looks
468   too tall. The neighbor then goes down to city hall, reviews the permit application, and discovers
469   that under an alternative interpretations of the ordinance, the building exceeds the height
470   restriction. Allowing the neighbor to appeal the issuance of the building permit at this point
471   would be unfair because the neighbor had all of the information necessary to mount an appeal—
472   notice of the permit's issuance and the information contained in the permit application—months
473   earlier, before the developer spent a substantial amount of money in reliance on the building
474   permit."



475   "We therefore conclude that the better rule is the rule we adopt in this case—that the appeal
476   period begins when the neighboring landowners receive actual or constructive notice of the
477   permit's issuance. This rule places on the permit holder the responsibility of providing notice of
478   the permit's issuance, whether it be by beginning construction or by some other means. And,
479   once notice is given, the rule places on the neighboring landowners the responsibility of
480   reviewing the available information to determine if they want to appeal the permit's issuance.
      Lehi City Planning Commission                   13                                      May 22, 2025
481   This rule balances the interests of the parties by assuring that the neighboring landowners have
482   notice of the permit and an opportunity to appeal, while giving the permit holder assurance of
483   the permit's finality and reliability."

513          Bret and Tawnya Fox (the "Foxes") sought to appeal Park
514   City's issuance of a building permit to Legacy Development Group,
515   LLC ("Legacy") . The Park City Planning Commission (the "Planning
516   Commission") held that the Foxes' appeal was untimely under the
517   ten—day appeal period contained in the Park City Land Management
518   Code (the "LMC") . The district court upheld the Planning
519   Commission' s determination that the Foxes' appeal was untimely
520   under the LMC and, accordingly, dismissed the Foxes' petition
521   for review .

522          While we affirm the dismissal, we hold that the district
523   court erred in applying the ten—day appeal period of the LMC.
524   Rather, the ten—day appeal period of Utah Code section 10— 9a—
525   704 applies. This appeal period began to run when the Foxes
526   received notice of the permit's issuance. Because the Foxes did
527   not appeal within this ten—day period, the Foxes' appeal was
528   untimely, and we therefore affirm the district court's dismissal
529   of the Foxes' petition.

603                                   STANDARD OF REVIEW

604        8111 When a district court reviews an order of a local land
605   use authority and we exercise appellate review of the district
606   court's judgment, we act as if we were reviewing the land use
607   authority's decision directly, and we afford no deference to the
608   district court's decision. 1 Like the review of the district
609   court, our review is limited to whether a land use authority' s
610   decision is "arbitrary, capricious, or illegal . A land use
611   authority's decision is arbitrary or capricious only if it is
612   not supported by substantial evidence in the record. "3 A land
613   use authority's decision is illegal if it "violates a law,
614   statute, or ordinance in effect at the time the decision was
615   made . "4 Because a determination of illegality is based on the
616   land use authority's interpretation of zoning ordinances, we
617   review such determinations for correctness, but we "also afford
618   some level of non—binding deference to the interpretation
619   advanced by" the land use authority. 5
