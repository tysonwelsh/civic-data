GENERAL SESSION

Chair Snow called the Planning & Zoning Commission meeting to order at 6:03 p.m. He
explained how the meeting would proceed. First, the Planning Department would brief the
Commission; then the applicant would speak to the Commission; the item will be opened to the
public for their comments on public hearing agenda items; the Midvale Deputy City Attorney
will then brief the Commission on their role on the given item; and then the Commission would
deliberate and decide.

   3. Steve Carson requests Conditional Use Permit approval for a fence with razor wire
      located at 631 W Commerce Park Drive in the Clean Industrial (CI) zone.

       The applicant requests conditional use permit approval to allow for a seven-foot fence
       topped with a foot of razor wire located at 631 W Commerce Park Dr in the Clean
       Industrial (CI) zone. The overall height of the fence will be eight feet.

       This proposal has been reviewed by Planning Staff, the City Engineer, and the Unified
       Fire Authority for compliance with the respective guidelines, policies, standards, and
       codes. Staff finds the proposal complies with requirements outlined in Midvale City
       Municipal Code 17-7-1.

       Public notice has been sent to property owners within 500 feet of the subject parcel. No
       written objections have been received as of the writing of this report.

         a. Commissioner training regarding turnover in Land Use decisions.
               i. What NOT to Say (6:10 PM – 6:45 PM)

  Mr. Olsen describes the training to focus on defining arbitrary and capricious. Mr. Wilcox will
  define those terms and their relevance to Planning Commission meetings. Utah law has two
  conditions for overturning Land Use decisions. The first condition is if a commissioner were
  to perform an illegal act. The second condition is when a decision is arbitrary and capricious,
  meaning the commissioner made the decision not based on the findings. The facts presented
  need to support the final decision made. This has not been an issue for Midvale City due to the
  clarity provided by staff and the opportunity to ask questions.

  Mr. Olsen describes different examples when commissioner or council member emotions
  were allowed to affect the comments shared in the past in different cities. This was to aid in
  showing Midvale City Planning Commissioners what not to say and when not to engage in
  sharing opinion.
