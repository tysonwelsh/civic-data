Before and After Traffic Study Provo/Orem Bus Rapid Transit - Jeff Harris, UDOT This is a study partners are MAG, UDOT, WFRC, and UTA and it’s to help the partners to better understand the change in VMT (vehicles miles traveled), air quality, and land use by implementing a high capacity transit system on an urban arterial. It is a technical study to determine the impacts/benefits of the BRT project on travel demand along the corridor. Detailed traffic data will be collected before the project is constructed and several years after the project is operating. This is the same kind of analysis that was done on the TRAX line on Salt Lake’s 400 South. The study begins this year and will run through 2019. The study area goes from Orem 800 North to Provo Center Street and the surrounding roads to understand how the BRT shifts the travel patterns and moves traffic off University Parkway to other roads. The study has 4 phases: 1. Initial Conditions Assessment - Define study area, collect data on initial conditions 2. First Year-of Operations Assessment - Analyze traffic counts, trends, land use changes, and construction effects - Reconcile traffic volumes and transit ridership - Analyze vehicle emissions, parking occupancy, and traffic crashes 3. Second Year-of Operations Assessment 4. Third Year-of Operations Assessment Mountainland MPO Regional Planning May 7, 2015 Page 3 Jeff will continue to update the committee as the study progresses. Adopt the Unified Planning Work Program 2015-2016 - Shawn Seager The Unified Planning Work Program (UPWP) is the annual budget for Regional Planning and illustrates where the planning resources are allocated. It describes the planned work, budget and funding sources that develops a coordinated transportation planning process to accommodate the Governor’s Office of Management and Budget (GOMB) Utah County’s estimate growth to a million people by 2040. The FY-16 $3,041,000 budget is 6% less when compared to last year’s $3,223,000 budget. Due to a decrease in pass-through contracts and special studies. $1,572,000 or 51% of the budget is for 12 full time equivalent MAG MPO staff. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning May 7, 2015 Page 4 American Fork Canyon Vision Project - Shawn Seager The 2014 Legislature appropriated $100,000 to study and create a vision for American Fork Canyon with Snowbird has contributing $50,000. The canyon gets 1.5 million visitors a year and the vision will addresses future recreation, preservation, transportation, and economic development issues. . There needs to be a careful balance between the natural environment, built environment, and social environment. The timeline for this process is January 2015 to January 2016. Utah State University will be conducting a year round public intercept survey in the canyon. A website has been established at www.afcvision.com. The Steering Committee representatives: MAG, Utah County, UDOT, UTA, US Forest Service, National Park Service, Cedar Hills, American Fork, Snowbird, and Save our Canyons. Parking at Timpanogos Cave, transportation in the canyon, economic development, Snowbird Ski area extension into the canyon are a few of the issues that are being studied. We are starting to work with Mountain Accord on similar issues and problems both canyons are experiencing. Any questions, become a member of the Steering Committee, or participate as a stake holder and attend any of the public outreach efforts contact Shawn. Local Option Transportation Sales Tax Straw Poll of Utah County Mayors - Andrew Jackson HB 362 enables the county commission to impose a 0.25% general sales tax for transportation after voter approval with 40% for the cities, 40% for UTA, and 20% for Utah County. Mayor Pengra asked staff to do a straw of the mayors to see if they would support the County Commission imposing a 0.25% the Local Option Transportation Sales tax, and which ballot year it should be on, November 2015 or November 2016. They were also asked if their city planned any bond measures or question on the ballot in either 2015 or 2016. The League of Cities and Towns estimate the county could receive approximately $17 million per year. If the option sales tax is put in place the cities, county, and UTA will receive a direct allocation with no involvement with the MPO. Twenty-four mayors responded to the poll with twenty-two or 92% stating they would support the tax with two or 8% not in favor of the tax, whereas thirteen wanted it issue place on 2015 ballot and eight on the 2016 ballot. This was a discussion will continue into future meetings as to when or if the Local Option Transportation Sales Tax will or should be on a ballot. A decision will need to be made by August in order for the counties to know which year to place this issue on a ballot.
