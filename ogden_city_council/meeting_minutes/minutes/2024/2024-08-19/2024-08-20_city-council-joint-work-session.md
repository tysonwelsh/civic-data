          The purpose of the Joint Work Session was to review the agenda for the City Council and Special Redevelopment
Agency meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.; discuss the Ogden Trails Network Sunset Report; discuss a proposed rezone of
4336 South 1650 West, Airport Commercial (A-C) to Flex Industrial (FLEX); a Council Code of Conduct; a Council member
initiative related to Lester Park and Lorin Farr Pool Upgrades; Nine Rails arts programming in the Adams Community
Reinvestment Area (CRA); Council/Board business; and Council/Board comments.

Agenda Review
         Acting Chair White and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agendas for the City Council and
Special Redevelopment Agency meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Ogden Trails Network Sunset Report
         City advisory committees have sunset provisions in their enabling ordinances which terminate their existence on a certain
date, unless reauthorized by the City Council. The Ogden Trails Network Advisory Committee (OTN) will sunset on June 30,
2024. If extended, the new sunset date for the Committee will be April 30, 2026.
         Michael Goodrick, OTN Chair, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the Committee’s Sunset Report. He
summarized the purpose and mission of the Committee; significant contributions of the Committee to date; discussed trail user
information and the trail counter network; future project planning and development; volunteer efforts and ongoing work; and
recommended future projects and goals for the Committee. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information
packet for the meeting.
         Discussion among the Council and Mr. Goodrick centered on needed ordinance amendments to regulate motorized
vehicles on City trails; grant funding for trail projects; and efforts to address conflicts associated with trail crossings on Mt. Ogden
Golf Course.
         Acting Chair White concluded there is an action item on tonight’s business meeting agenda to appoint members to the
OTN Committee and to extend the Committee’s sunset date.
Proposed Rezone – 4336 South 1650 West, Airport Commercial (A-C) to
Flex Industrial (FLEX)
         Planning Director Brierley explained MTK Holdings, LLC has submitted a petition to rezone 10.23 acres from the
Airport Commercial (A-C) zone to the Flex Industrial (FLEX) zone. The applicant anticipates that the wide range of industrial and
commercial uses of the FLEX zone will attract businesses to the site. Mr. Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
provide aerial photographs of the area encompassed within the zoning application, to provide a comparison of permitted uses in
the Airport Commercial Zone and FLEX Industrial Zone, summarize zone change guidelines, and to summarize the Planning
Commission’s recommendation of approval. The Planning Commission felt the requested zone change is consistent with the
General Plan, including the zone change guidelines in Ogden General Plan 8E1. Their recommendation of approval includes a
condition that the applicant completes a subdivision so the property line matches the zone boundary. For a copy of the
presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Discussion among the Council and Mr. Brierley centered on market patterns for commercial zoning; required parking
accommodations and signage standards in the FLEX zone; and consideration of a conditional overlay zoning designation for the
subject property to mitigate the concerns expressed by adjacent property owners and businesses.
         Council Assistant Executive Director Symes explained that action on this application is scheduled for September 3.

Council Business
          Council Code of Conduct: Council Senior Policy Analyst Burton explained that in January of 2024 the City Council
expressed interest in developing a code of conduct to provide guidance on Council member conduct in various settings. Council
staff have met with an ad hoc committee of Council members to prepare several draft options of a code of conduct, and he
facilitated a review of the draft document for the Council’s benefit. He also discussed the relationship between the Code of
Conduct and Council Rules and Norms that have already been adopted as well as any penalties for violating the Code of Conduct.
          Council staff engaged in discussion with the Council regarding the intent of the Code of Conduct document and its ability
to be effective; they discussed the impact the code may have on City Council members as representatives of the City as well as
whether it is necessary to have a code of conduct in addition to Council Rules and Norms. The Council decided to consider
adoption of a code of conduct within the Rules and Norms document and provided suggested changes to the document before their
next review.
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith indicated action on the matter is tentatively scheduled for September 3.
          Council Member Initiative – Lester Park and Lorin Farr Pool Upgrades: Council member Graf has proposed an
initiative to discuss the use of $10 million of American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds to make improvements to Lester Park and
Lorin Farr Pool.

Adams CRA – Nine Rails Arts Programming
          Economic Development Director Meess explained that City Administration proposes to enter into an eighteen month
“pilot agreement” with Ogden Contemporary Arts (OCA) to manage and expand the arts programming beyond the Dumke Arts
Plaza into the Nine Rails District. Funding for the $108,000 contract is proposed to come from the Adams Community
Reinvestment Area (CRA) budget. The Administration is hoping that the experience with the pilot agreement will inform future
art policy for other areas of the City. Ms. Meess used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the key projects identified
for potential development within the Adams CRA; discuss ongoing arts programming with OCA; and funding and next steps for
proceeding with the pilot program. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Council discussion regarding the proposal centered on topics such as diluting the amount of increment that will be
allocated to the taxing entity partners that supported creation of the CRA; inability of the OCA as a non-profit entity to generate
any increment; whether it is appropriate to use tax increment funding for arts programming/installations; criteria that will be used
to determine the success of the pilot program and whether to continue it past the initial 18-month period; optional ongoing funding
sources if the program is extended beyond the initial pilot program term; and publishing a request for proposals to secure a
program partner rather than awarding the program to OCA as a sole source provider.
          The Council concluded they need additional time to consider the proposal and would like the opportunity for continued
discussion at a future work session meeting before scheduling action on the proposal.

Council Business – Continued
          The Council heard from Mayor Nadolski and Acting Police Chief Sube regarding efforts of the Police Department to
increase traffic enforcement in the coming weeks and months as school instruction resumes at schools throughout the community.
Acting Chief Sube engaged in discussion with the Council regarding opportunities for the public to lodge a complaint with the
Police Department about any traffic concern near a school, and efforts of police officers to educate motorists before taking
enforcement action.
          Mayor Nadolski also addressed recent media reporting on the perception that the Police Department incentivizes officers
to issue traffic citations. Acting Chief Sube pointed out that the current officer evaluation system is designed to not prioritize one
type of enforcement over another, and he stressed that there is not a citation quota, that the evaluation system is based on criteria
that ensures that the community is still getting highly productive, hard-working officers. Acting Chief Sube emphasized that
officers would much rather stop a car and educate the motorist and leave on a positive note than to continually issue citations.
Giving the officers the latitude to make determinations on their own builds confidence in their abilities and leads to effective
interactions with the public.
