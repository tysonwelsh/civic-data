           1. 2   Mayor' s Report


         Mayor Kristie Overson explained that she had attended the Taylorsville High School Baseball
auction along with Council Members Burgess and Knudsen. She described it as a very captivating
event and said she enjoyed supporting as many school events as she could.

 Sandy City' s Business Appreciation Open House was held recently and the Mayor enjoyed
representing Taylorsville. She appreciated that Mayor Zoltanski acknowledged her and expressed
 her gratitude for the relationship the two cities enjoyed.
 The An Evening ofArt event had been held the previous week, with record attendance and world-
 renowned stained glass artist Tom Holdman who explained his artistic process. He also provided
 a piece called Tree of Life which was raffled off
 Taylorsville had again contracted with Y2 Analytics to conduct a survey of city residents to weigh
 their satisfaction of various aspects of city governance. A survey would be mailed out to a certain
 percentage of residents, and the Mayor wanted to make it clear it was legitimate in case anyone
 wondered about that when they received it. The survey findings would then be presented at a future
 city council meeting.

 The Taylorsville Journal had recently included a " casting call" for a yard in the city which was
 ripe for an upgrade to demonstrate waterwise methods of landscape design. This event was
 sponsored by Salt Lake County and Utah Waterways and the intent was to provide a free
 landscaping makeover while educating the community about waterwise landscaping options. More
 information was available on the city website.


  Mayor Overson explained that a new decorative safety wall would be erected on the east side of
  2700 West just north of fire station no. 118. It was hoped that construction would begin in March.
  The 2025 legislative session was well underway. John Hiskey would present a report to the council
  during the March 5th meeting .

            1. 4      Citizen Comments


 Chair Harker invited anyone who wished to address the City Council to step forward and do so.
Cheniel Hill pointed out that she had been advocating for a crosswalk for three months and felt
like she was being ignored. She called for local policies requiring regular compliance checks to
ensure that groups serving the community were not actually abusing it. She indicated she had had
unusual activity in her neighborhood which she continued to report and which she regularly caught
on camera. She indicated that she would happily make the recordings available to law enforcement
and that she would continue to show up and hold the city accountable.
There were no additional citizen comments, and Chair Harker closed the citizen comment period.

       Judge Christopher Bown began his presentation by pointing out the effectiveness of his
clerical staff and giving them a great deal of credit for the great numbers he was about to present.
He praised Clerk of Court Stacy Robbins for her excellent work, despite never having served in
that position previously. He provided the following statistical data for 2024:
           Traffic Cases Filed: 7, 195
           Traffic Cases Disposed: 7, 805
           Traffic Cases Appealed: 9
           Criminal Cases Filed: 1, 018
           Criminal Cased Disposed: 1, 351
           Criminal Cases Appealed: 13
           Small Claims Cases Filed: 414
           Small Claims Cased Settled: 228
           Small Claims Default Judgments: 209
           Small Claims Trials: 3
           Small Claims Cases Appealed: 2

 Judge Bown pointed out that the small claims numbers would be increasing because in January,
 the statute changed, increasing the dollar amount in controversy which justice courts now had
 authority to hear.

 He explained that 92%      of the criminal matters were being disposed of within 180 days, while 93%
 oftraffic matters were disposed ofwithin that time frame. Regarding small claims, the benchmark
 was     270 days, and 100%    of the cases were disposed of within that amount of time.

 Judge Bown commented that TVPD officers who appeared in his court were consistently prepared.
 If there were ever any issues, he had a good enough relationship with Chief Cottam that he could
 make him aware so that it could be addressed. He pointed out that the public defenders and
 prosecutors were also very effective in ensuring that court ran smoothly.
  Council Member Barbieri asked the Judge if he was still conducting many court hearings
  electronically. He responded that he made that option available to many defendants, believing that
  it was an access- to-justice issue, and he preferred that people appear electronically to them not
  appearing at all. However, defendants needed to appear in person for trial or when other witnesses
  were needed to testify.
