2. Common Consent
   a. Reapproval of Preliminary PRUD and Private Subdivision, for Valley View at 554 Simoron.
      (Attachment A)

    Mr. Montgomery stated this item is before the Commission due to the plat not being recorded in the
    time period allowed by Ordinance. He stated minor changes have been made since the last approval
    of the Commission but the number of units and layout of the subdivision has remained as was
    previously approved. The change proposed is in the architectural style of some of the homes, with
    four units being changed to a rambler style detached homes. He stated while the project meets the
    Ordinance requirements, there have been neighborhood concerns relating to the access to Simoron
    and increased traffic. The alignment of the access has been approved by Engineering and is designed
    to align close to the existing Hudson right-of-way. While some residents have suggested the access
    be from 9th Street via the existing Skyline Drive right-of-way, that design is impractical as it would
    create a 2800’ dead-end street, necessitate creating double-fronted residential lots and would have
    more of an impact on the surrounding community. He reviewed the history of Skyline Drive, stating it
    had been dedicated as a potential belt route through the eastern part of the Wasatch Front, but has
    never been developed as such, and much of the right-of-way has since been vacated. He stated the
    Hudson alignment is the least impactful and most convenient for the new neighborhood. The
    developer also has agreed to relocate the existing driveway, and has obtained written permission from
    the homeowner. He also has agreed to monitor the existing retaining wall and mitigate any impacts
    caused by construction. The project is limited to development on property with less than 30% slope
    and a drought-tolerant landscape design is proposed. A public access to the trail system is
    maintained and all work will be compliant with the geotechnical report and BLM requirements. The
    previous approval also suggested bollards might be installed to project the existing power pole,
    subject to being approved by Ogden City Engineering. No new concerns have been created, and Staff
    recommend both preliminary and final subdivision approval as well as PRUD approval subject to the
    same conditions established in July 2020.

    Commissioner Blaisdell asked if the Woods had agreed to the relocation of their driveway. Mr.
    Montgomery responded an agreement had been signed by both the developer and the Woods in
    December 2020 allowing the relocation of the driveway. Ms. Renee Woods, 548 Simoron, stated while
    they had agreed to the relocation of their driveway as proposed, it appears the road now has been
    further to the north and may still have an effect on access to their driveway. She noted both the
    subdivision and PRUD approvals have lapsed and asked if reconsideration as to whether a PRUD is
    appropriate is now warranted. She stated most residents are opposed to the installation of twin
    homes and would prefer a standard subdivision with individual single-family homes.

    Mr. Mark Kashmitter, 524 Simoron, felt the access to this area should be along the existing Skyline
    Drive right-of-way as the hill and curve at the intersection of Simoron and Hudson is unsafe, He felt
    no approval should be given until all concerns have been addressed.

    Mr. Rex Butterfield asked about the initial establishment of Skyline Drive, stating if it was not safe at

and he is committed to monitoring the existing retaining wall, stating it will be viewed by a

   and desires to create new residential units in the existing building as well as to construct a new multi-
   family residential building.

   Ms. Olson stated the petition is to rezone two existing parcels from PI to R-4. She stated while the PI
   Ordinance does allow multi-family so long as it is above professional or business offices. As the owner

6. Request for 12th Street Overlay Exception for:
   a. Development of Regency Villa Townhomes West at 1770 Canyon Road. (Attachment J)
   b. Development of Regency Villa Townhomes East at 1770 Canyon Road. (Attachment K)
   Mr. Stratford indicated these will be discussed as one item but voted upon separately.

  Mr. Chris Haertel stated a request was submitted last year for apartments at this location and due to
  concerns of residents, the concept has now been changed to townhome units.

  Mr. Montgomery stated this is one large parcel of property which is bisected by the Maxfield Drive right-
  of-way, requiring it to be developed as two projects. He reviewed the details of each project indicating it
  is in an area of transition between 12th Street and Ogden Canyon. Some details have yet to be
  completed such as recording the subdivision which eliminates the Canyon Road right-of-way and
  assuring the existing service of the canal can be maintained. He reviewed the site plans and identified
  areas which need to be rectified to satisfy Ordinance requirements, including eliminating the two-unit
  townhomes on the western project and eliminating guest parking on the eastern project. The total
  number of units would then be reduced to 178.

  He reviewed the standards of the 12th Street Overlay Ordinance, indicating while it requires review of the
  Planning Commission when there are more than 30 units, the intent is to assure the design is
  compatible but does not give the Commission authority to reduce the number of units. So long as it
  complies with the regulations of the Ordinance, the Planning Commission has no authority to deny a
  particular project.

  He identified items of concern expressed at the time the project was reviewed last year, including
  overcrowding of schools, increased traffic, safety of the connection of Maxfield to Canyon Road and
  street capacity. He stated Staff had contacted the Ogden School District and had received a response
  indicating the schools are being updated and are able to handle additional students as development
  occurs. The Maxfield Drive connection to Canyon Road was fist planned in 1977, and then reiterated
  when the Horace Mann Community Plan was developed in 2003. The connection was slated to occur
  as adjacent property developed, and now is the time for the road to be extended. He stated the previous
  subdivision approval has expired as the plat was not recorded within the time period allowed by
  Ordinance. He then reviewed each side and presented Staff recommendations:
  West
  This project consists of six 6-unit townhomes, one 7-unit and three 2-unit townhomes. These are
  proposed to be two-story on the front side and three story on the downhill side. He stated the proposed
  two-unit buildings are within the required setback area and recommended they be removed., The
  overlay zone also limits the height of buildings to 38’ to the roof peak, with a minimum of 6:12 roof pitch
  as well as requiring an 18” eave overhang. In addition, screening of vehicle lights is required, with Staff
  recommending a wall be installed at the west end of the driveways facing west. Staff recommendation

                                                                                                   Page | 8
  is for approval, subject to the following conditions:
    1. The applicant receive approval to vacate the portion of (old) Canyon Road that runs through the
         property at the south end of the site.
    2. The applicant complete the subdivision plat process through Ogden City to address any geologic
         issues and develop Maxfield Drive, and utility easements prior to permits being issued for the
         project.
    3. To meet building orientation requirements the three (3) buildings proposed on the west side of the
         site be removed and landscaped (going from the proposed 49-units to a recommended 43-units).
    4. Street trees be installed along Canyon Road and Maxfield Drive with type, quantities, and locations
         being approved by the Ogden City Urban Forester.
    5. All exterior lighting on the site be directed downward and shielded from the adjacent properties –
         the applicant providing a lighting plan for Planning Staff approval that shows 12 th Corridor Overlay
         Zone lighting requirements will be met.
    6. The eaves on all the buildings meet the required eighteen-inch (18”) extension from the building
         walls.
    7. The roof pitches in the development meet the minimum 6/12 pitch requirement.
    8. All the buildings meet the 38’ maximum height requirement.
    9. All mechanical equipment and utilities be screened from public view as required by ordinance.
    10. A 6’ high masonry rock-textured fence having earth-toned colors be installed on the west side and
         rear property lines to screen the adjacent properties from the vehicle traffic on the site.
    11. All department staff comments be obtained and satisfied.

East
Project consists of eleven 7-unit townhomes as well as three 6-unit, three 5-unit, four 4-unit and one 3-unit
townhome. The layout also shows accessory parking which extends in front of entry doors and should be
removed. The use of the trail is maintained near existing overhead power lines, All parking needs to be
screened and the same concerns with building height, roof pitch and eave overhang are identified as
noncompliant with the regulations of the Ordinance. Grading needs to be compliant with the regulations of
the geotechnical study which also could require installation of a retaining wall to control movement on the
hillside. Staff recommendation is for approval, subject to the following conditions:
    1. The applicant receive approval to vacate the portion of (old) Canyon Road that runs
       through the property at the south end of the site.
    2. The applicant complete the subdivision plat process through Ogden City to address any
       geologic issues and develop Maxfield Drive, utility easements, and public access
       easement to the trail connection at the northeast end of the current property prior to
