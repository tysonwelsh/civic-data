2. APPROVAL OF WEBER COUNTY POLICY- EXPRESSIVE ACTIVITY RESTRICTIONS AND
THE FINAL READING OF AN ORDINANCE 2021-26 OF THE COUNTY COMMISSIONERS OF
WEBER COUNTY AUTHORIZING RESTRICTIONS ON EXPRESSIVE ACTIVITY.

Chris Crockett (County Attorney) reviewed first paragraph of the First Amendment and the model that it is for
others around the world. Public Forum Doctrine as defined by the Supreme Court, including Public Forums,
Limited Public Forums (public land opened for expressive activity), and Non-Public Forums (public property
not accessible for expressive activity). Today’s goal is to codify what has long existed in the law already.
Weber County Policy defines Public, Limited Public, and Non-Public areas.

Public Comment (3 minutes each):

1. Rebecca Champion: If law was already in effect, why the need to codify it?

a. Mr. Crockett: Utah Supreme Court (11.61.104) requires Time, Place, Manner restriction to be
enacted by ordinance or adopt an ordinance to guide the adoption by policy or practice with
constitutional safeguards outlined.

2. Victoria Dortsbach, Ogden, UT: Woke up a couple of years ago to rights being trampled. Rights come
from God. Commissioners took Oaths of Office meaning citizens can hold you accountable. Title 42
section 1983 lawsuits available if one so chooses. Citizens can address Commission in any item of
business. Fraud of government is biggest problem of all. Attorneys turn peoples’ rights over; I would not
listen to attorneys but I would listen to ‘we the people’.

a. Mr. Crockett (response to citizens’ ability to address Commission on any topic): with Time, Place,
Manner restrictions, reasonable means must remain for public to address the Commission — public


hearings for example, related to proposed tax increase; lawsuits for redress of government; protest
on public sidewalks, etc.
3. Jennifer Pennington, South Ogden, UT: Chapter 188 of state law referenced by Mr. Crockett has nothing
compulsory in the language which means county is enacting corresponding county legislation voluntarily.
I find that to be completely unethical. County is taking this course to silence us because you do not agree
with our messaging.
a. Mr. Crockett: Unsure of meaning behind ‘ex post facto’ comment. As for adopting this law,
Weber County procedure being followed to adopt ordinance.

Commissioner Jenkins: Please explain Constitutionality of direction being taken.

Mr. Crockett: Provided background of both law and approach taken internally to ensure intent of Constitution
was protected, including using prior Supreme Court precedence and the level of internal scrutiny in the Weber
County Attorney’s office over several weeks.

Commissioner Froerer: Mr. Crockett’s report clarifies we are operating within bounds of the Constitution.

Commissioner Jenkins moved to approve the final reading of Ordinance 2021-26 of the County Commissioners
of Weber County authorizing restrictions on expressive activity; Commissioner Froerer seconded.
Roll Call Vote: Commissioner Froerer — aye; Commissioner Jenkins — aye; Chair Harvey — aye

Commissioner Froerer moved to approve Weber County Policy on Expressive Activity Restrictions;
Commissioner Jenkins seconded.
Commissioner Froerer — aye; Commissioner Jenkins — aye; Chair Harvey — aye
