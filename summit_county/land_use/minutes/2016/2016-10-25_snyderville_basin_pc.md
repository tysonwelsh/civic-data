Work Session
Continued discussion of the Canyons Master Plan Amendments- Tiffanie Northrup-Robinson, Senior Planner
Senior Planner Tiffanie Northrup-Robinson said that both the applicant and Staff will work to break this down so that the Planning Commission can have a better understanding and to get the information that they need.  She asked the Commission what information they would like to enable them to move forward to the next step.  
Commissioner DeFord said he doesn’t have a searchable copy of the Development Agreement.  Planner Northrup-Robinson said she has created her own searchable copy of the text, but not the exhibits.  This could be sent to the Commission.  
Planner Northrup-Robinson said the Canyons SPA allows density to be moved between commonly owned parcels.  That is essentially what is being proposed.  The density on some parcels that can’t handle the current allocation is being shifted to a different site.  
Commissioner DeFord said it has been mentioned that a review of the Canyons Master Plan will take place for the Commission’s benefit.  Attorney Brackin said it is her understanding this is already planned.  Hopefully, this will make more sense now that they have visited the site.  Planner Northrup-Robinson responded there are no uses that will change.  If a project is designated as multi-family housing, it will stay multi-family housing.  Commercial will stay as commercial.  
Commissioner DeFord said he hopes to gain a better understanding of the kinds of changes that will take place.  Based on what has come before them, there are some things in the Canyons SPA that the Commission would like to have changed.  He asked how the changes will tie in with the other things that are coming down the pipeline, such as the affordable housing component.  How does it tie in with the connectivity of the Canyons? 
Attorney Brackin said that Staff wants to give the Commission a “before” picture.  They want the Commission to know what is required, what was anticipated, what the concept was, and how that worked.  Then the Commission can compare the before and after.    
Commissioner DeFord said he thinks a missing component is the Canyons transportation plan.  Will that be connected to the County and Park City?  How will this tie together?  The Canyons should not be treated as an island, but a community member.  That is what they need to understand as they move into the “next-step” phase.  
Planner Northrup-Robinson said these are good comments and questions that will help Staff know what they need in order to move forward.  Commissioner DeFord said they 
need to have an understanding of “who’s who” at the Canyons.  They need to know if the entity they are talking about is the County, Vail, Replay, or any of the other players.    
Commissioner Collins said that Commissioner DeFord covered her concerns.  
Commissioner Lawson said they need some sort of matrix by parcel that shows what was approved and whether it was in square footage, units, or some other kind of quantification.  This will allow the Commission to see if they are staying within the original approval.  
Planner Northrup-Robinson said the current land use talks about height, use, and density.  The height is currently determined by stories.  It is difficult to determine what that really means.  This was established to protect view sheds.  In place of this, they may want to establish the maximum elevation, but that decision will be further down the line.  She said Staff will provide the Commission with what is approved on the land use chart, what is proposed, and where those changes will happen.    
Planner Northrup-Robinson said there is an obligation for an updated transportation plan to be provided.  Attorney Brackin said they have been working hard to get an updated plan.  It will be brought to the Commission.  
Commissioner Klingenstein said they don’t know where they are going unless they know where they have been.  He showed multiple binders that created a large stack.  These were all binders with information on the creation of the Canyons SPA.  
Commissioner Klingenstein said he thinks they have got the “cart before the horse.”  It is time to slow this train down.  It is fine with him if this takes all of 2017.  He doesn’t like it when development gets out in front of public policy.  
Commissioner Klingenstein said they didn’t have enough time for today’s field trip.  He is going to suggest at least one more field trip.  He suggested each Commissioner get on a bike or take a walk to actually see how it functions.  He feels strongly about this because they went through this same thing on November 15, 1999.  
Commissioner Klingenstein said after they have the history lesson and people start understanding, then they need a meeting to talk about what has and hasn’t worked.  Often, they hear from an applicant that the RVMA is responsible for a certain thing pertaining to application.  He said that should not happen anymore.  The RVMA should be nailed down on what is its role, what it does, and when it does it.
They need to know when the transportation master plan will be completed.  Workforce housing is still unknown.  Planner Northrup-Robinson responded they have done what they are obligated to do.  Attorney Brackin added they are meeting the benchmark.

Commissioner Klingenstein said a 3-D drive-through model at ground level would be helpful.  This will help them understand what it actually looks and feels like.  Are the global principals still germane today?  Do they need to be updated?  
Commissioner Klingenstein said he doesn’t want this to be development driven, but Staff driven.  Up to now, it has been development driven.  The Commission needs to be part of the team.

different than anticipated.  If the Commission wants to have a white board  session at the same meeting, they are talking about a 2-3 hour session.  She asked if they would like to have that at a special or a regular meeting.

Public hearing and possible action regarding a Plat Amendment to amend Lot 7 of the Cove at Sun Peak Subdivision Plat; 5420 Cove Hollow Lane; CSP-7; Jared Rakisits, applicant- Sean Lewis, County Planner 
Planner Sean Lewis said this application came before them in June for a plat amendment.  The applicant proposed to amend the building envelope and move the building site up the mountain to accommodate the driveway.  At that time, the Planning Commission requested the applicant come back with some changes.  
Since that time, the applicant has visited with Staff with a proposed change, which involved the house being located on a 30% slope.  This is not allowed by the 
Development Code.  A slide was shown of the most recent change proposal.  The building pad gets squared off.  The original and the proposed building pad were shown.    
Planner Lewis said Staff feels that the integrity of the intent of the plat is maintained.  The applicant has asked to remove the easement that goes to Olympic Park.  The recorded easement has already been removed.  This will allow a little more freedom on the northwest of the property to move the building site.  
Planner Lewis said there are two issues at play.  One is if the Commission wants to have the applicant stay within the original building pad or if they want to have a driveway that conforms to standards.  With the current proposal, the driveway conforms to standards.  It requires more cuts and fills than what was originally proposed.  To have a driveway that reduces the cuts and fills requires moving the building pad up the mountain and into the 30% slopes.  
Planner Lewis said that this proposal is the best of both worlds.  It balances the cuts and fills to a level that conforms to County standards, but maintains the integrity of the original building envelope.  The location of the home on the lot is not dramatically changed.  The applicant said he had nothing to add at this time.  
Commission Comments and Questions 
Commissioner Collins asked Planner Lewis how much is the reduction of the cut and fill.  Planner Lewis clarified that this proposal has more cuts and fills than what was proposed in June, but it is maintaining the integrity of the site in the original building envelope while keeping the house out of the 30% slope.  
Planner Lewis said that Staff is of the opinion that the good cause is maintaining the integrity of the site.  The house is being maintained in the original intent area.  More 
cuts and fills are being allowed to have an approved driveway.  If the Commission would like to see less cuts and fills, they will have to move the house into steeper slopes. 
Commissioner Lawson said he goes along with Staff’s recommendation.  He thinks it is a good trade off.  
Commissioner Klingenstein asked Mr. Rakisits, the builder, to explain why the new proposal is better. A slide was shown that demonstrated what was first submitted.  The Engineering Department didn’t like it because of the tall retaining walls.  It created an eyesore from Highway 224.  They felt the better option was to place the house up the hill.  The current proposal has shorter retaining walls.  The fill can be reseeded with natural vegetation.  
Planner Lewis said the Engineering Department has said while the original proposal has a compliant driveway, the second proposal is a better engineered design.  Commissioner Klingenstein said that it seems there is less impact on the overall site with the second proposal.  Planner Lewis agreed.  
Commissioner Lawson asked what the cut is behind the house.  Mr. Rakisits said it is less than 10 feet.  Commissioner Lawson asked if this has been dealt with by having two tiered walls.  Mr. Rakisits said that is correct.   A discussion ensued on the topo lines shown on the map. increase of 10,000 square feet of the earlier proposal.  He added Staff is okay with a modest increase because this keeps the house lower on the site and has a driveway that meets Code.  Commissioner DeFord commented the building pad would have probably extended into this area if the easement hadn’t been there when the lot was created.   
Commissioner DeFord said his next question is directed to Attorney Brackin.  He asked if they can consider a requirement to have a heated driveway.  Attorney Brackin said they can consider things that are within the Code.  As this is not found in the Code, it wouldn’t be appropriate.  
Commissioner DeFord said this seems like a square-hole, round-peg problem.  They are trying to put a house on a lot that is not very accommodating.  Changes are needed to the driveway and the cut and fill so the applicant can make this fit on the lot. Commissioner Klingenstein said he thinks this comes down to finding good cause.  As he understands it, this proposal integrates into the site better, is less impactful to the overall site, and they don’t end up with a wall of rocks going up the entire slope.  Although they may be able to design a different house, for him, Staff has done a good job of showing good cause.  
Commissioner DeFord said to him, the good cause is that the building pad would have probably been in this spot if the easement had not been established.  Commissioner Collins asked if this will become the biggest building pad in the entire subdivision.  She is concerned about how this will impact future development.  
Planner Lewis said he doesn’t think they will get any bigger house than what would have been there because of the limitation to get the driveway up the steep mountain.  The applicant has worked with the Engineer’s Office to find a way that does the best to give them a safe driveway.  It does have cuts and fill, it does have retaining walls, but it could be a lot worse and the Engineer would not be as happy with it.  Alternatively, it could move up the mountain and the house would be built on 30% slopes.  To him, they have come up with the best solution.  
The public hearing was opened.  There were no comments made and the public hearing was closed.

Findings of Fact:
Kim and William Wade are the listed fee title owner of Parcel CSP-7.  
Parcel CSP-7 is 10.56 acres in size.
Parcel CSP-7 is located at 5420 Cove Hollow Lane.
Parcel CSP-7is located in the Hillside Stewardship Zoning District.
The Cove at Sun Peak Subdivision plat was originally platted in 1996.
The Cove at Sun Peak Subdivision has twice been amended to adjust building pads on lots within the subdivision. 
The applicant is requesting the amendment to modify the approved building envelope to allow for construction of a driveway that complies with Summit County ordinances, and to remove a previously abandoned access easement to the Utah Olympic Ski Park complex.  
Conclusions of Law:
There is good cause for the amendment.  Evidence has been submitted that shows the existing building pad will require extensive cuts, fills, and retaining walls to install a driveway that complies with County Ordinances.  By amending the building pad, the amount of cuts, fills, and retaining walls are significantly reduced.

Discussion regarding the Discovery CORE Final Plat– Amir Caus, County Planner 
Planner Amir Caus said the last time this was heard, one of the key items was the project layout itself and integration of the unit types.  Since that meeting, the applicant has provided two additional layouts that integrate the different unit types.  He said he would let the applicant explain the different options.

Commissioner Lawson asked about Option #3, which has the elimination of the hammerheads.  Does this design meet Code?  Wouldn’t it be a safety issue to not allow fire vehicles to be able to turn around?  Engineer Horton said the 20-foot alleyways provide the ability for emergency vehicles to turn around.  Snow plows would be the same.  They can pull in and out or drive through the alley.   Commissioner DeFord asked questions about where the snow would be stored.      
Commissioner Collins referred to Section 10-4-9-C.  This section limits on-street parking.  She said that people who park in these parking lots will have to back into the street. The applicants were given time to comment.  Andy Kitchen said at the last couple meetings, there have been several questions about the CORE designation.  The main concern seemed to be centered on integration.  They have come up with three options.  A slide was shown of each option.  The first was one that was shown at the last meeting.  He invited Mr. Loomis to discuss this option.
Mr. Loomis said he recently attended a conference that discussed affordable housing integration.  It was stated that integration, as being interpreted here, does not have the desired effect.  Most jurisdictions don’t require it.  
Mr. Loomis added that almost every housing development in Summit County has not required integration of the affordable units.  He listed several developments where this has not been the case.  Bear Hollow is the only exception he can think of.  Most people who live in affordable housing are grateful to have a house in the County and not have to commute.  They go to the same schools and grocery stores.    
Mr. Loomis said he has reviewed Chapter 5, the County Council findings, and the minutes of their approval.  He found nothing that requires integration.  He read from the preamble of the CORE about the provision of workforce housing.  
Mr. Loomis said it is their preference that the Commission adopts option 1.  If they look at the requirements of the Code, as well as the findings found in the minutes, they will see this is a choice they can make.  
Mr. Loomis said there has been some reference to the map that was approved with the integrated units.  At the last County Council meeting, the number of units was reduced, which allowed a different configuration.  Vice-Chair Harte responded that at the last Commission meeting, there was consensus that integration was required.  Vice-Chair Harte said he would like to begin by allowing the Commission to ask questions on option 1 and the points made by Mr. Loomis.

information that the Council predicated their decision on.  That is why he believes there should be integration of the project.  
Commissioner DeFord commended Mr. Loomis and what Mountain Lands does for the community; however, he thinks Mr. Loomis is wrong on this particular project.  He believes the intent of the CORE calls for integration.  It is not just integration of housing types, but also what they are for.    
Commissioner Collins said that from the beginning she has pushed for integration.  She believes the original CORE zoning stresses integration.  Her interpretation of integration is a variety of housing types, a variety of options, and a variety of designs.
Commissioner Lawson said in his 50+ years that he has been involved in housing projects it isn’t very often that he sees multi-family units mixed with single family homes.  He asked the developers how the HOA would be set up.  Would the multifamily have an HOA, but the single family would not?  With integration, he can see some practical problems on how the property management would maintain the multi-family but not the single family.  This would get complicated and more costly.

Tom Romney said that option 2 is their first attempt in integrating the project.  He thinks that option 3 gets exciting.  They have added duplexes, which add another architectural feel.
