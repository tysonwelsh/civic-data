4.3. Training on Open and Public Meetings Act and Procedures. (Staff presenter, Fred
Donaldson) [LBA and RDA Open and Public Meetings Act training held in

Mayor Hall reported that the LBA and RDA Trainings will be held in conjunction.
City Attorney, Fred Donaldson, reported that the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act provides that
meetings and deliberations of public bodies are to be conducted openly. Meetings must be open to
the public unless properly closed and they can only be closed for specific reasons. The law favors
openness, and exceptions to holding open meetings will be strictly construed.

A “meeting” is defined as:

e A gathering of a public body.

e With a quorum present.

e That is convened by the person with authority to convene the public body following the

process provided by law.
e For the purpose of acting as a public body to receive public comment on a relevant matter,
deliberate about a relevant matter, or take action on a relevant matter.

e A meeting can be in person, electronic, or hybrid.
Mr. Donaldson indicated that in the past, gatherings of more than three Council members could
potentially invoke the requirements of the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act. That had been
clarified as stated above. In response to a question raised by Mayor Hall, he confirmed that a ribbon
cutting or other gathering is not a meeting even if a quorum is present.
A “public body” is an administrative, advisory, executive, or legislative body that:

e Was created by the Utah Constitution, statute, rule, ordinance, or resolution;

e Consists of two or more persons;

e Spends, distributes, or is supported by tax money; and

e Is vested with the authority to make decisions about the public’s business.
The following are public bodies:

e City Council;

e Planning Commission;

e City Redevelopment Agency;

e Local Building Authority;

e Historic Preservation Commission;

e Arts Advisory Board; and

e Healthy Bluffdale Coalition.

Mr. Donaldson remarked that City Recorder, Tami Timothy, ensures that the City meets all noticing
requirements outlined in the Meeting Packet. With the exception of certain public hearing items that
require specific notice, only 24 hours’ notice is required for public meetings. Written minutes must
be kept for all meetings, and approved minutes are the official public record of a meeting.
Open meetings are recorded, and the complete, unedited recording is public record. Members of the
public can also record meetings. Bluffdale public meetings are livestreamed on the City website.
All public bodies can conduct electronic meetings, but an anchor location is required so that members
of the public can participate in the meeting. That requirement can be suspended for certain public
health emergencies. Electronic meetings must follow the same procedures as regular meetings.
Mr. Donaldson reported that the legislature recently added the following language regarding
predetermining public body action to the Code:
Individuals constituting a quorum of a public body may not act together outside a meeting in
a concerted, deliberate way to predetermine an action to be taken by the public body at a
meeting on a relevant matter.
The language does not mean that Council members cannot discuss matters on a one-on-one basis, but
they should only deliberate during a public meeting. However, members of a public body should not
transmit electronic messages to each other during a convened meeting.
Meetings are open to the public unless closed for a specific reason, and no local government meetings
_ are legally required to be closed.
If a meeting is closed:
e A quorum of the public body must be present.
e Two-thirds of members present must vote to approve. The Mayor does not vote.
e Must first hold an open public meeting with proper notice before closing a meeting.

Mr. Donaldson reviewed meeting procedures. Bluffdale City Code § 1.60.020(1) states that:

Per the Bluffdale Policies and Procedures Manual, Chapter 7.20: Parliamentary Procedures:

e A “quorum” is defined as a simple majority of the membership of a public body.

e Councils follow a fixed pattern in conducting their meetings. The order is arranged for
convenience and enables members to know in advance when each type of business will be
considered. The order of business is at the Mayor’s discretion

e A written agenda is prepared. If any member desires to add an item, written consent must be
obtained from two members or the Mayor. Upon receiving a proper request, the City Manager
will add or delete agenda items as requested. Mr. Donaldson indicated that Bluffdale City
Code §1.60.020.E needs to be amended to correct a slight inconsistency on this item.

e Meetings are chaired by the Mayor or Mayor Pro Tempore. Once a member is recognized by
the Chair for the purpose of a statement, that person must be protected in their right to speak.
Debate is restricted to the measure under consideration, and motives should not be discussed.

e Council actions are done by motion, which is a formal statement of a proposal for the
governing body to consider and vote upon. Council members are encouraged to familiarize
themselves with the steps of phrasing and handling a motion. The steps for a motion are as
follows:

o Addressing the Chair.

o Recognition by the Chair.

o Proposing a motion. Proposals should be stated in the positive and in the form “I move
to approve...” even if a majority of the Council will vote against the motion.

o Seconding a motion. The Chair can call for a second if one is not offered. A second
does not indicate that the person seconding the motion supports or will vote in favor
of it. If no second is offered, the motion dies.

© Statement of motion by the Chair. Ifthe Chair restates the motion and the person who
proposed the motion disagrees, they can ask the Chair to restate the motion again. The
Chair should ask if the restated motion correctly expresses the proposal. If the request
is made for the purpose of delay or deemed to be unsuitable, the Chair can rule the
motion out of order.

© Voting ona motion. Prior to voting, the Chair may inquire if there is further discussion
or if the matter is ready for a vote.

o Amending a motion. Amendments should be to the substance of the motion rather
than how it was stated. A second and discussion of the amendment is required.

o Discussion is allowed after a motion is made.

In response to a question from Council Member Lord, Mr. Donaldson clarified that “I move that we
consider the approval of...” would be an acceptable form. Mayor Hall remarked that the intention is
to move the item to a vote, and proposing a motion does not indicate support.

Mr. Donaldson reported that Chapter 7.20 (G) provides detailed instructions on types of motions and
rules of precedence, and Chapter 7.20(H) includes helpful definitions. A copy of Robert's Rules of

Mr. Donaldson reported that a mudslide occurred in October 2025 on 14600 South near the railroad
crossing. The mudslide was caused by water seeping into the hillside. The Jordan Valley Water
Conservancy District was asked to reimburse the City’s costs associated with the cleanup. They

about a recurrence.

agreement will reimburse all costs the City incurred.

3. Staff Reports, Additional Council Discussion, and Calendaring Items.
City Manager, Bruce Kartchner, reported on the following:

e A construction crew damaged a water line on 14600 South between Redwood Road and the
train trestle. This normally would not be an issue. However, one valve was turned off due to
construction underneath the trestle, and the other valve had to be temporarily closed to perform
the repair. The work was completed within a few hours, and the water was not contaminated.
The contractor who damaged the line will be billed for the repair.

In response to a question from Council Member Lord, Mr. Kartchner stated that updates were
posted on the City website and social media during the outage. If someone were to file a
Government Records Access and Management Act (“GRAMA”) request about the incident,
the City would provide any records on the incident. Any violations are addressed, but that
information is not published.

Council Member Smith asked how costs are calculated in these circumstances. Mr. Kartchner
reported that actual costs for materials and labor are billed. If outside contractors are hired to
perform repairs, their charges are also passed through to the responsible party.

e The City Offices will be closed on Monday, January 19th in observance of Civil Rights Day.
Mayor Hall reported that all City Council Members are welcome to attend the Utah League of Cities
and Towns ("ULCT") Legislative Policy Committee (“LPC”) meetings. Members who wish to have
lunch must RSVP. Voting members for 2025 are Mayor Hall, Council Member Aston, and
Mr. Kartchner.

Legislative Youth Day on the Hill is Wednesday, January 21. The first LPC meeting will be on

January 26. Meetings will be held every Monday at 12:00 p.m. during the Legislative Session with

the exception of Presidents’ Day, February 16. That week’s meeting will be held on February 17.
