Proposed Resolution 2026-2 adopting the budget for the Redevelopment
Agency for Fiscal Year 2026-2027
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2026-
2027 budget came before the Board for consideration. The memo stated each year that the Mayor, serving as Executive Director
of the Redevelopment Agency, presents the Redevelopment Agency budget at a meeting in May as required by state law. State
law also requires that the annual budget be adopted no later than June 30. The Agency is staffed and managed by the Community
and Economic Development Department. The Redevelopment Agency Board is comprised of all members of the Ogden City
Council as Board members.
          Community and Economic Development Assistant Executive Director Sawyer summarized the memo and presented the
tentative Redevelopment Agency budget for Fiscal Year 2026–2027. He noted that a comprehensive annual report had been
presented approximately three months prior, and that tonight's presentation was intended as a high-level introduction only. A
detailed line-item budget will be published on the City's budget website the following day. A work session was scheduled for May
26 for deeper review, followed by a public hearing on June 2, 2026. Mr. Sawyer touched on the following:
               • Revenue: Total projected tax increment revenue is expected to increase from approximately $6.6 million to
                   $9.2 million in FY 2026–2027. The most significant driver of this increase is the Airport Community
                   Reinvestment Area (CRA), which is projected to grow from $300,000 to $2.5 million, largely attributable to the
                   recently approved Williams expansion project.
               • Transfers In and Out: Mr. Sawyer identified several significant fund transfers flowing through the
                   Redevelopment Agency budget, including funds for the Union Station land purchase, the St. Anne’s facility
                   loan payment, the WonderBlock bond payment, and the Quality Neighborhoods Housing Fund. On the
                   expenditure side, state statute requires a percentage of increment from each CRA district be committed to
                   housing. The FY 2026–2027 budget projects $1.5 million in additions to the Redevelopment Agency housing
                   fund. A portion of increment is also transferred to the general fund to offset staff costs for managing
                   Redevelopment Agency activities.
               • Debt Retirement: Mr. Sawyer noted that the 2016 Series A and Series B bonds, as well as a note associated with
                   the CBDD District (Solomon Center), are being paid off in full during this budget year.
               • Upcoming CRA Areas: Mr. Sawyer previewed a pipeline of potential future project areas, including an
                   expansion of the Airport CRA to capture approximately 15 unincorporated acres north of the airport, the Union
                   Station Housing and Transit Reinvestment Zone (HTRZ) application currently being prepared for state
                   submission, the Capital Square CRA, and longer-term considerations including a 24th Street/I-15 Interchange
                   CRA, an East Washington CRA, a Five Points CRA, and a South Wall CRA.
          Economic Development Director Gibson added remarks regarding the Redevelopment Agency Performance Dashboard,
available at www.Ogdencity.com/rda. Mr. Gibson noted ongoing improvements to the tool to increase transparency, including
plans to incorporate budget data and development agreements.
          Board member Washington asked why Capital Square did not appear on the performance dashboard. Mr. Gibson clarified
that the dashboard only reflects existing, approved CRA areas, and that Capital Square would appear once formally designated by
the Board.
          Board member Myers asked how the housing fund allocation was expected to be used going forward. Mr. Sawyer
explained that funds to date have largely been used to prepare sites for future housing, and that new Redevelopment Agency
housing fund allocation guidelines were being developed and would be brought before the Board in the coming months.
          Chair Hyer raised the issue of the Five Points area, expressing urgency that it is not overlooked and citing past missed
opportunities with a developer who had sought TIF funding for commercial development at the northeast corner of Washington
Boulevard and Second Street. Mr. Sawyer acknowledged the concern and indicated that staff would work with the Redevelopment
Agency Board to better define the scope of a potential Five Points CRA, suggesting a possible field trip or work session with
visual aids to gather Board guidance on the geographic boundaries and investment priorities for that area.
          Vice Chair Graf then introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2026-2, entitled:
                   “A resolution of the Board of Directors of the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency adopting the annual budget
                   for Fiscal Year 2026-2027 as required by Section 17C-1-601.5, Utah Code Annotated, 1953.”
          BOARD MEMBER MYERS MOVED A PUBLIC HEARING ON THE PROPOSAL BE HELD IN THE CITY
COUNCIL CHAMBERS ON JUNE 2, 2026, AT 6:00 P.M. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY BOARD MEMBER LOPEZ,
ALL VOTING AYE. Board member Richey was not present when this vote was taken.

Proposed Resolution 2026-3 authorizing the establishment of a survey area
comprised of a portion of parcel 06-106-0032 located on the Weber State
University campus bounded by the following streets – Playfield Way, West
Campus Drive, Village Drive, and the OGX bus line lane
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed Wildcat Square
Community Reinvestment Project Survey Area came before the Board for consideration. The memo stated the proposed area
consists of a portion of one parcel (06-106-0032) on the Weber State University campus. The property is currently used as a
practice field and is located on the southwest portion of the main campus between West Campus Drive, Village Drive, and the
OGX bus line. The proposal is to study the feasibility of creating a CRA to provide financial assistance to a development partner
for the construction of a new 103-unit student housing project.
          Economic Development Director Gibson summarized the memo and stated this action represents only the first step in a
much longer process—it would authorize city staff to conduct due diligence to determine whether the creation of a new CRA in
this area is feasible and beneficial to Ogden City. He provided background information regarding the proposal, noting in October
2025, Weber State University completed an RFP process and selected Kelly Development Group to develop a new student
housing project on approximately 6.625 acres of campus land. The proposed development is a four-story building providing 513
beds, primarily intended for out-of-state and international students, under an approximately 100-year lease. The developer
approached Ogden City in January 2026 to explore Redevelopment Agency support, as the site carries significant costs associated
with decades of fill material placed by the university and potential infrastructure needs yet to be fully quantified. Mr. Gibson
highlighted the following key elements of the project:
                • Taxability: Because the property is state-owned, it is not currently taxable. However, Mr. Gibson explained that
                    when state land is used for a private, for-profit business purpose, a "privilege tax" mechanism renders it taxable.
                    The city would be able to claim administrative and housing set-aside portions during the CRA period, with full
                    privilege tax collections available to all taxing entities once the CRA expires.
                • Projected Benefits: Mr. Gibson outlined several anticipated community benefits, including a new long-term tax
                    revenue stream, improved stability in the broader rental market by housing students in dedicated on-campus
                    units, increased ridership on the OGX Bus Rapid Transit line (with a BRT stop located adjacent to the site), and
                    support for Weber State University's strategic enrollment goals.
          Mr. Gibson concluded that if the survey area resolution were adopted, staff would proceed to develop a full project area
budget in coordination with Weber State and the developer, laying out development costs, the funding gap, TIF contribution
levels, and financial projections. That analysis would then return to the Board, along with public hearing processes at the City,
County, and School District levels, before any final decision on CRA creation.
          Board member Lundell inquired about how the Redevelopment Agency's involvement was critical to the project's
advancement. Mr. Gibson explained that, at this phase, only tentative reviews had been conducted to understand the developer's
financials, and the complete development costs and funding gap remained to be fully determined. Consequently, whether
Redevelopment Agency support was indispensable for the project had yet to be conclusively established through the formal
budget process.
          Vice Chair Graf introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2016-3, entitled:
                    “A resolution of the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency authorizing the establishment of a survey area
                    comprised of a portion of parcel 06-106-0032 located on the Weber State University Campus bounded by the
                    following streets: Playfield Way, West Campus Drive, Village Drive, and the OGC Bus Line Lane.”
          Vice Chair Graf allowed for public input regarding the proposal.
     Travis Pate, Linda Sanchez, and Laura Lewis raised concerns and opposition; Mr. Pate expressed skepticism about the City
absorbing the cost of preparing a site he characterized as a fill dump, arguing that hard numbers and site drilling should precede
any City commitment. Ms. Sanchez emphasized the importance of self-reliance and financial prudence, drawing from her own
experiences. She suggested involving students in preparatory work as a cost-saving measure and questioned potential personal
benefits from the project, particularly for financially struggling residents. Both speakers expressed concern that this project would
reinforce a campus enclave rather than directing student populations and investment downtown, which they felt was inconsistent
with the City's Transit-Oriented Development and college-town goals. Ms. Lewis stated that Ogden City had become "everybody's
piggy bank" for projects that could not independently pencil out financially.
          Board member Myers recognized Mark Halverson, Vice President of Weber State University, and asked for his input.
Halverson stated that on-campus housing development has been part of Weber State's master plan for 12 to 15 years and is
essential to the university's ability to attract traditional, out-of-state, and international students. He confirmed that existing campus
housing is at capacity with long waitlists, and that the demographic environment for higher education is increasingly competitive.
He noted that the privilege tax arrangement would generate revenue for the city, county, and school district that currently does not
exist, and acknowledged that rising construction and infrastructure costs require creative financing partnerships. Mr. Halverson
also confirmed that green space displaced by the development would be relocated to other areas of campus and had always been
intended as a placeholder for housing.
          Vice Chair Graf responded to public comments by clarifying that the survey area designation involves no expenditure of
City funds, no tax increment is triggered until a budget is formally approved and construction occurs, and that the resolution
represented an opportunity to convert currently non-taxable land into a source of long-term tax revenue—reducing future pressure
on residential property tax rates.
          Board member Lundell stated that he was comfortable approving the survey area specifically because it would allow the
"but for" question to be formally answered, that is, whether the project would proceed without Redevelopment Agency support—
which he considered central to any CRA justification.
          Board member Myers noted the significant scale of the project and the substantial potential tax benefit for the city and
school districts.
          Board member Washington questioned the fast-track nature of this proposal relative to the Capital Square project, which
had been in process since 2021. Mr. Gibson explained that Capital Square's timeline has been prolonged by ongoing
environmental remediation and unresolved site plan issues with the developer, whereas the Weber State request required a timely
response given the university's semester-driven construction schedule.
          Board member Lopez questioned why the City should bear even the staff-time cost of the survey, rather than requiring
the developer to fund that work. Mr. Sawyer and Mr. Gibson clarified that no formal contracted survey cost is involved—only
internal staff time—and that the City routinely provides similar preliminary review services at no charge.
          Chair Hyer asked whether the county's participation caps had been accounted for. Mr. Gibson confirmed that the county
has a stated policy of contributing a maximum of 50 percent of their increment share, directed only toward infrastructure costs,
and that this is consistent with their approach to other CRA areas.
          ON A MOTION BY BOARD MEMBER LUNDELL AND SECONDED BY BOARD MEMBER MYERS,
RESOLUTION 2016-3 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – BOARD
MEMBERS LUNDELL, MYERS, VICE CHAIR GRAF, AND CHAIR HYER. VOTING NO – BOARD MEMBERS
LOPEZ AND WASHINGTON. Board member Richey was not present when this vote was taken.

Executive Director Comments
         The Executive Director of the Redevelopment Agency was not present. Chief Administrative Officer Brown offered brief
remarks in his stead, commending the quality of discussion on all items, acknowledging the contributions of staff, and recognizing
Weber State University as a valued City partner.

Board member Comments
         Board member Washington inquired about the status of the Capital Square environmental cleanup, referencing a recently
approved Mayoral memo authorizing payment for the first phase of cleanup. Mr. Gibson confirmed that discussions with the
Capital Square developer are active and progressing. The anticipated first phase of development would include townhomes, a
small apartment unit, a pedestrian thoroughfare connecting to the Oasis Community Garden, and small retail space. He noted that
the developer has indicated TIF funding is not needed for this first phase, and that staff is currently evaluating whether to establish
a CRA now to begin capturing increment for use in future phases, or to wait until the first phase is complete.
         Board member Washington also raised concerns about the 7-Eleven at 24th Street and Quincy Avenue, noting it serves as
a grocery resource for nearby residents including those near Lester Park, and requested that the developer be made aware of the
Council's interest in ensuring the business is considered in future site plans. Mr. Gibson confirmed that future development phases
include plans for a larger grocery retail space, and that he would relay the Board's concern to the developer regarding the 7-
Eleven's potential relocation or inclusion.


       There being no further business to come before the Agency, BOARD MEMBER MYERS MOVED THE MEETING
ADJOURN AT 7:43 P.M. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY BOARD MEMBER WASHINGTON, ALL VOTING AYE.
