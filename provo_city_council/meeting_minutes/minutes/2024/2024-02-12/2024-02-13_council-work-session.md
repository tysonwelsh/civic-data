budget efficiency. Justin Harrison turned the floor over to Chief Administrative Officer Scott
Henderson, who commended the Council's collaboration with the administration.
John Borget, Director of Administrative Services, provided insights from the Quarterly Report,
highlighting a slowdown in revenue growth on state and national levels, greater than anticipated.
Borget noted the FY2025 budget may be slightly reduced from the FY2024 budget, posing

    2.    Sidewalks & Active Transportation Priorities
Presenter: Gordon Haight, Public Works Director
Gordon Haight presented on the state of missing sidewalks within the city, emphasizing critical
areas such as Wasatch Elementary and Shoreline Middle School. The estimated cost is
approximately $800,000, including $600,000 from Community Development Block Grant
(CDBG) funds and a $200,000 barrier cost. Haight also noted the use of a UDOT grant for 600
South, which coincides with storm drain work, allowing for additional sidewalk repairs near
Shoreline.
Councilor George raised concerns about 1450 East crosswalks and the state law requirement to
provide busing where no sidewalks exist. He noted that 90 students currently walk along
dangerous roads, with the total project cost potentially reaching $3-4 million. George
emphasized the liability risks for children and recommended confirming state law to ensure
compliance.


Council Discussion:
        Katrice MacKay inquired about the top three sidewalk priorities, and Gordon provided
         clarification.
        Becky Bogdin expressed appreciation for prioritizing West Provo.
        George Handley encouraged developing designated corridors for student travel to campus
         and discussed reallocating Transportation Utility Fund revenues to fund these projects
         more aggressively.

            o   Seconded by: Gary Garrett

Summary and Next Steps:
The Council affirmed that school-area sidewalks are a top priority over other projects, such as
the Carterville Trail, with both short-term and long-term improvements in mind. Councilor
Katrice MacKay reminded the group that utility fees will be reviewed around July, aligning with
the Council’s long-term mobility and safety goals.
The meeting concluded with Gordon summarizing the need for $1.2 million for new sidewalks,
in addition to the $500,000 annual maintenance fund. Councilor George Handley argued that the
current fund is unsustainable without increased funding. Councilor Rachel Whipple emphasized
finding a sustainable funding model to improve mobility and quality of life throughout Provo.

    3.   Code and Parking Enforcement Discussion
Led by Bill Pepperone, Director of Development Services
Bill Pepperone provided an update on code and parking enforcement, explaining that staffing
levels are currently sufficient to address complaint-based issues. However, proactive
enforcement in the past has occasionally triggered resident complaints. While most violations are
straightforward, occupancy-related issues are time-consuming to investigate and enforce.
Discussion Points:
        Councilor Craig Christensen: Expressed concern that proving violations can be overly
         labor-intensive.
        Councilor Katrice MacKay: Emphasized that housing remains the top priority, and
         addressing parking challenges can encourage homeownership by easing the burden of
         illegal rentals on homeowners.
        Councilor Travis Hoban: Asked if stricter parking enforcement could pave the way for
         more Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) by alleviating neighborhood concerns.
        Councilor Rachel Whipple: Noted that over-occupancy only concerns her when it
         impacts neighbors and recommended hiring more staff to manage the issue. She also
         asked how many additional personnel would be necessary to address parking concerns.
        Bill Pepperone: Explained that division manager Sandy Beus was able to create a new
         full-time enforcement position without increasing the budget.


LPR (License Plate Recognition) System Proposal:
        Bill Pepperone: Presented a proposal to acquire an LPR system to improve parking
         enforcement efficiency. The system, costing $101,958, would scan license plates and
         enable the use of two currently idle vehicles.
        Funding Options:
            o   John Borget, Administrative Services Director: Reported that approximately
                $115,000 remains unspent from hillside studies in the Capital Improvement Plans
                (CIP). Additionally, Bird Scooter revenues carried over from FY23 total $70,000,
                with another $63,000 collected so far in FY24, providing approximately $130,000
                in total scooter-related revenue.

(3:09:05)
    4.   Employee Talent Retention
Led by Scott Henderson, Chief Administrative Officer
Scott Henderson provided an overview of the City’s approach to employee talent retention.
Discussion Points:
        Councilor Craig Christensen: Asked if decisions about talent retention are determined
         by department heads or if input comes from employees in a bottom-up approach.
            o   Scott Henderson: Explained that talent retention strategies often involve a
                combination of both top-down and bottom-up input, depending on the context and
                needs of the department.
        Councilor Katrice MacKay: Noted that Cost-of-Living Adjustments (COLA) are
         determined through a top-down process, setting city-wide policies for compensation.
        Councilor Gary Garrett: Highlighted that compensation policies send a message to
         employees about who makes these decisions.
        Councilor Rachel Whipple: Expressed concern about the inefficiency of frequently
         hiring new employees, emphasizing that retention of current staff is more cost-effective
         and beneficial for organizational stability.
The Council acknowledged the need to maintain a strategic focus on talent retention to ensure
employee satisfaction and reduce turnover. This topic will be revisited in future meetings to
explore further retention strategies.
