GENERAL SESSION

Chair Snow called the Planning & Zoning Commission meeting to order at 6:05 p.m. He
explained how the meeting would proceed. First, the Planning Department would brief the
Commission; then the applicant would speak to the Commission; the item will be opened to the
public for their comments on public hearing agenda items; the Midvale Deputy City Attorney
will then brief the Commission on their role on the given item; and then the Commission would
deliberate and decide.

PUBLIC HEARINGS

2.   Jacob Ballstaedt requests amendments for expansion of The Mills project. These
     include an amended Large Scale Master Plan and Conditional Use Permit in
     conjunction with a Rezone request, Small Scale Master Plan, and Preliminary
     Subdivision located at 208-272 E 8000 S and 8082-8060 S 300 E in the SF-1/AO and
     RM-12 zone.

     Midvale Mills Phase 1 was approved on April 6, 2021, for a rezone, large scale master plan,
     and development agreement. More land has been acquired to expand the project, and as such,
     amendments are necessary for previous approvals. In conjunction with the amendments, the
     applicant is also seeking a rezone request, small scale master plan, and preliminary
     subdivision approval for a proposed Phase 2 and 3 of The Midvale Mills.

     The development agreement, conditional use permit, and large-scale master plan have been
     updated to include the newly acquired area.

     Staff recommends approval.


                                            ***



PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION JANUARY 25, 2023.
APPLICANT:

Jacob Ballstaedt, with Garbett Homes, explains the reasoning behind the request. The Mills
Project started years ago and the company had been trying to acquire this new land. This is to
expand the geographical area of the existing project. There will be 11 lots added to the initial
plans.

There has been neighborhood meetings held to try to appease the existing residents. The project
is very amenable to those around. The development agreement has specified that the same
housing plans are to be applied to these new lots.

The only concerns mentioned have been regarding the activation of the open space or green
space.


                                               ***


PUBLIC HEARING:

Chair Snow opened the public hearing.

Christian Blefzacker, neighbor to the West of the project, shares his concerns about the open space.
The concern is regarding noise levels from the Pickleball Courts. Mr. Blefzacker shares his studies
on other residential spaces with the same activation. He would like to know whether the location
of the courts can be relocated to an area further from the nearby houses.

The Planning Commissioners request to see the proposed layout of the open space and the
possibility of relocation.

Jonathan Voss, neighbor of the project, voices his concerns on the same matter. After attending
the meeting, he is upset at the proximity to the nearby houses. The popularity of the Pickleball
Courts will ensure a loud community at all allowable hours. Mr. Voss shares his research regarding
the noise levels of the sport. He feels that this type of activation is meant for a park, not the middle
of a small community. Mr. Voss feels that the noise levels will be magnified by being placed in
the center of the residential area.

Planning Commissioners call Mr. Ballstaedt back up.

Mr. Ballstaedt voices that the intentions were good and thought of as a benefit to the residents. As
a nearby resident himself, he does not see the same issue. Being amenable to the requests of the
neighbors, he has agreed to look at the relocation of the Pickleball Courts. It is part of the Midvale
City Code that activation is required, and this is currently a popular use. Mr. Ballstaedt expresses
that Garbett Homes has tried and will continue to try to make this a desirable place for the
community. No matter the type of activation, there will be noise. The developer would agree to go
down to one court if that is preferred.



PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION JANUARY 25, 2023.
Madison Smith, neighbor of the project, shares her worries about the noise levels as well. Her
primary concern is that it will not be a private Pickleball Court, but open to approximately 50
residences. The open hours of the courts would be an issue for those that work from home and the
small children in the neighborhood. Ms. Smith suggests that the courts should be moved to 600-
800 feet from any residence.

Mr. Voss returns to discuss the proposed gate that Mr. Ballstaedt had mentioned. The gate height
would be an issue if it wasn’t tall enough to keep non-community members out. He explains that
private and public courts are very different. The open hours of the courts would disrupt his and
others’ sleep schedules. Mr. Voss finds it unnecessary to have an activation of the space that would
cause such an issue.

DISCUSSION:

Commissioner Tippetts would like to know what the community would like in place of the
Pickleball Courts. It is in her opinion that activation of open spaces is vital to the city.
Commissioner Tippetts suggests that full advantage must be taken of any open space that is
available since it is so limited.

Vice Chair Anderson agrees. The limited open space must be used wisely.

Planning Commissioners call Mr. Ballstaedt back up.

Chair Snow suggests that the turf and grass area be moved West, against the residences there.

Mr. Ballstaedt agrees that adjustments can be made to appease the noise concerns. The suggested
layout was the only way to fit two courts. If it was taken down to just one court, it could be moved
closer to the parking area and further from the houses. At this time, it is not the concern of tonight’s
meeting but will be addressed and altered to ease neighbors’ worries.

Commissioner Erickson explains that other activation options will have a noise impact on the

Mr. Wilcox explains the nature of the motions to be made. The rezoning motion will be the only

meet qualifications of the Midvale City Code.

PUBLIC HEARINGS

3.   Gardner Jordan Bluffs LC requests Large-Scale Master Plan approval for a multi-
     family development in the area of Bingham Junction Blvd and Ivy Dr.

     The applicant requests large-scale master plan approval for View 78, located south of Ivy
     Drive and west of Bingham Junction Blvd. in the Jordan Bluffs Zone Subareas 1-3 (JB 1-3).
     A large-scale master plan is required for any area (pod) within Jordan Bluffs Subareas 1-3
     prior to any specific development applications. A Development Agreement (DA) is required
     for each largescale master plan within the overall zone. A draft DA has been submitted and
     review is on-going. A condition of approval is offered for consideration, allowing a
     recommendation of approval pending “approval of a development agreement in accordance
     with Section 17-7-10.12.2. E of the Midvale Municipal Code”.

     View 78 is a multi-family project consisting of 607 units on approximately 27 acres, with an
     overall density of 22.5 du/ac. Upon approval of a large-scale master plan, an applicant may
     proceed to site plan review, where greater detail pertaining to landscaping, architecture,
     amenities, and open spaces are reviewed for compliance with the Code.

     Staff recommends approval.

Liza Hart, with Garner, explains that the proposed plan at the previous workshop session has not
changed. There is no garage parking, one covered stall for each unit, and the uncovered parking
is first-come first-serve. The reduction of the 19 spaces is solely guest parking.

The intention for the exterior designs and variability will come once construction documents are
submitted for permits. Different colors and textures are in mind to create a better streetscape.

The representative from the Gardner Company expresses thanks for the collaboration and hard

PUBLIC HEARING:

Chair Snow opened the public hearing.

Wayne Bassham, Midvale resident, thanks the City for the notice he was sent regarding the
Gardner project. His concern is the capacity of the area and the excessive number of vehicles. Mr.
Bassham requests better lighting, speed bumps, and adequate parking for the new builds. He
expresses the lack of parking already available for the nearby condos and the visitors that come
along with that development.

Mr. Bassham also voices his concerns for the economic impact that this have with there already
being multiple other developments in the area. He asks for the consideration of the Commissioners
to not approve on of the upcoming residential developments. Mr. Bassham was more in favor of
the original idea of placing a hotel in this area instead of another residential development.
