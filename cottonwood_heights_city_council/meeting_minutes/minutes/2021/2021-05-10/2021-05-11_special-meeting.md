Mayor Mike Peterson called the meeting to order at 5:05 p.m. and welcomed those listening. He

pursuant to Utah Code Annotated §52-4-207(4).

2.      Attorney General Report on Events of August 2, 2020.

Mayor Peterson introduced the Attorney General Report regarding the events that took place on
August 2, 2020. The report was requested by the City Council shortly after the incident to allow
the City Council to be advised by an independent expert as to why the City’s response was
appropriate under applicable laws and law enforcement practices in the State. The Mayor
appreciated the time and effort invested by the Attorney General’s Office. He recognized that
sensitive information was to be presented concerning a very difficult day in the community. He
believed that the Council’s focus should be on understanding the findings, determining
implications, and moving forward in a manner that promotes healing and respect for all individuals
in the community. Because the Attorney General’s Office requested that they be allowed to present
the report without interruption, all of the meeting attendees, including the City Council, were able
to listen to the presentation but would not be allowed to ask questions or make comments. The
public would be able to make comments at a future City Council Business Meeting, which begin
at 7:00 p.m. on the first and third Tuesdays of each month with the exception of holidays. The
next City Council Meeting was scheduled for Tuesday, May 18.

The City Council was able to submit written questions to the Attorney General’s Office after the
report is presented. Questions would be responded to in the coming days based on available time
and resources. The Attorney General’s Office informed the City that a written summary of their
report will be submitted to the City following the presentation. The City will then post the report
on its website.

Utah State Attorney General’s Office Chief Deputy, Spencer Austin, introduced Special Agent,
Matthew Thompson, who made the presentation. Special Agent Thompson explained that the
audit serves as an opportunity to use time and hindsight to look at the events, practices, strategies,
issues, or anything else that is requested of the audit. In this case, they could view dozens of hours
of video and audio tapes in real-time and in slow motion. They were able to rerun and reevaluate
every interaction and critique and make recommendations based on those factors. The audit did
not account for the emotion, environment, or the real or perceived threat of those in the situation
making decisions. The audit was for the purpose of recommendations made in hindsight using
various tools. The audit was not for the purpose of criticism or second-guessing individuals who
were in the heat of battle nor was it an investigation being conducted for any other purpose.
Nothing in the audit was obtained through the legal process and was provided by those willing to
cooperate. Much of the material was posted on public spaces on the internet, which is accessible
to anyone.

Special Agent Thompson reported that when the Attorney General’s Office received the
assignment, they assigned Special Agent Ed Spann to the case. Agent Spann recruited Special
Agent Matt Thompson to assist him and work on the case. Agent Spann has been in law
enforcement for 44 years for the State of Utah and worked as a patrolman, detective, SWAT
member, supervisor, and administrator. He has also worked with various agencies on criminal
cases and is a highly decorated police veteran in the State of Utah. Agent Spann has extensive
training and experience investigating major crimes and defensive tactics, defensive weapons, and
munitions. He also has experience investigating the use of force issues.

Special Agent Thompson reported that he has been law enforcement for 22 years in Utah and has
worked as a patrolman, detective, and special agent and has worked with officer-involved critical
incident protocol teams, and served as a member of the Homicide Task Force and Major Crimes
Investigation Teams. He also has experience conducting internal investigations and evaluating
uses of force. He was previously a Field Training Officer and is now a Firearms Instructor. He
is currently assigned to the Special Investigations Public Correction Unit at the Attorney General’s
Office. Special Agent Thompson explained that they worked closely with prosecutors on the case
who have many decades of experience in private and public practice.

Special Agent Thompson explained that they would first review the August 2, 2020, incident and
show video clips, maps, and photos to illustrate the event as a whole as well as specific incidents.
They then will outline the audit points in terms of police response, policies, ordinances, laws, use
of force, and arrests. The materials presented were described as protected government records.
He was mindful that some involved in the incident have been criminally charged and their court
cases are still pending. Special Agent Thompson stated that the materials included in the
presentation were not edited.

The Attorney General’s Report is attached and incorporated into the minutes by this reference.

       Background


August 2, 2020 at about 1600 hours, approximately 130-150 citizens gathered at and
around the pavilion at Mill Hollow Park in Cottonwood Heights City. The event had been
advertised on social media as “March for Justice” with the hashtags,
#dancedanceforrevolution and #blacklivesmatter.
                                             Background Cont’d


Cottonwood Heights Police Officers contacted members of the group who had moved from the
park to the street in the adjacent neighborhood. Certain members of the group and police
officers had physical confrontations, which resulted in several arrests.
      Background cont’d


Cottonwood Heights City requested that the Utah Attorney General’s Office conduct an audit of
the incident and to consider the following points:

a. Did the protesters violate any laws or ordinances?
          i. Was the event properly permitted?
          ii. Was the use of public property/streets lawful?
b. Did the police have a need or a duty to respond?
          i. Was the police response appropriate?
          ii. What improvements could be made?
c. Were the response and actions of the police commensurate with the actions of the protesters?
          i. Were there any issues of excessive force?
          ii. Were any officers or uninvolved civilians injured/assaulted?
          iii.How many arrests occurred?

       Other points to consider:
•   What role did Councilmember Natalie Bruce have in the incident?
•   What role did Police Chief Robby Russo have in the incident?

    Summary of Review


1. City had not received any requests for assistance nor requisite permit applications, where available,
   for an event group of that size and purpose to assemble at Mill Hollow Park pavilion, to occupy the
   city streets with the pedestrian and vehicle traffic the group was observed to have done or to use
   loudspeakers at the park or adjacent streets that afternoon.

2. A small number of the event group were observed refusing repeated orders from police officers.
   Certain members were observed punching officers and/or shoving officers. Certain group members
   were observed physically blocking officers who were attempting to make arrests. A member was
   observed to have stolen an officer’s body worn camera after it had been knocked loose from the
   officer’s uniform.

3. The police had a need or duty to respond. The police responses and actions were measured and
   appropriate; showed deference to the group’s constitutionally protected activities. Police used
   appropriate discretion regarding the group’s violation of certain laws and ordinances, both pre- and
   post-contact. Police made reasonable attempts to de-escalate tense interactions and made clear,
   repeated, and lawful orders before taking arrest actions.
     Summary of Review

4.   A total of eight people were arrested and booked the day of the incident. One other was charged on
     a later date. Charges include, or investigations have considered, crimes of failure to obey lawful
     orders to leave the city streets, assaults against peace officers, disorderly conduct, interfering with
     arrest, obstruction of justice, theft and riot.

5.   Officers were observed to have suffered injuries related to the assaults, obstructive and/or
     resistive acts of some of the arrestees.

6.   Arrestees as well as some who were never apprehended/arrested were observed to have suffered
     minor injuries related to officers OC and or Taser deployment, baton strikes, and other physical
     force delivered in efforts to arrest individuals or protect other officers who worked to make
     arrests.

7.   No incidents of excessive force used against citizens were observed. The uses of force in this
     incident were found to have been necessary and justified to stop criminal violations of law, prevent
     escape from and interference with lawful arrest(s) and to protect the safety of other officers.

8.   The review found some (procedural) departmental policy violations.
          Summary of Review




9.   Councilmember Bruce was observed in several videos as being present during the incident and is among those
     criminally charged.

10. The review found that Chief Russo’s actions as a police chief and peace officer while on scene in this incident were
    appropriate. Chief Russo was observed interacting calmly with citizens and offering support to arresting
    officers, when necessary, and in accordance with his authority and assigned duties.
               Observations and Suggestions




1.   The city and police department have existing policies, ordinances and procedures related to the activities and enforcement
     demonstrated in this incident.

2.   The Cottonwood Heights Police should prepare for such events, to include training, pre-event planning, intelligence gathering,
     targeted communication with members of citizen groups and an operations plan and information dissemination to its own
     members.

3.   Suggested improvements include utilizing an incident command system, an operations plan with post orders, report writing, pre-
     event communications/outreach, event communication, equipment, call-out procedures, critical incident debriefing, screening
     procedures, and city ordinance and departmental/city policy training.

4.   The Cottonwood Heights Police should consider working with neighboring jurisdictions to streamline inter-agency responses
     to such incidents – to cooperate for a more uniform, organized and efficient response.
               Observations and Suggestions




5.   Cottonwood Heights City may consider reviewing ordinances and permit processes regarding the use of public spaces for
     spontaneous or planned citizen gatherings to confirm that the process is simple, timely, efficient, widely available and
     accommodates protected speech activities while also preserving the peace and safety of other citizens who reside, work or travel
     through the area. Consider posting (additional) signage.

6.   The review found many video-recorded examples of officers who had attempted to reason with event group members,
     including examples of officers pleading with group members for their compliance with requests/orders to leave the streets and
     to continue the event on the sidewalks.

7.   In general, most attendees obeyed direction from officers on scene and, overall, were observed to have been relatively
     peaceful throughout the incident.

8.   Some individuals among the group, either those who appeared to be part of “March for Justice” event leadership or those in their
     immediate fringe, seemed determined to challenge any member of the public and any police officer who made any attempt to
     dissuade or otherwise influence the group’s activity.
