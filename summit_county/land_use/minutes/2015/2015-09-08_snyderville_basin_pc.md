3. Public hearing and possible action regarding a Conditional Use Permit for a 
commercial horse stable and riding academy; 7905 Long Rifle Road; SL-E-309; 
Dawn Bowes, applicant – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner presented the staff report and provided a review of the site, which has a 
stable and a number of related amenities.  This property previously received a business 
license for this use, but the business license was not renewed in 2013.  Had the business 
license been renewed, this use could have continued as a non-conforming use.  Between 
the time the original business license was issued and the current date, this use became a 
conditional use in the Rural Residential (RR) Zone.   The applicant is requesting the 
ability to provide care for a maximum of six horses, provide lessons for owned or leased 
horses, train horses, and sell and lease horses.  A local equestrian club will also use the 
facility to practice for events.  He provided photographs of the existing facilities.  Staff 
recommended that the Planning Commission review the use and approve it based on the 
findings, conclusions, and conditions outlined in the staff report. 
 
Commissioner Peck noted that the applicant is requesting a maximum of six horses with 
only four stalls and asked if horses would be bedded down in the paddock.  Dawn Bowes, 
the applicant, replied that there are two additional paddocks that have water and covered 
shelter, and she would rotate the horses through them.  Commissioner Peck confirmed 
with Ms. Bowes that there would be no horse shows on the site.

4. Public hearing and possible action regarding amendments to Section 10-8-11; 
Preservation of Historically Significant Structures in the Snyderville Basin 
Development Code – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner presented the staff report and recalled that the Planning Commission 
held a previous work session to review this language that would replace the existing 
language in Section 10-8-11 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code with regard to 
preservation of historically significant structures.  He has incorporated the changes 
requested by the Planning Commission.  Staff recommended that the Planning 
Commission review the language and forward a positive recommendation to the Summit 
County Council. 
 
Commissioner Peck believed Planner Milliner had included the general principles that 
were discussed but felt they needed to be further addressed for a number of things.  She 
offered to give him a copy she had redlined and did not think it says what they need it to 
say.  Ms. Brackin stated that one thing Commissioner Peck pointed out is that 
“Historically Significant” needs to be a defined term, and they need to explain what that 
means.  Planner Milliner explained that is addressed in Section 3, which outlines the 
determination of historic significance.  Commissioner Peck explained that capitalized

terms become terms of art, and somewhere there needs to be a definition of what 
Historically Significant means.  Then it should be capitalized throughout the document.  
She also asked Planner Milliner to clarify other language in the amendments and 
suggested other edits to the document.  She asked about some of the uses described in the 
document, and Staff explained that they are in the Use Table in the Code.  
 
Commissioner DeFord stated that it looks like nothing will come to the Planning 
Commission unless the Community Development approves it, so he was not sure all the 
definitions are so important.  Planner Milliner explained that, because under State law a 
conditional use is an approved use unless the impacts cannot be mitigated, an applicant 
must meet certain qualifying provisions before they can apply for the CUP.  The process 
would be that, if an applicant believes they have a structure that is historically significant, 
they would first go through the qualifying provisions.  If those criteria are met, it would 
come to the Planning Commission to determine historical significance and review and 
approve the CUP for the proposed use.  Since the County does not have an inventory 
done by a professional historian, they need a process for determining whether a structure 
is historically significant.  Commissioner DeFord asked for clarification of some of the 
allowed uses.  Planner Milliner explained that the use must be within the historic 
structure, and they would have to go through the criteria to be sure that the use does not 
harm the historical significance of the structure.  Commissioner DeFord asked how they 
can determine material net cumulative adverse impact.  Planner Milliner explained that is 
left open ended, because they want the Planning Commission to be able to make a finding 
but not leave it so open that there is nothing to go on.  He adopted this language from a 
set of criteria in another jurisdiction, because it has been vetted by attorneys. 
 
Commissioner Klingenstein asked if they might have gone too broad with retail sales 
food and retail sales general uses.  He was trying to think of any unanticipated unintended 
consequences.  Commissioner DeFord stated that he had the same concern until he 
learned that the use has to be within the historic structure, which would help to limit the 
use.  Commissioner Klingenstein felt that retail sales general was a little too broad and
