Logan River Watershed Project Information regarding the Little Logan – Chair
Amy Z. Anderson (14:46)

Chair A. Anderson reported the assessment of the Logan River Watershed Project is still
being conducted. The primary objectives are flood control, agricultural water
management, recreational opportunities in a project area, and so forth. Each individual
community in the county has its own unique needs. Currently, the assessment is going
forward with two alternatives being reviewed. For clarification purposes and for the
benefit of the public, no alternative has been proposed that would enclose or pipe the
entirety of the Little Logan River or the Logan River.

“The Logan River Watershed Planning Team has received comments about two potential
alternatives which address concerns about the Little Logan. No alternative has been
proposed that would pipe or enclose the entire Little Logan

First Dam Alternative, under this alternative, the Little Logan would be supplied with
irrigation water to maintain flow during the irrigation season starting in River Hollow
Park just west of the diversion with the Logan River, including through Jens Johansen
Park, Merlin Olsen Park, the Cache County Fairgrounds, Willow Park, and Majestic
Park. Due to the need for a large diameter pipe to pressurize the irrigation system
throughout the Crockett Distribution System, the main water supply pipe would have to
be placed in the current Little Logan alignment below River Hollow Park to Crockett
Avenue. A new trail is planned to be placed over the pipe. Adjacent to the pipe and trail,


                                                                                     3 | Page
              Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ August 6, 2024
the project team is considering options to maintain an open channel to retain open flow
through River Hollow Park.

Under this alternative, the Crockett Diversion dam in the Logan River would be removed
and replaced with a series of steps and pools. The diversion removal would benefit water
quality, facilitate fish and recreational passage, and improve river aesthetics, and instream
flows would be provided in the Logan River.

Crockett Diversion Alternative, under this alternative, the Little Logan would be
supplied with water to maintain flow during the irrigation season through all the sections
of the Little Logan, including River Hollow Park, Jens Johansen Park, Merlin Olsen Park,
the Cache County Fairgrounds, Willow Park, and Majestic Park.

The Crockett Diversion dam in the Logan River would be replaced. Instream flows would
be provided in the Logan River to improve water quality, flow continuity, and river
aesthetics along the Logan River.”

Chair A. Anderson stated the public period has already closed, but they are still accepting
public comments or questions via email at loganriver@utwatershed.com.

Councilmember Simmonds indicated that the actual timeline of the watershed project is
in 2026 at the earliest, since the watershed project would need to be completed, reviewed,
and approved, and even then, funding will still be required for the project to go into effect
through Congress. If reviewed and approved by Congress, funds will be needed along

PUBLIC HEARING - Consideration of a proposed resolution indicating the Intent
to Adjust the Municipal Boundary between the City of Logan and Nibley City –
Resolution 24-27 – Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director (26:55)

At the July 16, 224 Council meeting, Community Development Director Mike DeSimone
addressed the Council regarding the proposed resolution.

Mr. DeSimone explained that the owners of the property described have a desire to
further develop their property, and these property owners believe their proposed
developments can be better facilitated by a boundary adjustment between the City of
Logan and Nibley City. Each City believes it is in the best interest of their respective
residents to have an easily identifiable common boundary between the two jurisdictions
to avoid duplication of services. Due to constraints associated with providing public
utilities, each City believes it is in the best interest of their respective jurisdictions to
agree to a boundary adjustment to facilitate service provision. This boundary adjustment
will rectify a municipal boundary discrepancy involving the existing Thomas Edison
Charter School property. UCA 10-2-419 requires that the Municipal Council of each
municipality so desiring to adjust their common boundary adopt a resolution indicating
their intent.

Councilmember Johnson requested clarification on the definition of standby.

Fire Chief Nate Thompson responded that typically a standby event is a non-emergency
event in nature for example a fireworks show. The Fire Marshall’s Office requires a fire
apparatus to be on standby for such events to ensure that grass does not catch or other fire
related incidents. In the case of USU events such as football games, an emergency
apparatus is on standby.

Councilmember Simmonds requested confirmation that the that the two-hour standby is
required for the airport (ARFF).

Chief Thompson confirmed that is the case, the airport certification requires there be
standby at the airport for when a visiting team is arriving or USU is departing.

Mayor Daines asked if a two-hour standby for the airport was sufficient.

Chief Thompson stated that the two-hour standby is more than sufficient time to cover
the arrival or departure. The FAA (Federal Aviation Administration) requires Fire be on
standby thirty minutes prior to arrival and twenty minutes or thirty minutes after
departure.

Chair A. Anderson inquired how the fire department knows who to bill when there is an
accident.

Chief Thompson explained the officers on site would document the information which
would be called "a fire report" and would have a separate billing process for vehicle
(auto) insurance companies, which is different and separate from the EMS billing system.

Chief Thompson responded that the practice has become more and more common in
order to cover fire department costs. Logan is currently the uncommon ones by not
charging a fee. Many of the newer fire departments have adopted the entire fee schedule
rather than just a portion.

Chair A. Anderson requested confirmation that the billing rate/price for

Chief Thompson confirmed that the billing rate for medication and/or supplies is passed
