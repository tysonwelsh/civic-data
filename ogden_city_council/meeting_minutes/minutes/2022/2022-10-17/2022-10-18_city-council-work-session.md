The purpose of the work session was to review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.;
receive an update regarding the Neighbor-Up Grant program; discuss proposed changes to the City’s Alarm Ordinance; discuss
proposed East Central Community Plan amendments, including: East Central Community Plan Update, Transit Overlay (TO)
Zone Standards and Rezones, Nine Rails Creative District (R-9) Zone Standards and Rezones, and Accessory Dwelling Unit
Amendments; discuss the Proposed Rezone of 682-712 7" Street from R-1-6 (Single-family Residential Zone) to R-3 (Multi-
family Residential Zone); discuss Council business; and hear Council comments.

Neighbor Up Grant Update
Council Office Manager Hawley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide background information and an
update regarding the Neighbor Up Grant Program; it was launched in 2018 to help neighbors better connect with each other to
build a sense of community, develop new relationships, and increase interactions between generations and cultures. It is also
intended to increase governance and organizational leadership of neighborhood organizations or associations. To date, 22 grants
have been awarded and Ms. Hawley presented a video illustrating past events. She then noted that in September 2022, the City
received a request from an individual who received funding in August 2022; he was seeking additional funding for another event
to be held in October 2022. The location and event sponsors were the same. Responsive to this situation, Council staff is
recommending an adjustment to the Grant Program; she summarized the proposal as follows:
e Limit grant awards to one per year for sponsor/or co-sponsor and neighborhood.
e Proposed language to add:
o To comply with State tax code/laws and to equitably supply grant funds city wide, only one (1)
Neighbor Up Grant award per calendar year shall be issued to a specific neighborhood, sponsor,
and/or co-sponsor.
e Adding this language will encourage funding to be spread out city wide and help with tax implications. Grant
awards over $600 requires the sponsor to report as income.
Ms. Hawley engaged in discussion with the Council regarding past events that have been organized using Neighbor Up
Grant funds; Council member Hyer stated that trying to steer event organizers away from the public parks in the City seems
counterintuitive. Ms. Hawley stated that there is no proposal to restrict use of City park space for a Neighbor Up event, but event
organizers will be asked to follow procedures for reserving a park space, which has not been done for some events in the past. She
has worked with the Arts, Culture, and Events Division of the City to ensure that she can communicate to applicants the required
reservation process and any activities that will not be allowed in a public park space. Council member Hyer stated that there has
been some confusion about the types of activities that are prohibited in a public park, or the types of activities that would require
the applicant to pay for liability insurance. Ms. Hawley stated that City staff is willing to work with applicants to advise them of
the types of activities that can be conducted at a City park that would not require insurance; she indicated that the size of a group

Proposed East Central Community Plan Amendments

Planning Manager Brierley introduced the Planning Division’s newest Planner, Brandon Rippien, formerly employed by
Layton City. Mr. Brierley then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize proposed updates to the East Central
Community Plan. Update objectives include planning for land uses hear the Ogden Express Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) project
route, incorporating the Nine Rails Creative District into the Plan, and considering allowance of accessory dwelling units (ADUs)
as an allowed land use in the R-1-5 zoned areas of the Plan area. He summarized the public involvement process for updating the
Plan, which commenced in July 2020.

Council member Choberka inquired as to the makeup of the steering committee that was created to consider Plan
updates. Mr. Brierley indicated it was made up of members of the community who expressed interest in participating, individuals
who were involved in creation of the Nine Rails Creative District Plan, and downtown business owners. Council member
Choberka asked how many people attended the open house, to which Mr. Brierley answered approximately 75.

Mr. Brierley then presented a map illustrating the layout of the BRT route, noting there are four BRT stops in the East
Central Community. He also identified key destinations within the Plan area expounded on the objective of creating transit-
oriented developments near BRT stops as follows:

e Capture the charm of the historic district.

e Allow small cottage and multiple family developments near the stops.
e Allow businesses and live-work units near the stops.

e Allow sidewalk vendors.

e Zone larger properties for mixed-use.

Quality transit stops will also promote mass transit; it is important that the stops be welcoming, safe, and secure. There
has also been a focus on appropriate zoning near the BRT stops; the proposal is to allow smaller lots that would accommodate
smaller cottage homes or accessory dwelling units (ADUs) on small lots. These lots would be allowed within two blocks of the
BRT line. Live-work units are also a proposed allowed land use near the BRT stops; this would allow a property owner to use
their home for residential and commercial purposes as long as no more than 50 percent of the property is used for the business. Up
to five outside employees would be allowed, sales would be allowed, and the structure must be compliant with the Building Code
and the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA).

Mr. Brierley then noted that the Nine Rails Creative District Plan includes a proposal for a transit overlay district to allow
different creative uses in the Plan area and within one block of BRT stops. The goal is to establish an epicenter for art and culture
where the creative community lives, creates, shares, and teaches for the benefit of Ogden’s artists, citizens, and visitors.
Objectives for meeting this goal include creating public spaces and streetscapes, programs and activities, a Central Business
District (CBD) overlay to allow mixed-use and creative uses, create a Nine Rails District Residential Zone (R-9), and seeking
funding for such improvements. He highlighted the area that is proposed for the R-9 zone and indicated that proposed permitted
uses would include:

e = Art gallery.

e Business office primarily related to fine arts.

e Indoor studio for the creation or teaching of the fine arts, e.g., dance studios, art studios. Art fabrication
involving woodworking, metalworking, forges, or kilns would require a conditional use permit.

e Live-work units. This would allow a resident to operate a business in part of a home, such as an art studio, art
gallery, or art business.

e Photo studio.

Council member Choberka suggested that artists are not looking for small spaces to work in, which is what would be
allowed in a live-work unit; they typically need larger open spaces and that is why they look for spaces where others to not want
to live that are less expensive. She suggested that the proposed amendments may only be attractive to particular types of artists,
but those who are creating large public art installations will not be doing so in small live-work spaces. Mr. Brierley agreed and
noted that the proposals are based upon feedback from the community.

Mr. Brierley then discussed proposed amendments relating to the allowance of ADUs in the Plan area; he identified areas
in the East Central Community where ADUs are currently prohibited. The prohibition relates to specific single-family
neighborhoods in the Plan area. He presented a news article indicating that Ogden is one of the most overpriced housing markets
in America and allowing ADUs may help to address that problem; the primary dwelling unit must be owner occupied and
allowing ADUs in the entire Community Plan area would allow the owner to convert a portion of their home to an ADU or builda
smaller unit on their property to be used for rental purposes. This led to discussion of the current number of ADUs in the City;
Council member Hyer noted he would like to know the current number of licensed and unlicensed ADUs in the City and the
number of Code Enforcement cases associated with ADUs. He also asked if there was pushback from the community regarding
the proposal to allow ADUs in the entire Community Plan area. Mr. Brierley stated there was some objection, but there was more
support for the proposal than there was resistance.

Mr. Brierley then concluded the Planning Commission’s recommendation is to amend the East Central Community Plan,
Zoning Ordinance, and Zoning Map to:

e Promote Mass Transit and incorporate the Ogden Express BRT;
e Implement the Nine Rails Creative District Master Plan; and
e Provide for Accessory Dwelling Units.

Council member White asked if this is the last area of the City in which ADUs are prohibited. Mr. Brierley answered no,

there is still a small pocket in the Mount Ogden Community Plan area in which ADUs are prohibited.

Proposed Rezone — 682-712 7th Street_from R-1-6 (Single-family
Residential Zone) to R-3 (Multi-family Residential Zone)

Planning Manager Brierley explained a petition has been filed to rezone seven properties on 7" Street from the current R-
1-6 zone to R-3. The subject properties are a combination of the petitioner’s three properties and additional properties to the west.
The petition is accompanied by letters of request from the other property owners. The petitioners do not currently have a proposed
development, but the rezone would provide consistent zoning on the north side of 7" Street in the area. He presented a map to
identify the properties subject to the zone change application; the properties total 1.55 acres in size and contain six single-family
homes. One goal of the City’s General Plan is to support and preserve the existing single-family nature of the area through
appropriate zoning classifications (about five units per acre). Multi-family should be limited to existing developed area within the
community. Additionally, a definite edge to a development and buffering between types of uses should be provided to protect the
integrity of each use, e.g., between commercial and residential uses and between types of residential uses. Zoning should reflect
the prevailing character of an overall district or neighborhood to the largest extent possible, with consideration being given to the
use and characteristics of individual properties. Where possible, properties that face each other across a local street should be the
same or similar zone. Collector and arterial roads may be sufficient buffers to warrant different zones. Zoning boundaries should
not cut across individual lots or developments (i.e., placing the lot into two separate zones). Illogical boundaries should be
redrawn to follow along property lines. The Planning Commission evaluated whether the proposed zone change is consistent with
these guidelines taken from the General Plan; they voted to forward a positive recommendation to the City Council to approve the
zone change.

Council discussion of the application centered on the existing development of the area and the potential development
options of the subject property if the zone change is approved; the Council expressed hesitance to consider the zoning application
without having some type of project description from the applicants. Mr. Brierley indicated the maximum density that would be
allowed in the R-3 zone is 20 units per acre and it is his understanding that the applicant would like to pursue a townhome project.
Council member White indicated that she does not feel it is appropriate to ask an applicant to spend a great deal of money on
architectural renderings of a project before knowing if the zoning they are seeking is approved. Council member Hyer stated he
does not want an official development package, but some type of preliminary or informal documentation of the type of project that
will be constructed on the property will be helpful in determining whether to approve the zone change.
