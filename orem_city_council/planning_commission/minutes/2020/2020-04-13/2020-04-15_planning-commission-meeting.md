Chair Cochran asked Mr. Harding to introduce Agenda Item 3.1:

Agenda Item 3.1 is a request by Ivory Homes for the Planning Commission to approve the preliminary plat of
Cascade Fields with eight (8) lots located generally at 1200 East 500 North in the R12 zone.

Staff Presentation: Cascade Fields is a 2.97 acre development at 1200 East 500 North. The area is already zoned
R12, so the preliminary plat is simply to divide the land up into the lots. The preliminary plat will contain eight lots.
The plat will connect to 500 North on the north and 1200 East on the west side of the subdivision. The lots all meet
the minimum requirement of 12,000 square feet.

There will be a twenty-three foot public Utility and Sidewalk Easement along the frontage of each lot providing a
buffered sidewalk. A masonry wall or privacy fence (to be determined by owner) will be installed at the back of Lots

Chair Cochran asked if the Planning Commission had any questions for Mr. Harding. Mr. Cook said he did not care
for the layout of this development and he wondered if lots with frontage on both streets was allowed. He thought there
was an ordinance that would not allow lots with frontage on two streets. Mr. Bench said the ordinance requires one of
the frontages to be fenced. This layout does meet that requirement as they are fencing the three lots on 500 North. Mr.
Spencer asked how much latitude the Planning Commission had, if it meets the requirements are they required to
approve. Mr. Bench said the layout meets the requirements as far as lot size, frontage, distance from the intersection,
so it does meet with Orem City code. Mr. Sakar said the layout does look different than the rest of the neighborhood
where everything is on a cul-de-sac and this is a pass-through street. He said if it meets the City’s ordinance
requirements are they required to approve it. Mr. Cook asked, following up on what Mr. Sakar said, is there a reason
they laid the development out this way. A cul-de-sac would fit better into the neighborhood.

Roger Dudley, Dudley & Associates, Inc. (participating via telephone), said they had looked at different options such
as the cul-del-sac, but they would still have double frontage lots. The other reason a cul-de-sac would not work is the
drainage and the sewer depth under the street coming from the west. Even if they placed a cul-de-sac, they still have
double frontage lots. These will be prime, custom homes.

Mr. Earl said regarding the question of double frontage lots and whether the City is required to approve those. City
code 17-7-10(E) says “Lots in a subdivision may not be designed to have double frontage unless, in the opinion of
the City, it is necessary to reduce traffic hazards, allow for unusual site conditions, or if a proposed subdivision and
any area adjacent thereto will be aesthetically enhanced thereby”. Mr. Earl said Mr. Spencer had asked if the Planning
Commission has to approve a subdivision with double frontage lots. As a general rule, double frontage lots are not
approved. Mr. Spencer asked which of those three conditions does this layout meet. Mr. Kelly said with driveway
access on 480 North, it makes 500 North and 1200 East as collector type roads safer. It would be the option to reduce
traffic friction or hazards making it safer. The discussion continued regarding traffic friction and safety.

Mr. Cook said he appreciated Mr. Kelly’s comment. He looked at Google Earth and in the development immediately
to the north, there are thirteen homes with double frontage. They are all cul-de-sacs, but they are double frontage. He
said he withdraws the comment about double frontage.

Skylar Tolbert, Ivory Development, said they intend to fence the entire area along 500 North. The access in and out
may receive a little bit of cut-through traffic, but with the bend in the road and cars parked on the road it will make it
more difficult to cut through there than to stay on the main roads. They are really excited about this project. They have
received comments from neighbors and they are very excited about this development. Mr. Earl said going back to Mr.
Spencer’s question as to which of those criteria apply. Mr. Kelly has said it reduces traffic hazards and it also meets
the unusual site conditions which allows for the Planning Commission to approve the double frontage lots. He cannot
see a way to develop this property without double frontage lots. There are no stub streets, the only way to develop this
is with double frontage lots.

Planning Commission Action: Mr. Cochran moved to approve the preliminary plat of Cascade Fields with eight

Chair Cochran asked Mr. Harding to introduce Agenda Item 3.2:

Agenda Item 3.2 is a request by Gabe Torres for the Planning Commission to vacate Lot 1, Prince Acres
Subdivision, Plat A and approve the final plat of Prince Acres Subdivision, Plat B located at 95 East 900 South in
the R8 zone.

Staff Presentation: The applicant recently applied for a building permit for an accessory structure. In processing his
application, it was discovered that his western-most property is designated as an easement for a future road. As
accessory structures are not permitted to be built in easements, his application was denied.

The applicant is now seeking to merge his two properties into one and have the future road easement vacated. Staff is
of the opinion that there is no longer a need for a future road in this neighborhood. There are already roads in place
that service the homes that this future road was originally intended to, rendering this easement irrelevant.

Recommendation: The Development Review Committee determined this request complies with the Orem City
Code. Staff recommends the Planning Commission vacate Lot 1, Prince Acres Subdivision, Plat A and approve the
final plat of Prince Acres Subdivision, Plat B located at 95 East 900 South in the R8 zone.

Chair Cochran asked if the Planning Commission had any questions for Mr. Harding.

Chair Cochran invited the applicant to speak up via telephone. Roger Dudley, Dudley & Associates, Inc., introduced
himself. This street was vacated years ago when it was determined the street would not go through. This is more of a
bookkeeping item. Mr. Cochran asked if the property lines and easements of other neighbors becomes open property.
Mr. Bench said no, they are owned by the property owners. Mr. Spencer asked what does “clean it up” mean. Mr.
Bench said these easements still exist on the old plat and will have to be dealt with that when the other property owners
come in.

Chair Cochran opened the public hearing and invited those who wanted to make a comment on this item to send in
their comments via email or chat.

Chair Cochran closed the public hearing and asked if the Planning Commission had any more questions for the
applicant or staff. When none did, he called for a motion on this item.

Planning Commission Action: Ms. Jensen moved to approve to vacate Lot 1, Prince Acres Subdivision, Plat A and
approve the final plat of Prince Acres Subdivision, Plat B located at 95 East 900 South in the R8 zone. Mr. Spencer
seconded the motion. Those voting aye: Mickey Cochran, Carl Cook, Camille Jensen, Barry Roberts, Haysam
Sakar, Ross Spencer and Mike Staker. The motion passed unanimously.

To the Persons of the Planning Committee:

Thank you for taking time to consider my communication. I have some concerns about the proposed development,
they are as follows:

1) From an outside perspective, it appears the development was planned to give maximum views of Mt. Timp,
rather than coming up with a cohesive design for the community that makes sense.

This is obvious to anybody who looks at it. It might make more money for the developer but it won't make for a
good community.

2) The proposed development does not follow the natural development already in place of the neighborhood.

The street on the east side of 1200 East is halfway developed. The proposed development will not follow the
expected line of development.

It will be an eyesore and jarring to have homes facing the wrong way (North and South, instead of West) and to have
an illogical entrance to the property.

It would be better if the development followed what was already established years ago and put houses sitting on
1200 East facing west, finishing what appears to have been originally planned out when construction first started in
this area.

This is a more logical way to develop the property.

3) The proposed development creates a sense of elitism and isolation by putting the proposed houses in their
own neighborhood, instead of letting them be a part of the natural neighborhood that already exists, where
possible.

4) The entrance to the development off 1200 East is an unnatural and illogical way to arrange the
development.

The logical way to arrange this development would be to have a culdesac facing the north side where there is
already another entrance that can be positioned in such a way so as to not create illogical traffic patterns and to have
houses on 1200 East facing west without another ill planned street.

The neighborhood north of the proposed development has only one entrance for twenty plus homes.

Surely a neighborhood of less than seven homes doesn't need two entrances?

Based on the previous planning of the city in this area, this would be a more natural way to design the development.

As an extra bonus to the developer, they will have more land they can develop/sell since another street entrance will
not be needed off 1200 East.

This is a win/win for everyone.

5) There will be many people who cut through the proposed development because it is a more direct path, this
will increase traffic in the development and make it less desirable, something that I would expect should be
avoided in a development of $750K+ homes.

Come examine the property and see the path where people already cut through it on a regular basis despite posted no
trespassing signs. This will be an ongoing issue if the development moves forward as proposed.

I hope you take these concerns seriously, because the plans in place will affect the neighborhood for many years to
come.
