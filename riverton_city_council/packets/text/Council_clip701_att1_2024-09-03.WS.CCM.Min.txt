10            Water System Presentation
11
12   Shazelle Terry, Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District, explained that PFAs (Per- and
13   Polyfluoroalkyl Substances) are synthetic chemicals commonly found in various products like
14   water-resistant clothing, firefighting foams, and nonstick cookware. These chemicals are known
15   as "forever chemicals" because they do not break down easily and accumulate in air, water,
16   soil, humans, and animals. Concerns about PFAs stem from their potential public health effects,
17   which include issues like changes in liver function, cholesterol levels, fertility problems,
18   suppressed immune systems, and increased risk of cancer. She said that in Utah, PFAs
19   sampling has been ongoing since 2013 as part of an EPA rule, with no detectable levels found
20   in samples from Riverton or Jordan Valley.

35            Ordinance 24-17 – Amending Riverton ty Code, Section 18.190.050(5), Home
36            Occupations to Clarify the Appeals Process, Amendments Proposed by Riverton
37            City
38
39   Mr. Lethbridge discussed the ordinance amendment related to the appeal authority for home
40   occupations, which are home-based businesses. The current ordinance contains contradictory
41   language regarding appeal processes. It designates the Planning Commission as the land use
42   authority for conditional uses but also incorrectly states that the City Council should act as the
43   appeal authority. In contrast, Section 6 specifies that appeals should go to the Board of
44   Adjustment, which is the correct body for reviewing procedural and legal issues. The proposed
45   amendment seeks to resolve this contradiction by removing the City Council's role as the appeal
46   authority and aligning the appeals process with the Board of Adjustment's responsibilities. The
47   Planning Commission has recommended approval of the amendment.
48
49   Councilmember Pierucci inquired about the history of the discrepancy regarding the appeal
50   authority for home occupations. In response, Ryan Carter, City Attorney, clarified that the
51   discrepancy arose because, at one point, the City Council was the appeal authority for certain
     Page | 6 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     September 3, 2024




 1   matters, including home occupations. However, a decision was made to consolidate appeal
 2   authority within the Board of Adjustment, which is an expert land use attorney appointed to
 3   handle appeals and ensure that decisions adhere to proper procedures and legal standards.
 4
 5   This change aimed to address issues with a previous system where citizen-appointed boards
 6   were handling appeals, which was deemed inappropriate. The shift to a professionally managed
 7   Board of Adjustment occurred approximately seven to eight years ago.
 8
 9   The Board of Adjustment handles land use appeals for administrative decisions made by the
10   Planning Commission, such as conditional use permits and variances. It does not address
11   legislative decisions, like zoning changes. The board reviews appeals based on procedural and
12   legal grounds rather than personal or economic reasons, focusing on ensuring fair application of
13   the code.

24            Ordinance 24-18 – Code Text Amendment, Amending Section 18.135.200,
25            Regulating Permits for Temporary Commercial Uses, Amendments Proposed by
26            Riverton City
27
28   Mr. Lethbridge discussed proposed amendments to the ordinance regulating temporary
29   commercial uses, such as Christmas tree lots and fireworks stands. The existing code lacks
30   clarity regarding the duration and frequency of these uses. Specifically, it does not define
31   whether the 60-day limit applies within a year or a six-month period or if it can be repeated.
32
33   To address this, the proposed changes include:
34
35           Duration Limitation: Temporary commercial uses would be limited to a maximum of 60
36            days within a 12-month period. Two approvals may be issued per property per year.
37           Fireworks Stands: These stands may receive one approval allowing sales on July 4 and
38            July 24, within the 60-day annual limit.
39           Property Requirements: Temporary uses must occur on commercially zoned property
40            developed under an approved site plan. The property must comply with applicable
41            ordinances and standards.
42
43   Mr. Lethbridge said that staff recommended against extending the duration to six months, citing
44   that most temporary food vendors do not operate for such an extended period. Staff suggested
45   that the existing permit holder should be considered legal non-conforming and allowed to
46   continue under their original permit terms. The Planning Commission's recommendation
47   included incorporating the six-month provision to accommodate an existing food vendor, but
48   staff's recommendation was to keep the 60-day limit for all new permits.
49

31            Resolution No. 24-88 – Fee Schedule Amendment (Animal Intake Charge)
32
33   Mr. Geer said that intake fees for animal services from January to August of 2024 were
34   presented as part of the agenda, showing a total of 26 intake fees and one second intake fee,
35   meaning one animal was flagged as being taken in more than once. This brought the total
36   number of intake fees to 27 for that period.
37
38   Councilmembers discussed simplifying the intake fee structure for animal services, particularly
39   for animals that are taken into the shelter. The goal was to streamline the fees, moving away
40   from a tiered system to a single intake fee of $100. This fee is higher than the base cost of $75,
41   which only covers the initial costs without any overhead, such as the animal control officer's
42   time.
43
44   Some Councilmembers expressed concerns that jumping from $65 (current initial intake fee) to
45   $100 might be too steep for residents, especially since boarding fees also accumulate quickly
46   ($10 per day). A suggestion was made to possibly set the intake fee at $75 or $80, to keep it
47   closer to the actual cost while still simplifying the structure.
