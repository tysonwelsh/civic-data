PRESENTATION AND DISCUSSION REGARDING A PROPOSED PERFORMING ARTS
FACILITY:
     Leisure Services Director Shane McAffee outlined the history of a proposed
     performing arts facility. The project was somewhat merged with the County
     Administration building project. Since the study was performed, Duncan Webb has
     evaluated the two proposed sites.

      Mr. Webb presented a PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics:
      Comparison of possible sites; Comparing sites for new SGMT facility; Map of
      downtown St. George; Town Square site; Site 2 – test fit; County building site; Site
      plan; Evaluation criteria; and Evaluating these two sites.

      After being asked by Councilmember McArthur, City Manager Adam Lenhard
      explained how these sites were chosen. The County has recognized there are some
      opportunities on their property.

      Dimon McPherson with the St. George Musical Theater (SGMT) explained they have
      been actively pursuing a proposal for a couple of years. They are excited that the
      County has jumped on board and would be happy to be located where the elected
      officials would like them to be. SGMT will raise $3 million towards the $7 million
      cost for the project. Their goal is to be open late 2022/early 2023. His preference is
      the City’s site as it would take less time to build on; however, the site on 200 East is
      more visible. They are confident they will be able to raise the $3 million.

      Councilmember Hughes commented that he is impressed with what has been done
      and is all in. He would rather use the County’s site due to issues with parking with
      the City site.

      Mr. McPherson asked that the Council not deliberate forever on deciding.

      Mayor Randall stated she liked that the County site as it would allow other art groups
      to use the space. The original proposed performing arts center had a cost of $80
      million, the City cannot cover the cost.

      Councilmember Larkin commented the County’s offer is generous as the City is not in
      a position to build a center to accommodate all of the arts groups.

      Councilmember McArthur commented like the openness of Town Square, but he
      believes the County property is a great location as well.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard explained with regards to timing he asked Mr.
      McPherson to remember that it will take several months for the design.

      Commissioner Almquist provided a history of how the County got involved. The
      County committed to the financial request when it was proposed to be on the City
      site and are still committed; however, the County is worried their site may not be
      large enough. He believes there needs to be further discussion regarding the
      proposal.

      Mayor Randall commented she would like to have additional discussions soon, to
      revisit the proposal and come up with a final decision.

      Councilmember McArthur commented the discussion a few months ago it was stated
      that if the facility is built on the City’s site, it would not provide additional space for
      other groups. It would be a straight in the round theater. He asked if an in the
      round theater would fit on the County’s site.

      Commissioner Almquist noted it would fit; however, due to spending community tax
      dollars, the County believes it would need to be a facility to accommodate other arts
      groups. He stated there are other groups who are having a hard time finding
      locations to accommodate them currently.

      Mr. Webb added he believes they need to go back to the drawing board to solve the
      issues brought up tonight.

PUBLIC HEARING/NAME CHANGE FOR PUBLIC STREET:
     Public hearing and consideration of changing the name of a public street
     from Hilton Drive to Auto Mall Drive located between Dixie Drive and Black
     Ridge Drive (north end).

      Assistant Public Works Director Wes Jenkins presented the request to change the
      name of Hilton Drive to Auto Mall Drive. This is located between Dixie Drive and
      Black Ridge Drive (north end). He showed a map outlining area of name change
      after which he outlined the notifications sent to nearby property owners.

      Mayor Randall opened the public hearing and noted there was a letter received in
      opposition from Mega Pro.

      Applicant Stephen Wade noted there is no longer a Hilton in St. George. The reason
      they feel this needs to be designated as an auto mall is because this is the area
      designed for automobiles per the general plan. He commented that he now owns
      40+ acres in the area and would like to bring in additional auto franchises. Mega Pro
      is one of the corners; however, the remainder of the businesses are auto dealers.

      Mayor Randall closed the public hearing.

      Councilmember Hughes commented that the concern from Mega Pro is a legitimate
      concern; they will have to change some things with a small expense. Most of what is
      in the area is car dealerships. He mentioned that there is a Hilton Garden Inn in
      town, just not in this area.

      MOTION:
          A motion was made by Councilmember Hughes to change the name
                    from Hilton Drive to Auto Mall Drive between Dixie Drive and Black
                    Ridge Drive.
      SECOND:
          The motion was seconded by Councilmember Larkin.
      VOTE:
          Mayor Randall called for a vote, as follows:

                    Councilmember Hughes – aye
                    Councilmember Smethurst – aye
                    Councilmember McArthur – aye
                    Councilmember Larkin – aye

                    The vote was unanimous and the motion carried.

AMEND CITY CODE/ORDINANCE:
    Consider approval of an ordinance amending Title 10-17B-8 of the St.
    George zoning code as it pertains to conditional use permits time frames
    and bar establishments.
St. George City Council Minutes
February 4, 2021
Page Six

      Planner Dan Boles presented the request to amend Title 10-17B-8 of the St. George
      zoning code as it pertains to conditional use permits time frames and bar
      establishments. He provided a PowerPoint presentation covering the following
      topics: City code 10-17B-8; and proposed language. Staff finds these changes to be
      necessary to clearly define substantial construction and how it pertains to bar
      establishments.

      City Attorney Shawn Guzman advised with these changes, it is clear when a
      conditional use permit expires for non-bar establishments as well as addresses bar
      establishments. The two changes he would like to propose are the following:
      1) Change “one extension for one year” to “one extension for up to one year” and
      2) Change “if the conditional use is for the bar establishment, the conditional use
      expires if the bar establishment does not its alcohol licenses” to “if the conditional
      use is for the bar establishment, the conditional use expires if the bar establishment
      does not its alcohol licenses or is not operating as a bar establishment within six
      months from the date of approval”.

      MOTION:
          A motion was made by Councilmember Larkin to amend Title 10-17B-8
                    of the St. George zoning code as it pertains to conditional use permits
                    time frames and bar establishments as presented by counsel.
      SECOND:
          The motion was seconded by Councilmember Hughes.
      VOTE:
          Mayor Randall called for a roll call vote, as follows:

                    Councilmember Hughes – aye
                    Councilmember Smethurst – aye
                    Councilmember McArthur – aye
                    Councilmember Larkin – aye

                    The vote was unanimous and the motion carried.
