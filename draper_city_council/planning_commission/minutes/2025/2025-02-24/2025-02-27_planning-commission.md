       C.      Public Hearing: Draper Town Center Station Area Plan General Plan
               Amendment Request.
               (Legislative Action)
               On the request of Draper City, a request to adopt the Draper Town Center Station Area
               Plan as part of the General Plan. Known as Application No. 2025-0033-TA. Staff
               Contact: Todd Taylor, (801) 576-6510, todd.taylor@draperutah.gov.

Planner III, Todd Taylor, presented the Staff Report for the Draper Town Center Station Area Plan.
The Plan is a response to House Bill 462, which was passed in 2022 and requires that municipalities
with fixed guideway transit stations adopt Station Area Plans.

Station Area Plans must:

      Include a 0.5-mile radius around the station platform.

      Meet objectives for promoting affordable housing, a sustainable environment, access to
       opportunities, transportation choice, and connections.

      Include a station area vision, map, and five-year implementation plan.

      Have public engagement.

      Be adopted by December 31, 2025.

An aerial map was displayed indicating the location of the Utah Transit Authority (“UTA”) Draper
Town Center TRAX station. The required 0.5-mile radius extends beyond City boundaries into
Sandy, past Eastridge Park, to the Walmart Neighborhood Market Shopping Center, south past Draper
Elementary School, and just past Draper Cove to the west.

The planning process began in 2022 when funding was received from the Wasatch Front Regional
Council (“WFRC”) and the firm of Township + Range was hired to lead the project. A Conditions
Analysis was conducted to identify key sites and conduct public outreach, including five stakeholder
meetings, three open houses, two online surveys, and several City Council workshops. The project
team then moved into developing alternative concepts, which led to the preferred concept that they
would review at the meeting.

Two key sites were identified as the UTA owned TRAX station parking lot and the former Fitzgerald
property that is now owned by the City. A potential third key site is the Intermountain Farmers
Association (“IFA”) property to the south of the TRAX station. West of the sites is an industrial area
that leads into the town center. City Hall is to the south, as well as county buildings and Draper Park.
Below that is the Bainbridge development. East is the Parkstone single-family neighborhood. North
is the commercial area along Draper Parkway.

As part of the initial stakeholder outreach, the project team developed the following station area goals:

               strategic, viable, implementable, and reflects the full range of stakeholder desires.

Using these goals, the project team created several alternatives that were presented to the public and
stakeholder groups. As a result of those discussions, 10 Station Area Plan Principles were created.

The Plan Principles are:

       1.      A walkable, active Main Street on Pioneer Road.

       2.      A nexus of trail connections.

       3.      Mixed-use town center on City site with destination restaurants and retail, community
               center, and some housing, that is engaged with and connected to Draper Park and other
               civic destinations.

       4.      Significant residential density on the UTA site in up to five-story buildings.

       5.      Flexibility toward IFA with the encouragement of future redevelopment.

       6.      Improved TRAX station access.

       7.      Rezoning of some areas around the station to allow and encourage transit-oriented
               development.

       8.      Preservation of the scale of Draper’s historic downtown, south and west of the transit-
               oriented area.

       9.      Quality transitions to established residential neighborhoods and historic resources.

       10.     Connectivity improvements throughout the station area.

The preferred concept was then created based on these principles. The City-owned site will include
community plazas along Pioneer Road, as well as an up to a three-story building with mixed-use in
front with multifamily or office above retail and restaurants. There will be a medium-sized
community arts center along Pioneer Road. The area will then transition to townhomes, then twin
homes, and an open space buffer to the Bainbridge development. There will eventually be a trail
connecting to Draper Park.

The UTA site will include multifamily buildings of up to five stories with mixed-use along Draper
Parkway and a step down to three stories along that frontage. The canal trail will be extended and
open space incorporated into the area. Parking will either be under the structure or wrapped within
the buildings. There will be a step down to four stories for the building to the west of Parkstone, as
well as a 100-foot buffer.

The IFA and industrial properties to the west will be up to five stories of mixed-use with a stepdown
along Pioneer Road. The transition to the historic Town Center will include up to four-story
buildings.

North of the Station Area Plan is the commercial centers, which will provide future opportunities for
redevelopment. They will be allowed to go up to three stories with the intention of remaining
primarily commercial, but could potentially incorporate missing middle housing. The additional
commercial area along Draper Parkway has the option to be included in the Town Center in the future.

Community Development Director, Jennifer Jastremsky, added that the existing Town Center area
would retain the Town Center zoning, which has a maximum height of three stories. Some Code
modifications may be needed to address the transition between the two areas.

Mr. Taylor next presented an aerial map of the existing site and renderings of possible concept
development and reviewed how the 10 Plan Principles were applied to the concept.

Chair Adams asked Ms. Jastremsky to speak to how changes to state and federal laws have been
addressed historically. Ms. Jastremsky stated that the state law requiring Station Area Plans was not
made in response to federal law. It was adopted by the Utah State Legislature as they were looking
at ways to solve the affordable housing crisis. The goal of the requirement is to have housing adjacent
to transit.

Chair Adams clarified that after the Planning Commission made a recommendation to the City
Council, the Council would then consider the matter. The discussions, comments, and objections
from that meeting would be considered by the City Council. The City has been asked to adhere to
the guidelines outlined in HB 462, which include affordable housing. The thought process is that
people who do not work in Draper would use the public transit system and live in the affordable
housing in the city. The median income for Salt Lake County is approximately $94,000, and it is
hard to buy a house if you make less than $100,000. The intention is to create affordable housing
near the TRAX station. Parking will be addressed because the intent of the plan is to accommodate
and enhance the TRAX station.

In response to a question raised by Chair Adams, Ms. Jastremsky stated that the Station Area Plan
Code does not specify a specific density, but it does call for transit-oriented development, which is
designed to be high-density housing to support the station. In 2022, the State Legislature considered
passing a bill that would require a set density of approximately 60 units per acre within a 0.5-mile
radius of all stations. The Utah League of Cities and Towns worked with the legislature to come up
with a bill that would instead allow each city to plan for their stations and create developments that
would fit their communities and address their individual concerns.

in accordance with Utah State Code Section 10-9a-404 and if the General Plan Amendment as
proposed by the City is consistent with the requirements of the State Code. Findings for approval or
denial must be based on those findings.

Motion: Commissioner Shah moved to forward a POSITIVE recommendation to the City
Council for approval of the Draper Town Center Station Area Plan General Plan Amendment,
as requested by Draper City, Application No. 2025-0033-TA based on the Findings for Approval
and Criteria for Adoption as listed in the Staff Report dated February 13, 2025, inclusive of the
following additional recommendations, modifications, or findings:

       1.       As noted on Presentation Draper-PC-SAP-021325, Page 33 of 62, which describes
                different residential and potential density ranges, Commissioner Shah asked that
                the City Council consider the Plan principles that include a mixed-use Town
                Center on the City site south of Pioneer Road and significant residential density
                on the UTA site with up to five-story buildings north of Pioneer Road.

Second: Commissioner Nixon seconded the motion.

Vote on Motion: 3-to-0 in favor.
