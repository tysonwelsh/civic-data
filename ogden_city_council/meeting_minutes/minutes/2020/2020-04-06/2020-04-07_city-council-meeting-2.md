          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a Small Business Loan Program
(SBLP) Emergency Loan Fund came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated as a part of the Annual Action Plan
(AAP) and the City's administration of Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) funding, program guidelines are
developed for specific programs targeting specific needs. The program guidelines serve as general parameters for how the City
will use funding for a program. These program guidelines are referred to in the AAP but are entirely developed and adopted by the
City. Amending the program guidelines is done through a resolution adopted by the City Council. Administration is proposing
amendments to the SBLP by adding an Emergency Loan Fund designed to assist businesses impacted by the coronavirus disease
2019 (COVID-19) pandemic. As proposed, the Emergency Loan Fund would be funded through donations and would be
administered under the SBLP. The SBLP would still adhere to the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD)
objective of supporting low and moderate-income individuals for federal dollars but would add a local objective to retain
businesses and prevent job losses for the use of non-federal funds. The Emergency Loan Fund could be used in the future if
donations are made to the City to fund the Program. An initial donation of$100,000 has been made to the City to help small
businesses affected by the economic impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic.
          An additional staff memo from the Comptroller regarding a companion budget amendment came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated the proposed amendments to the Fiscal Year (FY) 2020 budget would enable the City to accept an
anticipated donation, along with any further donations in total ofup to $1 million, to extend support to local small businesses
during the COVID-19 pandemic.
          Comptroller Stout summarized her memo and noted that funds have been donated to the City to assist small businesses
during the COVID-19 emergency by providing help with working capital. Spending will be monitored, and loans will only be
made from the fund as donations are received.
          Community and Economic Development Director Christopulos summarized his memo and provided an overview of the
terms of the loans that will be made from the SBLP for emergency funding purposes. He engaged in discussion with the Council
regarding the differences between City emergency loan funding and federal government emergency loan funding, noting that City

      Fiscal Year 2016-2020 Consolidated Plan Amendment #2 and Fiscal Year
      2020 Annual Action Plan Amendment #2
               A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider
      amendments to the FY2016-2020 Consolidated Plan (ConPlan). The memo stated, as a requirement to receive federal funding
      from HUD, each year the City's Community and Economic Development Department prepares and submits to the Council what is
      known as an Annual Action Plan (AAP). The AAP is used to outline how federal CDBG funds, HOME funds, and other federal
      and non-federal program funds will be used by the City in the upcoming FY. When the ConPlan is submitted to HUD in May of
      each year, specific dollar amounts for entitlement grant funds, program income funds, and carry-over funds are estimated for the
      next FY. Part way through the year, after the specific dollar amounts are known or when program income from loans and other
      programs are received, an amendment must be made to the Plan to update the AAP budget. Often when the mid-year amendment
      is proposed, changes in funding levels within certain programs and line items are proposed as well. The amendments proposed
      with this item are related to the increased funding to help with the COVID-19 related economic conditions. The proposed
      additional funding for this and other related programs is a combination of shifting funds between programs and additional
      program income received throughout FY2020. This is the second amendment to the FY2020 AAP. The FY2016-2020 ConPlan is
      proposed to be updated as well to include the Urgent Need national objective certification.
                Community Development Manager Ogden summarized the memo and stated the purpose of the proposed FY2020 AAP
      Amendment #2 is to update Program Income estimates for the current year. Amendment #1 was adopted on December 17, 2019
      with the purpose ofupdating the budget to reflect accmate carry-over amounts and updated Program Income estimates. Since that
      date, additional Program Income above the Amendment# I estimates has come forward, and these amounts must be included in
      the current budget to allow for current year expenditures. This reflects an increased velocity and amplitude of inveshnent in
      housing and business development projects. A second purpose is to introduce the CDBG Urgent Need national objective into the
,,1   current ConPlan and AAP. We must meet one of three national objectives when we use om CDBG funds - Low-Moderate Income
      Benefit, Alleviation of Slums and Blight, or Urgent Need. The Urgent Need objective is only allowed under declared emergencies.
      The current COVID-19 pandemic has been determined by HUD to meet this objective. To use this objective, we must include it in
      our ConPlan and om AAP. The advantage of this objective is added flexibility in documenting the creation or retention ofjobs for

recommending that additional bonds be issued in the amounts of approximately $20 million for Water Utility projects,

Proposed Resolution 2020-7 adopting a contract with HBME, LLC to
conduct the City's Fiscal Year 2020 to 2022 financial audits
         A memo from Council staff came before the Council to consider a resolution authorizing a contract for auditing services.
 The memo stated the City's contract for financial auditing consulting services has expired and anew financial auditor needs to be
selected beginning for FY2020. In January 2020, the Office of the City Council published a request for proposal for fnms to
perform auditing services. In response to the RFP, six firms submitted proposals. Of the six respondents, the City's internal audit
committee interviewed four of the firms and is recommending the Council enter a contract with HBME, LLC for a period of three
years with an option for an additional two-year extension.
         Mr. Symes summarized the memo and stated the audit committee felt that HBMC, LLC was best qualified based on their
experience in auditing cities with the same form ofgovermnent as Ogden, familiarity with the City's Enterprise Resource Plan
system, and their audits of municipal airports.
