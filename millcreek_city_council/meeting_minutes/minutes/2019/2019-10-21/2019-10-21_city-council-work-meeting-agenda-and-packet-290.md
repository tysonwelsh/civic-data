1. Discussion Items
1.1 Amendments to Various Code Sections (9.56.010, 9.56.020, 11.40.020, 14.12.100,

14.48.050 and Repealing section 14.04.140 and Chapter 14.28); Elyse Greiner, City
Recorder
Elyse Greiner said when she and John Brems were reviewing the code for updates, they
came across some sections that were no longer relevant to Millcreek. The term “real
estate section” was from the County and the chapter on canyon and rural roads only
applied to unincorporated County. She said Kurt Hansen told her that the code
enforcement officers wanted the term “junk” as defined in Title 19 to be added to Title 9
for consistency. The amendment in code section 11.40.020 was to align city code with
the practice of Unified Police Department. The Council said the code changes made
sense. Brems said state statute required a car to be parked in the same place on the street
for at least 48 hours before action could be taken in declaring it a nuisance, so the
amendment would align city code with state code. Mayor Silvestrini said the parked cars
issue was complaint driven. Bill Hodson, from the audience, asked about opportunity for
public comment. Mayor Silvestrini said the Council would take public comment at its

1.2 Amendment to Code Section 2.04.130 Additional Committees; John Geilmann, City
Manager
John Geilmann said staff recently discussed City-sponsored committees. He handed out a
list of current committees recognized in some capacity by Millcreek, including the
Recycling Committee, Youth Council, Utah Venture Outdoors, Millcreek Emergency
Response Committee, Millcreek Arts Council, Envision Committee, Promise Committee,
Millcreek Business Council, Wasatch Business Series, and Sustainability Committee. He
said the meeting packet included text from City Code 2.04.130 which references required
public meeting notices for committees and Utah State Code Ann. 52-4-103 which
addresses the definition of a public body. Some of the City’s committees could meet the
public body definition if recognized by the Council through legislation. Mayor Silvestrini
said some of the entities were independent of the City, such as the Millcreek Arts Council
and community councils. Rita Lund said Millcreek also needed a US Census Committee.
There was a conversation about whether some committees warranted public noticing for
meetings. Council Member Jackson asked if there was a draw back to not noticing the
committee meetings. John Brems said he could not think of one. Council Member
Marchant asked what other cities do. Mike Winder said West Valley City notices some
committee meetings. Brems said he would draft a proposed ordinance amending
Millcreek Code 2.04.130.

1.3 Strategic Planning; Laurie Johnson, HR-Finance Director
Laurie Johnson said the Council added the term “Engaged Community” at their last
strategic planning session to the strategic plan. She suggested the following definition for
it, “Millcreek seeks to engage the community through communication and transparency
to achieve long-term sustainable outcomes, process, and relationships.” Mayor Silvestrini
said the engagement was not the public to the Council but the Council to engage the
public. Council Member Jackson said the communication should go both ways. Council
Member Marchant suggested, “...be engaged with the community...” Mayor Silvestrini
suggested, “Millcreek seeks community engagement...” The Council concurred with
Mayor Silvestrini’s suggestion. Johnson said the Council needed to establish strategic
priorities for “Engaged Community.” She suggested adding a strategic priority about
fiscal responsibility. Council Member Marchant said governments could fail without
fiscal responsibility and liked the idea of adding it. The Council agreed to adding that
priority. Johnson said the strategic plan starts with strategic priorities, then strategic
objectives, then strategies, then goals and accomplishments. She said the hierarchy came
from the language of the General Plan. She said the Council would need to establish
strategic objectives and strategies for “Engaged Community,” establish a definition,
strategic objectives and strategies for “Fiscally Responsible Government,” and review
and discuss a specific strategic priority each month moving forward. Johnson handed out
draft copies of the strategic plan. She briefly went through the draft plan with the
Council. She said by the next meeting, she would draft objectives and definitions. Mayor
Silvestrini said a lot of the information needed could be found in the General Plan, but
the Council needed to provide input for the “fiscally responsible” strategy. Johnson asked
the Council to mark the draft plan handout and provide input on it to her within the next

1.4 Mayor and Council’s Discretion on Discussion Topic
Mayor Silvestrini said there was a code violation complaint at the property on 900 E. and
Van Winkle owned by Salt Lake City Public Utilities (SLCPU). The complaint was that
the area was being used by transients and was littered with syringes and the like. He said
Millcreek code enforcement had been in contact with SLCPU. He said SLCPU had
offered to turn the property into a park of sorts and Millcreek could join an interlocal
agreement with them for the use of the property. Council Member Catten suggested that
if the property was cleared out, it may become more of a resting place for transients and
suggested turning it into a different green space, like a dog park. Mayor Silvestrini
brought up water shed issues with Big Cottonwood Creek nearby. Rita Lund said part of
the property was designated as wetlands and she would reach out to the Army Corp of
Engineers about jurisdiction. Council Member Jackson asked if there would be money in
the budget to maintain it. Council Member Catten asked about having conversations with
Murray City because a new mixed-use development was developing in the old Kmart on
900 E. and those residents would use the space too. Mayor Silvestrini said he would
explore possibilities with the property. Council Member Jackson asked about access to
the property. Geilmann said the property could be accessed by foot through a gate on Van
Winkle. He said the property used to be a park because there is rock work for creeks and
ponds on the property and an access road to it on Murray Holladay Road. John Miller
said the property had a high water table. The Council said they were interested in Mayor
Silvestrini pursuing possibilities with the property.

Mayor Silvestrini said a young resident had reached out to him about the possibility ofa
bike park or pump track in the City. The Council explored potential property locations for
one on an aerial map, especially near Wasatch Boulevard.

Mayor Silvestrini said he met with the joint Sustainability Coordinator, Samantha
DeSeelhorst, about her duties. Millcreek is entitled to 6 hours of her week for Millcreek
specific projects. That said, he did not want her to come to a lot of city meetings. He said
they talked about doing a sustainability inventory for the contracted cities (Cottonwood
Heights, Holladay, and Millcreek). He said the Council needed to decide what she should
be doing for Millcreek and provide Kurt Hansen with feedback.

Mayor Silvestrini said the Unified Police Department (UPD) salary survey was
completed. The Department got a 6 percent salary increase in the current fiscal year. He
said other cities gave increases, so Millcreek held ground instead of advancing the
Department’s position against other departments in the County. He said Unified Fire
Authority (UFA) Station 112 would be rebuilt from the current fiscal year budget and
mentioned that other stations in the system need to be replaced and would be done
through a bond. He said a five-foot stretch of property would be sold to UFA from the
Olympus Hills Shopping Center for a reasonable price for inclusion in the Station 112
site.

Mayor Silvestrini said he had been telling the public that the Council would take public
