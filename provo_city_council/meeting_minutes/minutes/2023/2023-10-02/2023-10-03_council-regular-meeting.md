2.         Judge Romney's Annual Justice Court Presentation (23-072) 0:24:00

Judge Vernon Romney presented. He announced his retirement and discussed his career with Provo
City. He discussed the dedicated and efficient staff in the court who serve the often nervous and anxious
court customers. He mentioned the changes brought about by the COVID-19 pandemic and the decision
to schedule most court proceedings as live in-court hearings. He explained the role of a judge as an
impartial decision-maker in legal cases, ensuring fair presentation of evidence and legal arguments.
Additionally, Judge Romney shared his insights into being a judge, emphasizing the importance of
humility, self-awareness, and a sense of humor. He provided statistics on cases filed in the Provo City
Justice Court, highlighting the effectiveness of programs such as the mental health court and the
domestic violence calendar. He also mentioned collaborations with Brigham Young University and
invited Dr. Chelsie Romney to share the results of a court and customer satisfaction survey that was
conducted by her social psychology class. In closing, Judge Romney thanked the people he worked with
for the opportunity to serve as the Provo City Municipal Justice Court judge.

Dr. Chelsie Romney, a psychology professor at Brigham Young University, introduced a project
conducted by her students from a social psychology course at the Provo City Court. She introduced one
of the students, Andrew Moss, a psychology major from South Texas.

Mr. Moss presented the results of a research study conducted in collaboration with the Provo City
Justice Court. He highlighted the efforts of his fellow students, Sydney Christensen, and Andrew Bodily,
in assisting with the project. He said they administered a nationally validated survey to court customers,
measuring their access to court resources and their perception of fairness in court proceedings. Andrew
shared a positive anecdote about a court customer's change in attitude due to Judge Romney's
explanation of the consequences of his actions. After collecting and analyzing 372 surveys, they found
that the Provo City Justice Court provided equal access and fairness to individuals from diverse
backgrounds. He also emphasized the benefits of the project, which allowed students to become part of
the Provo community, gain a better understanding of court proceedings, and even provided internship
opportunities that prepared students for future roles in law and research. He encouraged the city of
Provo to continue providing opportunities for BYU students to engage with the community and
contribute to its development.

3.         An ordinance amending Provo City Code Chapter 14.33A regarding limits of disturbance
           and clustering requirements in the Critical Hillside Overlay Zone. Citywide Application.
           PLOTA20230163 0:43:35

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2023-39, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Dustin Wright, City Planner, presented a proposed text amendment to the existing Critical Hillside
Ordinance, which is applied along the foothills of the city to regulate development in that area. The
presentation aimed to add clarification and improve the ordinance for those who use it. Some key points
addressed in the proposed changes include: 1. Clarifying the definition of the "limit of disturbance" to
specify areas where development is allowed and where it is not. 2. Ensuring that the ordinance applies
to entire developments, not just individual lots. 3. Adding language to require a 25% Open Space
Preservation area for properties using the cluster option in that zone. 4. Changing the base density
increase from 50% to 25% for agricultural properties, still encouraging clustered development in these
areas. Dustin invited questions or comments from the council.

Vice-Chair Whipple opened the item for public comment. With none, she closed public comment and
brought the discussion back to council. She added that this item was heard at the Planning Commission
meeting on August 23, 2023 and this was the first hearing.

With no other council discussion, she called for a vote on the implied motion.

      Vote:        The motion was approved 5:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Handley, Hoban, Shipley, and
                   Whipple in favor. Councilors Fillmore and MacKay excused.

4.         A resolution approving appointment of individuals to serve on the Judicial Nominating
           Commission for a judicial vacancy in the Provo City Justice Court (23-072) 0:48:15

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Resolution 2023-43, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Mayor Kaufusi presented and recommended Brian Jones and Camille S. Williams for appointment to the
Judicial Nominating Commission. She invited Jim Peters to explain the process of hiring a new Justice
Court Judge.

Mr. Jim Peters, the Justice Court Administrator for the Administrative Office of the Courts, thanked
Judge Romney for his long-standing service and mentioned how his service impacted the entire state of
Utah. He explained the process for appointing a new Justice Court Judge. He said that the application
period for closed, and there were enough applicants to proceed. The next step involves forming a
nominating commission, consisting of members appointed by the local bar association, the county, the
local mayor's association, and the city council. The nominating commission's task is to review
applications, conduct interviews, and narrow the pool of candidates down to a manageable number
(typically 5 to 7). After this, the commission sends the finalists out for public comment, which lasts for
10 days. Public comments can extend the process if negative feedback is received, as applicants can
respond to these comments. Once the mayor receives the recommendations from the nominating
commission, she has 30 days to make a final selection. Following the mayor's selection, the city council

Mr. Peters said at a minimum, there will be a delay of at least a month because Judge Romney is retiring

appoint temporary judges if needed.

Vice-Chair Whipple asked what kind of backlog a one-month gap would produce without having
someone in that role.

Mr. Peters said considering that Judge Romney mentioned around 1,000 cases a year earlier tonight, I
anticipate that the caseload will accumulate rapidly. The positive aspect is that the transition from mid-
December to mid-January is likely to be less busy due to the holidays and other factors. He said he did
not believe there would be a situation where there is nobody available to handle these cases, but it is a
matter of gaining approval from the council and coordinating the assistance required. He said
historically, in this district, there has been a willingness to provide support.

John Borget, Director of Administrative Services, discussed the list that is already in place to fill in when
Judge Romney has been unavailable. He said a resolution will be drafted to update names for interim.
He added that ReAnnun, the Justice Court Administrator, will make sure there is coverage.
