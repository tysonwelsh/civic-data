      UCA 10-9a-103(34) and UCA 17-27a-103(36)
      “Moderate Income Housing” means housing occupied or reserved for occupancy by
      households with a gross household income equal to or less than 80% of the median gross
      income for households of the same size in the county in which the [city/housing] is located.

                                             RECOMMENDATIONS:
      Please note that all calculations should account for moderate income housing affordability for
      the full range of incomes between 0% and 80% of the Area Median Income (AMI).
      Affordable housing is any housing unit whose gross monthly rent or mortgage payment plus
      utilities is equal to 30% or less than a household's gross monthly income at a particular
      income threshold. It is strongly recommended that cities and counties estimate housing
      affordability separately for homeowners and renters at each of the following targeted income
      thresholds: 0%-30% AMI, 30%-50% AMI, and 50%-80% AMI.

      Please note that both UCA 10-9a-103(41)(b) and UCA 17-27a-103(44)(b) require cities and
      counties to revise projections of their five year moderate income housing needs every two
      years. At a minimum this requirement includes: 1) A current estimate of the moderate
      income population, 2) An estimate of the existing moderate income housing supply, 3) an
      estimated rate of growth of both the population and the housing supply over the next five
      years. In addition to considering the affordable housing needs of homeowners and renters
      with household incomes between 0% and 80% AMI, cities and counties should also consider
      the housing needs of vulnerable populations which may include the elderly, the disabled,
      racial and ethnic minorities, the homeless, ex-offenders, refugees, households living in
      poverty, minimum wage workers, and any other population identified as having a need for
      moderate income housing.

                                        FOLLOWING THE BIENNIAL REVIEW
      The city or county's legislative body should adopt amendments to the moderate income
      housing element of the general plan every two years. According to UCA 10-9a-404 and UCA
      17-27a-404, a city or county's legislative body is to adopt a plan to provide a realistic
      opportunity to meet estimated needs for additional moderate income housing if long-term
      projections for land use and development occur. Once the city or county's legislative body
      has revised five year housing needs estimates and has conducted a biennial review of the
      moderate income housing element of the city or county's comprehensive general plan, the city
      or county's planning commission should prepare appropriate amendments to the moderate
      income housing element. Appropriate amendments should reflect the housing needs
      estimates and projections produced as part of the biennial review. The planning commission's
      recommended amendments should include valid strategies for resolving the moderate income
      housing issues identified in the biennial review. The city or county's legislative body should
      then adopt the recommended amendments proposed by the city or county's planning
      commission by resolution after a public hearing. It is imperative that the legislative
      body work closely with its planning commission to ensure that amendments to the
      moderate income housing element clearly state measurable goals and outline
      well-specified objectives for achieving those goals.

      The city or county's legislative body should adopt a new moderate income housing element
      every 5 years, as prepared by their planning commission. Adopting a new moderate income
      housing element every five to six years will ensure that the city or county's goals and
      objectives address changing housing needs. The city or county's legislative body should
      provide a period of public notice and comment prior to a public hearing, once the planning
      commission has prepared a new moderate income housing element. After considering public
      input and making appropriate revisions, the legislative body should then adopt the new
      moderate income housing element by resolution. Cities and counties are still required to
      report the findings of their biennial review of the previous moderate income housing element
      in the year that the legislative body adopts a new moderate income housing element to
      remain compliant with UCA 10-9a-408 and UCA 17-27-408.

      Utah's Government Records Access and Management Act requires all local government plans
      be publicly accessible. Local governments should publicly post a newly adopted moderate
      income housing plan element and its subsequent amendements as soon as possible. Posting
      revisions to the moderate income housing element of the general plan on the city or county's
      website every two years will significantly increase the public's access.

      Anytime a city or county amends or adopts a moderate income housing element of their
      general plan, it should immediately send a copy of it to the Housing and Community
      Development Division of the Utah Department of Workforce Services and to the regional
      Association of Governments to which it belongs. A newly adopted or amended version of the
      moderate income housing element must be submitted with the biennial moderate income
      housing element review report.

Introduction
Section 10-9a-408 of Utah Code requires that the City’s legislative body review, every two
years, the City’s moderate income housing plan, and it’s implementation, and provide a report
to the State (Department of Workforce Services). The report shall include the following:
           1. “Efforts made by the city to reduce, mitigate, or eliminate local regulatory
                barriers to moderate income housing;
           2. actions taken by the city to encourage preservation existing moderate income
                housing and development of new moderate income housing;
           3. progress made within the city to provide moderate income housing, as
                measured by permits issued for new units of moderate income housing; and
           4. efforts made by the city to coordinate moderate income housing plans and
                action with neighboring municipalities.”
                                                                       (U.C.A §10-9a-408(2))
This report is intended to fulfill Sandy City’s obligation regarding the biennial review of the
moderate income housing plan as required by Utah Code §10-9a-408.

SANDY CITY GENERAL PLAN: HOUSING ELEMENT
The City’s current housing element of the General Plan was adopted on January 8, 2013. The
Housing Element is based on housing being recognized “as a basic necessity of life for all
people…” (page 1) and the State Code requirement that the housing element “shall consider
the Legislature’s determination that cities shall facilitate a reasonable opportunity for a variety
of housing, including moderate income housing…” The Housing Element determined “that
more moderate income housing will be needed in the future.” (page 12) and established
housing goals based on the following categories: quality growth, maintenance, community,
interaction, special needs and fair housing, and moderate income housing growth.

Housing Element Goals
1. Quality Growth. Promote a stable and sustainable community by providing the opportunity
for a variety of housing types and densities.
    1.1 Develop infill options that complement existing housing stock and neighborhood
    characteristics.
    1.2 Offer a Transfer of Development Rights (TDR) program to encourage responsible
    development, increased densities, in order to preserve open space and natural resources
    within the City.
    1.3 Create an opportunity to preserve lands that are better utilized for pedestrian and
    bicycle trails, wilderness fire protection, etc.
    1.4 Ensure a range of housing options to accommodate an aging population and growth
    trends.
    1.5 Encourage various housing types and sizes including carriage homes,
    lofts, live-work spaces, and other options as appropriate.
    1.6 The City should encourage a range of housing opportunities
    targeted towards all segments of the community when considering
    new development and redevelopment.
2. Maintenance. Maintain and enhance neighborhood characteristics while allowing for
modification and renovation of existing residences.
    2.1 Allow for additions and modifications to existing housing stock.
    2.2 Encourage remodeling and rehabilitation of existing units where feasible and when the
    character of the building contributes to that of the neighborhood.
3. Community. Encourage the preservation, upkeep, and maintenance of existing housing.
    3.1 Encourage investment in the community by owners, landlords, and renters.
    3.2 Maintain and upgrade aging infrastructure and housing.
4. Interaction. New and existing housing should complement the non-residential uses
throughout the City and region.
    4.1 Promote a balance of jobs to housing within the City.
    4.2 Provide regional access to housing, employment, and commercial uses.

                                                                                                      4
5. Special Needs and Fair Housing. The City strives to provide a range of housing
opportunities for all residents, including those with special needs, and to eliminate
discrimination in housing availability.
    5.1 Support the observance of all applicable local, state, and federal laws regarding anti-
    discrimination practices in housing.
    5.2 Provide an environment in which housing and shelter are
    available to all residents.
6. Moderate Income Housing. The City should make every effort to provide opportunities for
housing and community involvement to persons of moderate income.
    6.1 Provide reasonable opportunities for a variety of housing, including moderate income
    housing, to meet the needs of people desiring to live in the Sandy Community.
    6.2 Ensure persons of moderate income can benefit from and fully participate in all aspects
    of neighborhood and community life.

REPORT REQUIREMENTS
There are many things that the City is doing to meet the City’s housing needs and address the
goals of the Housing Element. This section identifies programs and activities according to the
State-required reporting categories.

Efforts to reduce regulatory barriers
     Residential Conservation Overlay adopted to provide greater flexibility to both new
        developments and existing homes, specifically allowing reduced setbacks.
     Hearing officer implemented to hear reasonable accommodations for residential
        treatment facilities.
     The City has maintained relatively low development fees.
     The City is currently preparing the development code to more user-friendly and more
        easily searchable online.
     Master planning to identify areas appropriate to various housing types.
     The ZoomGrants systems has been implemented to provide a more user friendly
        administrative system to applicants and to be consistent with other municipalities in the
        region.

Actions to preserve existing and develop new moderate income housing
     The City, through the EDA, has partnered with the Canyons School District on a program
       to provide moderate income housing. EDA housing funds are used to purchase and
       prepare buildable lots that are then donated to the District. The District
     The CDBG housing programs fund programs that provide emergency home repairs,
       accessibility improvements, and rehabilitation loans to LMI households.

LEGISLATIVE REIVEW
On December 12, 2017, information and analysis included in this report were presented to the
City Council by way of a PowerPoint presentation during a work session. Prior to the meeting,
the Council had the opportunity to review the Housing Element of the City’s General Plan.
During the work session, staff specifically reviewed the goals of the Housing Element.
In general the Council appreciated the moderate income housing needs in the City. The Council
also identified some of the challenges in addressing those needs. For those most part, the
                                                                                             6
challenges identified by the Council align with the first regional impediment to fair housing
choice listed in the Regional Analysis of Impediments to Fair Housing Choice (May 2014), which
is “segregation due to concentrations of affordable housing: nimbyism, zoning, and land
prices.” (page 2) It was discussed that many of the impediments identified are associated with
the relatively small amount of vacant land remaining in the City.

The Council expressed a desire to address housing in greater detail in the future, with new
members of the Council, to ensure that the goals of the Housing Element effectively address
housing needs and to ensure that City regulations translate into results that advance the goals
of the Housing Element.
