G. ACTION ITEMS:

     1. APPROVE A MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING WITH WEBER SCHOOL DISTRICT TO
        DEVELOP A FORMAL COLLABORATIVE PARTNERSHIP BETWEEN THE CENTER OF
        EXCELLENCE AND WEBER ADULT EDUCATION.
            Melissa Freigang (Executive Director, Weber Prosperity Center of Excellence): In the past, zero percent of
            our families completed post-secondary education since 2017. Currently 23% of our families are pursuing their
            post-secondary.
            Madeline McDonald (Community Liaison, WSD/Adult Education): Weber Adult Education has been in
            Weber County for the last 60 years. 18,000 residents in the county today without a high school diploma or
            GED. Current enrollment of 714 students, with 599 actively involved in school. Mostly direct teaching with
            very little online teaching. National average of eight attempts for an adult learner to complete an adult
            education program. Currently, our program is tracking to 169 graduates, compared to 113 last year. We
            partner heavily with Weber Technical College with some classes on their campus. We also partner with Weber
            Community Action Partnership. Though part of Weber School District, 100% funded through Utah State
            Board of Education and community grants. Our programs track above state averages. The proposed MoU will
            allow identification of students dealing with chronic intergenerational poverty and connect them with those
            resources available in the community. Students come into the program averaging a 3rd grade level of reading.

            Ginny Thurgood, (Counseling Secretary): Everyone assumes the bad when looking at this program. There
            are reasons and stories for each person’s situation. We want to remove barriers to their education. One ESL
            student has taken all 27 direct teaching classes in April. Her house caught on fire and she lost her car. She
            walked from Clinton to attend class, in spite of what happened.

            Commissioner Froerer: I’m excited about these partnerships within our community.
            Commissioner Harvey: Thank you so much for education us this morning. I had no idea there was a Weber
            County Adult Education. I appreciate you caring enough to help those who want a little more.
            Chair Jenkins: I think this program has got better and better as time has gone on. I’m excited about the MoU.
         Commissioner Harvey moved to approve a Memorandum of Understanding with Weber School District to
         develop a formal collaborative partnership between the Center of Excellence and Weber Adult Education;
         Commissioner Froerer seconded.
         Commissioner Froerer – aye; Commissioner Harvey – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye
    2. APPROVE AN AGREEMENT WITH WEBER-MORGAN HEALTH DEPARTMENT TO DETECT
       AND MITIGATE THE SPREAD OF COVID-19 IN HOMELESS SERVICES SITES AND OTHER
       CONGREGATE LIVING FACILITIES.
         Melissa Freigang (Executive Director, Weber Prosperity Center of Excellence): As you know, when Covid
         hit we had to marshall all of our resources; a huge mobilization was required. 4 deaths out of 250 cases in first
         outbreak among homeless. No deaths on next two outbreaks. This grant from the Department of Health
         provides an FTE allowing continued efforts for Covid –related mitigation. Governor Cox said “the work in
         Weber County is the best kept secret in Utah”.
         Chair Jenkins: We appreciate the work done by Melissa and her understaffed department during that time.
         Commissioner Harvey moved to approve an agreement with Weber-Morgan Health Department to detect and
         mitigate the spread of covid-19 in homeless services sites and other congregate living facilities; Commissioner
         Froerer seconded.
         Commissioner Froerer – aye; Commissioner Harvey – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye

    3. APPROVE A FIRST READING OF AN ORDINANCE AMENDING FEES FOR THE WEBER
       COUNTY TRANSFER STATION AND OTHER SOLID WASTE FEES (SEC 16-2-9 SOLID WASTE
       FEES).
         Sean Wilkinson (Director, Community Development): Improve operational efficiencies and cover cost
         For services provided at the transfer station. Increases are necessary to cover effects of inflation, and increased
         costs to salaries, benefits, fuel and other operations costs. In addition, the county’s Solid Waste Transportation
         and Disposal contract with Republic Services calls for an annual 3% fee increase that was absorbed by the
         county last year. But due to additional fuel and transportation costs to Republic’s subcontractor for
         transportation, MBI, the 2022 annual increase is 7.9%, leading to July 1st tipping fee increase of $3.65 per ton
         to a total of $44.14 per ton. Other proposed cost increases include:
                 $5 for mattress and box springs, over two per load
                 Screened fine mulch will go from $15 to $20 per yard and from $4 to $5 per bag
                 Colored wood chips will go from $25 to $30 per yard and from $5 to $6 per bag

         Proposed Ordinance adds fee schedule for residential solid waste collection and disposal services. A
         delinquent payment fee is also being added; fees collected via the county’s property tax notice billing and
         collection system with fees ranging from $10 minimum to up to 25%, whichever is greater.
         Commissioner Froerer moved to approve the first reading of an Ordinance amending fees for the Weber County
         Transfer Station and Other Solid Waste Fees (Sec 16-2-9 Solid Waste Fees); Commissioner Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Froerer – aye; Commissioner Harvey – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye

    5. RATIFICATION OF THE 2022 WEBER COUNTY TAX SALE.
         April Hatfield (Clerk/Auditor Office): The County completed the 2022 Tax Sale on May 23 rd, 2022. Tax sales
         are only for recovering tax money owed; overages go to the former owners or to Utah state unclaimed funds.
         Special thanks to Roger Brunker for his expertise. 78 properties were eligible for tax sale at the beginning of
         the year compared to 95 in 2021 and 129 in 2020. Five properties were up for sale; all were abutting properties.
         Three of the five available properties were purchased by adjacent land owners. Clerk/Auditor’s office is not
         aware of any objections within the state mandated 10 day grievance period.

         Commissioner Froerer moved to ratify the 2022 Weber County Tax Sale; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Froerer – aye; Commissioner Harvey – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye
