2.      A presentation regarding the development application process. (20-225) (0:49:16)

Bill Peperone, Development Services Director, and Gary McGinn, Community and Neighborhood
Services Director, presented. Mr. Peperone reviewed a flowchart illustrating the development
application and review process. During plan review, staff provide applicants with red-lined
versions of their plans, rather than simply written comments. Additionally, staff are required to
identify any significant issues within the first two reviews; previously, developers might only
learn of a significant issue after they had invested significant funds and time to get the project to
that point. Mr. Peperone and Mr. McGinn noted another common area where developers may go
through several iterations of review; if a concept plan is inconsistent with the requested zone, the
applicant can move forward but staff would recommend denial based on such inconsistencies.

Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked whether there was anything else that could be improved to
make Provo a more development-friendly city and in turn to contribute toward lower housing
costs. Mr. Peperone suggested the City better utilize Open City Hall to obtain neighborhood
resident feedback on development applications; meetings have moved to a virtual format during
the pandemic anyway, and this would provide better information and flexibility. Councilors
discussed the idea, and many were supportive of this suggestion. Karen Tapahe, Community
Relations Coordinator, shared more information about neighborhood meetings. Mr. McGinn also
clarified the nature of some projects which required only an Administrative review and approval.
Councilor David Harding also shared comments on the development process. Councilors were
generally interested in further discussing any recommendations from the Administration on how
to streamline the development review process. Presentation only.

4.      A discussion about the renewal of the Urban Deer Program. (20-224) (1:45:05)

Camille Williams, Deputy City Attorney, presented. She highlighted past years’ data from the
urban deer program. The primary decision point for the Council was whether to continue funding
the urban dear program, or perhaps a scaled-back maintenance program as recommended by the
consultant. Hannah Salzl, Policy Analyst, shared information on the times of day, dawn and
dusk, when the program is most effective. Councilors shared comments and feedback.

Councilor David Sewell recommended appropriating funds for a maintenance program.
Councilor George Handley did not feel the data demonstrated a significant reduction in vehicle
accidents with deer. Mayor Michelle Kaufusi clarified the feedback she hoped to receive from
the Council. The Administration intended to move this budget expense into the Animal Control
budget, in order to dispense with lengthy debates every year about this relatively small sum of
money. Mr. Sewell noted that anecdotally, it seemed that Councilors receive fewer complaints
about property damage because of urban deer since the program had been instituted. Councilor
Bill Fillmore asked about the program budget; Ms. Williams explained that last year, $12,400
was used of the budgeted $15,000. There are four sites at a cost of $2500 each, with a charge of
$75 per deer, with authorization for up to 35 deer. Several Councilors liked the Mayor’s proposal
to incorporate the program into the Animal Control budget. Mr. Fillmore observed that without
the program, the problem might be much worse. Some suggested that it could be a potential area
of research for a BYU professor studying wildlife. Presentation only.

5.      An update on the utility billing errors that stem from the system conversion in July
        2020. (20-235) (2:14:28)

Wayne Parker, CAO, presented. Mr. Parker reviewed the Provo 360 project, which has replaced
the City’s outdated software with more efficient and reliable software systems. This project
involved many functions at the city, including finance, permitting, licensing, utility billing,
inventory, work management, human resources, e-government, and data analytics. Mr. Parker
noted the many improvements and efficiencies this project introduced to the City organization as
a whole and briefly reviewed the associated timeline. Mr. Parker explained the factors which
impacted the utility billing, noting that a large data and system conversion also took place at the
start of the new fiscal year; it was a perfect storm and in hindsight, these launches should have
been more appropriately staggered and tested. Additionally, many users already see their highest

upgrade had originally been scheduled for the spring but was delayed due to the pandemic. Mr.
Parker shared details and insight into the utility billing, including tiered rates and the variable

Councilor David Shipley referred to the days of service on the example bill and how this
correlated with the calendar months. Mr. Parker explained that the billing system self-calibrates
and tracks since the last meter reading. He explained that the tiers do not self-calibrate; where
this occurred for customers, those bills will be realigned in the appropriate tiers. Mr. Shipley also
asked about any programs available to residents on a fixed income to help even out their utility
payments throughout the year. Mr. Parker indicated that was available through Provo 311. There
are also many other features available to utility customers on myusage.provo.org. Mr. Parker
outlined the steps moving forward, including new audit procedures, waiving of late fees and
disconnects, payment arrangements, and meter testing to alleviate any concerns. Councilor David
Harding shared concerns he has heard from constituents about disproportionate bill increases that
do not seem to be explained by Mr. Parker’s presentation. Mr. Parker explained that most
outlying bills could be tied back to excessive dates of service and they have worked through
these issues on a case-by-case basis. Presentation only.

6.      A presentation about the CARES Act OIG Audit Guidelines (20-227) (2:59:12)

Kelsey Zarbock, Budget Analyst, presented. She shared an update on the CARES Act allocation
in which the City received $8.5 million from Utah County’s federal funds. The guidance and
requirements as to use of these funds has changed dramatically and rapidly, as recently as two
weeks ago. In response to a question about how payroll savings were handled for public safety,
Ms. Zarbock explained that the City intended to set aside those funds in a designated account so
that the expenditures were guaranteed in the event that an allocation had to be repaid due to a
future audit. Councilor David Shipley asked if the airport project had been eliminated from the
potential CARES Act use. Ms. Zarbock indicated that was the case; most of the funding was
being put toward public safety payroll. John Borget, Administrative Services Director, offered
more detail on this decision as well. Presentation only.
