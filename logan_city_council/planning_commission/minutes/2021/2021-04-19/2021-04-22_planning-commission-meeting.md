Thursday, April 22, 2021. Chair Dickinson called the meeting to order at 5:30 p.m.

Eduardo Ortiz, Sandi Goodlander, Jordy Guth

PUBLIC HEARING

 PC 21-015 Logan City-Center Block Public Plaza Project [Design Review Permit] Mike
 DeSimone/Logan City, authorized agent/owner, request to construct a new 17,000 SF Public
 Plaza containing a portable ice rink, covered stage, restrooms, storage, splash pad, fire pits,
 entry features (east & west sides), shade structures, landscaping, lighting and street trees;
 demolish 47-67 N. Main buildings; interior/exterior renovation of 41-45 N. Main Street
 buildings and upgrade utility services where necessary located at 41-67 North Main in the
 Town Center (TC-1) zone. 06-025-0011, 06-025-0009, 06-028-0015, 06-028-016. (Ellis
 Neighborhood)

STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a new public plaza at 41-67 North Main street directly
across from the tabernacle. The proposal is to demolish buildings 47, 55, and 67 North Main
creating an opening for a new public plaza. Buildings 41 and 45 will have the exterior remodeled,
and the interior demoed for future tenant improvements. The two buildings are intended to contain
restaurants/retail uses on the main floor and office uses on the upper floors. The new 17,000 SF
Public Plaza will consist of a performance stage, portable ice rink in the winter and a water
feature/splash pad in the summer, new restrooms, fire pits, shade structures, flower planters, brick
gateway features, metal archway elements, and new lighting. The entire project area remains
within the existing footprint of the five buildings and leaves the interior surface parking lot
untouched. For typical plaza programming, the rear parking lot will satisfy the parking demand.
For max capacity events, there are approximately 2,900 off-street and 900 on-street parking stalls
throughout the larger downtown area. Commissioner Dickinson asked about parking requirements
for a park/splash pad and if there was a capacity limit for the ice rink and splash pad. R. Holley
said that for a splash pad the requirement for parking is based on spatial occupancy. The ice rink
capacity can be controlled by how many skates are rented, and the splash pad will most likely be
managed by on-site staff. Commissioner Goodlander asked about the water usage and the plan
for using recycled water. Mayor Daines said that decision has not been made yet, if the water is
recycled it must be treated, which could be more difficult to maintain.


PROPONENT: Mayor Daines said this plaza is part of the downtown revitalization, to make
downtown more pedestrian-friendly and draw people in and give them a gathering place. The HPC
has approved the demolition and City Council has approved the funding. The sale of buildings 41
and 45 will defray some of the cost of the project. Commissioner Goodlander asked about the
ongoing maintenance costs. Mayor Daines said that the Parks and Recreation department will be
handling the programming and maintenance of the plaza, and there are funds available for that.
Commissioner Croshaw talked about the encasement of the ice rink and how long it is intended to
last. Mayor Daines said that the rink should last 20-30 years. Commissioner Ortiz said that it will
be a successful project and asked if there were any follow-up projects scheduled? Mayor Daines
said she would like to follow up with the improvement of the center block walkway, possibly add
some public art.

PUBLIC: Gary Saxton representing the Logan Downtown Alliance stated that they are in support of
the project.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Goodlander said it is a beautiful project with ample parking.

 PC 21-018 Riverside RV Park Rezone [Zone Change] Jacob Howell/Rex Petersen,
 authorized agent/owner, request a rezone of 4.78 acres from Recreation (REC) to Mixed
 Residential Medium (MR-20) located at 447 W 1700 S; TIN 02-089-0028. (Woodruff
 Neighborhood)

STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a rezone of 4.7 acres at 440 West 1700 South from
REC to MR-20. The entire property is considered a floodplain area and development would
require fill material raising finish floor heights above flood levels. These land grading changes
would alter the flood dynamics of the property with the potential of adverse impacts to downstream
properties during flood events.


PROPONENT: Jacob Howell said the MR-20 zone would act as a buffer between the industrial
zone to the south and the NR-6 zone to the north. This parcel offers a lot of things the city is
looking for in MR-20 areas, compatibility, amenities, location transportation, and employment.
There are also several trails nearby, a dog park, a golf course, and soccer fields. Commissioner
Croshaw asked how residents would get to the trails that are across the highway. J Howell said
that for now, the plan was to just connect to the trail that runs along the south of Countryside
Manor for access to the soccer fields. Commissioner Guth asked if the floodplain was the same on
the north and south of the river. R. Holley said he was not sure of the exact elevations for the
north of the river versus the south. B. Young spoke about the surrounding streams in this area that
can back up and cause flooding.

PUBLIC: Rex Peterson, owner of the RV Park said if the railroad bridge is maintained and clear
from debris, the river doesn’t back up and flood.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Dickinson asked about the zoning history of this property. R.
Holley said the property was zoned residential in 1985, commercial in 1989, and recreation in
2000. The RV park was established in the late ’80s. Commissioner Dickinson stated that the city
did deem the property commercial at one point. Commissioner Guth stated that it is not prudent to
have high-density use in a flood plain area, it causes a lot of problems and expenses for the
taxpayers. Commissioner Goodlander stated she agreed with commissioner Guth.

MOTION: Commissioner Goodlander moved to deny a Conditional Use Permit as outlined in PC
21-018 with the findings for denial as listed below. Commissioner Croshaw seconded the motion.

FINDINGS FOR DENIAL
1. The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP) designates this property as REC.
2. This property has been zoned REC for over 21 years.
3. The environmental constraints on this property make permanent structures potentially
   problematic.
4. The permitted current land use is well suited for this location.
5. The project met the minimum public noticing requirements of the Land Development Code and
   the Municipal Code.
6. Engineering Department Finding. Current Flood Insurance Rate Maps show 100 % of this
   property in Special Flood Hazard Area (referred to as the 1% Chance Flood Area and
   previously the 100-year flood area), with some areas in a designated floodway. A proposed
   Physical Map Revision (PMR) being submitted to FEMA will show the majority of the proposed
   rezone area to be in a floodway. If the PMR mapping is accepted by FEMA, there cannot be
   any building in a floodway area. Any modifications proposed by the developer to the floodplain
