Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       In an effort to leverage the collective resources for the prevention, preemption,
       deterrence and investigation of terrorist acts that affect United States interests, and
       to disrupt and prevent terrorist acts and apprehend individuals. Law enforcement
       agencies of the greater Salt Lake Valley, State and Federal agencies have united
       efforts and resources to form the Joint Terrorism Taskforce (JTTF). The Police
       Department seeks to assign one (1) investigator to this position. This agreement
       clarifies the responsibilities of participating agencies to establish the groundwork
       for the administration and operation of the taskforce.

       This task force is authorized to coordinate intelligence, investigative, and
       operational responses to various criminal, cyber and national security threats,
       attacks, and intrusions pursuant to various statutory and executive authorities.

The City Council will consider Resolution 21-37 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
March 16, 2021 at 6:30 P.M.

COMMUNICATIONS
A.  STREET LIGHTING FEE
    Russ Willardson, Public Works Director, presented a PowerPoint presentation
    summarized as follows:

             Following is a recommendation for long-term funding to cover the cost of
             operating, maintaining, replacing, and upgrading the City’s street lighting
             system.

             What is proposed?
             A new street lighting fee of $3.00 per month per single family residential
             equivalent unit is proposed. Each single family and duplex unit would be
             charged $3.00 per month as an additional line item on the current City utility
             bill. Non-residential properties would be charged $3.00 per month per
             Equivalent Residential Unit (ERU), with one ERU equaling 80 feet of street
             frontage.
MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – MARCH 9, 2021
                      -6-



      Why is this fee needed?
      The current street lighting budget covers only minimal maintenance and
      energy costs for the City’s 7,488 streetlights. Additional personnel and
      equipment are needed to reduce downtimes on pole knockdowns,
      underground repairs, and lamp replacements. Currently 97 lights are
      inoperable and awaiting repair because of traffic accidents or underground
      wiring problems.

      Additional funds are also needed to begin a long-term replacement of the
      existing system. Many of the current street lighting poles are at or beyond
      their projected service life.

      Direct-buried fiberglass poles and underground wiring. These 2,592 Town
      and Country style lights were installed in the mid-1990’s, making them now
      25 years old. These low-cost lights were originally installed and owned by
      Rocky Mountain Power with direct-buried fiber-glass poles and direct-
      buried power lines, with no conduit, points of power disconnect or junction
      boxes.

      While the Town and Country poles are still intact, replacement will be
      needed in the next 20 years. Fiberglass poles have an expected life of 20-
      30 years. These fiberglass poles are now oxidizing and unraveling.
      Painting these poles to extend their life is not possible with the current street
      lighting staff of two.

      With streetlighting power lines buried directly in the ground, finding and
      isolating problems is often impossible. It is often more cost efficient to
      replace all the underground wiring and add junction boxes and disconnect
      points to facilitate future maintenance. Essentially, it becomes a rebuild of
      the entire underground system.

      One example is Lake Park Boulevard. Here 42 out of 61 lights are out due
      to underground wiring problems. The estimated cost to replace the
      underground wiring is $250,000. There is no funding available for major
      renovations to the system such as this.

      Wooden poles. There are 2,843 existing lights on wooden poles installed
      prior to City incorporation in 1980, in areas with overhead power. These
      poles are at least 40 years into their expected life of 50 to 80 years.
      Although none of the poles have failed, large cracks are visible, and some
MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – MARCH 9, 2021
                      -7-


      are beginning to show deterioration at the base. Current resources are
      inadequate to try to extend pole life with more maintenance.

      How much revenue will the proposed fee raise?
      A $3.00 per ERU street lighting fee would generate approximately $1.28
      million, with 70% coming from residential units.

      What other funding options exist?
      Street lighting in West Valley City was first funded by a property tax
      increase in the mid-1990s. The current budget of $491,000 comes from the
      General Fund. Current General Fund revenues are insufficient to fund a
      program of this magnitude.

      A $2.00 per month street lighting fee combined with the current $491,000
      General Fund budget would be equivalent to a $3 per month fee with no
      General Fund monies.

      Installation costs for streetlights in new developments are borne by the
      developer. Transportation funds are used for new street lighting on road
      projects. These practices reduce the capital construction needs for street
      lighting.

      How will new street lighting revenues be used?
      Additional personnel and equipment to improve operation and maintenance
      of the existing system is the top priority. One additional street lighting
      technician with a bucket truck and additional materials would be added,
      with an initial annual cost of $315,000 and an ongoing cost of $165,000.
      An old bucket truck would also be replaced at cost of $150,000.

      A capital budget for major repairs, replacements and additions to the system
      is also needed. The proposed $3.00 per month fee provides about $500,000
      annually for capital projects.
            This capital budget would cover replacement of 70-80 lights per
               year, (including poles, wiring replacement and junction boxes), or
               an area about the size of Operation My Hometown (OMH).
            An additional 120 lights are needed for streets with no current
               street lighting. The estimated cost is $900,000.
            A $3 street lighting fee would not provide sufficient capital for a
               major pole replacement program. It would, however, provide
               resources for a pole maintenance program. The estimated cost to
               paint or treat all poles is roughly $500,000.

             This is a minimal capital budget, but it’s a start and will provide
              means to address the most pressing problem areas or focus areas
              like OMH. With this funding level, it will take over 30 years to
              replace just the direct-buried fiberglass poles.

      Implementation
      No additional administrative costs are necessary. Street Lighting Fees can
      be added to existing City garbage and storm water utility bills. All property
      owners would be charged a fee including property-tax exempt parcels.

      Lamps in all streetlights have been converted to LED for energy savings.
      These lamps can be reused in new fixtures when poles and fixtures are
      replaced.

      What other cities charge street lighting fees?
      The following cities have chosen to fund their street lighting needs with a
      street lighting fee: Salt Lake City, Sandy, West Jordan, Layton, and Orem.
      The fees range from $1.71 per month in West Jordan to $3.73 per month in
      Salt Lake City, with an average fee of $2.73.
