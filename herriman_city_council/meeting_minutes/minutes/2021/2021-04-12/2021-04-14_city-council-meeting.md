identified the interactive map available on the Unified Fire Authority’s website. Interim City Manager Thomas

    2.3. Laguna-Malibu Development Discussion - Blake Thomas, Community Development
         Director
Community Development Director Blake Thomas oriented the Council as to the location of the proposed
Laguna-Malibu development and observed the limitations in the area including access, water supply, hillside
overlay zone, and abutting Camp Williams. The anticipated land use currently allowed for single family
residential development, resort recreational development and open space. The zoning of the property would
allow for a maximum number of 420 single family residential units; however, the hillside overlay and the
regulations in the FR-2.5 zone would severely limit the acreage where development may occur.

Director Thomas relayed his understanding the developer would like to request the Council to consider
allowing density for this area to be constructed closer to Mountain View Corridor. Mayor Watts clarified the
developer did not want to lose any density.

    2.5. Public Works and Public Safety facility discussion - Justun Edwards, Public Works
April 14, 2021 – City Council Minutes                                                   Page 4 of 9
         Director
Public Works Director Justun Edwards explained as the City had experienced rapid growth it had caused the
need to increase the work force and equipment to meet the demands of the community. Also, the Police and
Animal Services divisions have also been brought in house to serve the residents which has put a strain on
current facilities. He noted the current Public Works facility has served the City well and it has outgrown the
intended purpose and would not allow staff to effectively perform many day-to-day functions. Director
Edwards turned the time over to Police Chief Troy Carr to address the need of the Public Safety building.

Chief Carr acknowledged the financial strain adding a second building would be to the community at the
current time, and recommended the Council not move forward with the construction of the Public Safety
building. He opined the allocated space in the proposed Public Works building could be revamped to cover
all faucets necessary for the department to function appropriately. He suggested future buildings could be
substations as there would be a future need to have smaller locations in a couple different areas of the
community for response time. Chief Carr noted he would hope to maintain the police records division at City
Hall for customer service purposes. Deputy Chief Cody Stromberg added City Hall was not constructed initially
to house an entire Police Department as it was intended to be a substation where storage facilities were available
in other areas of the county under the Unified Police Department model.

    2.6. Discussion of the proposed Storm Water Ordinance amendment - Jonathan Bowers, City
         Engineer
City Engineer Jonathan Bowers explained the City created the Storm Sewer Utility Ordinance in 2020 and since
the State of Utah Department of Environmental Quality, Division of Water Quality, issued an updated
stormwater permit to the Jordan Valley Municipalities which includes Herriman City. The permit required cities
to ensure legal authority exists to control discharge and effectively prohibit illicit and non-stormwater
discharges through ordinance or other regulatory mechanism. He proposed the revision to add escalating
enforcement procedures to the existing code to provide greater clarification to the guidance regarding the
regulation of stormwater with the City.

     2.7. Discussion pertaining to the Emergency Declaration relating to signage, delinquent utility
          accounts, and business licensing - Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney
City Attorney Andrizzi explained in the spring of 2020, the City issued a declaration of local emergency related
to the COVID-19 pandemic. The City Council ratified the declaration on April 8, 2020 by resolution.
Subsequently, the Council also adopted two additional resolutions. First, suspending the City’s collection of
all business license renewal fees which would otherwise come due during May 1, 2020 to April 30, 2021. Second,
reduced the City’s water reconnection fee from $75 to $35 and suspended all delinquent water account fees.

Attorney Andrizzi recalled a recent discussion where the Council expressed desire to see additional waiver of
business license renewal fees with a maximum of up to two waivers. He asked the Council if there were any
questions or if they would like to have any modifications to the proposed resolution. There were none.

    2.8. Flag Lot Ordinance Review - Michael Maloy, City Planner

    10.3.  Approval of the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District Contract - Justun Edwards,
           Public Works Director
Public Works Director Edwards offered a background on the Jordan Valley Conservancy District Contract to
purchase water. The contract would commit Herriman to purchase a minimum of 5,867 acre feet annually and
would allow for an overage of 20-percent for the minimum and zone B water which would set the maximum
purchase amount at 6,907 acre feet annually. If water is available, the amount could be exceeded with prior
written consent by Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District at a higher tier 2 rate.

Councilmember Shields moved to approve the execution of the Herriman City Water Purchase Agreement with Jordan Valley
Water Conservancy District and authorize the Interim City Manager to execute the same. Councilmember Ohrn seconded the
motion.
