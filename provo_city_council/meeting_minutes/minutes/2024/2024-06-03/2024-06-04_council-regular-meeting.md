2.         A resolution attesting to the review of the Municipal Wastewater Planning Program
           (MWPP) Survey - 2024 in accordance with the Utah Pollutant Discharge Elimination System
           Permit (UT0021717) (24-048) 0:21:41

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Resolution 2024-29, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

David Torgersen, Wastewater Manager, presented on the completion of the survey for the calendar year
2023. He said the survey, completed a month ahead of the April 15 deadline, is a self-evaluation that
covers financial systems, capital improvement projects, the wastewater collection system, and the
treatment plant. He highlighted that the survey helps assess and manage the system, noting that there
were no significant overflows last year.

Councilor Christensen raised concerns about the survey, noting that key questions regarding the
establishment of a repair and replacement sinking fund, the funding of the sewer system through

answered "no." He asked for further explanation, as these issues seemed concerning.

Mr. Torgersen acknowledge the concern and said that during budget discussions, this issue had not been
thoroughly addressed. He noted that when reviewing the master plans and the projected needs for
upcoming projects over the next 20 years, the anticipated rates and fund balances are not sufficient to
meet those future needs.

6.         An ordinance amending the Zone Map classification of real property, located at 2662 West
           Lakeview Parkway, from Open Space, Preservation, and Recreation (OSPR) Zone to the
           Community Shopping Center (SC2) Zone - Provo Bay Neighborhood (PLRZ20240059)
           0:29:12

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2024-29, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Keith Morey, Assistant Director of Development Services, discussed Provo City's approach to managing
its revenue stream, emphasizing the need for creativity due to the city's limited tax base, as 52% of
property in Provo is tax-exempt. He highlighted the regional sports complex project, initiated in 2020,
which aims to be more than just a park—it is expected to have a significant economic impact. The
southwest corner of the complex was always intended for commercial use to support the sports park
and generate additional revenue. Mr. Morey emphasized the importance of creating consistent revenue
streams, noting that the sports complex and adjacent commercial opportunities could bring in an
estimated $30-40 million annually to the local economy, primarily through tourism. This commercial
area is expected to provide services for visitors, reducing the need for travel through nearby
neighborhoods and minimizing traffic impacts. The location is strategically designed to handle large
visitor numbers, with infrastructure such as directional signage and parking restrictions planned to
manage traffic flow efficiently. He also noted that the commercial corner was not part of the park’s
water conservation plan, as it was always intended to be a separate entity. The Parks and Recreation
Department has thoughtfully planned for the long-term operation and maintenance of the facility,
considering how the commercial area could contribute to the city’s budget. Although a deal for the
property is not yet finalized, the department is actively working to ensure that the sports complex and
surrounding area remain a productive and beneficial asset to Provo.

Councilor Bogdin asked Mr. Morey why the City wants to develop this now instead of waiting to see if
parking will be an issue. She added that parking is the biggest concern of her constituents.

Mr. Morey responded stating that the facility is designed with 81 to 86 parking stalls per soccer field,
exceeding the national standard of 70 stalls. This provides 200 to 250 more stalls than required. He
mentioned that there is flexibility to add a few more parking spots if needed. Mr. Morey emphasized
that the commercial area on the five acres was not intended for additional parking but to support
economic development by reducing traffic flow into neighborhoods. By providing services like gas and
snacks south on Lakeview Parkway, the goal is to prevent visitors from traveling north into residential
areas.

Councilor Whipple asked if the timelines would align between the park and this project.

Mr. Morey discussed the challenge of coordinating the opening of the regional sports complex with
commercial development. He acknowledged the "chicken and egg" situation where commercial
developers need justification for their investment, often requiring a certain level of activity in the area to
attract tenants. While the sports complex is nearing completion, Parks and Rec does not yet have a
tournament schedule to drive early demand. Mr. Morey expressed a wish that the complex and
commercial developments could open simultaneously but recognized that the timing might be
staggered as the complex becomes operational and developers secure tenants.

Chair MacKay opened the item for public comment.

Ms. Tiffany Laymans, of the Provo Bay Neighborhood, shared that residents are generally in favor of the
park and support the planned changes. However, she expressed concerns about potential traffic and
parking issues, drawing a parallel to problems experienced at Amelia Earhart Elementary with overflow
parking spilling into nearby areas. She emphasized that while the neighborhood is excited about the
park, they want to see it fully operational before making further changes. They are particularly
concerned about parking on collector streets and would appreciate clear no-parking signs to manage
potential issues. Overall, they are supportive but seek assurance that traffic and parking will be managed
and enforced.

Abby Owens, Destination Sales Manager of Explore Utah Valley, shared that she has been actively
engaging with tournament organizers who are interested in booking the new sports park. She confirmed
that the park's design and parking capacity are well regarded, even for large groups. Ms. Owens
highlighted the need for nearby services like restaurants, convenience stores, and gas stations, as
visitors will want to spend money in the community while attending events. Overall, she praised Provo
Parks and Recreation for their effective planning and expressed confidence in the park’s ability to meet
demand and attract business.

Councilor Garrett asked Ms. Owens to provide more details about her discussions with tournament
organizers interested in booking the park, particularly regarding any interest in pickleball events.

Ms. Owens reported having multiple discussions with major pickleball organizations, including the
Professional Pickleball Association and USA Pickleball, who are excited about the Sports Park and the
potential for 45 pickleball courts. The National Collegiate Pickleball Association also plans to visit the
park this summer. There is strong interest from these groups in using the park for events.

With no other public comment, Chair MacKay invited council discussion.

Councilor Bogdin noted that residents often resist losing street parking. She suggested that the
surrounding neighborhoods should solidify their stance on parking issues, especially concerning the 900-
foot buffer and restrictions on street parking, before presenting these concerns again to the council.

With no other council discussion, Chair MacKay called for a vote.

     Vote:        The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, Handley,
                  Hoban, MacKay, and Whipple in favor.

8.         An ordinance amending the Zone Map classification of certain properties in the Oak Hills,
           Indian Hills, and Sherwood Hills neighborhoods, to add the Transfer of Development
           Rights-Sending (TDR-S) Overlay Zone (PLRZ20240127) 0:53:59

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2024-31, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Aaron Ardmore, Planning Supervisor, presented. He addressed a question that was asked in the work
session by Councilor Handley regarding certain properties not listed on the front page of the staff
report. He said these properties, owned by Steven Grow, are in an A-1 zone and under Greenbelt tax.
The omission was likely due to software limitations, which cut off additional property numbers. These
properties are included in Exhibit A and would be part of the zone change if approved. He explained that
the request follows ordinance 1433.B, which allows property owners of hazardous properties to sell
development rights. This initiative, modeled after practices in cities like Mapleton and Park City, aims to
address properties identified as high or extreme risk. It provides owners the option to sell development
rights to other areas in the city, while placing a conservation easement on their land to prevent future
development. He added that owners are not obligated to sell their rights and can still develop the
properties if they choose. The goal is to ensure safer development practices and preserve hillside areas.

Chair MacKay opened the item for public comment. With no comments or council discussion, she called
for a vote.

      Vote:        The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, Handley,
                   Hoban, MacKay, and Whipple in favor.
