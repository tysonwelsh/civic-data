       e. Ordinance No. 25-11 Station Area Plan for the 5600 West Old Bingham Highway
          Station
          Senior Planner Tayler Jensen introduced the 5600 West Old Bingham Highway Station
          Area and showed designs for the South Jordan Downtown Daybreak Station Area Plan
          (SAP) for context and comparison. He introduced Emily Burrowes and Chris Geddes with
          Design Workshop, who would participate in the presentation remotely.

           Ms. Burrowes explained proposed plans for circulation in the 5600 West SAP, including
           wide sidewalks, crosswalks, and traffic calming measures along the Brickline. The Station
           Area along the West Jordan and South Jordan border would function as a transition zone.
           Responding to a question from Chair Lamb, Mr. Jensen said UTA did not currently have
           plans to close the 5600 West Station. Ms. Burrowes said the proposed SAP would include
           a transformation of Old Bingham Highway to revitalize what was currently a heavily
           trafficked road into a welcoming, pedestrian-friendly corridor that would seamlessly
           connect surrounding areas to the Station Area.

           Ms. Burrowes said the central area of the 5600 West SAP would be mixed-use with a
           commercial-lined main street. The south area would have a residential focus, and the
           north area would have a commercial and entertainment focus. Ms. Burrowes reported
           results of a public survey (166 responses).

           Council Member Green commented that the SAP extended a little into South Jordan and
           asked if South Jordan would also be doing an SAP for the 5600 West area. Mr. Jensen
           responded South Jordan would not be doing an SAP for the area. Council Member Green
           said he would be comfortable if the proposed Brickline road was closed to automobile
           traffic. Council Member Shelton said he agreed. Council Member Shelton asked how long
           it would take for the proposed plans to come to fruition. Mr. Jensen explained that because
           of limited opportunities for automobile traffic to cross the Trax line, vehicle traffic in the
           Brickline area was necessary for adequate circulation. He spoke of the potential to install
           gates to close the area off to traffic during events.

           City Administrator Korban Lee said the 5600 West SAP, more than any other SAP in West
           Jordan, was highly reliant on a single property owner. He said the plan could happen
           rather quickly when the one property owner decided to relocate or reinvest. Mr. Lee said


City Council Meeting Minutes                  Tuesday, March 11, 2025                              Page 5
           the property owner was not yet sure of their plans, and emphasized it was a good time to
           solidify the City’s vision for the site.

           Council Member Bloom said the proposed plans would obviously bring more traffic into
           the area and asked if the proposed parking would be adequate for the increased traffic.
           Council Member Bloom asked if the SAP included a goal percentage of the residential
           component to be designated for affordable housing. Mr. Jensen said the plan anticipated
           shared parking between the office and residential components, and exceeded the parking
           recommended by the Urban Land Institute (ULI). He said parking would always be a
           concern and would need to be evaluated as the project progressed. Mr. Geddes said they
           did not believe structure parking would be necessary based on current numbers.

           Mr. Jensen said there were different funding mechanisms residential developers could
           use, and the funding mechanisms would stipulate as to affordable housing. He said he was
           sure there would be a moderate-income housing component, but did not know what it
           would be at that time.

           Council Member Bedore said he liked the idea of closing the Brickline to automobile traffic
           and asked if the plan could be designed to accommodate a road for automobile traffic,
           leaving the Brickline closed to cars. Council Member Bedore said he supported family-
           focused entertainment, but suggested the City should consider including adult-centered
           entertainment options as well. He said he enjoyed Area 15 in Las Vegas with activities like
           golf simulators, axe throwing, and museums. Mr. Jensen said the planned entertainment
           area within the SAP would definitely accommodate such uses.

           Council Member Jacob suggested putting vehicle traffic to one side of the proposed
           Brickline cross-section, leaving an extra-wide pedestrian plaza area to achieve the best of
           both worlds. Mr. Geddes commented that vehicle traffic was necessary for commercial
           uses. He said the scale of development versus the scale of pedestrian space was important.
           Mr. Geddes suggested the type of pedestrian space the Council referred to would work
           surrounded by five or six-story buildings, but probably not with the proposed two-story
           buildings. He encouraged the Council to make sure they understood how public space
           came together.

           Chair Lamb opened a public hearing at 8:30 pm.
           Comments:
           Rob Bennett, West Jordan resident, expressed appreciation for the evolving plan and said
           he believed it was a plan the citizens of West Jordan could get behind. He expressed
           support for the proposed SAP.
           Chair Lamb closed the public hearing at 8:31 pm.

           Council Member Green spoke of the need to partner with UTA for events and activities,
           and said he believed the proposed SAP was a good concept. Vice Chair Whitelock thanked
           staff for the work and said she believed the proposed plan was a great base.

       f. Ordinance No. 25-10 amending City Code Section 13-8-23(B) regarding Balanced
          Housing, and enacting a new article 13-5O Corridor Zone
          Senior Planner Tayler Jensen said the proposed ordinance included a new hybrid form-
          based zoning ordinance, Corridor Zone (CZ), for Redwood Corridor, and a text amendment
          to City Code Section 13-8-23(B) granting Corridor Zone developments as an exception to
          the Balanced Housing Ordinance. Mr. Jensen showed properties that would potentially be
          able to use to the proposed CZ. The Planning Commission reviewed the proposed
          ordinance and forwarded a positive recommendation.

          Mr. Jensen said the CZ would only be available along Redwood Road, and would be
          governed by a master development agreement, with maximum floor area ratio (FAR) and
          height determined by the City Council. He explained proposed setbacks, proposed rear
          parking, basic design principles, and amenity and open space requirements (including a
          fee-in-lieu option).

          Council Member Green asked if charging both park impact fees and fee-in-lieu of open
          space would fall under exaction. City Attorney Josh Chandler said he did not have any
          concerns regarding exaction. Mr. Jensen said the park fee-in-lieu was essentially a density
          buy-up. Mr. Jensen said he previously worked in a community that used the option of fee-
          in-lieu of open space.

          Chair Lamb opened a public hearing at 8:53 pm.
          Comments:
          None
          Chair Lamb closed the public hearing at 8:53 pm.

          Council Member Green said he was uncomfortable with the fee-in-lieu proposal because
          of experience with a previous development. He did not like the idea of providing buy-up
          points for garbage cans and had concerns that proposed lot and bulk standards would
          essentially require a two-story building along Redwood Road. Council Member Green said
          he would like someone to be able to build a one-story building on Redwood Road.

          Council Member Bloom said she was comfortable with everything except the fee-in-lieu
          option because of the many variables involved with development. Responding to a
          question from Council Member Shelton, Mr. Jensen said the CZ was a zone for which

City Council Meeting Minutes                Tuesday, March 11, 2025                            Page 7
          developers would need to apply. He said staff did not have concerns about the lot and bulk
          standards but would also not be concerned if they were removed. Council Member Shelton
          said he did not feel developers would be forced to build at least two-stories on Redwood
          Road because they would not have to apply for the zone if it did not fit their needs. Council
          Member Jacob said he agreed that the zone would be a tool, not a requirement. Council
          Member Jacob said he was comfortable with the ordinance as proposed.

          Council Member Green expressed the opinion that haphazardly developing Redwood
          Road would not provide anything better than the city already had. He said he would like
          to make the ordinance broader and better. Council Member Bedore said he believed the
          proposed ordinance would move the city closer to where they wanted to be in the next 20
          years.

           MOTION:      Chair Lamb moved to APPROVE Ordinance No. 25-10 amending City
                        Code Section 13-8-23(B) regarding Balanced Housing and enacting a
                        new article 13-5O Corridor Zone.
                        Council Member Jacob seconded the motion.

          Council Member Shelton asked if the Council could simply rezone the entire corridor all at
          once. Mr. Jensen explained that with a blanket rezone, the Council would lose the ability
          to negotiate for the desired high-quality development through master development
          agreements. Mr. Jensen expressed the opinion that the most beautiful cities in the world
          developed as patchwork over time. He said incremental development was considered a
          beneficial way to develop lasting communities.

          Council Member Jacob said he was comfortable approving the ordinance to get the zone
          on the books and put the tool in the toolbox. Vice Chair Whitelock said it was important to
          make sure it was the right tool for the job.

          The vote was recorded as follows:
           YES:       Zach Jacob, Chad Lamb, Bob Bedore, Kent Shelton
           NO:        Pamela Bloom, Kelvin Green, Kayleen Whitelock
           ABSENT:
          The motion Passed 4-3.

 7. BUSINESS ITEMS
       a. Resolution No. 25-009 approving amendments to Council Policies and Procedures
          Policy Analyst & Public Liaison Warren Hallmark presented proposed amendments to
          Council Policies and Procedures regarding town hall meetings, pre-registration for online
          meeting participation, a one-hour limit for the Council Meeting public comment period,
          and revisions to Council committees, with grammatical and reference error corrections.

           Responding to a question from Chair Lamb, Mr. Hallmark said pre-registration would be
           required for both online and in-person public comment. Council Member Jacob pointed
           out the language did not specify that a comment card must be filled out in advance, it only


City Council Meeting Minutes                 Tuesday, March 11, 2025                             Page 8
           specified a comment card must be filled out. Council Member Jacob emphasized he would
           want individuals to be able to make a comment spontaneously during the course of public
           comments if desired. Council Member Bedore asked if those participating remotely would
           have the same type of opportunity to provide un-planned public comment during the
           course of the public comment period and asked about multiple people remotely watching
           and participating in a meeting from the same computer.

           Mr. Hallmark said the requirement was not meant to be inconvenient or dissuade
           individuals from participating. The intention was to ensure contact information was
           collected for Council meeting minutes and possible follow up. Mr. Hallmark said the
           language could be modified. Council Office Director Alan Anderson said public comment
           cards could be placed both at the lectern and on a table at the back of Council Chambers.

           Council Member Shelton spoke of individuals who may want to give anonymous public
           comment, and said he did not think individuals should be required to provide their name.
           He suggested that requiring identification might be limiting free speech. Mr. Anderson
           said he did not think it would be possible to validate the identity of everyone who spoke
           during the public comment period, but said he believed citizens who had something
           meaningful they wanted to say to the Council would provide their name and address
           willingly.

           Council Member Green stated the Open Public Meetings Act (OPMA) required the Council
           to include the name of commenters and substance of the comments in the minutes of their
           meetings. He said he believed the Council had to collect names. Council Member Green
           said he was comfortable with requiring a comment card for in-person comment and
           requiring pre-registration for remote public comment.

           Mr. Chandler said he agreed with Council Member Green’s assessment of OPMA in terms
           of collecting names of those who commented in meetings. He suggested removing the
           prefix “pre” from pre-registration to simply require registration for public comment. Vice
           Chair Whitelock said she agreed with removing “pre”.

           Vice Chair Whitelock said she believed a definition of town hall meeting needed to be
           added to the document to clarify that town hall meetings would not be used as a campaign
           method. She said she did not intend to vote in favor of the proposed resolution because
           she believed the proposed changes were too limiting for multiple methods of public
           comment. Vice Chair Whitelock said she believed the one-hour time limit for the public
           comment period was not necessary.

           Council Member Bedore suggested two Council Members should be needed to call a town
           hall meeting, consistent with requiring support of two Council Members to add an item
           to a meeting agenda. Council Member Bedore said he was against the proposed one-hour
           time limit for public comment and supported removing “pre” from pre-registration.

           Council Member Green said he believed requiring two Council Members to call a town
           hall meeting was too close to calling an official meeting of the Council. He said it was his
           understanding that proposed language was crafted to avoid any OPMA issues. Council
           Member Green said he understood the concern about the potential for politics, and

           suggested replacing “meeting with residents” with “purpose of discussing a project,
           specific issue, or providing information on a specific project.”

           Council Member Green said he believed adding the one-hour time limit was preventative.
           He said he believed public comments could be restricted to anything not on the agenda,
           or anything specifically pertaining to West Jordan. Council Member Green said he would
           like to send the issue back to the Committee of the Whole to work on the language.

           Council Member Jacob said he understood concerns about town hall meetings and
           campaigning, but said anything Council Members did in the scope of their job on the
           Council could be considered campaigning. He said he agreed with removing “pre” from
           pre-registration and removing the one-hour time limit. Council Member Jacob expressed
           the opinion that even with proposed guardrails for public comments, the options for
           providing public comment were better than were available several years ago.

           Council Office Director Alan Anderson recognized that everything the Council did could
           be seen as campaigning, but said the Council Office would be more cautious after a Council
           Member filed to run in an election.

           Council Member Bedore said two Council Members were recently involved in a town hall
           meeting, and said he believed it showed more Council unity. He suggested making sure a
           district representative was aware of and involved in town hall meetings.

           Council Member Bloom commented that not everyone had access or ability to use a
           camera remotely, and said she was against taking away the option for online
           participation. She said public meetings were meant to be accessible to all residents, and
           restrictive requirements could be seen as limiting public engagement. Council Member
           Bloom commented that some commenters may prefer anonymity, and said she believed
           the Council should move forward allowing more accessibility. She said she was opposed
           to the proposed resolution unless changes were made.
