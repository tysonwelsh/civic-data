CONENT AND APPROVAL OF 10-YEAR RESIDENTIAL WASTE COLLECTION
AGREEMENT/RESOLUTION:
     Consider approval of a resolution to give the City's consent and approval for
     Washington County Special Service District No. 1 and Republic Services to
     enter into the proposed 10-year Residential Waste Collection Agreement.

      Fay Reber with the Solid Waste Special Service District addressed the Council. Mr.
      Reber explained that the District is coming to the end of three (3) agreements: 1)
      10-year contract with Republic Waste for waste collection throughout the County; 2)
      10-year agreement with the City for billing and collection; and 3) Recycling. They
      considered several alternatives which included purchasing equipment and provided
      the services in house, going out for bid, and extending the contracts. He outlined
      the recommended fee structure.

      Chairman Shane McDonald outlined the plan on how they will proceed with an
      advertising program.

      After being asked by Councilmember Hughes, Administrative Services Director
      Trevor Coombs explained how it works when a customer who opted out moves to a
      new home in St. George.

      Mr. Reber explained how the recycling program works when a customer moves from
      one city another, within the district. The agreement includes the following language:
      “People who move outside the district to a residence in an included area or people
      who move from within the district to a new residence in an included area, regardless
      of a prior option to opt out, shall automatically be included in the recycling program”.

      City Attorney Shawn Guzman stated the City has an ordinance which is the law; the
      agreement needs to match the City’s ordinance.

      Councilmember Hughes noted he would like to strike this sentence. If a customer
      who has opted out moves within the City, they should remain opted out if they
      cho07ose.

      Mr. Reber stated the burden to sort through all of these situations relies with the
      City. He can change the language as the City sees fit and is approved by the City
      Attorney. Each City has its own opt out rules.

      Mr. Coombs noted the administrative costs is $.18. He provided a simplified
      spreadsheet to the Council outlining the increases on a rate table. There are some

PROPERTY PURCHASES:
     Consider approval of property purchases along River Road between
     Riverside Drive and 1450 South needed for widening of River Road.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard explained the city is acquiring property, utility and slope
      easements, and temporary construction easements, along River Road for the
      widening project that is going to start construction in the fall of 2020. There are a
      total of 21 separate parcels with 12 separate property owners. This project, including
      property and easement acquisition, is federally funded with a local match of 6.77%.
