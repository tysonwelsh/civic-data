       A RESOLUTION OF THE WHITE CITY METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL

       THE WHITE CITY METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL FOR THE USE OF THE
       “CODERED” EMERGENCY COMMUNICATIONS PLATFORM

                                          RECITALS

   A. The White City Metro Township (“White City” or the “Municipality”) is a municipality
      pursuant to Utah Code Ann. §§ 10-2a-401 et seq.

   B. The White City Water Improvement District (the “District”) is a Local District Pursuant to
      Utah code §§ 17B-1-101 et seq.

   C. The District has subscribed to use the CodeRED emergency alert system and
      communications platform (the “Platform”) to notify water users in the event of an
      emergency or delivery issue regarding their water service.

   D. The platform would also allow the municipality to communicate with residents regarding
      municipal emergencies and other community issues.

   E. The Municipality and the District can share the use and costs of the platform via an
      interlocal cooperation agreement pursuant to Utah Code § 11-13-203 et al.

ORDINANCE NO. __________________                              DATE: __________________, 2020

   AN ORDINANCE AMENDING TITLE 5, ENTITLED “BUSINESS LICENSES AND
 REGULATIONS,” OF THE WHITE CITY METRO TOWNSHIP CODE OF ORDINANCES,
   BY ADDING PUBLIC NUISANCE, CRIME-MAGNET, AND GENERAL WELFARE
JUSTIFICATIONS FOR REVOKING A BUSINESS LICENSE AND PROVIDING GREATER
    DUE PROCESS FOR BUSINESS LICENSE SUPENSIONS AND REVOCATIONS

                                            RECITALS

      WHEREAS, Utah Code Ann. §§ 10-1-203(2) and 10-8-84 authorize the White City Metro
Township Council (the “Council”) to regulate businesses within the Township through the
enactment of city ordinances; and

        WHEREAS, the Utah Supreme Court has recognized that a city’s municipal authority to
issue licenses and promulgate licensing policies is a principal “means of regulating businesses”
pursuant to the “exercise of its police powers” (Consolidation Coal Co. v. Emery County, 702 P.3d
121, 123 (1985)); and

       WHEREAS, the Utah Supreme Court has declared that a city “has broad discretion in
granting, denying, or revoking [business] licenses” (Whiting v. Clayton, 617 P.2d 364, 364 (1980));
and

       WHEREAS, Utah Code Ann. § 10-8-60 authorizes the Council to regulate public nuisances
by “declar[ing] what shall be a nuisance, . . . abat[ing] the same, and impos[ing] fines upon persons
who may create, continue or suffer nuisances to exist”; and

        WHEREAS, the Utah Supreme Court has affirmed that “cities have been granted the
specific authority to pass ordinances to regulate, by means of business licensing, offensive
businesses that have become nuisances” (Dairy Prod. Servs., Inc. v. City of Wellsville, 2000 UT
81, ¶ 38; 13 P.3d 581, 591 (2000)); and

       WHEREAS, the Utah Supreme Court has further declared that “a [business] license
revocation may be based on the existence of a nuisance as defined by [city] ordinance” (Whiting,
617 P.2d at 365); and

       WHEREAS, the Utah Supreme Court has stated that a business license represents “a
substantial property interest” and therefore “should not be destroyed nor disrupted arbitrarily, nor
without following fundamental standards of due process of law to guard against capricious or
oppressive administrative action” (Anderson v. Utah County Bd. of County Comm’rs, 589 P.2d
1214, 1216 (1979)); and




                                                 1
       WHEREAS, the Utah Supreme Court has noted that the “minimum requirements” for due
process in revoking a business license are “adequate notice and an opportunity to be heard in a
meaningful manner” (Dairy Prod. Servs., Inc., 2000 UT at ¶ 49; 13 P.3d at 593); and

       WHEREAS, the White City Metro Township Council desires to amend the business license
revocation provisions of the White City Municipal Code to provide for greater due process in
business license suspension and revocation proceedings and to authorize business license
revocation or suspension in situations where businesses that become public or private nuisances,
encourage and facilitate crime, or operate in a manner injurious or manifestly inimical to the
general welfare and prosperity of the community.

       NOW, THEREFORE, the Council ordains as follows:

        Section 1: Section 5.07.020, entitled “Grounds For License Suspension Or Revocation,”
is repealed in its entirety and replaced as follows:

       5.07.020 Grounds for License/Permit Suspension or Revocation

       In addition to any fine imposed, every license or permit issued by the license official or
       council may be revoked or suspended by the council for any of the following reasons:

           A. Licensee or permittee has been convicted of a felony or any crime involving moral
              turpitude;

           B. Licensee or permittee has obtained, or aided another to obtain, a license by fraud or
              deceit;

           C. Licensee or permittee has failed to pay personal property taxes, Utah state sales
              taxes, or other required fees;

           D. Licensee or permittee has refused to permit authorized officers or employees to
              make an inspection or to take a sample of a commodity, or has interfered with such
              officer or employee while making such inspection;

           E. Licensee or permittee has filed or encouraged another to file false information with
              the license office, the local law enforcement agency or any other agency as part of
              the license application;

           F. Licensee or permittee has contracted with two or more independent contractors
              whose business licenses were subsequently revoked in final unappealable decisions
              during a twelve-month period for engaging in unlawful conduct under the Utah
              Massage Therapy Practice Act or Prostitution under the Utah Criminal Code on the
              licensee or permittee's leased or owned premises;


                                                2
          G. Any conduct or act on the business premises, or any conduct, act, or condition
             permitted by the licensee, permittee, or any employee on the business premises,
             which renders or tends to render such business or its premises: (a) a public or private
             nuisance, as defined by applicable law, regulation, or ordinance; or (b) a menace to
             the health, peace, safety, morals, or general welfare of the City or its residents;

          H. Licensee or permittee is responsible for unlawful activities conducted on the
             business premises that are contrary to the laws of the United States, the State of
             Utah, the ordinances of the metro, or the rules and regulations of any agency or
             another governmental entity governing the operation of the business holding the
             license or permit;

          I. Licensee or permittee has permitted its employees, agents, or patrons to engage in
             illegal activities on the business premises or to use the premises for any unlawful
             purpose or enterprise or the maintenance of a public or private nuisance, as defined
             by applicable law, regulation, or ordinance;

          J. Licensee’s or permittee’s business activities or premises are conducted or kept in
             an illegal, disorderly, unsanitary, hazardous, or ill-governed manner;

          K. The failure of the licensee or permittee to comply with any condition or requirement
             under local, state, or federal law; or

          L. Any other reason, not arbitrary or capricious and permitted by law under the
             metro’s police power that justifies suspension or revocation of a license or permit.

        Section 2: Section 5.07.030, entitled “License Suspension Or Revocation – Procedure,”
is repealed and replaced as follows:

       5.07.030 License/Permit Suspension Or Revocation – Procedure

          A. If the license official has good cause to believe that a license or permit should be
             suspended or revoked, the official shall schedule a hearing before the council by
             providing notice to the licensee and permittee which notice shall contain the
             following:

                 1. The time, place, and date of the hearing;

                 2. A statement of the purpose of the hearing; and




                                                3
          3. A reasonably definite statement setting forth the alleged grounds under
             Section 5.07.020 justifying suspension or revocation of the license or
             permit.

   B. The hearing shall take place at least fourteen (14) days and not more than thirty
      (30) days from the date written notice is sent.

   C. At the hearing, the licensee or permittee shall have the right: (a) to appear
      personally or by counsel; (b) to hear the evidence against him; (c) to confront and
      cross examine any witnesses; (d) to present evidence and witnesses on his behalf;
      and (e) to state his case as to why the license or permit should not be suspended or
      revoked.

   D. After conducting a hearing pursuant to this section, the council may suspend or
      revoke any license or permit if the licensee or permittee fails to show cause by a
      preponderance of the evidence why the license or permit should not be suspended
      or revoked.

   E. If the licensee or permittee fails, neglects, or refuses to appear at the hour and date
      set for such hearing, the council may proceed to determine the matter in the absence
      of the licensee or permittee, or may, in its sole discretion, continue the date of such
      hearing to some later date, and at the later date proceed to act on the matter without
      further notice to the licensee or permittee.

   F. After due deliberation, the council shall announce its decision, and shall issue a
      written decision notifying the licensee or permittee of its findings and determination
      and an explanation of how the licensee or permittee may appeal the council’s
      decision.

      Section 3: Section 5.07.040, entitled “Appeals,” is repealed and amended as
      follows:

5.07.040 Appeals

   A. A licensee or permittee may appeal any decision rendered by the council under this
      chapter by requesting an administrative hearing with the metro’s administrative law
      judge pursuant to the process set forth in chapter 1.16, which shall govern appeals
      filed under this chapter.

   B. In addition to the provisions of chapter 1.16, the administrative law judge shall:

      1. Review the record, and may not accept or consider any evidence outside the
         record unless the evidence was offered to and was excluded by the council, the


                                         4
           license official or license official’s designee and the administrative law judge
           determines that it was improperly excluded;

       2. Review the council’s or the license official’s actions to determine whether the
          council’s decision was arbitrary, capricious, or illegal; and

       3. After reviewing the record and written and oral argument on both sides, affirm,
          reverse, alter, or remand the council’s decision for further review and
          consideration.

   C. The filing of an appeal does not automatically stay the council’s decision; however,
      the administrative law judge has the authority to stay the decision while the appeal
      is pending.

   D. A decision issued by the administrative law judge pursuant to this section is a final
      administrative order that is appealable to the district court pursuant to section
      1.15.130.

Section 4: Section 5.07.050, entitled “Effect of Revocation,” is amended as follows:

5.07.050 Effect of Suspension or Revocation

   A. If a license or permit is suspended or revoked, it shall thereafter be unlawful for
      any person to engage in or use, or permit to be used, any property for any business
      with respect to which the license or permit has been suspended or revoked.

   B. In the event of suspension or revocation as provided in this chapter, there shall be
      no refund or rebate of any part of the original license or permit fee paid by the
      licensee or permittee.

Section 5: Section 5.07.060, entitled “Effect of Revocation,” is amended as follows:

5.07.060 Waiting Period For New License Or Permit

   A. No person whose license or permit has been suspended or revoked under the
      provisions of this chapter, and no person associated or connected with such person
      in the conduct of such business, shall be granted a license or permit for the same
      purpose for a period of six months after the suspension or revocation has occurred.

   B. The council may, for good cause, waive the prohibition against persons formerly
      associated or connected with an individual who has had a license or permit
      suspended or revoked.
