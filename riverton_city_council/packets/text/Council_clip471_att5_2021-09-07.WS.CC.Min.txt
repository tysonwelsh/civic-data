19            Cemetery Rules and Regulations – Ryan Carter, City Attorney
20
21   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained that there have been some unevenly applied rules in the
22   cemetery. He said that there has been concern about how many headstones can be placed on
23   one burial plot. He said that in the new section the burial plots can be double stacked and the
24   names of both deceased persons buried there would be on one headstone. He said that the
25   rules and regulations state that an infant can be placed in a burial plot and an additional flat
26   headstone can be installed for the infant. He said there are times when there is no infant
27   involved and the family may want to do one upright headstone for person A and one flat
28   headstone for person B. He said that the Attorney’s Office and City Recorder’s Office has
29   determined that families should be able to have one upright headstone and one flat headstone
30   regardless of whether it is an infant or another person buried in the same burial plot. He said he
31   wanted to know if the Council had any problem with this decision conceptually.
32
33   Councilmember Stewart asked if the family could choose to do two flat headstones instead of
34   one upright and one flat headstone. Mr. Carter said that would be fine.
35
36   Councilmembers did not express concern over this new policy and Mr. Carter said he would
37   make the appropriate changes to the Cemetery Rules and Regulations.

20            Ordinance No. 21-23 – Text Amendment, Proposing Changes to Section 18.25.110,
21            Farm Animals, Regarding Residential Chickens in the RR-22 Zone, Amendments
22            Proposed by Riverton City
23
24   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that this a public hearing for a
25   Zoning Text Change proposed by Riverton City. He said this amendment clarifies allowance for
26   residential chickens in the RR-22 zone. He said the RR-22 zone carries farm animal rights,
27   including chickens. He said that in zones without farm animal rights, a limited number of
28   chickens are allowed, but no roosters. He said in the RR-22 zone, a minimum of .5 acres is
29   required to qualify for farm animal rights so the purpose of this amendment is to clarify for lots
30   below that threshold in the RR-22 zone, farm animal rights are not allowed but may have
31   residential chickens and are allowed to have a rooster. He said the typical impacts of a rooster
32   in a residential zone will be mitigated by the fact that roosters are allowed elsewhere in the RR-
33   22 zone.
