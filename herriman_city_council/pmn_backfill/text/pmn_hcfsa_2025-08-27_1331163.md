      3. Public Hearing
         3.1. Public hearing and consideration of a Resolution amending the Herriman City Fire
              Service Area fiscal year 2026 budget – Kyle Maurer, Finance Director
      Finance Director Kyle Maurer presented the proposed budget amendments to the board. He
      began by recommending an increase to the property tax budget, similar to the adjustment
      made in the Safety Enforcement Area, to reflect the tax increase adopted at the previous
      meeting. He then addressed the motor vehicle revenue, indicating an upward adjustment
      based on updated trend data. The revenue was coming in higher than originally budgeted,
      prompting the proposed revision.

      On the expenditure side, Director Maurer outlined a significant change involving the Fire
      Station 103 budget. He proposed moving this budget from the HCFSA into the general city
      budget. He explained that since the City of Herriman would own the station and had
      contracted the construction in the City’s name, not the District’s, it would be more
      appropriate and transparent to reflect the budget within the City's financials. Accordingly,
      staff proposed a budget amendment to transfer $7,361,840 to the City for the completion of
      the Fire Station 103 rebuild, which was recently approved by the Herriman City Council.

      Director Maurer further explained that this adjustment would involve removing the budget
      from the District and transferring the funds to the City’s capital projects fund, with the
      transfer being reflected in both budget meetings.

      Trustee Teddy Hodges raised a question about the operational and maintenance implications
      of city ownership of the fire station. He asked whether the City would be responsible for
      those costs and then seek reimbursement from the District. After clarification, Trustee
      Hodges suggested that the City could handle the operations and maintenance as the asset
      owner and then charge the district a maintenance fee.

      Director Maurer agreed with this approach, acknowledging that while these costs were
      currently being paid directly out of the District’s budget, it would make sense to shift them
