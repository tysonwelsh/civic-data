2. PUBLIC COMMENT
Ms. Karen Wyatt , a violinist with the Utah Symphony, spoke about the significance of 
Abravanel Hall to Utah residents.  She thanked Mayor Jennifer Wilson for her 
commitment to keeping Abravanel Hall, which was specifically built to showcase and 
enhance live orchestra music, in its current form.  She noted it would cost an estimated 
$400 million to make good on the promise that was made in the joint statement that was 
released last week by the Mayor, Smith Entertainment Group, and the Utah Symphony.    
Mr. Keith Carrick , a percussionist with Utah Symphony, stated it was vital that 
Abravanel Hall maintain its excellent acoustics.  The Utah Symphony is one of only 15 
full-time orchestras in the United States.  It has served the people of Utah for 85 years and 
has performed for nearly every child in the state for the last 50 years.  The County has an 
interest in maximizing revenue from the hall, and this can be done with a phased approach 
to save money.  A re -build could result in being without a venue for many years.  The 
Utah Symphony will exist for years to come.    
Mr. Allen Roberts stated the question was not whether to keep Abravanel Hall, but how 
to best do it.  The easiest way to get a building destroyed is to use exaggerated scopes 
and estimates to claim the project is too costly.  Estimates are only as valid as the 
information is specific.  There are many more goal -satisfying options than those already 
given.  He asked the Council to create an independent committee of experts to explore 
options on a cost-benefit basis.

RECITALS
A. Salt Lake County (the “County”) owns certain real property located at

identified as Parcel No. 15-33-128-045 (the “Property”), which is the 
West Valley Branch of the Salt Lake County Library.

B. West Valley City (the “City”) is constructing roadway improvements 
along 3650 South Street (the “Project”) and would like to acquire a 2,739
-square-foot portion of the Property (the “Parcel”) from the County and a 
temporary construction easement as part of the Project.
C. On March 26, 2024, the Salt Lake County Council (the “Council”) 
approved Resolution 6188, declaring the Parcel available for disposal and 
authorizing execution and delivery of a Real Estate Purchase Contract (the 
“March REPC”) to the City for the transfer of the Parcel.
D. After approval of Resolution 6188, the City withdrew the March REPC 
because it contained an erroneous appraisal value for the Parcel.  A 
portion of the Parcel is located within the existing right -of-way and is 
currently occupied by existing street improvements, which was not 
accounted for in the calculation of value.  The City does not need to pay 
for the portion of Parcel that is already located in the public right-of-way.
E. The size and shape of the Parcel has not changed, but the appraised value 
has changed to reflect only the amount required to be paid for the portion 
of the Parcel outside of the existing right-of-way.
F. The City has submitted a corrected Real Estate Purchase Contract (the 
“Corrected REPC ”) to the County for the acquisition of (1) the Parcel via 
a quit claim deed (the “Deed”) and (2) the temporary construction 
easement via a separate document (the “Easement”).
G. The corrected amount offered by the City for acquisition of the Parcel 
and the Easement is $30,300.00, which amount has been reviewed and 
approved by the Salt Lake County Real Estate Section as full and 
adequate consideration.
H. The Salt Lake County Library Board concurs with this conclusion and 
has recommended approval the transfer of the Parcel to the City as 
provided in the Corrected REPC.
I. It has been determined that the best interests of the County and the 
general public will be served by executing the Corrected REPC, the Deed, 
and the Easement.  These actions will be in compliance with all applicable 
state statutes and county ordinances.

A PROCLAMATION OF THE SALT LAKE COUNTY COUNCIL 
CELEBRATING AFRICAN HERITAGE IN UTAH AND SALT LAKE 
COUNTY
Whereas, On June 1st, 2024, African immigrants in Salt Lake County and 
Utah will celebrate the African Heritage Festival; and 
Whereas, The African Heritage Festival recognizes and commemorates the 
cultural heritage, diversity, courage, and freedom of African Immigrants and 
Refugees in Salt Lake County, Utah, and beyond; and 
Whereas, The African Heritage Festival was first organized and Celebrated 
during the first week of June in 2021 by the Ngoma y ’Africa Cultural Center; 
Page 22 of 25
County Council Meeting Minutes May 21, 2024
and 
Whereas, African Americans and Africans make up 2.9% of Salt Lake 
County’s population, a percentage that is higher than the percentage of the 
African American population in Utah; and 
Whereas, Utah's population includes 67,000 Black or African Americans, 
52% of whom live in Salt Lake County and more than 50% of whom are 
African Immigrants; and 
Whereas, since 2010, Utah ’s Black population grew 55% while the total state 
population grew only 18%, causing the Black share of the state population to 
increase from 1.6% to 2.1%; and 
Whereas, African Immigrants and Refugees are the primary contributors to 
this growth in the black share of the population; and 
Whereas, The African Hertiage Festival can serve as a reminder that the 
foundation of the United States is built by people from many nations and 
diverse cultures who recognize the importance of freedom and that of their 
God given rights; and 
Whereas, this Celebration also serves as a reminder of the crucial 
socio-economic ties between the continent of Africa, Salt Lake County, the 
State of Utah, and the United States of America; and 
Whereas, Ngoma y ’Africa Cultural Center houses, welcomes, builds, and 
assists Utah and Salt Lake County Residents who have immigrated from 
multiple African Countries; and 
Whereas, African Immigrants and Refugees have become a valuable part of 
the Salt Lake County Community; and 
Whereas, African Immigrants and Refugees have enriched Salt Lake County, 
and we celebrate their culture, traditions, and heritage; and 
Whereas, Residents of African descent have a profound and positive impact 
on Salt Lake County through their economic contributions, their strong 
commitment to our community, to faith, to hard work, and to their numerous 
acts of community service, and their civic engagement efforts; and

Whereas, we acknowledge the generations of Africans from Salt Lake 
County who have served in the Armed Forces, in government, as first 
responders, advocates, educators, scientists, lawyers, entrepreneurs,

public sector leaders; and
