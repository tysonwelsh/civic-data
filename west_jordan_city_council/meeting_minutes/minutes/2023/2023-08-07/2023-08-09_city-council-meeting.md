 5. BUSINESS ITEMS
       a. Resolution No. 23-048 authorizing a Real Estate Purchase and Sale Agreement with
          the Vicky R. Jones Survivor’s Trust, Bryan L. Jones, the Bryan L. Jones GST Trust,
          Leanna Jones, the Leanna Jones GST Trust, Jeneal Harshman and the Jeneal
          Harshman GST Trust for 2.86 acres of real property located at approximately 7382
          West New Bingham Highway and 1.50 acres of real property located at
          approximately 8801 South Bacchus Highway in West Jordan, Utah
          Assistant City Administrator Jamie Davidson oriented the Council with a Real Estate
          Purchase Agreement for purchase of approximately 4.3 acres at an appraised price of $1.3
          million for a possible future fire station.

           Council Member Whitelock requested the appraisal for the property in question and
           requested inclusion of appraisals in the packet in the future. She was concerned about the
           location of the property for a future fire station, and wanted to ensure the property had
           space for a police substation and a community room. She expressed frustration that she

           believed the Council voted on a total package that included infrastructure but did not vote
           to specifically approve infrastructure.

           Council Member Green said original discussions had been to place a new fire station on
           U111, and asked why the currently proposed location was not further west. City
           Administrator Korban Lee said a study was conducted, and the Fire Department was
           asked to find the best location to be able to operate five stations and service the City as
           effectively and financially efficient as possible. He said the proposed location was within
           the suggested area identified, and would give the greatest flexibility for north/south
           response and east/west response.

           Responding to a question from Council Member Green, Administrative Services Director
           Danyce Steck said the purchase was not included in the last budget amendment approved
           by the Council. City Attorney Josh Chandler advised the Council to postpone approval of
           the proposed resolution until a budget amendment was prepared.

           MOTION: Council Member Green moved to POSTPONE Resolution No. 23-048
                   until a budget amendment could be adopted at the same time.
                   Council Member Whitelock seconded the motion.

           Mr. Davidson asked to be able to present further information for consideration prior to a
           vote.

           Council Member Green moved to withdraw his motion for further discussion, and
           Council Member Whitelock seconded. All voted in favor and the motion passed
           unanimously (6-0).

           Mr. Davidson noted one of the provisions with the Jones family had been that the
           purchase close by July 31, 2023, unless both parties agreed to extend. He said the attorney
           representing the Jones family had agreed to extend to the current date, August 9, 2023.
           Mr. Davidson made the Council aware that approval would be needed from the Jones
           family to extend again.

           City Attorney Josh Chandler upon receiving further information that helped him better
           understand the circumstances, confirmed that funds were available for the purchase.
           Based on the additional information, he was comfortable with approval of the resolution.
           Ms. Steck noted that Council would be within State law to authorize the expenditure in
           the Water Fund because the amount would not exceed what was currently available in
           the Water Fund. A subsequent budget amendment would be needed to accommodate
           future expected expenditures.

           Mr. Davidson said he did not anticipate that an extension would be a problem with the
           Jones family. Council Member Green felt it was presumptuous of staff to assume the
           Council would approve the matter that evening. He believe Council had previously made
           it clear they did not like passing issues at the last moment. Council Member Worthen did
           not like feeling rushed. Mr. Davidson understood if the Council wanted to postpone, and
           said he would continue to work with the parties involved.

       c. Budget discussion items:
            • Review actual tax revenues received from Salt Lake County
            • Discuss possible Property Tax increase percentages
          Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck summarized Council philosophy or policy
          regarding property tax increases expressed in previous meetings:
               •   Property tax is the most stable revenue source.
               •   Discuss small inflationary property tax increases each year in order to avoid
                   double-digit increases.
               •   Tie any increases to a CPI as well as the 5-year forecast.
               •   5-year forecast has an intentional transfer to the CIP of approximately $500,000.

           Council Member Zach Jacob arrived at 7:02 pm

           Ms. Steck asked if the Council wanted to dedicate savings to the CIP Fund, or have an
           intentional transfer to the CIP Fund. She explained the proposed 4.8% increase in
           property tax for FY 2024, and showed a comparison of proposed increases in other Utah
           cities. Ms. Steck summarized budget changes requested by the City Council, and showed
           annual sales tax revenue beginning with FY 2019. The Council-approved FY 2024
           anticipated sales tax revenue was down 5.4% from actual sales tax revenue in FY 2022.

           Council Member Green would prefer to use reserves, and expressed concern that
           government in the last year grew twice as fast as property tax, with no growth in sales tax.
           He acknowledged that the positions were necessary but pointed out the need to recognize
           there would be no new growth money in the next year. He questioned if one-time reserve
           money could be transferred back to an enterprise fund for CIP. Ms. Steck responded that
           funds could not be transferred from the General Fund to the Water Fund or the CIP Fund
           to the Water Fund. She said funds could be transferred from the Capital Projects Fund to
           the General Fund if needed. She emphasized that the utility fund transfer could not be
           turned off and on.

           Council Member Jacob commented that the City laid off employees to balance the budget
           in 2020 and expressed the opinion that looking only at personnel growth was an
           incomplete picture. He stated the City was doing more with less than ever before. He
           suggested the philosophy of the utility fund transfer should be discussed beginning in
           January, not two weeks before approving the budget.

           Council Member Worthen noted an expected recession with less money for staff and
           taking care of the City. She questioned why the City would build another building that
           would put additional stress on resources and staff.

           Chair McConnehey read aloud a recent prediction that the U.S. economy would experience
           a soft landing rather than a recession and expressed comfort with the current proposal.

           Ms. Steck said staff would bring back a change for the proposed land acquisition on August
           23rd. Responding to a question from Council Member Green, Ms. Steck emphasized that
           the proposed increase in property tax revenue was very close to the cost of new positions
           in the City over the last year. A reduction in the proposed property tax increase for FY
           2024 would result in a reduced transfer to the CIP Fund in FY 2024, and possibly result in
           the need for a larger property tax increase in the future. She noted that one of the
           philosophies expressed by Council was a desire to not surprise residents with a dire
           situation and a large property tax increase.

           The Council discussed the proposed 4.8% property tax increase. Council Member
           Worthen said she would not vote in favor of a property tax increase with “wants” included
           in the budget. Council Member Whitelock was unsure that 4.8% was the right amount,
           and also noted she did not agree with including at least one of the positions included in
           the budget. She said she would prefer a lower increase.

           Council Member Jacob was comfortable with the proposed 4.8% increase. He further
           added that if the Council chose to reduce the increase, he did not want to cut personnel.
           Vice Chair Bloom was comfortable with 4.8% and would not cut personnel. Council
           Member Pack had always been in favor of small increases year after year, and was
           comfortable with the proposed 4.8%. He commented that no one liked a tax increase, but
           everyone wanted a high level of service.

           Council Member Green was leaning towards not voting in favor of a 4.8% property tax
           increase because of property tax increases proposed by other entities and the overall
           impact on citizens. He expressed the opinion that the property tax system was broken. He
           had concerns regarding sales tax projections and the amount of new growth. He suggested
           the City needed to be super careful in the next year and could not continue to hire more
           people than new growth.

           Council Member Jacob pointed out that if every increase proposed taxing entities were
           approved for FY 2024, the total property tax bill for an average citizen would still go down
           compared to the previous year. He spoke in favor of the proposed 4.8% rate increase.
           Chair McConnehey expressed the opinion that the system currently in place for figuring
           out property taxes and how they were communicated to the public left something to be
           desired.

           Ms. Steck thanked the Council for their input.
