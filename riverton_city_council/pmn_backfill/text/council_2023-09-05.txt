Discussion Items

         Town Days Discussion Continued

Ms. Garn led a discussion regarding the city's parade route for Town Days. The current parade
route, starting at 1930 West and ending at 12600 South (Riverton High School), was outlined. It
was explained that this route had been in use for over 20 years and was efficient for unloading
parade participants. However, concerns were raised about the suitability of this route for a
growing city, and the need for a longer location for unloading and offloading parade participants.
The proposal was to start the parade at CR Hamilton Sports Park, which had a larger parking lot
for unloading and getting participants in place. This route would increase the parade distance by
three-quarters of a mile, providing more space for spectators.

Ms. Garn acknowledged some challenges in this new parade route, such as the increased
distance for marching participants, especially children and marching bands. The proposal was
said to address traffic issues, with adequate public parking areas. One specific concern was the
roundabout on the proposed route and whether it could accommodate larger floats. Ms. Garn
mentioned using a drone to assess the float's maneuverability around the roundabout, which
appeared to work well.

Ms. Garn said the main point of contention was the additional cost for police protection due to
the need to close some neighborhoods along the route. The decision on whether to change the
parade route was left to the elected officials.

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained that the current parade route had issues related to
accessibility and created disadvantages for both parade participants and spectators. He said the
new parade route was considered more accessible from all angles.

Mr. Carter acknowledged there were some disadvantages of the new route, such as the
additional distance, but it was argued that the benefits in terms of accessibility and safety
outweighed this drawback. He emphasized that the proposed route was a result of careful
consideration and planning for public safety.

Ms. Garn discussed safety concerns related to the city's parade. Photos were shown to illustrate
the issues that have arisen during previous parades, emphasizing the need to address safety
concerns. The following points were highlighted:

         Crowd Encroachment: Photos showed spectators standing very close to the parade
         route, sometimes even crossing the white line meant to separate them from the parade.

         Swag Distribution: Concerns were raised about the distribution of swag (parade
         giveaways) to spectators. It was noted that spectators often rush to collect swag from
         parade participants rather than letting it be thrown to them, which can create safety risks.

         Spotter Challenges: Some parade participants, particularly those with large floats, face
         difficulties in ensuring the safety of spectators, especially children, who come too close
         to their vehicles despite having designated spotters.

         Safety Measures: Several potential safety measures were discussed, including the
         possibility of eliminating candy and swag throwing altogether. However, it was
         recognized that this could diminish the hometown feel of the parade.

         Fencing: The idea of using portable fencing to create a barrier between the parade route
         and spectators was mentioned. This measure has been implemented in other parades to
         prevent encroachment.

Ms. Garn suggested that a more in-depth discussion on parade safety and potential measures
be scheduled for the upcoming strategic planning session in January. This would allow for a
thorough evaluation of safety concerns and potential solutions.

Councilmember Wells acknowledged that stopping the distribution of candy and swag would
likely lead to dissatisfaction among parade-goers. He also pointed out that despite efforts to
enhance safety, it may never be perfect due to the spontaneous nature of spectator behavior
during parades. He appreciated the ongoing efforts to address safety concerns and suggested
waiting until January for further discussion on the matter.

Councilmember Pierucci expressed his interest in exploring multiple parade route options rather
than settling for a single alternative. He suggested having several choices to consider during the
January discussion. He requested input from Chief Barfuss regarding safety concerns and
alternative solutions.

Chief Barfuss explained that implementing the new route would require a significant number of
officers, crossing guards, barricades, and other resources to ensure safety and security. He
also noted that the current route could still be improved by increasing the presence of officers
on bicycles, enhancing motor patrols, better messaging to the public, and stationing personnel
at regular intervals.

Councilmember Buroker shared her perspective on the candy-throwing tradition during parades,
recalling a past attempt to eliminate it, which proved unpopular and short-lived. She suggested
conducting a survey to gauge residents' preferences regarding candy throwing, increased
barricades, and other safety measures. She expressed support for waiting until January to
decide on the parade route.

Councilmember McCay acknowledged the tradition of candy-throwing during parades and
suggested that limiting candy might not be well-received by the community. She supported the
idea of extending the parade route along 13400 South, as it provides more space and could
help keep people back from the parade route. She mentioned the ongoing construction and
how it could affect the parade route, regardless of the chosen option. She expressed her
support for implementing the longer route in the upcoming parade if possible.

Mr. Carter explained that there have been recent discussions about the possibility of
constructing a bridge over 13800 South to improve accessibility. While this is within the realm of
possibilities, there are ongoing negotiations regarding cost allocation and other factors. The goal
is to open 13800 South in time for the next school year, which would significantly impact traffic
circulation in the area.

Councilmember McCay asked about the bike routes on 13400 South. Mr. Necaise mentioned
that they've secured funding for the project and presented two options for widening the road.
The first option involves widening both sides of the road by about four feet each, while the
second option is to widen one side by approximately six feet and narrow the lanes to create a
bike lane.

Councilmember Pierucci suggested having announcers along the parade route to not only
provide commentary on the parade entries but also serve as a force multiplier for safety by
periodically reminding spectators to stand back. In response, Ms. Garn there have been three
speakers traditionally used in the past for the parade. It was acknowledged that setting up and
taking down these speakers can be challenging, particularly in windy conditions. The option to
increase the number of speakers was suggested, pending staff availability.

Councilmember McDougal pointed out that the wider road, like Redwood Road, doesn't
necessarily mitigate safety concerns as people tend to fill in around candy and goodies. He
acknowledged that while efforts can be made to communicate safety guidelines, there will still
be attendees from outside of Riverton who may not receive these messages. He emphasized
that there is no perfect parade route due to various constraints, and the goal is to find a route
that minimizes risks while acknowledging that some level of risk is inherent in large public
gatherings.

Councilmember Buroker said that using parade announcers to remind the crowd to stay back
can sometimes lead to a confrontational atmosphere. People may feel chastised, and it can
escalate into a negative situation quickly. To address this, it was suggested that a pre-recorded
general announcement could be played at various points along the parade route to remind
attendees without personal confrontation. This approach would depersonalize the message and
reduce the likelihood of adverse reactions from the crowd.

Councilmember Buroker suggested a traditional end of the parade to include tractors, vehicles,
and a sign indicating the end to prevent confusion. She said they could consider starting the
parade at 7:00 PM instead of 6:30 PM and set a maximum limit of 100 entries for the parade to
ensure a reasonable parade length. She also suggested incorporating older cars and
recognizing long-time residents in the parade to enhance its old-town appeal.

All Councilmembers agreed to research additional information regarding the parade route and
discuss in January during the Strategic Planning meeting.

         Resolution No. 23-70 – Approving Riverton City’s Entry into an Interlocal
         Cooperation Agreement with Salt Lake County to Carry Out Community
         Development Block Grant (CDBG), Emergency Services Grant (ESG), and Home
         Investment Partnership Program (HOME) Programs, Activities and Projects

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the agreement was a revised
version of an agreement that had previously been seen in June. A few other cities involved in
the agreement had made some modifications to their respective portions of it. These changes
did not impact Riverton City's part of the agreement. However, because the modifications
resulted in a new version of the agreement, it was brought back to the Council for an updated
signature.

Mr. Carter explained that Herriman withdrew entirely from the agreement, opting to establish a
direct connection with HUD at the federal level to potentially secure CDB-type grant funding.
This shift in approach does not exempt them from adhering to the policies outlined in the
agreement. This agreement essentially operates on a "pay to play" basis, historically benefiting
Riverton City with CDBG funding. However, it's worth noting that such funding opportunities do
not arise every year, and periodic agreement renewals are necessary to demonstrate eligibility
for potential funding.

Mayor Pro Tem McDougal clarified that this agreement was already approved in June. It
essentially establishes a gateway for the City to access certain funds if they ever decide to
pursue a project that aligns with the requirements. Right now, there are no specific projects in
mind.

Councilmember McKay inquired about how past projects funded by the Community
Development Block Grant (CDBG) were managed, with the response being that they were
handled through the County. She also brought up the Home Investment Partnership Program,
which can be used to promote affordable housing developments. She expressed her interest in
whether the City had explored this avenue, perhaps by informing developers of these grant
opportunities, to potentially encourage affordable housing projects in Riverton.

Mr. Lethbridge discussed the City’s efforts to inform developers about grant opportunities for
affordable housing projects, particularly in the west side of the City. He mentioned an upcoming
update to the moderate-income housing plan, which will include discussions on goals and
strategies related to funding and incentivizing affordable housing.

         Resolution No. 23-72 – Authorizing the Mayor to execute an agreement with Big
         Bear Electric for the completion of the City Hall Back Plaza Project

Craig Calvert, Purchasing Manager, provided an update on the City Hall Back Plaza Project.
The project had an initial budget of $1.2 million, but the bid came in at $1,375,000. This cost did
not include a restroom or pavilion, which would add an additional $150,000 to the project. He
mentioned that preliminary work had been done, including addressing tunnels and an
abandoned wellhead on the site. During construction, it was observed that the stairway on the
project had cracked due to water damage, and it would require replacement at an additional
cost of $80,000. He expressed hope that this cost could be included in the Community Center's
lower-level project using TRCC funding. Depending on the Council's decision, additional costs
for the project could be either $375,000 or $455,000. He sought guidance from the Council on
how to proceed with the project.

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, said he hoped that TRCC would be willing to
keep it at $1.2 million as they initially committed but cover some of the additional expenses,
including the stairs and other costs. This way, the City could potentially avoid a significant
budget amendment.

Councilmember McCay said that in situations where other projects experienced cost overruns,
TRCC has typically considered it the responsibility of the City to cover those additional costs.
Page | 9 Minutes – City Council Meeting
September 5, 2023




However, if the City were to approach TRCC about expanding the project to address safety
concerns with the stairs, it would likely need to be voted on by the TRCC board. This would not
be a general practice of automatically covering cost overruns, but rather a specific consideration
for the circumstances.

Councilmember Buroker asked if the stairs were required and Mr. Calvert said they were
required.

Councilmember Wells asked if TRCC doesn't agree to cover the additional costs and the stairs
are deemed a safety issue that must be addressed, where the funding to cover these costs
would come from within the City's budget.

Mr. Hicks said if TRCC does not provide additional funding and the City needs to cover the
$455,000 budget increase for the project, one option could be to allocate the necessary funds
from the City's fund balance and the 45 fund. However, this would require a budget amendment
and approval by the Council.

Councilmember Pierucci asked if the cost increase was from materials, labor or something else.
In response, Mr. Calvert said that the cost increases for the project are primarily due to the
rising costs of materials and labor. He discussed the process of bidding the project twice and
the challenges faced by general contractors, including time constraints and difficulty in securing
subcontractors, especially concrete contractors. The completion date was extended for the
second round of bidding, and two responses were received.

Mayor Pro Tem McDougal expressed his opinion on the project, highlighting the changes in the
market and the City's fiscal responsibility. He emphasized the importance of moving forward
with a budget amendment to cover the cost overrun, given the current positive sales tax growth
and the anticipation of rising construction costs in the future. He suggested that delaying the
project might lead to even higher costs down the road and recommended investing in it now.

Councilmember McCay expressed agreement with moving forward on the project but raised the
question of the project's timeline, particularly regarding the pavilion and restrooms. She
suggested the possibility of waiting for two weeks or until the next council meeting for
clarification or potential funding options.

Mr. Calvert responded by confirming that it would be possible to proceed with the contract for
Big Bear without immediately including the restroom and pavilion. He suggested the option of
revisiting the approval for the restroom and pavilion in the future. Mr. Hicks suggested that the
County might be open to including the restroom and pavilion as part of the project within the
original $1.2 million budget, highlighting the importance of exploring this possibility.

Mayor Pro Tem McDougal clarified that the resolution being adopted was for the completion of
the plaza, and the cost overruns could be addressed later after exploring other options. He
checked with the Council to ensure understanding and asked if there were any more questions
or if they were ready for a motion.
