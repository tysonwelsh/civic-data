D. THOUGHT OF THE DAY: Commissioner Harvey: Because of the sacrifice of many in the audience today, and I
mean the sacrifice on a lot of different levels, we in Weber County get to enjoy quite a few things that are diversified.
Weber County has several outside parks with varied activities. Observatory Park was built on an old landfill where
Utah State Athletic Association Cyclecross Championships have been held and home a world class archery park that
is used daily. Fort Buenaventura is a historic mountain man park that is widely used. Weber Memorial Park on the
way to Causey Reservoir, and the North Fork Park are available to us today due to the foresight of Commissioners
years ago that purchased lands with an eye to the future. Revenue in those parks is up 48% in the last year. Trail usage
at North Fork Park is up 500% in the last four years. We are participating very well in the Land Stewardship Program
to help ensure the health of those parks. These are another wonderful asset we enjoy in Weber County. Today is the
day we have the privilege and opportunity to exercise your voice by voting. A special thanks to the families that have
come to pay respects to our veterans of the Korean War today.

H. PRESENTATION: A SALUTE TO OUR KOREAN WAR ERA VETERANS
   1. Video Presentation listing the names all known living Korean War veterans currently living in Weber County.
   2. Chair Jenkins quoted former President Ronald Reagan from a 1985 Veteran’s Day address:
                  “Elections, like Presidents, come and go and always our nation remains, due primarily to the courage
                  and sacrifice of America’s veterans who exemplify and defend the ideals that the United States stands
                  for. When recruits enter any branch of the armed forces, they are aware that their future mission could
                  thrust them into harm’s way. Yet they are willing to fight and die for the cause of human liberty. Who
                  are these men and women? They are the ordinary individuals who, along with their families, make
                  great sacrifices and endure extraordinary challenges. What higher commitment is there than to place
                  one’s life on the line for humanity?”
         Chair Jenkins then read the Korean War veteran’s names, asked all in attendance to stand to be recognized,
         and passed out an award on behalf of Weber County. A group photo was taken afterwards.
