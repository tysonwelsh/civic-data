       Wasatch Front Regional Council Presentation

Andrew Gruber, Executive Director, explained the role of the Wasatch Front Regional Council.
He acknowledged the residents' desire for biking, walking, and strolling paths and praised the
Council's focus on this issue. He also highlighted the increased funding opportunities from
organizations like WFRC and recent successes in securing new funding sources for regionally
significant trails. He introduced Hugh Van Wagenen, Active Transportation Planner, as an
expert on trails in the area and encouraged the Council to direct any questions to him.

Mr. Van Wagenen presented the transportation plan which is included in the packet. He said
that the regional transportation plan is reviewed and readopted every four years, with
opportunities for amendments during that period. He highlighted the importance of local active
transportation plans developed by municipalities, including Riverton's standalone plan from
2015.

Mr. Gruber emphasized that having projects on the regional transportation is a crucial initial
requirement for accessing federal funds through organizations like WFRC, as well as state and
county funding sources. The regional transportation plan serves as a means of assessing the
feasibility of projects, even if their implementation is not anticipated in the immediate future.

Councilmember Pierucci inquired whether the planning primarily revolves around recreational
purposes or if there is a more holistic approach that considers safe walking routes for children
going to school and residents without access to cars.

Mr. Van Wagenen said their primary objective is to create transportation options and facilitate
trips from one location to another. While there is overlap with recreational trips, he emphasized
the importance of providing transportation options for individuals who cannot drive, such as
older adults, people with physical limitations, and those who are legally too young to drive.

Councilmember Pierucci asked what Riverton City could do to enhance the effectiveness of the
trail planning process. In response, Mr. Gruber said they should consider regional connections
between Riverton and neighboring communities. He mentioned the Jordan River Trail as a
primary north-south trail spine that can be utilized by residents to connect to places in both
directions.

Cary Necaise, Public Works Director, said it would be wise to showcase existing active
transportation infrastructure in surrounding cities on the maps when identifying areas where new
trails or connections are desired. This could help secure funding as it presents a cohesive
regional perspective and emphasizes the value of creating a comprehensive and connected trail
system.

Mayor Pro Tem McDougal said that focusing on both maintaining existing trails and actively
pursuing the addition of new trails is a great idea. The Council needs to collaborate with Nathan
Page, Public Works grant writer, to leverage available funding opportunities.

Councilmember Buroker asked if cities moved to wider sidewalks could they incorporate them
into the trail system. In response, Mr. Van Wagenen said while expanding sidewalks can be a
practical approach in areas with limited right of way, it's important to aim for a width that can
accommodate both pedestrians and cyclists comfortably. An eight-foot width is often considered
the minimum for a trail-like experience, while a ten-foot width is preferable. Balancing
constraints and considering the anticipated usage can help determine the appropriate width for
sidewalks-turned-trails in different contexts.

Mayor Pro Tem McDougal said ensuring the safety and comfort of trail users is crucial to
encourage more people to utilize the trails. Simply marking a bike lane or trail with paint on a
major road may not provide the desired level of safety and comfort. The goal is to create an
environment where people feel safe and comfortable using the trails, thereby promoting active
transportation and a healthier, more connected community.

         Engineering Standards Update

Matt Cassel, City Engineer, informed the Council about upcoming updates to the City's
engineering standards and specifications. He explained that these changes would be presented
at a future meeting for adoption and enforcement. The purpose of his presentation was to
provide a preview of the modifications being made to the standards. He clarified that although
there were 70 changes in total, they would only be discussing a select few during the meeting.

Mr. Cassel shared examples of the proposed changes such as the placement of a pipe in
relation to a box. Another example focused on streetlights, noting that the current standard
referred to a generation two LED light, whereas the new standard would reflect the use of
generation three LED lights.

Mr. Cassel offered to provide the full set of proposed changes in a PDF format upon request,
reiterating that the examples shared during the meeting represented the nature of the
modifications taking place.

Councilmember Pierucci asked how these standards are enforced. In response, Mr. Cassel
said that inspectors monitor the progress of construction to ensure adherence to the established
standards. If any deviations are identified, appropriate actions are taken, such as issuing a
notice of violation or implementing a stop-work order until the issues are rectified.

Councilmember Buroker asked Mr. Cassel to explain the change in the fire standard. Mr.
Cassel said that the previous standard called for a one-foot diameter call box, but the new
standard implemented by the International Fire Code requires a 96-foot diameter call box.

         Implementation of New Flag

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, provided information about Senate Bill 31, which established the
new flag for the state of Utah. However, the bill does not take effect until March 9, 2024. He
mentioned that currently, the state recognizes the historic flag as the official flag. While there is
no explicit statute mandating the flying of the state flag, there is a description of the state flag in
the statutes referring to the historic flag.

May 16, 2023




Mr. Carter discussed the potential public perception and reactions to flying the new flag before
its effective date. They mentioned that the intention behind the delayed effective date was to
allow cities and local governments to phase out the old flag and transition to the new one. He
said that the City recently purchased new flags of the historic design, and it would not be cost-
effective to replace them before they have been fully utilized. He recommended using the
current flags until they are worn out and then transitioning to the new flag.

All Councilmembers agreed with this recommendation.

         Resolution No. 23-34 – Adopting Tentative Budgets for the 2023-2024 Fiscal Year

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, clarified that the requirement for this meeting was
to tentatively adopt the tentative budget, as mandated by state code. He said that adjustments
to the budget would be made and further discussions and public hearings would take place in
June. The budget book provides a summary of the annual budgets by fund, with page 15
offering an overview of the budget for the year. Non-reciprocal transfers were explained as
accounting transactions where no cash is exchanged, as the City chooses not to charge itself
for certain services. He invited the Council to ask questions and provide direction during the
discussion on the budget, with the requirement to tentatively adopt the budget at some point
during the meeting.

Mayor Pro Tem McDougal suggested that the Council tentatively adopt the tentative budget first
and then move on to suggesting changes to the budget.

Councilmember McCay expressed concern regarding the organization chart included on page
seven of the budget. She disagreed with the proposed changes and preferred last year's

conflicts with the City's form of government and suggested that it should be voted on by the
residents. Additionally, she requested that in the budget document, any references to "city
administrator" be replaced with "city manager."

Councilmembers and staff discussed whether the tentative budget could be changed or should
be adopted as is.
