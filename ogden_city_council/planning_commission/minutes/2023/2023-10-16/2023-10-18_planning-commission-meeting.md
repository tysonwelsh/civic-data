1.   Discussion, Zone Ogden update

     Mr. Rypien indicated the intent is to update the Planning Commission of the ongoing process to develop
     a unified development code which entails combining the zoning, subdivision, sign and landmarks
     ordinances. He re-introduced team members working with Staff as Johin Jansen, Delaney Spillman
     and Jen Gardner.

     Jen Gardner began the presentation stating the first task was to develop a branding concept for the
     project. It was determined to incorporate the theme of “Make Ogden” by naming the process “Zone
     Ogden”. A web site has been established, which is now available as zone-ogden.com. The team then
     held listening sessions with key members of the community to determine their input relative to these
     regulations. Then, finally, they have reviewed each of the existing ordinances and prepared an
     assessment report. She reviewed the timeline for the process, indicating the update is proposed to be
     completed in four modules, with input from several community advisory committees and then
     workshops. The drafts will then be presented to the Planning Commission and City Council after some
     outreach hearings. She identified several themes they had heard from the community concerning the
     codes, such as predictability, reliability, transparency and collaboration as an effort to reduce the
     number of meetings. She stated there appears to be support for mixed use areas, particularly around
     the downtown, support of water conservation and design standards. She felt the intent and purpose
     of each zone should be clearly defined. Guiding principles include concepts such as the new code
     should be simple, consistent and predictable, as well as modernized. She stated the assessment
     report presents a project overview, giving a background of the project, public engagement process
     being defined and then a general assessment section with recommendations relating to organization
     of regulations.

     Commissioner Safsten asked if there were any particular parts of Ogden’s code which are particularly
     well written, or whether there are items of concern. Mr. Jansen responded, stating her felt the codes
     are overly complicated, and felt they would be difficult for planners to administer. He felt measures
     could be taken to simplify standards, and that better graphics, charts and tables would improve its
administration. He presented several recommendations, including eliminating or consolidating
chapters, ensuring codes comply with State law, making regulations simple to understand, indicating
if there is ambiguity, the development community’s interpretation is favored. He felt the application
procedure could be improved, particularly between legislative items or administrative items. Added
figures and tables are recommended, and regulations should fit the project. He then presented a
standard chapter format and discussed reorganization of chapters, suggesting the following;
General Regulations
Zoning Districts
Use Regulations
Site Development Standards
Supplementary Regulations
Signs
Landmarks
Subdivision
Application Procedures
Definitions

Discussion then centered upon recent State legislation relating to single-family, duplex and townhome
subdivisions and the effort to reduce the required number of meetings. Mr. Jansen also discussed
improvements to the application process and appeal procedures.

Ms. Delaney Spillman then reviewed sign codes, iconic historic signs, lighting of signs as well as
lighting in general, stating lighting should be useful, targeted, low-level, warm colored and controlled.
She state there are policy questions relating to each of these, and would like input from both the
Planning Commission, Staff and community. Commissioner Southwick asked if consideration could
also be given to their previous recommendation to the Council relative to limiting new signage on
Harrison to be monument signs only in an effort to protect the view of the mountains and reduce the
appearance of clutter along Harrison. Commissioner Graf also noted flag signs in the right-of-way
become battered and should be required to either be removed or replaced. He also noted that vinyl
cracks over time and can become unsightly, suggesting signs should be required to be better
maintained. It was indicated clear ordinance language improves the ability of Code Enforcement to
respond. Ms. Spillman then identified policy questions relating to landscaping standards and whether
low water designs are preferable. She presented photos from LocalScapes and stated the City should
determine whether low water options apply to residential, commercial and industrial uses. She also
discussed consolidating parking standards, and making more consistent regulations in the downtown.
Consideration should be given to how shared parking is regulated, assure parking standards comply
with current studies and stated buffering standards should be reviewed.

Mr. Jansen then discussed the style guide and identified items which are included in the first module:
Subdivisions
Conditional Uses
Permitted Uses
Appeal Authority
Definitions
Processes

It is anticipated that Module 2 would include Zoning Districts, Uses and Landscaping. Module 3 would
address subdivision regulations, signs, landmarks, parking and supplementary chapters, and then
Module 4 would address application procedures, with checklists which link to the code.

Commission noted the ongoing process for also updating the General Plan and asked if that should
not be completed in order to give direction to ordinance development. Mr. Rypien responded the items
identified in Module 1 would likely not be affected by the General Plan, and then as the General Plan
policies become updated, they can direct specific ordinance language for the final modules, noting tihe
Unified Development Code becomes the tool to incorporate the Plan.
     It was announced the team will be presenting these items to the City Council on October 24th and a
     public open house at Union Station on October 25th.


2.   Discussion, Utah APA Conference – what was learned.

        Commission discussed voting methods, indicating some communities vote by secret ballot so to
        not be influenced by the votes of other members. While some agreed in concept, it was felt it
        would be difficult to administer. Commission felt it important to have quality discussions before the
        vote to voice their ideas or concerns. They also felt it important to address the chair and to not
        allow interruptions from the audience during the Commission discussion.

        Some attended a presentation on short term rentals and learned that some communities require
        an owner to live within 100 minutes of their rental facility so they would be able to be on site if
        needed. Some communities require a property to be owned for a minimum of two years before it
        can be used as a short-term rental. As these may become a revolving door with little regulation,
        some communities have adopted severe penalties for violations such as 50% of the nightly rent
        for the first violation, or up to 100% or 200% of the nightly rent for major or ongoing violations.
        Others felt an violation should cover the salary of code enforcement officers. There is concern
        that these are commercial businesses in the middle of residential areas.

        Commission discussed accessibility for new construction stating it is less costly than to retrofit
        doors, entryways and restrooms to accommodate wheelchair access. It was felt design standards
        could be adopted to address these issues. Commission asked if there might be ways to provide
        incentives for developers to accommodate better handicap access.

        Commission also discussed the need for open space in the downtown, including plaza areas
        where people can gather. There is a need for options of places where people can be entertained.


Commission reviewed items for the upcoming Planning Commission on November 1st. Mr. Brierley
announced the November work session will be a training session with the City Council and reminded the
Commission there is a special planning commission meeting scheduled for November 29th.

As there was no additional business, the meeting was adjourned at 7:21pm.
