              Wildland Urban Interface Code Agenda Item

              The Utah Legislature passed HB 0048 in the 2025 legislative session. Amongst other things, HB
              48 requires counties and municipalities to adopt the wildland urban interface code by January 1,
              2026, which regulates construction techniques, defensible space, fire department access, and
              water supply for development in the Wildland-Urban Interface. Certain elements of the Town of
              Alta’s land use regulations conflict with provisions of the WUI code and staff will begin a process
              with the Alta Planning Commission to amend Town Code to resolve these conflicts and formally
              adopt the WUI code. We anticipate at least two planning commission meetings, to include a
              public hearing, with an agenda item for adoption by the Town Council in November or
              December 2025.

              Utah Renewable Communities

              The Town of Alta is still participating in Utah Renewable Communities (URC), an interlocal
              agency comprised of 19 Utah municipalities and counties pursuing development of clean energy
              resources sufficient to offset 100% of energy demand within those communities by 2030. URC
              continues its work toward the goal, and despite frequent headlines regarding the short-term
              future of US energy policy and ramifications for the renewables market, URC has made
              important progress in 2025:

                  •    URC’s application to create the Program has been submitted to the Utah Public Service
                       Commission (PSC) in its entirety, and URC anticipates the PRC will decide whether to
                       approve the program in the first quarter of 2026. Residents can submit public comments
                       to the PSC on the Program through December 2025 by emailing psc@utah.gov with the
                       subject line Docket No: 25-035-06.
                  •    After Rocky Mountain Power cancelled its 2022 All-Source RFP in April 2024, URC
                       prepared its own resource solicitation with the assistance of technical consultants and
                       legal counsel. URC’s solicitation was opened in May of 2025 and closed in July. URC
                       received 14 qualifying bids, which substantially exceeded expectations. Click here to
                       read a newsletter article describing what happens next in the resource solicitation and
                       program approval process.

              The Town has taken action a few times in recent years as required to remain a participating
              community in URC. The last step in the Town’s process to finalize its participation in URC is for
              the Town to adopt an ordinance once the PRC approves the Program Application. If the PSC
              approves the program, the Town will have 90 days to adopt the ordinance. The ordinance will

              notices will be mailed to all customers for two billing cycles, after which time a fee will be

              about $4 extra per month to participate in the program.

                                            August 31, 2025, Aircraft Accident in Alta


         At 5:21 pm, August 31st, Alta Central was notified by numerous sources of a small aircraft that
         had crashed in the Patsy Marley area of Albion Basin. Concurrently, the Salt Lake Valley
         Emergency Communications Center (VECC) received numerous reports of the accident. A large
         scale and multi-agency response was initiated. Deputy Roach and Unified Fire Authority
         personnel responded to the crash site and found one occupant of the aircraft deceased and the
         second occupant with life threatening injuries. The following briefing/summary is submitted to
         the Town Council regarding this event:


              •   An Air-Med helicopter was dispatched to the scene and transported the survivor to the
                  University of Utah Hospital.
              •   All available Alta Deputies were called in from off-duty status.
              •   Marshal Morey requested mutual aid from the Salt Lake County Sheriff's Office,
                  specifically asking for manpower, crime scene technicians, public information officer, a
                  mobile command post, and supervisor.
              •   Federal Aviation Authorities were notified and briefed.
              •   Upon arrival of Sheriff’s Office Command staff, Marshal Morey requested that Salt Lake
                  County take the lead in scene management and investigation while maintaining a joint
                  command structure with the AMO. This decision was made based on the need for
                  specialized crime scene processing equipment and investigative personnel, the need to
                  maintain security of the site for an extended period of time, and coordination with
                  hospitals and the medical examiner.
              •   Upon completion of site documentation, the deceased party was transferred to the
                  custody of the medical examiner at approximately 1am September 1st.
              •   Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) and National Transportation and Safety Board
                  (NTSB) personnel arrived in Alta on September 1st and conducted their respective
                  investigations.
              •   Scene security /integrity operations were conducted until approximately 1100am on
                  September 2nd at which time a recovery team began aircraft removal.
September 10, 2025 Alta Council Meeting Packet                                                         Page 13 of 109




              •   Aircraft removal and traffic control operations ceased at 5:30pm, September 2nd.
              •   Agencies involved in this response included the Alta Marshal’s Office, Unified Fire
                  Authority, Sandy City Fire Department, Salt Lake County Sheriff’s Office, Unified Police
                  Department, Air-Med, Salt Lake City Public Utilities (Watershed), FAA, NTSB, and United
                  States Forest Service.
              •   The operational period of this incident was approximately 48 hours in duration.
              •   The Albion Summer Road was closed to non-essential travel during the operational
                  period.


         The Marshal’s Office extends our deepest sympathy to the individuals and families that have
         been tragically affected by this event. We also want to extend our profound gratitude to our
         partner agencies for their professional and unyielding support of the Town of Alta.


         Many in the community witnessed this event as it occurred and have been affected by it.
         Please reach out to us if we can help in any way.

HB 48-Wildland Urban Interface (WUI) updates:
House Bill 48 (HB 48), titled “Wildland Urban Interface Modifications,” was passed during the 2025 General
Legislative Session in Utah. The law focuses on reducing wildfire risks in the Wildland Urban Interface (WUI) and
establishes new responsibilities, standards, and fees for homeowners, municipalities, and counties.

Based on HB48, Counties and Municipalities in Utah are required to:

    1. Adopt and enforce Utah’s WUI code by January 1, 2026.
    2. Create a WUI Code map by January 1, 2026, identifying areas the Code will apply, based on wildfire risk.

    The link below is a risk map developed by Utah Forestry Fire and State Lands (FFSL).

         https://wrap.wildfirerisk.utah.gov/Map/Public/#map-themes

A key tool in the development and adoption is the Utah Wildfire Risk Assessment Portal (UWRAP) map with the focus
on the Structure Exposure Score hosted by Utah Division of Forestry, Fire State Lands. This score ranges from 1-10 with
10 being the highest potential risk. UFA’s Fire Prevention, Wildland & Emergency Management and liaisons have been
very engaged in learning and applying supportive measures to ensure we are in step with the forthcoming changes.

For additional questions or concerns related to HB48, contact Battalion Chief Jay Torgersen jtorgersen@unifiedfireut.gov
801-824-3716 or Fire Marshal Watkins wwatkins@unifiedfireut.gov 801-450-3808.

Recruit Camp 60 Update: The 23 Firefighters from Recruit Camp 60 are about four weeks into their 15-week training
camp. The group is progressing nicely, and we have seen improvements in the areas of firefighting, group camaraderie,
and overall fitness. The highlights of the past month have been focused on apparatus familiarization, hose evolutions,
ladders, introduction to flashover and fire behavior, and some live-fire training scenarios. Throughout the months of
camp, they will continue to drill down into each aspect of the job, honing the skills and tasks of the job for accuracy,
timeliness, and overall proficiency. Recruit graduation is scheduled for November 13 at the JATC.

Lithium-Ion Battery Safety: UFA is launching a safety education effort for proper use, storage and disposal of Lithium-
Ion Batteries. Further information is available through UFA Social Media pages and the UFA homepage.

Public CPR Courses: UFA’s EMS Division continues to offer a monthly public CPR certification course for those
needing to maintain their credentials. The 4-hour course costs $60 and includes a 2-year American Heart Association BLS
certification, issued by UFA. Registration is available through the UFA website. If your city would like to host a class,
please contact us — we’re happy to accommodate.

Safety Message: Back-to-School Safety

Emergencies can strike without warning, so being prepared is key. At home and in the classroom, always identify at least
two ways out of every room. Your main exit may be blocked by smoke, fire, or obstacles, which makes a secondary route
critical for survival. Practice these escape routes regularly so they become second nature. Make sure doors and windows
can be opened easily, and keep pathways clear of clutter.
September 10, 2025 Alta Council Meeting Packet                                                               Page 15 of 109
Just as important, every family and classroom should have a designated reunification location, a safe meeting place
outside and away from danger, such as a neighbor’s house, a flagpole, or a playground and safe location. Once outside,
never re-enter the building. Having a clear reunification plan allows firefighters to focus on the emergency and ensures
everyone can be accounted for quickly. Preparedness builds confidence: knowing two ways out and where to meet could
save your life.

         2. DISSCUSION REGARDING THE TOWN OF ALTA MISSION AND VALUES

    00:00:40

    Chris Cawley began by outlining goals to refine the Town’s mission and values and prepare for a
    September strategic planning meeting. Cawley reviewed the history of past discussions, noting that the
    January 2024 retreat produced priorities such as sustaining the Marshal’s Office, addressing capital
    needs, and pursuing long-range planning. Cawley explained that the mission statement initially drafted
    by staff emphasized safety, quality of life, environmental protection, growth management, and the
    local economy, with values including accessibility, transparency, accountability, and equity. After
    further discussion, Cawley proposed a more concise mission statement: “to provide service with
    integrity,” accompanied by revised core values. Councilmembers generally supported the shorter
    mission statement and debated whether the values should include integrity, equity, or alternatives
    such as fairness and equality. Anctil suggested balancing the wording, Byrne raised concerns about
    misinterpretation of “equity,” and Morgan emphasized the usefulness of the statements for
    onboarding staff and guiding decisions. Heimark recommended that values be reviewed annually to

    Alta Town Council Meeting
    August 13, 2025                                      1
September 10, 2025 Alta Council Meeting Packet                                                         Page 44 of 109


    ensure consistency with actions. Morey described how the Marshal’s Office applied its guiding
    principles in practice, reinforcing the importance of simple standards. After further discussion, the
    Council reached consensus to retain the concise mission statement, remove “integrity” from the list of
    values, and replace “equity” with “equality,” resulting in four core values: accountability, community,
    environment, and equality. The Council agreed that this item could be acted upon in the regular
    meeting agenda.

         3. DISCUSSION REGARDING UPCOMING COUNCIL RETREATS
    00:34:20

    Cawley opened the discussion by emphasizing the need for additional dedicated time outside of
    regular council meetings to address big capital projects and their financing. Cawley noted that Alta, like
    many small governments, is facing a situation where past infrastructure has been built without
    sufficient savings for future replacements, making the scope of upcoming projects feel daunting. The
    last comparable project was the town office in 1994, illustrating that the council has limited experience
    with large-scale municipal projects.

    Cawley outlined that the first strategic session is scheduled for September 4, 9 a.m. to noon,
    potentially at Our Lady of the Snows (OLS), and will focus on:

         •    Existing conditions of buildings and water/sewer infrastructure
         •    Public finance options and constraints
         •    Facilities plan outcomes

    Cawley emphasized that this would be a facilitated session, with Julie DeLong engaged to manage the
    discussion, allowing staff to participate fully. A public finance expert will also be present to answer
    questions. Drafts of the facilities plan and the water/sewer master plans will be distributed in advance,
    acknowledging minor gaps in floor plans in prior drafts.
    John Byrne highlighted that by extending the timelines for water and sewer projects to 10–12 years,
    rate increases could fund these infrastructure needs, likely keeping them under 15–20%, leaving
    external financing primarily for the new town building. The focus will first be on understanding the
    projects themselves, then discussing financing options.
    The council discussed the format of the strategic session, valuing the dynamic, collaborative style of
    prior retreats (tables with staff, interactive exercises), while acknowledging challenges in recording for
    public transparency. Elise Morgan suggested hiring a professional videographer familiar with public
    meetings to capture the discussion, enabling a video record that could be posted online or transcribed
    if needed.
    The session would combine structured presentations for clarity with interactive discussion for
    engagement. Byrne suggested that agenda planning should prioritize projects first, then financing, to
    help educate the public and facilitate understanding. The session is legally considered a special public
    meeting, and appropriate notice must be given, but no changes to the regular council meeting calendar
    are necessary.

         3. ALTA SKI AREA UPDATE, MIKE MAUGHAN

    Mike Maughan provided updates on upcoming events, construction projects, and operations in Alta.

    terrain data with LIDAR measurements for snow depths, providing a more accurate base map for
    future projects. Regarding the Supreme lift project, all terminals and footings are now aligned and

    Maughan also provided an update on the Albion Day Lodge expansion, noting that footings and precast

    completion of the Wyssen tower project, ongoing trail work beneath Baldy, and the Sugarloaf lift
    retrofit, which involves motor replacement and expansion of the operator house. The crosstow project

    preparing snow-making capabilities by November 1.

              Re: Work session on Shrontz Estate Petition to Rezone Patsey Marley Hill property

              Background

              The Estate of Joanne L Shrontz (the Estate) proposes to rezone a portion of the Patsey Marley
              Hill property to Alta Zoning District FM 20 and build a condominium project on that portion of
              the property, while applying a conservation easement to all land area within the property above
              the Albion Basin Summer Road (ASBR) and donating that easement to a qualified easement
              holder. The Alta Planning Commission held a public hearing on the Estate’s petition to rezone
              during its July 15, 2025 meeting, and forwarded a neutral recommendation to the council to
              approve the rezone subject to several conditions on the development and the process that must
              occur for the rezone to be finalized. For an extensive background on the Estate’s proposal,
              please review the staff report on the petition to rezone, which can be found on pages 13-22 of
              the July 15 meeting packet—click here to view the packet.

              Staff recommends reviewing the minutes from the July 15 meeting, which were approved in the
              commission’s August meeting and can be found here.

              Goals for September 10th Town Council Meeting

              Due to the large scope of the petition to rezone and all its potential implications, Staff along
              with the Estate thought it was important to first focus on the Council’s response to the proposal
              before evaluating the process and details of amending the 2014 Development Agreement and
              Stipulation and the Conservation Easement. The petition is in front of the Council as a work
              session and no vote or decision will be made at this meeting.

              Summary of Key Topics

              Development of the Patsy Marley Hill property is an extremely complex topic due to the
              overlapping jurisdictions present in Alta, the Estate’s access to drinking water, past litigation
              between the Town, SLC, and the Estate, and the fact that the Patsy Marley Hill property exhibits
              significant environmental and social value while being one of the largest currently developable
              private parcels in the Cottonwood Canyons. The future of the property has been a key question
              in the Town’s future planning for generations.
September 10, 2025 Alta Council Meeting Packet                                                                      Page 56 of 109




              Staff recommends the Town Council focus on the core of the Estate’s proposal, which is to
              develop a condominium project, in a single building below the ABSR, while applying a
              conservation easement above the ABSR, instead of a ten-lot subdivision covering the entire 26-
              acre parcel.

                  •    Condominiums: Some are concerned that allowing the Estate to build condominiums
                       could lead to the Town allowing condominiums throughout the Base Facilities Zone,
                       which has been discussed frequently in the past but never approved. Is the Estate’s
                       open space proposal, as well as the proposal to develop an alternative to the Estate’s
                       subdivision access plan across the ABSR, worth whatever expectations could be created
                       for the Town to allow condominiums in the Base Facility Zone?
                  •    Condominiums, continued: Some fear that the units in the Estate’s building could be
                       “dark rooms” if unit owners do not choose to rent. Rentals are allowed in the FM-20
                       zone proposed by the Estate. Others suspect that ten, 8,500 square-foot homes on the
                       snow-covered ABSR would also be infrequently occupied.
                  •    Massing: Is the building itself, which the Estate proposes to be up to 160,000 square
                       feet above and below grade, a less significant visual impact on the landscape than the
                       development of ten homes that can be up to 8,500 square feet? Is one very large
                       building preferrable to ten not as large buildings, spread across the property, each with
                       their own driveway, utilities, and other site improvements? The Estate claims there will
                       be 85,000 square feet of “saleable” floor area, and the rest of the floor area would be
                       comprised of “back of house” uses such as parking, hallways, lobbies, amenities such as
                       a spa, etc. However, the Town does not have a definition of saleable floor area and
                       hence has no way to regulate it.
                  •    Density: Is the density and intensity of use appropriate for the site? In exchange for the
                       10 homes, 33 units are proposed. 3 of those units would be workforce or affordable,
                       and one would be an ADA-accessible unit. Under the FM-20 zone, the units can be
                       rented on a short-term basis.
                  •    Planning Commission Conditions: The council should discuss the planning commission’s
                       conditions.

Patsey Marley Development Alternative
1. Preservation of Patsey Marley Hill
 • 18.6 acre parcel above Summer Road protected with Conservation Easement.
 • 6.5 acre parcel rezoned for condo development, with 1.5 acres of parcel above Summer Road protected with Conservation Easement.
2. Building height consistent with Snowpine lodge (25’ above Summer Road)
 • FM-20 Zone permits height as approved by Planning Commission.
 • Proposed project consolidates density in small corner of 25 acre property, pushing up height in that corner and leaving 20 acres above Summer
   Road untouched.
3. Proposed Density
 • Project limited to maximum 29 condos with maximum 85,000 sf salable area.
 • Project limited to maximum 132 bedrooms/guest rooms.
4. Workforce Housing
 • Project proposing minimum 3 workforce housing units with minimum 8 bedrooms in addition to the salable condo units.
5. Access
 • Widen shared section of Summer Road for OSV on NE (high) side with pavement for cars on SW (low) side.
 • Widen Summer Road on Patsey Marley property for OSV parking just past start of project driveway.
 • Alternative separate driveway is possible, but subject to cooperation from USFS and Ski Corp.
6. Water
 • Based on water meter data from similar properties in Deer Valley, proposed condo project should use less water than is currently allocated to the
   10 approved single family lots.
 • Project team pulling water consumption data from properties within Town of Alta for comparison.
 • Salt Lake Public Utilities has provided letter confirming support for project.
 • DDW has granted exception based on consultant’s analysis.
 • SLCPU has reviewed consultant report and DDW exception and is supportive of proposed condo alternative.
7. Summary
 • Proposed rezoning facilitates preservation of 20 acres of Patsey Marley Hill.
 • Development Agreement would clarify entitlements and obligations for property.
 • Final building design subject to Conditional Use Permit.

       Dear Mr. Livsey:

       The Division of Drinking Water (the Division) received the plans and specifications for the Patsey
       Marley Subdivision Waterline Project from your consultant Darin Hawkes, P.E. with Aqua
       Engineering on May 5, 2018. Darin Hawkes, P.E. of Aqua Engineering certified the results of the
       hydraulic analysis, and that the hydraulic model indicates compliance with the flow and pressure
       requirements for public drinking water systems. Updated version of drawings and specifications were
       received on May 30, 2018

       Our understanding of this water line project is that Patsey Marley plans to develop 10 service
       connections, which will serve 30 permanent residents. This proposed system will receive water from
       the Town of Alta’s (System#18049) 365,000 Gallon Tank (ST001). In this first stage of construction
       there will be installation of approximately 1,800 feet (Station 0+00 to 18+05) of 12-inch HDPE
       PE4710 DR11 water line from the water tank to the parking structure where a future pump station will
       be installed consisting of two types of pumps (fire flow and culinary variable frequency drive (VFD)
       pumps). The pumps will be installed in 2019 or later in the second phase of construction. The layout of
       the pipeline from the pump station will be 10-inch HDPE PE4710 DR11 pipe for about 771 feet
       (Station 18+05 to 25+76). Plan approval for the future pump station will be required.

       Part of the Town of Alta residents do not have adequate pipe sizing for fire flow and experience
       freezing pipes in the winter in their 2-inch existing HDPE pipeline. The Patsey Marley subdivision’s
       pipeline extension will serve the Town of Alta during emergency situations and alleviate freezing
       issues. This project will also provide a higher level of fire protection with the installation of new fire
       hydrants.

       Hawkes on May 17, 2018 and learned of the wetland concern regarding the location of the proposed
       pump station to be constructed in 2019 or later. This concern will be resolved with the UDOT right of

              Managed IT Services

              The Town currently contracts with Executech to manage our IT services and needs. We have been
              contracted with them for many years. From what I gather, the relationship started off strong, but
              unfortunately, we have had some growing pains in the last few years. For a long time, we had a
              dedicated technician assigned to us that was familiar with our infrastructure, unique needs, and
              our staff, and whom we could contact directly. In recent years, staff turnover at Executech has
              made that approach unfeasible and we were ultimately transferred to a service-desk model that
              has resulted in some undesirable outcomes and challenges.

                  •    Inconsistent response time to service requests, often not meeting the service-level
                       agreement targets
                  •    Failure to comply with BCI requirements necessary for providing IT services to our Police
                       Department and Justice Court
                            o Due to access to sensitive material like criminal justice information, all support
                                technicians who access our devices and network have to be vetted and certified
                                to do so – this became impossible when we were transferred to a global-service
                                desk as we never knew who would be assisting us
                  •    Difficult to budget as service-model and costs fluctuate at vendors discretion

              In May, Executech alerted us that they are transferring all their clients to yet another new service
              model by July 1, 2025. While the new model has some clear benefits, it does come with a cost
              increase of about 45% (approximately $7,500/year). Due to this unexpected jump in price, we
              decided it was in our best interest to issue an RFP for these services. The RFP was issued in July
              and the Town received 14 proposals.

              Proposals were evaluated by a committee on the following criteria, with a minimum threshold of
              75 technical points required to advance to the cost-scoring portion of the process. Five of the
              proposals met that threshold. After the cost-analysis was completed, we had a clear winner in
              Tech Legion with a grand total of 91.81 points and scoring the highest technical score of 82.17.
