Councilmember Harker reported that Aaron Johnson who served on the Parks and Recreation

various locations at the request of residents but typically, once they were in place the City received
multiple requests to have them removed. It was determined that the City Engineer would make a
presentation to the City Council at the next meeting.

2.      City Council Chair and Vice-Chair Election

City Recorder Jamie Brooks reminded those gathered that at the previous meeting when only four
councilmembers had been present, the vote for council chair had resulted in a tie. The list of
nominees remained unchanged—Councilmembers Harker and Knudsen had been nominated for
chair, while Councilmembers Cochran, Harker and Knudsen had been nominated for vice-chair.

City Council Coordinator Kris Heineman distributed new ballots for chair. Once the completed
ballots were tallied by Ms. Heineman and witnessed by Ms. Brooks, it was announced that the
Council had elected Bob Knudsen as the Chair for 2023. Therefore, his name would be removed
from the vice-chair ballot.

Ms. Heineman distributed ballots for vice-chair and once the votes were tallied and the tally was
witnessed, it was announced that Councilmember Cochran had been elected to serve as vice-chair
for 2023.

It was determined that Anna Barbieri would continue to act as chair for the rest of the evening.

The Mayor explained how very grateful she was for Senator Karen Mayne’s many years of service
to Taylorsville residents, and all those on the west side of the Salt Lake valley. She had long been
a champion to her constituents, and the Mayor wished her the very best.

Mayor Overson reported that city leaders had recently held their annual legislative breakfast, with
attendance by Senators Karen Kwan and Wayne Harper as well as Representative Jim Dunnigan.
This breakfast was an opportunity for city officials to hear from local legislators and for those
legislators to hear about Taylorsville’s concerns and priorities.

Earlier today the Mayor and the Youth Council attended Local Officials Day at the State Capitol.
Mayor Overson appreciated Councilmembers Harker and Knudsen for their participation. She said
it was always an enlightening and interesting experience to spend that time with the teens who
took part in the event. They conducted a planning exercise before later taking part in a mock debate
regarding the issue of restricting cell phone devices in Utah public schools. Later in the day the
group heard both the Governor and First Lady Abby Cox speak. The Mayor appreciated ULCT for
sponsoring the event and Ms. Heineman for coordinating the Youth Council’s involvement.

Now that the Utah State Legislature was in session, the ChamberWest Legislative Affairs
Committee and Legislative Caucus were meeting weekly so that members could keep a close eye
on bills that would affect local government and business. The ULCT also had a legislative policy
committee that met weekly to discuss pending legislature with current legislators. Mayor Overson
was very thankful for the work John Hiskey continued to do for Taylorsville, ensuring that the
Council and Administration remained abreast of the many issues that could and would affect
residents and businesses within the city.

The Granite School District had recently invited local mayors to meet and discuss the many things
that had an impact on local youth. These included district policies, varying points of view, and
concerns for the future. It was anticipated that this meeting would be held on a quarterly basis.

     Scott Harrington had presented some budget amendments earlier in the month and the Mayor
expressed appreciation to those councilmembers who had been willing to dive into the budget and
ask questions. The administration tried to keep budget adjustments to a minimum, although there
would always be some fine-tuning that became necessary here and there. She appreciated Mr.
Harrington for continually keeping an eye on the budget and ensuring that the City remained
fiscally responsible.

Mayor Overson reported that the Business License Technician position had just been filled. There
were still openings for a code enforcement officer, a victim advocate and substitute crossing
guards.

3.      REPORTS

        3.1      Salt Lake County Public Works Report – Crystal Hulbert

      Director of Salt Lake County Public Works’ Operations Department Crystal Hulbert
explained that her division’s primary activity in the previous several months was obviously snow
plowing. Plows had been out in Taylorsville approximately 15 times so far that season. She
indicated there had been quite a bit of employee turnover since the previous year, so training was
conducted at the beginning of the season since some were new to the work. She was very proud of
her crew who worked twelve-hour shifts when it snowed for four days straight. She said that they
did the best they could with such a large volume of snow.

Between snow storms, the operations division worked on filling pot holes and clearing storm drain
grates to ensure that water did not cause any localized flooding. The next priority after that was
crack sealing the roadways.

Councilmember Cochran commented that despite the City issuing reminders in various formats,
he had noticed quite a few vehicles had continued to park on the side of the roads during snow
storms and wondered if that presented challenges for the snow plow crews. Ms. Hulbert agreed
that leaving cars on the roadways during and after a snow storm increased the chance that those
cars could be inadvertently damaged by plows and at times, her staff simply had to leave some
streets un-plowed because there were so many cars parked on the sides of the road.

Ms. Hulbert reminded anyone listening not to pass snowplows, explaining that doing so put the

       3.2       West Valley Animal Services Report – Melanie Bennett

      Melanie Bennett presented statistical information pertaining to the second half of 2022.
There had been 471 animals impounded during that time compared to 390 during the same period
in 2021. The shelter was not currently accepting owner turnovers because there simply was no
room due to all the strays that were being housed. She stated that the Humane Society was full as
well and that the rest of the nation was experiencing overcrowding as well.
    • There were 1,692 animal control case reports compared to 1,448 in 2021
    • 332 park patrols were conducted (looking for opportunities to provide education
       regarding leash laws)
    • Response times had been dramatically reduced
    • There were currently 145 animals housed at the shelter, while the daily average had been
       132 between January and June 2022.
    • The veterinarian’s schedule had increased from two days per week to three. She had
       completed 1,006 surgeries in 2021 and 1386 in 2022.
    • Listed several adoption events that were held during the previous several months. 243
       animals had been adopted during the second half of 2022
    • WVPD held a “Paw Patrol” event wherein a handful of their police officers walked some
       of the dogs in public parks and talked with the citizens as they did so. It was good for the
       animals to get out, and also good for the community to be able to speak with officers in a
       positive setting.

Ms. Bennett reminded the Council that at a previous meeting she had introduced a Riverton High
student who had been conducting research involving feral cats. She reported that the exceptional
student took her research project to a national agriscience competition in Indianapolis and was
awarded the third-place prize.

In response to a question from Councilmember Cochran, Ms. Bennett explained that there was a
volunteer application on the West Valley City website for anyone who was interested in donating
some time at the shelter.

         6.2    Fraud Risk Assessment Presentation – Scott Harrington

     Mr. Harrington reminded the councilmembers that in recent years the state auditor had begun
requiring a fraud risk assessment which he then displayed on the screen. The assessment was
designed to ensure that each governmental agency had checks in place to protect tax-payer funds
from potential fraud. The City had recently scored 375 compared to its score of 355 points the
previous year because all city employees and elected officials were now required to annually
commit to abide by a statement of ethical behavior. There was a link to a fraud hotline on the
City’s website and although a written policy that addressed ethical behavior and reporting of
fraudulent activity was not yet in place, one was being drafted.
