3. Consideration of Concept approval for the Willow Point, a 420-lot residential
   development located at approximately 4100 West Hardman Way.
   Mr. Struthers reported on the concept plan. He suggested that they add a condition that those
   issues be addressed at the time of preliminary plat that includes easements for public utilities.

    Councilor Koivisto inquired about the requested zone change. Mr. Struthers clarified that there
    is a development agreement that allows them to have a certain number of residential units in
    the commercial zone. He said this is a very unique situation that applies to both of the concept
    applications. The development agreement overrides the zoning. There is a maximum allowed
    density in the agreement and this should use all of that.

    Councilor Koivisto stated that she received many emails from residents asking if it was
    possible to table this item until residents can come to a Council meeting in person to express
    their concerns. Councilor Southwick asked if the concern from the residents was the zone
    change, because if that’s their concern, we already have a development agreement that needs
    to be honored. Councilor Koivisto stated that they would still like to voice their opinions in a
    public meeting.

    Mayor Johnson stated that they’ve done everything they can to provide an avenue for the public
    to comment on these issues. He explained that it’s the applicant’s right to present their
    application to the Council. Councilor Albrecht inquired if Councilor Koivisto had suggested
    that the residents submit their comments. Councilor Koivisto stated that she did suggest that.
    Councilor Albrecht said that she was surprise then that the residents didn’t at least avail
    themselves with that opportunity if they felt so strongly. Councilor Koivisto stated that she
    told the residents that she would at least ask the applicant since we are so close to returning to
    public meetings. She said she had a handful of emails that she’d be willing to forward to the
    other Councilmembers.

    Brandon Watson was representing the applicant. He stated that this is part of the overall
    development agreement to allow residential in the commercial zone. He said that they would
    not feel comfortable waiting for an in-person meeting. Councilor Southwick stated that the
    Council has requested that residents submit their comments by email or online, so he was
    concerned that the Council would be giving special privileges for this one application if they
    choose to table it.

    There was further discussion about tabling the item until the public can comment at an in
    person meeting.

    Mayor Johnson stated that the applicant is here for concept plan, so the things that they should
    be discussing tonight are things like layouts, colors, and architecture. The density has already
    been decided per the development agreement. He said that they tabled this in the pervious
    meeting because there was concern with the utilities layout and easements and that has been
    resolved.

    Mr. Watson stated that they asked residents of the Exchange what they can do better for this
    project. They also wanted to make it more walkable. This will have different color schemes
    with new architecture. Councilor Albrecht stated that with this many units, variation is very

    important, so she encouraged design innovation for this project. Mr. Watson stated that the
    units will have some individuality with surface materials and colors.

    Councilor Koivisto asked if the applicant would be willing to reach out to TSSD and get a
    positive recommendation from them. Councilor Condie stated that TSSD is not a land use
    authority, so it may not be appropriate to ask the developer to require their recommendation.
    There was discussion regarding the relationship with TSSD and how they plan for capacity.
    Mr. Wood stated that it hasn’t been the tradition to ask for a positive recommendation from
    TDDS and even if they said no, he doesn’t believe they have any legal standing to stop
    development. Lorin Powell stated that a developer shouldn’t have to work with TSSD. It’s the
    city’s job to work with TSSD and ensure there is adequate capacity for current and future use.

    There was discussion about the development agreement. It rearranges some of the density and
    brought it along 2100 North. It allows a certain amount of units to be added to a commercial
    zone. Mr. Wood stated that the development agreement went through the public process and
    was voted on and approved by the City Council. He said those rights are now vested with the
    developer.

    Motion:     Councilor Condie moved to approve the Concept for the Willow Point, a 420-lot
                residential development located at approximately 4100 West Hardman Way;
                subject to the completion of all Development Review Committee and Planning
                Commission comments. Councilor Southwick seconded the motion and requested
                that the motion include the comments from Mr. Struthers regarding the need to
                address utility easements and concerns at preliminary plat. Councilor Condie
                amended his motion to include those comments. Councilor Southwick stated that
                his second stands.

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Yes; Councilor Southwick, Yes;
    Councilor Hancock, Yes; and Councilor Koivisto, Yes. The motion passed unanimously.

    Councilor Koivisto noted for the record that she voted yes because of the developer’s vested
    rights.

5. Consideration of Resolution #2020-37, approval of the Goulding Annexation Agreement
   on 55.37-acres of property located at approximately 3000 West Traverse Mountain
   Boulevard with proposed TOD zoning and associated TOD Land Use Plan.
   Jerrod Morgan was representing the applicant.

    There was discussion regarding the public land dedicated to the City and that if the TOD
    doesn’t come to fruition, then that land is given back to the developer. Councilor Albrecht had
    concerns with this because the density has already been granted as part of that public land
    being donated.

    Mr. Morgan stated that if there is no public transit station at all then they would like the
    property back and they would then come back with a different plan. He said that the City will
    still be able to determine the zoning for the property.

    Councilor Hancock stated that the density has already been granted so if there is no transit
    station then there will already be too much density and anything else build on the donated 6
    acres could be problematic. If the transit station doesn’t come then he would like that dedicated
    property to be a park or open space.

    Mr. Struthers stated that if the transit station doesn’t come then the developer would need to
    come back and amend their land use plan.

    Mr. Wood stated that if the developer decided to max out all the residential units that they are
    entitled to in the next 20 years, and the transit station doesn’t happen, then the City is not
    obligated to give them additional density in place of the transit station. If they don’t max out
    their density and wanted to move residential units to where the station would have been, then
    they would need to amend the land use plan.

    Councilor Southwick suggested that they work with the developer after 20 years if the station
    is not built.

    Jason Walker stated that when this was originally approved the motion included that the park
    impact fees stay in the development, but that was not included in this agreement because he
    understood that the Council’s intent was not to allocate park impact fees solely for this
    development.

    Motion:     Councilor Hancock moved to approve Resolution #2020-37, approval of the
                Goulding Annexation Agreement on 55.37-acres of property located at
                approximately 3000 West Traverse Mountain Boulevard with proposed TOD
                zoning and associated TOD Land Use Plan; and include in the developer’s
Lehi City Council Meeting                         4                                     May 26, 2020
                agreement that at the 20 year mark if the transit station is not built, then the
                developer and the City will meet to determine what would be the best solution or
                use for the transit station property, and note that it could be left as open space.
                Councilor Koivisto seconded the motion.

                There was discussion about the park impact fees being distributed throughout the
                city and not exclusively for this development. Mr. Wood stated that the agreement
