2. Administrative Reports
    2.1. Discussion regarding an amendment to the interlocal agreement with Salt Lake County
         for the Copper Creek Channel Improvement Project – Blake Thomas, Community
         Development Director
Community Development Director Blake Thomas explained this amendment request between
Herriman City and Salt Lake County was for construction costs incurred for the Copper Creek
channel. He reported all work for this project was complete was operational. The purpose was to
gather runoff water from fields and Copper Creek and direct it into Midas Creek to reduce flooding
risk for downstream developments. He recalled the original approval had stated the developer
would be reimbursed with impact fees, but the project was not actually impact fee eligible. The
City had worked with Salt Lake County to acquire funds to reimburse the project. He explained the
amendment would reallocate $700,000 from Salt Lake County to Herriman for construction costs.
The funds would not be spent until appropriately received and budgeted by the City, which would
require a budget amendment.

    2.2. Discussion regarding a purchase agreement for 6400 West right-of-way acquisition –
         Blake Thomas, Community Development Director
Community Development Director Thomas reported Herriman City had applied for a grant to
purchase right-of-way along 6400 West. He referred to the purchase agreement and indicated
there were two recorded owners. He oriented the Council as to the location of the right-of-way
and indicated two of these properties were owned by The Last Holdout. Director Thomas added
the larger parcel was adjacent to the Heritage development, and the other development was close
to Teton Ranch. He explained contractually, they were obligated to incorporate these areas into an
already existing subdivision and noted the budget discussion revolved around amending the
agreement. The overall project would be stretched out over multiple fiscal years.

   2.3. *Discussion of a proposed amendment to the Creek Ridge Master Development

    2.4. Discussion to Identify Principles that Guide the Legislative Priorities of the City
Assistant City Manager Tami Moody reported every year, issues were placed before State
Legislature which impacted local government. The City Council was asked to approve a platform of
legislative priorities for the upcoming session. She explained it was helpful for staff to have
established principles and priorities for bills introduced at the last minute to allow for the City to
quickly form a response in case staff was not able to contact each member of Council.
August 24, 2022 – City Council Minutes                                        Page 3 of 8



Assistant City Manager Moody identified three to five key principles which would provide a
framework for the City’s response to state and federal legislation. She further elaborated the
intention was to assist staff in addressing legislation in a way which promoted the interest of
Herriman. The importance of focus and flexibility at the State level was noted as well as the need
to advocate for local control. Assistant City Manager Moody asked Council for direction on their
desired priorities. Council consensus was to proceed with priorities as presented.

Councilmember Shields discussed there was currently lateral authority over land management
between the City and State and felt there should be unilateral land use authority. He thought land
use management required local coordination and needed the support of annexed areas.
Councilmember Henderson agreed local control must be a focus. Councilmember Ohrn also
emphasized local control and less governmental oversight on private property. She opined local
jurisdiction should be allowed to deal with local issues.

Councilmember Ohrn felt short-term rentals should be overseen by the local jurisdiction.
Councilmember Shields agreed. Councilmember Ohrn heard the League wanted bans or
restrictions on short-term rentals and agreed decisions regarding rentals were left for the Council
to consider, not another authority.

Councilmember Shields noted the difficulties various City employees had experienced in finding
local housing. He opined the Council could help with employee retention through facilitating
housing programs for first responders, teachers and other employees who have been forced to
commute due to housing prices. Councilmember Shields relayed a previous discussions
surrounding the lack of public transportation within city limits which makes Herriman ineligible for
regional transportation funding. Mayor Palmer agreed and suggested there should be a separated
set of rules for eligibility on transportation projects. Councilmember Ohrn said there was a lot of
legislative power in Weber County and thought it would be beneficial to collaborate with cities in
Weber County to help with transportation conversations, since they were in a similar situation.
Mayor Palmer agreed the City should advocate for light rail tracks or an express bus from Utah
Transit Authority (UTA) and suggested to partner with surrounding communities to help with
advocating efforts. He wondered if bus rapid transit (BRT) would be the best solution.
Councilmember Ohrn expressed concern if adding a BRT line would increase crime rates.
Councilmember Shields thought the city needed to be more forward thinking and should consider
new opportunities available for transportation. Mayor Palmer stated it would become more
important as the population continued to grow.

Councilmember Shields referenced past discussions regarding community resources and asked if
home ownership could be encouraged in high inflation areas. City Manager Cherpeski said staff
had been paying attention to the signs and working towards progress. He reported last year,
Herriman had been approved for a study on transportation land use connection to examine
preserved corridor in Olympia Hills. It was noted the study would commence shortly and was
anticipated to take several months to complete.

   2.5. Storage for non-city groups at Butterfield Park – Nathan Cherpeski, City Manager
City Manager Cherpeski explained several non-city groups store equipment for their events at
Butterfield Park. He offered several examples and questioned if there was adequate storage to
accommodate everyone.

Councilmember Henderson acknowledged the limited space and noted the City property should be
utilized for City purposes as there are several functions held at Butterfield Park. Assistant City
Manager Thomas noted the baseball leagues purchased equipment needed for field maintenance.
This equipment was stored at the park, and clarified it was not individual sports equipment.

Councilmember Shields suggested a compromise and noted there were not a lot of other activities
going on at the pavilion during football season. He proposed to allow trailer to be stored
somewhere on property during the football season to allow for a more efficient distribution of
jerseys to the players. Once the season was completed, the trailer would need to be moved to an
offsite storage area. He felt it would be fair in the immediate future, although acknowledged it
would not be able to be a continued practice. Councilmember Henderson had some hesitation
given the volume of items needing to be stored and expressed caution given uncertainties.

Councilmember Shields felt the storage of items on City property seemed like the main issue and
Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn agreed. She added she would be willing to waive restrictions about
trailers on private property if the groups could find a resident willing to allow equipment to be
stored on property. Councilmember Hodges said it was difficult and costly to move equipment
every weekend. It was clarified while Mountain Football Conference and Mountain Ridge
Conference used Butterfield Park for games and practice, Herriman Football used the football
fields at Riverton due to the lack of space.

Councilmember Shields acknowledged the difficulty of this decision and praised schools and
organizations such as the football league, as it taught individuals unique and transferable skills.
Councilmember Shields lamented the lack of space for these organizations to store their
equipment. He anticipated public works spaces could be available in the future, though it could be
as far as ten years in the future. City Manager Cherpeski explained his intention was to have a
general conversation about this issue and understand the Council’s stance so he could better
direct staff.

    6.2. Councilmember Teddy Hodges
Councilmember Hodges gave an update on the Sewer Board expansion report. He indicated there
were new road development projects, and noted they were currently working through staffing data
to assess how many hires would be necessary, as well as determine what an adequate pay scale
should be. He said due to the shift in management, there would be more changes soon as to how

Councilmember Hodges expressed appreciation to staff for the ‘Cookies and Communication’
event which was recently held.

   6.3. Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn
Councilmember Ohrn thanked Communications Specialist Destiny Skinner for her help in
coordinating the Youth Council. She praised their goal setting for the year.

Councilmember Ohrn addressed some questions and concerns she had received pertaining to the
Wasatch Waste and Recycling Board. She noted there were several factors which impacted
garbage costs and services offered varied across different neighborhoods. She specified Riverton
had cheaper costs for waste since less services were offered. Herriman did not subsidize costs,
which was another reason the cost to the residents was higher. She acknowledged how
complicated the process had been and said a member of the Board would be speaking at a Council
meeting in the future to discuss the rationale behind costs. She also noted the fight to keep drivers
and volunteers, and employee retention was proving to be an issue. She apologized for the

confusion and discrepancies and noted sanitation was important to the community.
Councilmember Ohrn observed a food waste pilot program being implemented.

Councilmember Ohrn went on to discuss the Jordan Valley Water District Board. She said water
conservation continued to be important. It was noted the District held a Truth in Taxation hearing
to solicit public input regarding a proposed water increase. Councilmember Ohrn acknowledged
the concern regarding the “flip your strip” program and relayed stories about neighbors lashing
out at neighbors who failed to adhere to the program. She urged people to be kind.

    6.4. Councilmember Steven Shields
Councilmember Shields offered an overview of the Wasatch Front Regional Council regional
growth report. He said there was limited housing units for families and reported a state legislator
commented Salt Lake City had built 15,000 housing units in the downtown area, and these were
almost exclusively studio or one-bedroom units. Councilmember Shields claimed in the last year,
Salt Lake City did not have any single units constructed intended for a family. He opined this would
change the culture of Salt Lake City if housing was not available for families. He acknowledged the
Utah housing crisis for low-income families and implored the Council to consider existing
communities when finding solutions for low-income housing.
