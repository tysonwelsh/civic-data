2.     APPROVE THE COUNTY HOLIDAY SCHEDULE FOR 2018
(Track A, 18:00)
Commissioner Graves pointed out the Elected Officials had discussed during another meeting removing the Personal
Preference Day from the calendar, instead choosing Monday December 24, 2018 as a Utah County holiday.
Commissioner Lee noted he would like to discuss Christmas Eve and New Year’s Eve as potential holidays, especially
to accommodate the Utah County Sheriff’s Office employees who do not always get to celebrate time off. Utah
County Human Resources Director Cammie Taylor mentioned government entities generally allow Personal
Preference Days for religious or personal holidays; she has received feedback from employees that they would prefer
the Personal Preference Day, and recommends the Board keep that option on the calendar. Commissioner Graves
countered it is a waste of time to decide things during Elected Official meetings if there is no follow-through on the
decisions. He stated several Department Heads send people home on Christmas Eve without requiring use of vacation
or holiday gift hours, which consequentially punishes other employees.
UTAH COUNTY BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS
MINUTES – December 5, 2017
Page 6

Utah County Human Resources Director Cammie Taylor mentioned her office was closed to the public for half the
day on Christmas Eve in 2016, but employees were still in the department. Commissioner Graves explained that is
unfair to other departments which must stay open per state law. He and Ms. Taylor discussed how Sheriff’s Office
work hours are calculated for employees, with Ms. Taylor noting public safety employees are given the option to
accept payment in place of gifted holiday hours.

(Track A, 28:10)
Utah County Under-Sheriff Mike Forshee gave input on Personal Preference Days compared to holidays, noting a
deputy who works on Memorial Day is paid eight holiday hours plus the actual 12-hour working shift. In the past,
Personal Preference would not be paid in the same way. Commissioner Ivie asked what would be ideal for the Sheriff’s
Office, and Mr. Forshee suggested allowing Personal Preference, as well as gift holiday hours, to be paid the same
way as regular holidays even if worked. Human Resources Director Cammie Taylor pointed out that can be corrected
through policy. Utah County Public Works Director Richard Nielson supported Mr. Forshee’s solution, explaining it
would also benefit his employees which are on-call during holidays. Lastly, Provo resident Robert Stevens encouraged
the Utah County Commissioners to consider giving employees more flexibility to attract ideal workers.

                   Commissioner Ivie made the motion to approve the County Holiday Schedule for
                   2018 as detailed in Consent Agenda Item No. 2. The motion was seconded by

       PROJECT
(Track A, 1:38:10)
Spanish Fork City Councilmember Mike Mendenhall came forward with Spanish Fork Economic Development
Director Dave Anderson. Mr. Mendenhall mentioned the city has been working with the County Health Department
for several months, through grants from Intermountain Health Care and the Utah League of Cities and Towns, on
improving physical activity for residents. He noted Marla Brannum of the County Health Department has provided
‘QPR’ training for city employees: Question, Persuade, and Refer. Mr. Mendenhall summarized ‘QPR’ addresses
mental health in the way ‘CPR’ (cardiopulmonary resuscitation) is used for physical health. Quoting an article from
The Daily Herald newspaper (http://www.heraldextra.com/news/opinion/herald-editorials/herald-editorial-utah-s-
increase-in-youth-suicide-demands-community/article_a3fde089-4f36-57bd-8fb9-43b2174edb86.html),                 Mr.
Mendenhall stated, “‘Between 2011 and 2015, the crude suicide rate among Utah youth aged 10-17 years increased
by 136.0% whereas the crude rate among U.S. youth aged 10-17 years increased by 23.5%;’ we are leading the way
in a place we don’t want to lead the way.” He talked about a young woman who worked on the Youth City Council
and was very active in the community, and her death motivating a commitment to mental health – at least $10,000 of
the annual grant is designated for citizen care.

Mike Mendenhall went on to provide history on the Spanish Fork City River Bottoms Vision Project. He mentioned
the Utah County Jail currently sits on what used to be his family’s farm. Pointing out there were initially many
concerns when the land was sold for that purpose, Mr. Mendenhall joked about the many law enforcement officers
now readily available nearby for the protection of residents – ‘Good luck ever trying to rob a place in Spanish Fork!’
He spoke of his family legacy of River Commissioners, and the city seeking to engage the 115 owners of more than
1,800 land acres regarding annexation of the River Bottoms.

Dave Anderson summarized the River Bottoms consists of approximately 2,000 acres of fertile valley south of the
Spanish Fork River. Spanish Fork City has included the area as part of their general plan and annexation policy for
decades, with the caveat of preserving agricultural use – requests to annex for development have not come to fruition
because the city wants to maintain farming. The city would like to be more pro-active in first determining the desires
of the community for that land and, if upon discovering agricultural use is a priority, identify the best means to achieve
that end. Mr. Anderson explained Spanish Fork has contracted with Logan Simpson Design Incorporated as a
consultant for this project, and is inviting the county to participate in finding a unified goal. Commissioner Lee asked
about crossover with other cities as part of the annexation plan, and Mr. Anderson said there are none. They discussed
how the Board can be involved in the process.
