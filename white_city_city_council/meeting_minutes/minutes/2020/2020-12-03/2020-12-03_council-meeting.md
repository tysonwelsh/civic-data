                                        Public Hearing

Resolution No. 20-12-01 – White City Metro Township 2021 Budget

              THIS BEING THE TIME heretofore set for a public hearing to consider the 2021
White City Metro Township Budget.

                Council Member Little stated his concerns about moving forward with the public
hearing for the 2021 budget because he did not feel that the documents were available to the
public in a timely manner.

                Council Member Perry, seconded by Council Member Price, moved to open the
public hearing for the 2021 White City Metro Township budget. The motion passed 4 to 1, showing
Council Member Little voting “Nay”.

               No one appeared in favor or opposition.

               Council Member Perry, seconded by Council Member Price, moved to close the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

              Council Member Price, seconded by Council Member Dickerson, moved to
approve the following Resolution No. 20-12-01. The motion passed 4 to 1, showing Council
Member Little voting “Nay”.

RESOLUTION NO. 20-12-01                                               Date: December 3, 2020

       A RESOLUTION OF WHITE CITY METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL ADOPTING
       THE 2021 WHITE CITY METRO TOWNSHIP FINAL BUDGET

      WHEREAS, the White City Metro Township (“White City”) is a municipality pursuant to
Utah Code Section 10-2a-401 et. seq.; and

         WHEREAS, the White City Metro Township Council (the “Council”) is the municipal
legislative body for White City pursuant to Utah Code Section 10-3b-501; and


                                               2
 DATE           THURSDAY                                                  DECEMBER 3, 2020

        WHEREAS, the Council has complied in all respects with State Law, including holding
public hearings, in establishing budgets for the Calendar Year beginning January 1, 2021 and
ending December 31, 2021; and

       WHEREAS, pursuant to fiscal policy for funding the 2021 White City Budget, the Greater
Salt Lake Municipal Services District (the “MSD”) adopted the 2021 Budget for the MSD on
October 22, 2020, releasing the funding for the 2021 White City Budget; and

       WHEREAS, the noticing for the public hearing to consider and copies of the 2021 White
City Tentative Budget, (Attachment “A”) were made available for public consideration at least ten
(10) days prior to the date of the public hearing for the Budget; and

      WHEREAS, the adoption of the 2021 Budget will allow White City to comply with State
Law and the Uniform Fiscal Procedures Act for Cities;

        THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE WHITE CITY METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL
as follows:

       SECTION 1. The White City Metro Township Council hereby adopts the 2021 White City
Final Budget, Attachment “A”.

       SECTION 2. That upon the final adoption, the budget shall be in effect for the budget
year and subject to later amendment as provided by law.

       SECTION 3. A copy of the final budget shall be posted and made available to the public
on the City’s website: www.whitecity-ut.org

       SECTION 4. This Resolution shall become effective immediately upon passage thereof.

       APPROVED AND ADOPTED THIS 3RD DAY OF DECEMBER, 2020 by the White City
Metro Township Council, White City, Utah.

                                           Public Meeting

Reports from other Municipal Providers

Unified Fire Authority (UFA)

              Captain Ken Aldridge stated the 2021-2023 Strategic Plan is in the final stages.
Comments have been taken externally, internally, and from surveys to form the plan. The
Captains process was completed, with one promotion. There has been a recent surge in COVID-
19 cases. He emailed the Council the summary of events and call times for November.

                                  −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Unified Police Department (UPD)

                Officer Paula Stinson stated tonight is the last time she will be giving an update
on statistics as she will be retiring this month. There were a couple of burglaries in vacant homes,
other than those, there are no significant calls. A replacement has been assigned to White City.
She thanked the Council for allowing her to be part of the White City community.

                Chief Randy Thomas stated thanked Officer Stinson for the great job she did in
White City. It is always good to see police officers and fire fighters retire with good mental and
physical health. Out of the handful of applicants to take Officer Stinson’s place, Officer Jeff Fenton

                                                  4
 DATE            THURSDAY                                                  DECEMBER 3, 2020

was chosen. UPD has been fortunate with the amount of COVID cases it has had. Midvale City
UPD has three officers out with COVID. They are recovering and are experiencing mild
symptoms. Around this time of year, fraudulent activity goes up. He heard of an IRS scam that
requests Walmart gift card numbers. The IRS, Police Department, Water Department, etc. will not
ask for Walmart gift cards in lieu of payment. UPD has started a Major Incident Review Board to
review incidents deemed necessary by command staff. Former Council Member Cody Cutler was
appointed to serve on the board.

              Officer Jeff Fenton stated he has been an officer for over 13 years. He is excited
for the new position and opportunity to serve White City. He provided his contact information to
Mayor Flint and requested that she forward it to the other Council Members. He has never been
assigned specifically to White City but has responded to many calls while working in Midvale City.

Resolution No. 20-12-03 – CARES Act Funding for Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District

             The Council reviewed the following Resolution to use CARES Act funding for
Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District:

RESOLUTION NO. 20-12-03                                                 Date: December 3, 2020

       A RESOLUTION OF WHITE CITY METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL RATIFYING,
       APPROVING AND AUTHORIZING THE EXECUTION OF AN AMENDED
       INTERLOCAL COOPERATION AGREEMENT WITH THE WASATCH FRONT
       WASTE AND RECYCLING DISTRICT INCREASING WFWRD’S TOTAL PAYOUT
       TO $2,011.02 FROM WHITE CITY’S CARES ACT FUNDING, AN INCREASE OF
       $770.33, FOR WFWRD’S COVID-RELATED EXPENSES

       WHEREAS, the White City Metro Township (“White City” or the “Municipality”) recognizes
our need for municipal-types services; and

         WHEREAS, the Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District (“WFWRD”) is a Local
District that provides trash collection and recycling services to White City; and

        WHEREAS, Salt Lake County is a County pursuant to Utah Code Title 17: COUNTIES
and is the County where White City is located; and

       WHEREAS, on October 5, 2020, pursuant to White City Resolution 20-10-01, the Council
approved and authorized the Mayor to sign a subgrant agreement with WFWRD and pay WFWRD
$1,240.69 to assit them with their unbudgeted COVID-related costs; and

        WHEREAS, since October 5, WFWRD as accrued additional unbudgeted COVID-related
costs; and


                                                5
 DATE           THURSDAY                                              DECEMBER 3, 2020

       WHEREAS, on November 19, 2020 White City received a second requires from WFWRD
asking for an additional $770.33 from White City’s CARES Act funds to cover WFWRD’S
unbudgeted COVID-related costs, hereafter known as Attachment B to this resolution; and

        WHEREAS, amending the subgrant agreement to provide the additional $770.33 to
WFWRD benefits White City and softens the impacts caused to a service provider due to COVID-
19; therefore be it

       RESOLVED, the White City Metro Township Council ratifies, approves, and authorizes

Council Member Reports

Unified Fire Authority (UFA) & Unified Fire Service Area (UFSA)

                  Council Member Perry stated the market wage adjustment for employees was
put off until fall to then be looked at again. The Board feels comfortable moving forward with the
market wage adjustment for January. An update on the wildland fire season was provided.
Because of COVID station were seeing vacancies averaging 1.6 individuals in September to 4.4
vacancies in November. A performance review was completed on Chief Dan Peterson and Chief
Legal Officer Brian Roberts. Both of their reviews were positive; the agency is moving in a good
direction and continues to improve. UFSA hired Racheal Anderson for the District Administrator
position and she continues to be legal counsel. The updated impact fee and 2021 tentative budget
were approved. A $2.1 million grant was awarded to address seismic issues at the fire stations.
Interlocal agreements were approved for Herriman and Riverton Cities withdraws.

Unified Police Department (UPD) & Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA)


                                                9
 DATE            THURSDAY                                                   DECEMBER 3, 2020

                Mayor Flint stated three resolutions were passed; policy for reporting fraud and
abuse, direction to allow research on the Chief Executive position, and a committee to support
the UPD model. There has been a debate if there is enough transparency with the budget process.
Several things have changed with the UPD model to make budgets more transparent. David
Brems, Emigration Canyon Metro Township, is the Chair of the Benefits and Compensation
Committee. A study to look at the financial impact from separation from the Salt Lake County
Sherriff’s Office was conducted. A resolution was passed for SLVLESA amending the calendar
year and the Chief position for the new Taylorsville Police Department was discussed.

                                 −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
South Salt Lake Valley Mosquito Abatement District

                Council Member Price stated the Board approved a cost of living and a merit
increase of 3.3 percent for employees. An employee requested tuition reimbursement and the
District did not have a policy for it. It was decided that a line item of $15,000 a year would be
allocated to tuition reimbursement. Any employee who is eligible can apply and if they are
approved, they are required to stay with the District for two years after their education or they
have to pay back the tuition. The upcoming conferences for 2021 are either being cancelled, or
they are being held virtually. There were not safety accidents this year and the staff is working on
projects to get ready for 2021.

                                 −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (GSLMSD)

                 Mayor Flint stated the Board looked at the status of COVID-19 funding. Letters
were submitted to the GSLMSD and some individuals presented information on issues in the
canyons. The fee schedule for the GSLMSD and a resolution to accept electronic payment fees
were approved. A presentation on Olympia Hills has been requested so the GSLMSD can stay
informed. It is front loading a lot of the work for Salt Lake County Regional Development. She is
requesting an actual agreement be drafted because there is a danger of money going where it
should not. Expectations need to be drafted so the funding is transparent.

                                 −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District (WFWRD)

               Council Member Dickerson stated residents need to remember to put waste in a
