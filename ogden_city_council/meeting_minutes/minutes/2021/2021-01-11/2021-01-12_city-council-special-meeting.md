Consideration of the appointment of Eric Young as the Police Chief for
Ogden City
          The Mayor is requesting advice and consent on the appointment ofEric Young as Ogden Police Chief, effective January
 16,2021. Chief Young will replace Chief Watt whose resignation becomes effective January 15,2021.
          Mayor Caldwell stated it is his honor to bring this recommendation to the City Council. Public safety is paramount to a
community's prosperity and well-being and the search for and selection of the best person to lead the City's Police Department
was a critical one. He considers this action to be one of the most important that he will carry out as Mayor of Ogden City. As City
Administration was moving through the process of selecting a new police chief there were several critical areas of consideration
including education, experience, communication skills, and extensive training and knowledge of police operations- especially in
the Ogden community. He is pleased to present, for the Council's advice and consent, the appointment of Eric Young to serve
Ogden as police chief. Deputy Chief Young is a highly qualified and experienced law enforcement professional with a long
history oflaw enforcement in Ogden City. In his 27-year career with the Ogden Police Department (OPD), Deputy Chief Young
has consistently excelled in his profession, received awards for valor, and advanced through leadership roles. He has been
instrumental in establishing the Ogden Tactical Analysis Center, which has garnered national attention and is looked to by many
institutions as the gold standard in crime prevention. He was also a leader in the implementation of Project Safe Neighborhoods,
which is recognized by the United States Attorney General's Office for its tremendous success. Only six cities in the entire nation
have received a similar recognition. Deputy Chief Young has actively pursued post graduate education and elite national training
to ensure he is well versed in modem police practices and operating procedures employed by the most sophisticated law
enforcement agencies in the nation. He received his bachelor's degree in criminal justice from WSU, a master's degree in
leadership and management from Western Governor's University School of Business and is a graduate of the Federal Bureau of
Investigations National Academy. He was selected from more than 100 applicants for the inaugural International Association of
Chiefs ofPolice/MacArthur Foundation Law Enforcement Leadership Institute on Juvenile Justice and is cited as a co-author of a
study of disproportionate minority contact which has been presented at multiple national conferences. Mayor Caldwell stated it is
likely that a national job search may have yielded another candidate with the training and years of service, but nowhere could the
City have found a candidate who possesses all of Deputy Chief Young's qualifications and his knowledge of Ogden City. The
Police Department has contacts with many community organizations, leaders, and stakeholders with whom Deputy ChiefYoung
has forged relationships. One of the things Administration has heard repeated over the past year relates to the critical nature of the
relationship between OPD and the community and he is not sure it would be possible to observe and meet that need if someone
were to be brought in from the outside. Deputy Chief Young truly understands the City's greatest needs and challenges and is a
natural fit to ensure effective continuity of operations. Serving as deputy chief for nine years, he has played a key role in OPD's
recent successes in making the community safe. Over the past four years, Ogden has experienced a 34 percent decrease in part one
crime, 27 percent of which is specific to violent crime reduction. Ogden City residents will benefit from a police chief who
understands exactly how those successes were obtained so that he can reach for more, all for the benefit of Ogden residents.
Choosing a leader from OPD's ranks also benefits employees City-wide by sending the message that they are valued and have
opportunities to excel in their respective departments. This is especially important at a time when recruitment and retention of
police officers is at an all time low nation-wide. Mayor Caldwell said he has personally been watching Deputy Chief Young over
recent years to gauge his potential to be the police chief. This has been a four-year extended job interview, although Deputy Chief
Young did not know that was the case. Over that time, Mayor Caldwell has drawn the following conclusions: Deputy Chief
Young is smart, hardworking, and dependable, follows through on details while managing a tremendous workload, is a
tremendous communicator who is cool under pressure and has a knack for deescalating stressful encounters, he is an effective
problem solver, an excellent leader who leads by example, and he is experienced and passionate about Ogden City. Mayor
Caldwell concluded he is absolutely confident that Deputy Chief Young is the right leader for OPD at this time and he highly
recommends the Council vote in support of this appointment.
