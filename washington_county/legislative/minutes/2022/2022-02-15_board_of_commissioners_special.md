WORK MEETING

DISCUSSION OF ANIMAL FEEDING OPERATIONS (AFOS) AND CONCENTRATED
ANIMAL FEEDING OPERATIONS (CAFOS), AND POSSIBLE CHANGES TO TITLE

10, ZONING REGULATIONS, TO COMPLY WITH NEW STATE LAW

Deputy County Attorney Victoria Hales presented regarding the proposed regulations with AFOs
and CAFOs. She explained that nutrient management plans are highly regulated by the Division
of Water Quality (DWQ) and the Utah Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (UPDES). They
reflect federal requirements for facilities to prevent the discharge of pollutants to protect surface
water and ground water quality. All liquid manure handling systems must receive approval prior
to construction.

Ms. Hales explained that Animal Feed Operations (AFOs) are lots or facilities where animals have
been are or will be stabled or confined and fed or maintained for a total of 45 days or more in any
12- month period; and crops, vegetation, forage growth or post-harvest residues are not sustained
in the normal growing season over any portion of the lot or facility. Concentrated Animal Feeding
Operations (CAFOs) confine or stable as many or more than the number of animals specified as
follows: Beef, calves, heifers, or veal: 1-299; Cows (milking and dry): 1-199; Layers or broilers
(wet system): 1-8,999; Other than layers (dry system): 1-37,499; Layers (dry system): 1-24,999;
Turkeys: 1-16,499; Swine (55 pounds or more): 1-749; Swine (less than 55 pounds): 1-2,999;
Sheep: 1-2,999; Horses: 1-149; Ducks (dry system): 1-9,999; and Ducks (wet system): 1-1,499.

The State is requesting the smaller to medium size counties to adopt a land use ordinance in order
to attain the ability to restrict the siting of large CAFOs. A land use ordinance would create County
code definitions and structure for AFOs and CAFOs; create an Intensive Agriculture (IA) zone;
create a map for CAFO siting; and meet new State law for CAFO siting.

Kelby Iverson requested for the Commissioners to be prudent when making regulations and adding
restrictions regarding the smaller and medium size AFOs.

Sherrie Staheli Tate stated that county is an agriculture county and expressed hesitation with
regulations for producers.
