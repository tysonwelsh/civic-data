1.       A presentation regarding utility billing. (21-062) (0:10:01)

Wayne Parker, CAO, presented. He shared history of the Provo 360 project, which replaced the
legacy software programs used by the City with updated, integrated systems. There are still
intricacies within the new system for the utility billing components. Mr. Parker refreshed the
group on the activities of last summer’s software transition and implementation, impacts of the
pandemic, and timing of rate changes. A combination of factors created a perfect storm for the
errors which occurred as a result. Mr. Parker reviewed details of some of the specific transition
issues experienced relative to utility billing, and the actions taken by the City to address issues,
correct problems, and to proactively avert further issues in the future. The City contracted with
Squire & Company to conduct an external audit of the utility billing system. Dave Brown
worked on the audit and shared more details about the process for the audit. Squire & Company

Mr. Parker reiterated the lessons learned from the process. In the future, they intend to introduce

2.      A presentation regarding Google Fiber. (21-008) (0:38:19)

Dixon Holmes, Assistant CAO, presented. Mr. Holmes shared an update on Google Fiber and he
highlighted several notable changes with Google Fiber:
    Discontinuance of TV services (which have moved to streaming vendors exclusively)
    Franchise fee restructured to factor internet usage only
    The first payment of this restructured franchise fee will be received in the fall. It is
       anticipated the total will increase; the rate is lower, but due to a greater number of
       internet users the final total will likely be higher.
    Google Fiber had initially offered seven years of free basic service. On a rolling basis,
       this free basic service is discontinuing. Individual contracts may vary depending on when
       the free service was initially installed in a particular area. Presentation only.

4.      A discussion regarding Elected Officials Compensation. (21-026) (0:48:31)

Cliff Strachan, Council Executive Director, presented. Mr. Strachan referenced a report
presented in April from the Elected Officials Compensation Commission. He indicated that the
two proposals addressing Mayoral and Councilor compensation were on the agenda for the
Council Meeting that evening. The Commission recommended slight adjustments as a result of
their study. Although the changes may seem significant, these changes account for any changes
in the market and in similarly situated cities over the last several years, as the commission is only
convened every four years. Mr. Strachan displayed the draft ordinances and reviewed the
proposed revisions to the Councilor compensation and expenses in detail, item by item.

Councilor Travis Hoban asked for his colleagues’ opinions on the proposed changes. With it
being a tough year for many people during the pandemic, he was concerned about the optics of
making such changes. He was interested in other Councilors’ thoughts on the timing and general
concept. Mr. Hoban acknowledged the amount of work that Councilors do. Mr. Strachan added
additional context on the history of the commission, which was structured in part after the model
used at the state level for the legislature and the governor. Mr. Strachan noted that the
recommendations were made by outside commission members.

Councilor Shannon Ellsworth acknowledged the awkwardness inherent in the situation. Ms.
Ellsworth noted the competing argument, of wanting to do right by the people who will run after
her. Economic and professional stability is an important factor for potential Council candidates
and future Councilors; she hoped that these changes would facilitate having a broader and more
inclusive pool of candidates in the future. This income could make a significant difference for an
individual who would need to reduce their working hours to meet the commitments of being a
Councilor. Councilor David Harding thanked his colleagues for their comments. In the past, he
has only ever served in volunteer roles in civic settings. He endorsed Councilor Ellsworth’s
remarks; Council service should not be the domain of only people who could afford to take time
off of work to serve. Mr. Harding suggested there were other ways to reinvest in the community
with this additional compensation. Ms. Ellsworth hoped the Council could have a public hearing
and plenty of citizen input. This item was only a small element of the budget, but it meant a lot to
citizens and she hoped the Council could offer plenty of opportunities to hear about and
comment on it. Councilor David Sewell shared his recollection of the history of the commission
as well, noting the institutional history involving former Mayor John Curtis, who introduced the
idea of a commission which was later adopted by the Council. Presentation only. This item was
already scheduled for the Council Meeting on May 18, 2021.

6.      A discussion on tentatively adopting a tentative budget for Redevelopment Agency
        of the Provo City Corporation for the fiscal year beginning July 1, 2021 and ending
        June 30, 2022. (21-015) (1:26:02)

John Magness, Policy Analyst, presented. Mr. Magness reviewed the Redevelopment Agency
budget. He displayed a chart showing projections alongside actuals for both revenues and
expenditures. Some costs were paid late in the calendar year and consequently appeared on the
following fiscal year actuals. Mr. Magness highlighted the tax increment fund revenues and
expenditures and noted some of the changes to this taking place over time. Mr. Magness
responded to questions from Councilors. Councilor David Shipley asked for clarification on the
drop-off in revenue in FY17. Mr. Magness did not have the details readily available but indicated
he would share more data following the meeting. Councilor David Harding expressed concern
about revenue being received in a different fiscal year than initially projected; such variations in
the budgeted vs. actual figures made it difficult to track whether the financials were on track for
the Agency. Mr. Magness indicated that they did look at fund balance overall as a more
comprehensive figure; he noted that the fund balance depicted on his slides demonstrated simply
what was being added or taken out from fund balance (as opposed to the total amount of fund
balance). The total fund balance number was helpful to understand the larger picture.
Presentation only. This item was already scheduled for the Council Meeting on May 18, 2021.
