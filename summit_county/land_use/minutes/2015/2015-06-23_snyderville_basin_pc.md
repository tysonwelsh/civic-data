Commission previously reviewed this proposal in work session.  The proposed tower will 
be 86 feet tall with a radio antenna on top.  He clarified that the tower is actually 80 feet

Nefi Garcia, representing Verizon Wireless, recalled that, at the work session, the 
Planning Commissioners asked what other options are available, such as small cell sites, 
and why the site needs to be at this location.  He noted that there is a letter in the packet 
that answers those questions. 
 
Commissioner Klingenstein stated that he is still struggling with an 80-foot tower and 
asked what other locations were evaluated that might have been less intrusive.  He asked 
why they do not consider Quarry Mountain or Lewis Peak, which already have numerous 
antennas.  Mr. Garcia explained that Verizon is already located on Quarry Mountain and 
already serves this area.  However, that site is far past capacity, and they need to locate a 
site where development is occurring in Kimball Junction.  The engineers give him a small 
search ring and ask him to find a location within that area where they could lease or own 
to give them coverage, and the search ring was from this proposed location to just the 
other side of SR 224.  He confirmed that they also have a site on Lewis Peak, and now 
they are working on fill-in sites where development is occurring.  Planner Lewis 
explained that a cell phone tower has a certain radial coverage area, but with the data 
requirements and technology developed in the last five or six years, as they get a load on 
the tower, the circle of coverage shrinks.  He explained that they are seeing more and 
more applications for towers, because the coverage on existing towers is not sufficient for 
the additional needs.  Commissioner Klingenstein asked if co-location is market driven 
or controlled in some way.  Mr. Garcia replied that it is market driven, and the carriers 
have lease templates in place for additional carriers to locate on a tower.  The number of 
additional tenants depends on the design and the height of the tower.  Planner Lewis

Principal Planner Ray Milliner presented the staff report and recalled that the Planning 
Commission has been discussing this item for several months.  He provided a map 
showing the location of the site and reported that the applicant has made some 
modifications to the plan.  They now propose 20 units of affordable housing to be located 
on the second floor of Building B, which will increase the square footage of the site by a

that affordable housing does not count in density requirements, so the 20,000 square feet 
of affordable housing will not count in the 61,000 square feet of density allowed in the 
development agreement.  The applicant has increased the number of parking stalls from 
264 to 289, and the minimum number of parking stalls allowed in the development 
agreement is 279, but the additional 10 spaces will be used for the affordable housing and

Planning Commissioners about the community benefits, affordable housing—noting that
