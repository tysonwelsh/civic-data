Staff Presentation: Recently, there has been a surge of retail tobacco specialty businesses coming into Orem City.
The Department of Business Licensing has determined that it would be in the best interest of the City to impose
additional restrictions on retail tobacco specialty businesses because they pose a potential health risk to residents.
Additionally, it has been determined that Orem has more vape shops per capita than any other city in Utah except for
Salt Lake City. These proposed restrictions would help curb this trend.

A summary of the proposed text is provided below. The complete text is provided in an attachment to this report.

The proposed restrictions put limitations on these businesses in two ways.

    1.   Separation - The proposed text would require that these businesses be at least 1,000 feet away from
         community locations, at least 600 feet away from another retail tobacco specialty business, and at least 600
         feet away from properties used or zoned for residential or agricultural uses. This language is taken directly
         from the Utah State Code. Business Licensing is proposing that Orem City adopt these restrictions as part of
         its own code in order to make it easier for potential businesses to understand and abide by these laws.

    2.   Per Capita - The proposed text sets the maximum number of these businesses in the City at 1 per 10,000
         citizens. This language is taken from Orem City Code 22-14-21(A)(3) regarding check cashing and other
         credit services. The City placed this per capita limit on check cashing businesses in order to prevent being
         overrun by them and preserve the neighborhood feel of the City. Business Licensing is proposing that this
         same per capita limit be applied to retail tobacco specialty businesses.

Recommendation: The Development Review Committee determined this request complies with the Orem City
Code. Staff recommends the Planning Commission forward a positive recommendation to the City Council to enact
Section 22-14-21(D) and Standard Land Use Code 5945 of the Orem City Code pertaining to requirements for retail
tobacco specialty businesses.

Agenda Item 3.5 is a request by Development Services to forward a positive recommendation to amend portions of
the Orem General Plan including updating the Land Use Map and the Pedestrian and Bike Path Map pertaining to
the Southwest area of the city.

Staff Presentation: The southwest area was annexed by the City in 2015. At the time of annexation, a general plan
map was adopted by the city and generally included a light industrial designation on the west side of Geneva Road
and low density designation on the east side of Geneva road.

With the announcement of the Temple site located generally at 1471 South Geneva Road and the additional interest
in development in the general area, the City Council asked staff to review the current general plan for the area.
Based on our review process, staff is proposing several changes to the General Plan for the southwest area.

Staff held an online neighborhood meeting on May 13, 2020 to discuss the proposed amendments. A total of 2,455
notices were mailed to the entire Lakeview neighborhood, with 38 participating in the online meeting. Many
comments were received through the process which are part of the staff report for your review. During the online
meeting staff presented four options relating to land use. Based on comments received, staff developed a preferred
option which was also presented to the City Council in a work session on May 26, 2020 for discussion. In addition
to the land use designations, staff also proposed the addition of several trails within the southwest area as shown on
the Pedestrian and Bike Path Plan.

The proposed changes include an update to the Land Use Map that will include a new land use designation of Low
Density Residential/Southwest (LDR/SW) this new designation would allow a density between 4-8 units per gross
acre. Based on this new land use designation, a modified Planned Residential Development ordinance will be
developed for the LDR/SW designation that would be reviewed at a later date. Several areas are proposed to be
designated as LDR/SW as shown on the proposed land use map.

In addition, two commercial areas will be designated on the revised general plan including at the corner of 2000
South Geneva Road and at the crossroads of the future Lakeview Parkway and Geneva Road as shown.

The proposed Land Use Map also shows future areas not part of the City; the intent of the map indicates future land
use designations if those properties are annexed in the future. The overall density including future areas are
included in the overall calculations pertaining to all applicable utilities.

The current general plan designations for the southwest area were calculated at a total density of 1,248 equivalent
residential units (ERUs). The proposed amendments to the general plan for the area will increase the ERUs to 2,500
as shown on the density table provided for your review. Once the General Plan is amended the City plans to update
the Impact Fee Analysis (IFA) and Impact Fee Facilities Plan (IFFP) and amend the impact fees based on the
increased ERUs for the southwest area subject to the revised studies.

Agenda Item 3.6 is a request by Development Services for the Planning Commission to forward a positive
recommendation to City Council to adopt the Sunset Heights East and West Neighborhood Plan and the Lakeview
and Cherry Hill Neighborhood in Appendix “Z” of the Orem General Plan.

Staff Presentation: The Sunset Heights East and West Neighborhood Plan and the Lakeview and Cherry Hill
Neighborhood Plan were created by participating neighborhood residents and Orem City planning staff. It was
developed to record residents’ visions for the neighborhood and potential ways to achieve those visions, as well
residents’ concerns about how the neighborhood is currently developing. While neighborhood plans are not designed
to solve all problems immediately, they allow the local community to influence planning in their neighborhood and
guide future development in a way that is cohesive with the neighborhood character.

The creation of these two plans was guided by two advisory committees – one for the Sunset Heights East and West
neighborhood and one for the Lakeview and Cherry Hill neighborhood. These advisory committees met nine times
over the past several months to hear from city staff members in each department, formulate a vision statement to
reflect their aspirations for the future of the neighborhood, and list goals to achieve those aspirations. Many of the
themes for the neighborhoods’ vision statements included maintaining the character of the neighborhood, ensuring
that the neighborhoods stay safe, clean, and family-friendly. Other themes included working to improve their
neighborhoods through better walkability, increasing multi-modal transportation options and improving green space,
and overall better environmental practices. Lastly, residents expressed desire in ensuring that new development is
responsible and encouraged a stronger working relationship for UVU future development to balance with the
neighborhood concerns.

The plan document itself contains seven different sections: current demographics, land use, transportation, economic
development, public works, neighborhood preservation, and implementation strategies. Each of these sections
addresses the neighborhood’s current conditions, future projects, and goals for achieving the neighborhood’s vision
statement.

Public participation was a key component of this process. In addition to the nine advisory committee meetings that
were held, two neighborhood meetings were held; one at the beginning, where we had approximately 120 attendees,
and one at the end, where we had 59 participants (due to Covid-19 restrictions the second meeting was held through
an online webinar format). Notices for these open houses were sent to every resident in the neighborhood and the
meetings had over one hundred residents in attendance. There who participated in the planning process represented a
wide range of community stakeholders who may be impacted by decisions regarding development within these
neighborhoods, including property owners, business owners and tenants, and residents and representatives from UVU.

Recommendation: The Development Review Committee determined this request complies with the Orem City
Code. Staff recommends the Planning Commission forward a positive recommendation to City Council to adopt the
Sunset Heights East and West Neighborhood Plan and the Lakeview and Cherry Hill Neighborhood in Appendix
“Z” of the Orem General Plan.

     However, the city council and city staff went on to explain in some detail why the sewer could not
     support additional residential density. Why was sewer-sizing an indisputable showstopper then
     and not now? Text in the Vision Statement such as “Our focus is on encouraging green space” and
     “creation of aesthetically pleasing neighborhoods” is not consistent with our experience. We have
     honestly tried to think of any time when Orem City has focused on encouraging green space in
     Lakeview. Until we read the draft plan, we would have said the density compromises at the time
