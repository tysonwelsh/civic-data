3.0       REPORTS

          3.1    Visit Salt Lake – Tyson Lybbert, Chief Sales Officer.

Chief Sales Officer, Tyson Lybbert, reported that Visit Salt Lake is a 501(c)(6) nonprofit
corporation similar to a chamber of commerce. Their mission is to promote visitors and tourism
in Salt Lake County. They are responsible for booking the Salt Palace Convention Center and
Mountain America Expo Center, as well as sporting and ski events for individuals and groups.
Most similar organizations are Destination Marketing Organizations (“DMO”) as marketing is a
primary focus. Visit Salt Lake’s primary focus is on delivering experiences, and they are moving
toward becoming a Destination Sales and Experience Organization (“DSXO”). Their three pillars
are recruiting, retaining, and activating with signature events like the skijoring event in downtown
Salt Lake City.

Mr. Lybbert reported on the importance of Salt Lake County’s visitor economy, which:

      •   Supports 56,300 jobs;
      •   Offsets household tax burdens by nearly $1,700 annually;
      •   Generates $575 million in tax revenue; and
      •   Is a $6.15 billion industry.

Most out-of-state visitors to the county are from nearby states like Colorado and Idaho, but many
are also from Texas and eastern states. A total of 14% of visitors go to Big or Little Cottonwood
Canyons, second only to downtown Salt Lake City. Big Cottonwood Canyon is the number one
area attraction on TripAdvisor, followed by Temple Square. However, only 5% of visitor dollars
are spent in the canyon area.

        4.2      Utah Renewable Communities Update - Planner III, Sheldon Howa.

Planner III, Sheldon Howa, provided an update on the Utah Renewable Communities (“URC”)
Community Clean Energy Program. The Utah Public Service Commission (“PSC”) issued an
order approving the program on March 4, 2026. The order addressed key details such as the initial
program rate and customer opt-out details, with more complex issues to be addressed in a follow-
up ruling. This allowed the URC program to move forward and started the 90-day clock for
communities to consider adopting the ordinance. The deadline to do so was June 2, 2026. During
that time, the URC board was collaborating with other parties to propose solutions to the remaining
issues. They were also in negotiations with four renewal energy contractors.

Council Member Birrell stated that her understanding was that the URC had committed to no rate
increases so depending on the number of participants, they may need to drop a project. But, they
currently have four projects so drop from four to three. Mr. Howa confirmed that the PSC had
determined a flat rate for residential participants. It is possible that the rate could increase
depending on the chosen resources. The ruling indicates that the $4 program fee will cover
administration costs and develop reserves to mitigate project startup costs, and they anticipate that
rates will actually decrease over time. Council Member Hyland stated that the fee is subject to
annual review and can increase. Cottonwood Heights would have a vote, but there are seven

metering program.

Council Member Holton asked if the URC Board had considered a usage-based fee. Mr. Howa
reported that to RMP initially requested variable program rates, but the URC determined that a
flat, predictable rate would attract more residential participants. Higher users will still pay a higher
electric bill. The rate for business users will be based on usage.

Mr. Howa reported that the low-income program is available to RMP customers with a household
income at or below 150% of the federal power level. Eligible customers can apply for the Home
Energy Assistance Target Program or RMP Home Electric Lifeline Program. Once approved,
there is no charge to participate in or terminate the program.

Non-residential customers will pay a volumetric rate of $0.00609 per kilowatt hour (“kWh”)plus
the $0.12 monthly slow-income program surcharge. For example, the fee for a medium-sized
office building that uses approximately 7,000 kWh would be approximately $42, or a 5% increase.
The fee will vary depending on the specific utility rate. Council Member Hyland indicated that
she spoke with Dan Dugan, who indicated that 20% of residents were projected to opt out of the
program, but almost all commercial customers were expected to opt out due to the cost.

In response to a question raised by Council Member Hyland, Mr. Howa reported that, to his

termination fee. The fee for commercial customers is based on their actual fee. Commercial
customers that consume over one megawatt of energy will receive a site visit from RMP. The City
is responsible for expenses related to the two initial notices.

Ken Garner has been a resident of Cottonwood Heights since 2008 and is a licensed professional

        increased revenues.

In response to a question raised by Council Member Hyland, Mr. Jurges reported that the
percentages listed at the top of each presentation slide reflected the percentage of the budget that
had been collected at this point in the year. Collections are not equal across revenue sources. For
example, 90% of property taxes are collected November through January, but most motor vehicle
fee in-lieu taxes are booked in at the start of the fiscal year in July. Sales tax revenue is collected
two months in arrears and fluctuates, with higher collection during Christmas and the summer.

Mr. Jurges reported that most revenue is booked in November, December, and January each year
as that is when property taxes are received. General Fund Revenues were at $19.711 million year
to date, $197,000 ahead of FY 2025. In response to a question raised by Council Member Hyland,
Mr. Jurges clarified that items like licenses and fees were not included in the presentation. He
believes that item had been underbudgeted in the past and had increased that budget by
approximately $107,000 for FY 2027.

General Fund expenses were at $17.749 million year to date, $292,000 ahead of 2025 spending.
