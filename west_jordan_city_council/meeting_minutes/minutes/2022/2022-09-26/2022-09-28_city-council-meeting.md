 5. PUBLIC HEARINGS
       a. Ordinance No. 22-31 amending the General Plan Land Use Map for Mountain
          America Redevelopment from Professional Office to Community Commercial, and a
          Rezone from the P-O Zone (Professional Office) to the SC-2 Zone (Community
          Shopping Center).
          Derek Lis with Evergreen Development, representing the applicant, commented that the
          banking world had changed in recent years, and brick and mortar buildings were not as
          needed as they used to be. He said Mountain America Credit Union was closing some
          branches and disposing of the real estate, and stated that Evergreen Development had the
          subject property under contract. Mr. Lis said he believed rezoning the property to SC-2
          Zone would open up many opportunities for commercial use. He said Evergreen
          Development did not have a specific commercial user lined up, but would submit a site
          plan when they did.

          Associate Planner Lisa Elgin showed a concept plan submitted by the applicant, and
          stated the application met City Code. The Planning Commission forwarded a positive
          recommendation.

          Vice Chair Green opened a public hearing at 7:40 pm

          Comments:
          Ted Manwill, West Jordan resident, said he owned property directly east of the Mountain
          America property. He provided history of the subject property and surrounding area. Mr.
          Manwill emphasized that initial criteria for the P-O Zone on the property included quiet,
          normal business hours, closed one day a week. He said he sent a copy of a letter to the
          City Council with further information.

          Vice Chair Green closed the public hearing at 7:44 pm

          Mr. Lis said he understood the history of the property. He said he felt growth and change
          on Redwood Road had been significant over the last 20 years. Mr. Lis said Evergreen
          Development was willing to work with staff to provide some kind of noise buffer if
          needed. He said the Future Land Use Map showed significant commercial growth in the
          area. Mr. Lis said the concept plan showed the maximum size building that would be
          allowed on the property.

          Responding to a question from Council Member McConnehey, Ms. Elgin confirmed the
          proposed rezone would carve out an island of SC-2 surrounded by professional office. The
          island of SC-2 would be considered part of the SC-2 Zone across Redwood Road.
          Responding to a question from Council Member Jacob, Ms. Elgin said the minimum
          acreage for the SC-2 Zone was 5-25 acres. She referred to another situation in the City in
          which an island of SC-2 was carved out, with additional SC-2 across a busy street.

          Council Member Jacob said the minimum acreage requirement gave him heartburn, and
          suggested that lumping the proposed SC-2 acreage with the SC-2 Zone across the wide
          street met the letter of the law, but not the spirit of the law. He said he estimated the
          property would not be able to hold more than two retail units, and would not have a
          significant impact on the neighboring subdivision. Council Member Jacob said he would
          be able to vote in favor of the rezone, knowing that current concerns would be handled
          during a future subdivision process.

          Council Member McConnehey said he heard an argument in another situation that a
          seven-lane highway would serve as a buffer. He commented that the property was
          adjacent to residential backyards, and said the request felt like spot zoning. Council
          Member McConnehey said he could potentially be in favor of a rezone to SC-1, but not SC-
          2.

          Vice Chair Green said he agreed with Council Member McConnehey, and said he did not
          like the idea of saying the small subject property would be part of the zoning on the other
          side of Redwood Road. He said there had been some discussion about requiring a master
          development agreement with every rezone so the City knew what they were going to get.
          Vice Chair Green said when he first saw the request at the Design Review Committee, he
          had not realized the Mountain America Credit Union building would be removed. He said
          commercial development was needed in West Jordan, but said the request looked to him
          like spot zoning. Vice Chair Green said he wanted to see commercial at the location, but

       b. Ordinance No. 22-45 amending 2009 West Jordan City Code amending residential
          building heights in residential and agricultural zones sections 13-5A-3 and 13-5B-
          3A.
          City Planner/Zoning Administrator Larry Gardner oriented the Council with the
          proposed amendment to change the height of building definition to: “The vertical distance
          under the roof or any roof element to the nearest measure of existing grade. Maximum
          height may be regulated by maximum number of stories. A story is equal to twelve feet.”
          The proposed ordinance would amend sections 13-5A-3 and 13-5B-3 and allow an
          increase in residential building heights from 30 feet to 35 feet. The height increase would
          affect all dwelling types in R-1, R-2, R-3, RR, RE, A-1, A-5, and A-20 Zones.

          Mr. Gardner answered questions from the Council, and clarified that the height of building
          definition would apply only to dwelling structures on a property, not agricultural buildings,
          flagpoles or other landmark items.
