2.     Open and Public Meetings Act Training.

City Planner/Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, provided the Utah Open and Public
Meetings Act training. State law requires every public entity to have an annual Open and Public
Meetings Training and reported that the Planning Commi ssion and Advisory Arts Board are subject
to the public notice to the same extent as the C ity Council or the School Board would be. If there is
a quorum, the meeting must be noticed. A chance social meeting does not fall under the stri ctures of
the public meeting laws. Meetings must be noticed at least 24 hours prior. Electronic meetings are
also permitted w ith the same noticing requirements.

Minutes of the meeting must be taken and contain the agenda, date, time, place, attendance of the
public entity members, the substance of what is discussed, and voting record of each member of the
public entity when votes take place. The wri tten m inutes must contain the substance of what was
discussed. In addition, those who make comm ents must state their name for the public record. Audio
recordings are also kept of all meeti ngs.

If the actions of a public entity go to court, the entire public record is used, including the audio, video
recording, and emails. Site visits and traveling tours do not need to be recorded, provided no official
action is taken. The public body must have an established procedure for approving written minutes.
The written minutes need to be available within a reasonable amount of time, and the audio recording
is available from the City Recorder within three days. The ideal for written minutes is to have them
available within one or two meetings.

It was noted that closed meetings are allowed under certain circumstances, which were enumerated;
however, it is highl y unlikely that the Planning Commission or Advisory Arts Board would ever need
to have a closed meeting. Minutes are not taken of a closed meeting but a recording of the meeting
must be kept. It was noted that the audio recording of tonight's meeting must also be made available
to the public within tlu·ee business days . Mr. Crowell reported that the penalty for violating the Open
Meetings Law is considered a Class B Misdemeanor. He reiterated that since audio recordings are
kept of all meetings, participants should be mindful of what they say.

       Caitlyn Miller, Staff Presenter.

Senior City Planner, Caitlyn Miller presented the staff repo11 and stated that a few months earlier the
