1.      WELCOME, INTRODUCTION AND PRELIMINARY MATTERS

      Chair Cochran called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. and welcomed those in attendance.
A roll call was conducted wherein all council members were present.

        1.1      Opening Ceremonies – Pledge/Reverence – Council Chair Cochran
                 (Opening Ceremonies for August 18, 2021 to be arranged by Mayor
                  Overson)

Chair Cochran directed the Pledge of Allegiance before offering a reverence.

        1.2      Mayor’s Report

       Mayor Kristie Overson reported a brief meeting she attended with Congressman Burgess
Owens. He would soon be driving through Taylorsville to visit the pending Bus Rapid Transit
(BRT) route. She appreciated the opportunity to strengthen relationships with other elected
officials.

Taylorsville was one of five cities recently recognized by the Economic Corporation of Utah
(EDCU) as a development-ready community. She, Wayne Harper and Jean Ashby had recently
participated in an EDCU town hall, and she wished to thank the Economic Development team for
taking proactive steps to highlight Taylorsville and to prepare the city for business development
opportunities.

Mayor Overson also appreciated the city’s partnership with ChamberWest. She and Sgt. Scott
Lloyd of TVPD were members of the Chamber’s Business Theft Taskforce Committee. The
committee was currently focused on the theft of catalytic converters from vehicles. She was
grateful for the opportunity to work with ChamberWest, as well as other nearby communities to
see what could be done to address the very widespread problem. Senator Karen Mayne had
recently opened a bill file to address the issue at the next legislative session.

The mayor explained that she had a seat on the Chambers’ Housing Sub Committee of the
Legislative Committee. An interesting discussion was held regarding Accessory Dwelling Units
(ADUs). Although Taylorsville already had an ordinance in place, ADUs were still a problem in
certain parts of the city. She had previously made a commitment to the council to see what the city
could do to better understand the process. A meeting had been held with all the staff involved in
that process— staff responsible for business licensing, building permits, code enforcement, GIS,
etc. The group would continue to meet monthly to see what could be done to ensure that ADUs
were appropriate.

After a challenging year, all city employees were back working on site. She recognized that
COVID was still a threat, so the city continued to take precautions. The previous year had shown
that staff could be flexible and work remotely at times, while continuing to serve the community.

Regarding public safety, Mayor Overson chaired the Unified Fire Authority Board and in that
capacity had recently attended the grand opening of a new Midvale fire station. The station would
benefit not just Midvale but the entire valley. She also attended the swearing-out ceremony for
Engineer Pickle who had recently retired.

Taylorsville Police had been operating for a month and was heading smoothly in the right direction.
Creating a very competitive salary range for Taylorsville officers seemed to have created a wage
war across the valley and she was very glad to see those who put themselves in harm’s way were
being paid better.

The mayor pointed out that Centennial Plaza outside city hall looked beautiful and was nearly
complete. Artwork would be featured, and a ribbon-cutting would take place the following month.

Business licensing had received 81 license applications in July. Combined with others that were
submitted in June, 129 business licenses were issued in July. Code Enforcement had opened 96
cases and these cases were important to ensure that Taylorsville remained an attractive, inviting
community. One hundred seventeen code enforcement cases were resolved in July which left 437
active cases. The Planning department had received 7 new applications in July, with 5 being
completed. As for the Building department, 146 permits had been issued, and 34 were currently
active. Although their work was inconvenient for some, Google had exceeded the city’s
expectations regarding the speed of their progress towards improving connectivity in Taylorsville.

        1.2.1    Brandon Ruiz and Cheyenne Ruiz Wrestling Championship Recognition

        Mayor Overson introduced Cheyenne Ruiz and her father Brandon and invited them to step
forward and be recognized. Ms. Ruiz had recently become the 6A girls wrestling state champion
for her weight class. Her father, Brandon Ruiz was her coach and he had also wrestled at
Taylorsville High as a youth and since then had won national and world championships in
Brazilian Jiu Jitsu. The mayor was delighted to recognize both Ruiz’ for their athletic
accomplishments.

The Ruiz family then posed for photos with their family and the city council.

        1.3      Citizen Comments

     Chair Cochran reviewed the citizen comment procedures for the audience. He then asked if
any citizens wished to be heard.

Dan Harrie explained that he was newly retired and had developed a love of pickleball. It was his
understanding that the city had $100,000 to spend on pickleball courts and he did not want it spent
on new ones. Instead, he wished for the existing courts to be improved and re-oriented to sit north
and south as opposed to their current east/west orientation. Currently the courts were not usable
because the sun shone directly in a players’ eyes both in the morning and in the evening which
was when pickleball was typically played. He stated that no one played pickleball on
Taylorsville’s courts but pointed out that both Murray’s and West Jordan’s north/south facing
courts were always busy. He also asked for fencing around the courts.

Trisha Blank wished to discuss voter fraud and ranked-choice voting (RCV). She had friends who
wished to make a presentation to the council at the next meeting. She felt RCV allowed citizens
to have more than one vote which was inappropriate. She also stated that in the past, she had been
allowed to vote without showing identification which was problematic. She felt only those who
asked for a mail-in ballot should have one and that otherwise, voting should be conducted in the
traditional manner and in person. She also asked the city to investigate the efficacy of voting
machines.

There were no additional comments, and Chair Cochran closed the citizen comment period.

        3.1      Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District – Pam Roberts

       Pam Roberts recognized WFWRD employee Ryan Jones who had recently been named the
2021 National Driver of the Year Award. Additionally, Pam introduced Renee Plant who had
filled a new position—Administrative Manager—and would, along with Ms. Roberts, be working
with communities, community partnerships and resolving safety issues on private roads.

WFWRD had recently gone before the Salt Lake County Council to request permission to
reorganize as a Local District. She felt that having six WFWRD board members (including Board
Member Barbieri) physically present at the meeting and another four attend electronically) helped
illustrate to the County Council that the WFWRD board was very supportive of the proposition.

The recent Taylorsville Annual Cleanup Event had resulted in the disposal of the following:
   • 6.7 tons of waste
   • 2.6 tons of green waste
   • 1.5 tons of glass

There were 1,272 green waste subscribers in Taylorsville as well as 121 glass recycling
subscribers. Ms. Roberts reported more good news when she explained that the cost of recycling
had taken a much-needed dive from $65/ton to $20/ton.

WFWRD’s Sustainability Team had made 52 classroom presentations during the 2020-2021
school year, 12 of which were in Taylorsville.

Before ending her report, Ms. Roberts took the opportunity to reiterate the reasons that the
dumpster program had been modified and the modifications continued to be necessary.

Councilmember Burgess spoke very highly of Ms. Roberts and her staff and the work that they did
during the clean-up event held earlier in the summer.
