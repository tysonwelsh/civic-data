2. Administrative Reports
    2.1. Review of the Herriman City Public Improvement District (PID) Policy – Alan Rae, Finance
          Director
Finance Director Alan Rae said the new City Attorney had reviewed the proposed Public Improvement District
(PID) Policy, and recommended few changes. The policy was described as the City’s standard or requirements
for a PID, and Attorney Andrizzi recommended they change the policy to state this was the City’s “minimum
standard” for PIDs. Director Rae then went through the process of creating and approving a PID, noting the
Council could put specific requirements in the governing documents of the PID. Having a policy in place did
not require the City to approve a PID, and there was no liability to the City if they denied a PID even if the
proposal met all of the standards outlined in the policy. There was specific language in the policy to protect
the City in this manner. If the Council did not intend to allow any PIDs in the City, they could create a policy
that states such. Regarding location, staff recommended including a map that depicted areas were a PID could
be considered.

Councilmember Henderson was not comfortable with a PID in general. When this idea was brought to the
Council a few months ago, he was under the impression this was something they might consider for the
automall property only. The policy before them now seemed to have morphed into something more. He
understood there was some sense of urgency in putting a policy in place, but it wasn’t a requirement the City
have such a policy. A PID could still be created following the State’s policy. The safest thing they could do
was to create a policy stating that PIDs were not allowed in Herriman. Director Rae reminded the Council
that they don’t have to approve a PID, even if it meets the standards of the policy. Councilmember Henderson
was concerned about the unintended consequences of jumping into a policy so quickly when they really didn’t
need to. He would feel more comfortable if they took their time to explore the potential outcome of such a
policy. If they do accept this policy, he recommended that they limit it to the automall property only. That
could be broadened later, if the Council felt it was appropriate. Councilmember Henderson expressed
concerns about this specific developer being able to make a PID work at all.

The Council agreed that a PID was not appropriate for residential development.

Councilmember Shields was also concerned about the unintended consequences of allowing a PID in the City.
The policy itself seemed to be drafted well, and he couldn’t see any harm in adopting the policy. However, he
was not comfortable moving forward with an actual PID at this time. There were still many unanswered
questions.

Councilmember Smith felt that having a policy in place would give the City more control over any PIDs
proposed. It would act as a guide for any parties looking to use this option. They would not be adopting this
because they wanted to see a specific PID, but because they know they would eventually be approached with
one.

     2.2. Review on General Plan and Zoning Amendment proposal for the 11800 South Master
          Development Agreement – Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy provided an update regarding the Planning Commission meeting from the previous
week and stated that they could have another work session regarding the MDA for this project. Last week, the
Planning Commission held a public hearing on the General Plan amendment and proposed rezone, and the
Planning Commission made recommendations for both of those requests. The subject property was
undeveloped, and the developer was proposing to do an office development, natural open space, and an area
for indoor and outdoor storage. The proposed General Plan amendment would create ten-acres of commercial
or office use, 11-acres of light industrial and storage, and the rest would be open space. The Planning
Commission recommended approval of the request, with a zoning condition on the M-1 Zone which would
limit the outdoor storage to recreational vehicles only. They had also recommended a zoning condition that
would create a building height restriction in the M-1 zone, since there currently was no height restriction in the
zoning ordinance. City Planner Maloy reported the comments received during the public hearing primarily
centered around the open space, traffic generated by the proposed uses, and the height of the buildings and
their impact on privacy. It was suggested by a resident that they restrict land use by allowing the rezone to
Professional Office rather than C-2. City Planner Maloy identified the location of the indoor and outdoor
storage and noted that the proposed outdoor storage area was over a natural gas line, where they could not
build any structures. He asked the Council if they would prefer to have a work session regarding the MDA on
a separate night from the potential approval, or to have both items on the same night. The Council agreed
that the discussion and the action item should be on separate nights.

   2.3. Infrastructure funding for East Herriman discussion – Blake Thomas, Community
        Development Director
Community Development Director Blake Thomas explained that he and Director Rae had been analyzing the
CDA for East Herriman. Director Rae had coordinated with Zions Bank to get the budget analysis updated,
and Director Thomas was examining the infrastructure that had been done and how much money had been
spent by the developer.

Director Rae first addressed the budget analysis, stating they were looking at the project as a whole and
determining if there was more money available there. They found there were numerous things on the expense
list that the City had already reimbursed through impact fees. They also had Zions Bank do an evaluation of
the potential income of this CDA. The results turned out to be pretty close to what staff had calculated. After
the amount to the school and the county, the City would be able to receive $39,663,000, which was within half
a million of what they projected previously.

Councilmember Henderson asked if the loss was from the project taking so long to complete, and Director
Rae said that was part of it.

Director Thomas reported Rosecrest had provided documents including contracts, change orders, and other
items pertaining to infrastructure. Using that information, he mapped out those improvements in the GIS.
There were five categories of infrastructure improvements, including road improvements, storm drain, culinary
water, secondary water, and sanitary sewer. He said it had been difficult to nail down exactly how much was
May 13, 2020 – City Council Minutes                                                Page 4 of 11
spent on each item, since projects often included multiple infrastructure aspects, and he hadn’t received actual
invoices from Rosecrest yet. Referencing a map, Director Thomas addressed a few specific areas. There had
been some infrastructure improvements that have been done in the CDS area that the City had reimbursement
agreements for, and they would be reimbursing the developers with impact fees.

Councilmember Henderson said the infrastructure was budgeted at about $65 million, and they were expecting
to get $40 million from the CDA, with impact fees making up the difference. He asked if it were possible to
use as much impact fee money as possible so that they could free up CDA money for other uses. Director
Thomas said it was a complicated issue, because they collected impact fees from all over the City, not just
within the CDA.

Councilmember Shields acknowledged the complexity of the issue. He asked if they had enough money
between impact fees and the CDA revenue to cover the full reimbursement for the improvements that had
been done. Director Rae said that it would be close. Councilmember Shields requested more information
regarding how impact fees were assessed, collected, and distributed.

Councilmember Henderson suggested they reevaluate their obligations and priorities. With all of the MDAs,
the City was committing to reimburse them with impact fees, which would eventually stop coming in. He
wondered whether they would be able to meet those obligations in the long-term. They needed to understand
their capacity to collect impact fees in general.

    2.4. City Manager Updates – Brett Wood, City Manager
City Manager Brett Wood said there had been discussions with other cities in the area about ordering antibody
test kits for the coronavirus. Originally, the City had considered ordering 1,000 kits, but that was lowered to
500 kits. The cost would be about $29,000. The order had not placed yet, because he wanted input from the
City Council. There was a concern that the antibody test wouldn’t be beneficial to the City at this time.

Unified Fire Authority Assistant Fire Chief Riley Pilgrim commented the antibody testing was still relatively
new, and there was a variety of opinions regarding the benefits of such testing. The Unified Fire Authority
was interested in this testing, but they were currently not pursuing. The nasal swab was the best and most
accurate form of testing available right now. Once more data was collected regarding the antibody testing, they
would feel more comfortable pursuing it.

City Manager Wood said there had been a few scares with City employees, but none of the employees or their
families had contracted COVID-19. He felt confident about the safety measures they had put in place.

Councilmember Smith commented the initial idea behind getting the antibody testing in Herriman was to
provide another avenue. However, if there was question about the accuracy of the test at this time, they didn’t
need to pursue it. The Council agreed not to order antibody tests at this time.

City Manager Wood noted there was a plan in place for the City going from an orange to a yellow level on the
Risk Assessment scale. Their intention was to protect the City employees and public as much as possible.

Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas reported on the parks and events as the City went to an orange level.
They had opened the skate park, with signage regarding social distancing. Staff was also cleaning the restrooms

Assistant City Manager Thomas said more and more people were coming to Blackridge, and made the
recommendation to close the beach and the water until they had a solid plan in place. The Governor’s Office
had come out with guidelines limiting private gatherings to 20 people; however, was not meant for gatherings
run by organizations, such as the City. It would then fall onto the City to make sure people were socially
distancing themselves when they come to City areas. Assistant City Manager Thomas said they usually start
staffing Blackridge after Memorial Day, but they’ve had to begin earlier because of the increased activity there.
They had determined that capacity was determined by the stalls in the parking lot. Once the stalls were full,
cars would be turned away at the gate. They anticipated having the area fully staffed by May 22, 2020.

Mayor Watt was concerned about Memorial Day, based on past holiday weekends at Blackridge. He wasn’t
sure they would be able to actively manage the facility on a holiday weekend with only a week’s notice. He
suggested that they consider opening after the holiday.

Councilmember Smith liked staff idea of using the parking stalls as an indicator of capacity. He asked if they
could close off the reservoir to walk-in traffic as well as vehicles. Assistant City Manager Thomas said they
would not close to walk-in traffic. Normally, 20 cars per day were just trail users. If they reserved those stalls
for trail users, that would lighten the load on the beach. Councilmember Smith said Memorial Day weekend
had the potential to be very busy. It would be better to staff the area to help with social distancing and capacity,
because the people would come weather the beach and water were open or not. They needed to manage the
crowd that would be there.

The Council and staff discussed the issue further. Councilmember Ohrn was in favor of staffing Blackridge,
if staff was confident that they could handle it. Assistant City Manager Thomas said they would only open if

     10.4. Discussion and consideration for the Herriman City Shop Around, an Economic
          Development Program – Tami Moody, Assistant City Manager
Assistant City Manager Tami Moody explained the situation with COVID-19 had left businesses looking for
relief until lift could get back to normal. Many businesses had to close their doors because they were unable to
meet the State’s orders and directives. The proposed Herriman City Shop Around was the next thing the
Economic Development Program was doing to help stimulate local businesses. The program would introduce
Herriman Bucks in the form of a business card or gift card, and it would be sent to each household in Herriman.
The amount would be $10, which would be used toward a purchase of $20 at any of the participating businesses.
At the conclusion of the set timeframe, businesses would be able to submit those cards back to the City for
reimbursement. She confirmed that the Finance Department believed this was doable within the current budget
and would not place a hardship on the City. This program could generate $2,500 in sales tax. If this was
approved today, staff could send letters out to local businesses tomorrow asking if they were interested in
participating.

    10.5. Discussion and Consideration of a Resolution adopting and approving a Public
         Improvement District (PID) Policy – Alan Rae, Finance Director
Finance Director Alan Rae said the Council and staff had discussed the proposed Public Improvement District
(PID) Policy at the past several work meetings. He explained that the PID allowed for landowners to assess a
property tax on their property in order to build infrastructure. This would replace impact fees on those
properties. The proposed policy would be the minimum standards for PIDs in Herriman City, but that did
not bind the Council to accept any proposed PID, and there was no liability to the City for rejecting a proposal.
He briefly explained the process of approving a PID. Director Rae emphasized the policy prohibited the use
of PIDs for residential developments. He then presented a map depicting the area where a PID could be
considered in Herriman City.

Councilmember Shields said they had a robust discussion about this item during the work session. There were
concerns expressed about moving forward with any approval of any specific PID, and whether approval of the
policy would open the door to approval. There was an understanding that the automall property owner was
interested in pursuing a PID. There were many questions that needed to be answered before a PID was
approved; however, Councilmember Shields felt that the policy itself was appropriate.

Councilmember Ohrn said that she had to leave the work session early, but there was a comment about having
legal counsel provide some pros and cons regarding a PID. She was in favor of continuing the item to allow
for further discussion.

Councilmember Smith said that he didn’t have any strong objections to moving forward with the policy, since
the meat of any project would be in the governing documents.

Mayor Watts had no objection to continuing the item for further discussion.

Councilmember Shields moved to continue Item 10.5 adopting and approving a Public Improvement District Policy to the May
27, 2020 City Council meeting. Councilmember Ohrn seconded the motion and all voted aye.
