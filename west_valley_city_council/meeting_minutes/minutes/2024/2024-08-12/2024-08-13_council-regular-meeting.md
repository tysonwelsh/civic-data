PUBLIC HEARINGS
A.   ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING THE BUDGET OF WEST
     VALLEY CITY FOR THE FISCAL YEAR 2024-2025
     Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council
     Meeting scheduled August 13, 2024, in order for the City Council to hear and
     consider public comments regarding the Budget of West Valley City for the Fiscal
     Year 2024-2025

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

              This resolution adopts the final budget for West Valley City for the 2024-
              2025 fiscal year and sets a date for public comment at a hearing to be held
              on August 13, 2024.
              West Valley City will adopt a final budget that will be made available for
              public inspection during regular office hours in the City’s Recorder’s office
              and gave notice of a hearing to receive public comment before the final
              adoption of this tentative budget for FY 2024-2025.

       Jim Welch, Finance Director, provided an overview of the budget hearing for the
       fiscal year 2025, noting that it also serves as a "truth in taxation" hearing. This
       process allows a governmental entity within the state to adjust property tax rates.
       Jim highlighted that this year's budget is $113 million, with two-thirds of the
       revenue coming from taxes—primarily sales taxes and property taxes. Sales tax,
       being volatile, poses some economic challenges, while property tax remains stable
       and controlled by the city, as permitted by state law. The budget's expenditures are
       largely directed towards public safety, with over 50% allocated to the police and
       fire departments. Other city operations, including public works and parks, account
       for the remaining expenses.

       Jim emphasized that the hearing specifically addressed the collection of a judgment
       levy, not a property tax increase. A judgment levy occurs when businesses
       successfully appeal their property assessments, resulting in rebates. To cover this
       shortfall, the city is allowed a one-time tax rate adjustment. For this year, the
       judgment levy is $192,200, affecting the average residence by about $3.28 and a
       business by $5.96. This adjustment is not a recurring increase; it's a one-time fix to
       balance the budget due to the appeal. Jim also explained that property taxes are
       based on revenue rather than rate, meaning even if the rate decreases, higher
       property values might still result in increased taxes. Recent legislative changes

  require the city to declare that no recurring ad valorem tax revenue will be generated
  from this adjustment and to disclose the proposed consolidated rate changes within
  the county. West Valley City is the only entity adjusting its rate for a judgment levy
  this year. Jim concluded by inviting questions and confirming that the hearing met
  all statutory requirements.

  Councilmember Huynh asked if the City studies sales tax rates to reduce the
  property tax rates.

  Jim emphasized the importance of accurate revenue projections, noting that a
  significant amount of time and effort is devoted to the process. He explained that
  the budget process began nine months ago, and after its completion, there will be a
  brief break before resuming again in December. The city closely tracks all revenue
  streams, particularly sales tax, on a monthly basis. These projections are based on
  historical trends, economic expectations, and the city's growth. Jim highlighted the
  volatility of sales tax revenue, which can fluctuate with the economy. In contrast,
  property tax is much more stable, with the city historically collecting 99% of due
  property taxes. This stability is recognized by bond rating agencies and the state,
  which is why the legislature allows city councils to influence property tax rates.
  While sales tax revenue can be strong, it varies from year to year. The city does its
  best to attract new businesses to boost sales tax revenue, which is a key component
  in avoiding the need for property tax increases. The city's economic development
  department plays a crucial role in this effort, making economic growth a high
  priority.

  Councilmember Nordfelt stated that one of the documents in the presentation shows
  a 100% tax increase but in actuality, the tax rate is actually going down. He
  indicated that because this is a judgment levy, the rate is going from zero.

  Councilmember Fitisemanu asked if it’s normal for West Valley City to be the only
  entity in the county capturing a judgement levy.

  Jim emphasized the consistency of the city's approach, stating that West Valley
  City is probably the most consistent entity in Salt Lake County when it comes to
  handling property tax adjustments. He noted that while Salt Lake City and other
  entities might impose larger judgments or more frequent adjustments, West Valley
  City has been steady and measured in its approach.

  Jim explained that when a judgment is awarded, it's not just imposed on West
  Valley City; it affects all the entities to which the particular business pays property
  taxes, such as the school district or service districts. The judgment levy is just a
  small piece of the overall tax burden, but it's important to address it openly. He

  mentioned that this year's judgment levy of $192,000 is relatively minor, equating
  to about $3 per year for a residence. However, Welch stressed the importance of
  discussing it publicly, as these amounts can accumulate over time. For example,
  $192,000 this year could be followed by $300,000 next year and more in subsequent
  years, potentially leading to a significant shortfall in funds for public services. He
  concluded by highlighting that while other cities may have different philosophies
  regarding judgment levies, West Valley City prioritizes equity and transparency,
  seeking to avoid the need for larger operational tax increases in the future by
  addressing these smaller adjustments as they arise.
