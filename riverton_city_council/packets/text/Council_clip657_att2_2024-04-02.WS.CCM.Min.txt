38          Duplex Discussion
39
40   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, said this discussion item was a revisiting of a
41   previous discussion regarding single-family housing types. He noted that the current city code
42   only permits attached units such as duplexes and triplexes in multifamily zones, with acreage
43   limitations typically applying to areas of five acres or more. Accessory dwelling units are
44   allowed, but the property must be owner-occupied.
45
46   Mr. Lethbridge said the previous meeting covered considerations such as lot size requirements,
47   owner occupancy, parking, and utilities, along with examples of such housing units. However,
48   there were concerns raised about whether all issues had been adequately addressed.
49
50   Councilmembers discussed this issue further and asked clarifying questions. Councilmember
51   McDougal expressed discomfort with further increasing density, especially considering the
     Page | 2 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   existing traffic issues that have yet to be addressed. He advocated for maintaining the current
 2   zoning policies and not introducing additional density measures.

 4   Councilmember Pierucci highlighted issues with parking enforcement that have been brought up

 6   associated with zoning changes before moving forward. He acknowledged the value of allowing

18             Operational Standards for Entry into the Riverton Town Days Parade Discussion
19
20   Brett Coombs, Assistant City Attorney, introduced a resolution regarding parade guidelines and
21   procedures, stemming from new guidance received from the Utah Local Governments Trust.
22   This guidance aims to provide municipalities with recommendations for organizing parades to
23   limit liability and ensure the safety of participants and spectators. The resolution presents a
24   parade policy titled "Riverton City Town Days Parade Policy," developed based on the
25   guidelines provided by the Utah Local Governments Trust. The policy is intended to be flexible
26   and adaptable to the specific needs and circumstances of Riverton City.
27
28   Councilmember Pierucci acknowledged the importance of weighing risks against rewards when
29   making decisions, and recognized the role of insurance in providing protection. However, he
30   stated his belief that the Council should consider broader policy decisions with the community's
31   interests in mind. He argued that allowing candy and other objects to be thrown enhances the
32   parade experience, based on feedback received from the community. He expressed his
33   willingness to accept the potential risks and liabilities associated with allowing candy throwing,
34   believing that the likelihood of accidents is low when proper safety precautions are in place.
35
36   Councilmember McCay said she was open to learning more about how other cities have
37   managed candy distribution, and indicated a preference for deferring a final decision on the
38   matter to a later meeting. Mayor Pro Tem Buroker agreed and noted that as floats and crowds
39   grow larger, so does the associated risk. She mentioned Draper as an example of a City that
40   has limited candy throwing to individuals walking alongside floats, and expressed interest in
41   learning more about how this approach has worked for them. However, she clarified that she
42   was not yet ready to make a decision on the matter and deferred further discussion to a later
43   time.
44
45   Councilmember McDougal echoed the sentiment of deferring a decision on the matter and
46   expressed reluctance to jeopardize the unique and fun aspects of the parade.
47
48   Councilmember Haymond acknowledged the potential for tragic incidents but expressed a belief
49   in personal responsibility. He believes that citizens should have the right to enjoy events such
50   as parades without excessive regulation.

30             Ordinance No. 24-03 – Amending Riverton City Code Title 13, Chapters 5 and 10
31             (Culinary and Secondary Water)
32
33   Brett Coombs, Assistant City Attorney, explained the purpose behind the proposed ordinance
34   changes which is to simplify utility billing procedures, particularly regarding culinary water and
35   secondary water bills. Currently, these bills are sent to both property owners and renters, which
36   has led to difficulties when renters move out abruptly, leaving bills unpaid or causing confusion
37   about who is responsible for payment. The proposed amendments aim to address this issue by
38   requiring all utility bills to be sent to the property owner only. This will enable property owners to
39   manage bill distribution to renters or collect payments from them as necessary. Additionally, the
40   changes seek to update outdated ordinances and align them with existing policies for better
41   clarity and efficiency in billing processes.
42
43   Mayor Pro Tem Buroker raised a concern about the lack of clarity regarding the responsibility for
44   fire hydrants, particularly in homeowner associations (HOAs). She mentioned past issues where
45   it was unclear whether the City or the HOA was responsible for maintaining the fire hydrants.
46   She sought clarification on whether the proposed ordinance was the appropriate venue to
47   address this issue and include language specifying responsibility for fire hydrant maintenance.
48
49   Mr. Coombs acknowledged that there are other sections of the City code where language
50   regarding the responsibility for maintenance of fire hydrants could potentially be included. He
51   suggested that it could be incorporated into the culinary water secondary water ordinance or
     Page | 6 Minutes – City Council Meeting

11             Resolution No. 24-40 – Approving a Change Order to PO Contract 170619 adding
12             five restrooms that are in the Civic Center to the Civic Center Remodel Project
13
14   Craig Calvert, Purchasing Manager, explained that they had initially budgeted for restroom
15   renovations in the building and the civic center for the next fiscal year. However, upon further
16   consideration, they decided it would be more practical to include the restroom renovations within
17   the ongoing project to avoid opening the civic center only to close it again for remodeling later.
18   He requested a change order to incorporate the restroom renovations into the current project
19   timeline, allowing for simultaneous completion and avoiding the need for subsequent closure.
20   The contractor, Big Bear, provided a price for the restroom renovations as part of the overall
21   project.
22
23   Ms. Garn presented slides showcasing the current state of the restrooms, noting that while they
24   are well maintained and clean, they are outdated, having been last renovated in 2003-2004.
25   She highlighted that the restroom fixtures, including plumbing, are 20 years old and in need of
26   replacement due to wear and tear.
27
28   Councilmember McDougal MOVED that the City Council approve Resolution No. 24-40 –
29   Approving a Change Order to PO Contract 170619 adding five restrooms that are in the
30   Civic Center to the Civic Center Remodel Project. Councilmember Pierucci SECONDED the
31   motion. Mayor Pro Tem Buroker called for discussion on the motion. Mayor Pro Tem Buroker
32   called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: Buroker-yes, Haymond-yes, McCay-yes,
