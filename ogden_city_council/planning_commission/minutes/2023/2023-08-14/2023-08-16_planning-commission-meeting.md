1. Discussion of Development Code Update

    Mr. Simpson stated the City has been wanting to update the Zoning Ordinance for some time,
    and has now received funding from Wasatch Front Regional Council towards that effort. The
    Planning Department has entered into a contract with a consultant team who has begun doing
    research and has met with some citizens in an effort to understand the needs of the
    community. This discussion is to bring the Commission up to date with the progress and
    obtain input relating to the process.

    Mr. Rypien introduced John Janssen, and Jennifer Gardner from Logan Simpson consultants.
     He stated in reviewing the existing codes, it has been determined it would be best to create
    a unified development code which would incorporate the Zoning, Subdivision, Sign and
    Landmarks Ordinances, where all standards would be in the same place. The intent is to
    make it more user-friendly by adding more graphics and tables as well as making it a more
    searchable document, making it more clear and concise with all standards in the same
    location. This will be a two-year process. This presentation will be an overview of what has
    been accomplished and begin the involvement of the Planning Commission.

    Commissioner Safsten asked if it could be adopted in sections. Mr. Janssen stated due to its
    length, the intent is to create several modules and present sections for adoption throughout
    the two-year process, and then a final review of the entire document. Commissioner Graf
    asked if other communities have turned to a unified development code and whether they seem
    to simplify the process as has been presented. Mr. Janssen indicated these are becoming
    fairly common and are preferred by the development community. He stated another concern

making changes which impact development.

He then introduced the team, indicating he and Jennifer are the lead consultants with others
working on graphics, public outreach, conformance with State law and other details. He states

He stated the team had visited Ogden last week and had gone on a tour of the City and had

In reviewing the current code, he has noted there are some standards for both Engineering
an Building Code, stating while there is some relationship between these and development
standards, they are located elsewhere in the Municipal Code and when changes occur, it is
cumbersome to make those same changes in the development code. The intent is to
reference those codes with a hyperlink. While the development community wants easy
standards, there needs to be a balance to assure the vision of the community is satisfied.
Chair Sandau commented while standards may be made easier to understand, it does not
necessarily mean the City is lowering its standards. Mr. Janssen commented many of those
interviewed support mixed use, particularly near the downtown, but that the current mixed
use ordinance is difficult to understand and to regulate by both the City staff and the
developers.

Mr. Janssen indicated the first priority is to complete the subdivision code as the State made
some changes which need to be codified by next February. He stated there is a 15-day time
period for Staff review once a complete application is received, after which time it is forwarded
to the Commission for approval.

Discussion continued relating to the public process, and whether public comment should
always be asked for. Mr. Janssen indicated that legislative items always require a public
hearing, while administrative do not. While many communities invite comments for
administrative items, there is no ability for the Commission to deny action if standards of the
code are satisfied. This process could create a false perception of the public in that they
expect they could change the mind of the Commission and creates a lack of public trust.
Commission also discussed the allowance of exceptions, stating when the ordinance is not
followed, it creates a lack of credibility. Mr. Janssen stated in the past the Commission had
more authority relating to conditional use permits, but current State law limits the ability of the
Commission to deny a conditional use permit unless they can identify particular impacts that
cannot be mitigated. In reality, a conditional use now is a permitted use in which conditions
of approval may be attached to mitigate impacts of the use. Because of this, it is anticipated
many items now listed as conditional uses will be made either permitted uses. He further
discussed necessary changes to the Subdivision Ordinance to meet State law, and then
stated the upcoming process will be to create a structure and format for the Unified
Development Code, including a table of contents, design style and format.

Discussion followed relating to the construction process and while it appears the code will be
easier for developers, consideration needs to also be given to citizens who may be doing

2. Discussion of Short-Term Rentals in Downtown

Mr. Simpson indicated that a request has been made by Visit Ogden to allow vacation rentals in
the downtown. The current ordinance regulates residential vacation rentals but is limited to
residential zones. The proposal is to allow these in the C-ENT and H25 zones in the downtown.
The Commission had tabled action during its public hearing and requested more options for
executive housing which might be a short-term resident working on a contract for an unspecified
time period.

He defined short-term rental as a self-contained dwelling unit with full kitchen, bathroom and
living amenities which are not typical in a hotel room. Stays are limited to 30 days and residents
have a primary permanent residence, while hotel stays may be up to 90 fays. They also differ
from hotel uses in that the property owner is typically not living on-site where hotel uses have a
live check-in procedure. A hotel use in the downtown is required to have a minimum of 15
rooms while a short-term rental may be a single dwelling unit.

He stated Staff has obtained information from the Police Department which indicates up to five
calls per year for each property is common. However, there may be other uses in the same
building and it is unknown whether the call was specific to the vacation rental use. While Staff
can see a benefit to providing visitor housing in the downtown, there is concern as to who bears
the burden of the use. It may be existing property owners who are affected by noise and
nuisance, police and code enforcement staff or potential residents seeking long-term housing
which is no longer available due to the use of short-term rentals. There also may be a burden to
the property owner in maintenance of the unit, but as they receive money from the use, the
burden to the owner is somewhat mitigated. There is concern that these be limited, and
separation standards considered. Staff is proposing some language to regulate these in the
downtown, specifically to not allow both apartments and short-term rentals in the same building.
Staff also is proposing executive housing or mid-range housing may be allowed in these
dwelling units. He felt there could be a balance where these can be allowed, but regulated
enough to ease the burden on the public.

Discussion continued as to whether a mix of long-term and short-term rentals may be self-
regulated in that if there are continuous problems associated with one, the other tenants would
either complain or move. Mr. Chris Tremea stated there are numerous calls complaining about
short-term rentals throughout the City to both Code Enforcement and the Police Department.
He also commented many of the concerns are due to the lack of management by the property
owner. Commissioner Safsten asked about the ability to enforce the limited length of stay to 30
days. Mr. Simpson stated the City typically relies on complaints from neighbors as there is no
way to monitor these. Mr. Tremea responded several hundreds of dollars and hundreds of man
hours have been spent investigating vacation rentals. If the use has a business license, the
citation process can begin and could result in the loss of license. However, if the use has no
business license, the process would go directly through the court system. Commission also
expressed concern that the use of short-term rentals further limits the existing housing stock by
eliminating homes for long-term residents. Commissioner Graf commented short-term visitors
leave less of a footprint on the home as they are there for a short time, and the property owner
makes more money for the weekend use as a short-term rental. It was suggested there should
be more opportunity for long-term rentals in neighborhoods, and Mr. Simpson stated by allowing
these in the downtown, some of the burden to neighborhoods could be eliminated. Discussion
continued relating to the number of guests allowed in a short-term rental based on its size,
which would be identified in the business license. Mr. Simpson stated the intent of regulating
these is to assure there is a balance of uses in the downtown. Commissioner Aaberg expressed
support for allowing a mix of short-term and long-term rentals in the same building, stating it
would be better than to have empty units, and if long-term renters are made aware of the short-
term use, they could determine whether or not they desired to live there. He felt the impact of
mixing the uses is minimal and should be allowed. He also felt if there are too many restrictions,
owners would bypass the license process. Mr. Simpson indicated the mix of uses would be
more difficult to enforce as they would all have the same building address. Mr. Tanner
commented while all cities require licensing, the State legislature has limited the ability of cities
to check on these uses. Commission discussed the importance of a baseline of data relating to
complaints so changes can be made if needed. Mr. Simpson indicated it is important to have
standards in the code so enforcement is consistent. Commissioner Graf expressed support of
the concept considering it may help to populate existing empty buildings in the downtown.
Commissioner Safsten commented restricting the use to either long-term or short-term rentals
would be easier to enforce, indicating the size of the building might affect its use. He felt smaller
buildings could house either short-term or long-term rentals, while larger buildings would
typically be dedicated to long-term rentals. Due to the mix of buildings in the downtown, there
would likely be a mix of how residential units were occupied, creating the desirable diversity of
uses.

Mr. Simpson stated rentals can be advertised on VRBO or AIRB&B without qualifications that
they are short-term or that the owner has a permanent residence elsewhere. He expressed
concern that these may become homeless shelters. Commission discussed the lack of social
service uses not only throughout Weber County, but Ogden bears the burden for all of northern
Utah including drug rehab centers and transitional housing. While these services are needed,
there needs to be a balance in their location so the greater Ogden area is better served. It was
indicated the lack of affordable housing contributes to the homeless population. Because Salt
Lake is not building more homeless facilities, those residents have the ability to quickly ride the
train and come into Ogden. Mr. Tremea stated tenants at the homeless shelter are reluctant to
leave as they are able to have a free bed for the night as well as three hot meals each day.
There is concern that the high price of rent as well as low wages often contribute to people
becoming homeless. Commissioner Williams commented it is difficult for young adults to

Chair Sandau felt it is important that on-site management is available, whether a stay is for one

while a unit could be a permanent residence for long-term rentals.


Commissioner Williams asked if there is a way to put the burden back on the property owner,
such as losing the ability to use the property as short-term rental if a particular number of
complaints to either police or code enforcement and the owner is unwilling to remedy problems.
Commissioner Graf suggested a first offense might be merely a conversation with the owner
while a second offence would create a more costly fine than typical for code violations.

Commission discussed the potential of existing residents moving to other areas due to the
impacts of a neighboring short-term rental. It also was indicated the market will somewhat
regulate these uses. Commissioner Sandau suggested an on-site manager in these buildings
might allow a mix of short-term and long-term rentals in the same building. Discussion
continued relating to on-site management or doorman options to regulate a mix of short-term
and long-term rentals. commission also requested more firm data relating to calls coming into
Code Services as well as the number of repeat offenders. Mr. Simpson acknowledged while the
City is aware many are in use and not licensed, the adoption of this ordinance amendment
would assist in enforcement of both those licensed and unlicensed.


Commissioner Graf expressed while he welcomed the new commission member, Bryan Schade
had contributed much to the City and hoped the Commission would do something to recognize
his service. He suggested a cake or a card signed by commission members to thank
Commissioner Schade for his dedication and decades of service to the Planning Commission.


Commission then reviewed items which are to be scheduled for the September meeting.
