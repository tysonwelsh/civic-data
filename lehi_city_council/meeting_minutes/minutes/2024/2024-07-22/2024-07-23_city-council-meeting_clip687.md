10. Public Hearing to receive input respective to the LBR study, report, and
    recommendation for the Creation of a New School District.
    Mayor Johnson stated that this is the second required public hearing related to the potential
    creation of a new school district.

    Mayor Johnson opened the public hearing.

    Julie King, a resident of Saratoga Springs, expressed support for the Central Interlocal and
    praised the collaborative experience.

    Mac Sims, Lehi resident, agreed with forming an Interlocal Agreement and encouraged a
    continued commitment to district employees and students. He also emphasized the
    importance of collaboration for long-term success.

    John Madsen, Lehi resident, was in favor of putting it on the ballot for voters to decide and
    he was in favor of the split.

    Joylynn Lincoln, a resident of Saratoga Springs, shared her perspective on Alpine School
    District's growth and the need for more local control.

    Emily Lockhart, Lehi resident, was in support of putting the split on the ballot and was for
    the split itself, citing the importance of resident decision-making and the potential benefits
    for students. She also cautioned against the challenges of change and encouraged
    consideration of the needs of all Alpine School District students, including those from
    Saratoga Springs.

    Steve Brown, Lehi resident, expressed support for local hiring in Alpine School District. He
    also advocated for accountability and community involvement.

    Stacy Bateman, Lehi resident, highlighted her relationship with the Council emphasizing
    their long-term collaboration on city needs. She stated that forming an interlocal agreement
    presents residents with the best option for a split. She stated her support for the split.

    Lisa Cook, Lehi resident, stated that she can appreciate the need for more local control, but
    was concerned that west Lehi may not get the needed funding with the proposed alignment.
    She was in favor of a split but was unsure if the proposed boundary was the best option.

    Mayor Johnson closed the public hearing.

    Mayor Johnson shared that he initially did not want to get involved in the school district split.
    However, after the last school bond election failed, there were some concerning numbers that
    prompted the need to have further discussions about a potential split. He was pleased that any
    final decision would ultimately be made by the voters. He expressed gratitude for the
    collaborative work and partnership between the City Council and the school board members.

    Councilor Stallings emphasized the importance of hearing all perspectives on the proposed
    school district split. She expressed gratitude for the public's input and acknowledged the
Lehi City Council Meeting                         4                                      July 23, 2024
    council's thorough research and consideration of the complex issue. She conveyed the
    Council's commitment to a thoughtful, inclusive process as they determine whether to put the
    split on the ballot.

    Councilor Hancock stated that he has been thoroughly researching the issue for an extended
    period. He believes the split will provide better local control and the ability to address the
    unique needs of each area. He also highlighted the importance of representation, noting that
    the central district's representation would be more closely aligned with student population.
    Additionally, Hancock pointed out that the three proposed districts would still be among the
    largest in the state, maintaining economies of scale. He also believes this specific configuration
    will best serve the interests of students, teachers, and taxpayers in the area.

    Councilor Newall emphasized the critical importance of the financial implications in this
    decision, as they directly impact students, teachers, and classroom resources. She believes the
    central district configuration would result in less tax increases compared to staying with Alpine
    School District. She noted the west side's strong motivation to split from Alpine, leaving a two-
    way or three-way split as Lehi’s options. She highlights a concerning $21 million annual deficit
    in the south part of the current district, projected to grow to $31 million within 5 years. Newall
    worries that addressing this deficit through school closures or raising taxes in the north would
    negatively impact students and teachers, as funding would go towards buildings rather than
    programs and personnel. Overall, Newall's primary concerns center around finding a
    sustainable financial solution that best serves the needs of the community.

    It was noted the Council Condie expressed his support at the previous public hearing.

    Councilor Albrecht expressed respect for the Council's evolving decision-making process as
    they collected more data. As a former special education teacher in Alpine School District, she
    understands the concerns of teachers, parents, and students. She stated that an interlocal
    agreement was necessary after seeing the challenging conditions faced by students and teachers
    in Saratoga Springs, where a new high school was denied. She believes Alpine School District
    has outgrown its ability to equitably address student needs across the region. She was in
    support of putting the school district split on the ballot and personally favors creating a central
    school district, as she believes it will better serve the interests of students and teachers.
