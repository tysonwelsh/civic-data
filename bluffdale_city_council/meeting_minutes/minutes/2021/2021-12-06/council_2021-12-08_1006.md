         a.     Discussion Related to the Point Framework Plan. Presenter, Alan Matheson.

Alan Matheson, Point of the Mountain State Land Authority Executive Director, presented an
overview and update on the Point of the Mountain Project that is now nationally recognized. The
Point of the Mountain State Land Authority was created by statute and given the directive to create a
project that will benefit the community and the State. It operates under an 11-person board that
includes the Lieutenant Governor and Representative Lowry Snow as Co-Chairs. There are four
legislators, 2 mayors, heads of state agencies, the Comm issioner of Higher Education, and individuals
with private sector real estate experience . The board was established by statute to ensure that the
project includes all possible elements that have been discussed in previous meetings. They adopted
Guiding Standards to keep the project in accordance w ith the desired outcome, the first of which is to
promote the public interest.

One of the main objectives is to provide a return to the community in terms of quality of life as well
as an economic return by creating a model community. Concerns about growth have increased
dramatically over the last several years, inc luding concerns about traffic, air quality, and t he cost of
housing. They are interested in growing in thoughtful ways with a long-term vision. Because it is an
enormous project, they want to involve as many people as possible. All information is 01lline and
accessible to the public. They have received input fro m over I 0,000 individuals over the last several
years. While it is not possible to please everyone, the goal is to listen to and address the most pressing
concerns to create a vibrant, mixed-use, interesting community that is active and has a great
transportation system. It should include future-focused transportation that minimizes the need for
vehic les on the site,job creation at the highest level, and innovation that allows research to create jobs
and solve problems.

The planning team, which consists of Skidmore, Owings, & Merrill, took the ideas and put them into
a Framework Plan to guide the desired outcome. T his w ill be the country's newest 15-minute City,
mean ing access to day-to-day needs within a 15-minute walk of the center of the site. Some of the
key connections to the existing trail systems and features include the River Deranged Trail that
connects the Jordan JQver Parkway over 1-1 5 into the regional trails and the Corner Canyon Trails,
and several parks and nature areas. The municipal plaiming standard is to have a park within a I 0-
minute walk of resid ents. The proposed plan provides a park and open space within a two-minute
walk of every resident and employee.

An area will be designated for an insti tutional anchor. They are working w ith the Utah System of
Higher Education to develop an innovation district with lab space, workspace, and research
opportunities to facilitate innovation. There will be a transpo1tation system within the proj ect with a
mix of residential, commercial, office, retaiI, and ente1tainment. Mr. Matheson presented the transit
system that will connect to the road system. He repo1ted that they are working with the Utah Transit
Authority ("UTA") and other transp01tation agencies to maximize ridership. They coined the term
'One-Car Conm1unity ' for this project, meaning that members of the community will need only one
vehicle due to the ease of getting around the site and the amenities available.

The Transit Plan will include an internal circulator that w ill be autonomous or electric and get people
within a short walk to all of the site amenities. T here are 38 spots where it is possible to connect from
roads to greenways on a bicycle and access the entire site by walking or biking without having to get
on a road. They are coordinating with the Utah Department of Transportation (" UDOT") and
Mountainland Association of Governments, and the Wasatch Front Regional Counci l (" WFRC") to
ensure that it can fit in within the broader transportation system. UDOT is working to develop one-
way fro ntage roads along 1-1 5. Mr. Matheson stated that they are looking at possible slip ramps to
provide ingress and egress from the site between major intersections.

Mr. Kartchner reported that the State provided a revi sion to the law in 2020 allowing the City to
extend the collection period for its EDA and RDA areas for an additional two years. In the case of
the Gateway RDA, the proceeds will likely all go to either the low-income housing unit in that facility
or the current developer. He explained that the developer paid off the bond and is still short the total
amount that was collected. There was a benefit to them that ultimately will benefit the City but it is
not necessarily a direct financial remuneration to the C ity.

The Jordan Narrows EDA a lso has an overall cap of $20 million that cannot be exceeded. He expected
that to be met before the collection tenn is done. The goal was to max that out to the total amount
that can be collected.

The Eastern Bluffdale EDA has been most impacted by COVID-1 9 but will benefit from an extended
co llection period. In that area, the hotel was being developed and they pulled back since they did not
know what was going to happen in terms of travel. Definite slowdowns had been seen as a result of
COVTD- 19. Once the extensions are passed, they will be filed with the appropriate state taxing
authorities and pick up an additional two years of collections.

Dave Kallas asked if it could be extended beyond two years with the increment to be used toward
Public Works. Mr. Kartchner explained that the General Fund benefi ts the expiration of the tax along
with the funds available to promote economic development. Each of the project areas has different
collection amounts that are paid to the City in the fonn of a Management Fee. Currently, the City 's
property taxes represent 14% of the total increment. The difference between the Management Fee
and the Property Taxes that the City would receive directly wi ll benefit the C ity. More Genera! Fund
revenue allows them to accomplish the primary focus of the City services. For a two-year delay, the
City would likely benefit overall by being able to promote additional businesses in the area that would
also raise the increment. Mr. Kartclrner assured the Council that they will work to make sure that
they are paying for the Public Works facility.
