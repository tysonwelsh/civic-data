The purpose of the factfinding work session was to receive a presentation regarding boundary changes to Municipal
Districts.

Boundary Changes to Municipal Districts

Every 10 years, after each census, the City Council is responsible for adopting new municipal district boundaries. New
municipal district boundaries must follow the new precinct boundaries—which were established by the county in December
202 1—and various minor changes will need to be made accordingly. This must be done within six months of the state establishing
legislative boundaries. The goal is to adopt new municipal district boundaries and voter participation areas (VPA) on March 15,
2022, but the final deadline to adopt new district boundaries is May 10, 2022.

Council Policy Analyst Watkins reported that on January 6, 2022, the Council office met with GIS Analyst Josh Jones
and Chief Deputy City Attorney Mark Stratford to review potential municipal district map options. During that meeting, Mr. Jones
used an interactive redistricting GIS application to show how municipal district boundaries could be adjusted to line up with
precinct boundaries. The primary consideration was to preserve a substantially equal population between each district. Three
different options were presented to the Council during a work session on February |, 2022, and a general consensus was reached
that two of the options should receive further consideration. He presented the two maps; the intent of map one was to adjust
municipal districts in such a way that they would most closely resemble current district boundaries. Some adjustments were
necessary to make up for larger precincts, but the general shape and location of each district was preserved. The intent of map two
was to show what districts would look like if they were to extend horizontally, from east to west. In December 2021 there was
some discussion among council members about the viability of spreading council districts in this arrangement. He reiterated that
May 10 is the deadline for adoption of the updated Municipal District Map.

Council member Choberka expressed her appreciation to Mr. Watkins and Mr. Jones for their responsiveness to the
feedback she has provided in representation of her constituents; some have communicated to her their concern that map two will
reduce representation for those living on the west side of the City. She asked that a quadrant map be created that would provide
such representation, but that map did not make it to final consideration by the Council.

Chair Nadolski then invited public input regarding the maps.

Kathy Darby stated that when she was running for a State Legislature seat, the district boundaries seemed odd to her; the
district that she was seeking to represent included so many different residents in many different areas that had nothing in common
whatsoever. She stated that the map that has been presented to the Council as option two is very similar in nature and she prefers
option one as it provides a like-grouping of residents.

Angel Castillo referenced the sport of golf, which provides for a handicap to be assigned to players who are less
experienced; this allows them to play with players who are more experienced. The handicap provides equity, but there is a
difference between equity and equal; equal means that everyone takes the same number of swings, but equity allows less
experienced individuals to participate. She referenced an email sent by Dr. Jenny Gnagey, Weber State University (WSU)
Professor and data scientist; the email discusses the fact that certain districts in the City participate more in elections. Harrison
Boulevard is a natural dividing line in the City based upon property values; those who live east of Harrison Boulevard tend to
have a better economic status. When one has their basic needs met, they have extra time to participate in civic work and events.
There are people in the community who do not have that time. When reviewing the voting records of the community those living

There were no additional persons appearing to be heard.

Council member White asked if efforts were made to ensure that current elected officials remain in their current district.
Mr. Watkins answered yes but noted that the Council will consider an ordinance under Agenda Item Four in tonight’s meeting that
addresses a situation where someone may be moved out of their district based upon the action taken on the district map.

Council member Hyer stated that he does not see a major problem with map option two; he does not feel the geographical
layout of the boundaries is too problematic. Council member Choberka stated that when considering the voter turnout related to
boundaries, she understands the comments made by those who spoke during the public comment period.

Chair Nadolski thanked those who took the time to participate tonight and noted no action will be taken during this
meeting.

The meeting adjourned at 5:25 p.m.
