Council Business

National Flood Insurance Program

             Mayor Kelly Bush stated this is a Federal Emergency Management Agency
(FEMA) program. The Council is required to adopt the resolution to ensure that FEMA will cover
any flood damage within the Kearns Metro Township area. If this is not adopted, then Kearns
Metro Township will not get any assistance. The Municipal Service District will administer this
program.

               Council Member Snow, seconded by Council Member Butterfield, moved to adopt
the following Resolution No. 20-05-01. The motion passed unanimously.

RESOLUTION NO. 20-05-01                                                      Date: May 20, 2020

         A RESOLUTION OF INTENT FROM THE KEARNS METRO TOWNSHIP
         REGARDING THE NATIONAL FLOOD INSURANCE PROGRAM

       WHEREAS, certain areas of Kearns Metro Township are subject to periodic flooding,
causing serious damages to properties within these areas; and

      WHEREAS, it is the intent of the Kearns Metro Township Council to require the recognition
and evaluation of flood hazards in all official actions relating to land use in areas having these
hazards; and

       WHEREAS, the Council has the legal authority to adopt land use and control measures to
reduce future flood losses pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 10-3-701.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, effectively immediately, that the Kearns Metro Township
Council:

1.      Assures the Federal Emergency Management Agency that it will enact as necessary, and
maintain in force, in those areas having flood hazards, adequate land use and control measures
with effective enforcement provisions consistent with the criteria set forth in Section 60 of the
National Flood Insurance Program Regulations; and

2.     Vests the Mayor with the responsibility, authority and means to:

       (a)    Assist the Administrator, at his/her request, in the delineation of the limits of the
area having special flood hazards.

        (b)   Provide such information concerning present uses and occupancy of the
floodplain, mudslide (i.e., mudflow) or flood-related erosion areas as the Administrator may
request.

         (c)      Maintain for public inspection and furnish upon request, for the determination of
applicable flood insurance risk premium rates within all areas having special flood hazards
identified on a Flood Hazard Boundary Map (FHBM) OR Flood Insurance Rate Map (FIRM), any
certificates of floodproofing, and information on the elevation (in relation to mean sea level) of the
level of the lowest floor (including basement) of all new construction or substantially improved
structures, and include whether or not such structures contain a basement and if the structure
has been floodproofed, the elevation (in relation to mean sea level) to which the structure was
floodproofed.

        (d)    Cooperate with Federal, State and local agencies and private firms which
undertake to study, survey, map and identify floodplain, mudslide (i.e., mudflow) or flood-related
erosion areas, and cooperate with neighboring communities with respect to management of
adjoining floodplain, mudslide, (i.e., mudflow) and/ or flood-related erosion areas in order to
prevent aggravation of existing hazards.

        (e)     Upon occurrence, notify the Administrator in writing whenever the boundaries of
the community have been modified by annexation or the community has otherwise assumed or
no longer has authority to adopt and enforce floodplain management regulations for a particular
area. In order that all FHBM’s and FIRM’s accurately represent the community’s boundaries,
include within such notification a copy of the map of the community suitable for reproduction,
clearly delineating the new corporate limits or new area for which the community has assumed or
relinquished floodplain management regulatory authority.

        (f)     Submit an annual report to the Administrator concerning the community’s
participation in the program, including, but not limited to the development and implementation of
floodplain management measures.

3.      Appoints Lupita McClenning, the Planning and Development Services Director for the
Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (designate agency or official) with the responsibility,
authority, and means to implement the commitments as outlined in this Resolution.

COVID-19 Virus Relief Fund

                 Mayor Kelly Bush stated this is on the agenda today to obtain permission from
the Council for her to sign a letter requesting CARE Act Fund money for Kearns Metro Township.
The money is a grant from the Federal Government and can only be used for specific items
relating to the COVID-19 virus. Kearns Metro Township has qualified for an amount up to $29,974
per household. The funds will be available in June and need to be spend down by October. The
Council will need to set aside a specific meeting to determine how these funds will be disbursed.

               Council Member Snow stated an application will be needed. The Council will
need to follow the federal guidelines relating to this fund.

              Council Member Snow, seconded by Council Member Butterfield, moved to grant
permission to Mayor Bush to sign any documents relating to this fund. The motion passed
unanimously.

Unified Police Department (UPD)

    UPD Budget

               Council Member Peterson stated during the monthly meeting with the UPD to be
held May 21, 2020, there will be a motion to adopt the 2021 tentative budget. This budget includes
a 2 percent pay increase for officers. He asked the Council if it would like him to vote in favor of
this increase.

               Mayor Bush stated she was in favor of the 2 percent pay increase. The budget
does not include any cost of living adjustment (COLA) for this year.

last year.

    Weighted Voting

                Council Member Peterson stated the Council has already unanimously agreed
that it did not like weighted voting. Two additional options relating to weighted voting will be
presented at the UPD Board meeting tomorrow night. He wanted to make sure the Council was
still in agreement about weighted voting.

                 Mayor Bush stated there are a lot of deeper issues with the UPD then just
weighted voting. Cities are leaving or threating to leave unless these issues are addressed. After
talking with Council Member Peterson, she thought it might be a good idea to tell the UPD Board
that Kearns Metro Township Council might be willing to come up with a compromise on weighted
voting but not until the bigger issues are fixed. She wanted to be very careful and make it very
clear that it did not support weighted voting, but these other areas needed to be addressed.

               Council Member Snow asked if the UPD Board had time to address these other
issues before the vote on the weighted voting.

                Council Member Peterson stated there will not be a vote tomorrow night. He
would just like to present a second option to the UPD Board. Also, a third option will be presented
tomorrow evening as well. He asked the Council if it was okay with them for him to present this
second option.

               Council Member Snow asked about the third option.

                Council Member Peterson stated the third option is broken down into
percentages with the money paid and the number of residents. Any entity over 5 percent would
get three votes, under 5 percent gets one vote. There are four entities that would only get one
vote, which include: White City Metro Township, Brighton, Copperton Metro Town, and Emigration
Canyon Metro Township. Salt Lake County would get three votes as well with Council Member
Bradley having two votes, and Deputy Mayor Litvack with one vote.

              Council Member Snow asked what option Council Member Peterson liked and if
the Council should discuss the third option to decide how it felt.

               Council Member Peterson stated he liked the second option the best, then the
third option. The first option would be his last choice. After the options are presented at the UPD
Board, he will bring them back to the Council for discussion and a vote.

              Council Member Bush stated what the Council needed to decide is if it is for
weighted voting or not. Kearns Metro Township is not gaining anything from it. However, she has
