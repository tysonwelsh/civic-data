       A.      CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE NO. 2021-0-16 EXPANDING
               FIREWORK RESTRICTIONS IN MIDVALE DUE TO EXISTING
               DROUGHT CONDITIONS
Matt Dahl said as a result of ongoing drought and the resulting dry conditions and fire
danger, Midvale City is considering the expansion of areas in the city where the use of
fireworks is prohibited. Utah State Code (State Code) limits the authority of cities to
prohibit fireworks, requiring that specific conditions and timelines be observed. Midvale
City staff and the Unified Fire Authority (UFA) are of the opinion that State Code grants
the Midvale City Council the authority to restrict fireworks at this time, based on a finding
that "existing conditions" merit the restriction of fireworks in designated areas.

The process for expanding firework restrictions is found in Subsections 202.5(b)(1) and
202.5(b)(2) of State Code and requires that the legislative body:

Midvale City currently has firework restrictions along the Jordan River and covering most
of the Jordan Bluffs neighborhood (i.e., Holden Street to the Jordan River, south of 7800
South). This designation was made several years ago based on historic conditions as
provided in State Code.

Based on the restrictions in State Code and existing conditions, Midvale City staff in
consultation with representatives of UFA propose that we expand the areas where
fireworks are prohibited as follows:

        700 West to the Jordan River: Staff proposes that we expand the current firework
        restrictions to 700 West. This would create a buffer around the Jordan River Trail.
        the Jordan Bluffs Neighborhood, and the Bingham Junction Linear Parks, which
        given current drought conditions meet the definition of hazardous areas.
        Additionally, staff feels that 700 West is the nearest identifiable feature that can be
        easily communicated as a boundary line for the public.

    a   1300 East: Staff proposes that we reinforce that 1300 East is being used as the
        western boundary for fireworks restrictions in the Salt Lake Valley. 1300 East is
        Midvale's eastern boundary, so maklng this designation will not expand restrictions
        in Midvale.

    a   All City-Owned Properties: Staff proposes that fireworks by prohibited in all Midvale
        City-owned facilities, building, parks, and open spaces, excluding rights-of-way not
        contained in the current and proposed fireworks restricted areas. Midvale City has
        the authority to determine how its property is utilized by the public, and therefore
        the prohibition on fireworks is not subject to Section 202.5(b) of state code.

The Midvale City Council must make its own determination of the areas where fireworks
will be restricted, and pass a resolution or ordinance enacting the restrictions per State
Code. Any action by the City Council enacting restrictions would need to make a finding
regarding the existing conditions and meet the requirements of 202.5(b).
