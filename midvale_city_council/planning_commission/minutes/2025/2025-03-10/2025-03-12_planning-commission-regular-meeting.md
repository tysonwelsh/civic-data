REGULAR MEETING

Chair Erickson called the Planning & Zoning Commission meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. She
explained how the meeting would proceed. First, the Planning Department would brief the
Commission; then the applicant would speak to the Commission; the item will be opened to the
public for their comments on public hearing agenda items; the Midvale Deputy City Attorney
will then brief the Commission on their role on the given item; and then the Commission would
deliberate and decide.

PUBLIC HEARINGS


1.     Vision Properties Utah, LLC requests Preliminary Subdivision approval of a 12-
       unit development located at 7838 S Holden St located in the Multifamily
       Residential-Medium to High Density (RM-25) Zone.

       The applicant requests Preliminary Subdivision approval for a 12-unit development on
       0.5 acres at 7838 S Holden St. The 12 units are currently under construction in 3, 4-unit
       buildings. The development was approved by an Administrative Conditional Use Permit
       and Site Plan application in May 2024 and met the development requirements of the RM-
       25 zone, Midvale Municipal Code 17-7-4. This proposal has been reviewed by all
       members of the Development Review Committee (Planning, Engineering, Public Works,
       Legal, Unified Fire Authority, and the Building Official) for compliance with the
       respective guidelines, policies, standards, and codes. Staff finds the proposal complies
       with the preliminary subdivision requirements in Midvale Municipal Code Chapter 16.02.

       Public notice has been sent to property owners within 500 feet of the subject parcel.
       Additionally, a sign was placed on the property and will remain until the Planning
       Commission hearing on 3/12/2025. At the time of writing this report, no public
       comments have been received.

       Based on compliance with the requirements of Chapter 16.02 of the Midvale Municipal
       Code demonstrated in the application, Staff recommends the Planning Commission
       approve the preliminary subdivision with the following findings:

       Findings:
              1. The application is for a preliminary subdivision to allow 12-units to be
                 subdivided at 7838 S Holden St.
              2. The application complies with the preliminary plat requirements outlined in
                 Midvale Municipal Code Chapter 16.02.
              3. The Development Review Committee has reviewed the application and
                 forwarded the item on for the Planning Commission to render a decision.

PUBLIC HEARING:

Kenneth Harper, a local resident of Holden Street, hopes to remain on his long-term family
property. He is concerned with the term “preliminary” approval being used for this meeting when
there is 80%-90% of the project is complete. The secondary concern is parking and public safety.
This issue became clear throughout construction during the past months. In such a small area,
twelve single-family units are going to overwhelm the existing parking. Mr. Harper’s third concern
is with the green-space requirement and the appearance that this project is not meeting zoning
standards. The fourth concern is the second-story windows and fire-safety standards for emergency
exits. His fifth concern is the location of the air-conditioning units for the project being extremely
near to his house. His sixth concern is the repair or reimbursement for the damage to current
residents’ properties done during construction. The seventh concern is the smell of chemicals and
waste coming from the outhouses at the construction site. Mr. Harper’s final concern is with the
dust coating the interior of his house and the rodent issue that has only occurred since construction
began.

Janelle L. lives across the street from the construction site. She does not understand how twelve
units can be approved for such a small lot. She has had to pay for car repairs for construction
supplies left in the road, ruining her car. She asks whether the city intends to build high-density all
around her property. She is concerned that the community will drastically change by bringing in
multi-family housing. Overcrowding and lack of parking are major concerns.

DISCUSSION:

Chair Erickson hears the concerns of the residents. She suggests that the process of new
development should be discussed.

Commissioner Tippetts explains that a Preliminary Subdivision means that the development has
been previously approved. The subdivision being discussed at this meeting is to allow the units to
be sold individually for homeownership opposed to rental units.

**Commissioner Candace Tippetts is excused from the meeting at 6:35 PM**

Commissioner Liedtke does state that the only item being discussed this meeting is the availability
to subdivide the lot per unit so that they can be purchased to encourage homeownership and not
rental units. This has nothing to do with the advertisements for apartments on Holden Street.

Wendelin Knobloch, Planning Director, clarifies that the development was approved in 2024.

Commissioner Liedtke states that any remaining vacant lots will not have plans for development
until after it was purchased, and a developer brings plans to Midvale City Staff for approval.

Mr. Knobloch explains the Zoning map tool available to the public on the city website. Anyone
can look at any property and the allowed zoning regulations. He states that zoning is regulated by
the City Council and any dissatisfaction with current zoning can be discussed with the Council
Member assigned to the area in question.

Jonathan Anderson, Planner II, describes the previous process to get to this point. This went
through an Administrative Conditional Use Permit. This occurred twice, once in 2021 and the
second time in 2024. Letters would have been sent out to the residents within 500-feet, once for
each of those comment periods as required for an Administrative Conditional Use Permit, which
would allow for public comment for input and concerns. The 0.5-acre lot does allow for the current
use as the allowed number of units for the RM-25 zone is 25 units per acre.

Commissioner Snow states that not all Commissioners are pushing for high-density. He explains
that this is an issue that would need to be taken to City Council as the Master Plan and legislation
must be adhered to. At this point, it is past the Commission’s control to alter zoning and change
the direction of this project.

Vice Chair Anderson discusses that the option to potentially change the current development plan
would have been in 2021 and 2024.

Chair Erickson appreciates the participation in the meeting even though the timing is not ideal for
the resident sharing their concerns. She advises residents to reach out to their Council
Representative for their district.

Commissioner Edwards mentions that this meeting can only control the individual sale or mass
rental of the project. There is nothing else the Commissioners can do to stop this process.

Daniel Van Beuge, Deputy City Attorney, states that this is an administrative process. If the
proposed subject meets all the ordinance requirements it must be approved.
