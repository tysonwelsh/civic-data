2.       A discussion regarding the future of parking policy. (21-028) (0:14:55)

Councilor David Harding presented. He highlighted the history of parking issues in Provo, which
has gone back many decades. Mr. Harding gave a detailed explanation on the progress made for
parking in the last several years. During his presentation, Mr. Harding referenced several
parking-related planning and visioning documents that the Council has approved, or which have
been created by the City regarding its parking management system.

Mr. Harding shared a draft policy statement which he invited the Council to consider:

        The Provo City Municipal Council is deeply concerned about public parking
        management in the City. We acknowledge and appreciate the positive steps that have
        been taken to implement the 2015 Strategic Parking Management Plan but feel that far
        more needs to be done, and with more urgency, to address the negative impacts that
        unmanaged and poorly-managed public parking have on the quality of life of our
        residents and the economic potential of our businesses. We invite the Administration to
        partner with us in addressing this important issue. We request that a full-time parking
        coordinator be hired as soon as is practical and provided with sufficient support to enable
        them to quickly implement the initial steps of the Strategic Parking Management Plan
        and the Vision and Policy for Downtown Parking adopted in October of 2019. The
        Council will provide support by prioritizing legislation and funding for this effort.

Mr. Shipley invited comments and questions from the Council. Their comments included:
    Councilor George Handley supported many elements of the Strategic Parking
       Management Plan. He was unsure whether or not this was something that required a full-
       time position. He felt that element of the draft policy statement merited more discussion
       and feedback from the Administration. Mr. Harding responded to these comments. He
       noted that the Council had previously approved a budget for a fulltime parking
       coordinator and perhaps it was still included in the city budget.
    Councilor Shannon Ellsworth was generally supportive of the intent of the policy
       statement. She would appreciate having more time to research and read the parking
       materials. She also noted that some of the bills currently considered by the Legislature
       may have impacts on Provo’s staffing and parking situation, so it may be helpful to wait a
       month or so until the legislative session was ended.
    Councilor Bill Fillmore spent time researching the parking issues; he is fully in favor of
       having a fulltime parking administrator. Given the history, he did not see a part-time staff
       member having sufficient resources to tackle the key issues.
    Council Chair David Sewell agreed that many parking problems have been going on for
       decades. From a business standpoint, if there is a big challenge, particularly an ongoing
       one, usually you want to assign a full-time employee to handle it, versus someone who
       can only divert part-time efforts to it. Mr. Sewell also referenced the book “Good to
       Great,” which advocates having the right people in the right positions to make significant
       progress. To accelerate progress, they need someone who has the proper expertise and is
       devoted to this problem fulltime. The long-term need for that is unclear, but he thought a
       lot needed to be done in the short-term. Mr. Sewell was also impressed with a recent

3.       A presentation on the Quarterly Report for the quarter ending 9-30-2020 for Fiscal
         2021. (21-007) (0:54:40)

John Borget, Administrative Services Director, summarized the quarterly report for the quarter
ending in September 2020. Mr. Borget highlighted details for the various revenue sources and

5.       A presentation on the Parks and Recreation Department Accreditation Process.
         (21-022) (1:23:21)

Scott Henderson, Parks and Recreation Director, introduced the presentation and process for
achieving accreditation. The department officially received recognition in October 2020 for their
accreditation. Alicia Christensen acted as the Accreditation Manager for the department and she
highlighted the benefits both to the department and the community. She reviewed the process
that the department underwent to receive their accredited status. It is a challenging process, with
annual reporting requirements once the status is awarded, plus the additional challenge of
completing the process virtually during a pandemic. Provo is one of 186 accredited agencies out
of the over 10,000 parks and recreation agencies nationally. Ms. Christensen invited questions
and comments from the Council. Council Vice-chair David Shipley congratulated the department
on this accomplishment. Council Chair David Sewell also congratulated Ms. Christensen and the
team. He asked whether they have learned new standards and skills to help them operate at a
higher level. Ms. Christensen and Mr. Henderson agreed that the requirements of the
accreditation process will continue to improve their department with the new standards and
procedures they have implemented. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked what has made success
happen for them in their department. Mr. Henderson noted that the process highlighted for him
the talent that exists in their employees throughout the department. Presentation only. This item
was scheduled for a presentation at the Council Meeting on February 2, 2021.

7.       A discussion regarding the Administration’s proposal to support Council policy
         priorities. (21-027) (2:03:32)

Mayor Michelle Kaufusi introduced this idea of identifying items or topics on which the
Administration could present to the Council in a deep dive format. The Mayor’s Office had noted
that the Council’s main tool seemed to be Council committees; they sought to provide another
tool the Council could utilize to be more effective and this seemed like a way of working on
issues pertinent to the whole Council. Isaac Paxman, Deputy Mayor, and Wayne Parker, CAO,
shared additional insight or suggestions about the proposal.

Councilors shared comments and questions. Councilor George Handley felt this was a promising
possibility, though he wished to keep some flexibility for the Council to be responsive and
address issues as they arose. Mr. Handley noted, too, that City staff members had enormous
expertise but may at times feel deferential to the Council or underutilized. He thought this tool
could offer opportunities for productive collaboration. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth expressed
her hope that City staff at all levels would be willing to share ideas with the Council as part of
their policy discussions. Councilor David Harding noted times in the past when staff have
brought solutions to the Council’s attention and the Council has had great experiences with this
in the past. Council Chair David Sewell suggested the Council discuss topics at the next meeting
and further explore the idea in the meantime. Councilor Bill Fillmore also thought this was a
wonderful idea for collaboration. Presentation only.

10.      An ordinance amending Provo City Code Chapter 6.32. Citywide Application. (21-
         025) (2:42:58)

Javin Weaver, Planner, presented and highlighted the changes to city code which would align the
code with recent changes to state statute. Jessy Antonino, Business Licensing specialist, shared
details about the regulations pertaining to food trucks and mobile food courts. Mr. Weaver
reviewed the specifics of the proposal. Mr. Weaver and Ms. Antonino responded to questions
from Councilors regarding the changes. Councilor David Harding asked for clarification on the
buffer requirement; Mr. Weaver clarified the language and how this complied with the state
requirements. Council Chair David Sewell asked for clarification on the signage changes; Mr.
Weaver indicated that the code was simplified so it did not restrict or require certain elements on
signage. Signs were still limited to a specific size and needed to be clear of obstructing paths.
Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked about the permitting for private events and the numerical
threshold which constituted a mobile food court. Mr. Weaver and Ms. Antonino shared more
insight into the permitting requirements and treatment of the issue, which was largely dependent
on whether public property/the street was being utilized or not. Presentation only. This item was
already scheduled for the Council Meeting on February 2, 2021.
