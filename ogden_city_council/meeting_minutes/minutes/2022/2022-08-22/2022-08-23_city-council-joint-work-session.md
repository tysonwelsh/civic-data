Agenda Review

Redevelopment Agency — Draft Developer Selection Policy

The Board will review and discuss a draft policy establishing procedures for the selection of developers and professional
services for the Redevelopment Agency (RDA). Board members requested this policy in 2021. The Board and administrative staff
have worked with the City Attorney to draft a policy that balances the interests of the Board with providing flexibility for the
administrative staff and honoring the role and responsibility of the executive director. Input from Board members will be noted,
and any appropriate changes made prior to adoption of the policy.

Council Executive Director Eller-Smith referenced materials sent to the entire Board that provides historical information
regarding the make-up and intent of the RDA and past decisions that have been made by elected officials that may have influenced
more recent decisions made.

Community and Economic Development Director Cooper used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss
Administration’s draft Developer Selection Policy. He read the following preamble for the record of the meeting:

There seems to be an unfounded perception in the community that the Agency does not have a formal,
organized process for selecting projects or any objective criteria for screening selected projects or developers.
Instead, some believe the Agency's decisions are largely political or, in many cases, arbitrary or haphazard.
While tonight's ongoing review and discussion of Agency projects represents a decidedly limited sample,
nevertheless, it is strong evidence this perception of politically motivated or mismanaged project selection is, at
the least, overstated, and at the most, in and of itself politically motivated, inflammatory, and unproductive.
Nonetheless, the Agency's lack of published procedures for identifying, vetting and selecting projects can
undoubtedly leave the Agency exposed to such contentions. More importantly, the Agency’s limited
communication regarding the relationship between selected projects/developers and identified goals may
underscore the public uncertainty. Thus, the community lacks the information needed to understand the
Agency’s basis for selecting one project over another. Therefore, tonight’s discussion seeks to further this
conversation and establish implementable measures, now and in the future, to bring resolution to this
unfounded perception and effectively streamline Agency operations.”

Mr. Cooper then noted topics of his presentation include RDA history, purpose/authority, and activities; identified best
practices; current proposed changes; and future proposed changes. Relative to history, the RDA law was first approved in Utah in
1979 as the “Utah Neighborhood Development Act” to address concerns over urban decay. Significant changes have been made to

that Act in 1983, 1991, 2006, and 2016. Tax increment finance (TIF) if the primary funding tool and is typically paid out as a
post-performance incentive. The use of eminent domain and other policies differs from that of a municipality. The Ogden RDA is
a separate political entity but is mostly governed by the City. The purpose of Ogden’s RDA is to contribute to the responsible and
sustainable growth of Ogden City by elevating the quality of life in all neighborhoods through the facilitation of a wide range of
housing, employment, and wealth creation opportunities. He then presented a pie chart containing development activities and
projects since 2010; 51 projects have been completed and the selection of developers for those projects can be broken down as
follows:

Direct negotiation — 29 percent.

Unsolicited proposal — 47 percent.

Request for proposal (RFP) — 20 percent.

Other — 4 percent.

For RDA specific properties, 43 percent were completed through direct negotiation, 33 percent were unsolicited
proposals, and 24 percent were through the RFP process. He then discussed project contributions from the RDA, including land
incentives, TIF incentive, sale of property to developers through real estate purchase contracts (REPC), land/TIF incentives, bond
incentives, cash incentives, and no contribution. He cited a few projects that fall into each of the provided categories, after which
he discussed identified best practices for developer selection:

e Project selection and prioritization.

e Public involvement and communications.
e Agency leadership, staffing, and training.
e Sound management practices.

Mr. Cooper then expounded on each of these practices and assigned the Agency a grade for their performance in each; at
this point in the presentation, there was discussion among the Board and Mr. Cooper regarding specific projects details;
opportunities for public involvement in the developer selection process; the earliest point in the developer selection process where
RDA involvement would be most appropriate; and staffing levels in Ogden compared with staffing levels in other cities. He then
discussed currently proposed changes to the developer selection process; staff suggests creating an annual notice of interest to the
development community; this notice could be sent to the developers that are included in the City’s developer database, which will
include company information, their financial capacity, qualifications, and past processes. Additionally, a notice of exclusive
negotiation agreement would be given within 30 days of the selection process; this would entail public solicitation, direct
negotiation using the established database, and unsolicited proposals. The Board, Board staff, and Mr. Cooper discussed the idea
of a notice of exclusive negotiation agreement; this would give the Board and Council advanced notice of ongoing negotiations to
prevent situations from arising where the Boad does not hear information about a project until it has reached a point of formal
recommendation to the Board and Council. City Attorney Williams stated that he would only recommend that these process
changes apply to specific redevelopment/development projects. If the Board is considering hiring professionals, such as real estate
agents or brokers, that process should be treated just like a City solicitation of proposals for such services. He stated it is his
opinion that there is no reason to provide different processes for the City and RDA for service selection processes. Mr. Cooper
agreed and stated that change can be made in the next version of the draft. He then discussed future proposed changes:

e Project selection and prioritization.
o Formal adoption of Agency Mission/Objectives.
e Public involvement and communication.
o Specific project communication plans.
o Enhanced public education.
o Create forum for engagement.
e Agency leadership, staffing, training.
o Improve open lines of communication between staff/Board.
e Sound management practices.
o Improve data collection and access.
o Centralize records and project tracking.
© Improve interdepartmental coordination and communication.

Ms. Eller-Smith then presented a mock-up of a website that could be used to advertise development opportunities;
developers could visit the website to provide their information to be included in the database and the developer selection policy
could be contained on the site as well.

Mr. Cooper then discussed the ‘why’ behind the RDA; Ogden is the gateway to Hill Air Force Base and small business
defense innovation; Ogden provides access to outdoor recreation, a vibrant downtown, a mountain-to-metro experience, quality of
place, a growing technology base, and plays a central role in the defense ecosystem.

Discussion regarding the developer selection process included topics such as harmony between the General Plan and
Community Plans and the manner in which those documents should influence various development/redevelopment projects; the
role of the Planning Commission in the developer selection process; additional areas for improvement that were not discussed
during the presentation; lessons learned from past projects; the need for the Board to receive a status update on ongoing projects;

Council/Board Business

Welcome to Ogden Sign: Council member White stated she was contacted by a resident about the aging ‘welcome to
Ogden’ sign on Harrison Boulevard; she asked if there are plans to replace or revitalize the sign. Chief Administrative Officer
Johnson stated that the City has had discussions in the past with Weber State University (WSU) about replacing the sign with a
joint ‘welcome to Ogden/welcome to WSU’ sign, but those discussions stalled. It may be time to revisit the issue, but the City
does not own the land where the existing sign is located.

Trail System Markers: Council member White then stated there was a suggestion in the past about placing markers on
the trail systems to notify people of how far they have walked. She wondered if that would be beneficial at this time. Mr. Johnson
stated that such an amenity may not be needed given the broad use of personal fitness trackers.

Fiber Infrastructure: Council member White then asked for an update on the status of efforts to bring fiber
infrastructure to the community. Mr. Johnson stated that the City is in discussions with a few different vendors about bringing
fiber infrastructure to the City, but it could take a while for such a project to build out. He suggested that the Council hear from the
City’s Information Technologies (IT) Division about the project. Council member White suggested that the City speak with other
communities that have completed fiber projects to understand the lessons they have learned. There was a brief discussion among
the Council and City Administration regarding the differences between the business models of several fiber companies and service
providers, with Mr. Johnson noting the goal of the City is to partner with a company that can provide high speeds and
affordability.

Sustainability Position: Council member White then asked when Administration plans to hire a sustainability employee.
Mr. Johnson stated he hopes the position will be filled soon. Council member White stated she would like for the City’s website to
include a dashboard that contains information about air quality and water usage; many people are saving water, but there is no
indication of how those efforts are impacting the City’s water resources. Mr. Johnson stated that is correct; the water levels at
Pineview Reservoir are higher than expected and Administration is looking for a way to say thank you to residents who have
sacrificed and conserved water.

Open Space at Taylor Canyon School Site: Council member White then stated the Taylor Canyon School is going to be
torn down and she would like to reach out to the school about their plans for landscaping the open space they plan to preserve. She
stated that open space does not necessarily need to be green space that requires water. Mr. Johnson stated that the media article
that he read was surprising; there are many different optional uses of the property, such as housing in that area of the community,
that would be beneficial.

Water Conservation Program: Council member Hyer stated that he visited the Water Savers website to try to
understand the experience that residents have when they attempt to make an application for rebates. Before completing the
application, the website indicates the amount of funding available. He asked how often the City’s funding source will be
replenished. Mr. Johnson stated that it is the City’s intent to replenish the funding as needed, but he cannot speak for Weber Basin
Water Conservancy. He briefly discussed some of the complaints and feedback the City has received regarding the program thus
far, namely complaints about funding not being available to private communities.

Initiative Tracker: Ms. Eller-Smith reviewed the Council initiative tracker and engaged in discussion with the Council
and Administration regarding the status of various projects in the City; there was a focus on efforts to fill vacant employment
positions in the City that are needed to respond to some of the initiatives included on the list.

Lester Park: Acting Chair Lopez asked Administration to address the recent media article regarding Lester Park. Mr.
Johnson stated this is a heavy topic of discussion among City leadership; there is an increased homeless population in that park,
and they are very angry and making the park unsafe for residents to use. Various Departments of the City are working to address
the problem and to develop a plan to accomplish long term improvements. Acting Chair Lopez agreed a strategic response to the
problem is needed.

Council Calendar: Ms. Eller-Smith briefly reviewed the Council calendar for the next several weeks.

The meeting adjourned at 5:50 p.m.
