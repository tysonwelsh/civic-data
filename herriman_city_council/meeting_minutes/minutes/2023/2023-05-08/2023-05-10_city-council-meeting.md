       2. Administrative Reports
          2.1. Discussion Regarding Changes to the City Council Rules of Procedure – Nathan
               Cherpeski, City Manager
   City Manager Nathan Cherpeski recalled the desire to look at how public comment was handled
   and how to provide the means for Council to ask questions, if deemed necessary. Additionally,
   staff proposed language do differentiate abstentions and recusals. Councilmember Steven
   Shields recommended striking the word “financial” from the policy. City Attorney recited state
   code specifying there needed to be a financial conflict of interest for a Councilmember to recuse
   themselves from a vote. The Council discussed perceived conflicts of interest. Council
   consensus determined to maintain the “financial” component stated in the policy.

   The Council further discussed the public comment policy and the difference between public
   comment and public hearing agenda items. The Council concluded it would be up to the
   discretion of the chair to determine if there was a need to request additional information from
   staff and ask clarifying questions of the citizen. This change would be for both public hearings
   and the public comment portions of the meeting.

          2.2. (Continued from May 4, 2023 meeting) FY 2023-2024 Budget Discussion – Kyle
               Maurer, Finance Director
   City Manager Cherpeski reminded the Council of the data received at the previous meeting as
   he turned the time over to Finance Director Kyle Maurer. Director Maurer indicated he reworked
   the numbers and found a $1 million dollar deficit for the City and a $500K deficit for the police.
   City Manager Cherpeski stated staff was looking to plan and commit to a certain funding level.
   He looked to integrate the master plans with the Capital Improvement Plan and the budget to be
   able to balance available resources. New development could change when projects are
   conducted and reimbursement agreement also cause hurdles.

   Councilmember Shields indicated those decisions needed to be applied and dial down on
   inconsistencies. He indicated he was not taking any solutions regarding the deficit off the table.
   Councilmember Henderson indicated there were fundamental problems with the budget which
   ultimately affects everything else. He stated raising taxes will fix it for the next couple of years.
   He suggested misinformation had been given and wanted to know if the problems had been fixed.
   Councilmember Henderson stated inflation was a part of the problem; however, it wasn’t the

           2.3. Discussion of Community Based Programming in the Old City Hall – Wendy
                Thomas, Assistant City Manager
   Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas talked about the satellite facility for the Salt Lake
   Community College (SLCC) programming being conducted out of the Community Center. Over
   the course of the past year, Herriman City staff have started to utilize the office areas of the
   building which had been vacated by SLCC due to space limitations at City Hall. There were
   several community-based services and programs which could be offered at the Community
   Center including independent aging programs, youth and teen programs, art programs and
   activities, enrichment programs and outdoor recreation programs.

   She offered an overview of the potential one-time costs for furniture, supplies, kitchen remodel,
   concrete repair, and asphalt repairs needed that were associated with utilizing the space as part
   of the services and programs which totaled approximately $173,000. Assistant City Manager
   Thomas explained staff had planned on using a federal grant to replace the air handler units
   which ultimately could save on electricity costs. She also highlighted the possibility that some of
   the repairs and maintenance could be funded by the Community Development Block Grant.

   Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn asked about current funding allocated for aging services. Assistant
   City Manager Thomas explained Herriman City is currently paying $15,000 annually for
   transportation services to shuttle senior citizens to Riverton. Councilmember Jared Henderson
   relayed his preference to surplus the asset.

   Operations Director Monte Johnson observed the downside to maintaining the building to which
   Councilmember Henderson stated it would not be the best option for the City to utilize and
   maintain. Mayor Palmer acknowledged the opportunity to bring an amenity to the community
   and suggested further conversations should commence with the County to see if there would be
   an interest in initiating a partnership. Councilmember Shields recognized the concerning need
   for administrative space but didn’t know what the progression would entail. If the plan presented
   could help serve needs in the community and there could be financial assistance associated with
   it, he could find himself able to support the direction; however, he didn’t feel it would be
   appropriate to start providing services that were being conducted by other entities. He wanted
   to fully understand the outcome prior to deciding. Councilmember Henderson asked how much
   of the operation and maintenance could be offset by programming.

   Assistant City Manager Thomas expressed the desire to move the Events Department into the
   space. Councilmember Henderson asked how much space would be left for programming if the
   City started utilizing the office space. He also requested information relating to potential cost
   sharing opportunities. Assistant City Manager Thomas indicated this would be discussed at a
   future meeting.

          10.3. Discussion and consideration of a request by John Lindsley (authorized agent) to
                amend the Official Herriman City Zoning Map for ±545 acres located generally
                at 4461 W Juniper Crest Road and 15750 S Mountain View Corridor Highway in
                the FR-2.5 Forestry Recreation Zone to apply the Large Project Master Planned
                Community (LPMPC) Zone for the development of a residential planned
                community known as Laguna. (File No. M2022-049) – Michael Maloy, City Planner
   City Planner Maloy indicated he would be presenting agenda items 10.3 and 10.4 together;
   however, would require separate motions for the proposals. He oriented the Council as to the
   location of the project and the proposed Master Development Agreement component of the
   project. Together, the project has been called “The Preserve” which included 815 acres and 1,285
   dwelling units. The proposal included design guidelines, amenities, main features, and elements.
   City Planner Maloy indicated the proposal was not consistent with the General Plan, there were
May 10, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                                    Page 10 of 13


   adverse effects of the adjacent property as it was not a compatible use, and there were not
   adequate facilities and services in the area needed for the development of the property which
   created an increased financial burden for the City.

   City Planner Maloy relayed the Planning Commission’s recommendation to deny both projects
   for the same reasonings outlined. He asked if there were any questions. There were none.

   Representing the applicant, Mr. Reed Dixon indicated this project had a long history and
   indicated they had been in continued negotiations with the City for a Planned Unit Development
   (PUD). He offered an extensive background of how the property was incorporated into Herriman
   City in 2007. Mr. Dixon indicated the agricultural or forestry recreation zones placed on the
   property was considered a “holding zone,” similarly to the newly created LMPC zone adoption.
   Mr. Dixon highlighted the ordinance was altered to allow this property to be considered in the
   newly created zone.

   Mr. Dixon indicated the previous year during conversations with the City, he was positive the
   project would move forward; however, the demeanor of the City had changed since that time.
   Placing the LMPC zone on the property does not entitle the property to anything and encouraged
   the Council to start and document the development process for this property. Mr. Dixon
   observed the development partner did not turn down any request suggested or recommended
   by the City Council.

   Mr. Dixon highlighted the various meetings held with Camp Williams officials and outlined eight
   specific items they wanted addressed including, but not limited to, lower residential dwelling
   units, cluster elsewhere, create berms, and provide fire and recreational buffers. He recalled
   yearly communications happening with the Camp, and then abruptly, Camp Williams Officials
   were supposedly asked to stop communications with the developer.

   Mr. Dixon acknowledged the City was entertaining the Public Infrastructure District mechanism
   to alleviate any obligation to the City to construct the necessary infrastructure for the
   development. The project would be self-sustaining.

   Councilmember Shields asked about the previous discussions regarding the consideration of the
   LMPC zone. He indicated it would need to be conducted in conjunction with a Master
   Development Agreement and suggested changing the zoning without a plan in place would be a
   premature decision. He asked if the request was denied, would if affect the developer’s ability to
   reapply. City Planner Maloy indicated the developer would need to wait a year to file the same
   application for consideration. He indicated if there were significant changes, it could be
   considered as a different proposal.

   City Planner Maloy indicated this application was being treated as a zoning amendment. He
   informed the Council, if they denied the applicant, the request could be subject to arbitration. He
May 10, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                                Page 11 of 13


   acknowledged the LMPC language outlined standards of review for request, not a fully mature
   proposal.

   Councilmember Henderson indicated he did not see any changes since the last time it had been
   presented to the Council. He recalled issues raised and there didn’t seem to have any of those
   concerns mitigated and no fundamental changes were conducted. He stated he didn’t have any
   additional feedback to offer as his concerns are still not answered. Councilmember Henderson
   relayed his desire to not want to rehash the conversation that happened a year ago.

   Councilmember Shields indicated there were elements of the design were appealing and noted
