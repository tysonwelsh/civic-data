Discuss Park City’s Water Supply and Potential Continuation of Leasing Surplus
Water to Weber Basin through Provisions in the Western Summit County Project
Master Agreement:
Clint McAffee, Public Utilities Manager, presented this item and displayed a chart of the
City’s water supply. He stated 45% of the water came from abandoned mine tunnels.
Another third of the supply came from the intake of Rockport Reservoir. When 3Kiings
Water Treatment Plant (3KWTP) was finished, all the water sources would be treated.
He noted the untreated water would irrigate outside, serve the golf course, and remain
in the streams.

Council Member Toly asked why the water demand went up and then was projected to
go down in 2027. McAffee stated the City had a five-year contract to lease water to
Weber Basin so the 2027 numbers did not reflect a contract renewal at this point.
McAffee indicated July and August were the highest demands on water, and sometimes
winter usage was high depending on snow making.

McAffee referred to the master agreement and indicated it was a cooperative
agreement between the water suppliers to jointly monitor and track the water supply and
deficiencies. In the short-term, they wanted all the systems to be interconnected to use
the existing surplus among all the districts. The long-term water supply provision was
that Weber Basin would construct the next water development project. The lease had a
10-year horizon. Each entity listed their surplus water. The first five years of the lease
was binding. If there was a deficiency, it was perpetual. Park City currently had a
surplus. If the surplus diminished, the City could back out of the contract.

Council Member Doilney asked if the City increased the amount of water it provided, to
which McAffee stated the City was able to choose how much surplus to lease. He noted
the contract was modified to include a provision regarding drought or a disaster. In
those cases, the City could reduce the surplus amount it would lease.

Council Member Gerber asked if Weber Basin produced water or just leased Park City’s
water. McAffee stated they produced water wholesale. Council Member Gerber asked if
the City was the only entity in the cooperative that had surplus water. McAffee stated
each entity had their own sources, but there wasn’t enough water to meet their
demands. McAffee reviewed that leasing water was a significant revenue source for the
City and indicated it accounted for 25% of the water revenue. The Council was in favor
of continuing to lease water.

program. She indicated this item went to the Historic Preservation Board (HPB) and the

Empire Pass Master Owner Association (EPMOA) Mining Structure Historic
Preservation Update:
Caitlyn Tubbs, Planner, and Doug Ogilvie, representing EPMOA, presented this item.
Tubbs stated this was an informational presentation to fulfill requirements by the
EPMOA agreement. She explained the City and EPMOA updated the MOA and each
entity was required to provide $40,000 each year for 10 years to preserve the mining
structures on Flagstaff.

Tubbs reviewed recent preservation projects. The Daly West Headframe was restored
and moved 20 yards from its original location. The nearby hydrant shacks were also
restored. The King Con Ore Bin partially collapsed and was restored. Upcoming
projects included revegetation and signage. She noted there were other structures not
on the property that needed restoration and she asked for Council feedback on
restoring those.

Ogilvie stated the Kings Con Ore Bin project was a Friends of Ski Mining project. The
EPMOA had almost finished the Daly West Headframe and he indicated the budget was
overspent. The way the MOU was written, the EPMOA was able to advance money to
get the project completed, so the funding committed to date would take them through
2024. If both entities advanced funding three years, they could complete the renovation
projects. He noted there were other structures not on the Flagstaff annexation area, but
they were important structures.

Military Installation Development Authority (MIDA) Presentation by Paul Morris,
MIDA Executive Director:
Paul Morris indicated MIDA was a state authority and an economic development entity
with a military focus. He reviewed the purpose of MIDA was to create project areas to
facilitate land development to support the U.S. military mission in Utah, issue bonds,
provide municipal services, distribute legislative appropriations, levy taxes to support
the project area development, assist in long-range planning and serve as trustee for the
Air Force payment-in-kind account. He displayed a slide showing the economic impact
that the military had on Utah. He showed the Falcon Hill project that spanned various
cities in Northern Utah, and noted 4,000 jobs were created as a result of that project.

Morris discussed the current project in Wasatch County. He reviewed the Air Force
owned property on Red Maple, but Park City didn’t want them to build a hotel there. So
MIDA transferred their ownership to Wasatch County. Dave Williamsen, MIDA, gave the
history of making the resort work and stated it took a minimum of 175 rooms to pay for
the resort oversight and management. Heather Kruse, MIDA, displayed the area
established by Wasatch County for recreation and the area of Hideout. She stated the
parcels within the boundaries had to have the landowners’ consent to construct the
project. This took time to achieve. Some of the project achievements included the
Jordanelle Parkway. Also, 500 parking spaces were completed at the resort. They had
140 new residences and 815 new subdivision lots. They would also create 5,000-7,500
new jobs. They estimated tourists would spend $4 million per year. She indicated the
Mayflower Hotel would have 387 rooms and part of those would be reserved for military
personnel only. There was conference space as well. There would be a skier services
complex that would include hotels, condos, and skier services, such as a ski school and
retail outlets. Things they knew they needed in order to make the project viable was
transit and affordable workforce housing. She was grateful for the roll out of transit in
Wasatch County. There were four buildings in the project designated for short-term and
long-term rentals. This was the first phase and more affordable housing was planned in
the future.

The applicant has committed to using water eﬃcient low ﬂow ﬁxtures
and Energy Star rated appliances in all units; designing the building
envelopes to be energy eﬃcient; using water wise native landscaping;
and providing recycling locations throughout the site. The applicant is
encouraging multimodal transportation choices by offering alternative
modes of transportation on site including locating a bus station, e-bike
share, connection to the rail trail, and providing covered bike storage in all
buildings. In addition the applicant is proposing to reduce their overall
parking to prevent excess paving and increase open space. All lighting
will meet the City’s Dark Sky Ordinance LMC § 15-5-5(J).
