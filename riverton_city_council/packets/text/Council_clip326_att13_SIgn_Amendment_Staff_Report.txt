                 TYPES FOR NONRESIDENTIAL SIGNS IN RESIDENTIAL ZONES, AND 18.150.110(2)
                 SIGN STANDARDS, AMENDMENT PROPOSED BY RIVERTON CITY



The Planning Commission, following a public hearing, voted to recommend approval of this
ordinance amendment. Minutes from that meeting are attached below. The Planning Commission
recommended the following motion:

I move the City Council ADOPT Ordinance #20-01 , amending Section 18.150.110 (1) and 18.150.110(2)
allowing temporary promotional signs and LED signs residential zones for non-residential uses and
amending existing standards for size, as described in Exhibit “A”.

Background

A representative of the Saint Andrews Catholic Church on 11800 South and 3600 West approached staff
with questions regarding available signage for that location, specifically temporary banners and an LED
monument sign. The RR-22 zone does not allow temporary promotional signs, and while it does allow
monuments signs, LED, or electronic message boards, are not allowed. In discussing the issue, staff felt
there was sufficient merit to the question of allowing these signs for non-residential uses to present the
issue to the City.

The included text shows the relevant section 18.150.110 (1), which a table is showing various sign types
and the zones in which they are allowed. The proposed text would allow as a conditional use monument
signs for non-residential uses in all residential zones, and allow LED or electronic message boards as part
of the monument signage. The sign ordinance currently restricts LED components to no more that 50% of
the sign display area, and that restriction would remain in place. Additional space allowance is included for
civic signs on public property. Temporary signs would be permitted in all residential zones for non-
residential uses. On the attached exhibit, elements in red are new or modified text.

The Planning Commission recommended approval of this text amendment.

6.     ZONING ORDINANCE TEXT AMENDMENT, RIVERTON CITY IS PROPOSING
       AMENDMENTS    TO   SECTION    18.150.110 ALLOWING    TEMPORARY
       PROMOTIONAL BANNERS AND LED SIGNS AS A CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT
       FOR NON-RESIDENTIAL USES IN RESIDENTIAL ZONES WITH FRONTAGE ON
       A MAJOR ROADWAY.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the request is for a proposed change
to the City’s Sign Ordinance, specifically to the table that shows the allowance of signage.
Staff was approached by the St. Andrews Catholic Diocese who proposed possible signage
on the site. The property is zoned RR-22 and a church is an allowed use within the zone.
The current table does not allow promotional banners or LED components in the RR-22
zone. It was noted that monument signs are allowed.

In discussing the matter with the applicants, they looked at the circumstances under which
they would be used. They concluded that allowing an electronic message board component
would not be out of character. Promotional banners were considered for promoting events
in the context of the non-residential uses allowed in the zone. Mr. Prestwich reported that
the proposed ordinance would allow temporary banners as a use within residential zones
and LED or message boards as a conditional use for non- residential uses in residential
zones. The conditional use would be an opportunity to look at the visual impact and the
proximity to other types of signs. The table was displayed, which shows the proposed
changes.

Commissioner James asked if the size requirements remain the same. Mr. Prestwich stated
that they would and clarified that LED signs are not allowed to be freestanding but are
allowed as a component to a monument sign but cannot take up more than 50% of sign. In
response to a question raised, Mr. Prestwich explained that LED refers to an electronic
message text board and video type boards.

Commissioner Endrizzi asked how ‘temporary’ is defined. Mr. Prestwich explained that the
ordinance defines temporary as a 14-day display period. There is specific language
addressing how long and how many times per year temporary signs are allowed. There are
also holiday exceptions where they are allowed without a permit. Mr. Prestwich confirmed
that it is well defined in the ordinance and is the same standard that applies to commercial
properties. A permit would be required and there are location limitations. It was noted that
four, 14-day periods are allowed per year.

Chair Russell opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public
hearing was closed.

Mr. Prestwich described the reasoning for the request and stated that typically ordinance
changes are not made for one user, however, they felt that the question raised was
legitimate as to why these types of signs are not allowed for non-residential uses such as
schools. It was noted that more specificity could be added in terms of narrowing the focus.

Commissioner Hartley moved to recommend APPROVAL of the proposed text
amendment to Section 18.150.110 (1) making temporary promotional signs and LED
signs conditional uses in residential zones for non-residential uses, as described in
Exhibit “A”. Commissioner James seconded the motion. The motion passed with
the unanimous consent of Commission.
