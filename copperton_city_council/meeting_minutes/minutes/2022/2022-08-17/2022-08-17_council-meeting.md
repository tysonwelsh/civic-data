Citizen Comments

              Joel Brienholt stated he was concerned about the storm drains being clogged.
This was reported by many County staff in response to the weekend’s storm.

                Council Member Bailey stated Public Works cleaned out the storm drains weeks
prior, which lessened the need.

                Mayor Clayton stated Public Works was out and waiting with the vacuum truck.
Saturday, they cleaned all the dirt and debris from the road, and they also cleaned all the drains,
even those that had been cleaned previously. Some of the residents did not move their cars,
which impeded the cleanup. A storm drain study has been commissioned for September to help
prioritize projects. Copperton received $750 thousand for infrastructure programs, and this did

              Brian Denton stated Copperton had a similar storm in 2019. He lives on Park
Street and when that street floods, the water goes into his house. He questioned what the study

Unified Fire Authority (UFA)

                Ed Walden, UFA, stated UFA recently lost one of their recently retired members
to a motorcycle accident and the funeral will be this Saturday. Having completed the initial testing

to work because there is a national shortage of paramedics. They received a case of smoke
detectors that are available for residents. Construction on stations is moving forward and they
will soon start seismic upgrades on the Copperton station.

Recent Weather Impacts

                Mayor Clayton stated he recently met with Chief Clint Mecham and Jim
Woodward’s counterpart. They are doing a beta test on the app that enables residents to report
damage to their property. They recommend that Copperton survey the residents. Some key
questions are: Is the damage water or mud related; Is it over 16 inches (high enough to reach
outlets); Is the residence habitable; Is there any visible structural damage; Does it reach FEMA
levels (400 minimum units affected). There are other levels, though, which is important to
understand. For example, there is an SBA level that would allow residents to get a low interest
loans. Homeowners’ insurance does not cover flooding. Flood insurance is usually paid annually.
It is expensive, and the deductibles are high. Copperton has flood zones and people who live in
those zones tend to pay even more. He handed out a map of the flood zone to members of the
Council. He asked everyone to report issues so they can contact appropriate people to do the
work. He felt the contractors did a great job with the resources they had.

                Ryan Perry, Rio Tinto, reported on the rain events over the weekend. It was an
incredible experience to see a town come together at a moment’s notice to address the rain
events. He is happy it was not worse than it could have been. They did an analysis and had
additional crews attend. They have provided the Alders with some temporary housing and have
tried to take care of their immediate needs. They have hired a cleanup company to help with this.

                Brendan Murphy, Rio Tinto General Manager for Minerals, stated Rio Tinto had
some equipment the following day and built some berms to reduce further risk. The think they
have completed the initial work. The next step is to gather information. They have set up an
investigation team. They use essential factors for investigation and are hoping to have more
information next Friday and report the following week.

               Mayor Clayton stated it will be important during the information gathering phase
to refer anyone who has input to the Council. People should reach out to their neighbors.

Oquirrh View Blvd Update

                 Council Member Stitzer stated she was contacted by the Greater Salt Lake
Municipal Services District (MSD) to get feedback for the boulevard. She emailed everyone on
the Council for feedback and only heard from one person. Subsequently, she went with what she
and Mayor Clayton had previously discussed. She reviewed a map to the Council. She feels that
to run another north/south freeway close to Copperton is worrisome. She feels it needs to be
pushed further east. Everything that has been proposed so far has been approved. While
development is inevitable, it should be done responsibly.

       Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD)
       Council of Governments (COG)

Justice Courts. There are some concerns with the Salt Lake County Justice Court and there are
investigations underway. He would like everyone to think about contracting with other courts.
Kearns and Magna are possibilities.

will be realignments in the future. They are more than likely building ten schools in the near future,
and these will be flexible schools. They are raising starting pay for teachers and they will be
raising property taxes to pay for this. He mentioned the possibility of buying land from Rio Tinto.
