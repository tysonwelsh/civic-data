PRIORITY HOMES DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT

  City Administrator Seth Atkinson explained to the council that Priority Homes have requested to enter
into an agreement to be allowed to pull a building permit for the Loveless Estates Subdivision despite
not having all infrastructure installed. A physical road with asphalt is already installed in front of the lot
where they want to build the unit. The road was built when the Gardner Village infrastructure was
completed. Mr. Atkinson explained that due to the extraordinary weather pattern that has occurred
this winter, he recommended a development agreement that mirrors the one with Roderick Enterprises
that would allow Priority Homes to proceed with this first permit with the condition of them completing
the subdivision process and bonding for the outstanding infrastructure items.
  Councilor Memmott moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the development agreement for the
Loveless Estate, Plat “A” Subdivision. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed on a
unanimous vote.

ORDINANCE 03-21-23 -AMENDING SECTION 10.15.5 (S) ADOPTED:

  Mr. Atkinson updated the council that on February 7th, the Council passed an ordinance amending
Section 10.15.5 of the Nephi City Code that dealt with recreational vehicles and recreation parks. The
ordinance stated that RV parks shall enter a written rental agreement with each individual or entity that
rents an RV space and that the rental agreement would be for no more than thirty days. The Utah State
Code states that transient room tax (TRT) can be imposed by a city to rent temporary lodging for stays of
less than 30 consecutive days. Staff recognized the need to amend the ordinance to say that the RV
parks rental agreements with each individual or entity for no more than twenty-nine (29) days.

RECREATION VEHICLE PARK OWNER AGREEMENTS APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council that over the past several years there has been certain
activities and behaviors at RV Parks that draw police presence and have created safety hazards. To date,
the Council has used a provision in the code that requires users to stay for 30 days or less in an RV Park
to govern and regulate this undesirable behavior. The Council elected to charge RV Parks in the city with
a violation of this section of the city code. During the litigation process, the City Attorney has been
meeting with the RV Park owners to address the concerns from Nephi City residents. Through those
negotiations, a new ordinance governing RV Parks has been adopted that more directly addresses the
concerns emanating from RV Parks.
   In addition to the ordinance that has been adopted, agreements have been drafted by the City
Attorney for each of the RV Parks. These agreements also came about through negotiations with each
of the RV Park owners. This will bind the owners to adhere to the new RV Park ordinance. Some items,
like minimum lot size, will be grandfathered but will be required if expansion occurs. High Country RV
Park also has 10 long-term resident spots that have been grandfathered as a condition of annexation.
The High-Country RV Park agreement will differ with the other agreements for this reason.
   Councilor Memmott moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the RV park owner agreements.
Councilor Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

FAA AGREEMENT APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson explained that smaller airports give their FAA entitlements to other airports to so that
they can accomplish larger projects. Nephi City completed an airport apron expansion project using
entitlements received from other airports. The agreement before the council enabled the FAA to
provide those entitlement from the Nephi Municipal Airport to other airports so they can complete their
projects.
   Councilor Callaway moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the FAA agreement to share entitlements.
Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

RESOLUTION 03-21-2023 MUNCIPAL WASTEWATER PLANNING PROGRAM ADOPTED:

   Mr. Atkinson presented the annual self-assessment report of the Nephi City Sewer System. The
report helps the water and sewer department identify deficiencies and problem areas within the city-
wide sewer system. The City is required to submit this annual report to the department of
environmental quality (DEQ) and more specifically a sub-group of DEQ known as the Water Quality
Board each year for their review. As part of that review DEQ requires that the City Council review the
Municipal Wastewater Planning Program Self-Assessment Report for 2022 and confirm that all
appropriate actions have been taken to maintain effluent requirements in accordance with the Utah
Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (UPDES) Permit.
   While completing the annual self-assessment report no deficiencies were identified for the Nephi City
Sewer System. Councilor Memmott noted that he had discovered a error in Part IV page 29 of the
report. The number of new commercial/industrial connections added, and the number of new
residential sewer connections needed to be switched.
  Councilor Ostler moved to adopt Resolution 03-21-2023 with the change noted by councilor
Memmott on page 29. Councilor Parady seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.
