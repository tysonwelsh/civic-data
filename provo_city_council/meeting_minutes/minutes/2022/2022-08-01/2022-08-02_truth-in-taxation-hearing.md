Action Agenda

1.       A public hearing regarding setting the real and personal property tax levy for
         municipal purposes for Fiscal Year 2022-2023. (22-016) (0:14:38)

Karen Tapahe, Community Relations Coordinator, presented. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth what
the common questions were from the public. Ms. Tapahe said many members of the public had
experienced confusion about the assessed value and had questions about the different taxing
agencies. Councilor Rachel Whipple asked Ms. Tapahe to talk through some of the numbers. Ms.
Tapahe explained that the Council approved up to a 2.9% increase for the City’s general tax. The
Council then heard public comments for the public hearing portion of the agenda.

Viviana Alonso, Provo, said her taxes gone up have gone up so she was paying $536 more per
year. The Council indicated that staff could help talk her through her bill.

Linda Harmon, Provo, shared her concern that taxes will go up over $500 next year, most of
which was for the Provo City School District at a 43% increase. She is on a fixed income and as
a senior citizen has no kids in school, but she is paying exorbitant school district taxes.

Pam Jones, Provo, echoed what the last commenter said about residents on fixed or low incomes,
who have lost employment, or are otherwise victims of inflation. There is no defense against this
situation and she wondered if the Council have determined other ways to cut costs.

Richard Holloman, Provo, has similar concerns. His concern is a matter of trust—this hearing is
described as truth in taxation. He felt the explanation of tax increases was unclear or incomplete.

Paula Grace, Provo, said her taxes went up $724 and she wanted to find a resource to perhaps
cap her taxes because she’s on a fixed income and seeking some help for seniors.

Nevin Andersen, Provo, said he knows the Council is just addressing the Provo City taxes that
evening. His taxes actually went down for the city but increased due to the school board taxes.
He was in favor of small increases on a regular basis.

Kathleen Christensen, Provo, had questions about the city hall. She thought there should be an
accounting of where this money will go and why they need to have such a tax hike when things
continue to get worse.

Council discussion and questions (0:48:20)

Councilor George Handley briefly addressed Ms. Christensen’s concerns about the old city hall
building, noting that it would soon be demolished. Due to supply chain delays, the Council had
not moved their meetings into the new city hall but they hope to soon. After that, this building
would be leveled and redeveloped.

Councilor Shannon Ellsworth addressed the comment about how taxes are spent in Provo City.
She noted that Provo publishes a citizens budget each year, which is a high-level version of the
budget. Residents can also read the full budget report and see down to the cent how money is
budgeted, which is also available every year. She addressed another comment about tax relief for
seniors. She noted that the proper appeal authorities are the Utah State Legislature and the Utah
State Tax Commission, who create the rules around how political subdivisions of Utah can tax.

Councilor David Shipley noted that there is a program for seniors over age 66. If someone is on a
fixed income and are below a particular income threshold, there are some options through the
state, which are administered locally by Utah County. Councilor Rachel Whipple also shared
information about these programs—she invited seniors to go to the Utah County Treasurer or
Auditor’s office at 100 East Center Street. There are five different property tax relief programs,
including programs for seniors, for veterans, for indigent individuals, and armed service
members. Utah County has more information and applications for those programs.

Mr. Handley also shared additional comments in response to Ms. Christensen’s comments. He
noted that part of the motivation is the Council has serious concerns about covering the public
safety needs in the City. Every single year they need to hire more police officers than they have

consider other ways to cover the gap for the equipment. This tax increase will increase the City’s

Mr. Shipley shared additional comments about taxation in Utah. He noted that implementing a
cap on property tax within the state would be a decision of the State Legislature. He noted that
different states have different policies; this could be changed at the state level. Truth in taxation
is also the state’s terminology and policy. He thinks it’s a good policy to have hearings and share
information, but state law dictates that policy. Councilor Travis Hoban addressed the general
misunderstanding around property tax bills going up by hundreds of dollars—only a few dollars
of such increases are coming from Provo City.

Ms. Tapahe offered additional comments about how the property taxes work at the County level.
Mr. Jones also clarified some things about what the county does and is able to do. Mr. Handley
noted that costs at the city are going up. Mr. Shipley explained that the alternative is to not raise
taxes and keep the status quo, but the city’s ability to keep consistent services is costing more.
Sales tax has helped with this, but they need sustainable sources of funding for sustainable types
of services like public safety that our citizens have come to expect. Ms. Ellsworth explained this
in terms of a household economy and how a household budget operates.

Councilor Bill Fillmore said that raising property taxes even in small increments is not fun but
the City has to face reality of inflation and increasing costs. The City has to pay more for
salaries, infrastructure, and as the City grows they also have more people to serve. In 31 years,
Provo has only raised its certified tax rate once. It would be imprudent not to take that seriously
and the City cannot go into debt to cover the difference. Provo has been the recipients of some
major funding from the federal government and has also benefitted from increases in sales tax
revenues, but Provo cannot rely on these only.

Councilor Katrice MacKay noted that a few people asked about appraisal of homes. This has
gone up a lot at the County. She appealed it one year with her house and it was a simple process
involving sending a letter to Utah County along with comparisons from a realtor.

Ms. Whipple noted there are eight different taxing entities. She is glad she gets one bill from the
County so it’s consolidated, even though it might be a little more confusing. Provo hasn’t raised
rates because they know these other entities are raising rates and don’t want to pile on. But it
means they haven’t done anything since 2015 and it isn’t a sustainable course of action—this is a
modest and measured approach. Presentation and discussion only.
