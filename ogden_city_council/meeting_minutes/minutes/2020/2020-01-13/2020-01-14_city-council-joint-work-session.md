         The purpose of the joint work session was to receive Open and Public Meetings and Ethics Training; discuss the Fiscal
Year (FY) 2020 Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) Amendment; discuss the FY 2020 Budget Amendment; discuss Council/Board
business - and more specifically, Council Rules and Nonns Amendments; and hear Council/Board comments.

Open and Public Meetings and Ethics Training
          City Attorney Williams provided the City Council with annually required training regarding the Open and Public
Meetings Act (OPMA) for the State of Utah as well as the Municipal Officers' and Employees' Ethics Act. Throughout the
training, Mr. Williams engaged in discussion with the Council to clearly communicate the types of actions the Council could take
that could be construed as a violation of the OPMA as well as any activity in which an individual Council member could be
involved that would be a conflict of interest and a violation of the Ethics Act. He provided the Council with guidance regarding
the types of activities or private business relationships that Council members should disclose to avoid a claim of a conflict of
interest. There was also brief discussion about various benefits that the City provides to full-time employees and whether Council
members are eligible to receive similar benefits.

Fiscal Year 2020 Capital Improvement Plan Amendment
          Council Policy Analyst Mabey reported that Council staff received a request from City Administration to remove
this item from the agenda for this evening.

Fiscal Year 2020 Budget Amendment
          Ms. Mabey reported City Administration has proposed budget amendments that will result in a net increase of the Fiscal
Year (FY) 2020 City budget revenues by $5,636,250.
          Comptroller Stout explained this proposal includes three central budget amendments, including designating $4,000,000
for water utility infrastructure improvements and purchasing ten new Compressed Natural Gas (CNG) refuse trucks. This
proposed budget opening includes funding for one FY2020-2024 Capital Improvement Plan project: Water WUO 15 - Distribution,
Fire Flow & Pressure Improvements. This project addresses the various needs of Ogden's culinary water distribution system.
          Public Services Department Deputy Director Anderson briefly discussed the scope of the water utility infrastructure
projects that will be completed using the $4 million allocation. This includes water line connections on Pierce and Buchanan
A venues, a waterline project on 20 th Street, replacement of pressure reducing valves, and infrastructure design work for the Bus
Rapid Transit route. This led to discussion among the Council regarding other miscellaneous capital projects awaiting funding.
          There was brief discussion about the City's schedule for purchasing new fleet vehicles as well as the expected life of
CNG refuse trucks. Council member Blair asked why Administration prefers purchasing the vehicles outright rather than leasing
them as the City has done in the past. Ms. Stout answered that the health of the refuse fund makes it possible for the City to
purchase the vehicles rather than financing them.

Board/Council Business
          Council Rules and Norms Amendments: Ms. Mabey summarized the proposed amendments to Council Rules and                         )
Norms. This included an adjustment to rules to allow for the Council to convene in a Closed Executive Session from a work               •
session rather than a regular meeting. The Council discussed the transparency of this proposed amendment and concluded they are
comfmiable considering the change so long as consideration of adjourning into a Closed Executive Session from a Work Session
is clearly advertised.
          An additional potential amendment deals with defining a practice to allow for leadership or Council staff to conduct a
'straw poll' among the Council regarding an initiative that may be recommended by an individual Council member or City
Administration. The Council concluded they are comfortable with the practice of allowing a 'straw poll' so long as the practice is
legal, and the outcome of the poll is non-binding.
          Ms. Mabey concluded the final proposed amendment to the Rules and Norms document is to provide guidance for
Council staff or leadership to respond to an individual seeking to make an object lesson or presentation as part of their public
comments in a business meeting. Council member Hyer suggested that the rules that apply specifically to the public be printed on
the back of meeting agendas so that a member of the public is not surprised when a certain rule is being enforced.
          Upcoming trip to Hof, Germany: Chair Choberka encouraged the Council to inform her of any areas ofinteresttheymay
have regarding the upcoming Hof, Germany visit. She stated leadership can work with Council staff to facilitate meetings or visits
to points of interest during the trip. The Council collectively suggested that the visit be documented in the form of video or audio
recordings that could be streamed through the website or a podcast forum for the benefit of the public.

Board/Council Comments
         Economic development/planning updates: Council member Stephens stated he would like for the Council to regularly
receive a report of economic development or planning matters in the community. Specifically, he would like an update on projects
that may have received approval from the Planning Commission or City Council, but for which no activity has occurred. He
referenced the Quick Quack Car Wash project in the northern end of the City or the redevelopment of the old Fred Meyer site as
examples. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith stated that it is difficult for Administration or Council staff to ask for an update
on development of private property. For those projects for which an incentive has been provided by the City or the Redevelopment
Agency, Administration can request regular updates from the developer. Council member White suggested that for private
developments, it would be simple for the Council to receive an update regarding the status of a project based upon whether the
owner or a developer has applied for permits for their project. This led to discussion regarding the availability of information
regarding private development projects and the ability of Council staff to gain access to that information for updates to be
provided to the Council. Chief Administrative Officer Johnson added that some information is private, and Administration does
not have access to it; if certain information is shared, the owner or developer of a project could pursue legal action. He stated he
will defer to the City Attorney's Office to provide an opinion regarding the type of information that can be made publicly
available to the City Council.
