6.    Public Hearing: Exeter (Summit Commerce Center) 2nd Amended Development
      Agreement (Legislative Item).

On the request of Mark McKee representing Exeter Draper Land, LLC and ARCO Design Build,
to amend an existing Development Agreement to permit additional wall signage in excess of
current ordinance limits. The site is approximately 26.94 acres and located at approximately
13004 S. Pony Express Road known as Application #DA-226-2022, Staff contact is Todd Draper,
(801) 576-6335, todd.draper@draperutah.gov.

Todd Draper, City Planner, presented the Staff Report and stated that what is proposed is a second
amendment to the original Development Agreement. The property location was described and
identified on a map displayed. The subject property is next to Pony Express Road and I-15. The
land use is Industrial/Manufacturing and the zoning is M1 (Light Manufacturing). This
Development Agreement amendment applies more specifically to Building 1, which fronts Pony
Express Road. The request is for an amendment to allow for additional signage. Currently, the
regulations regarding wall signs allow for one square foot of wall sign for every linear foot of
building façade. There is also a maximum allowed sign size of 250 square feet. The proposed
Development Agreement would allow the applicants to place one large sign of approximately 720
square feet on the building and additional square footage and wall signs on the eastern façade
with a total of up to 1,157 square feet.

The façade is 570 feet long so they would typically get only 570 square feet. The request is for
just over two square feet of wall sign per linear foot of building facade. Site photos were
presented where there are a couple of non-approved illegal banner signs that have been installed
on the building. The illegal signs need to be removed before a new sign permit can be approved.
Another site photo showed four additional illegal banner signs having been installed that would
mimic the wall signs they would like to have there. A photo showing what was existing on the
building was displayed. The proposed wall signs will be internally illuminated. A photo of
additional large banner signs that would need to be removed was also shown.

Mr. Draper reported that the existing main wall sign is about 247 square feet in size and complies
with the ordinance. What is proposed is a 720-square-foot sign. A photo simulation showing
what it would look like in the space was displayed. By comparison, the property is not far from
IKEA and RC Willey, which are both part of a Commercial Special District (“CSD”), where two
square feet of wall sign area is allowed per lineal foot of building facade. In the Dahle Retail
CSD they are allowed to have larger wall signs. It was noted that the letters in IKEA’s wall signs
are about the same height as the proposed main wall sign and are 516 square feet in size and they
have two or three of these signs on the building. Mr. Draper stated that the IKEA building is also
permitted a 600-square-foot banner sign that is frequently changed.

Mr. Draper also compared the Furniture Row/Denver Mattress building which meets the sign
ordinance and only have signs that are one square foot per lineal foot of building wall. Their
largest approved wall sign is approximately 100 square feet in size.

Mr. Draper reported that Living Spaces is the entity that will be benefited by the amended
Development Agreement. Currently, they have 209 square feet of signage approved on the north
façade. The maximum permissible on that façade is 260 square feet. There is 222.5 square feet

of signage on the south façade with a maximum of 260 square feet allowed. On the east façade,
they currently have 341 square feet with a total of 570 square feet allowed. The proposal would
allow for 1,157 square feet of wall signage on the eastern facade with the largest wall sign being
720 square feet in size.

Commissioner Fowler asked about the first amendment to the Development Agreement. Mr.
Draper stated that it was to add a restaurant use back into the list of allowed uses. It was removed
from the allowed uses in the initial Development Agreement and Living Spaces later wanted to
have a restaurant in their building. All other ordinance requirements would be adhered to. The
request tonight pertains only to the amount of signage.

In response to another question raised by Commissioner Nixon, Mr. Draper stated that the signs
are restricted mainly by size. With regard to lighting, if the applicants were to request some type
of lighting that is not allowed under the ordinance, they would again need to ask to amend the
Development Agreement. The current proposal, however, includes lighting that is in compliance.
It was clarified that the proposed Development Agreement includes the total square footage for
all of the wall signs on the eastern facade. The approved signage has permits and complies with
the current code. The applicants do not have enough square footage to get all of the signage they
want without approval of this amendment to the development agreement.

Lighting issues were discussed. Commissioner Ogden expressed concern about the applicants
being allowed to have as much lighting as they wish and possibly even more than is allowed in
the Code. Jennifer Jastremsky, Planning Manager, clarified that the Development Agreement
does not mention lighting. As a result, any lighting on the signs must comply with the current
Code. The applicants are getting a deviation only to what is specifically listed in the Development
Agreement, which is the sign area. Everything else must comply with the Code. In terms of foot
candle levels, the Sign Code does not list a maximum. It does, however, encourage backlighting.
If a light is separate from the sign that is shining on it, there are foot candle levels that must be
met.

In response to a question raised about signage next to the freeway, Mr. Draper stated that in this
case, the building is too close to the road to qualify for the exceptions in the ordinance. Larger
signs are allowed further away from the road. Ms. Jastremsky stated that the freeway frontage
code regulations mainly allow for pole signs and extra height. Commissioner Nixon commented
that in her opinion what is proposed is very tastefully done.

The applicant, Mark McKee, was present representing Exeter Development. Brian Saltikov from
Living Spaces was also present. Mr. McKee stated that the intent is to increase the size of the
sign, which is important to the tenant. Mr. Saltikov stated that the desire is to maintain
consistency with the rest of their stores. The logo banners were put up during their grand opening.
The applicant reported that the banner signs have since been taken down.

Commissioner Fowler asked why they did not include the size of the signs in the Development
Agreement initially. Mr. McKee responded that at the time the Development Agreement was
written, they did not know that Living Spaces was going to be the tenant.

Vice-Chair Hawker opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public
hearing was closed.

Commissioner Fowler commented that the comparison of signage size with the buildings down
the street is a good scale to consider and seemed to match. The other commissioners agreed.

Commissioner Nixon asked about the conditions included in the staff report and if the conditions
regarding the banner signs were still needed. Mr. Draper stated that staff had not yet had an
opportunity to verify the claims of the applicant and noted that it might be best if they remain.

Motion: Commissioner Nixon moved to forward a POSITIVE recommendation to the City
Council for the Amended Development Agreement as requested by Mark McKee,
representing Exeter Draper Land, LLC and ARCO Design Build, Application #DA-226-
2022 subject to the following:

Conditions:

       1.      That the property owner or tenant remove the temporary banner signs from
               the building and obtain necessary sign permits for any temporary banner
               signs before any are placed on the building or property in the future. If sign
               permits for temporary banner signs are issued, applicable time limits and
               occurrences shall be calculated from the date each set of current banner signs
               was installed.

       2.      That the property owner or tenant bring the property into compliance with
               the requirements of the zoning ordinance before any new or outstanding
               applications or permits for the property are approved.

       3.      That the property owner or tenant obtain the necessary sign permits for the
               installation of all wall signs prior to their installation and complete all
               inspection requirements.

Finding:

       1.      The proposed Amended Development Agreement complies with the
               requirements of DCMC Section 9-5-200(C) and (D).
