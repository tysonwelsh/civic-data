2.     Public Hearing: Welling Fence Conditional Use Permit (Administrative Action).
       On the request of John Welling, for a Conditional Use Permit in order to increase the allowed
       fence height within the front yard on property located at 1128 East Walden Lane. Application
       USE-042-2022. Staff contact is Tevai Haunga, 801-576-6522, tevai.haunga@draperutah.gov.

City Planner, Tevai Haunga, presented the Staff Report and stated that the application is for a
Conditional Use Permit to increase the fence height in the front yard to six feet on the .47-acre
property located at 1128 East Walden Lane. The current zoning is RA2 single-family residential
(20,000 square foot minimum lot size). The property is located on the corner of Walden and Cindy
Lanes and the land use designation is Residential Low Medium density. The applicant is requesting
a six-foot fence along the front yard property line. The current Code does not allow fencing greater
than four feet in height in front yards. Six-foot fencing is also requested along the back and sides of

3.       Public Hearing: City Initiated Water-Wise Landscaping and Site Amendments Text
         Amendment (Administrative Action).
         On the request of Draper City for a Text Amendment to Titles 9 and 17 of Draper City
         Municipal Code in order to amend regulations regarding definitions, site plan review, site
         design, landscaping including water-wise landscaping, screening, parking area design,
         outdoor storage and display, improvement completion assurances, and related subdivision
         requirements. This application is known as the City Initiated Water-Wise Landscaping and
         Site Amendments Text Amendment. Application: TEXTMAP-187-2022. Staff contact: Todd
         Draper, (801) 576-6335, todd.draper@draperutah.gov.

City Planner, Todd Draper, presented the Staff Report and stated that the matter was being brought
back after a long delay. The intent tonight was to review what has changed. A memo provided to
the Commission set forth all of the proposed changes.

The changes were identified as follows:

     •   A minor change was made to the definition of Street Tree. The current definition calls for
         public improvements. New State law specifies that public improvements must be maintained
         by the City. The new definition states that in cases where the City is maintaining them, they
         are public improvements. Otherwise, they are simply required improvements.
     •   With respect to the site plan review, the second grading plan for landscaping was eliminated
         and landscape elements were included into one grading plan.
     •   For the site design elements, in Chapter 22 the requirement for all amenities to be located next
         to the front door was eliminated. Staff felt that some amenities might not be appropriate there.
     •   Language was updated regarding the installation of the required landscaping, guarantees, and
         completion assurances. Changes were made based on feedback from the City Council.
     •   Landscape deviations were removed and items were added that staff typically sees as
         exceptions or allowances within the ordinance.
     •   The deviations section was repurposed to include the rest of the Jordan Valley Water
         Conservancy District (“JVWCD”) standards that were not listed elsewhere in the ordinance.
         The standards are needed to allow residents to take advantage of the financial benefits to the
         City of purchasing water at a lower cost from JVWCD and the water conservation programs
         that are offered to residents.
     •   A prohibition was imposed in the landscape design criteria for grass areas that are less than
         eight-feet wide.
     •   For most single-family and two-family dwellings, the front yard needs to be at least 50%
         landscaped. There are exceptions but generally, the idea is that the entire front of a property
         will not be in concrete. The remainder of the landscaping area is at the discretion of the
         homeowner.

   •   A clarification regarding the requirement of the Soils Analysis was added for commercial and
       multi-family projects, smaller residential projects are not required to have a soils analysis
       where it is listed as a recommendation.
   •   Modifications regarding irrigation included the reduction of some previously proposed
       requirements down to meet the JVWCD standard. Others were modified to come closer to
       what the language in the JVWCD standard is.
   •   The park strip landscaping requirements were simplified. It is not mandated that other
       landscaping go in the park strip besides trees. Tree installation and maintenance will fall to
       the property owner as necessitated by the changes the State made to improvement completion
       assurances. Bonds are not required upfront unless they are public landscaped improvements
       that the City maintains. A property owner will have a certain period of time from when they
       move in to get it done. Staff will have to follow up with enforcement after that.
   •   Parking lot landscaping requirements were changed to make it clear that turf is prohibited in
       parking lots.
   •   Land use buffer requirements for fencing would include multi-family to install a fence the
       same as commercial and mixed-use projects.
   •   A modification was made to the temporary suspension of landscape installation section. The
       clause was rewritten and language was added regarding days that are over 100 degrees and
       the five following days when installation would be prohibited.
   •   To the planting tables for perimeters, an exception clause was added to allow for a reduction
       in the number of trees required when there is an adjacent tree canopy.
   •   Outdoor storage was modified to require a 10-foot screening fence. A lower fence may be
       allowed if approved by the Planning Commission. Fencing with masonry, solid metal or a
       material that matches the building, is required for outdoor storage.
   •   With regard to the Street Tree Plan, the developer of a subdivision would have to create the
       plan, get it approved, and provide a copy to the homeowner when the property is sold. The
       intent is to alert future homeowners that they need to have their street trees put in and inform
       them of the requirement. The timeframe for installation was set one month into the next
       growing season to allow sufficient time to meet the requirement. Street trees should be planted
       first.
   •   A change was added to specify that if an applicant is required to provide public landscape
       improvements that will be maintained by the City, or other infrastructure improvements, State
       law should be followed. They should either be planted or a guarantee must be posted.
   •   The improvement completion assurances point to the Subdivision Code with regard to how
       they are processed.
   •   With regard to street trees, the developer or property owner must install the street trees as
       detailed on the approved plans for the street frontage that abuts the property. Installation
       should not occur in the winter and shall be completed no later than April 30 of the calendar
       year following the commencement of the first of the use or the issuance of an Occupancy
       Certificate for the property. After installation, property owners shall irrigate and care for the
       trees.
   •   A change was proposed to specify that required landscaping, with the exception of street trees,
       shall be installed no later than September 30 of the following calendar year after an approved
       use has commenced, a Certificate of Occupancy is issued, or one year from the date an
