GPZ-2-2023
Nick Mingo
5998 W Parkway Blvd.
General plan change from Large Lot Residential to Light Manufacturing
and a zone change from A-1 and A to M
37.53 acres

Nick Mingo has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for a 37.53-acre parcel
located at 5998 W Parkway Blvd. The property is currently zoned A-1 (Agriculture, minimum
lot size 1 acre) south of the Riter Canal and A (Agriculture, minimum lot size ½ acre) north of
the Riter Canal. The current General Plan designation is Large Lot Residential (2 to 3 units/acre)
for the portion of the property that is west of Stegner Way with the balance being Medium
Density Residential (7 to 12 units/acre). The proposed zone is M (Manufacturing) and the
proposed General Plan designation is Light Manufacturing. The property is currently owned by
Ivory Land Corporation.

Surrounding zones include M to the north, A to the east, and A-1 to the south and west.
Surrounding land uses a new industrial project to the north that is partially developed; Rocky
Mountain Power transmission lines and the Mountain View Corridor to the east; Wallace Stegner
Academy charter school, a 2.7-acre single family home lot, and Magna Water well property to
the south; and vacant property to the west. The subject property is currently vacant.

Development Proposal

The intended development for this property is an industrial project with a total of 533,294 square
feet of buildings of varying sizes as reflected in the concept plan submitted by the applicant. In
the M zone, all uses on properties greater than 10 acres are reviewed as a conditional use. Hence,
if this application is approved, the applicant would need to apply for a conditional use permit to
pursue the proposed industrial development.

Property History

A General Plan/zone change application (GPZ-2-2013) was submitted on this property in 2013
by Nick Mingo acting as Ivory’s agent. At the time, the property was 44.4 acres and included
what is now Wallace Stegner Academy and Stegner Way. The request was to change the zoning
from A-1 and A to R-1-10 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 10,000 square
feet) and RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential) and to change the General Plan from Low
Density Residential (3 to 4 units/acre) and Rural Residential (1 to 2 units/acre) to Very High
Density Residential (over 20 units/acre) and Low Density Residential. The proposal included a
94-lot single family home PUD and 230 apartments in 3 story building. Both the Planning
Commission and the City Council voted to deny the application. The meeting minutes for the
Planning Commission and City Council meetings where this prior application was denied are

included with this report. One of the reasons stated for the denial was the nearby presence of
Rocky Mountain Raceway which no longer exists.
When the City updated the General Plan in 2015 the General Plan designation on the subject
property was changed to the current designation of Large Lot Residential and Medium Density
Residential.

Issues to Consider

Since the adoption of the City’s first General Plan in the 80’s, the Riter Canal has been the
dividing line between industrial uses to the north and residential uses to the south in the
northwest part of the City. However, much has changed in the northwest part of the City since
the 80’s. The following list of current conditions may warrant consideration of M zoning on the
subject property:

        The Northwest Economic Development Area (EDA), which was created in 2012,
         includes the subject property. The Northwest EDA covers approximately 1,000 acres in
         the northwest part of the City and makes available incentives for commercial
         development.
        As indicated on the City’s Major Street Plan, the City plans to eventually extend 5900
         West between Beagley Road and Parkway Blvd. on the Rocky Mountain Power property
         directly east of the subject parcel. This road extension will enable industrial traffic in the
         area north of Beagley Road to access Mountain View Corridor without using Parkway
         Blvd. In other words, the subject property would be next to two roads with significant
         industrial traffic – Beagley Road and 5900 West.
        The charter school and Magna Water property to the south could be seen as a buffer
         between the residential property on the south side of Parkway Blvd. and the potential
         industrial development on the subject property.
        The zoning needed to develop the property as Medium Density Residential, the current
         General Plan designation on the east part of the property, is RM. However, to be eligible
         for RM zoning, the current zoning ordinance requires a property of this size to have
         access and frontage on a street with a planned right-of-way of at least 100 feet as
         indicated on the Major Street Plan. Currently, the only streets the subject property has
         access and frontage on are Stegner Way and Beagley Road, which are not roads with a
         planned right-of-way width of at least 100 feet. This means that this property is not
         eligible for RM zoning.
        The City will be widening Parkway Blvd. to 5 lanes between Mountain View Corridor
         and 6400 West. This widening project is anticipated to begin construction in late summer
         of this year and to be completed in the fall of 2024.
        The property on the north side of Beagley Road has received conditional use approval
         for two large industrial buildings totaling over 600,000 square feet. The west building
         has already been completed.

Access
If the Commission believes M zoning is appropriate here, the primary access would be off of
Beagley Road. A future driveway connection to 5900 West may be possible at such time this
road is extended south and if the owner is able to secure permission to construct an access across
Rocky Mountain Power’s property. Access to Stegner Way is also possible; however, mixing
industrial and school traffic could be problematic, particularly during drop off and pick up times
for the school.


The applicant’s concept shows a new cul-de-sac at the north end of Stegner Way with an access
for the subject property onto the cul-de-sac. This cul-de-sac is in response to City ordinance that
requires stub streets to be either extended through a property or terminated in a cul-de-sac. To
prevent the mixing of industrial and school traffic, the Commission could recommend a
development agreement where the property does not access Stegner Way.

Zone change from A to M.
0.88 acres

Samuel Wright originally requested a zone change on two parcels totaling 1.19 acres at 7091 and

After Mr. Wright’s application was submitted, staff noticed that 7091 W Gates Avenue was

Surrounding zones include M to the north and east and A to the south and west. Neighboring
uses include a storage yard to the north, vacant property to the east and west, and single family
homes to the south. The subject property is currently licensed as an auto wrecking yard, which is
a nonconforming use in the A zone.

Development Proposal

The applicant has submitted a concept plan and written description for his development proposal
for the subject property. If this application is approved, the applicant intends to build two 8,400
square foot buildings to house his business, Wright Timberframe, and to rent out the remaining
space. Wright Timberframe builds custom timber frame structures such as pergolas, pavilions,
and trellises.

If this rezone is approved, a conditional use permit will be required for this use given the
adjoining residential use to the south. A 6’ tall masonry wall will be required along the west and
south sides of the property.

Development Agreement
Beginning in 2017, approved rezones from A to M along the south side of Gates Avenue have
been accompanied by a development agreement. The first was for the properties at 7091 and
7079 W Gates Avenue. The second was for the properties at 7055, 7041, and 7035 W Gates
Avenue. Copies of these agreements are included with this report for reference. In summary, the
first agreement limited the hours of operation from 8 AM to 6 PM, required a covered entry
feature on the north side of the building, and limited the uses to those allowed in the Light
Industrial (LI) zone. The second agreement limited the hours of operation from 7 AM to 10 PM,
limits the uses to those allowed in the LI zone (outside storage is still allowed), addresses the
phasing of the development, addresses setbacks, and includes renderings of the proposed
building.

Staff shared the concept plan with the Public Works Department for preliminary comment.
Public Works has requested that the development agreement limit the subject property along
with the parcel to the east to one shared access. They also requested that the applicant install off-
site improvements on the south side of Gates Avenue from the frontage road to the west edge of
the property at 7055 W. The City would reimburse the applicant for improvements installed east
of the property at 7091 W.

The applicant is requesting that the following items be addressed in the development agreement:

      The hours of operation limitation imposed on the property at 7091 W Gates Avenue by
       the first agreement be changed from 8 AM - 6 PM to 7 AM - 10 PM.

      The setback along the west side of the property be allowed to be reduced from 20’ to as
       low as 0’ since the General Plan designation on the adjoining property to the west is
       Light Manufacturing.

      Similar to the second development agreement along the south side of Gates Avenue, do
       not prohibit outside storage in the agreement.

Given the precedent for development agreements along this street, the request from Public
Works, the request from the applicant, and the adjoining residential to the south, staff is
recommending a development agreement that addresses the following items on the properties at
7091 and 7103 W Gates Avenue:

   1. As recommended by Public Works, there would be only one shared access for the two
       parcels being developed (7091 and 7103 W Gates Avenue).

   2. As recommended by Public Works, require the developer to design and install
      improvements (pavement, curb, gutter, sidewalk, and streetlights) along the south side of
      Gates Avenue between the frontage road and the east boundary of the property at 7055 W
      Gates Ave. The City would reimburse the developer for the improvements in front of
      7079 W and 7067 W Gates Avenue.

   3. Similar to the building at 7079 W Gates Avenue, the fronts of the new buildings would
      include a covered entry feature and/or other enhancements.
   4. As with the other development agreements on Gates Avenue, limit the hours of operation
      given the neighboring residential uses to 7 AM to 10 PM.

   5. Allow the minimum setback along the west side of the property to be reduced to 10’.

   6. As with the other development agreements on Gates Avenue, limit the uses allowed to
      those allowed in the LI zone. The one exception would be outside storage, which would
      still be allowed.

ZT-2-2023
West Valley City
An ordinance change to Section 7-7-119 of the zoning ordinance to add
screening requirements for outside storage that occurs on property
adjacent to a High-Image Arterial Street as listed in Section 7-10-102

City staff is proposing an ordinance amendment to Sections 7-7-119 of the zoning ordinance to
add screening requirements for outside storage that occurs on property adjacent to a High-Image
Arterial Street as listed in Section 7-10-102. Currently the ordinance requires the screening of
outside storage from public view by means of fencing, landscaping, building placement,
topography, berming, or other appropriate method. The proposed ordinance change would
require outside storage that occurs on property adjacent to a High-Image Arterial Street as listed
in Section 7-10-102 to be screened from public view by means of a building or a precast concrete
wall having a minimum height of 6’. The proposed ordinance also specifies the wall pattern and
color for consistency and clarifies that the Planning Commission can require up to an 8’ tall wall
when needed to effectively screen storage items over 6’ in height.

In addition, the proposed ordinance includes a provision that allows the Planning Commission to
waive screening requirements when topography changes or the distance of storage areas from
rights-of-way would make screening ineffective. An example of this would be the storage yard at
Ready Made Concrete on the northeast corner of Parkway Blvd. and 2700 W. As a car travels
west along Parkway Blvd. over I-215, the road is elevated such that even an 8’ tall wall would
not effectively screen the outside storage. However, as the road slopes down toward the grade of
the Ready Made Concrete site, a wall would effectively screen the outside storage.
For reference, the list of High-Image Arterial Streets from Section 7-10-102 includes the
following roads:

   1. 3500-3300 South (SR 171) from 7200 West to 2700 West;
   2. 5600 West (SR 172) from 3100 South to the City’s south border;
   3. 2700 South (Parkway Blvd and Lake Park Blvd);
   4. 2100 South Freeway (SR 201), including the north and south Frontage roads;
   5. Bangerter Highway (SR 154);
   6. I-215 Freeway;
   7. Mountain View Corridor (SR 85);
   8. 4100-3900 South;
   9. 4700 South;
   10. 3100 South;
   11. 3200 West;
   12. 3600 West;
   13. 2700 West;
   14. 5400 South (SR 173); and
   15. 6200 South.

Staff is proposing this change for the three reasons. First, screening methods like a chain-link
fence with slats or landscaping are not as effective at screening as a precast concrete wall.
Second, precast concrete walls are more durable and weather better than other fencing materials
like metal panels, wood, or chain-link with slats. Third, the proposed wall pattern and color
would, in staff’s view, enhance the aesthetics of the wall and make the walls consistent with
other City installed walls along roads like 2700 W.

HH-Salt Lake, LLC
Amendments to Sections 7-1-103 and 7-6-301 of the zoning ordinance
to remove the definition for Lingerie Store and to remove Lingerie
Store from the Commercial and Manufacturing Use Table

Scott Sabey, who represents HH-Salt Lake, LLC, has requested ordinance amendments to
Sections 7-1-103 and 7-6-301 of the zoning ordinance to remove the definition for Lingerie Store
and to remove Lingerie Store from the Commercial and Manufacturing Use Table. The reasons
for the requested amendments are outlined in the attached document from the applicant.

Section 7-1-103 defines Lingerie Store as “a retail establishment meeting any of the following
criteria:
         a. Over five percent of the floor area is devoted to the sale of lingerie, adult novelties,
         and adult marital or sexual aids;
         b. Lingerie, adult novelties, and adult marital or sexual aids account for five percent or
         more of gross sales; or
       c. The establishment is marketed as a Lingerie Store or adult establishment.”

Section 7-6-301 allows Lingerie Stores in the MXD (Mixed Use), C-2 (General Commercial), C-
3 (Transitional Commercial), and M (Manufacturing) zones. Lingerie Stores are prohibited in the
BRP (Business/Research Park), CC (City Center), C-1 (Neighborhood Commercial), and LI
(Light Industrial) zones as well as within the Decker Lake Station, Jordan River, Bangerter
Highway, and 5600 West Overlay Zones. There are just under 9,000 acres of property within the
City where Lingerie Stores are allowed. This represents 46% of property (excluding roads) in the
City.

The term “Lingerie Store” was first introduced in the zoning ordinance with the adoption of
Ordinance 15-03 in 2015. This ordinance expanded the list of prohibited uses within the CC
zone. The term “Lingerie Store” was later defined in Section 7-1-103 and added to the
Commercial and Manufacturing Use Table in Section 7-6-301 with the adoption of Ordinance
17-10 in 2017.

The City has long used overlay zones to prohibit certain uses within areas where other uses are
preferred. For example, within the Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone located east of I-215 and
generally between 3100 S and 3500 S, the City has encouraged entertainment uses like the
Maverik Center and Harmon Hall, restaurants, and hospitality uses.

In addition to Lingerie Stores, there are several other uses that are prohibited in all of the overlay
zones mentioned above. These uses include: Agricultural Business and Industry, Alternative
Financial Service Provider, Used Automobile Sales, Automobile Service, Bail Bonds Dealer,

Blood Plasma Center, Cannabis Production Establishment, Cargo Container, Self-Storage
Facility, Sexually Oriented Business, and Tattoo Establishment.

Merritt Rinard – Pet Aquamation
2052 West 2200 South
M Zone (1.47 Acres)

Merritt Rinard is requesting a conditional use for a pet cremation facility at 2052 West 2200
South. This property is zoned M, Manufacturing, and the West Valley City General Plan
designates this area as Light Manufacturing. The surrounding zone is M on all sides with Ut
Hwy 201 to the north.

In the West Valley City Municipal Code, Cremation is listed under Heavy Industrial, which is a
conditional use:

       “Heavy Industrial” means asphalt and concrete plants; crematories; electric generating
       plants and facilities; sand and gravel excavation, processing, and storage; manufacturing
       and storage of chemicals, explosives, and fuels; Outdoor Gun Ranges; outdoor
       manufacturing; indoor sewage treatment and disposal facilities; and water purification
       facilities. “Heavy Industrial” does not include Outdoor Waste Management Facilities.

As outlined in the attached applicant letter you will see the details of this specific use which are
not the typical cremation/flame process but instead is a water based process. The applicant has
also submitted a letter from Granger Hunter Improvement District that indicates they do not have
any concerns with the proposed use and outlined their review process upon approval of the use
through West Valley City.

The lease space is 1,000 sq ft and the business will operate by appointment only. There is
adequate parking on site to accommodate this use. The site itself was recently under review with
a separate business license so all of the landscaping and parking surfaces have been updated and
are maintained.

Staff Alternatives:

      Approval, subject to the resolution of any issues raised at the public hearing and the
       following conditions:

           1. Must comply with all other relevant requirements set forth from applicable

C-7-2023
Cascade Collision Center
5662 W 4700 S
M Zone (3.75 acres)

Silver Fox Construction, LLC is requesting conditional use approval for a new commercial
development that includes an outside storage yard at 5662 W 4700 S. The property is zoned M,
Manufacturing, and the West Valley City General Plan designates the area as Light
Manufacturing. The zoning for all adjacent properties to the south, east and west is also M.
There is a railroad line running north of this parcel, and the parcel north of the railroad track is
Zoned A, Agriculture. Adjacent land uses include a 7-Eleven on the east side of the property, a
self-storage facility to the west, and a Ken Garff truck facility to the south. The property to the
north is vacant.

The owner of the property operates an auto-collision repair business, with six other locations in
Utah. The proposed development is for two buildings. One will be for the auto collision
business, and the other will be a tire service center. The auto collision building will be roughly
25,000 square feet and include about 3,000 sq. ft. of office space. The tire service center will be
about 7,500 square feet and includes six service bays. Both buildings are required to meet the
Commercial Design Standards.
The property has frontage on 5600 West and 4700 South but will only have access onto 4700
South. The development will share access on the east end with the 7-Eleven property. An
additional access point is not allowed on 4700 South, so the applicant is looking to share access
on the west end with the storage unit property. The layout of the site will work regardless of
whether access is obtained on the west side. 141 parking stalls are proposed for the
development, which exceeds the minimum number of stalls required by the code.

There will be a fenced, outside storage area in the north-west corner of the property. This area
must meet the requirements outlined in 7-7-119. The Planning Commission is considering a
change to this section of code that requires either a building or masonry wall to screen the
outside storage areas on property adjacent to High-Image Arterial Streets. The code change
includes a provision that gives the Planning Commission the ability to waive the masonry wall
requirement when changes in topography or the distance of the storage area from rights-of-way
would make screening with a masonry wall ineffective. The placement of the two buildings
will screen a large majority of the storage area from the south and east. The closest point of the
outside storage area from the 5600 West right-of-way is about 230 feet. Due to this large
distance, staff feels that a masonry wall is not necessary for screening. The applicant is
proposing a solid fence around the perimeter of the storage yard, so that it will be screened
from public view.

The landscaping requirement in the M zone is 5%. Over 10% landscaping is proposed on the
site. The landscaping along both frontages must also meet the High-Image Arterial
Landscaping requirements found in 7-10-103.

No signage is being proposed with this application. Any future signage on the property or
building will need to comply with Title 11.

Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any issues raised at the public hearing and
the following conditions.

    1.   The building and site shall be constructed per the approved plans.
    2.   Both buildings shall meet the Commercial Design Standards outlined in 7-11-300P.
    3.   Parking on the site shall meet all the requirements outlined in 7-9.
    4.   Outside storage shall only occur in the designated area indicated on the site plan. It
         shall be paved with concrete or asphalt and shall be screened from public view. Due
         to the distance of the storage area from the rights-of-way, a masonry wall is not
         required for screening.
    5.   The landscaping shall be completed in accordance with applicable standards in the
         West Valley City Municipal Code, including the 7-10-103 requirements along both
         frontages.
    6.   Signage shall comply with the West Valley City Code.
    7.   All requirements of effected departments and agencies shall be met.
    8.   Subject to review upon valid complaint.

Weir Minerals – Outside Storage
4927 West 2400 South
M zone, 17.32 acres

The applicant, Weir Minerals, is requesting a conditional use amendment for outside storage at
4927 West 2400 South in Building 3 of the Lake Park Commerce Center. The zoning for this
property is M, Manufacturing and the General Plan designation is Light Manufacturing. The
surrounding zones are M, Manufacturing to the north, east and west and the zone across the Riter
canal to the south is A2, Large Lot Residential. The surrounding uses include additional Lake
Park Commerce Center buildings to the east and west, H&E Equipment and Wheeler Machinery
to the north and vacant land to the south across the Riter Canal.
This request is for approval of outside storage for Weir Minerals, which is a mining technology
company. The tenant occupies the west half of the building so the storage area will be located to
the south of their lease space behind building 3.

The material to be stored will consist of pump components, pump assemblies, hoses and crated
items waiting for shipment. The items will be rotated based off shipments and orders and are not
intended to be material that is stored outside permanently. There will be no junk or discarded
materials stored outside. All of the products that are stored outside will be below 6’ in height and
will be screened by the existing landscaping, berm and 6’ masonry wall. There is one space that
will be surfaced with compacted road base as indicated on the plan and all other areas are paved.

This space was accounted for in the original design of building 3 so there will be no parking
displaced for this use. The landscaping on site will be slightly modified from the original plan
however, there is no reduction of setbacks or materials, plant material was just slightly
shifted/relocated. The masonry screen wall and berm on site will remain as originally designed.

The Fire Department has been contacted and the applicant is aware of the fire lane and gate
access and will comply with those standards.
