RESOLUTION NO. 2023-06-02                                                   DATE: June 6, 2023

       A RESOLUTION OF THE COPPERTON METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL
       AMENDING THE 2023 COPPERTON FEE SCHEDULE TO INCLUDE CIVIL
       PENALTIES FOR ZONING VIOLATIONS

                                          RECITALS

WHEREAS, the Copperton Metro Township (“Copperton”) inherited the version of Salt Lake
County’s Code that was in existence on the date of Copperton’s incorporation in 2017; and

WHEREAS, as a result, Section 19.94.070 of the Copperton Metro Township Code (the “Code”)

                                              3
DATE            TUESDAY                               JUNE                                    6, 2023

set forth the civil penalties for zoning violations (the “Civil Penalties”) within the Code rather than
within the Copperton Fee Schedule; and

 WHEREAS, as part of Copperton’s recodification of its Code, the Copperton Council (the
“Council”) desires to move the Civil Penalties from Title 19 of the Code to the Copperton Fee
Schedule; and

WHEREAS, when the Council adopted the 2023 Copperton Fee Schedule on December 21,
2022, the revisions to Title 19 were still in process and, as a result, the Council adopted the fee
schedule subject to the civil penalties remaining in Title 19 until such time as Title 19 could be
restated to remove the Civil Penalties; and

WHEREAS, Copperton has adopted a final version of Title 19 that does not include the Civil
Penalties; and

WHEREAS, because Title 19 no longer includes the Civil Penalties, the Council desires to amend
the 2023 Fee Schedule to include the Civil Penalties that were previously set forth in Section
19.94.070.

NOW, THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED BY THE COPPERTON METRO TOWNSHIP
COUNCIL that effectively immediately:

1. The attached amended 2023 fee schedule is adopted: and

2. The previous 2023 fee schedule shall be replaced in its entirely by the attached amended 2023
fee schedule; and

3. Copperton staff are instructed to file this resolution and the attached amended fee schedule
with the Copperton Metro Township Clerk; and

4. Staff are instructed to post this resolution and the attached amended 2023 fee schedule to the
Copperton Metro Township website and to the “Municipal Resolutions Book” within Copperton’s
Municode page; and

5. The 2023 fee schedule will go into effect on the date this resolution is approved and adopted.

APPROVED and ADOPTED this 6th day of June, 2023.

Copperton Law Enforcement Services

               Mayor Clayton stated he and Nathan Bracken recently met with Salt Lake County
Mayor Jennifer Wilson, her staff, Salt Lake County Sheriff Rosie Rivera, Brighton Mayor Dan
Knopp, all the other township mayors, and legal counsel. The metro township mayors wanted to
know what the intention was of Salt Lake County regarding law enforcement services. Statutorily,
the County cannot leave Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA), but the
metro townships can. Mayor Wilson reassured the township mayors that she has no intention, in
the short term, to abandon SLVLESA.

Mayor Clayton’s interpretation following the meeting was that the County and Sheriff’s office want
to create a new law enforcement agency to replace Unified Police Department (UPD), and that
the metro townships could contract to receive those services, which would be severable, as there
would not be an interlocal agreement. There would not be a board, but there might be an advisory
board. Mayor Clayton explained to Sheriff Rivera and Catherine Kanter, Salt Lake County Deputy
Mayor of Regional Operations, that he would prefer to have a menu of services from which to
choose.

Mayor Clayton also met with Mayor Dirk Burton to see if West Jordan City would be open to
contracting for law enforcement, justice court, and animal services. Mayor Burton was open to
this possibility. Mayor Clayton also has similar meetings planed with Herriman Mayor Lorin
Palmer, Riverton Mayor Trent Skaggs, and South Jordan Mayor Dawn Ramsey.

                Nathan Bracken stated since the meeting with Mayor Wilson, he wants to make
sure all the metro township mayors are unified in their resolve to stay with SLVLESA for now and
to run the proposed legislation to become cities/towns. Secondly, he would lie to hire a facilitator
to help with this discussion because none of the mayors or city attorneys can take on this roll, nor
can Mayor Wilson. Thirdly, he would like Copperton Metro Township to be involved in the Utah
Association of Special Districts (UASD). The metro townships are willing to discuss costs but are
concerned about using the term “feasibility study” because it might cause confusion. A feasibility
study would not be legally required in this circumstance.

               Council Member Olsen stated he did not have any updates since the last
meeting.

              Mayor Clayton extended an invitation to up to two Council members to meet with
him and Mayor Wilson at 11:00 at the County Government Center that coming Friday since this
would not implement a quorum.
