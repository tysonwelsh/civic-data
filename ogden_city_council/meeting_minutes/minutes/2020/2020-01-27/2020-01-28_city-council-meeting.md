     Proposed amendment to the Lynn Community Plan
               Council Deputy Director Symes explained a petition has been filed by Shawn Strong to amend the Lynn Community
     Plan to allow either R-5 orR-3 zoning on approximately eight acres of property at 140-152 West 2nd Street. The Lynn Community
     Plan currently allows for the R-3/CO, P-I/CO or C-1/CO zoning options on the property directly adjacent to the corner 2nd Street
     and Wall Avenue but restricts the property to the west to low density residential. Mr. Strong has acquired the property on the
     corner which recently received approval for a townhome project under the R-3/CO zoning and is interested in acquiring the
     additional eight acres to the west. The petition to amend the community plan would allow expansion of an updated multi-family
     project. The initial petition requested the option of the R-5 zoning but was updated to include either the R-5 zoning or the R-3
     zoning with a conditional overlay approval. The Planning Commission reviewed both options and is recommending denial of the
     petition for either the R-5 or R-3 option. Since the Planning Commission forwarded a recommendation of denial, per standard
     practice no ordinance approving the community plan amendment has been drafted by the City's legal department. If the Council
     wishes to consider the proposed amendment, an ordinance will need to be drafted. Once drafted, the item would then receive
     proper notice and would be placed on a future regular Council meeting agenda for consideration. If the Council does not wish to
     consider the amendment, the item will be placed on a future regular Council meeting agenda and the Council may vote to uphold
     the Commission's recommendation thereby denying the petition. If in the process ofreviewing the petition at the regular meeting,
     the Council wishes to consider the amendment, the Council will need to table or extend the consideration of the item and give
     direction that an ordinance be drafted for consideration at a future meeting.

more dense zoning designation for property that will directly abut single family uses and open space. Mr. Montgomery stated the
Planning Commission recommended denial of the R-5 zone because they felt that a stepped-up density from the corner to the
single-family properties to the west was unreasonable. The R-3 zone does allow multi family development, but at a lower density

petition to change the Community Plan and zoning designation. Mr. Montgomery stated that the residents living on 2 nd Street

However, staff has determined that the traffic increase will not be dramatic and the existing roads can handle any additional
traffic.

historical aspects of the area. Mr. Montgomery stated that Planning staff has been working with property owners on 2 nd Street for
several years with regard to historic preservation; there is not enough historical continuity to justify the creation of a historic
district.

Arts Ordinance Amendment
         Council Policy Analyst Mabey explained Council Staff received a request from City Administration to ainend the City
ordinance relative to project support grant funding to no longer require non-profit status for project support arts grant recipients.
         Arts Coordinator Buckley explained City ordinance currently provides that two types of arts grants can be awarded to
support and promote arts development in Ogden.

             Minutes of Work Session of Council of Ogden City, Utah, January 28, 2020 Page 29


                  1.    General Support Grants: These grants are designed to provide general support for organizations who have a
                        significant focus on arts programming. These grants are only available to non-profit organizations who have
                        their principal place of business in Ogden City.
                   2. Project Support Grants: These grants may be awarded to individuals or organizations. Currently, an
                        organization receiving a project support grant must either be a 501 ( c)(3) nonprofit corporation or have a Utah
                        nonprofit charter. Special Arts Grants also fit within this category, per Ogden Code Ann. § 4- 1C-8(D-2), and
                        are considered separately from the regular grant cycle.
              All awarded arts grants require City Council approval and the completion of a study showing a benefit to the City, per
    Utah Code§ 10-8-2. Recipients of any arts grants are required to enter into an agreement with the City to use grant monies for the
    specific purpose that the grant has been awarded. The City must receive equal value in return for any grant expenditure, whether
    measured in terms of tangible or intangible benefits. Administration proposes the Council consider an ordinance amendment to no
    longer require non-profit status for an organization to receive City arts grants for project suppmt. This proposed change does not
    modify the non-profit requirement for general support arts grants.
              The Council discussed the types of individuals or entities that would be applying for project support grants and
    acknowledged that there may be organizations that could benefit from funding regardless of whether they are a nonprofit or for-
    profit entity. The Council concluded that the amendment may provide for greater inclusivity by giving all arts entities the ability to
    apply for funding. Ms. Mabey stated that the Arts Advisory Committee expressed concern about the fact that this proposed
    amendment was not submitted to them before being submitted to the Council; it is her understanding that this is a technical
    adjustment that does not require Advisory Committee input. Ms. Buckley agreed this is a staff recommended change and it is
    independent of the comprehensive arts ordinance amendments that were considered last year. She added that the amendment will
    ultimately broaden the applicant pool.
              In conclusion, Ms. Mabey summarized the technical ordinance language amendments and indicated that the approval
    process should not change dramatically; the Council will still have final approval authority for grant awards.
              Council member Lopez asked if culinary arts programs or events can apply for grant funding. Ms. Buckley stated
    culinary arts is not excluded from the ordinance and a culinary arts grant application would be considered ifit complies with grant
    program guidelines. This led to a brief discussion about the historical arts grant awards given by the City to community entities or
    events in the past.
              Ms. Mabey stated that the proposed timeline for this amendment culminates with Council action on February 11, 2020.
