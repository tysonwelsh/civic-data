1.      WELCOME, INTRODUCTION AND PRELIMINARY MATTERS

Vice Chair Harker called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. and welcomed those in attendance. A
roll call was conducted wherein all councilmembers were present except Chair Barbieri who was
excused.

        1.1      Opening Ceremonies – Pledge/Reverence – Council Member Harker (Opening
                 Ceremonies for November 2, 2022, to be arranged by Councilmember Burgess

Vice Chair Harker directed the Pledge of Allegiance and offered a reverence.

        1.2      Mayor’s Report

     Mayor Kristie Overson reported on the Utah League of Cities and Town’s Annual
Conference. She stated that she and Council Members Burgess and Knudsen attended, as did John
Hiskey. She found it to be very informative and a great opportunity to network with colleagues
who represented other municipalities.

Mayor Overson explained that the police department had recently spearheaded a two-night, multi-
agency operation to saturate high crime areas and conduct proactive law enforcement. She was
grateful to see various agencies work together for the good of the entire Salt Lake valley. The
mayor also noted the police officers were wearing pink shirts and badges in observance of breast
cancer awareness month.

Mayor Overson commented on the pancake breakfast hosted earlier in the month by the Unified
Fire Station No. 118. She expressed her sincere gratitude for all those who participated in the event
which fed more than 800 residents. She was delightfully surprised at the high turnout and looked
forward to repeating the event the following year.

Mayor Overson explained that after 35 years of making the best burger in the state as well as
amazing cookies, Bell’s 48th Street Deli had recently closed its doors for the last time. She thanked
Phyllis Kim for her many years of hard work and although she would be missed, the mayor wished
her good luck on her next venture.

City staff hosted the 12th Annual “Great American Tomato Festival” recently where employees
brought a variety of homegrown tomato dishes to share. She commented on the many dishes
presented and stated it was a lot of fun for everyone.

      The TRCC (Tourism, Recreation, Culture and Convention) Board had recommended
approval of a funding application the city had submitted for ten new pickle ball courts and also for
small restoration improvements at the historic museum. She was guardedly optimistic that the Salt
Lake County Council would approve the TRCC Board’s recommendation. An official decision
would be made in 4-6 weeks.

It was leaf bag time again in Taylorsville. The mayor expressed her appreciation to Wasatch Front
Waste and Recycling District for donating the bags to city residents. They were available for pick
up not only at city hall but also at the county library. Once filled with leaves, the bags should be
left at either Vista Park or at the softball complex on 2700 West.

Taylorsville resident Spencer Harrison had recently received the National Tenacious
Telecommunicator of the Year Award as a 911 Dispatch Supervisor working for the Summit
County Sheriff’s Office. Mayor Overson was happy to participate in recognizing him and being
part of his award presentation.

        1.3      Citizen Comments

      Vice Chair Harker invited any members of the audience to step forward and address the
council on the topic of their choosing.

Alex Prentice commented that she had been reviewing Taylorsville’s city code regarding animal
licensing exceptions and had found that the city was not compliant with the Americans with
Disabilities Act exemptions with respect to psychiatric service animals. She asked that the code
be amended to include such animals.

Vice Chair Harker introduced City Attorney Tracy Cowdell and asked him to comment. Mr.
Cowdell noted that clearly the ADA exceeded the City’s code. He stated Ms. Prentice was correct
about the law and indicated the city would review the code to see what changes were needed.

       3.2       Indigent Defense Report – Heidi Chamorro

      Mr. Cowdell introduced Heidi Chamorro who presented the Indigent Defense Report. Ms.
Chamorro explained that she worked for Stowell Crayk Law and briefly explained her background.
She enjoyed working on defending the indigent and reported that her firm’s caseload in
Taylorsville Justice Court was higher during that quarter than in the previous several quarters.
They had been appointed to 773 cases during the quarter. She noted that her office enjoyed
working with the prosecutors and felt that they were determined to understand the root of the
problem in each case rather than being focused on punishment alone. She spoke very highly of the
city’s justice court.

Vice Chair Harker and Councilmember Burgess were both pleased that the goal was to help people
with their specific needs.

Council Member Knudsen noted that the increase in cases that quarter was significant—over 30%.
He wondered if Ms. Chamorro had any thoughts regarding the cause of the increase. Ms. Chamorro
stated it was possible the courts were seeing more in-person cases than during the height of the
pandemic. It was also possible there were simply more people requesting and being granted a
public defender.
