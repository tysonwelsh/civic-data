PC 24-043 Rooftop Investments STR-CUP Amendment [Conditional Use Permit] Andrea
McCulloch/Rooftop Investments LLC, authorized agent/owner is requesting to amend their existing
conditional use permit for a short-term rental at 148 W 300 S to relocate the permit to use at the
property to the south at 154 West 300 South in the Traditional Neighborhood Residential (NR-6)
zone; TIN 02-344-0002 (Woodruff Neighborhood).

Staff: Aimee Egbert, Logan City Planner, reviewed the request to amend an existing Conditional Use
Permit (CUP) for a short-term rental (STR) at 148 West 300 South and move the permit to the
adjacent property to the south at 154 West 300 South, where a new home will be built. The new
single-family home will have three bedrooms and an unfinished basement with a separate outside
entrance from the back of the home. An attached train car on the west side of the home is where the
STR will be located. The STR sleeps up to four people and has a total occupancy of six. The existing
CUP at 148 West 300 South will need to be abandoned and moved to 154 West 300 South once a
certificate of occupancy is obtained for the home.

Proponent: Andrea McCulloch, the property owner, spoke about the renovations that have been
completed on the existing home that currently has the STR license and the plans for the new home.

Commissioner Guth asked about access to the new home. Andrea McCulloch said that a shared
driveway was included in the plat when the property was subdivided.

Commissioner Peterson asked about the STR proposed to be owner-occupied. A. McCulloch said
that she is not sure yet if the home will be owner-occupied or not.


Public: Joy Saddler who owns the property to the west asked if the train car is going to be the living
space, how many occupants it can have, and if the home to the north will remain as a short-term
rental as well.

Leyann Wilkins said she is concerned about how many people will be living in the home and where
they will park.

A. Egbert said that the STR will only be located within the train car and can fit up to 6 occupants.
The new home and STR are required to have three parking stalls (two for the residence and one for
the STR), which are being proposed in front of the garage. The existing home to the north will no
longer be able to operate as an STR once the new home and train car are built.

Commissioner Peterson questioned the required one parking stall per two bedrooms for an STR. A.
Egbert said the parking requirement for STRs changed with the recent code amendment from
requiring one parking stall per bedroom to one parking stall per two bedrooms.

PC 24-041 Willets Rezone [Zone Change] Jed Willets is requesting a zone change of a .24-acre
property located in the southwest corner of the Royal Point Subdivision at 1354 North 220 West
from Mixed Residential Low (MR-12) to Mixed Residential Medium (MR-20) to add a four-plex unit;
TIN 05-041-0056 (Bridger Neighborhood).

Staff: Russ Holley, City Planner, reviewed the request to rezone lot six of the Royal Pointe
Subdivision, a 1.80-acre property located at approximately 1360 North 200 West, from Medium
Residential Low (MR-12) to Mixed Residential Medium (MR-20). Jed Willets owns a .24 acre portion
of the larger 1.80 acre property originally described as Lot Six of Royal Pointe Subdivision.

R. Holley explained the history of the property: The seven existing 4 plex buildings (28 total units)
were originally constructed on Lot 6 of the Royal Pointe Subdivision in 1994/1995. Following the
construction of these buildings, the owners of Lot 6 illegally subdivided Lot 6 into eight (8) separate
parcels through the County without receiving property City permits or approvals. The eighth illegal
parcel (0.24 acres in the southwest corner of lot 6) was subsequently sold off and is not the subject
property for this request. In 2000, the zoning changed to MFH-14, allowing 14 units per acre, which
caused lot 6 to become “legally existing nonconforming” because it was developed at an overall
density of 15.5 units per acre with the existing, seven (7) permitted fourplexes. In 2013, the zoning
changed to MR-12, allowing 12 units per acre, and the property remained nonconforming.

The applicant (Jed Willets) purchased the .24 acre portion of lot six thinking he could build a duplex
because it was zoned MR-12. After discovering the illegal subdivision, the City rezoned the property
twice to MR-20 in 2016 and again in 2023 to allow the applicant to build a duplex. Both times, the


applicant failed to follow through on obtaining building permits and the construction of the duplex.
The most recent entitlement for the duplex, a Design Review Permit, was issued on March 9, 2023.
The applicant submitted a duplex building permit application in March of 2024 but did not request or
obtain an extension on the Design Review Permit (PC 23-015). The Logan City Building Department
canceled the building permit application in September of 2024, after six months and failure on the
applicant's part to finalize the building permit requirements and pay the application fee. The site was
rezoned back to MR-12 on July 18, 2023.

The applicant is now requesting a rezone to build a four-plex rather than a duplex. If rezoned to MR-
20, lot 6 could support 36 dwelling units. With seven existing fourplexes (28 units) that would allow
for an additional 12 units to be built. Staff is recommending denial of this rezone because the Bridger
Neighborhood has deliberately made numerous and consistent efforts to downzone remaining
developable areas into single-family zones, to better balance out the housing stock that currently has
a much higher concentration of multi-family as compared to other Logan City neighborhoods. Going
from a duplex to a fourplex is contrary to this neighborhood goal.

Commissioner Peterson clarified that if this rezone is approved, all of lot six would be rezoned, not
just the .24 acre portion. R. Holley said that is correct, the entire 1.8 acres (lot six) would be rezoned
if approved.

Commissioner Guth asked if the applicant could build a duplex as the property is currently zoned. R.
Holley said that the property is currently zoned MR-12, allowing a total of 21 units on the 1.8-acre
property. With the seven existing fourplexes, the property has 28 units creating a legally existing
nonconforming or grandfathered property. This is the reason the property has been rezoned twice
to MR-20 to allow the applicant to legally build a duplex.

Proponent: Jed Willets said his first permit expired due to COVID-19, causing him to work 14 hours
a day with UPS and therefore not having the time to complete the building permit. Mr. Willets spoke
about the second building permit review process and the application being unfairly expired by the
Building Department while his engineer was responding to all of the City’s comments on his building
plans. Mr. Willets questioned why lot two in the Royal Pointe Subdivision is currently zoned MR-20,
the need for a stormwater retention pond on his property for all of lot six, and density and parking in
other zoning.

Commissioner Peterson said it is difficult to recommend a rezoning without a specific plan in place
for the development.

Public: Jeannie Simmonds, City Council said she would like to see a timeline showing when the
rezones were approved, when the permits were issued, and when they expired.

Leann Wilkins, who owns a home on 1330 North, complained about the weeds that grow on the
property and explained her frustration with the City not enforcing on the weeds and with the proponent
for not developing the property as promised.

Commission: Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director said that the proposal is only for
a rezone. The applicant has had two opportunities to build a duplex. Design Review Permits are
good for one year and the applicant can request an extension for another year if more time is needed.
The most recent Design Review Permit expired in March of 2024 and the applicant was allowed
another six months to complete the building permit process. The applicant did not request an
extension prior to the date of permit expiration which was March 9, 2024. The Design Review Permit
issued for this project clearly stated that a building permit has to be issued before the Design Review
Permit expires.


R. Holley clarified that Lot 2 of the Royal Pointe Subdivision was rezoned to MR-20 so the property
owners could un-condominimize the property. The City will be rezoning the property back to MR-12.

Darren Farar, City Engineer said the proponent has had ample time to complete the conditions for a
duplex. The stormwater detention requirement for the Willets project was a standard requirement for
any development.

Commissioner Peterson said if the applicant is now requesting a four-plex they need to present the
rezone including a proposal for a four-plex. Commissioner McNamara said that this proposal has
gone through the approval process twice with a rezoning request and the plan for a duplex.
Commissioner Lewis agreed that there needs to be a formal design review submitted with the rezone.

Motion: Commissioner Peterson made a motion to forward a recommendation of denial to the
Municipal Council on a zone change for PC 24-041 Willets Rezone with the findings for denial as
listed below. Commissioner McNamara seconded the motion.

FINDINGS FOR DENIAL

1. The Logan City FLUP identifies the area as MR. The surrounding areas are zoned MR-12 and
this property should also be zoned MR-12 for consistency.

2. The Bridger Neighborhood was specifically downzoned to reduce the number of new multi-
family developments in order to better balance housing stock in this neighborhood as compared
to other Logan City neighborhoods.

3. The MR-12 zone would prevent future multi-family development as this 1.80-acre lot is already
above the maximum density of 12 units per acre.

Moved: E. Peterson Seconded: S. McNamara Approved: 4-0

Yea: Guth, Lewis, Peterson, McNamara Nay: Abstain:
