        1.1  Opening Ceremonies – Pledge/Reverence – Mayor Overson (Opening

Mayor Overson directed the Pledge of Allegiance before explaining that as she reflected upon what
was happening in the country, she wished to share the following from author Maryanne
Williamson:

        “Our deepest fear is not that we are inadequate. Our deepest fear is that we are powerful
        beyond measure. It is our light, not our darkness that most frightens us. We ask ourselves
        ‘Who am I to be brilliant, gorgeous, talented, fabulous?’ Actually, who are you not to be?
        Your playing small does not serve the world. There is nothing enlightened about shrinking
        so that other people won’t feel insecure around you. We are all meant to shine as children
        do. We were born to make manifest the glory that is within us. It is not just in some of us,
        it is in everyone. As we let our own light shine, we unconsciously give other people
        permission to do the same. As we are liberated from our own fear, our presence
        automatically liberates others.”

        1.2      Mayor’s Report

       Mayor Kristie Overson reported on various construction projects taking place in the city. It
was exciting that the 4800 South bridge was finally being built. It would be very similar to the one
that had been removed for safety reasons.

The pickleball courts at Mill Race Park had been repainted and re-oriented so that players would
no longer need to face the sun.

Mayor Overson had recently stopped by the museum and noticed that work was being done on the
roof line of the museum. She was grateful that there was progress on the site.

Many things were happening in Taylorsville, and it was exciting to see so many things that were
planned long ago finally come to fruition. Still, it was also challenging because of the current
shortages of both materials and labor.

The Mayor reminded those gathered that it had been a year since the ribbon-cutting had been held
for the Mid Valley Performing Arts Center. There had been many wonderful performances held
since then. Earlier in the week city administration had met with Salt Lake County representatives

to discuss what was working well with the partnership and what could be improved upon. She
appreciated the good relationship between the two entities.

In her capacity as a member of the Homeless Overflow Shelter Subcommittee, Mayor Overson
had recently participated in the ranking of the submittals that all municipalities had recently
provided for potential overflow shelter sites. They were ranked according to specific criteria, and
Taylorsville’s sites had landed roughly in the middle of the pack. The next step was for the
committee members to contact property owners of the sites. The committee would meet again soon
and hoped to have a recommendation for the August Council of Government’s meeting. The
specific site locations were not yet public knowledge.

The Tourism, Recreation Culture and Convention (TRCC) board had met a few weeks before and
the grant application window was now open. Taylorsville would apply for some of these funds and
the administration was currently narrowing the options down.

ChamberWest had held its annual golf tournament earlier that day. The Mayor said it was a great
opportunity to meet with local business representatives and she appreciated the value that
ChamberWest added to Taylorsville.

Starry Nights at the Plaza was proving to be a successful new tradition. She expressed her great
appreciation to the Arts Council for their presentations of Willy Wonka Jr. and Taylorsville’s Got
Talent in the outdoor amphitheater. The food trucks would return the following Saturday and a
band would play to go along with the movie that would be aired.

Taylorsville had a new Plaza +Art program and soon there would be additional pedestals placed
so more art could be displayed at Centennial Plaza. Mayor Overson thanked the Council for
approving the funding that would make that possible. The selection committee had chosen new
pieces which would be unveiled on August 27th, coinciding with the last Starry Night event of the
season.

The Mayor expressed her appreciation to Boy Scout Troop #117 who traded work at the museum’s
Dairy store in exchange for being allowed to hold their meetings there. Their most recent project
was to repaint fire hydrants in the Ivory Highlands area.

The Youth Council had recently repainted the gateway sign on 4700 South. She appreciated the
Youth Council for being so civic-minded and doing so much for the City. The project was not
quite complete but was nearly so.

The Mayor then read a letter from a citizen who had written to compliment Wes Butterfield for
the great way he responded to a citizen concern and had come up with a plan of action to fix a
problem.

        5.1      Ordinance No. 22-07 – An Ordinance of the City of Taylorsville Approving a
                 Zoning Map Amendment for the Property Located at 4645 South 1175 West
                 from Single-Family Residential R-1-10 to R-1-7.

      Community/Economic Development Director Wayne Harper explained the zoning change
the applicant sought. He displayed a vicinity map which highlighted the subject property, a
“zoomed in” version of the map, and several photos of the property from different vantage points.
He displayed the current zoning map which depicted an R-1-20 zone directly west of the subject
property as well as R-1-5 to the east. The properties directly north and south of the subject property
were zoned just as it currently was, which was R-1-10. There was also a golf course south and
southwest of the subject property. He explained that the General Plan designated the area as “low
density.” If the rezone proposal was approved, the applicant’s intent was to create a flag lot to the
east of the property, maintain the existing house and build an additional out-building to the south
of that.

As a reminder, Mr. Harper pointed out that two years before, the applicant had sought a rezone of
the same property to R-1-5. The Planning Commission had forwarded a unanimous negative
recommendation to the City Council who ultimately voted 4-1 to deny the application.

Regarding the current application, a public hearing had been held before the Planning Commission
on May 24th and public comment involved neighborhood opposition dealing with concerns over
establishing a precedent for more housing in the area, the current state of the property, additional
traffic and safety concerns, and a suggestion that the proposed flag lot would detract from the
existent character of the neighborhood. The Planning Commission had again forwarded a
unanimous negative recommendation to the City Council. Mr. Harper explained that some staff
members were comfortable with the new application while others were not. This decision was a
legislative decision for the City Council to make.

Chair Barbieri invited the applicant to address the Council about her application. Lana Bryner
agreed that she had previously applied for a rezone to R-1-5 which had followed the General Plan.
Since her initial application was denied, a different developer in the same neighborhood submitted
a rezone application for R-1-6 and R-1-8 and that application had received unanimous approval by
the Planning Commission. That approval had prompted her to submit a new application for R-1-
7, assuming it would be approved since it fell in the middle of the two zoning levels that were
approved for the other developer. She was quite surprised when the Planning Commission denied
her second application. She had tried to understand how her application differed from the ones that
were approved by the other developer and could not come up with an answer. She felt that both
were in a low-density residential area, both requested similar zoning, and both requests were
opposed by the neighbors for the same reasons. She was only proposing to add one house, so she
did not feel a concern about increased traffic was justified. Neighbors complained that her property
was an eyesore. She was proposing to make improvements which would directly address that
problem. Ms. Bryner felt that a precedent for similar zoning had already been set so she felt that
to claim that approving her application would set a new precedent was baseless.

Councilmember Harker explained that she had read all the minutes from the previous application,
and she had voted in favor of it the first time. While she had a great deal of respect for the Planning
Commission and the work that the commissioners did, she also understood the applicants plea for
approval. If the property was currently an eye sore, why not support a plan to change that? She
appreciated that the neighbors came to voice their concerns, but she did not share them. She wasn’t
sure she could vote to deny the application.

Councilmember Burgess commented that there had not yet been an improvement on the exterior
of the current building, and he asked if such improvements were planned. The applicant responded
that in fact she had painted the house, replaced the doors and some of the windows. She said she
had also done work on the interior of the home such as replacing the heating and electrical systems.
The home was older, and she did not intend to do much more to it although she hoped to demolition
an existing garage, level the backyard and install new landscaping including a sprinkler system.
Additionally, she intended to get rid of the trees on the north side of the property and build a two-
car garage for the existing home.

Chair Barbieri stated that she was on the Planning Commission when Ms. Bryner’s first application
went before that body. While she was sympathetic to property owner rights, she was also
sympathetic to neighborhoods that surrounded those properties. She had read the minutes of the
Planning Commissions review of the current application and agreed with their determination that
approval of Ms. Bryner’s application would not be appropriate.

Councilmember Harker stated that the applicant had a good point that the City had changed the
zoning just down the street from her and the precedent had already been set.

A voice from the audience exclaimed that the property had been an eyesore up until earlier in the

6.      FINANCIAL MATTERS

        6.1      Resolution No. 22-17 A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville Determining the
                 Certified Tax Rate Upon all Real and Personal Property within the City for
                 the Fiscal Year beginning July 1, 2022 and ending June 30, 2023 – Scott
                 Harrington

      Assistant City Administrator and Chief Financial Officer Scott Harrington explained that the
resolution now before the Council was to adopt a proposed certified tax rate. The rate needed to
be adopted by June 22nd at the latest. It was proposed a 10% property tax increase was needed. He
gave a brief overview of the Truth in Taxation process, explaining that the proposed certified tax
rate first needed to be adopted, after which tax notices would be sent to residents (in early July)
which would show a proposed tax rate for Taylorsville as well as the date of a Truth in Taxation
hearing which he anticipated to be August 16, 2022. The hearing date would also be noticed
publicly in several places and after the public hearing took place, the Council could choose to
adopt the certified tax rate. A 10% tax hike would yield approximately $338,000 in needed revenue
for the city. He displayed an example of the public notice which showed that the average value of
a house in Taylorsville was $432,000 and that a 10.03% increase would equate to an addition of
$16.16 of taxes owed by the homeowner of such a residence per year. A business worth the same
amount would cost the owner $29.37 more per year.
