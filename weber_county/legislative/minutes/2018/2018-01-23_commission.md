                                                      MINUTES
                                          WEBER COUNTY COMMISSION
                                         Tuesday, January 23, 2018 - 10:00 a.m.
                                Commission Chambers, 2380 Washington Blvd., Ogden, Utah
 In accordance with the requirements of Utah Code Annotated Section 52-4-203, the County Clerk records in the minutes the names of all persons who
 appear and speak at a County Commission meeting and the substance “in brief” of their comments. Such statements may include opinion or purported
 facts. The County does not verify the accuracy or truth of any statement but includes it as part of the record pursuant to State law.

OTHER STAFF PRESENT: Ricky D. Hatch, County Clerk/Auditor; Christopher Crockett, Deputy County Attorney;
and Fátima Fernelius, of the Clerk/Auditor’s Office, who took minutes.

D. THOUGHT OF THE DAY – Chair Harvey
E. PRESENTATION ON THE ANNUAL PLANNING DIVISION REPORT.
    Rick Grover, County Planning Division Director, gave an overview of 2017 that included completing the
    Resource Management Plan and LIDAR maps, the effects of Senate Bill 140 (that provided options to eliminate
    county islands & impacted almost 400 lots), there was considerable growth at Powder Mountain, Western Weber
    County had the first two PRUDs and a few multiple cluster subdivisions, and there was a phenomenal amount
    of ordinance revisions. He gave statistics for design reviews, zoning petitions, land use permits, building
    permits, and code enforcement. With county department/division report highlights an employee is selected to
    use the “Stall of Fame” for one month who has done excellent work and Mr. Grover recommended Steve Burton.

G. ACTION ITEMS:
    1. RESOLUTION APPOINTING MEMBERS TO THE POWDER MOUNTAIN WATER AND SEWER DISTRICT –

         Commissioner Ebert moved to adopt Resolution 6-2018 appointing Mark Schroetel (through 12/31/2020),
         Robert Behrendt and Gary Urdang (through 12/31/2021) to the Powder Mountain Water and Sewer District;

    2. APPROVAL OF THE WEBER COUNTY INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY POLICY 17.1, ACCEPTABLE
       COMPUTER/INTERNET USE POLICY.
         Quinn Fowers, County Information Technology Department Director, presented this revision to reflect

         Commissioner Ebert moved to approve the Weber County Information Technology Policy 17.1, Acceptable
         Computer/Internet Use Policy; Commissioner Gibson seconded.
         Commissioner Gibson – aye; Commissioner Ebert – aye; Chair Harvey - aye
    3. CONTRACT WITH STANLEY CONSULTANTS FOR CONSTRUCTION PROJECT REVIEW.
         Ricky Hatch, County Clerk/Auditor, stated that over the past few years the county has been involved in
         significant road construction projects. The internal auditor, who has conducted some of the audits,
         recommended to the Audit Committee the hiring of an outside consultant with industry expertise. With the
         Audit Committee’s recommendation, Request for Proposals were issued and Stanley Consultants was
         selected. The first review is for a limited scope audit for the 12th Street project and is to establish a plan
         moving forward with potential future audits.
         Commissioner Ebert moved to approve the contract with Stanley Consultants for construction project
         review; Commissioner Gibson seconded.
         Commissioner Gibson – aye; Commissioner Ebert – aye; Chair Harvey - aye
    4. REQUEST FOR RESOLUTION APPROVING WEBER AREA COUNCIL OF GOVERNMENTS RECOMMENDATION OF
       TOBY MILESKI AS NEW REPRESENTATIVE ON UTAH TRANSIT AUTHORITY'S BOARD OF TRUSTEES.
         Christopher Crockett, Deputy County Attorney, stated that the County Attorney’s Office was recommending
         holding this item in order to follow the procedural requirements under the appointment statute—including
         receiving a signed document by the municipalities within the voting district (which includes with Weber
         County, Willard, Perry and Brigham cities of Box Elder). This item was placed on the agenda after receiving
         notice that Mayor Taylor, a UTA board member, is being deployed to serve our country. However, before
         an appointment is made there has to be a vacancy that is created in one of two ways: either a position is
         recalled by the appointing body or by a voluntary resignation. Subsequently, the same original appointment
         procedure is to follow by a simple majority of the executive officers. Once the signed document is received,
         the item can move forward. Commissioner Gibson expressed the urgency of moving forward with this item,
         noting that there is a meeting tomorrow, and that our community does not have current representation.
    5. REQUEST TO ALLOCATE $10,000 IN COUNTY FUNDS TO THE COMMUNITY FOUNDATION OF OGDEN VALLEY.
         Commissioner Ebert noted that this was voted on this recently and this is to fulfill a requirement that the
         Commission agrees when giving money to a nonprofit that the entity contributes to the safety, health, moral
         wellbeing, convenience of the county residents, etc. He read the Foundation’s statement of their many
         diverse donations over the past years including to various youth sports, Weber Pathways, school enrichment
         programs, retreats for deployed military families, money to emergency responders, and to the arts and music.
         Commissioner Ebert moved to approve the request to allocate $10,000 in County funds to the Community
         Foundation of Ogden Valley; Commissioner Gibson seconded.
         Commissioner Gibson – aye; Commissioner Ebert – aye; Chair Harvey - aye
H. PUBLIC COMMENTS:
Lori Brinkerhoff, Hooper City councilmember, said that today’s agenda items lead to the hot topic of transportation
and she supports agenda item G.3. She referred to the Planning Division’s report and of the growth, which leads to
demands for infrastructure and quality transportation. She encouraged the county to pursue the BRT, which was
discussed in WACOG’s December meeting, where there was a feeling that UTA needed restructuring. All projects
need accountability. She felt the comments were inappropriate from a State department head in the Standard
Examiner regarding a current investigation. She encouraged elected officials’ attendance at the Legislature.
Toby Mileski, of Pleasant View City, had been looking forward to attending tomorrow’s UTA meeting, addressing
the BRT and obtaining a cost analysis. He is working with mayors to release transportation/right-of-way funds.
Commissioner Ebert encouraged Mr. Mileski to attend the UTA meetings and said that the Commission has had
numerous meetings with UTA, which has come to the table on the BRT. Ricky Hatch, County Clerk/Auditor,
reported that his office contacted UTA asking for documentation on the amount of money spent in Weber County.
That is now being reconciled, including Prop 1 funds. UTA created an equity council tasked with the protocol for
allocated funds through UTA for equity between revenues/expenditures. Commissioner Ebert said that there has
been talk of disparate treatment in funding to counties but the numbers are not showing that. Moving forward there
needs to be accountability/scrutiny and due diligence done into UTA spending.
