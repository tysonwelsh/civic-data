2.         A resolution imposing fire restrictions due to hazardous environmental conditions. (22-
           048) (19:23)

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Resolution 2022-27, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Chief Jim Miguel requested approval of fire restrictions which went into effect May 18, 2022. He
explained that the Fire Marshall has the ability for a 30-day suspension, but anything longer than 30
days requires council approval. He also addressed the areas where this would take effect.

Councilor Whipple asked about the expiration date as the resolution states it would remain in effect
until it is rescinded in writing.

Chief Miguel responded that they expect the resolution to expire when snow falls. He mentioned the
city would follow the National Forrest Service recommendations.

Chair Hoban opened public comment. With none, he closed public comment.

With no other council discussion, Chair Hoban called for a vote.

      Vote:        The motion was approved 5:0 with Councilors Fillmore, Handley, Hoban, MacKay, and
                   Whipple in favor. Councilors Ellsworth and Shipley excused.

8.         A public hearing for a budget for Provo City Corporation for the Fiscal Year Beginning July
           1, 2022 and Ending June 30, 2023. (22-016) (49:09)

Ms. Zarbock presented and shared this would be the first of two public hearings.

John Magness, Interim Council Executive Director, explained that this would be the appropriate time to
do a truth in taxation. He said a motion to adopt the reconciliation would need to take place prior to
truth in taxation for suggested changes to be adopted with the tentative budget.

Chair Hoban opened public hearing.

Melanie McCoard, of Provo, shared her thoughts on the need for a Budget Advisory Committee.

With no other comments, Chair Hoban closed the public hearing.

Councilor Handley thanked the finance staff for their work on the budget. He shared his concern for the
reliance on sales tax revenues. He said he thinks the city is losing its buying power over time and asked
to hear other council members thoughts on entering a truth in taxation hearing.

Councilor Fillmore agreed with Councilor Handley. He said the council has a heavy responsibility to
assure the citizens of a viable, long-term economic base to be able to continue to provide the city
services we are known for.

Councilor MacKay voiced all the extra funds the city has received this year due to the pandemic. She
mentioned with the federal aid, people spent more which allowed us more sales taxes. She expressed

Councilor Ellsworth clarified that the motion would be specifically for inflation and not for a desired
outcome.

Councilor Fillmore agreed and added that some have expressed their desire for some funds to be
directed in fair measure to public safety though it is not part of the motion.

Councilor Ellsworth said she feels like more research should be done prior to establishing a percentage
based on need.

Mr. Magness shared that by statute to enter truth in taxation, the council must declare a maximum
percentage.

Councilor Handley clarified information presented.

Councilor Ellsworth shared the public might respond better to an outcome driven percentage versus
tying it solely to inflation.

Councilor Fillmore responded that the intent of his motion is to tie the increase to inflation.

Councilor Whipple shared why she believes tying the increase to inflation would be more beneficial than
a specific outcome. She also clarified which property taxes would be increased.

Councilor Handley said it would be helpful to give the public an idea with how the city will use the funds
from the property tax increase.

Councilor Ellsworth said it will be harder to ask the citizens for money without a purpose or plan.

Councilor Whipple suggested that after we have firm numbers, we could give the public an idea of what
the funds will be used for.

Councilor Handley agreed with Councilor Whipple.

Councilor Fillmore emphasized that no councilmember is excited to increase property taxes. He also
echoed Councilor Whipple’s earlier statements that only the city’s portion of property taxes would be
increased.

Councilor Ellsworth stated the council should do their homework and know exactly what the funds will
be used for.

Councilor Handley shared his desire to use the money for public safety, specifically the funding for
another Police Officer.

Councilor Fillmore said the council has received many briefings on the needs of the Police Department.

Councilor Ellsworth said a number is needed and the taxes should be raised according to that number.
