for temporary structures to be used for film, television, or photography on a per 
request basis; 7600 Brown’s Canyon Road; Parcel SS-79; James Rogers, applicant 
– Sean Lewis, County Planner

Planner Lewis stated Staff issued a short-term temporary use permit (TUP) on July 22, 
2016 for this project.  The Code states that any use, not listed in the Use Chart, is eligible 
for a TUP.  He said that the short-term TUP allows for a use to occur up to 16 days in a 
60 day period.  This particular TUP expires on Saturday, August 6, 2016.  The project is 
located in Brown’s Canyon (behind the white barn) on property owned by Garff Rogers 
Ranch, LLC.  Ben Rogers and Forest Ringwood were in attendance representing the 
owners and applicants.   
 
Planner Lewis said the application is to establish a movie set with the façade of an old 
western main street.  The applicant would like to make this a permanent fixture, but it 
is currently not allowed in the Code; therefore, they have applied for a long-term TUP to 
permit the site for one year.  The goal is to come back before this body within the year’s 
time with an application to provide Code amendments that would allow this type of 
setup.  At that point, the movie set could be established on a permanent basis.   
 
Planner Lewis said some of the structures will have foundations and will be more 
permanent.  This will allow for some interior filming.  This is done with the 
understanding that this application could be denied at any point along the way.  If that 
should happen, the structures would come down and the site would be returned to an 
equivalent condition.     
 
Planner Lewis said the Findings of Fact and Conditions of Approval are close to what 
Staff used to grant the short-term TUP, with the changes being the pertinent dates.  The 
hours of operation will be 8:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m.  All filming will take place within 300 
feet of the "town center."  County regulations in regards to filming must be complied 
with.

Commissioner Ure asked if the neighboring gravel pits could have a negative effect on 
filming.  Mr. Rogers said there is a half-mile in all directions to any other structures.  He 
added that 99% of the shooting will take place during the specified hours.  Planner 
Lewis displayed some pictures taken from Brown’s Canyon Road of the current project.   
 
Commissioner Wharton said his concern is when a neighboring property owner 
decides to build a structure in the background that would present a challenge for 
filming.  Mr. Rogers said there are different ways to handle this.  A green screen could 
go up or a tree be planted.   
 
Mr. Rogers said the ranch has to have sources of revenue to pay for itself.  It is hoped 
that this will be another stream of revenue.  An advantage of the ranch as a movie set is 
that it has over 10 square miles of land and 1,500 feet of elevation change.  There are 
aspen groves and big vistas.  It gives a lot of variety for someone who wants to shoot a 
western.  Commissioner Henrie said the application stated that all filming will be 
within 300 feet of the site.  Mr. Rogers said that anything beyond that would require a 
permit.   
 
Mr. Rogers said the Governor’s office is excited about this project.  They receive about 
10 requests a year for a western town.  He listed some missed opportunities because of 
not having a western town.  The only western town in the State is located in Kanosh, 
which is where John Wayne used to shoot.  The only hotel in that area was built in 1961.   
 
Mr. Rogers said one of the advantages of this location is that the actors could fly in from 
Las Angeles at 6:00 a.m. and be on set at 11:00 a.m.  An alternative is they could stay at 
close hotels.  In that case, they could be on set within 30 minutes.       
 
Commissioner Henrie asked Planner Lewis to explain the film permit.  Planner Lewis 
said all filming permits are processed through the Clerk’s Office and the Special Events 
office.  This allows the affected County offices to be aware that filming is taking place.

The Health Department has an interest when filming occurs because there is catering.  
It gives Planning and Engineering a chance to look at the land use.  It gives the Sherriff’s 
Department an opportunity to look at the traffic or if they need to block a road.  The

Commissioner Willoughby asked if this is the same process they would have to go

is, but what is of concern to the Commission is the approval of a long-term TUP for the

Commissioner Henrie asked if parking is contained within the designated 300 feet.  Is 
there a defined parking area?  Mr. Rogers said there is a gravel road base which has 
parking for about 200-300 cars.  This area is located within the 300 feet.  
Commissioner Wharton asked if there will be complete buildings.  Planner Lewis said 
there will.  He showed a picture of the site as of one hour before the meeting.   
 
The public hearing was opened.   
 
Ben Rogers gave the background of the project.  It started with a phone call from the 
Utah Film Commission.  They told him there is a movie set that needs to be removed or 
demolished at the location where the prison is going to be built.  The movie set cost 
about $600,000 to construct.  They offered to give it to him.    
 
Mr. Rogers said he realized that accepting this gift would mean he had to take it down 
and move it.  That meant he had to hire someone to be a foreman and a number of 
people to help take it down.    
 
Mr. Rogers said there will be a couple of permanent structures with interior spaces.  
They will need to have a bar, a saloon, a jail, and the other things associated with a 
western town.  The other structures would be built either on sleds, or like a tough shed.

Mr. Rogers said they plan on investing about $200,000 to $300,000.  They are hoping to 
work with Summit County to make this permanent.  The Governor’s office has been 
very enthusiastic about this project.  They volunteered to come, but he told them he 
didn’t think the one-year permit would be difficult.  He doesn’t think this would have a

Vice-Chair Clyde said while the Commission appreciates their intent, it needs to be 
brutally clear that all they are receiving at this meeting, if approved, is a one year TUP.  
Commissioner Henrie asked if the County would have any input on the kind of films 
being made.  It is portrayed as a western town.  Planner Lewis said the County would 
not.  The application is portrayed as a movie set.   
 
Mr. Rogers said he thinks it will be used as a western town.  He said the Park City movie 
lot is also very excited about this.  They don’t and cannot have a back lot.  That creates a 
huge deficiency for them.  This location is about 6 to 10 minutes away from the Park 
City movie studios.  This would give them the back lot they need.     
 
Commissioner Henrie asked how things like explosions, shootings, and fire would be 
handled.  Planner Lewis said loud noises would fall under the noise ordinance.  It would 
have to be no more than the allowed decibels at the property line at certain times of 
day.  They would have to work with the Fire Marshall if there were explosions or fires.  
Those kinds of issues would be handled through other processes.  He encouraged the 
Commission to not think about these types of events.  What they are approving is the 
land use for a movie set.  Mr. Rogers said the concerns that have been expressed are 
also the concerns they have had as owners of a functioning ranch.  They don’t want to 
have the ranch catch fire.

FINDINGS OF FACT:

1. The applicant proposes to build several movie set structures on Parcel SS-79.    
2. Garff Rogers Ranch LLC is the listed fee title owner of Parcel SS-79. 
3. Parcel SS-79 is 125.90 acres in size. 
4. Parcel SS-79 is located at 7600 Browns Canyon Road. 
5. Parcel SS-79 is located in the Agriculture Grazing 100 zoning district. 
6. Section 11-3-13 of the Eastern Summit County Development Code states: “Any 
use not listed as an allowed use or a conditional use within a zone district 
may be considered as a temporary use pursuant to and in accordance with 
the provisions of Section 11-4-13 of this title.”   
7. Outdoor Movie Set (or similar use) is not listed as a use in the chart of 
allowed and permitted uses as found in section 11-3-14 of the Eastern 
Summit County Development Code.   
8. The use is proposed for a period of one year, beginning on August 8, 2016. 
9. The applicant proposes to limit hours of operation at the site from 8:00 a.m. 
to 8:00 p.m.  
10. Filming activity will take place within 300 feet of the physical set. 
11. Film companies which would use the site are required to obtain Summit 
County Film Permits in advance of filming activities.   
12. Any additional equipment, set pieces, dressing room trailers, catering 
services, etc. are proposed to remain within the defined parking area.   
 
CONCLUSIONS OF LAW: 
1. The proposed movie set complies with the development evaluations 
standards in Chapter 2 of the Eastern Summit County Development Code. 
2. As conditioned, the proposed movie set will not be in violation of any County, 
State, and Federal laws.  
3. As conditioned, the movie set will not adversely affect, in a significant 
manner, the public health, safety, and welfare.
