Agenda Review
         Chair Choberka and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting.
Council Executive Director Eller-Smith referenced informational items that have been provided to the Council in their meeting
packet for their review.

Proposed Southeast Ogden Community Plan
          City Administration has proposed an update to the Southeast Ogden Community Plan. The Plan covers the area generally
south of 36th Street and east of Gramercy Avenue. The Plan sets forth vision statements for land use, housing, parks and open
space, and transportation. The Plan prescribes vision strategies for topics and issues within each of the vision statement areas. The
Southeast Ogden Community Plan update would be the first major rewrite of the Plan since 1987.
          The Council reviewed the proposed Plan at the February 11, 2020 work session. At that meeting, questions ar·ose about
accessory dwelling units (ADUs), code enforcement and student housing. The Council requested an additional work session for
discussion and asked that Planning Commission leadership be invited to share their perspective about the Plan. Representatives
from the City's Code Services Division will be present to discuss code enforcement issues.
          Deputy Planning Manager Spencer made a presentation to address topics raised during the February 11, 2020 work
session, including ADUs, rental property regulations, code enforcement, and student housing. He discussed the history of a
petition to amend the ADU ordinance by prohibiting ADUs in the Southeast Ogden Community Plan area. The petition was
ultimately denied in 2018, but the City Council communicated to the petition sponsors and City staff that they felt the issue could
be resolved through a Community Plan update. He reviewed comments that were provided throughout consideration of the
petition, emphasizing that some felt allowing AD Us Citywide was too broad, but that they would support a narrower focus in the
College Heights and Forest Green neighborhoods of the Community Plan area. Staff utilized that feedback to begin the
Community Plan update process. Mr. Spencer stated community plans are developed to be more specific in addressing unique
neighborhood issues that occur in each community within the City. Purposes of community plans include the following:
               I. To balance interests

              Mr. Spencer then discussed community involvement specific to the neighborhoods in the Southeast Ogden Community
    Plan. Dating back to 2016, the City has considered student housing projects and ADU ordinance amendments prior to
    commencing the public process for updating the Plan. The first open house meeting was held in January of 2019, followed by
    eight steering committee meetings. Meetings were held with Weber State University (WSU) regarding the future of student
    housing. During the steering committee meeting regarding housing, there was a focus on ADU s in the Community Plan area; the
    committee recommended limits on ADUs by establishing appropriate areas for the land use as determined by the cmmnunity. The
    community supports ADUs in the northwest neighborhood, College Heights, Forest Green, and the southern area of the
    community. The proposed Plan specifies community desired limits for ADUs, with a zoning ordinance amendment to commence
    after the Plan is adopted. Staff and the Planning Commission will work with the Housing Steering Committee to determine
    appropriate numbers of AD Us per each area, with a complete ADU ordinance revision to be completed by the summer or fall of
    2020. If ADUs are operated as intended, the land use can serve as a benefit to the community. As a best practice principle, ADUs
    should be an option for those that intend to follow the rules; however, there will always be those who do not follow the rules.
    Enforcement measures include recording a certificate on the title of a property indicating its nature, i.e. single family only. Mr.
    Spencer presented an online map created by the City that can be provided to identify the location of licensed ADUs so that
    neighbors understand what is taking place in their neighborhood.
              Relative to student housing, the steering committee discussed the following housing options:
                   • New multi-family housing along Harrison Boulevard
                   • Mixed-use zoning on Grandview A venue
                   •    Transit-oriented zone along Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) lines
                   •    Student housing overlay zone to allow additional occupancy vs. single family occupancy
                   • Architectural/amenity controls for new housing
                   •    Work with WSU on future student housing options
               Council discussion throughout Mr. Spencer's presentation centered on matters such as pressure felt by the steering
    committee to be more restrictive for ADU s in the Plan area, components of the City's current ADU ordinance, the City's ability to
    enforce ADU regulations, and whether the proposed regulations will incentivize licensure or illegal operation of an ADU.
               Chair Choberka recognized Planning Commission Chair Southwick, Vice Chair Graff. She also recognized Code
    Services Manager Evans and invited the Council to address any questions to these individuals about their area of expertise.
              Council member Hyer asked Ms. Evans to provide general information about the practices used by the Code Enforcement
    Division relative to ADUs. Ms. Evans explained that her staff responds to complaints about illegal AD Us or those that are causing
    a nuisance for neighboring property owners. Her staff also deals with short-tenn, or vacation rental properties. She provided
    examples of the types of complaints that her office receives as well as the noticing process that an enforcement officer follows to
    resolve an issue with the property owner. This requires a great deal of investigative work on the part of the officer.
               Planning Commission Chair Southwick provided the Council with an overview of the current ADU ordinance, which he
    feels is a well thought out ordinance. It promotes home ownership and allows residents to retain their home in situations where
    they may otherwise be forced to sell and vacate. He cannot see how an ADU changes the character of a neighborhood as it is rare
    that an ADU takes the form of a separate structure in the rear yard of a property. There is an ongoing, chronic affordable housing
    crisis across the nation and allowing ADUs is a step toward providing low-impact, affordable housing for those desiring to live in
    Ogden. Discussion among the Council and Planning Commission leadership centered on the impact that public clamor may have
    had on the development of the current ADU ordinance as well as the ADU provisions included in the proposed Plan. Planning
    Commission Chair Southwick stated that many of the residents in the Plan area have lived there for a long time and they view the
    allowance of AD Us as a negative change. In the planning process, most people do not want to see change, specifically an increase
    in density; however, those matters must be balanced with private property rights and the needs of the community.
              Mr. Spencer stated that when the process to update the Plan commenced, those who were involved in the process were
    given direction to handle the matter of AD Us within the Plan document. There was an understanding that the decisions made in
    the Plan would be suppmted by the City Council. The public had an understanding that they would handle issues facing their
    community via a thorough community involvement process. Many residents in that community feel that the proximity of their
    property to WSU puts them at risk of having more rental properties in their neighborhood. This concern influenced the
    recommendations included in the Plan, as well as identification of areas within the Plan area that ADUs should be allowed. There
    was general discussion regarding the appropriateness of ADUs in the Plan area and across the City in general.

Council Business
          "Shelte1· in Place" order relative COVID-19: Ms. Eller-Smith stated that while no official order has been issued by the
Weber/Morgan Health Department to shelter in place, such an order may be issued soon. Council staff is evaluating options for
holding electronic public meetings and continuing to get work done. Council member White stated that in addition to facilitating
electronic processes, it will be necessary for Administration and the Council to consider the potential fmancial impact quarantine
orders could have on the City's revenue stream. Council member Nadolski agreed and said the City should also begin preparing to
make it possible for employees to work from home. Chief Administrative Officer Johnson indicated that Administration is
determining which employees can work from home and taking steps to make that option a possibility for them. He discussed other
preparations underway that are aimed at preventing the spread of the virus in the community, including a briefing of work being
done by other community partners and health care providers to help to limit exposure. A fair amount of time is being spent
considering this issue because the level of concern continues to increase from day to day.
          Council member White encouraged Administration to consider recommending a budget amendment that would set aside
funding to be used in the emergency response phase of COVID-19. Mr. Johnson stated the State of Utah has declared a state of
emergency so that they have access to federal emergency funds.
          Council Calendar: Ms. Eller-Smith reviewed the City Council calendar for the remainder of the month of March and
briefly discussed items included on upcoming meeting agendas.
          Youth Council Leadership Academy: Ms. Eller-Smith provided the Council with an update on the progress of creating a
Youth Council. This included a review of the proposed vision of the Council, goals for the Council, and the process for recruiting
youth in the community to be involved in the Council. She noted that this issue will be discussed in depth during the joint meeting
with the Ogden School District Board that is scheduled for later this week. The Council discussed the purpose of the Youth
Council and funding sources for the group.
