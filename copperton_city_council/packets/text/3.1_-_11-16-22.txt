Council Business

Tentative 2023 Budget and Tentative 2022 Amended Budget

                Mayor Clayton shared the 2023 proposed budget. There were no increases over
the prior year, and revenue was holding strong. There was a slight change to the election line.
Anything left over will go to the Capital Improvement Fund. There was some Coronavirus Aid,
Relief, and Economic Security (CARES) Act money that Copperton gave to United Fire Authority

Legislative Update

                Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, spoke about a house bill sponsored by
Representative Jordan Teuscher that would seek to remedy concerns that the County is collecting
money from municipalities that are not receiving services from UPD. This bill would remove
Sheriff Rosie Rivera as CEO of Unified Police Department (UPD). There is a work group of some
of the cities working to address the potential conflict of interest. Rep. Teuscher is open to dialog
and not asking for a vote currently. His concern, and the concern of some of the other attorneys
he has spoken with, is that the bill language is very broad, and it might preclude the County from
participating in Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA) at all. Sherriff Rivera
spoke out against the bill, as did Mayor Kelly Bush of Kearns.

                Mayor Clayton asked Mr. Bracken if he thought the intent of the bill was for cities
to have a list of services that they are willing to pay for and only pay for those services.

                Mr. Bracken stated he did not think the intent of the bill was to invalidate SLVLESA
or UPD, but it is written so broadly that it might require that. The funding language needs a lot of
work in his opinion.

                 Detective Harry Holt, UPD, stated there was a late change to the agenda for
tomorrow’s UPD meeting to discuss the matter and decide whether to the UPD Board will take an
initial position on the pending legislation.

              Mr. Bracken stated there is a perception among some that removing Sheriff Rivera
as CEO of UPD will make the problem go away. However, there are several State statutes that
require the Sheriff to perform specific duties and this bill neither addresses nor acknowledges
that.
