Council Business

VECC Discussion

                 Nathan Bracken stated Mayor Kelly Bush of Kearns asked him to research how
the metro townships are represented on the Salt Lake Valley Emergency Communications Center
(VECC) board. Currently, the metro townships are represented by UFA and UPD. They pay dues
out of their budget, but do not have a specific seat on the board. Other cities that pay dues directly
have seats on the VECC board. The difference is that they pay dues directly to VECC. VECC is
not enthusiastic about Copperton having a seat and some people did not understand that
Copperton is a township and represented by Salt Lake County. Being represented by UFA and
UPD would allow a higher voting percentage. The downside is that UFA and UPD represent a lot
of interests in addition to Copperton. Some of the metro townships desire to have their own seat
on the VECC board. He suggests Copperton talk to UFA and UPD first. The MSD members
have decided to take it back to their councils. For this to work, all the metro townships would
need to be on board.

             Council Member Bailey asked if Copperton gaining a seat on the VECC board
would cause UFA and UPD to have to give up a seat.

Park Discussion

              Council Member Stitzer stated Jerry Dunaway and his wife and son are going to
be caretakers for the Copperton park. Jerry asked for the opportunity to talk about some
expectations and long-term goals for the park.

               Mayor Clayton stated this can be left on the agenda for the next few months.

               Jerry Dunaway asked who decides the hours. He is currently allowed to work 50
hours split between three people, and that is nowhere near enough.

                Council Member Bailey stated the Council can ask to have the hours increased
but it will come out of their budget.

             Mayor Clayton stated he does not have a problem paying for what Copperton
needs because the park is its crowning achievement.

                 Mr. Dunaway stated most of the time is spent just caring for dead trees alone and
everything else is getting neglected. There are goat heads everywhere. He thought the County
would be spraying, but instead they have delivered backpack equipment for him to do it. Spraying
requires certification, which he happens to have, but the County does not know that and spraying
needs to be done in the morning. There are also broken sprinkler heads. He does not have
access to the timers or anything. He cannot get anyone to come out and show him how the
system is set up. He works with Clayton Eastman and has asked him to help facilitate a meeting.

               Council Member Stitzer asked if there has been any training or orientation.

                Mr. Dunaway stated he has received nothing. Caretakers who make $13 per hour
typically do not do mowing, weeding, or grounds keeping.

              Council Member Stitzer stated if Copperton is already paying for a service, they
should not have to have their own employees perform those tasks. They need to get Mr. Dunaway
the resources he needs.

               Council Member Stitzer stated there are broken pipes and valves and the weeds
are taking over. The grass will grow back but the weeds need to be controlled.

               Mayor Clayton stated the title for the park was never transferred to Copperton.
The County is working on it. He will be proposing having the sprinkler system redone in the capital
project request in June.

               Council Member Bailey stated she sent an email to the County requesting a
meeting.

Arborist Tree Update

               Council Member Bailey stated Copperton received a quote from Super Trees to
plant forty-one trees for about $15,240. This does not include maintenance. There are twelve
trees that need immediate removal. The other trees have a fungus and if they are drilled, it could
put black mold into the air. One species of tree has this black mold all over it and this needs to
be removed by someone with the right certifications.

               Council Member Stitzer asked if this mold spreads easily.

               Council Member Bailey stated it does spread easily and can make people sick.
The arborist has not yet priced the removal of the moldy trees yet. The price to chop down the
remaining forty to fifty trees would be $14,000 if done by Super Trees or $11,000 if done by Parks
and Recreation. All things being equal, Copperton should go with the lower quote. The question
is who will maintain the newly planted trees if not Super Trees. Parks and Recreation will not
take care of them. Copperton can buy a 4-wheeler with a water tank, but the year warranty for
the newly planted trees will only apply if they are properly cared for. The quote for tree
maintenance is $12,300, and this would include eighteen total visits, replacement of any trees
covered by warranty, and one visit per month during irrigation season.

              A motion was made by Mayor Clayton, seconded by Council Member Stitzer, to
approve the purchase of forty-one trees along with the maintenance package. The motion
passed unanimously.

                A motion was made by Mayor Clayton, seconded by Council Member Stitzer, to
spend $11,000 to remove the forty to fifty trees and grind the stumps. Council Member Bailey
stipulated that her vote only applies if stump removal is included in the $11,000. The motion
passed unanimously.

              Mayor Clayton stated the diseased trees need to come down as soon as
possible because he does not want the black mold to spread.
