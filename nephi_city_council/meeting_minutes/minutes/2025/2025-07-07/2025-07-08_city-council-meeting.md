1. Kat Linford with UMPA introduced a new program called Shared Solar. The UMPA provides
   power to six member cities, including Nephi. The Shared Solar program is designed for
   residential customers.
       a. UMPA has a 4-megawatt plant in Spanish Fork. The power from that has been
            reserved for residential customers that want to participate in renewable energy at a
            lower cost with the potential to save them on monthly energy costs.
       b. Solar energy is sold in 200 kWh blocks, with enrollment based on customer’s usage.
       c. SharedSolar is a renewable energy program that lessens environmental impacts,
            reduces air pollutants, and lowers greenhouse gases from traditional fossil fuel
            generation.
       d. SharedSolar allows the option of renewable energy production on a utility scale to
            customers that couldn’t otherwise install rooftop solar: Cost Prohibitive;
            Apartment/HOA/Rentals; Trees/Shade
       e. The rate of $21.24 for 200 kWh is locked in for the duration of the project (about 20
            years)
       f. Out of 3,000 solar blocks, 2,419 are available for lease. Customers can cancel any
            time after the initial 2-year commitment.
       g. The program is more cost-effective than traditional rooftop solar (net-metering) for
            many customers.
       h. Ms. Linford also talked about Net Metering and the many misconceptions about net
            metering by showing an informational draft video which will eventually be
            accessible on the city’s website.
       Mayor Seely and council members discussed the potential benefits and challenges of
       shared solar and net metering for customers and the city. The council will need to
       approve the city moving forward with using the SharedSolar program. If approved
       billing codes will be added into the city’s current software; UMPA will provide training
       and support to Nephi personnel to handle customer inquiries; and UMPA will provide
       marketing efforts to promote the program and awareness.

2. City Administrator Seth Atkinson showed the Nephi City code governing the parking of large
   vehicles. The NCPD and Code Enforcement have enforced this law recently. Some trucks
   are parked in areas that wouldn’t affect the general population but are on city streets. The
    council discussed the potential impact of allowing large vehicles to park on city streets,
    including noise, sight distance, and maintenance issues. Mayor Seely and others shared
    personal experiences and opinions on the matter. The council considers the need for
    guidelines and potential changes to the code to address concerns and will continue the
    discussion in future meetings.

3. Mr. Atkinson stated that the golf cart code is one of several codes under review for changes.
   Once changes are made to each of the codes under review, they will all be brought to the
   council at once.

4. Mr. Atkinson introduced to the council a proposal for rural water rate adjustments for
   customers outside of city limits.
       a. During a recent audit of utility accounts, we identified at least 33 locations outside
           the Nephi City limits that are currently connected to the city's culinary water
           system. While we were able to locate several of the original agreements related to
           these “Special Water Permits,” some documentation could not be found.
       b. Many of the agreements were recovered, some dating back to the mid-1970s,
           include language indicating that the applicant would knowingly pay 40% more for
           their water usage. However, due to changes in billing systems over the years, there
           is currently no identifiable “special rate” in place for these accounts.
       c. Given that these properties are located outside city limits, it would be reasonable to
           implement a rural rate structure to reflect the additional costs the city incurs in
           maintaining water service beyond municipal boundaries. This approach is consistent
           with how we currently bill for natural gas services in rural areas outside Nephi City.
       d. To address this, we propose implementing a 20% rate increase for culinary water
           users located outside Nephi City limits.
       e. The council considers the appropriateness of increasing rates for these customers to
           reflect the higher costs of maintaining the system. The potential impact on
           customers and the incentive for annexation into the city is discussed. The council
           considers the timing and implementation of any rate increases, including the
           possibility of incremental increases over time. October was mentioned as the
           possible date of implementation.
