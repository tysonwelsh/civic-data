                  A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed amendment to Fiscal Year
        (FY) 2021-2025 Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) and FY 2021 Annual Action Plan (AAP) came before the Council for
        consideration. The memo stated as a requirement to receive federal funding from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban
        Development (HUD), the City's Community and Economic Development Department prepares and submits to the Council what is
        known as a five-year Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) and an Annual Action Plan. The Annual Action Plan (AAP) is used to outline
        how federal Community Development Block Grant funds, HOME funds and other federal and non-federal program funds will be
        used by the City in the upcoming fiscal year. When the Annual Action Plan is submitted to HUD in May of each year, specific
        dollar amounts for entitlement grant funds, program income funds, and carry-over funds are estimated for the next fiscal year. Part
        way through the year, after the specific dollar amounts are known or when program income from loans and other programs are
        received, an amendment must be made to the plan to update the Annual Action Plan budget. Often when the mid-year amendment
        is proposed, changes in funding levels within certain programs and line items are proposed as well. The amendments proposed
        with this mid-year update include recognizing additional funding from the Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security
        (CARES) Act related to CDBG-CV funding, small amendments to the Quality Neighborhoods program and ConPlan goals, minor
        amendments Own in Ogden program and other program income and carry-over adjustments.
                  An additional memo from the City Comptroller regarding a proposed FY 2021 budget amendment pertaining to the first
        FY 2021 AAP amendment came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated this action amends the City's budget to
        reflect the changes proposed in the Annual Action Plan budget submitted to HUD.
                  A third memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed amendments to the
        Microenterprise Loan Program guidelines came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated City Administration's
        proposed amendments to the Program would require a business owner with three or fewer years of experience to complete a
        training course. The program funding could then be used to reimburse the cost of the training in addition to providing loans to the
        business for operating expenses. The Microenterprise Loan program meets the national HUD objective ofbenefiting low/moderate
        income business owners.
                  Community Development Manager Ogden summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
        discuss the ConPlan and AAP amendments. The ConPlan and AAP are a combined document in the HUD system and no changes
        are being proposed to the actual ConPlan; Amendment#! only changes the AAP for the period July 1, 2020 to June 30,2021. The
        ConPlan is the mechanism for the City to receive HUD funds and the application to renew entitlement status for the CDBG and
        HOME grants. It is also a strategic planning tool to identify and prioritize housing, community development, and economic

       Proposed Ordinance 2020-64 to amend the golf cart fees for El Monte and
       Mount Ogden Golf Courses
                 A memo from the Public Services Department regarding a proposed mid-year fee adjustment came before the Council for
       consideration. The memo stated Ogden City Administration is proposing mid-year amendments for golf cart related fees.
       Increases are being proposed to better align with comparable entities and to fund increasing expenses related to this service.
                  City Comptroller Stout summarized the memo and explained motorized golf cart fees have remained unchanged over the
       last two GolfDivision fee increase periods and are due to be updated relative to the cost of providing the service and market. The
       Division currently operates two golf cart leases in the amount of$27,600 and $34,900 annually for its fleet of 100 golf carts with
       Yamaha Finance. Due to ever increasing cost of leasing/ownership along with the growing amount of abuse and damage at the
