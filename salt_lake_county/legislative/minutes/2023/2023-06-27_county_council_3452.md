4.2 Informational Update Concerning Salt Lake County’ s 
Declared State of Emergency Due to Flooding
23-0685
Attachments: Staff Report
Presenter: Amy McCormick, Engineering & Flood Control Fiscal Manager & 
Kade Moncur,
Engineering & Flood Control Division Director (Approx. 3:40PM)
Mr. Kade Moncur , Director, Engineering and Flood Control Division, 
stated there are currently no flooding problems in the County.  The 
temperatures will start rising into the upper 80s and 90s, and snow will 
continue to run off the mountains down Little Cottonwood Creek and the 
larger watersheds.  The water in the creeks will be above ordinary water 
levels, but there is not enough snow to cause a problem.  Flood Control still 
has equipment on Big and Little Cottonwood Creeks, and it will continue to 
monitor those creeks through mid -July.  Then, it will move onto maintaining 
the canals and dredging the detention basins to get ready for spring runoff 
next year.  With regard to sandbag disposal, the County has set up nine 
locations where cities can bring their sandbags.   The County will pick 
sandbags up from those locations and take them to a center in South Salt 
Lake.  Flood Control is still trying to figure out what to do to repurpose all 
the sand.  It has put up signs on social media telling people they can come 
and get sand if they would like it.  It also has thousands of good bags it does 
not have room to store and is trying to decide what to do with those.  Flood 
Control has approximately $ 1.45 million of uncommitted funds that was 
approved in the budget for this emergency response, and that will help with 
the cost of the sandbag disposal as well as dredging the detention basins .  
Flood Control estimated it would need about $ 680,000 to dredge all ten 
detention basins it maintains, excluding Sugarhouse Park.

Ms. Amy McCormick , Fiscal Manager, Flood Control Engineering 
Division, delivered a presentation on the costs of the flood emergency as of 
June 23, 2023, totaling $ 2,308,078.57, reviewing a detailed summary of the 
flood emergency payroll totaling $ 273,019.81, flood emergency operating 
expense of $ 1,464,428.41, and flood emergency encumbrances of 
$570,630.35.  She also reviewed flood emergency contracts, as well as the 
flood emergency budget adjustment approved by the Council on May 2, 
2023, a one -time fund balance transfer from the General Fund to the Flood 
Control Fund, to cover additional expenses.    
Mr. Clint Mecham , Salt Lake County Emergency Manager, Unified Fire 
Authority, stated Emergency Management is still working within the incident 
command system that was established early on for this.  There had been 
tremendous multi -disciplinary, multi -municipality efforts across the County to 
prepare and to make sure damages were minimized.  As a result of those 
efforts, the County incurred a much lower damage rate than it anticipated .  
The County is now in the recovery mode of cleaning up sandbags and 
making sure it is not doing anything to create future problems.  Moving into 
the 4th of July weekend and the summer, there will be continued high flows in 
all the creeks, and the water will be cold.  Emergency Management  is trying 
to get the message out to the public about those flows.

Recorder Appeal Authority Ordinance
Presenter: Gavin Anderson, Deputy District Attorney  (Approx. 3:55PM)
Mr. Gavin Anderson , Deputy District Attorney, reviewed the ordinance, 
stating the Legislature enacted H .B. 351 this year, which required the creation 
of a statewide county recorders' standards board to come up with standards 
about how county recorders do their jobs, as well as required all counties to 
create an appeal authority to hear and decide appeals.  To keep things 
simple, the County is going to create a process by which an appeal would go 
through the County’s existing administrative appeal process.

urgency in getting this approved to meet the deadlines.

2023.
