         Utah Independent Redistricting Commission – Beau Bayless

Beau Bayless is a graduate intern working with the Utah Independent Redistricting Commission.
He said that the Utah Independent Redistricting Commission was formed from Proposition 4 in
2018. He said they are an independent, state funded, bipartisan commission. He said that they
will get their census data in about two weeks and then will begin working on mapping. He said
they are doing education and outreach to the public. He said they are hoping to get input from
the Mayor and Councilmembers on how they can ensure getting representation for Riverton.

Mayor Staggs asked Mr. Bayless to share the website so the Councilmembers could provide
feedback.

Councilmember Stewart said that he lives in zip code 84096 and the census showed them as
Herriman City. He said this area is being shown in Herriman numbers and he wants it to be
accurate when they are creating boundaries. Mr. Bayless said he will reach out to Riverton staff
to make sure the boundaries are accurate.

Councilmember Buroker asked if they rely on other information besides the zip code to
determine boundaries. Mr. Bayless said that other information is considered.

Casey Saxton said that the City provided the census bureau with the map of city boundaries
prior to the beginning of the census. He said that everyone in the 84096 zip code area from
Riverton received the census mailer and it said Herriman so they have been nervous that some
of Riverton residents are being counted in Herriman City.

         Ordinance No. 21-21 – Removal of Park Restrictions – Ryan Carter, City Attorney

Mayor Staggs said that Ryan Carter, City Attorney had been working on changing the ordinance
for the main park.

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, said that the change to the ordinance came because it has been a
unique year. He said that the flood at Midas Creek Park pushed leagues that were using that
park out. He said the school district has also put a moratorium on the use of their fields due to
water restrictions. He said that leagues have looked elsewhere for space to practice and play
sports. He said that the main park was not built for league use and he does not recommend
long term league use. He said that that current ordinance prohibits league sports and believes
that removing the park restrictions would allow the main park to be used for situations like this
Page | 5 Minutes – City Council Meeting
August 3, 2021




year. He said that it will be up to the City Manager to decide if the main park will be used for
leagues and what the fees and costs associated with this use will be.

Councilmember Stewart said that the City should try to educate leagues that the facilities are
not just for the sport using the park; the facilities are for everyone.

Councilmember Buroker said she is not supportive of this change to the ordinance. She said
that she would like to discuss and not make an action tonight. She asked how many parks the
City has, how many parks are active and passive, how do they make the decision as to whether
they are active or passive parks, what’s the value of a passive park and how will the new
regional park help. She said she is not supportive of this change.

Councilmember McDougal said he is supportive of this change but wants to know what the
broader strategy is for the use of the parks. He said he would like to preserve the main park
until after the 4th of July. He said that he would like to evaluate the park policy and believes this
document will change over time.

Councilmember Wells said that he would like to have a discussion about park usage and
restroom facilities.

Councilmember McCay said that she would like to move forward with the change to the
ordinance and believes the City still has the ability to decide what will happen with the main
park.

Councilmember Stewart MOVED that the Riverton City Council adopt Ordinance No. 21-21
removing prohibitions against use of Riverton City Main Park by sports leagues. He
would like to have a broader discussion about Councilmember Buroker’s questions.
Councilmember McDougal SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the
motion. There being none, he called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: Buroker-no,
McCay-yes, Stewart-yes, McDougal-yes, and Wells-yes. The motion passed.


         Riverton City Sign Ordinance, Initial Discussion of Current Ordinance to Review
         Potential Revisions and Updates

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, said it was time to update the sign ordinance
for various reasons including simplifying the code and to address current issues. He said that
one area he wanted to change is digital signs which are currently limited to a 50% max of the
sign. He said that the technology is more cost effective and more businesses are wanting to
have a larger percentage of their signs be digital.

Councilmember Stewart said that some of the concern in the past is about pixilation. He said
that the ordinance could include language about the pixilation being at a certain level to be able
to have a larger percentage of digital signage.

Councilmember Wells said there was an issue about a sign on Bangerter which was too bright.
He said that this should be taken into consideration. Councilmember Wells asked if Paul
Wayman was trying to put a sign on his fence.

Mr. Lethbridge said that the code for civic signs are exempt but must meet the same standard
as a commercial sign. He said that the needs for a City getting out information is different than
Page | 6 Minutes – City Council Meeting
August 3, 2021




for a business. He said a lot of cities have a separate category for civic signs and he would like
this included in the ordinance. He said that Paul Wayman wanted to attach a sign to one of the
panels in the fence. He said the current code says that the monument sign has to be free
standing and the language precludes that. He said there are ways the code could be written to
accommodate this.

Mayor Staggs said he would like to address the 50% digital signage language. He said that
Casey Saxton and Brook Bowen are working on some mockups for the monument signage that
has been budgeted $150,000 out of the RDA. He said he is not opposed to changing the
ordinance so that Paul Wayman could have signage along his fence. He said that this
ordinance will need to go before the Planning Commission.

Councilmembers and staff discussed various options for signage language for this ordinance
and about Paul Wayman’s sign request.
