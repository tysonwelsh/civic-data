. Dy A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed text amendment pertaining
to height limits in the R-3 zone came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposed text amendment will
set a maximum height of four stories (or 50 feet) within the R-3 zone and clarify and modify setback standards for tall buildings
adjacent to residential or open space zones.

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and stated the R-3 zone currently does not have a base height limit,
though it does have some limits based on proximity to other properties. The City Council recently considered potential uses on a }
vacant R-3 property at 3841 Jackson Avenue, which the Ogden School District had declared as surplus. The Council expressed
concems that a tall building could be built there with the current zoning, and consequently, Administration asked the Planning
staff to propose a reasonable height limit. Planning staff proposes to set a maximum height of four stories, or 50 feet, for the R-3
zone and to modify setback standards adjacent to residential/open spaces and for nursing homes. He summarized maximum
residential densities currently allowed in the R-3 zone and referenced existing developments as examples of the types of projects
that have been allowed based upon current regulations. He added that the R-3 zone also limits heights based upon existing
development of a block within the R-3 zone; ‘ if seventy five percent (75%) of the linear block is developed with dwelling
structures, (a new) building shall not be taller than the tallest dwelling structure on that linear block regardless of the zoning height
allowances.’ He referenced the Ogden School District property that was the catalyst for this discussion and provided a rendering
to highlight the types of buildings and building heights that could be built on the subject property. He concluded the Planning
Commission has recommended that the Council amend the R-3 zone as follows:

e Limit height in the R-3 zone to four stories or 50 feet.

© Modify setback increases for tall buildings to apply only to buildings adjacent to single-family and open space
zones.

e Apply the same height limits to nursing homes in the R-3 zone as for other uses.

Vice Chair Richey referenced communication from interested parties, which was included in the Council’s meeting
packet; one landowner had the misconception that because an eight-story building has been allowed across the street, he could
pursue the same type of development of his property, but that is not the case because the eight-story building is anon-conforming
use. Mr, Brierley stated that is correct and the current zoning would not have allowed for that type of development. Vice Chair
Richey asked Mr. Brierley to explain how the proposed text amendment may actually help that landowner. Mr. Brierley stated that
the proposal is to apply height restrictions and the height/setback standards to apply to single-family residential areas as well.

Chair Choberka introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2023-15, entitled:

“An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Subsection 15-4-5.J;
by amending Section 15-13-5; by amending Subsection 14-14-4.D to set a maximum height of four stories or
50-feet in the R-3 zone and to modify setback standards for tall buildings adjacent to residential or open space
zones; and providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final passage.”

A copy of the proposed ordinance was deposited with the Chief Deputy City Recorder and ordered that the City Recorder
have at least one copy available for public inspection in her office during all business hours.

Chair Choberka then called for public input regarding the proposed ordinance.

No persons came forward to be heard regarding this matter.

ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER WHITE AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER HYER,

Public Comments

Kevin Lundell stated he is the former Vice Chair of the Diversity Commission; he thanked the Council for voting to table
appointments and reappointments to the Diversity Commission earlier this evening. On March 28 he received a letter from Mayor
Caldwell informing him he would not be reappointed to the Diversity Commission; this was a surprise as he was recently voted to
serve as the Vice Chair by his fellow Commissioners and he has served for less than one year. After requesting a meeting with
Mayor Caldwell to ask why he was not being reappointed, he was told a meeting would ‘not be productive’ and that ‘turnover is
common’. While turnover is common, it is unprecedented for a City Commissioner in good standing with his fellow
Commissioners to not be reinstated, let alone a sitting Vice Chair. While the Mayor has full discretion as to who is appointed to
the Diversity Commission, he refused to reappoint him without consulting the Chair, Diversity Affairs Coordinator, or the
Commission. A good leader utilizes their colleagues when making key decisions, like appointments. Without an explanation given
by the Mayor, he has spent a lot of time pondering what he may have done to warrant being expelled from the Commission. Last
month he wrote an op-ed defending diversity efforts from the likes of Senator John Johnson that would have outlawed diversity
programs in higher education. He unapologetically used language comparing Johnson to some of the most notorious politicians in
the past who have stood in the way of racial progress. Given the fact that Senator Johnson was protested last week by students at
Weber State University (WSU), it seems his opinion is in line with many who care about diversity efforts in the community. It is \
his perspective that he was expelled from the Diversity Commission for defending diversity. He spent many hours this weekend
discussing the issue with past and present Diversity Commissioners and the words he keeps hearing to describe the Commission
are ‘performative’ and ‘tokenizing’; it leads him to believe this is just the tip of the iceberg. During his year of service, the Mayor
has not attended a single Diversity Commission meeting, nor has he had any interaction with Diversity Commission leadership.

Council member Comments

Council member Lopez asked Mayor Caldwell if he will grant an extension for the eviction of the Wildlife Rehabilitation
Center. Mayor Caldwell stated he is not prepared to make a commitment regarding that matter tonight. He reiterated he is listening
to the feedback and working with legal counsel to evaluate the contractual obligations related to the building that is currently
occupied by the Center. Council member Lopez then noted he is proud of those who spoke tonight and thanked them for their
civility. He then noted that he would not be sitting where he is today if it were not for mentors that he had throughout his journey
of immigrating to the United States over 25 years ago; he has experienced extreme oppression, discrimination, and racism and he
is grateful for the allies who were on his side and who advocated for him. He addressed the frustrations that have been voiced this
evening and noted that he cares about their feelings and wants to help them; the only power he has is his voice, but he is just one
of seven members who can vote on certain matters. He then addressed Chief Administrative Officer Johnson, noting he has }
repeatedly asked if Administration would be willing to evaluate the section of Monroe Boulevard between 9" and 12" Streets to
determine if a guardrail can be installed to prevent further deaths at that location. He has not received a response and noted it
would be nice to receive an update. Mr. Johnson stated that he is still working on the matter with City staff who are not convinced
a guardrail is needed at the location.

Council member Nadolski also addressed the comments made about the Diversity Commission. He stated that he has
spoken with Mr. Lundell several times over the past several years and he has not always agreed with him, but he has always tried
to take the time to understand him and his position. It is unfortunate to hear the feelings of those involved with the Diversity
Commission about their input and service being disregarded. He commended everyone who spoke this evening and thanked them
for expressing their frustration in candor and kindness.

Council member Lopez stated he would like the record to reflect his formal request to extend the eviction notice for the
Wildlife Rehabilitation Center for one year rather than six months.

Council member Hyer stated he served on the Planning Commission for several years and it was not uncommon for the
City Council or Mayor to disregard recommendations from that body; he can understand the frustration that has been expressed by
Diversity Commission members this evening, but he understands that the Planning Commission does not have the same role as the
City Council and his concern is that if members of City Commissioners are expecting for all of their recommendations to be
followed, that is an unrealistic expectation and they should be advised accordingly. Chair Choberka agreed and noted that the
Council has not always followed recommendations of many different advisory committees; however, she was one of the first
groups of Council members who helped to assemble and nominate the first members of the Diversity Commission. This was a
very big deal and she had high hopes for the Commission, and she is sad to hear that their efforts have not been appreciated or
their goals realized. She does not want to take an action that would condone doing damage to people who have volunteered their
time and talents. She stated she takes many different diversity issues personally and it may be necessary to take a step back and
decide what side of history the City should be on in terms of diversity. Council member Hyer stated he was also part of the
Council that assembled the first Diversity Commission and he is familiar with the entire process.
