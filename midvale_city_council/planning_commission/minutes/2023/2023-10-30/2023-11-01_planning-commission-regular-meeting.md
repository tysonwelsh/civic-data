PUBLIC HEARINGS

1.   City-initiated request to amend the Housing Element as part of the General Plan to be
     in compliance with the State of Utah.

       This city-initiated request to amend the Housing Element as part of the General Plan is to
       be in compliance with the State of Utah.

       Because Midvale City has a fixed guide-way public transit station, the Housing Element
       includes a strategy to develop and adopt a station area plan, and also must include at least
       one of the following strategies in the code: G, H, or Q, as described below, including a
       five-year implementation plan.

         G. Amend land use regulations to allow for higher density or new moderate income
           residential development in commercial or mixed-use zones near major transit
           investment corridors

         H. Amend land use regulations to eliminate or reduce parking requirements for
           residential development where a resident is less likely to rely on the resident's own
           vehicle, such as residential development near major transit investment corridors or
           senior living facilities

APPLICANT:

Meggie Troili, RDA Program Manager, discusses the desired Strategy “Q” to be implemented to
reach compliance with the State of Utah and benefit Midvale City. Ms. Troili explains the
Housing and Transit Reinvestment Zone (HTRZ) and how this will affect the residents as well as
how the seven strategies included will cover the State’s required five strategies. The core
objectives of an HTRZ include; greater use of public transit, increased housing/affordable
housing, water conservation, improved air quality, increase mixed-use development, and
investment in transportation infrastructure.

There are a few specific requirements for this strategy but are manageable for Midvale City.
HTRZ programs have more opportunities for funding than other options offered by the State and
all taxing entities are required to participate. Having an HTRZ does prioritize the City for
funding sources, such as the (TTIF) Transit/Transportation Investment Fund. These funds would
go toward new projects. Salt Lake County has limited the maximum number of HTRZ zones to
eight, within the county limits. Currently, there are three other cities within Salt Lake County
moving forward with these zones. Midvale City has identified the focus of this project around the
Midvale Fort Union Station.

There are currently pieces of the plan being started to help move the project along. There are
three Station Area Plans having studies conducted at this time and this will be included in part of
the recommendations to make Code Amendments and move forward with being approved by the
State. A gap analysis will help form the best financial plan for the City moving forward with the
HTRZ implementation. The proposal must be submitted to the Governor’s Office of Economic
Opportunity (GOEO) to conduct the gap analysis. Once submitted and analysis completed, a
public hearing mut be convened. Once approved, the HTRZ is established, and the taxing entities
begin their role.

DISCUSSION:

Commissioner Liedtke would like to know the timeline of the HTRZ implementation if that is
what is chosen.

Nate Rockwood, Assistant City Manager, explains that there is not one specific timeline to follow.
There are many different steps in reaching the implementation of the plan. Mr. Rockwood
estimates between six months to one year to complete the project. Once recommendations and
studies have been made, both the Planning Commission and City Council will have to approve
each step of the process. Depending on how long that process takes within the city, it will
determine the timeline.

Chair Snow is curious if there is a minimum land size since a maximum was mentioned.

Adam Olsen, acting Planning Director, explains that a minimum land size would not be required
but the project must be for mixed-use and not strictly residential.

Commissioner Liedtke recognizes that this will help also implement the Master Plan previously
presented and that the city will have a fair say in the matter.

Mr. Wilcox, City Attorney, explains that this is a legislative decision, and the City Council will
look at the Planning Commission’s recommendation.

Chair Snow wanted to compare the recommended option to Strategy “G”, suggesting higher
density, that is listed as well.

Mr. Olsen explains that Strategy “G” is one of the strategies that was available. There are existing
zones that allow for higher density and the new Strategy “Q” is just a new tool provided by the
State and would be most beneficial in this area.

Commissioner Liedtke would like to know if more than one station could be included in the HTRZ.

Mr. Olsen explains that Strategy “Q” would be most likely to be approved and satisfy all the State’s
requirements. The one station chosen would be more likely to be approved. Overall, it would be
most advantageous to future development within the designated area in Midvale.

Mr. Rockwood explains that the HTRZ can also be applied to the UTA land within the quarter
mile radius of the station.

Vice Chair Anderson would like to clarify that there is not an apparent down-side that would be
affecting the city.

Mr. Olsen states that for Midvale City, there is not much public upset or other foreseeable issues
likely to stir due to the few single-family residential homes nearby. The prominently commercial
zone would benefit from the future development with the support of the HTRZ.

Chair Snow would like to ensure that the Public Notice would be noted as very important and that
residents are sure to be heard. Chair Snow would also like to know if the State has an affordable
housing number set already.

Mr. Olsen and Mr. Wilcox explain that the set number is currently 80% AMI or below. This will
be an income-based calculation and will vary slightly per income.
