Information: WFRC FY’16 Activities and Accomplishments
Andrew Gruber, WFRC, reiterated the Wasatch Front Regional Council Mission Statement and
then reported on the previously adopted goals and priorities for FY’16. These goals, which
provided direction regarding areas of focus for this past year, are:
1- Begin development of the 2019-2050 Regional Transportation Plan, Utah’s Unified
Transportation Plan, and updated Wasatch Choice land use and development vision.
2- Support efforts to fund the needs identified in the 2015-2040 Utah’s Unified
Transportation Plan.
3- Further develop, integrate and promote WFRC programs that benefit local communities.
4- Strive for organizational excellence including maximizing employee job satisfaction and
performance.
With the help and support of member communities, WFRC was able to make significant
progress towards accomplishing their FY’16 goals. Mr. Gruber thanked our members for their
hard work and dedication to these efforts. Included in the packet is an executive summary, as
well as the full report, entitled “FY’16 Activities & Accomplishments Report.”
ACTION: WFRC FY’17 Goals and Priorities
Mr. Gruber then presented the proposed set of goals and priorities to help shape the activities of
WFRC for the next year. These goals were developed through review of WFRC’s adopted
Mission & Roles, reflection on the agency activities and accomplishments for the previous year,
and a collaborative and full discussion among the WFRC staff, in consultation with the WFRC
Executive Committee. The following are DRAFT goals and priority areas for WFRC in FY’17,
and there is a summary included in the packet materials. Many of these items reflect a
continuation of ongoing agency priorities. This is not intended to be a comprehensive list of all of
WFRC’s activities. The WFRC FY’17 Unified Planning Work Program (UPWP), adopted by the
Regional Council at its meeting in May 2016, comprehensively lists all of WFRC’s activities.
1- Collaboratively develop long- and short-range plans and programs, and assist local
communities to implement those plans.
2- Integrate the consideration of transportation, land use, and economic development
through a performance-based approach.
3- Support transportation policy-making.
4- Strive for organizational excellence.
The members of the WFRC Executive Committee have reviewed these items and, along with
WFRC staff, are submitting them for the Council’s review, discussion and consideration for
approval at this Council meeting.
Commissioner Kerry Gibson moved to approve the WFRC goals for FY2017 and Mayor Mark
Allen seconded the motion. The vote to approve was unanimous.

ACTION: Mountain Accord Interlocal Agreement
Mayor Tom Dolan gave a brief update regarding the Mountain Accord Interlocal Agreement. The
process is well underway to create the Central Wasatch Commission (CWC). In order to
implement the principles and actions of the Mountain Accord, the CWC is being formed by
interlocal agreement and includes the following entities: Salt Lake City, Salt Lake County,
Cottonwood Heights and Sandy City. The CWC will coordinate and oversee the Mountain
Accord effort, including assuming responsibility for budgeting, accounting and contracting. As
such, the two Mountain Accord contracts currently held by WFRC will be transferred and
assigned to the CWC. That assignment will be effectuated through another interlocal agreement
to which WFRC will be a party, the Mountain Accord Interlocal Assignment, Assumption and
Consent Agreement. With the transfer of the contracts to the CWC, WFRC will also transfer any
funding remaining for these contracts, pending final approval of the transfer from UTA and the
Governor’s Office of Economic Development. The budgetary impacts of this transfer of funding
will be reflected in subsequent amendments to the WFRC budget.
Mayor Kelvyn Cullimore made a motion to authorize WFRC to be a Party to the Interlocal
Assignment, Assumption & Consent Agreement, pending minor revisions agreed to by the
Parties to the Agreement. The motion was seconded by Mayor Carmen Freeman and the voting
in the affirmative was unanimous.

ACTION: 2015-2040 Regional Transportation Plan (RTP) Amendment #2
Jory Johner, WFRC, reported that WFRC staff has received requests to amend the 2015-2040
RTP to incorporate the changes described in-depth in the packet materials. Projects within
Amendment 2 were presented to the RGC’s Technical Advisory Committees and the respective
county’s Council of Governments (COGs). On May 19, the RGC approved a formal public
comment period for Amendment 2, which took place from May 23, 2016 to June 24, 2016. The
WFRC staff has analyzed the potential financial implications of these projects and feels that the
RTP is able to maintain its fiscal constraint while accommodating construction of these projects
in Phase 1 (2015-2024). Further, the WFRC staff has reviewed the air quality impacts to ensure
that all applicable air quality conformity requirements are met.
Commissioner James Ebert moved to approve Amendment 2 to the 2015-2040 RTP, with the
exception of the portion of Redwood Road project north of 12600 South (which requires further
study and review) and Mayor Mike Caldwell seconded the motion. The voting was unanimous to
approve.

Air Quality Committee Report
Mayor JoAnn Seghini provided an update on the Air Quality Committee.
The August 2016 AQC meeting was cancelled; however, Bryce Bird, with the Department of Air
Quality, presented an update on air quality issues during the August 18, 2016 Regional Growth
Committee. The Wasatch Front’s failure to meet the PM2.5 standards by 2014 means that the
EPA will likely re-designate most of the Wasatch Front from a “moderate” to a “serious” nonattainment area for PM2.5. A new, more aggressive State Implementation Plan for controlling
PM2.5 related emissions will be required to show attainment of the standard by 2019. The entire
Wasatch Front, with the exception of Brigham City, exceeds the ozone standard based on the
three most recent years of data. In coming years, a State Implementation Plan for controlling
ozone will also be required.
Volkswagen agreed to a $10 billion settlement regarding falsified emission testing for its small
diesel vehicles. Utah will receive $32 million of this settlement to be used for cleaner vehicles
for fleets, airports, and freight operations.
Salt Lake Council of Governments also unanimously supported a consideration for the
Legislature to again revisit the fireworks standards for Utah. During the summer holidays, there
are many safety-related fire issues with the standards as they are now.
