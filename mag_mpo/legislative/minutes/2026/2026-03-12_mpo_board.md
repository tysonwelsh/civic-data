Holly Thomas, representing her family’s property at 1185 W 820 North in Provo, thanked the MPO Board for its work on the 820 North corridor and explained that her family has owned the property for 47 years and has been under contract to sell it to Provo City for about six months. She described how evolving plans for the corridor’s roadway expansion and long ‑ term character changes have made clear that their property will be significantly impacted, leaving the family in limbo between selling to the city or listing privately. She emphasized the serious health and emotional strain this uncertainty is causing the elderly trustees (ages 85 and 88, with serious medical issues including partial blindness, pneumonia with oxygen use, and cancer). Holly Thomas requested the opportunity to work with the city on a voluntary sale at fair market value, stressing that the family does not oppose the project, but wants a solution that supports the city’s goals while being fair to them and allows everyone to move forward constructively. Legislative Update (00:10:10) Michelle Carroll gave an overview of key 2026 state legislation affecting transportation and transit in Utah County. She highlighted HB 62, directing construction of a new state highway connection between Mountain View Corridor in Saratoga Springs and Cedar Valley Highway in Eagle Mountain, improving west ‑ side access. HB 425 clarifies how transportation utility fees (TUFs) can be implemented, requiring local studies, allowing different residential/commercial rates, and excluding the lowest ‑ traﬃc day (typically Sunday) from counts to address concerns of religious institutions. SB 242 allows second ‑ class counties (including Utah County) to use a portion of the 0.25% transportation sales tax for both transportation and public safety, with a transitional period where a slice of transit funds flows directly to counties before shifting part of the revenue to TIF commuter rail and part to counties for capital and O&M. She explained HB 575, a temporary gas tax reduction from 37.9¢ to 31.9¢/gallon (July 1–Dec 31), which cuts the state transportation fund by about $40M but holds cities and counties harmless via an $11.9M general ‑ fund transfer. HB 492 moves $100M from TIF into a new State Housing Infrastructure Partnership revolving loan fund to finance large ‑ scale system infrastructure (transportation, water, sewer) that unlocks primarily affordable and owner ‑ occupied housing, with ties to station ‑ area plans and regional facilities. Finally, she summarized SB 197, which restructures UTA governance into a seven ‑ member Transit Commission (appointed by the Governor, Speaker, and Senate President, with COG ‑ nominated candidates from Utah/Tooele counties, no elected oﬃcials, four ‑ year staggered terms, governor ‑ chosen chair, and the UTA Executive Director reporting to the Governor). SB 197 also incorporates a funding provision Page 2 of 5 capturing 5% of state sales ‑ tax growth starting in FY 2028 into TIF for transit, roughly doubling transit investment by FY 2035 before the growth capture sunsets. MPO Regional Growth Forum (00:25:48) Kevin Feldt described a shift from creating a formal MPO Regional Growth Committee to establishing an MPO Regional Growth Forum to tackle broad, fast ‑ growing ‑ county challenges in Utah County. Using population and employment projections, he showed Utah County is expected to surpass Salt Lake County in population growth by 2055 (to about 1.3 million residents), while still lagging in employment growth, underscoring the need to strengthen the county’s jobs base to reduce northbound commuting congestion. The proposed forum would bring together elected and appointed oﬃcials, agency staff, and industry professionals to discuss cross ‑ cutting issues like transportation, economic development, housing/affordability, water, environment, technology, tourism, data, and legislative/policy changes, rather than focusing only on traditional MPO transportation topics. Feedback from the Technical Advisory Committee (TAC) led staff to pivot from a rigid committee structure to this more flexible forum model, with MAG staff providing support; as a next step, MAG will send a growth ‑ challenges survey to board members to prioritize topics and will return to the MPO Board with recommendations on forum structure and focus. TIP Project Ideas and Studies Approval (00:33:03) Bob Allen reviewed the current TIP (Transportation Improvement Program) selection cycle, explaining that MAG received 45 project ideas totaling about $250 million, even though only about $110 million per year is available, so prioritization is necessary. Staff conducted an initial rating and then held a five ‑ to six ‑ hour TAC workshop on February 23, where each project was briefly presented and TAC members provided scores and feedback via a follow ‑ up survey. Bob highlighted three study requests that, under MPO policy, do not need to advance to the more detailed “concept” stage and instead came directly to the Board for funding decisions: the Meadows Crossing Study (to refine a new crossing between the American Fork FrontRunner Station and the Meadows commercial area over the railroad and I ‑ 15), the Provo West Side Transit Study (to evaluate transit needs and options serving the airport, Epic Sports Park, and west Provo employment areas), and the Payson Canyon Trail Phase 2 Feasibility Study (to connect Elk Ridge and existing canal trails to a trail into Payson Canyon and improve bike/ped safety off the roadway). After discussion—including concerns about regional prioritization and equity of information across cities—the Board requested to make a separate motion for each study. Commissioner Skyler Beltran moved to vote on each study individually Mayor Jared Gray seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Commissioner Skyler Beltran moved to approve a study for the Meadows Crossing in American Fork. Mayor Jared Gray seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Mayor Marsha Judkins moved to approve a study for the Provo Westside Transit in Provo. Page 3 of 5 During discussion of the motion, the board members raised questions about whether this area should be prioritized over other parts of the county with significant transit needs and expressed concern that jurisdictions more familiar with MPO processes may have an advantage in getting studies funded. In response, it was explained that west Provo’s needs are closely tied to the airport, Epic Sports Park, and nearby employment centers, and that current on ‑ demand and bus service is limited, leaving gaps for workers and travelers. Additional comments noted that this concept had been discussed in earlier RTP workshops as a regional issue—especially for access to the airport and the Lakeview Parkway corridor—rather than solely a local Provo concern. It was also emphasized that Utah County should plan for transit county ‑ wide and long ‑ term, with regional connections in mind. After this discussion, initial reservations were withdrawn. Mayor Marsha Judkins seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. 2055 RTP: Goals and Objectives Adoption (00:58:07) Kendall Willardson presented draft 2055 Regional Transportation Plan (RTP) goals and objectives that shift the MPO from largely project ‑ type goals (e.g., build a grid network, expand freeways, add transit) to more value ‑ based, outcome ‑ focused goals aligned with the Wasatch Choice Vision. The proposed goals are to: (1) improve access to economic, educational, and other opportunities, (2) maintain manageable and reliable traﬃc conditions, (3) provide quality, multimodal transportation choices, (4) ensure safe, user ‑ friendly facilities (with emphasis on vulnerable road users), and (5) support clean air. He explained that objectives under each goal will guide how projects are evaluated and prioritized—for example, improving access to jobs and housing, providing roadway redundancy and removing choke points, strengthening transit and active ‑ transportation networks, incorporating Safe Streets for All safety work, and meeting air ‑ quality conformity requirements. These goals and objectives will form the foundation for RTP project screening, prioritization, and phasing work over the coming months. Mayor Marsha Judkins moved to approve the 2055 Regional Transportation Goals and Objectives as presented. Councilmember Tim DeGraw seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. 2055 RTP: Project Screening Workshops Update (01:08:05) Kendall Willardson reported on the 2055 RTP project ‑ screening workshops, thanking board members and local staff for strong participation and noting that the MPO received hundreds of comments (roughly 600–700) from the MPO ‑ board ‑ level sessions alone. He summarized key themes that emerged: the need for better connections to high ‑ capacity transit (especially FrontRunner), a strong emphasis on east–west mobility, particular attention to Northwest Utah County, safer pedestrian and bike crossings over major barriers such as I ‑ 15 and rail lines, and sensitivity to right ‑ of ‑ way constraints and community impacts when widening or adding roads. Staff are now Page 4 of 5 using this input to refine the initial RTP project list, apply a color ‑ coded screening, and identify where multiple alternatives or further consensus are needed, including how to integrate PEL study outcomes. Kendall outlined the next steps: bring a draft updated project list back to the Board at a future meeting, then spend the spring and summer developing a project ‑ prioritization and phasing methodology based on the adopted RTP goals, with another round of workshops planned in the fall and a final RTP project list targeted by the end of the year. Corridor Preservation Process Update (01:13:13) Cody Christensen provided a brief update on the corridor preservation process, reporting that the working group met earlier in the day to review a draft scoring system for evaluating corridor preservation applications. The group offered substantial feedback and requested revisions, so staff will now refine the scoring criteria and methodology, circulate updates to the working group, and reconvene them to work toward a final recommendation. Cody indicated the goal is to finalize the scoring system by next month, at which point it can be brought back to the MPO Board for consideration and use in future corridor preservation funding decisions. MPO Director Report (01:14:29) LaNiece Davenport reported on internal MAG planning department initiatives following a consultant ‑ led organizational assessment, outlining an action plan through 2028 built around three priority areas. First, strengthen trust, transparency, and proactive regional leadership by holding annual coordination meetings with members and partners, standardizing clear communication and presentations, making MPO project ‑ pathways more transparent, and supporting implementation of the Wasatch Choice Vision. Second, improve program and project compliance and implementation by identifying near ‑ term RTP priorities, aligning revenue forecasts with project phasing, and enhancing oversight and coordination to meet local, state, and federal requirements. Third, strengthen department capacity and internal alignment by ensuring staff are in the right roles, strategically prioritizing staff time, shifting from activity updates to goal ‑ based progress reporting, and regularly reporting back to the Board using a green/yellow/red (“on track/needs attention/off track”) status. She emphasized that staff work and time will now be explicitly guided by these goals and invited ongoing feedback from board members.
