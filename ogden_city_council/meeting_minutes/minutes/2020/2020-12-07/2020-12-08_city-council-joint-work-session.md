    Mid-year Fee Changes - Golf
              0 gden City Administration is proposing mid-year amendments for golf cart related fees. Increases are being proposed to
    better align with comparable entities and to fund increasing expenses related to this service.
              Golf Manager Brenkman explained motorized golf cart fees have remained unchanged over the last two Golf Division
    fee increase periods and are due to be updated relative to the cost of providing the service and market. The Division currently
    operates two golf cart leases in the amount of $27,600 and $34,900 annually for its fleet of 100 golf carts with Yamaha finance.
    Due to ever increasing cost of leasing/ownership along with the growing ainount of abuse and dainage at the hands of the
    irresponsible golfer has led to the need to adjust rates to provide the quality product that the customer has come to expect. Repair
    costs grew significantly during 2020 when rounds were at a ten-year high and fuel costs must also be factored into the rental rate.
    The request will also adjust the Monday-Thursday 18-hole special rate as the discount is figured by adjusting the cart rate as well
    as the Mount Ogden Golf Course punch pass which has a cart rate factored into the discount. He noted that if the 2021 golf season
    is comparable with the 2020 season in terms of patronage, the City stands to realize an increase ofrevenue of$55,000 associated
    with the fee adjustment. This is important as the Division is still working towards being self-sufficient.
              Discussion among the Council and Mr. Brenkman centered on the drainatic increase in the number of golfers on the
    City's courses throughout the COVID-19 pandemic; opportunities for adjusting cart rates for golfers that are golfmg 18 holes
    rather than 9 or who are golfing during off-peak hours; pursuing promotions or events that will boost pro-shop retail sales.
              Council member Hyer stated that he visited Mount Ogden Golf Course several times throughout the year and he was
    pleased with the condition of the course and golf carts; it felt safe to golf and it was great to be in that enviromnent. Mr. Brenkman
    agreed and stated that he and his staff take great pride in keeping the public safe. Many golfers have asked him to pass along their
    gratitude to the Council and Administration for keeping the golf courses open to give them a place to escape their homes during

              As a requirement to receive federal funding from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urbai1 Development (HUD), the
    City's Community and Economic Development Department prepares and submits to the Council what is known as a five-year
    Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) and an Annual Action Plan. The Annual Action Plan (AAP) is used to outline how federal
    Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) funds, HOME funds and other federal and non-federal program funds will be
    used by the City in the upcoming fiscal year. When the Annual Action Plan is submitted to HUD in May of each year, specific
    dollai· amounts for entitlement grant funds, program income funds, and carry-over funds are estimated for the next fiscal yeai·. Part
    way through the year, after the specific dollar amounts are known or when program income from loans and other programs are
    received, an amendment must be made to the plan to update the Annual Action Plan budget. Often when the mid-yeai· amendment
    is proposed, changes in funding levels within certain programs and line items are proposed as well. The ainendments proposed
    with this mid-year update include recognizing additional funding from the Coronavirns Aid, Relief, and Economic Security

          Project Manager Fuentes used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the ConPlan and AAP amendments. The
ConPlan and AAP are a combined document in the HUD system and no changes are being proposed to the actual ConPlan;
Amendment#! only changes the AAP for the period July 1, 2020 to June 30, 2021. The ConPlan is the mechanism for the City to
receive HUD funds and the application to renew entitlement status for the CDBG and HOME grants. It is also a strategic planning
tool to identify and prioritize housing, community development, and economic development needs. It outlines the City's plans to
utilize HUD funds for the next five years and if a project is not in the ConPlan, it cannot receive HUD funds. The AAP for Fiscal
Year (FY) 2021 is the first-year implementation plan for the five-year ConPlan; it is the City's application for CDBG and HOME
entitlement funds, defines the programs and projects to be funded in the FY, outlines the goals and accomplishments for the year,
and sets a detailed budget for the use of HUD funds. Amendments to the AAP are required to add or delete a program; change a
program's purpose, scope, location, or intended beneficiaries; or implement a budget change of20 percent or greater of the City's
HUD budget. The proposed FY 2021 amendment would:
               • Adjust entitlement amounts;
               • Update program income estimates for the year;
               •   Revise carryover budget from estimates to actual;
               • Allocate the additional funds; and
               • Modify goals for the year.

     Proposed Amendment to the Definition of Linear Block
\              Council Deputy Director Symes explained a petition has been filed by Thaine Fischer to amend the definition of a linear
 y
     block. The amendment, as proposed, would change the definition of a linear block to include only one side of a street rather than
     both sides. The intent of the amendment is to allow additional drinking establishments within the downtown area, particularly on
     25th Street. The Planning C01mnission has recommended denial of the petition. The Council reviewed the petition at its April 7,
     2020 work session. The Council requested additional information at that meeting which was reviewed at the November 10, 2020
     meeting. After discussion at the November 10, 2020 meeting, Council members indicated a desire to develop a proposal that
     would allow additional drinking establishments in the downtown area and gave City staff direction to work on a proposal. After
     the November 10, 2020 meeting, City staff consulted with Council leadership and developed a proposal to allow three (3) drinking
     establishments on each side of a linear block with any drinking establishments on corners counting toward the total number on
     that block. The proposal would apply only to the Central Business District Intensive (CBDI) zone.
               Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the Council with background
     information regarding the discussion of this item to date; the present linear block regulation defines linear block as both sides of
     the street within multiples of 100. The petition focuses on drinking establishments and not other uses that may use linear block as
     a means of avoiding concentrations of a specific use. Under the present regulation, there is only one area of nonconformity on
     upper 25 th Street. Following the November 10, 2020 meeting, Planning staff has met with Council staff, leadership, and the
     petitioner to provide additional options for Council consideration. The current proposal for consideration is to allow three drinking
     establishments per each side of the street in the CBDI zone; there are some areas along 25 th Street that will adhere to State
     restrictions due to the presence of a community location, such as a school or church. All other areas of the City's downtown would
     be governed by the present regulation. Additionally, there will be a change to the City's business license ordinance and zoning
     regulations. Comer buildings will be counted as one of the three establishments allowed per block; outside the CBDI, the property
     address determines block requirement.
               Discussion centered on the specific differences between present regulations and proposed regulations, with Council
     member Hyer stated his concern that there is the potential to increase the number of drinking establishments on 25 th Street from
     two to six. He stated he wondered why the City would allow an increase in density of that type ofuse when there are opportunities
     for expansion under the present ordinance on other streets in the CBDI, such as Washington Boulevard. Mr. Montgomery stated
     that due to recent changes in State law regarding' the ratio of food/alcohol sales and how that influences the classification of a
     drinking establishment, there was a reduction in the number of licenses allowed in the City. The petitioner has indicated that for
     some establishments where food is the key element, the sales for alcohol will still exceed the ratio allowed by State law and that is
     why he is pursuing this amendment to increase the number of establishments that are allowed; however, the total number that will
     be allowed is still less than would have been allowed in 1992 or even 1980 when the City began to work to revitalize and
     redevelop 25 th Street. Assistant City Attorney Stratford added that it is important to take into account that a comer property will be
     counted towards the total number of establishments allowed on one side of the street.
               Mr. Symes concluded by noting that ifthere are no objections from the Council, staff will proceed with scheduling final
     action on the item early in January 2021.
