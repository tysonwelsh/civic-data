CONSIDERATION _ OF RESOLUTION _NO._R-2019-2516, A RESOLUTION
APPROVING THE PURCHASE OF 22.73 ACRES OF LAND IN THE RED CLIFFS
DESERT RESERVE

Chairman Cox stated that as part of the Washington County Habitat Conservation Plan
(HCP), the facilitation of the governmental acquisition of private property inside the Red
Cliffs Desert Reserve has been initiated. Washington County has budgeted $1 Million
dollars of endangered species impact fee funds as a local match for the state grant. Brennan
Holdings is requesting that the County purchase the land at the federally appraised value
due to the length of time the grant is taking to be finalized. The federal grant process is not
expected to be finalized until early 2020.

Commissioner Iverson clarified that funds used to acquire the land come from the HCP
budget which is funded from impact fees when property is built on land that would
otherwise be protected.

CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. _R-2019-2517, A RESOLUTION

APPROVING TOURISM FUNDS TO SPRINGDALE FOR A VISITOR AND
HISTORY CENTER

Springdale Councilman Jeff Carlson explained that the Town of Springdale desires to build
a visitor and history center in collaboration with Zion National Park, Paiute Indian Tribe
of Utah, Springdale History Society, Zion Canyon Visitor’s Bureau, Zion Forever Project,
Rotary Club of Zion Canyon and Z Arts to support local tourism programs and events and
to educate visitors about the area.

Deputy Tourism Director Brittany McMichael explained that Springdale Town requested
$390,000 for the project. The Tourism Tax Advisory Board (TAB) recommended $390,000
be allocated for the construction, maintenance and operation of the visitor and history
center from the Tourism funds.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Almquist to Adopt Resolution No. R-2019-
2517, A Resolution Approving Tourism Funds to Springdale for a
Visitor and History Center. Motion seconded by Commissioner Iverson
and carried by unanimous vote, with Commissioners Cox, Iverson and
Almquist voting aye.

APPROVING AN AGREEMENT BETWEEN WASHINGTON COUNTY AND
PROGRESSIVE CONTRACTING, INC. FOR THE ENTERPRISE LIBRARY
PARKING LOT CURBING AND PAVING PROJECT

Washington County Library Director Joel Tucker explained that an additional parking lot

CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. R-2019-2519, A RESOLUTION
APPROVING AN AGREEMENT BETWEEN WASHINGTON COUNTY AND
PROGRESSIVE CONTRACTING, INC. FOR THE NEW HARMONY LIBRARY
PARKING LOT CURBING AND PAVING PROJECT

Mr. Tucker explained that a pavilion was added to the New Harmony Library parking lot
as part of the Recreation Arts and Parks (RAP) grant. In addition, a new parking lot will
provide necessary parking near the pavilion as the current parking lot is gravel. The project
was anticipated to cost $25,000, however, the cost came in at $46,046.86. Mr. Tucker
explained that there are funds available in the library’s budget to offset the unexpected cost.

County and Progressive Contracting, Inc. for the New Harmony
Library Parking Lot Curbing and Paving Project. Motion seconded by
Commissioner Almquist and carried by unanimous vote, with
Commissioners Cox, Iverson and Almquist voting aye.

CONSIDERATION _OF RESOLUTION NO. R-2019-2520, A RESOLUTION

US DEPARTMENT __OF_ _ AGRICULTURE _ NATURAL _ RESOURCES
CONSERVATION SERVICE FOR THE EWP WASHINGTON COUNTY NEW
HARMONY AND GRASS VALLEY PROJECT

Washington County Public Works’ Director Ron Whitehead explained that Emergency
Watershed Protection (EWP) funding will be used to complete the design and installation
of EWP measures in the Grass Valley and New Harmony areas.

Mr. Whitehead further explained that this contract will likely come before the Commission
to be resigned as the dates will be slightly modified from the current contract. The fiscal
year for the Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS) ended on Monday,
September 30th resulting in the funds having to be returned for redistribution. It is
anticipated that funds will be received quickly once the redistribution has taken place.

from the US Department of Agriculture Natural Resources

and Grass Valley Project Subject to New Contract and Legal Review.

CONSIDERATION _OF APPROVAL OF PUBLIC WORKS' QUARTERLY
REPORT

Mr. Whitehead reported that the Geographical Information System (GIS) Department is
working on updating the mapping and parcel data service simultaneously. The Weed
department fell behind in the spring due to the abnormal wet weather. Certain roads were
sprayed multiple times due to weeds continuing to come back. Mr. Whitehead mentioned
that the Weed Department head, Dale Gubler, will retire soon.

The Road Department has completed the Pinto Bridge replacement. The Mesa Road has

been chip sealed and has been improved tremendously. Final road grading for the fall is
being worked on to make sure that the roads are in good condition before winter.

COMMISSIONERS' REPORT

County Administrator Nicholle Felshaw mentioned that the parking lot between the
Boulevard and Administration Building is anticipated to be opened by October 2, 2019.

Commissioner Almquist reported that he had the opportunity to speak with Lieutenant
Governor Spencer Cox to discuss Washington County’s current water issues.
