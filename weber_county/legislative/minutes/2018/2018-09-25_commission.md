                                                           MINUTES
                                              WEBER COUNTY COMMISSION
                                            Tuesday, September 25, 2018 - 10:00 a.m.
                                    Commission Chambers, 2380 Washington Blvd., Ogden, Utah
In accordance with the requirements of Utah Code Annotated Section 52-4-203, the County Clerk records in the minutes the names of all persons who appear and speak at a
County Commission meeting and the substance “in brief” of their comments. Such statements may include opinion or purported facts. The County does not verify the accuracy
or truth of any statement but includes it as part of the record pursuant to State law.

WEBER COUNTY COMMISSIONERS: James “Jim” H. Harvey and Scott K. Jenkins. James Ebert was excused.
OTHER STAFF PRESENT: Ricky D. Hatch, County Clerk/Auditor; Christopher Crockett, Deputy County
Attorney; and Fátima Fernelius, of the Clerk/Auditor’s Office, who took minutes.
A. WELCOME – Chair Harvey
B. INVOCATION – Leanne Kilts
C. PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE – Nathan Hale
D. THOUGHT OF THE DAY – Chair Harvey
F. PRESENTATIONS:
   1. PRESENTATION FROM THE WEBER COUNTY CREATIVE ALLIANCE.
      Scott Patria, Weber County Creative Alliance Vice Chair, stated that the Alliance works to advance
      the connection between all forms of creativity and the business community, that there is a strong link
      between economic development and the creative sector, that cultural activities lead to additional
      spending at local businesses (sometimes as much as 75% of the business), etc. He outlined examples
      of significant economic impacts in Utah cities including that Park City’s Sundance Festival generated
      $191 million in economic activity and 3,300 jobs. He summarized the Alliance’s accomplishments in
      the past year with only a $25,000 contribution and asked for the Commission’s continued support.
      2. PRESENTATION FROM THE UTAH CULTURAL ALLIANCE.
         Crystal Young-Otterstrom, Utah Cultural Alliance Executive Director, stated that the Alliance works
         to amplify cultural contributions to Utah’s economic family and community life. A study by the
         Economic Development Corporation of Utah last year cited that the number #1 factor for a business
         in choosing a location is a vibrant cultural scene. She cited figures for the State and said that Utah is
         one of the two fastest growing states for cultural job growth, that in Weber County there are 8,694
         industry jobs generating $189.7 million, and that $5.6 million is added by non-profits, such as
         Alliance. The low amount of money that they are spending is definitely being returned exponentially.
         She asked for a contribution of $50,000 for 2019, of $75,000 for 2020 and of $100,000 for 2121.
