Planning Manager, Caitlyn Tubbs reported that Staff would make one presentation followed by a
presentation by the applicant. Because each legislative item requires its own individual public
hearing, the Commission would open public hearings for each item prior to casting votes.

Ms. Tubbs presented the Staff Report and for the 99 at the Grove Bringhurst Station Project, which
encompasses a Development Agreement, General Plan Map Amendment,Zoning Map Amendment,
and Text Amendment of the Bringhurst Station Project Plan Zoning Ordinance. The subject
properties were identified. The northern parcel is already located within the Bringhurst Station
Project and is zoned for those entitlements. However,the applicant revised the Project Plan to include
the southem parcel as well. That parcel is currently zoned A-5 Agricultural and designated Very-
Low Density on the General Plan and thus requires the aforementioned amendments. The subject
property was originally entitled to 13 detached single-family homes and a hammock park, but the
applicant revised the plan for the area to incorporate 99 townhome imits with private amenities and
open space.


A requirement for properties in special zones such as the SD-X Bringhurst Station Zone is that the
developer meet with the City Council in a Work Session to discuss the project and address any
questions or concerns prior to filing applications. The applicant went before the City Council on May

22, 2024, and presented a Concept Plan similar to the one included in the Meeting Materials Packet.
Feedback from the City Council indicated that active open space and access to it is of paramount
importance in this neighborhood and the Council expected open space amenities to provide those
opportunities.


Ms. Tubbs reviewed the Concept Plan, which includes a road that connects to a Salt Lake County-
owned parcel to the south and a northern road that will eventually connect to Railcar Lane at the
northern end of the existing Bringhurst Station development. The property is flat in some areas. In
other areas,there is a somewhat steep slope to the east toward the Jordan River. The slope is steepest
in the northeastern comer of the property, which the applicant proposes to maintain as native open
space. The existing mature trees will be preserved as part of the project. A privately owned and
maintained playground and seating area will be installed in the northem portion ofthe project. The
northwestem seating area is proposed to connect to the Jordan River Trail.

Ms. Tubbs noted that Bluffdale City and Salt Lake County have been working on an Interlocal
Agreement to exchange City-owned property for the property to the south ofthe subject property for
development of a pocket park.

The development will include both full public right-of-way and private streets. The private streets
will be 35 feet wide, not 52 feet as required for public rights-of-way. One guest parking space is
required for every four townhome units and the applicant will be providing 32 parking spaces, which
exceeds the 25 required. In addition, each townhome unit will have two off-street parking spaces
within a two-car garage as well as a 20-foot driveway.

One reason the City and applicant decided on a Development Agreement for this phase ofBringhurst
Station was with regard to amenities. The amenities that were approved as part ofthe original project
plan were labeled as disc golf, a model train park. Bluff Park, trails, open space, etc. As part ofthe
project review. Staff conducted site visits and determined that all the previously required and
approved amenities had not yet been installed. They discussed the matter with the applicant and
drafted the proposed Development Agreement to ensure that the value of those amenities would be
incorporated into the newest phase ofthe project. As part ofthat conversation,the applicant provided

In response to a question raised by Acting Chair Luker, Ms. Tubbs stated that the Moderate-Income
Housing Plan is an element ofthe City's General Plan that is mandated by the State to support housing
affordability.   Bluffdale is required to provide information annually regarding its housing
development goals and the measures the City is taking to advance them.

Commissioner Flynn asked why the private sections ofthe.roads will be narrower than the standard
52 feet for public streets. Ms.Tubbs stated that the proposed public roads will provide opportunities
for connection to adjacent properties if those properties are developed in the future. The narrower
width on the private, dead-end streets will hopefully discourage thru traffic in those areas. In response
to a follow-up question, Ms. Tubbs indicated that the grading challenges were one of many
considerations in opting for the narrower roads.


In response to a question from Commissioner Kraupp, Ms. Tubbs reported that the Fire Marshall has
reviewed the Site Plan and submitted a letter expressing his support for the project which was included
in the Meeting Materials Packet as Exhibit J.


Acting Chair Luker asked ifemergency vehicles can still get through if cars are parked on both sides
of the road. Ms. Tubbs clarified that the public streets are wide enough to allow parking but it will
be up to the developer to determine if parking is allowed on the private streets.

Commissioner Kraupp asked for Staffs perspective on the $198,000 contribution for open space.
Ms. Tubbs stated that they believe it will be enough to purchase a small play structure and benches
for the future pocket park. In response to a question from Commissioner Kraupp regarding the
underwater stormwater storage, Ms. Tubbs reported that the water would be piped out to other
drainage facilities.


Brandon Watson with Edge Homes thanked the Commission and noted that Staff had been very
helpful. He then provided an overview ofthe project.

The Bringhurst Station project includes single-family and townhomes. Mr. Watson commented that
he is proud ofthe community. He believes the Grove will help Bluffdale meet its attainable housing
goals. The project will have direct access to Redwood Road through Bringhurst Boulevard and the
roadways in the area can handle the additional traffic. To access the Grove, Edge Homes must build
a large access across both the Jordan River Trail and Utah Lake Distributing Canal. They are working
with Salt Lake County and the Canal Company to ensure that they preserve those accesses. They also
received approval for a regional sewer lift station from the Jordan Basin Improvement District. The
upfront costs to prepare the site will be approximately $2 million. Mr. Watson noted that the
townhomes will be for sale.


Community Amenities will include the following:

       Two-car garages and two driveway parking spaces.
       46% ofthe site will be open space.
       Picnic tables, park benches, and other outdoor amenities.
   • Enhanced Jordan River Trail along the property's frontage.

Mr. Watson reviewed the Site Plan and indicated that there will be two styles of townhomes. One
style will be three stories with three bedrooms and two-and-a-halfbathrooms. The other is two stories
with a basement that is initially three bedrooms and two-and-a-half bathrooms but can be expanded
to five bedrooms, and 3    bathrooms. A train playground will be installed on the northwest of the
property in keeping with the SD-X Bringhurst Station Zone. The playground is directly adjacent to
the Jordan Narrows Trail and will include seating, a pergola, a ping-pong table, and a chess/checkers
board. The wooded area to the northeast had a lot of deadfall, but also many healthy mature trees.
Edge Homes intends to clean out and preserve the area.


With respect to the $2,000 per unit contribution in addition to Impact Fees, Mr. Watson stated that
some amenities were planned for Bringhurst Station that looked great on paper but were not feasible
due to the very steep slopes in areas of the development. That is part of the reason they are
contributing the $198,000 to the City in addition to the approximately $650,000 in Park Impact Fees.

If the land exchange with the County goes through, they hope the funds will be used for the nearby
pocket park.

Mr. Watson noted that this portion of the Jordan River Trail is highly traveled and Edge Homes
intends to improve the trail frontage to include waterwise landscaping and a four-foot wrought iron
fence to separate the public and private spaces. Due to the difference in grade, a retaining wall will
also be installed. The townhomes will be set back approximately 30 feet from the trail.

The picnic area will include seating under pergolas, picnic tables, ping-pong, and chess/checkers
tables. The concept for this area includes a second trail at a slightly higher grade. Mr. Watson
explained that the road will cross over the Jordan River Trail, and the second trail is to allow
community residents access to the trail.


Architectural enhancements for the townhomes along the trail include additional popouts and
different materials. Planned community accessories and concept plans for each townhome style were
then reviewed.


Commissioner Kraupp asked Mr. Watson to address Commissioner Fl)am's earlier question regarding
road width, as well as to discuss Edge Home's planned timeline for the project. Mr. Watson clarified
that the stubs being provided for future connection to properties to the north and south would be wide
enough to convey traffic to those outside properties. For the private roadways, they wanted to keep
the townhomes as far from the wooded area as practical, and those roads would be more like shared
driveways to access the townhomes. Commissioner Flynn asked about parking enforcement on the
private roads. Mr. Watson stated that parking will be restricted to one side ofthe road to ensure room
for two cars or emergency vehicles to pass. They will install red curbs or signage to denote this
restriction.


Edge Homes is anxious to begin work on the project and intends to move immediately into the
Preliminary Plat process once City Council approval is received. They plan to begin the project in
2025 and hope to start construction ofthe townhome units by next winter.

Commissioner Flynn asked if the retention wall near the Jordan River Trail will include drainage.
Mr. Watson clarified that they typically run a storm drainpipe from the street and around the rear of
the units, with Nyloplast boxes at regular intervals for drainage. City Code requires that the water be
drained away from the units. They typically install a 10-foot wing wall on each unit to separate the
yards. There will be restrictions for the homes along the trail that prohibit the installation of an
additional fence.


In response to a question regarding pricing, Mr. Watson stated that potential homeowners will need
an annual household income ofmore than $100,000 to afford the units. The units will likely be in the
$400,000 to $500,000 range. Commissioner Kraupp expressed concern that the price point may not
meet requirements for moderate-income housing or be consistent with those objectives. Ms. Tubbs
reported that moderate income is based on the County-wide average, which is typically between 60%
and 80% of the County's average income. In response to a follow-up question fi*om Conunissioner
Griffis, Ms. Tubbs stated that she did not know what the average is but could obtain that information.
Commissioner Kraupp indicated that the information was important because they were making the
point that the development would meet those objectives.

Community and Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, clarified that the State's goals
include quantities of housing and targeting specific income. Bluffdale's plan indicates that the City
should look to rezone areas for higher density or mixed use,so the development will qualify regardless
ofthe income requirements.

In response to a question fi-om Acting Chair Luker, Ms. Tubbs confirmed that the General Plan Map
Amendment and Zoning Text Amendment were required to include the southern 5.06-acre parcel and
equal the 11.4 total acres for the 99 at the Grove and bring it into the SD-X Bringhurst Station Zone.
They also need to consider the Development Agreement, which is a contract between the City and
Edge Homes and will entitle the project. The agreement covers amenities, timing, costs, etc. The
General Plan Map Amendment and Zoning Text Amendment are specific to the southern parcel.
Because the General Plan does not currently support a higher density use on that parcel, it must be
amended. The Zoning Map Amendment necessitates the General Plan Map Amendment,and the SD-
X Zone must be amended to incorporate the new entitlements.
