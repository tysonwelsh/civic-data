      C. "SAXEY REZONE”, PLZ-26-4003, AN APPLICATION TO REZONE
         APPROXIMATELY 2.82-ACRES, LOCATED AT 12317, 12325, AND 12341
         SOUTH REDWOOD ROAD, FROM THE RR-22 AND RM-6 ZONES TO THE
         RM-14-D ZONE, AND TO AMEND THE GENERAL PLAN FROM MEDIUM
         HIGH-DENSITY RESIDENTIAL TO HIGH-DENSITY RESIDENTIAL.
         APPLICANT – ERIC SAXEY.

City Planner Tim Prestwich reported that the item was a request to amend the General
Plan to designate the property High-Density Residential and rezone it to RM-14-D. An
aerial map was displayed indicating that the subject parcels are deep and narrow, and
each property had an existing single-family home. The southern parcel was currently
zoned RR-22. The two northern properties were rezoned from RR-22 to RM-6-SD in
2022. However, that developer did not follow through with a Site Plan, and the properties
remained single-family residential. The applicant, Eric Saxey, had purchased the three
properties with the intention of developing them.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the General Plan and Zoning Maps match in most areas of
the City, but some areas such as the east side of Redwood Road have a different future
vision. The current General Plan designated the area and subject property as Medium-
High-Density Residential, which allows for between five and height dwelling units per acre
and correlates to the RM-6 and RM-8 Zones. The applicant had requested rezoning to
RM-14-D, which would allow up to 14 units per acre and require a General Plan
Amendment to change the designation to High-Density Residential. He noted that the
High-Density Residential designation specified eight to 12 units per acre, but there were
several higher-density zones in the City. That issue would be addressed when the
updated General Plan Land Use Element was adopted.

A 2001 General Plan Map was reviewed indicating that the subject area was previously
designated for multi-family or other high-density residential. Mr. Prestwich noted that the
map was amended to the current designation in 2006.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the RM-14-D Zone would allow for condominiums,
townhomes, and residential planned developments such as the RM-14 attached
townhome development behind Petersen’s Fresh Market. The applicant was not required
to present a Conceptual Plan but had done so to help the Planning Commission visualize
the potential development. The plan would not be adopted and had not been reviewed
for compliance with City Code. The 39-unit Conceptual Plan was reviewed, and Mr.
Prestwich indicated that any modifications to meet setback or other requirements would
result in fewer units.

Notices were sent to property owners within 1,000 feet of the subject property and multiple
comments were received. All comments received before 5:00 p.m. had been forwarded
to the Planning Commission, and any received after that cutoff would be reviewed by the
City Council.

      E. “TABLE OF COMMERCIAL USES,” PLZ-26-5001, RIVERTON CITY IS
         CONSIDERING AMENDMENTS TO CITY CODE TITLE 18, CHAPTER 90
         “TABLE OF COMMERCIAL USES.”

   Mr. Prestwich reported that the amendment was required to correct technical issues.
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                                11
March 12, 2026


   Utah Code 10-20-507 now requires that the process for evaluating uses not listed in
   the Table of Commercial Uses be specified, as well as how the table will be interpreted
   and amended. The City’s standard process was being codified, but no changes to the
   process were proposed.

   Five text changes were also proposed to clarify items that could be better defined.

      1. A “Churches, Religious and Cultural Buildings” Land Use Category would be
         added to the table. This would unify City Code across multiple zones and
         eliminate confusion, and the use would be conditional in all zones.

      2. The “Bed and Breakfast” Land Use Category would be changed to “Not
         Permitted” in all commercial zones. As short-term rentals are allowed as
         conditional uses in residential zones, this use could be eliminated from
         commercial zones as they would fall within the Commercial Hotels category.
         This would reduce requests to convert an older home to commercial use but
         utilize part of the home as a bed and breakfast.

      3. The following categories would be removed as they reference specific outdated
         activities that could be reasonably included in the “General Retail and
         Wholesale” category:
             a. Apparel Alteration and Shoe Repair
             b. Apparel and Accessories, Retail and Rental (apparel, accessories,
                 tailoring, fur, shoes)
             c. Artist and Related Services
             d. Books, Stationery, Art/Hobby Supplies, retail

      4. The “General Retail and Wholesale” Land Use Category would be changed to
         a conditional use in the C-N and C-PO Zones to add a layer of scrutiny to
         enforce the goals of these zones.

      5. The “Manufacturing, Fabrication, and Product Assembly, internal use only”
         Land Use Category would be changed to a conditional use in the C-R Zone to
         expand available uses in that zone.

Vice-Chair Beck opened the public hearing. There were no comments. The public

Commissioner Cluff moved that the Planning Commission recommend APPROVAL
of Application PLZ-26-5001 amending Section 18.90.010, Table of Commercial
Uses, with amendments as described herein. Commissioner Marzo seconded the
motion. The motion passed with unanimous consent of the Commission.
