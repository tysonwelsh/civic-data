Study Session

       Update: Major Road Capital Improvement Projects
       City Engineer Scott Cooley provided an update on current and planned road capital
       improvement projects and answered questions from the Council.

       Presentation: 2024 Moderate Income Housing Report
       The Moderate Income Housing Plan was adopted into the General Plan in 2022,
       with an annual report and update as required by the State. Todd Taylor, Planner III,
       presented a 2024 Moderate Income Housing Report.

       Responding to a question from Councilmember Green, staff said the State had not
       been specific about the number of residential units expected to be included in
       Station Area Plans (SAPs). Mr. Taylor said the current draft of the Town Center SAP
       included approximately 560 residential units. Councilmember Green said he
       wanted to include as few residential units as possible. The Council discussed that
       the City’s annual Moderate Income Housing Update must be approved by a third
       party (Wasatch Front Regional Council) with somewhat ambiguous approval
       standards. Councilmember T. Lowery asked to see the most recent draft of the
       Town Center SAP. The Council and staff talked about the need for location-
       appropriate residential units and parking.

     July 16, 2024                          OFFICIAL LETTER OF COMPLAINT

     Mayor Troy K. Walker
     Council Member Mike Green
     Council Member Bryn Heather Johnson
     Council Member Tasha Lowery
     Council Member Fred Lowry
     Council Member Cal Roberts

 Honorable Mayor Walker and Distinguished Council Members:
       We, the undersigned neighbors along the corridor of Willow Creek between 300 East and 150 East, do
 hereby petition the City of Draper to address the problem of six foot+ high Russian Thistles growing along the
 creek bed in the above-mentioned location. We have repeatedly brought this ongoing problem to the
 attention of your Code Enforcement Officer, to Greg in your Draper Parks Dept. and to Drew in your Storm
 Water Maintenance Dept. We began kindly advising your Code Enforcement that the thistles were growing
 rapidly on June 3, 2024. We reached out to Greg in the Parks Dept. and were referred to the Storm Water
 Dept. who returned our call on July 3. 2024 and informed us that Draper had sprayed the road bed along
 the creek and that the thistles were the responsibility of Salt Lake County Flood Control. When we called
 Salt Lake County Flood Control, they tell us that they are only responsible for the flow of the water and
 that Draper owns the land and they are responsible for the out-of-control vegetation. We call Draper and
 they point the finger at the County. and we call the County and they point the finger at Draper. That is our
 problem.

       Let it be said that the residents on the east side of 300 East on the Willow Creek corridor have a
 beautifully manicured lawnscape that is maintained by the City of Draper. Please be advised that
 those of us residents on the west side of 300 East on the Willow Creek corridor pay our taxes
                                                                                                       5|Page
iust like the people on the east side of 300 East. We are entitled to the same amenities as the
people on the east side of 300 East.

       We are not asking for a manicured lawnscape. We are requesting that the six feet tall Russian
Thistles be cut down and removed immediately. They must be removed to prevent the spread of the
thistles because the seeds travel even when the thistles have been cut down. When these noxious weeds
bloom, they spew forth seeds that infest our properties and take hours to remove in addition to the cost of
pesticides to control them.

     When we do not follow the Draper laws in maintenance of our properties, we are issued a citation
by code enforcement. When Draper does not follow the Draper laws in maintenance of their property,
they are allowed to ignore our requests and there is no price to pay for Draper City and they conveniently
point their finger at the Salt Lake County Flood Control.

     We respectfully bring this problem to your attention in an effort to establish better communication
between us, the residents, Draper City and Salt Lake County Flood Control. We are willing to work with the
City, but we are now held hostage by the finger pointing of one government dept. versus another government
dept. This is not effective or efficient. For the taxes we pay in Draper, we expect better from our Draper City.


Respectfully submitted by the following residents:
