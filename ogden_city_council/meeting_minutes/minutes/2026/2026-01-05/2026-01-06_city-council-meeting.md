Fiscal Year 2024–2025 Audit Report; AND
Fiscal Year 2024–2025 Annual Comprehensive Financial Report
         The Fiscal Year (FY) 2025 Audit Report and Annual Comprehensive Financial Report (ACFR) came before the Council
for consideration. The ACFR is prepared by the City Administration each year as part of the fiscal year-end budget process and
provides an overall picture of the City’s funds, investments, assets, and debt – information over and above the regular annual
budget. The City’s budget provides a plan for revenues and expenditures, while the ACFR provides an overview of the prior
year’s financial activity. The Auditor’s report is a summary of the auditor’s review and findings regarding the City’s overall
financial position. The report also identifies any issues with internal controls and may provide findings that will need to be
addressed before completion of the next audit cycle.
         Finance Director Sorensen introduced the audit report and noted that it had been uploaded to the City website for public
access. He thanked Administration, the City Council, and Management Services staff, especially Korahle Jensen, Assistant
Finance Director, for their work in preparing for the audit. Mr. Sorensen highlighted that the fund balances for governmental and
enterprise funds increased by 3.38 percent over the prior year, indicating the City is in a better financial position than last year. He
noted the City increased long-term debt by about $9 million related to the purchase of the Forest Service building and the special
purpose entity created for new markets tax credit. He also pointed out that the City invested over $110 million in capital
infrastructure, including the Canyon Pipeline, Marshall White Center, and downtown parking structure.
          Jeff Miles, Audit Partner with HBME, LLC, briefly discussed the City’s annual audit process and his coordination with
City Administration; he presented the audit results, reporting that his firm had issued a clean or unmodified opinion on the
financial statements. He stated there was one finding related to State compliance regarding the budget of the new special purpose
entity, which was formed late in the budget year and had some expenditures not budgeted for. He reported that the City's internal
controls were strong with no significant deficiencies identified and no proposed journal entries during the audit. He concluded by
congratulating the City’s financial staff on a job well done.

           Several residents addressed the Council regarding the closure of a trail through the Mount Ogden golf course. Speakers
included Chloe Reynolds, Kim Haslam, Chase Reynolds, Heath Satow, Wesley Calgren, Zach Colton, Marilee Blackham, Tom
Carlton, Thor Rush, Jason Bach, Ashley Davis, Curt Ward, John Wheelwright, Lindsey Anderson, and Justin Haslam.
           The trail users expressed frustration with the City's decision to permanently close the trail and with the process that led to
the decision. Their main concerns included:
                ● The survey methodology was flawed, with emails sent to 25,000 golfer addresses while trail users had to rely
                     on social media and word-of-mouth.
                ● QR codes for the survey were posted at golf courses but not at trailheads.
                ● The timing of the data release coincided with the final decision, leaving no opportunity for trail users to provide
                     input on the results.
                ● The survey data showed initial support for the trail (around 80% in the first 18 days) before a spike in opposing
                     votes in the last few days changed the overall results.
                ● Some survey respondents who voted "no" actually stated in comments they wanted the trail to remain open,
                     suggesting confusion about the question.
                ● The trail had existed for decades before being closed, with no documented safety incidents.
                ● A compromise reroute option had been developed with City officials but was ultimately rejected.
                ● The possible Bureau of Reclamation easement or right-of-way that might affect the City's ability to close the
                     trail.
           Laura Lewis spoke in support of the trail closure, stating that as both a hiker and golfer, she felt the right decision was
made for safety reasons and noted the trail was never officially part of the golf course.
           Theresa Bramwell welcomed the new council members, noting that three live in East Central Ogden. She expressed hope
that East Central would receive more attention after years of having tax money diverted to other areas of the City.
           Several residents also raised concerns about Flock surveillance cameras in East Central Ogden. Taylor Knuth and Sean
Bishop questioned why cameras were concentrated in neighborhoods below Harrison Boulevard and requested information about
which agencies can access the data, how long it's stored, and the expansion of the system beyond its original scope.
Council members thanked citizens for their comments and engagement.
           In response to the public comments, several Council members expressed concern about the trail closure process while
acknowledging it was an Administrative decision outside the Council’s authority.
           Council member Lundell noted he had taught his son to ride a bike on the trail in question and recognized its importance
for accessing higher trails from different neighborhoods.
           Council member Washington acknowledged the frustration in the room and committed to continuing conversations about
the trails, Flock cameras, and East Central development issues.
           Council member Richey suggested exploring off-hours trail access as a potential compromise with the community that
desires the trail to be reopened.

Mayor Comments
           Mayor Nadolski addressed public comments about the golf course trail. He explained that he had worked in good faith
with trail users to find a compromise route and put the proposal out for public input. He stated that the email about the survey was
sent once at the beginning, without follow-up emails, and relied on social media and trail community networks for further
distribution. The Mayor clarified that he was trying to make a decision for all recreational users of the area, viewing both golf and
trails as important parts of Ogden's outdoor recreation identity. He stated that the final decision to maintain the closure was based
on the survey results which showed a balanced community response, not a clear majority either way. Regarding the Flock
cameras, the Mayor stated they were placed based on criminal activity patterns, traffic flows, and data analysis, not based on
neighborhood demographics. He denied that Ogden police were working under the direction from federal Immigrations and
Customs Enforcement (ICE) and offered to have the Police Chief meet with concerned residents to answer questions about the
camera system's use and privacy protections.

Council member Comments
         Council member Choberka, in her final meeting after eight years on the Council, reflected on the importance of
considering who has access to government officials, who gets responses to inquiries, and who benefits from decisions. She
encouraged the Council to think about power imbalances in community engagement and decision-making.
         Chair Hyer acknowledged the difficulty of the trail decision and encouraged citizens to continue communication with
Council members through email and phone.
