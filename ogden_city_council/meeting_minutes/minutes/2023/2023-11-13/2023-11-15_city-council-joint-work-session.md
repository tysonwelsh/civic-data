Affordable Housing and Home Ownership Discussion
The City Council is interested in ascertaining whether there are additional policies or programs that the City could
undertake to facilitate home ownership. The Council has invited developers, experts in commercial lending, non-profits, and
investment groups to share their expertise with the Council. The Council hopes that these discussions will lead to new
opportunities for home ownership in Ogden. This discussion will focus on project financing and the developer perspective.
Bill Knowlton, Pathway Communities, LLC, stated he is an affordable housing developer and attorney, and he used the
aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss Ogden’s barriers to homeownership. He provided information about the history and
experience of Pathway Communities, LLC, which is based in Salt Lake City and Phoenix, Arizona. He presented photographs to
illustrate the effects of the housing crisis across the United States, which is an increase in homelessness in large cities throughout
the country. In the United States, there is an annual shortage of 3.6 million affordable rental homes; home prices are rising faster
than wages in roughly 80 percent of U.S. markets and the average home price in Ogden is $451,900. The average rentalratein |
Weber County is now $1,300, which requires an income of $52,000 to qualify as a tenant. Utah's housing shortage decreased from
56,800 units in 2017 to 28,400 units in 2022; however, as homebuilding activity contracts, new households will outnumber new
housing units. Consequently, Utah's housing shortage will likely increase to over 37,000 units by 2024. He presented a graph
illustrating the increase in housing shortage from 2010 to 2014, noting the 2023 forecast for the number of residential units

fact that LIHTC projects cannot include for-sale units; the availability of affordable housing funds for municipalities; examples of

Ward and his team for the great deal of information provided tonight and indicated it will be necessary to continue the discussion

"Fiscal Year 2023-2024 Budget Amendment — Commercial Airline Incentive
Community and Economic Development Director Cooper explained Administration is proposing a Fiscal Year (FY)
2023-2024 Budget Amendment of $500,000 to provide a $250,000 incentive to anew airline that will begin operations at Ogden
Airport in February 2024. The new airline is Breeze Airways, and they would like to begin operations at the Ogden Hinckley
Airport (OGD) in February of 2024. He discussed the history of Breeze Airways, which began operations in 2021. They have 17
A220’s in their fleet with 63 more on order. They are serving 45 communities in 27 states and have flown two million guests in
2023. They will start by flying four weekly flights from OGD to John Wayne Airport in Orange County, California, with plans to
expand to other locations as demand dictates. The $250,000 incentive will be broken into three payments: $84,000 in March 2024,
$83,000 in July 2024, and $83,000 in November 2024. He discussed the potential revenue benefits to the Ogden Airport, including )
parking fees, landing fees, fuel flowage fees, car rentals, restaurant rental, ground handler rent, and TSA rent. He concluded the
source of funds, if approved, will be Business Depot Ogden (BDO) lease revenue. Breeze is demonstrating their commitment by
investing approximately $750,000 for startup costs plus additional ongoing costs thereafter. There is no further guarantee of
passengers. All Airport revenue, including entitlement funds, must ‘stay on the airport’ and can be used for any Airport
Improvement Program eligible project (AIP) that is solely for Airport use such as ramps, taxiways, runways, sewer, water, power,
etc.
Discussion among the Council and Mr. Cooper centered on Breeze’s plans to expand their services to other airports; the
markets they have withdrawn from in the past and the reasons for those withdrawals; the impact, if any, the pilot shortage is
having on Breeze’s operations; whether Breeze will provide return flights to OGD; differences between Breeze and Allegiant
Airlines; the option of breaking the incentive into three different budget amendments rather than a single budget amendment for
tracking purposes; and the risk being assumed by the City in conjunction with the incentive.
Council member White asked that the incentive agreement include a mechanism that will provide for any unused
incentive amount to be dedicated back to the General Fund for future appropriation or reallocation to the BDO lease revenue fund.
