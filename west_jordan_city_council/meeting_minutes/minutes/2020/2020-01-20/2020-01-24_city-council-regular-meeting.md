VI              PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE DISTRICT( PID)

Rob Wall introduced Randy Larsen and Aaron Wade from Gilmore Bell, a national public
finance law firm.


Randy Larsen explained that PIDs were a new economic development tool available to
cities. He recommended the City have a policy in place for protection prior to any formal
review of an application          for PID.


Aaron Wade explained that a PID would allow cities and counties to create an independent

local district as a financing tool, typically for new development. The new property tax
would be generated             from those who benefited        from the improvements.        The PID was
designed to balance city, resident, and developer interests.

Mr. Larsen        explained     the difference    between    a PID, special     assessment   bond,   and tax

increment financing. A PID could be used in similar situations to the other financing
methods, but would bring in new revenue, not redistribute existing property tax. He stated
PIDs are      most     often   used   for greenfield (   undeveloped     land), with only the city and the
developer       at the table. Homeowners/ commercial            owners    come into the picture   later. Mr.

Larsen said a PID would protect the city and its balance sheet in ways that are superior to
other tools.



Mr. Wade emphasized that a PID is a tool to be used at a city' s discretion, and said that if
a city were going to use the tool, consistent rules for application would be needed. Costs
associated with review of a PID application could be passed on to the developer. The
boundaries  of a PID would not have to be contiguous,  but 100% of property owners and
voters within the PID bounds would need to agree in order to establish a PID.


Mr. Wade said a PID can be used to finance different types of infrastructure that would not
necessarily need to be in the PID boundaries, but would need to benefit the community
within the PID boundaries.


A PID      would     be managed       by the governing     document. Over time, as residents      moved   in,

the PID board would transition to an elected board of residents within the district. The
governing document would state how and when the transition from developer to resident
would occur. When the bonds were paid off, the PID would dissolve on its own terms.


Mr. Wall explained that a PID could be used to incentivize commercial development, or to
facilitate master- planned communities instead of hodgepodge development. Rulon Dutson
with Daybreak Communities explained that a PID can help to stimulate and accelerate
development.


Mr. Larsen explained that once established, a PID could do either a limited tax revenue
bond or an assessment bond. A limited tax revenue bond is an enhanced property tax on
property within a PID, with limits set by the governing document. He commented that a
property owner could not prepay with a PID. Special service districts, and benefits of a PID
being separate from a city were explained. Responding to a question from Mr. Wall, Mr.
Larsen explained that a city could set a mill levy cap, but reserve the right to reduce the
cap.

VII.             MAYOR' S COMMENTS

           Mayor Burton talked about the importance                         of community,         the importance     of
           treating people well and giving the benefit of the doubt, and the importance of
           providing good customer service.
           The Mayor reported that Trans- Jordan Landfill would cease green- waste service in
           2023. The Sustainability Committee was asked to look into available options.
           Mayor Burton said he would like Councilmembers to continue to serve on City
           committees.

           Mayor       Burton      spoke     of the need to improve           east/ west   traffic   to accommodate

           existing and future development. He suggested an east/ west freeway that would
           extend over the mountain as one option, and asked for opinions from the Council.
           Councilmember Whitelock said she liked the idea. Councilmember Lamb said he
           was       100%      against. Councilmember Worthen said there was something to be said
           for not cutting through the City, but agreed that east/ west traffic needed to be
           improved.           Chair McConnehey              said he would be supportive                of starting the
           conversation. Councilmember Green said he believed engineering options needed
           to be explored for improving east/ west traffic. Councilmember Jacob agreed with
           starting the conversation. Councilmember Lamb said he would be supportive if all
           the land needed were within West Jordan, but pointed out that a lot of the land was
           in    other   cities.    Councilmember             Pack   said   he agreed      with   the   importance   of

           beginning the conversation, regardless of the complexity. The Mayor and Council
           discussed the need to work with Legislators on the issue.
           The Mayor reported that UDOT work on 9000 South was delayed until 2022 or
           2023.
