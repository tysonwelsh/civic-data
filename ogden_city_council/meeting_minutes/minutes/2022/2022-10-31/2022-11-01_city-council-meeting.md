Neighbor Up Grant Guidelines

A staff memo from Council staff explained that the Neighbor Up Grant Program launched in FY2018 for the purpose of
helping neighbors better connect with each other by strengthening a sense of community and improving the quality of life in
Ogden’s neighborhoods. The goals of the Program are as follows:

1. Build sense of community.

2. Develop or renew neighborhood relationships.

3. Encourage interaction between generations and cultures.

4. Increase governance and organizational leadership of neighborhood organizations or associations.

Staff is proposing the following language to be added to the guidelines: To comply with State tax code/laws and to
equitably supply grant funds city wide, only one (1) Neighbor Up Grant award per calendar year shall be issued to a specific
neighborhood, sponsor, and/or co-sponsor. This addition will allow for fair funding city wide and help with tax implications.
Currently anything awarded over $600 needs to be reported on an individual’s income tax. Citizens who receive grant funds are
not subject to paying taxes as the grant fund maximum is $575.

COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED TO ADD THE PROPOSED LANGUAGE TO THE NEIGHBOR UP
GRANT PROGRAM. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA, ALL VOTING AYE.

Proposed Ordinance 2022-45 amending the budget for Fiscal Year 2022-
2023 by increasing the anticipated revenues and appropriations for a gross
increase of $194,900 transferring CIP funds to the Redevelopment Agency
to_purchase and improve the Union Stockyard Exchange Building and
recognize new grant awards (Tabled from October 11, 2022)

A memo from the City Comptroller regarding a proposed amendment in the Fiscal Year (FY) 2023 budget came before
the Council for consideration. The memo stated the budget amendment would increase the FY2023 City budget by $194,900; it
involves transferring funds from the Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) budget to the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) for purchase
of the Exchange Building and for developer incentives ($845,000). The proposed budget amendment also recognizes revenues
from United Way for police vehicles to support homeless outreach efforts ($88,200), recognizes grant funds from the Utah
Commission on Criminal and Juvenile Justice (UCCJJ) for police equipment ($14,500), and recognizes RAMP EZ Grant funds for
purchase of equipment for the Golden Hours Center ($4,000).

Comptroller Stout summarized the memo and stated this proposed budget amendment is related to the Union Stock
Exchange Building project, which was during the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) meeting earlier this evening; Administration is
proposing to transfer $845,000 from the Ogden City Capital Improvement project #CD0 132 — Stock Exchange Building Purchase,
to the Redevelopment Agency. Agency staff proposes to use the transferred funds to purchase the Stock Exchange Building from
the current owner, OBE Vision, per the terms of the Master Land Transfer and Development Agreement. If approved by the
Board, Q Factor will purchase the Exchange building from the Agency for $200,000. Per the Participation and Incentive
Agreement, the Agency would provide $800,000 in incentives to complete redevelopment of the Exchange Building. The Agency
will also provide $40,000 in additional funds to cover fencing and boarding of the building. She also cited the budget amendments
relating to the receipt of funding from the United Way and grant funds from the UCCJJ and the Weber County Recreation, Arts,
Museum, and Parks (RAMP) grant program.

The Chair then called for a public hearing on proposed Ordinance 2022-45, entitled:

“An ordinance of Ogden City amending the budget for the Fiscal Year (FY) July 1, 2022 to June 30, 2023 by
increasing the anticipated revenues for a gross increase of $194,900 from sources as detailed in the body of this
ordinance; and increasing the appropriations for a gross increase of $194,900 as detailed in the body of this
ordinance; and providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final
passage.”

The Deputy City Recorder presented written notice of said hearing and affidavit of the Posting on the Utah State Public
Notice Website on the 30th day of September 2022, specifying the time and place of this meeting as the time and place when and
where the proposed ordinance would be given a public hearing and be considered for final passage.

Angel Castillo indicated she already expressed her concerns regarding the Union Stock Exchange matter during the RDA
meeting this evening. Relative to the other budget amendments, she is supportive of the intent of the adjustments as she
understands the importance of providing proper resources to the City’s homelessness advocates and relocation specialists. The
City needs to be doing its part to help those unhoused residents in the community rather than relying solely on Weber County.
Efforts to enable such individuals to become productive members of society are appropriate for the City to pursue.

There being no further persons appearing to be heard, COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED THE PUBLIC
HEARING BE CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA, ALL VOTING AYE.

Proposed Ordinance 2022-44 amending the Ogden Municipal Code to
revise the dimensional, architectural and material building requirements in
the Business Historical Zone (BEH), and to establish parking reduction
provisions in the BEH Zone (Tabled from October 11, 2022)

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed zone text amendments to the
Business Exchange Historic (BEH) Zone came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated a petition has been filed by
the development firm Q Factor to amend the City’s BEH zone to allow the development of the area around and connected to the
historic Stock Exchange Building in the Trackline Economic Development Area. The proposed amendments would reduce side
and rear yard setbacks, increase the maximum height, amend requirements for several architectural elements, and propose an
option for reducing parking requirements. The City’s BEH and Business Exchange Industrial (BEI) zones were created in 2015 as
part of the redevelopment of the historic stockyards in west Ogden. As development has taken place since that time, a number of
amendments have been made to the BEH and BEI zones to accommodate changing uses and needs.

Planning Manager Brierley reviewed the staff memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
applicant’s redevelopment plans for the Stock Exchange Building within the BEH zone, noting that much of the proposed redesign
is harmonious with existing development in the area. The proposed zone text amendments are as follows:

1. Reduce the minimum side and rear yard setback in BEH to 10 feet.
2. Increase the maximum building height in BEH to 45 feet with allowances for step back third floors to go to 50
feet.
3. Allow a reduction to parking requirements in the BEH zone based on approval of a parking management plan.
4. Modify design standards to:
a. Eliminate the requirement for “expressed sills and lintels” in the BEH zone.
b. Allow glass walls on a third floor in BEH.
c. Modify the standard for modular bays and columns to be at most 36 feet apart.
d. Allow cement-based building materials.

The purpose of the BEH zone is to provide for development surrounding the existing Stock Exchange Building that is in
scale with the historic building and architecturally compatible; a strategy identified in the General Plan relating to historic
properties is to provide economic, zoning and administrative incentives for rehabilitation of qualifying historic properties. Staff
believes that the proposed text amendments help to provide incentives for rehabilitation of the Stock Exchange Building. The
Planning Commission reviewed the proposal at its August 3, 2022 meeting and forwarded a recommendation of approval.
Commissioner Schade voted against the recommendation indicating that he was uncomfortable with the proposed height.
Additionally, the Landmarks Commission reviewed and approved an application for a Certificate of Historic Appropriateness for
the proposed development of the Union Stock Exchange Building.

Chair Nadolski introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2022-44, entitled:

“An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Section 15-22-1 to
amend dimensional requirements in the BEH Zone; by amending Subsection 15-23-8.C to revise building
material requirements in the BEH Zone; by amending Subsection 15-23-8.D to revise building architecture
requirements in the BEH Zone; by adopting a new Subsection 15-23-8.J to establish parking reduction
provisions in the BEH Zone; and by providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon
posting after final passage.”

A copy of the proposed ordinance was deposited with the Deputy City Recorder and ordered that the City Recorder have

Proposed Ordinance 2022-46 amending the zoning map to reclassify as
multiple-family residential zone (R-3) property heretofore classified as
single-family residential zone (R-1-6), generally located at 682 - 712 7th
Street

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed rezone of property at
682-712 7" Street came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated a petition has been filed to rezone seven
properties from the current residential R-1- 6 zone to residential R-3 zone. The subject properties are a combination of the
petitioner’s three properties and additional properties to the west. The petition is accompanied by letters of request from the other
property owners. The petitioners do not currently have a proposed development, but the rezone would provide consistent zoning
on the north side of 7" Street in the area.

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and stated the subject area is addressed in the Hillcrest/Bonneville
Community Plan, which was adopted in 2004; Vision Strategy 6.A of that Plan discusses the support for and the preservation of
the single-family nature of the area. Additionally, one goal of the City’s General Plan is to support and preserve the existing
single-family nature of the area through appropriate zoning classifications (about five units per acre). Multi-family should be
limited to existing developed areas within the community. Additionally, a definite edge to a development and buffering between
types of uses should be provided to protect the integrity of each use, e.g., between commercial and residential uses and between
types of residential uses. Zoning should reflect the prevailing character of an overall district or neighborhood to the largest extent
possible, with consideration being given to the use and characteristics of individual properties. Where possible, properties that
face each other across a local street should be the same or similar zone. Collector and arterial roads may be sufficient buffers to
warrant different zones. Zoning boundaries should not cut across individual lots or developments (i.e., placing the lot into two
separate zones). Illogical boundaries should be redrawn to follow along property lines. The Planning Commission evaluated
whether the proposed zone change is consistent with these guidelines taken from the General Plan; they voted to forward a
positive recommendation to the City Council to approve the zone change. In conclusion, Mr. Brierley provided a list of permitted
and conditional land uses in the R-3 zone:

Institutional uses:

Mr. Brierley also provided a rendering illustrating the shift in housing density based upon the proposed zone change; one
building could be built on the primary properties and it could contain 18 apartment units. Or, four buildings could be built on the
same properties, with three units in each building, for a total of 12 units.
Chair Nadolski introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2022-46, entitled:
“An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Zoning Map of Ogden City, as adopted by Section 15-3-3 of
the Ogden Municipal Code, to reclassify as multiple-family residential zone (R-3) property heretofore classified
as single-family residential zone (R-1-6), generally located at 682-712 7" Street, and providing that this
ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final passage.”
A copy of the proposed ordinance was deposited with the Deputy City Recorder and ordered that the City Recorder have
at least one copy available for public inspection in her office during all business hours.

Minutes of Regular Meeting of Council of Ogden City, Utah, November 1, 2022

Zach Spencer stated he appreciates the Council’s consideration of this application; he was notified by Mr. Brierley of
some concerns the Council has expressed regarding the proposed development plan for the subject properties. He stated he does
not plan to build any type of institutional use on the site, but would pursue a project that would help to restore and improve the
area for surrounding property owners. He noted there was a fire in one of the homes on one of the properties and that home has
since been torn down and the property is vacant. The other homes are very aged, and one has been vacant for quite some time;
they are not creating any positive value for the neighborhood. He would like to proceed with a project that will yield homes that
buyers are proud to live in.

Council member Hyer stated that he grew up in the area and the surrounding properties that are zoned R-3 have been
developed in a style that is more in line with the R-2 zoning designation; he asked Mr. Spencer if he would be willing to accept
the R-2 zoning designation. Mr. Spencer stated that he would be willing to consider that zoning designation, but would need to
more closely examine the zoning standards of the R-2 zone, such as setbacks and minimum lot sizes. He stated that he is mostly
concerned with creating the best project possible and is not concerned with pushing the maximum density possible. He has visited
neighboring property owners in the area, and he believes they support his plans to improve the subject properties in a way that will
make a positive contribution to the community.

Chair Nadolski then called for public input regarding the proposed ordinance.

Angel Castillo thanked the Council for their careful consideration of this application and the focus on the uses that could
be placed on the property if the zoning were changed; however, she believes that this particular project is appropriate for the area
and she would even ask the Council to consider taking one additional step: one conditional use included senior homes and she
feels that would be a great use in this area because of the aging population of the community. Many developers experience a
challenge when trying to build in the community relating to gaining approval of conditional uses and she encouraged the Council
to consider changing senior living from a conditional use to a permitted use. If that were done, housing issues can be addressed
more swiftly in the future when there is a demand for senior housing. She stated she is grateful to Mr. Spencer for engaging the
community and being a good steward of the land in this neighborhood.

No additional persons came forward to be heard regarding this matter.

Council member Choberka referenced Council member Hyer’s question about R-2 zoning; she asked how that would be
better for the area. Council member Hyer stated that after listening to Mr. Spencer, he believes the R-2 zoning would be more
appropriate and he feels it would provide him with an incentive to begin working on the design of the project. Ifhe finds he cannot
make his project fit under the R-2 zoning after working on the design, the Council could consider R-3 overlay zoning to address
any issues that may arise with setbacks or other zoning standards. Council member Blair agreed, but asked if there are any
concerns about creating a small pocket of R-2 zoning. Council member Hyer answered no and indicated that the surrounding areas
have been developed in a way that conforms with R-2 zoning, even if the zoning is actually different. He feels the end result will
be harmonious with its surroundings. Mr. Brierley added that he has no concerns about assigning the R-2 zone to the property.

Council member Richey stated that Mr. Brierley informed Mr. Spencer of the Council’s concerns about the application
and Mr. Spencer made an effort to reach out to Council members to discuss his ideas with them; he thanked Mr. Spencer for being
proactive.
