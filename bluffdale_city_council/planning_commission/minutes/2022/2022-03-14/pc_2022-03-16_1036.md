5.     Training on Open and Public Meetings Act - Staff Presenter, Todd Sheeran, City
       Attorney.

City Attorney, Todd Sheeran, provided the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act training, which
by law is to be conducted armually.

Mr. Sheeran gave an overview of how noticing takes p lace and explained that the Platming
Commi ssion must provide at least 24 hours notice before each Planning Commi ssion Meeting.
In the unlikely event there are unforeseen circumstances and a meeting must take place
immediately, there is leeway in Utah Code to allow fo r such a situation. Some items require
more noticing time than others. The General Plan and rezoning require at least a 10-day public
notice.

T he public notice must include an agenda and the date, time, and place of the meeting. T he
notice must be posted on the Utah Public Notice website, the Bluffdale C ity website, and in the
newspaper. It must include the topics as separate agenda items to be considered at the meeti ng.
Li sting only the resolution number is insufficient and must also include a summary of the matter
to be considered.

Mr. Sheeran emphasized the importance of ensuring that discussions about an agenda item take
place during a public meeting. Chance encounters of members of a public body do not constitute
a meeting. As a matter of caution, Bluffdale City will occasionally post public notice of an event
at which a majority of the members of the public body will be in attendance; however, no
business will be conducted.

With regard to communication, Mr. Sheeran stated that it is important to be extremely careful
with email s and text messages about issues that could potentially constitute vio lations of the
Open and Public Meetings Act (the "Act"). Exchanges between two members would likely be
legal; however, a member of the public body sending an email to other members to advocate a
certain position would be a violation of the Act. Texting or sending any type of electronic
message to other members during a public meeting is expressly prohibited by the Act.

Mr. Sheeran shared an example of an Open and Public Meetings Act case that was argued before
the Utah Supreme Court. Non-public meetings caimot occur before a public meeting to decide
what will be said during the public meeting. Public discussions about a contentious issue must
take place in public.

Another example of a violation of the Act involved a school di strict where a citizen invoked the
Government Records Access and Management Act ("ORAMA") to obtain a copy of email s and
texts that were shared among members of the school board to prove that the Act had been
violated.

Mr. Sheeran next addressed conflicts of interest and stated that State Code specifies that
members of a public body need to state a potential conflict of interest. The conflict, however,
does not preclude the individual from participating in the meeting. Commissioner Loumis asked
if an oral disclosure was sufficient. Mr. Sheeran was unsure and would research the issue. He
stated that although State Code allows a person with a conflict of interest to participate in a
di scussion, public perception leads members of a public body to recuse themselves so that they
do not appear to be exerting undue influence on a decision. Mr. Sheeran stated that caution
should be exercised whenever there is a conflict of interest.

In response to a question raised by Commissioner Swanson regarding changes that came out of
the latest Legislati ve Session, Mr. Sheeran stated that there were changes regarding noticing. IT
primarily dealt with the posting of public notices.
