Proposal 
The applicant, John Sale, representing  Vail Resorts, operator of Park City Mountain, in 
conjunction with the Canyons CVMA are requesting Low Impact Permit  approval for

Pine Lodge.  The gondola is proposed to be 6,233 feet in length with a vertical rise of 1,104 feet,

zone and clearing the gondola line corridor.

grading at the base and top terminals, and construction of the  gondola plaza and
supporting infrastructure.

Background 
The approval of the Canyons SPA plan and exhibits in November of 1999 included provision for 
expansion of the mountain master plan including additional ski lifts and ski terrain.  These 
improvements were originally laid out in a document titled the Ski ’98 plan and then 
incorporated into the Canyons final SPA approval.  An important element of the planning for 
the Canyons SPA approval was an analysis of the amount of ski infrastructure necessary to 
match the bed base that was part of the entitlement.  This is referred to in the Global Policies 
and elsewhere in the SPA document as the Comfortable Carrying Capacity or (CCC) for the 
mountain.  Section 2.3.3 of the Canyons SPA discusses the entitlement of this infrastructure and 
specifies the Low Impact Permit (LIP) approval process as the required process for review and 
approval prior to construction.   
Sun
rise Gondola is classified as a transportation lift, meaning it cannot be skied back to the base 
terminal directly. Mountain guests would have to utilize other lifts or ski terrain  to get back to 
the base area.  Additionally, t he proposed re-alignment of the Sunrise Gondola will be less 
susceptible to wind, versus the existing Red Pine Gondola and Orange Bubble express, requiring 
fewer wind holds and keeping the lines moving  and getting guest on the mountain faster . The 
proposed Sunrise Gondola will improve both the current and future demand to access the 
mountain terrain from the Canyons Village core.

Anal
ysis and Findings 
Section 10-3-4(E) of the Snyderville Basin Development Code requires that any use qualifying 
for a Low Impact Permit must meet the following criteria: 
Criteria 1 : The use does not significantly increase vehicular traffic, unless the increases are 
consistent with previously approved plans for which appropriate mitigation has been 
contemplated and which has been implemented in an appropriate manner to accommodate 
the
 proposed amendment.  
Ana
lysis: The proposed Sunrise Gondola will not significantly increase vehicular traffic at 
Canyons Village. Guests will now have an upgraded third option to directly access the 
mountain to the Red Pine area that is near the Forum area and conveniently accessed 
from the surrounding lodging options.  Guests will also be able to arrive via the existing 
shuttle and transit service and the current traffic management plan in the upper village 
will remain in place. 
Cri
teria 2: The use does not significantly increase the demand for parking; unless the increases 
are consistent with previously approved plans for which appropriate mitigation has been 
contemplated and which has been implemented in an appropriate manner to accommodate 
the proposed amendment.  
Ana
lysis: The proposed Sunrise Gondola will not significantly increase the demand for 
parking in Canyons Village. This project has two main goals: efficient guest distribution 
across the mountain and improved uphill capacity during peak morning hours. The 
bottom terminal's placement will effectively mitigate any parking concerns as this 
location will mainly serve lodging and residential guests and day skiers and 
snowboarders who often reach the resort via the Cabriolet lift, consequently minimizing 
potential increases to parking demand escalation. 
Cri
teria 3: The use does not intensify the likelihood of pedestrian and vehicular conflicts. 
Ana
lysis: The project effectively addresses pedestrian and vehicular conflicts by 
implementing measures that reduce the likelihood of such conflicts. By incorporating 
prominent wayfinding, designated and accessible crossing points, and dedicated shuttle 
drop- off, the project creates a safe environment for both pedestrians and vehicles. The 
proposed plaza is designed to be fully ADA accessible, ensuring guests of all abilities are 
able to safely and easily access the lift. Overall, the project's thoughtful lower terminal 
design will improve the area’s existing conditions by prioritizing safety and minimizing 
the potential for conflicts between pedestrians and vehicles.

Project Description: 
T
he approved CUP/MPD application for the High Valley Transit (HVT) Operations & 
Maintenance Facility houses HVT’s administration and operations now and in the future.  The 
project is currently under construction in phases.   
This application is an amendment to the approved CUP/MPD application and proposes a 5,621 
sf expansion of the 11,370 sf Maintenance Building for a total building area of 16,991 sf. The 
expansion, as noted in the original application, will provide additional service capacity with 2 
additional vehicle maintenance bays. The expansion will connect to the south end of the 
Maintenance Building currently under construction. Of note, the battery storage appendage at 
the south end of the original Maintenance building was planned to be sacrificed with the 
eventual expansion. As a result, batteries will no longer be stored at the facility in favor of a 
commercial partner arrangement. Additional openings have been designed and added to the 
south wall of the original Maintenance building to allow improved access and function.  
Additional vehicle parking will also be provided with the expansion. Proposed site elements 
immediately adjacent to the expansion including a dumpster enclosure, electrical transformers, 
and an emergency generator, will be relocated to allow safe access to the overhead doors, 
proper spacing and clearances for the utilities, and snow storage.  
Site clearances are critical to the operation and must allow for maneuvering the largest bus with 
its corresponding turning radius. The Maintenance building and surrounding site is planned for 
one way circulation with drive through bays. This strategy allows the buses and small vehicles to 
safely maneuver into and out of the Maintenance building and then enter the Bus Storage 
building or adjacent parking areas.  
The small vehicle service bay will include 3 small vehicle lifts and an alignment rack. The lifts and 
rack will be matched to mini-van and cut-a-way vehicle weight and size. Both future bays will be 
bus service capable requiring the same size overhead doors and interior clear height as the 
original to allow buses to be raised on portable lifts for service. (A permanent, adjustable axel 
lift may be installed in the first expansion bay in the future.)  
The Maintenance Building expansion will comply with the original CUP/MPD Application and all 
applicable codes and ordinances.

conduct a public hearing and forward a positive recommendation to the County Council.  
 
BACKGROUND 
 
On September 9, 2021, the Summit County Council adopted comprehensive amendments to 
the accessory building regulations in the Development Code. The amendments created 
regulations limiting the size and number of accessory buildings that a property owner could 
have on a lot.  
 
Highlights included: 
 
• Creation of Minor Accessory Buildings 
o Allowed in all zones. 
o Size of building = 75% of principal building footprint up to 2,000 square 
feet (2,667 square foot principal building = 2,000 square foot footprint of 
the Minor Accessory Building). 
o Secondary structure allowed up to 400 square feet footprint (this 
structure would be for a garden shed or smaller storage shed). 
o Combined Minor Accessory Building, Secondary structure cannot exceed 
90% of principal structure footprint. 2,667 square foot principal building = 
2,000 square foot Minor Accessory Building + 400 square foot secondary 
structure).  
o Maximum height = 26' 
132
Code Amendments     
• Creation of Major Accessory Buildings 
o Conditional Use Permit required in all residential zones.  
o Lots smaller than one acre not eligible. 
o Maximum size depends on size of the lot, with max size allowed 12,500 square 
foot footprint.  
o Only one allowed per lot or parcel 
o Maximum height = 26' 
Since the ordinance was adopted, staff has run into a couple of issues/unintended 
consequences applying the ordinance. The purpose of this amendment is to correct those 
issues.  
Issue #1  
The definition of a Major Accessory building requires that the building be at least 2,000 square 
feet or more in size. This means that if a property owner has a 1,500 square foot minor 
accessory building on the lot and would like to build another approximately the same size, he 
can’t do it because the ordinance states only one minor accessory building is permitted. 
Assuming the property owner does not already have a major accessory building, his only option 
would be to increase the proposed footprint of the second structure to at least 2,000 square 
feet and apply for a major accessory building. 
To fix the problem, staff is proposing language that allows a property owner to have either two 
minor accessory buildings on the lot, or one minor accessory building and one major accessory 
building. This would give property owners more options without requiring that they increase 
their building footprint to meet the minimum requirement to be a major accessory building.  
Issue #2 
The size of the buildings is based on the footprint. Currently there is no definition of footprint in 
the Development Code. Generally, the footprint has been calculated by measuring the 
perimeter of the cement foundation of a structure. However, many accessory buildings have 
cantilevers that are supported by posts but not a foundation and are therefore not included in 
the calculation of a footprint. This can have a significant impact on the mass and scale of the 
building. To clarify, staff is proposing the following definition:  
“BUILDING FOOTPRINT. The total area of the plan view projected to grade, of a Building 
or Structure, measured from the outside edge of all exterior walls and supporting 
columns. Cantilevered portions of; roofs, bay windows, and upper-level balconies, up to 
a maximum projection of 36 inches from the wall or supporting beam line are not 
included. Uncovered; exterior stairs, ramps, patios, and decks, less than 30 inches above 
grade are not included.”
