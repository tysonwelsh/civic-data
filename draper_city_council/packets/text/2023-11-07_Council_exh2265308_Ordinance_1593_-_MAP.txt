        AN ORDINANCE OF DRAPER CITY AMENDING A MASTER AREA PLAN DESIGN
        GUIDELINES PER THE LAND USE AND DEVELOPMENT CODE OF THE DRAPER
        CITY MUNICIPAL CODE RELATING TO VISTA 600 WEST MASTER AREA PLAN.

      WHEREAS, Utah State law grants to Draper City the authority to regulate uses of
property by zoning districts; and

        WHEREAS, the terms and provisions of the TSD (Transit Station District) zoning
classification were originally adopted and established in the Draper City Municipal Code
through the adoption of Ordinance 859, adopted on November 3, 2008 and were
subsequently revised through the adoption of Ordinances 985, 1024, 1085, 1132, 1313, 1328,
1380, 1394, 1418, and 1438 between October 13, 2011 and May 19, 2020; and

        WHEREAS, the terms and provisions of the TSD (Transit Station District) zoning
classification require a Master Area Plan be applied for, reviewed, and approved prior to
application for development of any property within the TSD zoning classification; and

      WHEREAS, the Vista 600 West Master Area Plan was adopted on March 6, 2018 and
the applicant is now proposing changes to the adopted Vista 600 West Master Area Plan
Design Guidelines; and

      WHEREAS, the City Council of Draper City has determined that the amended Vista 600
West Master Area Plan Design Guidelines have provided for and addressed all the narrative
and graphic submittal requirements of Sections 9-14-060(C)(1) and (2); and

       WHEREAS, notice has been issued according to the requirements of the Utah Code
Annotated and Draper City Municipal Code for public hearings before the Planning
Commission and City Council to receive public input regarding the revision of the Master
Area Plan; and

       WHEREAS, the Planning Commission and City Council have each held a public hearing
to receive public input regarding the revision of the Master Area Plan Design Guidelines.

      NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE CITY COUNCIL OF DRAPER CITY, STATE
OF UTAH, AS FOLLOWS:

       Section 1.     Findings. The City Council of Draper City has made the following
findings that the proposed amendment to the Vista 600 West Master Area Plan: 1. The
proposal complies with all of the required elements under DCMC Section 9-14-060. 2. The
master circulation plan adequately serves the MAP area and areas surrounding the project.
3. The MAP provides for a community character substantially consistent with the purposes

The Vista 600 West project is located on the northeast corner of the intersection of Bangerter Highway
and 600 West. The parcel consists of approximately 15 acres of land (See Exhibit A) to be developed into
a mixed-use project with retail buildings, a hotel and multi-family residential units.

The north half of the site (6.49 acres) is currently under construction for 174 multi-family units. The
southern half of the site (8.75 acres) will include a hotel, 70 multi-family units, three retail buildings and
a convenience store/gas station. The project will allow for up to 250 total multi-family units.

The proposed project fits within the area plan specifications with uses such as multi-family residential,
retail and hospitality.

The main vehicular entrances to the project will be on the west side of the parcel onto 600 west. The
buildings will be oriented to the west towards 600 west, south towards Bangerter Highway, and north to
the curve of 600 West. The main entrance into the project off of 600 West is currently under
construction (in 2023) as part of the north side multi-family development.

The transportation systems within the Master Area Plan (MAP) area are already in place, including
collector and arterial streets (Bangerter Highway, 600 West, Vista Station Blvd, Frontrunner Blvd and 200
West). Also, rail transit such as FrontRunner with the Draper Station stop less than one mile in walking
distance.

Storm drainage and other utilities (wet and dry) have been reviewed for capacity and are available for
the site development.

There are no major public improvements or off-site infrastructure improvements or facilities required
with the development of the project.

The estimated sequence and timing of development will happen in three phases as outline below: Phase
1 – 170 multi-family units on the north side of the project (currently under construction in 2023), Phase
2 – hotel, then multi-family, then retail buildings, Phase 3 – will happened once the tenant is secured.

The site plan and the proposed design guidelines have been reviewed and approved by the Architectural
Control Committee (see Exhibit B).

Architectural and site design standards for the MAP are outlined in the Development Guidelines,
including setbacks, signage, parking standards, etc. The project will be designed in such a way as to
promote compatibility between the MAP area and the adjoining properties.

Development Guidelines
Each building/site plan shall comply with the following development guidelines.

Retail
1.       Minimum building height shall be one-story
2.       The minimum setback from the right-of-way or exterior property lines shall be five feet (5’).
3.       More than one material type shall be required on all building sides.
4.       Building primary materials shall consist of granite, metal, composite metal panel, stone, brick
         stucco/EFIS, GFRC, tile cement board.
5.       Buildings shall have notable entry features which could include: arch or canopy, colors or
         materials, entry doors that extend beyond or are recessed beyond the main footprint of the
         building.
6.       Dumpster shall be enclosed within a decorative masonry, split face CMU or honed CMU
         enclosure Color scheme shall match the associated building(s).
7.       Temporary use permits shall be allowed.

Hotel
1.       The minimum building height shall be three (3) stories.
2.       The minimum setback from right-of-way or exterior property line shall be feet (10’).
3.       More than one (1) material type shall be required on all building sides.
4.       Building primary materials shall consist of granite, metal, composite metal panel, stone, brick
         stucco/EFIS, GFRC, tile or cement board.
5.       Building shall have fifty percent (50%) primary materials on each building side.
6.       Building shall have notable entry features which could include arch or canopy, colors or
         materials, entry doors that extend beyond or are recessed beyond main footprint of the
         building.
7.       Building elevations that face a public street or private street or open space area shall have either
         a porch, balcony, private patio or window.
8.       All mechanical equipment shall be screened from view, either by enclosure, parapet wall or line
         of sight.
9.       Dumpster shall be enclosed within a decorative masonry, split face CMU or honed CMU
         enclosure. Color scheme shall match the associated building(s).
10.      Temporary use permits shall be allowed.

Residential
1.     The minimum setback from right-of-way or exterior property line shall be twenty feet (20’).
       Parking and garbage dumpsters shall be allowed in the setback areas.
2.     Each building shall utilize at least three (3) material types.
3.     Building material placement and colors shall be used to distinguish residential building
       elevations from other adjacent residential buildings in the immediate area.
4.     Building elevations that face a public street or private street or open space area shall have either
       a porch, balcony, private patio or window.
5.     All buildings shall share the same architectural theme including, but not limited to, color,
       materials, rooflines and entries.
6.     Multi-family buildings shall have roofs clad in asphalt shingles, wood shingles, standing seam
       metal or a combination of these materials. Flat roofs are allowed.
7.     Any pitched roof covering the main body of the structure shall be hip style, monopitch or shed
       style. Flat roofs shall have symmetrical gables.
8.     Roof forms shall be designed to emphasize the residential units where possible and shall denote
       building elements and functions, including primary pedestrian entrances.

9.      A variety of floor plans/unit sizes is required within the residential project area.
10.     A community clubhouse with an indoor and an outdoor amenity shall be provided for the entire
        residential project, other amenities to be determined by the developer based on the product
        type. These amenities will be shared by all the residential units.
11.     Each separate building will be connected by internal walkways with shared use of outdoor
        common areas.
12.     Pedestrian pathways shall be provided between access points, entryways, public gathering
        nodes and parking areas.
13.     Temporary use permits shall be allowed.

Landscape Design
1.     The landscape design shall define and enhance primary public entrances into the project.
2.     Landscape design shall be used to provide intimate outdoor spaces and pedestrian pathways.
3.     Landscape design shall promote water conservation by using efficient drip irrigation and drought
       tolerant plans where practical.
4.     A ten foot (10’) landscape buffer shall be required along the east property line.
5.     The tree palette shall be a combination of deciduous and ornamental trees.
6.     The tree palette shall include evergreen trees or shrubs.
7.     The initial deciduous tree size shall have a minimum two inch (2”) caliper.
8.     All evergreen trees shall be a minimum of six feet (6’) in height.
9.     Parking lots are to be screened with landscaping when possible.
10.    A minimum of five feet (5’) of landscaping is required around the perimeter of the entire project.
11.    Public Use Space (public or private) may be in the form of landscape areas, landscape areas with
       walkways, hardscape areas, pool deck/pool/spa areas, hardscape areas with a change in color or
       material, area with lighting such as bollard or pole lighting and the 600 West pedestrian zone
       (landscaping, four feet (4’) soft path and eight foot (8’) concrete sidewalk). See Exhibit C.
12.    All multi-family projects shall submit a landscape plan in accordance with city ordinances unless
       otherwise specified within this document.
13.    Detention ponds shall be adequately landscaped and maintained.

Parking
1.      Retail buildings (including convenience store/gas stations) shall be parked at a minimum of 1.87
        per one thousand square feet (1,000) of net usable building area.
2.      Parking stalls shall be eighteen feet (18’) in length and nine feet (9’) in width.
3.      Landscape islands shall be placed at either end of parking rows longer than sixteen (16) stalls.
4.      Hotel buildings shall be parking at a minimum of .75 stalls per room
5.      Residential buildings shall be parked at a minimum of 1.68 stalls per dwelling unit. Driveways
        and garages will count towards this requirement. All residential driveways shall be a minimum of
        twenty feet (20’) deep.

Lighting
1.       Parking lot lighting in retail areas shall provide adequate illumination at a minimum of one half
         (0.5) foot-candles and maximum of four (4) foot-candles.
2.       Parking lot lighting in retail areas shall utilize cutoff type fixtures
3.       All residential area s shall submit a lighting plan that illustrates lighting accommodations for
         automobile and pedestrian networks.
4.       Interior site lighting shall be pedestrian in scale.

5.      Low level bollard lighting is encouraged for the illumination of pedestrian pathways.

Signage
1.      One (1) monument sign is allowed at each of the three (3) vehicular access points into the site.
        Each monument sign shall have a uniform, architecturally compatible design, with faces not
        larger than one hundred (100) square feet. Monument signs shall not exceed seven feet (7’) in
        height and fourteen feet (14’) in width. An Individual monument sign shall be allowed for the
        hotel and the convenience store/gas station.
2.      Permanent Wall signs on residential buildings shall be allowed at a ratio of two (2) square feet of
        signage for every one (1) linear foot of building elevation length. Permanent wall signs for retail
        buildings shall be allowed for each tenant per the standards of this section. Permanent wall
        signs for the hotel shall be allowed per the standards of this section.
3.      Suspended or blade type projecting signs are allowed provided they are not more than fifty (50)
        square feet of sign area. Each building will be allowed one suspended or blade type sign.
        Suspended or blade type signs may be provided along pedestrian walkways and shall maintain a
        minimum clearance of seven feet (7') above the pavement.
4.      Two total tower signs shall be allowed for the entire project, one for the hotel and one for the
        convenience store/gas station. The tower sign may be multi-tenant and shall be no taller than
        forty feet (40’) with a maximum sign area of two hundred (200) square feet.
5.      The location of all signs must be approved by the City Engineer to ensure clearance of sight
        distance requirements.
6.      Wayfinding signs shall be allowed in the project area. The overall height shall not exceed eight
        feet (8’) and one hundred (100) square feet in sign area.
7.      Flags and banners shall be allowed on all private street/alleys and on private site lighting poles.
8.      Temporary banner signs shall be allowed for each building.
9.      Flags and A-frame signs shall be allowed.

Electronic Signage
1.      Full color messages or images only and the use of single colored text and images is prohibited.
2.      Each message/advertisement displayed on an Electronic Message Center (EMC) shall remain
        static for a minimum of eight (8) seconds. Animation, flashing, starburst or other similar frame
        effects are prohibited. Pixel pitch shall be as follows:
        a.       EMC’s within the MAP, but not directed toward Bangerter Highway may shall have a true
                 pixel pitch between one millimeter (1 mm) and ten millimeters (10 mm) due to closer
                 proximity of travelers to signs.
        b.       EMC’s directed toward Bangerter Highway shall have a true pixel pitch between one
                 millimeter (1mm) and sixteen millimeters (16 mm) due to higher speeds and distance
                 between travelers and signs.
        c.       No EMC shall utilize a white or solid colored background for greater than or equal to fifty
                 percent (50%) of the sign area.
        d.       All EMC’s are required to comply with the following LED sign illumination requirements:
                 (1)      All permitted EMC’s shall be equipped with a sensor or other device that
                          automatically determines the ambient illumination and is programmed to
                          automatically dim according to ambient light conditions, or that can be adjusted
                          to comply with the 0.3 foot-candle measurements. In addition, EMCs must have
                          a default mechanism to turn off the sign within twenty-four (24) hours of a
                          reported malfunction.

                (3)      The illuminance of an EMC shall be measured with an illuminance meter set to
