         The purpose of the joint work session was to review the agendas for the City Council and Special Redevelopment
Agency (RDA) meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.; participate in Council/Mayor discussion; discuss Board/Council
business specific to the Marshall White Center (MWC); and hear Board/Council comments.

Agenda Review
         Chair Blair and members of Council staff briefly reviewed the items listed on the agendas for the City Council and
special Redevelopment Agency (RDA) meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Council/Mayor Discussion
            Mayor Caldwell first spoke about his pride in the City staff; they have worked through very difficult conditions over the
last twelve to 18 months, namely related to the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic. He reported on metrics in various City Departments
and thanked the Council for their support of City staff. There was a brief discussion between Council member Hyer and the Mayor
about the partnership between Ogden City and Weber County relative to solid waste and recycling programming.
           Council member Lopez then noted the Mayor was asked to provide information regarding the City's use of American
Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds, the 2022 Utah Legislative Session update, the Marshall White Center (MWC) and homelessness
issues in the community. Council member Stephens added he would also like the Mayor to touch on a media piece that ran last
night regarding an increase in graffiti in Ogden City. Mayor Caldwell stated there has been a huge increase in graffiti and the City
has implemented a bonus program that provides a $500 incentive for anyone who reports graffiti, and their report leads to
successful prosecution of the issue.
           Chair Blair stated that he has heard from residents about an oddity on their December 2021 utility bill; there are two
December bill due dates: December 3 and 31, 2021 and he asked why that has occurred this year. Mayor Caldwell stated he is
unsure and he will reach out to the Utility Division for an answer.
           Mayor Caldwell then responded to Council member Lopez's request for information regarding the City's use of ARPA
funds. In June 2021 the City received $12.8 million and will receive an additional $12.8 million in June of2022. The money must
be encumbered by December of2024 and spent by December of2026 and City Administration is working to prioritize the use of
those funds. The City has issued a request for qualifications (RFQ) for a professional firm that can advise the City on the
allocation and use of the funds as there are many technical guidelines for the use of the fund. He noted that government entities
initially received CARES funding in the early days of the COVID-19 pandemic, but there were very few rules and guidelines for
those funds. However, use of ARPA funding is much more regulated, and he would hate for the City to be audited and forced to
repay any misused funds. He then engaged in discussion with the Council regm·ding their ideas for projects to be funded by the
ARPA funds, with a heavy focus on vital infrastructure projects; he noted that he is engaged in conversations with State leaders
about options for leveraging the funds to receive increased State funding for various projects and programs. Council member
Nadolski wondered if a community center project, such as rebuilding the MWC is an eligible use of ARPA funds. He added he
