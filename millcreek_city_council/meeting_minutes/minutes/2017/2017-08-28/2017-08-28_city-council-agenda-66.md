Agenda Item 2. Discussion with residents Glen and Marjorie Willardson, proposing an ordinance that
would allow accessory apartments in Millcreek

Mayor Silvestrini introduced Marjorie Willardson, who requested that the council consider an ordinance
amendment that would allow for an accessory apartment to allow seniors to remain living near their
families. Ms. Willardson explained that her family has deep roots in the Millcreek area and outlined her
family’s involvement in the community.

Ms. Willardson then explained that the reason for the request was due to medical issues that her family
had encountered requiring assistance if they wanted to stay in their home. Family members would be
interested in moving into an accessory apartment to provide that assistance. Ms. Willardson indicated
that she knows of several families that have been required to move because they couldn’t have the
assistance they needed from family members. Ms. Willardson distributed a copy of the Sandy City

1

ordinance and outlined several aspects that she suggested should be considered by the council to assist
the senior members of the community.

Mayor Silvestrini stated that he has no objections but is concerned that there is a risk that the change
would allow conversion of too many residences in Millcreek into duplexes. Ms. Willardson pointed out
the section of the Sandy ordinance that limited this type of accessory building to relatives by blood,
marriage, adoption or up to 2 unrelated individuals functioning as a single household unit.

Alex Murphy, Millcreek planner, explained that previously the ordinance did not allow for walls be
erected to separate the units and that would have to be the major change.

John Jansen, Millcreek planning consultant, stated that the legal accessory dwelling units biggest issue is
having two separate living units and that it is a good idea to have a contract stating that the property
owner must live there. Mr. Jansen added that West Valley City adopted a similar ordinance but was
unsuccessful in that a problem developed with too many people living inone home. Mr. Jansen added
that there will be a session at the League meeting on this question as well as on tiny homes, an
additional issue that has arisen.

Mayor Silvestrini stated that there is an existing accessory building ordinance and.asked Mr. Brems to
look at the Sandy ordinance and bring some ordinance revision suggestions to the council for
consideration.

Mr. Murphy added that he has seen a medical necessity allowance for accessory units utilized in other
municipalities and he would be willing to provide the council with that information.

Agenda Item 5. Update on SLVLESA issues

Mayor Silvestrini reported that he had discussed the SLVLESA issues with John Brems and John Geilmann
along with representatives from Lewis Young but that he had not yet received the final report. Lewis
Young had been asked to complete additional work based on the question of a fund balance distribution
if Millcreek chose to withdraw from SLVLESA. Mayor Silvestrini additionally noted that Lewis Young has
been working with the SLVLESA financial officers and will amend the report to reflect the methodology
for a fund balance distribution determination if Millcreek determines to leave the district. Mayor
Silvestrini explained that the property taxes collected in Millcreek were not tracked as Millcreek prior to
2016 and that estimates would have to be made adding that a change would be the growth of real
property values. Mayor Silvestrini stated that an argument had been made at the SLVLESA Board
Meeting by Councilmember Bradley, a UPD board member, that the growth is happening in the west
side of the valley and that should be considered in the process.

Mayor Silvestrini added that he had requested that Lewis Young make inquiries related to potential
revenue anticipation funding and that he was interested in exploring the possibility of a lawful funding
strategy to pay for policing services without being required to establish a taxing entity as well as
determining if there are funders who are willing to work with Millcreek,

Mayor Silvestrini noted that the Millcreek share of the fund balance based on what Lewis Young had
developed would be in the range of $2M adding that agreements are already in place for the amounts
that should be paid to Riverton and Herriman because they have better historical information.

Mayor Silvestrini explained that he would like to have all the information available to the residents as far
in advance of the public hearing as possible and outlined the planned time line.

Mayor Silvestrini also explained that he has had positive feedback from residents based on the
information that has been distributed. Councilmember Marchant indicated that he has had some
concerns expressed that there could be an impact to other areas of the county. Mayor Silvestrini
responded that the other communities will have the benefit of the centrally assessed taxes of which
none would go to Millcreek.

Mayor Silvestrini reported that the SLVLESA board has voted for an additional 5% increase in 2018 to
assist in keeping the fund balance healthy. Mayor Silvestrini explained that additionally, based on the
Lewis Young report, Millcreek currently receives 40% of the SLVESLA revenues but contributes 54% of
the taxes collected adding that Jason Burningham from Lewis Young had explained that if Millcreek
leaves SLVLESA, the city could have $500,000 to $700,000 more to spend in Millcreek on policing
services which could potentially mean the addition of five to seven officers.

Councilmember Marchant added that if the other areas were to have a substantial impact, Millcreek
alone should not be required to mitigate the impact, that it should be more regional.

Mayor Silvestrini suggested that to levy a property tax, Millcreek would have to withdraw from the
district by December 31% and that once the other two cities leave SLVLESA, those votes would be lost
and Millcreek would have the same vote as the metro townships on the board.

Agenda Item 6. Discussion with John Jansen about proposed revisions to M Zone

Mayor Silvestrini introduced John Jansen and stated that the planning commission has made a
recommendation to the city council to combine the M1 and M2 zones. The mayor outlined the amount
of public involvement that has been involved and stated that there are concerns from the business
community particularly some in the west Millcreek area. Mayor Silvestrini added that in order to make
certain to allow for adequate notice for input from the concerned business owners that he would like to
add the item to the agenda for September 25". Mayor Silvestrini outlined the process that has been
followed and is certain that adequate notice has been made.

Mayor Silvestrini then stated that in response to comments that had been made at the Planning
Commission, the Commission has revised its recommendations and that three work sessions and one
public hearing have already been held.

Mr. Jansen then explained that the notice letters had been mailed today. The first notices had gone out
with addresses from the Assessor’s office that had been a defective list but the list for the second
mailing had been corrected. Mr. Jansen expressed his feelings that Millcreek has gone the extra mile to
be certain that affected property owners were notified through three hearings, three work sessions,
phone calls and emails.

Mayor Silvestrini added that some notices have gone to property owners who are out of state or that
the notices had been received by tenants but that there is a good network of property owners in the
area and there has been a good turnout at the last two hearings and that good input is being received.
The mayor explained that the process has been evolving throughout the last three months.

Mr. Jansen stated that he feels there are three major points of confusion: there are property owners
who don’t like changing the rules or the: zoning; the non-conforming language in current ordinance leans
toward heavy manufacturing and the new ordinance leans toward light manufacturing; and some
property owners are concerned about the changes related to open storage.

Mr. Jansen explained that the proposed changes would mainly apply to the area from State Street
running west and added that one oddity had showed up at a small remainder piece on the north side of
the Brickyard, a vacant property with a boarded-up building that is zoned manufacturing going back to
the time of the actual Brickyard and that property would also be affected by the proposed change.

Mr. Jansen added the definition of light manufacturing which includes uses inside an enclosed building
and the C3 zone provisions have been added allowing for more uses in those zones. Mr. Jansen added
that county ordinance currently states that “any use larger than an acre” becomes a conditional use and
that the section required changes.

Mr. Jansen then explained the conditional and permitted use lists, including height, parking lots and
signs. Mr. Jansen added that the light manufacturing would reflect the nuisance theory and that
changes to the storage issue include a requirement that would require that the section of building close
to the fence would be lower and higher in the interior of the parcel.

Mayor Silvestrini explained that nothing is being taken away from existing uses and that they may not
even become a conditional use. Mr. Brems suggested adding some language to ensure that new
owners could continue the existing use.

Ms. Jansen then discussed design standards stating that the window size has gone from 50% to 45%
along with additional small changes. Mr. Jansen added that the original proposal had stated that once a
non-conforming use is gone it cannot be reinstituted and that an addition has been made stating that a
non-conforming use that could be improved through changes such as streetscaping would allow a
property owner to retain a non-conforming use.

Mayor Silvestrini commended the Planning Commission for making the process as open and honest as
possible and that the commission has made a number of changes to address the comments that have
been received. Mayor Silvestrini added that the small area plan shows transition to mixed use in the
area and to at least make the manufacturing use areas have a better look, that many outdoor storage
uses in this area are basically junkyards and that these changes would help the area transition but still
accommodate the existing businesses.

Councilmember Marchant additionally thanked the Planning Commission.for their. work and asked about
any potential legal risks. John Brems, city attorney, stated that since this is azoning change, the council
has a lot of discretion but there is potential for legal challenges. Mayor Silvestrini stated that the courts
on several occasions have agreed that cities should have the ability to shape their communities and that
he is confident that Millcreek has utilized an appropriate process adding that the proposal does not
include any takings, there are no limits on existing uses and that cities have the option of guiding new
uses. Mayor Silvestrini stated that the council is planning the city but that they are also reacting to
citizens comments.

Mr. Jansen stated that Alex Murphy, Millcreek planner, suggested a “zoning verification letter” that
outlines the property owner’s status and that such a letter could be provided.

Mr. Murphy asked about the difference between the use being non-conforming or that the conditions
being non-conforming. Mr. Jansen stated that the proposal refers to the “use”, not the conditions.

Tom Stephens, Millcreek Planning Commission Chair, further added that the Planning Commission is a
group of very experienced individuals who represent a wide range of expertise and skill sets and that a
majority of the members have been in attendance at all meetings. Mayor Silvestrini again commented
that he has been pleased with the accommodations that the Planning Commission has added to mitigate
some property owner’s concerns.
