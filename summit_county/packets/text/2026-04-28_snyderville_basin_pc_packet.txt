Background 
Based on a 2020 Settlement Agreement, this parcel is a legal lot of record and is therefore 
eligible for development.  The property is zoned Rural Residential.  In addition to the proposed 
Accessory Building, the applicant plans to construct a single-family dwelling and a detached 
Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU).  Only the Accessory building is dependent on this Conditional 
Use Permit because the ADU is an allowed use. The Planning Department is simultaneously 
processing the Low Impact Permit required for a single-family dwelling on a parcel outside of a 
subdivision. The single family dwelling is 13,295 square feet of finished living space. 
 
Section 10-2-10 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code contains the Chart of Allowed and 
Permitted uses.  In the Rural Residential zone, an Accessory Building requires a Conditional Use 
Permit subject to review and approval by the Snyderville Basin Planning Commission. The 
applicant applied for a Conditional Use Permit on December 4, 2025.  The code defines an 
Accessory Building as: 
 
ACCESSORY BUILDING (OR ACCESSORY STRUCTURE): A Building or a Structure which is 
incidental to the Principal Use, is customarily associated with that Principal Use, is under 
the same ownership or control and is located on the same Lot as the Principal Building 
or Use. An Accessory Building or Structure supports or compliments the Principal Use 
but does not change its character or function. 
 
4407 N Old Ranch Road is 3.25 acres.  According to section 10-8-17 (amended Ordinance 1009) 
of the Code, the total combined footprint of all Accessory Buildings may not exceed 10% of the 
Lot area, up to 14,000 square feet. By this calculation, they would be capped at the 14,000 
square foot maximum based on the size of the lot. They are utilizing 5,468 square feet of that 
amount.   
 
The application was routed to all service providers for review and comment. The application 
meets the minimum requirements for approval, as conditioned.

Recommendation 
Staff recommends that the Snyderville Basin Planning Commission (SBPC) review the proposal, 
conduct a  public hearing, and approve the  application, pursuant to the following findings of 
fact, conclusions of law, and conditions of approval. 
 
Findings of Fact: 
1. On December 4, 2025, the Summit County Community Development Department 
received a complete application for a Conditional Use Permit (CUP) for an Accessory 
Building at 4407 N Old Ranch Rd., Park City (the property).  
2. The property is in the Rural Residential Zone. 
3. An Accessory Building requires a Conditional Use Permit in the Rural Residential Zone.   
4. The property is identified as Parcel PP-112-F and is 3.25 acres.  
5. The property is governed by the 2020 Settlement Agreement (Exhibit A) and is 
considered a Lot of Record. This document was signed on December 9, 2020. 
6. According to Summit County records, the property is owned by Sweetwater Ranch 
Propco LLC. A letter from the Department of Commerce was submitted showing Shauna 
Lyn Gamett as the owner over the LLC. 
7. Shauna Gamett signed the Conditional Use Permit application.   
8. There are currently no structures on the property. 
9. The property is being developed concurrently with a single-family dwelling (SFD) and a 
detached Accessory Dwelling Unit.  
11
11 
 
10. A lot may be developed with Accessory Buildings up to 10% of the Lot area, up to 14,000 
square feet. The Lot is 3.25 acres which means they are permitted up to 14,000 square 
feet. 
11. The combined building footprint for all accessory structures on the lot is 5,468 sq ft. 
12. The intent of this proposal is to permit a new Accessory Building on the property.  
13. An Accessory Building  is defined as a building or structure which is incidental to the 
Principal Use, is customarily associated with that Principal Use, is under the same 
ownership or control and is located on the same Lot as the Principal Building or Use. An 
Accessory Building or Structure supports or compliments the Principal Use but does not 
change its character or function. 
14. The maximum height for an Accessory Building is thirty-two (32) feet. The proposed 
height of the Major Accessory is 31’. The cupola will not be working and will be 
decorative only and therefore counts as part of the building height without exception. 
15. The setbacks of the proposed Accessory Building are as follows: front = 104’6”, south 
side = 13’, north side = 237’, rear = 246’6”. 
16. The property has a Perennial Stream (noted as Red Pine Creek) that runs north to south. 
The closest structure is 100’ from the setback of the Ordinary High-Water Mark. 
17. The property has a 10’ Public Utility and Trail Easement within the property lines and is 
free from any structures or obstructions. 
18. The Applicants have submitted a Floodplain Permit which has been reviewed and 
approved by the Floodplain Manager.  
19. The Accessory Building is not intended for public use.  
20. Neither the design/siding/roof materials of the proposed Major Accessory Building are 
prohibited design styles. The longest façade is not greater than 60’ and is therefore not 
required to have a façade shift.  
21. All lighting for the Accessory Building are full cutoff fixtures that are downward directed. 
There are seven wall sconces on the building and the total outdoor light output is 
approximately 2,310 lumens combined (330 lm each). They are all mounted under 12’ 
from finished grade. The lighting plan states that they will comply with 10-4-21 which 
entails that the lighting for the entire property will be under 20,000 lumens. The lighting 
for the entire property was reviewed under this CUP as well as the LIP for the Single-
Family Residence and complies with this amount.  
22. The proposal will not combine accessory structures.  
23. The proposed building is 62’ from the Single Family Dwelling and 39’ from the proposed 
guest house. 
24. There are no signs proposed. 
25. The principal use of the lot is residential.  
26. Staff and service providers reviewed the proposal. 
27. The application was routed to relevant county departments and service providers.  
28. Property owners within 1,000 feet of PP-112-F received a postcard notifying them about 
the project and the public hearing with the Planning Commission.    
29. Public noticing was published in the Park Record.
