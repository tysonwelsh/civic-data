ZONE TEXT CHANGE APPLICATION
ZT-16-2019 Petition by APC TOWERS requesting a zone text change to amend the provisions applicable to
           monopole towers. (Staff - Jody Knapp at 801-963-3497)

              Discussion:

              Jody stated that the current standard states you can have a tower that is 60 feet tall, flush mounted
              (slim canaster), and can’t have full array antenna or it needs to be stealth. Stealth means it needs to be
              concealed as something else. Applicant is looking for a tower in a wide-open area which doesn’t
              allow for the stealth option. Therefore, the applicant is looking to change the ordinance to a 100-foot,
              full array antenna. Jody stated that this antenna would only be allowed in a manufacturing zone and
              the general plan is heavy industrial. Changes are highlighted in the pp. Commissioner Woodruff
              asked if they are thinking of just keeping a standard pole or some sort of lattice. Jody explained that a
              lattice is prohibited, so just the single monopole tower. Brent then asked about the tower needing to
              be a certain distance away from the residential zone. Jody explained that the distance from the
              residential zone and other cell towers would still apply. Jody explained the exception would just be
              for the height and width of the cell tower.


ZT-17-2019    Petition by WEST VALLEY CITY requesting a zone text change to amend the criteria used to
              determine whether a property is eligible to be rezoned to the RM zone, parking lot lighting standards,
              and the multi-unit residential design standards. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545)


              Discussion:

              Steve Pastorik started giving some background information, stating in September of last year the City
              passed a moratorium or temporary land use regulation. The moratorium is for any zone change to the
              RM zone. Also, in the moratorium there was a temporary ban put in place for any changes to the
              code as it relates to the current multi-family housing standards. During that time there were a couple
              of conversations with City Council as to where current multifamily housing is and what areas can be
              developed as a multifamily. Steve then presented the map of the City showing housing developments
              that are existing, under construction, or in areas that have been approved for development but are not
              currently under construction. The next map showed the vacant areas in the City. This gives an idea as
              to what could be developed. The next map showed areas in the General Plan where multi-family
              housing is anticipated as well as areas where developers have shown interest in multi-family housing.

              Steve then explained 2 questions discussed with the Council. First, where should multifamily
              housing be developed? Second, what should the standards be? The City Council then recommended
              some ordnance changes. Steve gave the Planning Commission a copy of these proposed changes and
            explained each one individually. The first part of the zoning ordinance amendments covers what is
            needed to apply for the RM zone. Steve Pastorik, then summed up the first page of amendments
            stating there would be a reduction in the minimum size of the property and property within a half mile
            of any of the train stations would be eligible to apply.
            Steve Pastorick, then addressed the amenities for stacked and attached units. Some amenities are
            being decreased since they are already included as a requirement.

            Commissioner Fuller then asked if we have anything that shows the predictions for the future
            development. Steve Pastorik presented the map again that has the vacant areas in West Valley City
            that could be developed. Commissioner Fuller then stated he was wondering if there was an overlay
            of what has been developed and what we have left to develop. Steve Pastorik stated that he can have
            that overlay map for presentation.

            Commissioner Fuller then asked why we had 2 acres as a minimum before and now it is one acre?
            Steve Pastorik explained that the City Council was back and forth on the minimum requirement and if
            it is felt that we need to go back to the 2 acres we can readdress this issue. Commissioner Lovato
            asked if someone owns a building but would like to sell the back two acres would the one-acre
            minimum effect the sale? Steve Pastorik stated that they would still need to apply for that zone
            change and the council can decide if it makes sense or not. This amendment is more of a basis for
            who can apply

            Steve Pastorik stated that the Council is raising the bar on the quality of products being built with the
            new standards. There was then discussion about not building many 3-4-bedroom apartments. Steve
            Pastorik explained that part of the reason is the market demand. However, there is a better return for
            developers with the one-bedroom apartments. Steve Pastorik also addressed that some apartments are
            now tacking on a fee for certain extra services provided, an example could be walking your dog.
            Someone will walk your dog for an additional fee. You will then see a rise in the rent prices.

 Z-6-2019      Petition by MARK HAAGA requesting a zone change from A (Agriculture, minimum lot size ½
               acre) to R-1-10 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 10,000 square feet). The
               property is located at 3751 S 6000 W on 1.4 acres. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545)

               Discussion:

               Steve Pastorik explained that this property is currently zoned A. The request is R-1-10, which is
               the same zoning as the parcel to the north. The property has some stub streets to the north in
               anticipation of development to the south of those stub streets. They would create a lot for the
               home that is already on 6400, and then subdivide the property into an additional four lots. The
               code does allow for properties under 1 acre to not be RE. Given that everything to the north has
               already been set up for the R-1-10, it would be challenging to make the property a RE zone due to
               the spacing of the road and the lots. The concept plan could take different forms. On the west
               side the road would be extended through, and the anticipation is that the property to the south
               would follow suit and put in a cul-de-sac. We would need to work with engineering on how to do
               the road improvements. The ordinance requires that when you have stub streets, you either need
               to extend the road through or put in a cul-de-sac. Commissioner Lovato asked if there would be
               any downstream effect for someone living on the end of the cul-de-sac. Steve Pastorik added that
               would need to look at drainage, utilities etc. for these homes at the end of a cul-de-sac. Steve
               Lehman stated that they have already talked about this, with engineering and they are not sure if
               the roads on the west will be through roads or if they are putting in cul-de-sac there.
