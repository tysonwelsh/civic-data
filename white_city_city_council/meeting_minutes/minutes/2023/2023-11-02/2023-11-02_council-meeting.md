Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area Potential Tax Increase

                Rachel Anderson, Legal Counsel, Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service
Area (SLVLESA), stated SLVLESA is a special district that can impose a tax on residents of the
district. Taxes are used to pay for law enforcement services. Legislation was passed this year
that caused the Salt Lake County Sheriff to separate from the Unified Police Department (UPD).
The Sheriff will be reestablishing the Sheriff’s Office and UPD will be reorganizing. This leaves
SLVLESA in the position to decide where funding is going to go and how services will be provided
to residents. On August 10, 2023, the SLVLESA Board of Trustees voted to pursue a 7 percent
tax increase. The 7 percent tax increases the tax rate to the statutory maximum property tax rate.
The split between UPD and the Sheriff’s Office is causing a lot of uncertainty. The economy of
scale will be lost and there will be two administrations. It is also dealing with inflation and there is
a risk of going below the 5 percent fund balance reserve.

                Rick Moon, Treasurer, SLVLESA, delivered a PowerPoint presentation reviewing
the history of SLVLESA and the need for a tax increase. SLVLESA is looking to do a 7 percent
tax increase. This year SLVLESA is projected to end at an 18 percent fund balance with the
following year projected at 5 percent.

              Mayor Flint gave the public an opportunity to speak about the SLVELSA tax
increase. There were no public comments.

Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area Tax Increase

                 The Council reviewed the following Resolution approving a 2024 tax rate in excess
of the certified tax rate by the Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area:

RESOLUTION NO. 2023-11-01                                                Date: November 2, 2023

       A RESOLUTION OF THE WHITE CITY METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL
       APPROVING A 2024 TAX RATE IN EXCESS OF THE CERTIFIED TAX RATE BY
       THE SALT LAKE VALLEY LAW ENFORCEMENT SERVICE AREA


                                                2
DATE           THURSDAY                          NOVEMBER                                  2, 2023

WHEREAS, the Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (“SLVLESA”) was created by
Resolution of the Salt Lake County Council dated August 18, 2009; and

WHEREAS, the area of the White City Metro Township is included in the boundaries of SLVLESA
for the funding and provision of law enforcement services to the areas within the boundaries of
SLVLESA; and

WHEREAS, SLVLESA has proposed to budget a 7% increase in the tax rate in excess of the
certified rate as defined in Section §59-2-024 of the Utah Code in 2024 to fund the increased
costs of providing local law enforcement services and has proposed to increase property taxes
solely for the purposes of maintaining its fund balance, paying increases in the costs assessed to
SLVLESA by the Unified Police Department and, as needed, to other providers such as a newly
reinstituted Salt Lake County Sheriff’s Bureau, and allowing new growth in its tax base to be used
for the provision of required new services; and

WHEREAS, pursuant to Section 17B-1-1003 of the Utah Code, SLVLESA submitted a report on
the proposed tax increase to the White City Metro Township Council (“Council”) at a duly noticed
meeting on November 2, 2023; and

WHEREAS, the Council allowed time during the duly noticed meeting on November 2, 2023 for
comment on the proposed tax increase from members of the Council and the public; and

WHEREAS, Section 17B-2a-903 of the Utah Code requires the prior approval of an increase in
the certified rate by SLVLEAS from the legislative bodies of any municipality and county whose
territory is located within SLVEASA, or approval of a majority of the municipalities and two-thirds
(2/3) of the County legislative body; and

WHEREAS, before the SLVLESA Board of Trustees considers to levy a tax rate that exceeds the
certified tax rate, the SLVLESA Board of Trustees shall hold a public hearing to provide to all
interested parties the opportunity to be heard regarding the proposed tax rate increase; and

WHEREAS, SLVLESA shall provide notices of the public hearing as required by Section 59-2-
919 of the Utah Code; and

WHEREAS, after considering all public input from the public hearing and all other information
available to them, the SLVLESA Board of Trustees will vote on whether to levy a tax rate that
exceeds the certified tax rate.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE WHITE CITY METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL
as follows:

       1.      The Whtie City Metro Township Council hereby approves for 2024 the budgeting
of a 7% increase in property tax revenues in excess of revenues budgeted in the prior year and
the imposition of property taxes in excess of the certified tax rate to generate the increase in
budgeted property tax revenues as defined in Section 59-2-924 of the Utah Code.

APPROVED BY THE COUNCIL in White City Metro Township, Salt Lake County, Utah this 2nd
day of November 2023.

Council Reports

Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD)

              Mayor Flint stated the MSD is considering giving Salt Lake County a 180 notice
to move out of the Government Center. The board toured a potential location to move into. Salt
Lake County is considering a hiring freeze and that will cause problems with the projects that
were bonded for. The MSD needs to get the Salt Lake County engineers moved over to the MSD.
A meeting may be set up with engineers to determine what their needs are. The MSD can hire
independent engineers to help them if necessary. All of White City’s services are provided by an
independent district and cannot rely on Salt Lake County in the future.

Pocket Park

Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling (WFWRD)

               Council Member Shelton stated WFWRD’s budget is 15 percent below what it
was expected to be. A rate increase has been discussed; however, the board has requested that
WFWRD continue to squeeze the budget. Twelve new trucks are being budgeted for. Ideally, it
wants to replace a quarter of the fleet every four years. The Town of Brighton will see a rate
increase on front-load dumpsters. A survey was conducted and showed 83 percent confidence
for blue can services and 93 percent satisfaction with recycling.
