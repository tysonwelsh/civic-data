2.  Public hearing and possible action on amendments to the Chapter 11-6 of the 
Eastern Summit County Development Code to create regulations for the installation 
and placement of the Solar Arrays in Eastern Summit County - Ray Milliner, Principal 
Planner 
 
Planner Milliner shared the background for this application. There was a Public Hearing 
for this item previously and the Planning Commission has provided feedback. Some 
changes were made based on that feedback. Purpose statements were added. A new 
category of Free-standing Solar Energy System was created. A Major System is to be used 
commercially, with requirements discussed. Roof-mounted Systems are allowed on

The Public hearing was opened.

Commissioner Benson stated he has heard there is a halo around some systems and 
asked if there should be a limit on the amount of glare allowed. Planner Milliner answered 
there is a requirement included that states any Solar Energy System must be situated to 
eliminate concentrated glare. There have not been any complaints to the county about 
glare from any existing Systems. 
Commissioner Clyde shared some of his concerns that some of the enforcement wording 
is too vague and could be difficult to defend and enforce, including the definition of a 
Freestanding System in paragraph 5. Reflectivity is important, but how much is too much? 
There needs to be a better standard. Paragraph 7 discusses Major Systems and there needs 
to be clarification of when written confirmation is needed in the timeline. The capacity for 
Major Systems needs to be defined. Accessory buildings need to be allowed to have Roof-
mounted Systems to receive solar energy. Do the effects to Green Belt-like pivot corners 
need to be addressed? The use of pivot corners in Green Belt should be encouraged, as

Solar Energy Systems are compatible with agricultural use and do not disqualify Green Belt. 
Screening from adjacent public roadways should be required. 
Chair Sargent stated that harmonizing language should be deleted. 
Commissioner Sonntag asked if it is necessary for Major Systems to connect to a grid. 
Commissioner Clyde stated there needs to be proof that a utility will buy the power if the

should not be forced to sell to a power company. 
Commissioner Wheaton stated that a Major System can apply for a Conditional Use 
Permit (CUP) and asked if the areas where the CUP would be appropriate should be 
identified. Planner Milliner answered that the appropriate areas for a CUP for Major 
Systems would be addressed by zoning. Commissioner Clyde stated the concern is valid 
for highly visible areas. Chair Sargent stated that through the Conditional Use process if a 
condition cannot be made to address the impact, then the CUP cannot be approved. 
Screening should be defined better. 
Commissioner Clyde suggested there be a size limitation on acreage for a Major System. 
Planner Milliner stated that an MPD (Master Planned Development) could be required 
and include compatibility requirements. Commissioner Sonntag suggested a rezone to 
Light Industrial could be required for selling a Major System. Commissioner Clyde 
suggested a newly created Solar Overlay Zone. 
 
 
The Solar Energy System amendments discussion is Continued to a future meeting for 
revision to the Ordinance based on the discussion tonight.

1. Discussion Regarding the Eastern Summit County General Plan Amendments – Ray 
Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner shared the background for this discussion. There was a brief discussion 
in December about General Plan Amendments and the Planning Commission was asked to 
read the General Plan to be ready to discuss possible changes. 
Director Putt stated the Planning Commission is not being asked to itemize changes 
chapter by chapter but should be looking at a first cut of priorities. There will be a meeting 
with the County Council on January 20th to discuss the Planning Commission’s Work Plan, 
which includes a General Plan review and Code Amendments. 
Commissioner Rydalch stated there should be more discussion about housing needs and 
transportation and infrastructure. Agribusiness and tourism should be encouraged. 
Commissioner Wheaton read the Snyderville Basin General Plan for comparison and liked 
the more specific language. The Eastern Summit County General Plan should include more 
specific sustainability language, with a separate chapter for sustainability goals. Climate 
mitigation should also be included. Municipalities working together should be encouraged. 
There needs to be a greater public purpose with more global thinking. Transportation 
connectivity and protection of property values should also be addressed.

Commissioner Wilde stated his priorities are environmental quality, specifically water 
quality issues, and housing. The housing issues that occurred with Promontory need to be 
fixed. 
Commissioner Clyde stated that infrastructure is a big priority. The lack of sewage 
treatment is insurmountable in housing. A plan needs to be developed for expanding 
infrastructure while recognizing that expanded infrastructure is growth-inducing. Historic 
preservation and adaptive reuse are needed. An inventory of historic buildings should be 
created. TDR (Transfer of Development Rights) language should be removed. Weed control 
and nuclear waste are not General Plan items. 
Commissioner Benson stated that there needs to be an infrastructure plan with the same 
level of detail that is in the Snyderville Basin General Plan. The County needs to work with 
property owners to preserve agriculture as a viable industry. 
Commissioner Sonntag stated that sustainability with a focus on economic sustainability 
accommodates responsible growth. A Land Use Map may be used in overlay areas. 
Different types of growth need to be accommodated, including infrastructure. Public 
infrastructure districts could be encouraged. 
Chair Sargent stated that the General Plan speaks of maintaining the rural small-town 
character. This character needs to be enhanced while still maintaining responsible growth 
through rural design principles. On-site and off-site employee housing should be required 
in any major development, with incentives for additional accessory dwelling units. 
Commissioner Sonntag stated that a multi-family zone is needed for affordable housing.
