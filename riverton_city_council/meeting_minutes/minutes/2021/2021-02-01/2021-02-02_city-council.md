Presentations

       UTA Presentation - Carlton Christensen, UTA Board Chair, Jaron Robertson, UTA
       Innovative Mobility Solutions Director, and Laura Hanson, UTA Planning Director

Mayor Staggs introduced Carlton Christensen who is the UTA Board Chair and turned the time
over to him for his presentation.

Carlton Christensen showed the presentation entitled “Utah Transit Authority Update” which
provided an update on both the microtransit project and the five year service plan.

Jaron Robertson, UTA Innovative Mobility Solutions Director, talked about the UTA on Demand
by Via program. He explained that COVID-19 has caused a decline in ridership and said they
would extend the pilot through August 2021.

Mayor Staggs said some of the councilmembers had questions and asked if the flex routes
would replace microtransit?

Mr. Robertson said that the two programs have operated side by side and now, due to demand,
they will suspend the UTA flex routes.

Councilmember Stewart asked if suspension of the flex routes would be permanent.

Mr. Christensen said that they would give the public the chance to comment if they ever decide
to suspend the service. He explained that they will suspend them right now but if it needs to be
permanent then they will have public hearing.

Laura Hanson, UTA Planning Director, showed the flow chart for the five year service plan. She
explained that when they asked members of the county what they would like, they asked UTA to
improve service on the west side, namely 3600 West, 5600 West and 3100 South by increasing
frequency and hours of service. She said they were considering microtransit in the south end of
the valley, which would include Riverton. She said the vision for their five year plan included
expanding rail service, expanding bus service, and providing a cleaner fleet.

Councilmember Stewart asked if there was decision on where they would expand the light rail
since the plan currently shows it running through Draper and he asked why everything was
being pushed to the East side? Also, what future projects did they have for the Riverton
community?

Mr. Christensen explained that they were working on the future plan with the County.

Councilmember McCay asked if there were any services near Mountain View Village.

Mr. Christensen said that the VA app was the current service and that through the app someone
can request pick up from their home and the driver will connect them to the rail. He also
explained that there are plans that show a connection at Daybreak but he is not sure if there are
more cost effective solutions than light rail.

Mayor Staggs said that the most telling slide in the presentation is the slide that showed a big
hole in the southwest valley for mass transit. He said that more service should be added to the
southwest side of the valley because this is where half of the undeveloped property is in Salt
Lake County.

Councilmember McDougal said that she echoed what the Mayor said about the hole in the
southwest part of the valley and explained that we need those services desperately. He asked
if funds were set aside for the project?

Mr. Christensen said that the recent transit tax funds are still there and available for some
construction to take place but the projects are a year behind.

Councilmember Stewart expressed concern over “ridership” saying that it is hard to increase
ridership without the infrastructure to do it. He said that UTA needs to come up with a different
factor than ridership.

Mayor Staggs expressed appreciation for the UTA presentation.

         Latest Designs of the Potential Tracy Aviary in Riverton City - Mayor Staggs &
         Councilmember Buroker

Councilmember Buroker introduced the potential for having a satellite of the Tracy Aviary in
Riverton City and explained that a significant amount of work had already been done and that
there was still a lot to do but it has been a slow process due to COVID-19. She said she is an
advocate of the project because Tracy Aviary is willing to take and maintain this space for
Riverton City.

Tim Brown, representative for Tracy Aviary, explained that 5-6 years ago the Tracy Aviary
Board looked at building another location close to the Jordan River. He said that
Councilmember Buroker convinced them that Riverton would be a great site and they were
excited about it. He said the potential Tracy Aviary near the Jordan River would be a nature
based site where during the day they would do fields trips and educate families and at night
there could be celebrations such as weddings.

Mr. Brown explained that the lease agreement has a lease boundary of about 4.83 acres. In
planning for this project, he said there are two budgets to consider, capital budget and operation
budget. He said he projects the capital to build the site would be about $4 million dollars and
would cost about $250,000 a year to operate.

Mayor Staggs said that the lease that was approved by the council in April 2020 and that the
lease boundary was slightly different than the drawings. He said that the Parks and Trails
Committee had provided feedback to adjust this boundary so that there was an easement for
the parking lot and that it was not too close to the clubhouse. He thought that initially the project
was about $2 million upfront but the presentation showed $4 million and asked if he could
explain the difference.

Mr. Brown explained that the initial numbers were incorrectly quoted. He said that the $4 million
estimate included the increase in construction costs and the anticipated fence that would be
built around the entire boundary of the project. He said that they would appreciate a contribution
from Riverton.

Mayor Staggs asked for any additional comments.

Councilmember Wells asked if the fencing would be chain link.

Mr. Brown said it would be a good quality fence and would not be chain link.

Mayor Staggs said that Riverton City has contributed to the design that was approved by the
Council and those funds have been applied to the design and research of this facility. He said
that the council would have further discussion about the design and costs.

Public Hearing(s) / Action Items

       Reconsideration of Ordinance No. 21-03 – Proposed Rezone of approximately 2
       Acres Located at 12814 South Sunday Drive from RR-22 to R-3 - Eric Schroff,
       Applicant

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that on Jan 19, 2021, the City
Council voted to reconsider a proposed rezone application, which had been denied by the City
Council on January 5, 2021. He said this is a public hearing for a Rezone of approximately two-
acres, located at 12814 Sunday Drive at the southeast corner of Sunday Drive and Dutchman
Lane (approximately 12800 South and 4400 West). Mr. Lethbridge explained that the property is
approximately 180-ft by 484-ft, with the long side facing Dutchman Lane. Mr. Lethbridge said
the current zoning of the property is R-1, which is a Single Family zone with animal rights and
40,000-SF lots.

Mr. Lethbridge said that he had received multiple public comments regarding the widening of
Dutchman Lane. He said that the widening of Dutchman Lane is not tied to the proposed
zoning but will be necessary. He said the Planning Commission had recommended approval.

Mayor Staggs asked the Councilmembers if there were comments.

Councilmember Stewart said that he received emails and phone calls and he believes that there
is a proposal that would make everyone happy. He said that he appreciated everyone reaching
out and appreciated the Council being willing to reconsider the ordinance.

Councilmember Wells said that he drove the area during rush hour and said that the barn had
been taken down. He said the road is very narrow and will need to be widened.

Mayor Staggs said that he had also received phone calls and the following email from David
Montierth who lives at 4383 W. Aspen Cottage Lane:

    “I live a block from the rezone that is coming before the council again. I disapprove of
    the 1/3 acre rezone that is being proposed. Please do not allow this parcel be rezoned
    as such. The lots on the North side of that street are an acre or more and I feel that this
    parcel should only be allowed to match what is already there. This would likely reflect
    the feelings of those who already live on Dutchman. Making smaller lots on the South
    side of Dutchman wouldn't fit the vision of what is already established.”

Mayor Staggs opened a Public Hearing and called for public comment.

Eric Schroff, Applicant, said that he was owner, developer and builder of the property. He said
that after speaking with the Council and receiving comments, he would like to change his
request to R-2 half acre lots. He said that his goal as a builder was to make money but it was

Eric Schroff said that he knew they would need to widen the road and took it into account in the

Discussion Item

         Resolution No. 21-08 - Authorizing Purchasing to order the FY 21/22 Police
         Vehicles - Craig Calvert, Operational Director

Chief Hutson explained that in order to get the police vehicles for 2022, they need to order them
now. He said the payment for the vehicles will be in July and will come out of the fiscal year
2021-2022. He said that the lease contract with Zions Bank will go to City Council in July 2021
and the first year's payment will not exceed $99,000.00 and will be made after the contract is
executed.

Councilmember Wells said he was surprised that the yearly mileage in the lease contract was
so low, to which Chief Hutson explained that they pay for a 50,000 mile lease and they feel
comfortable that they can stay within that range. He also explained that he monitors the mileage
and rotates the cars to make sure that they stay under the 50,000 mileage.

Councilmember McCay asked him to clarify whether they were buying seven cars total or 14,
and Chief Hutson explained that Riverton Police Department inherited 26 vehicles from Unified
Police and they ranged from being a few years old to very new. He said that they hired 35
officers when they began the police department and so they leased 11 vehicles initially,
because it is important to have a couple of vehicles in reserve in case you have incidents where
you need an extra marked car. He said that he believes leasing is the best option because it
keeps the cars in good shape and they don’t have to pay mechanical fees. The second year
they leased 12 vehicles and then they will lease 12 more for a total of 35 vehicles.

Councilmember McCay MOVED that the Riverton City Council approve Resolution No. 21-
08 Authorizing Purchasing to order the FY 21/22 Police Vehicles now. Councilmember
Wells SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion; there being
none, he called for a roll call vote and the vote was as follows: Councilmembers Buroker-Yes,
McCay-Yes, McDougal-Yes, Stewart-Yes, and Wells-Yes. The motion passed unanimously.
