BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Zoning Map Amendment for approximately
0.92 acres located on the west side of 300 East, at approximately 13530 South 300 East
(Exhibit C). The property is currently zoned RA2. The applicant is requesting that a Zoning
Map Amendment be approved to rezone the property from RA2 to R3 in order to subdivide
the property.

The property contains a single family home built in 1970. The applicant has stated, if
approved, she would like to subdivide the property into two lots.

General Plan and Zoning. The Land Use Map of the General Plan calls for the Residential

The property has been assigned the RA2 zoning classification, supporting approximately
two dwelling unit per acre (Exhibit E). According to Draper City Municipal Code (DCMC)
Section 9-8-020 the purpose of the RA2 zone is to “foster low density development with
little impact on its surroundings and municipal services; to generally preserve the character
of the City’s semi-rural areas; and to promote and preserve conditions favorable to large-
lot family life, including the keeping of limited numbers of animals and fowl. This zone is
intended to promote a combination of retail and service facilities which, in character and
scale, meet day to day needs of nearby residents.” The property abuts the RA2 zone on the
north, south, east, and west. The property also abuts the RA1 (Residential Agricultural,
20,000 square foot lot minimum) zone on the North West corner.

Criteria For Approval. A Text and Map Amendment is a matter committed to the legislative
discretion of the City Council and is not controlled by any one standard. However, in
making a recommendation to the City Council, the Planning Commission should consider
the following factors in Section 9-5-060(E) of the DCMC:

        1. Map Amendments:
            a. Whether the proposed amendment is consistent with goals, objectives and policies
               of the city's general plan;
            b. Whether the proposed amendment is harmonious with the overall character of
               existing development in the vicinity of the subject property;
            c. Whether the proposed amendment is consistent with the standards of any
               applicable overlay zone;
            d. The extent to which the proposed amendment may adversely affect adjacent
               property; and
            e. The adequacy of facilities and services intended to serve the subject property,
               including, but not limited to, roadways, parks and recreation facilities, police and
               fire protection, schools, stormwater drainage systems, water supplies, and
               wastewater and refuse collection.

Engineering and Public Works Divisions Review.

   1. The adequacy of facilities and services intended to serve the subject property, including but not
      limited to roadways, parks and recreation facilities, police and fire protection, schools, storm
      water drainage systems, water supplies, and waste water and refuse collection;

       Other than noted below, we are not aware of any inadequacies of the facilities intended
       to serve this property.

          a. The increase in density from one residential unit per acre zoning to approximately
             three units per acre constitutes a minor increase in the amount of traffic to and
             from the subject area based on the subject parcel. One single family residential
             structure and associated agricultural uses constitutes up to nine daily trips and
             two peak hour trips, based on industry standard vehicle trip tables. With the
             acreage of this rezone, an existing home exists on the lot and no additional traffic
             is projected. In the future six or so vehicles could be added to the surrounding
             streets if the parcel were to be subdivided. The anticipated increase in traffic is well
             below the 100 peak hour trips that would require a Traffic Impact Study.

          b. Connectivity with the subject parcel to a public right-of-way is on 300 East, a major
             collector street, or on 13560 South, a local residential street. Access locations will
             be required to meet Draper City Standards as outlined in our Master
             Transportation Plan. It is recommended for the access to be on 13560 South, in
             order to meet Draper City standards.

          c. There are existing storm drain facilities located on 300 East. Provisions for onsite
             storm drainage, along with the connection to the storm facilities on 300 East will
             need to be addressed with any subdivision or site plan application, and shall
             comply with the provisions of the development requirements within the Draper
             City Municipal Code.

          d. Sanitary sewer facilities will be provided by South Valley Sewer District. Any
             subdivision application will require a commitment to serve from the Sewer District
             that facilities are adequate to provide service for the proposed uses.

          e. Drinking water facilities will be provided by WaterPro. Any subdivision application
             will require a commitment to serve from the WaterPro that facilities are adequate
             to provide service for the proposed uses.
