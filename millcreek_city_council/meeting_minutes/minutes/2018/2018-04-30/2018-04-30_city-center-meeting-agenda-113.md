DEVELOPER MEETING — 4:00 p.m.
TIME COMMENCED: 4:01 p.m.

Mayor Silvestrini said the purpose of the meeting is for the City to engage with the business
community in “the wedge,” (between 1300 E. and Highland Dr.) and with the development
community. He said one of the priorities as a new city was to start the general plan process.
Millcreek has been engaged in that process for about 16 months, and some results of those
efforts are that residents are asking for a place to gather, to shop, and eat. The City has been
receiving a lot of applications for large housing projects and the Mayor said if the City does not
properly plan for it now, then the area will end up being haphazard. He said that businesses in the
City have also requested that the City invest in the area. The City’s goal is to enact a town center

ordinance by the end of the summer. The Mayor requested that the developers have an open
dialogue with city staff of their needs and wants for that center.

Francis Lilly provided a handout to the developers and emphasized that the City would like the
following two questions answered:

e What makes a successful place?

e If! were visiting the City Center, what would I like to see?

Lilly said through general plan feedback, Millcreek lacks an identifiable center. He said there
was an online poll and a significant majority of residents are interested in investing in a town
center at “the wedge.” The surveys have yielded responses wanting a sense of place but what that
is has differed among respondents. Some said the City needs more plazas and gathering spots, to
provide more art and entertainment, needs places for food trucks, want specific commercial
businesses, to not make Millcreek like Sugarhouse, and that the area needs better design
standards. There seems to be support for 2-3 story townhomes but not anything taller than that.
The survey asked about mixed use and community amenities. Respondents favored pedestrian-
only retail areas, housing above retail, and a city campus. The most popular amenity in the
survey was plazas. Lilly said tools the City has to accomplish the city center goal are: the general
plan, an overlay zone, master plan and future ordinance updates, community reinvestment area
and opportunity zone, and municipal boundary adjustments.

Michael Winder said the City just created a Community Reinvestment Agency and they will be
meeting on May 14". The City was able to get the area federally designated as an opportunity
zone. He mentioned that Millcreek is looking to annex the Brickyard from Salt Lake City.

Lilly said keys for success are: transit; design; understanding and overcoming constraints such as
land values, parking, and faultline concerns; public space; and commercial space.

Winder said the City does not want a hodgepodge area as he showed possible concepts for the
project. Lilly showed pictures of existing walkable streets and plazas in other cities and states.
He said he hoped to receive candid feedback from the developers as this is the first meeting.

Lilly started the discussion by asking the following questions: What do you want to see in your
new City Center? How do we create a one-of-a-kind City Center for Millcreek? What are we
overlooking? What makes great streets? How do we maintain the character of neighborhoods
surrounding the City Center? What concerns do you have?

Josh Woodbury said he thinks a successful place is a village; where one can live, work, and play
with all sorts of real estate uses. He would like a neighborhood to be activated for as many hours
a day as possible.

Jeremy Carver said there needs to be a lot of parking and connectivity for there to be walkability.

Daniel Woodbury said a successful place needs to have a focal point that draws people there,
such as public space, statue, or water feature.


Lilly asked if Millcreek has any icons that can be built around now.

Russ Sorensen said the waterwheel on Wasatch Blvd. is a focal point that could be themed into
the City since Millcreek was named after it.

Derril Barnum said he is the Water Master for the Lower Millcreek Irrigation Company. He
expressed concerns about preserving the irrigation because developers have caused problems in
building over ditches in the past. He said there are three or four laterals that come from Highland
Dr.

Lilly asked what the right mix of commercial and residential is.

Scott Howell said he likes the thought of adding to the workforce. The City needs density to
support the tax base. He said sales tax drives everything. He said the City also needs to look at
housing affordability because he wants his kids to live here. The reality is smaller lots with
townhomes. He said this is a once in a lifetime opportunity to develop something that will make
Millcreek a unique and affordable place.

Lilly asked how to maintain the character of neighborhoods surround the City Center.

Mayor Silvestrini said the obvious answer is to transition it; the first tier of buildings from the
neighborhood is two stories and then they go up from there. It was brought up that there is
almost a 16 ft. elevation change between 1300 E. and Highland Dr. Winder said that by leveling
the grade out, there could be room for underground parking. There could also be tradeoffs with
the residents, for example, in exchange for density and benefits for the developer on the west
side of Highland Dr., the residents on the east side could possibly get a sound wall or new lilac
bushes.

Lilly asked what is being overlooked.

Stan Hanks mentioned that Holladay has a food truck plaza, but they have issues with parking.
He said it will be hard to not create congestion in the surrounding neighborhoods. He said the
retail would make sense where the traffic count is. Lilly said it is hard to have retail without
close proximate street parking and there is no parking on the busy streets.

Jake Christensen said the City should keep a focus on Wasatch Choice 2050; bike-ability,
walkability, and transit friendly. He said wide sidewalks with trees in the park strip and narrow
roads make a street more walkable.

Josh Woodbury said he is worried about traffic being an issue. He thinks moving traffic will
continue to be important for the region and city. He said Highland Dr. and 3300 S. are not
pedestrian friendly.

Winder said he is partial to the walkable street in the middle of the design along the fault line
because buildings cannot go there, but he is open to suggestions. Lilly said the middle road in the
center is driven by what development goes in.

Chad Christensen asked if city staff has considered having pedestrian streets without car traffic.

Kyle Jardine said the east-west streets have potential for on-street parking.

Jeremy Carver said if there was public parking on either side, then the public can walk where
they want, such as at City Creek Center.

Carol Elliott said she hears complaints about City Creek and the parking. She said customers like
parking next to the business they are going to. The complaint is that the mall is too large, and
that people do not like having to walk very far to get where they want to go.

Ryan Heath said to use the streets the City has and make them better instead of adding new
streets.

Ryan Simmons said that inside “the wedge” that there are 40-50 property owners, so the City’s
biggest opportunity is working with the infrastructure not the property owners.

Howell said the City needs to think about smaller cars for transportation because that is the
future trend. He said the City needs bold leadership in the Planning Commission for determining
parking ratios.

Chris Ensign said he would hate for parking to drive this development. He likes how Holladay
has created an identity. He thinks Millcreek needs a thematic grip on this project to identify to
people that they are in Millcreek. He would like to know how the internal street would happen.
Winder said it will be a hard process with creativity, political will, and give/take with developers.

Lilly asked the audience to fill out the survey sheet. He would like the group to meet regularly.

Mayor Silvestrini said there are a lot of things to think about. He asked the developers to stay
engaged in the process as there will be more meetings.

The meeting adjourned at 5:05 p.m.

STAKEHOLDER MEETING — 5:30 p.m.
TIME COMMENCED: 5:40 p.m.

Mayor Silvestrini welcomed everyone and started the meeting by asking how many people have
participated with the general plan and how many receive the City Newsletter. He encouraged
people to sign up for the weekly newsletter on the City website at millcreek.us. He also said
residents could email him or the Council personally if they ever needed to contact them.

He said the City asked residents what they would like to see through the general plan process. He
said through that process, residents have said they would like more places to gather, shop, eat,
and ride their bikes. The Mayor said the meeting is about “the wedge” area (between Highland
Dr. and 1300 E. and 3000 S. and 3300 S.) and there will be a contest for a better name later. He
said that business owners have asked for the City to invest and redevelop the area with more
lighting and walkability. He said the vast majority of the property in the wedge is zoned
commercial and residential high density. He said a lot of the zoning is what the City inherited
from Salt Lake County. The City has revised some zoning to add mixed use. He said Millcreek is
getting applications for large apartment building projects in the area, and under the current
zoning, they are allowed. The problem is that residents would like a town center and the City is
getting applications that will set what goes there for the next 60 years. The market is putting
development pressure on the City. The convergence of the applications without a plan is why the
City has called the meeting tonight. Mayor Silvestrini said the whole point of the meeting tonight
is to find out what the residents and property owners of Millcreek want. He said the meeting was
set up as group tables so that every resident has an opportunity to talk and be heard. Mayor
Silvestrini said he hears from residents, “don’t make Millcreek like Sugarhouse,” but he wants to
know what residents mean by that. He said this is the first meeting the City will have on the
topic, but it will not be the last. Mayor Silvestrini said the City Council was present tonight
because they care.

Francis Lilly, Planning Services Director, thanked the residents for the turnout and polled the
residents about how they heard about the meeting whether through news outlets, social media,
mailings, or the City’s newsletter. He said the City is here to listen to the residents about their
expectations and concerns for a city center. Lilly said in the general plan outreach, the question
came up if the City should have a city center and the majority of respondents were lukewarm or
in favor of one. He said in summary, that staff is hearing that Millcreek does not have a sense of
place and should remain unique. He went over the keys for success in implementing a city
center, which are: understanding and overcoming constraints, design, transit, public space, and

commercial space. He said the City did a number of surveys with residents and respondents were
favorable to mixed use options, a city center, and plazas.

Michael Winder, Economic Development Director, said there is a trade off with economic
development and density. He showed the audience concepts from developers of possible city
centers and then pictures of walkable streets and plazas. Lilly showed one picture example of
Belmar in Colorado and mentioned that there are height limits on the buildings. He showed a
picture of Lakewood, Colorado and said that there is a plaza town space that is restaurant seating
in the summer and an ice rink in the winter.

Lilly asked city staff to spread among the groups to facilitate conversations in answering the
following questions:

e What makes a successful place?

e If! were visiting the city center, what would I like to see?

The table groups then convened in independent conversations. The general meeting reconvened
approximately 30 minutes later.

Lilly said the plan is to take the comments received at this meeting to craft an overlay zone that
addresses the big issues, knowing that this will be an ongoing discussion for about a year. This
will be done as an interim step in June/July to assist the developers making applications now. He
said the general plan process will continue to engage through the fall. In the winter the City will
be doing a master plan to incorporate everything that has been discussed. Then next spring he
anticipates zone changes to finalize the plan. He told the audience that the City will have similar
meetings like the one held tonight, in the future.

Mayor Silvestrini said this planning effort is being funded through a grant that the City applied
for. The Wasatch Front Regional Council awarded Millcreek with $75,000. He said he has
approached Salt Lake City about doing a boundary adjustment around Brickyard and there might
be a negotiation that Millcreek has to work out with Salt Lake City in order to do so.

Mayor Silvestrini opened the floor to questions from the audience.

Kumar Shah said it is important to think of the long-term and that the wisdom is with the people
not the legislature.

Scott (last name unknown) asked if a developer can build more than 4 stories in the current
zoning, and if it can be done now before the city center is done. The Mayor said the zoning
currently does allow for more than 4 stories. The audience echoed that height is a concern.

A resident said that Sugarhouse feels claustrophobic and Millcreek should not enclose
themselves and lose the views of the mountains and trees. He would like to preserve green space

for the mental health of residents.

Mayor Silvestrini asked the audience to sign in so that they can be notified of future meetings.

Joel (last name unknown) said he does not want to see TRAX come up to the City. Mayor
Silvestrini said there has been a thought that the S-line could come south to the City. He said
transit could be an option, but it is a long way off because it is an expensive proposition.

Francis Lilly said the residents can email questions and comments to citycenter@millcreek.us.

Earl Shepherd wondered if the city center could be on 700 E. by the Western Governors
University instead because there are taller buildings there. Mayor Silvestrini said there is a larger
area of land already zoned commercial in “the wedge” and more pressure for development there
too. He said the City must work in tandem with the market.

Chris (last name unknown) said he is young and he does not plan to use public transportation in
the future because cars are more convenient. He would like to hear about other options to
improve the roads and make it more drivable. Mayor Silvestrini said a lot of roads in Millcreek
need help. He said the County will be enacting a transportation quarter percent sales tax that will
help Millcreek obtain about a million dollars a year in transportation funding. He said the City
has secured grants for 2 major road projects; one on 900 E. between 3900 S. and 4500 S. and one
to rebuild 3900 S. between 2300 E. and I-215 freeway to the east. He said when dealing with
roads, it is better to preserve the life of the pavement with slurry seals. The City has plans to
resurface 3900 S. from 700 E. to 700 W. this summer. Millcreek tries to keep all the roads at
least at a 70% level condition on a scale of 1 to 100. He said the City also must address transit.
The future holds ride-sharing programs and autonomous cars, so it is something to consider
when planning for the future of the City.

Julie (last name unknown) said she was involved in a discouraging development process with the
County in which the development was approved. She said she hopes Millcreek will give equal if
not higher priority to the interests of residents over that of big developers. Mayor Silvestrini said
the Council has the residents’ interests at heart, but the City must honor property rights too.

A resident asked the Mayor, in his opinion, if the County has been reasonable in the sharing of
available funds. Mayor Silvestrini said most of the available funds is tax money that is divided
by the State of Utah and given out to cities according to either population, where people shop, or
based upon how many road miles are in the city. He said the County generally does not have a
say in that, but he has seen inequities with distributions of the quarter of the quarter tax. He felt
that some municipalities were favored, but there were a number of layers of decision making in
that process. He said most of the tax revenue Millcreek get is because Millcreek is entitled to get
it through how the State distributes it. The City has done what they can do to make sure they are
getting that. Mayor Silvestrini mentioned that the City’s Business License Administrator has
been finding businesses that were not giving their sales tax to Millcreek who should have been,
so staff is working to correct that.

Mayor Silvestrini thanked the many residents for coming out to the meeting.

The meeting adjourned at 7:02 p.m.
