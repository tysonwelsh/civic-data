            G.2. UTA micro transit pilot and five-year service plan. (By Director of Planning, Steven
                 Schaefermeyer and UTA, Carlton Christensen)

Carlton Christensen, UTA, introduced the presentation. Jaren Robertson and Laura Hanson, UTA,
reviewed a prepared presentation (Attachment A).

Council Member Shelton asked if there are plans to expand the micro transit service to cover all of South
Jordan? Mr. Christensen said they will be looking at that. Their current contract does not allow for it. He
said they are procuring a provider that could be used in the 5 year plan and sub away from the flex routes
to micro transit. He said they will be looking at that in 2022 or beyond.

Council Member Shelton asked about the timing of the rapid bus on 5600 West? Mr. Christensen said
they are continuing to work on that. It is within a 5 year window. They are continuing to work on park
and ride lots. He said it won’t be done in 2022, but will be done before the 5 years are up.

Ms. Hanson said even within UTA, there are some services on 5600 West that will be running in the year
2022-2023. They won’t be at the full level of service, but it starts to lay the foundation.

Council Member Shelton asked when they expect to have electric busses in service? Mr. Christensen said
in 2020, they started with 3 electric busses. They don’t have a maintenance facility to handle a larger
fleet, but that is currently being constructed. They will be adding to the fleet. They are currently working
on how to charge the busses so they don’t overburden the power grid.

Council Member Harris asked if the wheelchair accessible vehicle patrons are taken to their final
destination or do they go to trax? Mr. Robertson said they have not looked at the origin and destination
data for those specific trips. He said some were going to transit stations, but since covid, most of the
service is staying in the local service area.

Council Member Harris said he is surprised at the amount of increase in ridership in wheelchair accessible
vehicles during covid. He thanked UTA for getting those people to their destinations. Mr. Christensen
said it is an important service to provide, particularly to their aging population.

            G.3. South Jordan Police Department Accreditation (By Police Chief, Jeff Carr)

Mayor Ramsey indicated that South Jordan has an outstanding Police Department and the City Council is
grateful for their efforts.

Val Shupe (Executive Director of the Utah Chiefs of Police Association) recognized Chief Jacobs who
was present. Mr. Shupe indicated that the South Jordan Police Department has re-certified. He reviewed
some of the standards that have to be met and said this is a big positive for the city. They have to re-
accreditate every 5 years. They are also required to submit a yearly report of compliance. He noted that a
plaque has been given to the police department.

Police Chief Carr thanked Deputy Chief Jason Knight and Master Officer Kevin Tingey, who recently
retired. He said they are grateful for this accreditation. Deputy Chief Knight thanked Kevin Tingey, who
was tasked with this assignment. He said it was not an easy process.

Council Member Harris asked what are a couple of things that they gain from doing this? Police Chief

            H.1. Resolution R2021-04, amending FY2020-21 Mid-Year budget. (By CFO, Sunil Naidu)

CFO Naidu reviewed the background information on this item. He reviewed new revenues as well as
restricted revenues. He reviewed the carryovers for projects that are not yet complete. He reviewed the
capital projects fund and noted the summary of where the funds came from originally. He noted a change
in the fee schedule related to the storm drain.

Council Member Marlor said he is comfortable with the budget and mid-year changes. He said one thing
that is not in the budget is about Mayor Ramsey. He said they are not compensating her appropriately. He
said he serves on the compensation committee and Mayor Ramsey is 28% below average in compensation
for Salt Lake County for cities our size. She has been modest about her role and compensation. Everyone
on the City Council and staff and many residents know that she is not a part time Mayor. Mayor Ramsey
is more than a full time Mayor and has done a tremendous job and represents us well. The City is getting
more bang for our buck than we deserve. He said he hopes that inadequacy will be addressed and
compensated. The city has benefitted probably millions as a result of her great work. He said the City
Council and staff have all participated, but without Mayor Ramsey behind the wheel, the city would not
have had the benefits that she has helped us get. He would like that issue addressed soon. He thanked
Mayor Ramsey for everything she is doing for the city. Council Member Shelton concurred and said there
needs to be some measure of recompense for sure.

Council Member Shelton thanked Mr. Naidu for his review of the budget. He said he likes the idea of
changing the notes that the projects are ongoing and they expect the funds to be used in the future.

Mayor Ramsey thanked Council Member Marlor for his kind words. She said she is humbled and grateful
for the opportunity to serve the city. It is not about money to her. She has chosen to serve as Mayor over
other opportunities that have come along. She said the salary for the Mayor should be appropriate,
whatever that means, and regardless of who is serving.

Council Member Harris made a motion to approve Resolution R2021-04. Council Member Shelton

            H.3. Ordinance 2021-02, repealing Chapter 16.36.070(F) of the South Jordan Municipal
                 Code regarding Temporary Commercial and Residential Development Signs-Banner
                 Signs on city light poles and amending Chapter 16.36.120(B) of the South Jordan
                 Municipal Code regarding Prohibited Signs. (By Director of Planning, Steven
                 Schaefermeyer)

Planning Director Schaefermeyer reviewed the background information on this item.

City Attorney Loose said there was a legal case with Gilbert Arizona that clarified if you give access to
public spaces for commercial speech, you have to give the same access for all the other protected speech.
Several years ago, the city did a re-write of the sign Ordinance, but the banner signs were overlooked.
There are no signs of any speech allowed in the right of way, except directional signs.

City Commerce Director Preece said this was put in the code several City Manager’s ago, as a way to
earn money for the city. It was never a good idea. Mr. Schaefermeyer said staff is continuing to work to
clean up the sign code.

Mayor Ramsey opened the public hearing. There were no comments. She closed the public hearing.

Council Member McGuire made a motion to approve Ordinance 2021-02. Council Member Harris
seconded the motion. Roll call vote. The vote was 4-0 in favor, with Council Member Zander not
present at the meeting at this time.

        I. Staff Reports and Calendaring Items

CM Whatcott said when the weather is better, they are planning two ribbon cuttings. They want to
celebrate the opening of the new section of the cemetery, and do a “hose cutting” for Station 64.

Fire Chief Dawson said the Fire Department had an evaluation by the Insurance Services Office (ISO).
That rating determines how well they are able to respond to commercial and residential fires. They look at
things such as the water supply, the 911 program, community outreach, etc. The scale is 1-10, with 1
being the best. The average rating is 5. The city was previously at a 3, but is now a 2, which means they
are in the top 2 percent in the nation. He said they are close to a 1. As the city expands and grows, he feels
they will achieve a 1 rating in the not too distant future. He said it was a big leap for the city to go from a
3 to a 2; they are proud of that.
