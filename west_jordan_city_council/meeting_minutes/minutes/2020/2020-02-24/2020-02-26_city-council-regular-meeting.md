       d.    DISCUSSION           AND POSSIBLE          ACTION REGARDING                     RESOLUTION          20-
             13 ADOPTING            THE CITY TRANSPORTATION                      MASTERPLAN                FUTURE
             ROADWAY NETWORK MAP WITH CHANGES TO 6400 WEST, WELLS
             PARK        ROAD        AND     6700 WEST         BETWEEN            THE        NEW      BINGHAM
             HIGHWAY AND THE OLD BINGHAM HIGHWAY
Bill Baranowski explained the recommendation that two current master planned collector
roads,      6400 West and 6700 West, be combined to one single road between Wells Park
Road and Old Bingham Highway ( 10200 South). The new collector road would be called

6600 West. The future 6600 West would connect to New Bingham Highway from Wells
Park Road via a diagonal road running north by northwest. Wells Park Road would end at
6600 West instead of 6700 West.


If the master plan change were allowed, it would eliminate the need to bisect the two large
industrial developments ( VAST and HULA) and permit them to share in the cost of
constructing        only   one    road   instead of two     separate   roads.   It would not be possible to
eliminate both future roads as the traffic model predicted a future traffic demand of at least
12, 000 vehicles per day on this vital north/ south connection. Therefore, eliminating both
roads in this area was not under consideration.


Councilmember Lamb expressed a desire to respect the neighborhood of homes between
6400        West   and   the   proposed      6600   West,   and suggested a buffer along 6600 West.
Councilmember Green expressed agreement.


Councilmember Whitelock said she did not think the proposed diagonal road connecting
to New Bingham Highway made sense.

Chair McConnehey invited property owner Mr. Jones to comment.

Mr. Jones asked the Council to not approve the proposed diagonal portion of 6600 West
until he had a development plan to propose.


Chair       McConnehey           expressed   concern
                                                        regarding      the   safety     of   the   proposed    6600
West/ Wells Park Road intersection being right next to a residential neighborhood, and said
he     would   prefer      for 6700 West to be         constructed     as    planned.    Councilmember          Pack
expressed agreement.




Councilmember Green asked if VAST would need direct access to a north/ south road. Mr.
Baranowski commented that at least one of the collector roads would be necessary. Dave
Murphy explained dedicated easements in the area.

Councilmember Lamb expressed a preference to postpone discussion until the City knew
what would happen on the Jones property.

MOTION:            Councilmember Green moved to deny Resolution 20- 13 and send it back
                   to Staff for further      review.   Councilmember Lamb               seconded     the   motion.

      X.            WORK SESSION( CONTINUED)
             a.     POTENTIAL              CONCEPTS                FOR RON WOOD PARK

      Dave Murphy explained that impact fees would provide funding for the Ron Wood Park
      project ($ 3 million). Staff explained that Salt Lake County facilitated rec programs for
      West         Jordan. The          City     Parks      Department       provided              park   maintenance.   Mr.   Murphy
      requested Council feedback regarding the two design concepts presented to the Council in
      work session prior to the Council meeting.

      Councilmember Green                      said    he   preferred     Concept           1,   and suggested combining the two
      proposed smaller fields to make one larger field. He said he was fine with six pickleball
      courts. Councilmember Pack agreed with the suggestion to combine the two smaller fields
      into one larger field. He commented that lines for pickleball could be painted on tennis
      courts       as well.




      Councilmember                Whitelock commented that the north grass portion of the proposed park
      would        cost    the full $ 3 million          currently available for the project, and suggested the City
      wait       until    more     funds   were       available.    She said     she   preferred          Concept 1, and repeated her
      desire for the skate park to be included in the first phase of construction.


      Councilmember Worthen                       said      she    like Concept        1.        She suggested involving volunteer
      community members in the construction and landscaping phase.

      Chair McConnehey agreed with the suggestion to combine the two smaller fields in
      Concept 1 into one larger field. The Council discussed the possibility of removing proposed
      sidewalks between fields to provide more multi- use capability.

      Mr.        Murphy discussed parking options with the Council. Councilmembers Whitelock,
      Pack, and       Worthen indicated they would prefer that construction of the skate park and
      pickleball courts occur in the first phase of construction.

      c.   DISCUSSION OF WOOD RANCH
Ray McCandless reported that the proposed Master Development Plan for Wood Ranch
included a street width of 20 feet, meeting the Fire Department minimum requirement.

Mr. Wall suggested Staff go through the Master Development Plan with the Council in a
separate work session to explain how their concerns could be addressed.


Councilmember Whitelock questioned whether the City wanted to agree to a 30- year PID
term, and expressed concern with the idea ofpublic artwork, questioning who would decide
what artwork would be acceptable.


The Council indicated agreement with the following aspects of the Wood Ranch Master
Development        Plan:


           Preservation of Hillside Landform and Ridgeline Open Space Park
           Public Trail System with trailheads and unique amenities
           Walkable neighborhoods with private amenities to support high tax values
           HOA maintenance of private amenities, parks and trails


The Council requested further information and discussion regarding:

           City ownership and maintenance of parks, roads, and lanes
           PID as proposed in Master Development Plan


Councilmember Green said he believed the Council should have been discussing some of
these issues at the start of January, not the end of February, and expressed frustration with
the lack of time available. He agreed with the following dates suggested by Staff:

           Council Hearing/ Action on March 11
           PCFB Zone
           Wood Ranch Master Development Plan


           Council Hearing/ Action on March 25
           Master Development Agreement
           Annexation
