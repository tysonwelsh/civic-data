   a. Proposed Subdivision Amendment, Rolling Oaks, Lot 11.
      Mr. Simpson reported the property owner has recently requested a remnant parcel be annexed and is now
      asking for the plat to be amended to combine it with his existing lot in the Rolling Oaks Subdivision so that
      a home can be built along with potential accessory structures allowing his use of the full property. While
      the existing lot meets the regulations of the Zoning and Subdivision Ordinances, the addition of this 50’
      strip of property will add to its usability. The 50’ strip had been an easement owned by the US Government,
      but has since been deeded to each individual property owner along its borders. Because of the US
      ownership, it was not within the boundaries of either Ogden City or South Ogden City when the subdivision
      was approved. The request is consistent with good planning practices, and will satisfy all regulations upon
      its annexation. Staff recommendation is for approval subject to the completion of the annexation process
      for the vacant 50’ parcel and obtaining and completing all departmental staff and private utility company
      requirements.
      MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to recommend approval based on the findings
                  there is good cause for the subdivision amendment and the subdivision plat amendment will
                  comply with City ordinances by completing the annexation of the vacant parcel and obtaining
                  and satisfying all department staff and private utility requirements. Approval is subject to
                  completion of the annexation process for the vacant parcel west of Lot #11 and obtaining and
                  satisfying all departmental and private utility requirements. Motion was seconded by
                  Commissioner Garner and passed unanimously with Commissioners Boykin, Garner, Safsten,
                  Sandau, Schade, Southwick, Stoker and Graf voting aye.

   b. Preliminary Condominium Approval, for Senior Living on Washington at 2955 Washington Boulevard.
      Mr. Montgomery reported this is a request to create two separate ownerships in the building in order to
      meet the financing requirements for the project to take advantage of low and moderate income
      opportunities. The individual units will be rentals, and common spaces within the building allow tenant use
      of storage, common area, computer room, etc. The proposal satisfies the requirements of the
      condominium section of the subdivision ordinance, with the only correction required would be to assure
      the numbering system on the condominium declaration is consistent with the numbers shown on the plat.
      Staff recommendation is for approval subject to the numbering system to be revised and meeting other
      departmental requirements.
      MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to recommend approval based on the findings
                  the condominium will comply with City ordinances, requirements of the condominium process
                  and development requirements for the project proposed. Approval is subject to the numbering
                  system being revised and all departmental Staff requirements being satisfied. Motion was
                  seconded by Commissioner Garner and passed unanimously with Commissioners Boykin,
                  Garner, Safsten, Sandau, Schade, Southwick, Stoker and Graf voting aye.

   c. Conditional Use Permit Amendment for antenna replacement at 836 36th Street.
      Ms. Corbridge stated this request is to replace the existing array with three new antenna panels as well as

      would no longer have the stealth appearance. In considering the conditional use permit, the Commission
      is to determine no new impacts are created, including its visual appearance. Staff recommendation is for
      approval subject to the antenna panels being painted to match the pole in an effort to conceal the panels.

3. CBD Site Plan, to replace exterior building surface of home at 164 27th Street.

   Mr. Anthony Carmelo stated while he is not opposed to the recommendations of Staff, he wanted to inform
   the Commission of actions which have taken place since the initial code violation letter in July which indicated
   the exterior of the home needed to be maintained and/or repaired. Upon receiving the notice, the owner hired
   a contractor to complete the work, but when the contractor found asbestos shingles were the previous siding
   material, he was unable to continue. The owner then worked with the State health department to safely
   remove the asbestos and the contractor then ordered vinyl siding material which was delivered prior to his
   application for permits. When the permit application was then made, it was found approval from the Planning
   Commission is required. Upon visiting with Staff, the owner has investigated replacing the vinyl with
   hardiplank, but the company where the vinyl was purchased does not produce hardiplank.

   Ms. Olson stated this request is to replace the finish material on an existing home within the CBD District with
   vinyl siding. The previous material was green asbestos and the original material was wood siding. Single-
   family homes were permitted in the CBD until 1972 and no review of finish material was required until after
   1984. The review of the Commission is to determine whether the proposed material is compatible with
   existing buildings in the surrounding neighborhood. She identified existing finish material in the area, stating
   while there is some vinyl, most of the residential buildings are brick or wood finish. New construction in the
   area is mostly hardiboard and stucco. Staff suggests the appearance of horizontal wood lap would be
   preferred with non-metal or non-vinyl material. The Commission is to determine whether the proposed vinyl
   siding is in character with the area. Consideration can be given to the fact there are existing homes with vinyl
   siding and this is an interior lot and not highly visible. Staff recommendation is for approval subject to the
   following conditions:
   1. Hardie plank or similar non-metal, non-vinyl materials that create a horizontal wood lap appearance on the
      front (south façade) of the dwelling, fronting 27th Street.
   2. The use of the vinyl material is limited to the back (north façade) and sides (east and west facades) of the
      dwelling.
   3. The vinyl materials are to be the same width and color as the Hardie board materials required on the front
      façade of the dwelling.
   4. All necessary reviews, permits, and inspections be completed, and staff comments be satisfied.

   Commissioner Southwick asked what had prompted the case by Code Enforcement and whether their
   conversations with the homeowner had included appropriate building material for the area. Ms. Olson
   responded Code Enforcement’s case was due to the dilapidated appearance of the home, and their letters
   typically indicate there is a violation and direct them to the permit desk. It appears the owner was unaware of
   any requirement for Planning Commission approval until permits were requested, and that the subcontractor
   had purchased the vinyl material prior to applying for a permit. Discussion continued relating to whether vinyl
   could be installed in a way so that it would have the appearance of wood or hardiboard as well as the
   chronology of events by both the City and the property owner. Commissioner Southwick stated there are vinyl
   buildings in the area and the use of vinyl is not out of character with the area. Commissioners Safsten and
   Garner expressed concern that the appearance of the CBD not be compromised, and care should be taken to
   improve the appearance of buildings in the area. Chair Graf felt there are standards in place which should not
   be compromised. Mr. Stratford commented the CBD Ordinance does not prohibit particular materials and the
   review of the Commission is only to determine whether a particular material is compatible with existing
   materials in the area. Commissioner Southwick felt the use of vinyl is not incompatible with other properties

4. CBD Site Plan for rooftop addition at 2570 Washington Boulevard.

  Mr. Joshua Lindgren stated this request is to add rooftop dining for the existing restaurant. He stated the
  structure will not be visible from Washington Boulevard and would seat 49 guests.

  Mr. Simpson stated the application is for a 4,662 square foot rooftop patio which would be set back 6’ from
  the west (front) wall of the building. Access would be from within the building and would include a cabana
  rooftop bar with 4’ cedar railing surrounding the dining area as well as string lighting. Consideration by the
  Commission is to determine whether the addition is compatible with the building and surrounding structures
  in the area. The existing building has a brick and glass exterior with some stone. The proposed addition is
  accessory to the restaurant and would appear as an accent on the top of the building. Its material of wood
  and metal are compatible with the area and the horizontal lines of the cedar walls and railing provides an
  accent to the architecture of the building. Staff recommendation is for approval subject to appropriate
  permits being obtained and all departmental staff comments being satisfied.

  Commissioner Schade stated cedar fencing is not now existing in the CBD and its height at 4’ would not be
  screened by the existing 1’ parapet on the building. Mr. Lindgren stated cedar was chosen due to its lighter
  weight than other fencing material. Commissioner Schade felt wrought iron fencing would be more
  appropriate, and if cedar is used, the height of the parapet should be increased to screen its appearance. Mr.
  Lindgren indicated the wood also blends with the décor of the interior restaurant. Commissioner Safsten
  asked if lighting should be shielded so as to protect the night sky of the downtown as well as to not attract
  undue attention to the top of the building. Mr. Simpson indicated the Commission is unable to consider the
  interior décor of the building but is limited to the compatibility of new construction with existing features of
  the CBD.

  Mr. Dan Schmelling stated he is not opposed to providing shielding for the lighting, and the intent of the
  lighting is to direct patrons to the patio area.

   MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Boykin to recommend approval of the rooftop addition
           based on the findings the colors and materials are compatible with the site and surrounding
           buildings, the architectural lines of the new construction are compatible with te site and
           surrounding buildings and the site does provide adequate screening. Approval is subject to the
           patio lighting being shielded from the sky and all departmental requirements being satisfied.
           Motion was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed 8-1 with Commissioners Blaisdell,
           Boykin, Garner, Safsten, Sandau, Southwick, Stoker and Graf voting aye and Commissioner Schade
           voting no. Commissioner Schade explained his vote stating he felt the use is a good idea but felt
           the choice of materials is not compatible with the downtown and should be either revised or
           screened by a larger parapet.

5. CBD Site Plan, for 2301 Kiesel Avenue for wall and gate between Sonora Grill and Coopers.

   Mr. Cameron Cook stated this request is to screen the existing area between two restaurant uses as it is
   seldom used by pedestrians and is an area where grease traps and storage is located.

   Ms. Corbridge stated the applicant is requesting to block the existing storage area from pedestrian traffic. She
   stated the area is a narrow space between two existing restaurants. The request is for a 6’ tall fence to match
   the existing gate, with the proposed wall to match brick of the surrounding buildings The area would then
   become a storage area rather than a walkway. The applicant feels the walkway as it now exists is narrow and
   uninviting to pedestrians and screening of the storage area and grease traps is a more appropriate use of the
   area. Staff recommendation is for approval subject to all City Staff departmental requirements being satisfied,
   stating there would be little effect on pedestrian traffic and would screen the utility area for the restaurant.

   Commissioner Blaisdell stated this area had been created as a pedestrian access area on purpose in order to
   provide options so pedestrians could walk between Kiesel Avenue and Washington Boulevard. Chair Graf felt
   options should be available for the future use for the Cooper’s restaurant space, indicating the grease trail
   should be cleaned rather than hidden. Commissioner Southwick asked if there is a reason to eliminate the
   walkway. Commissioner Schade noted there is excessive storage in this area.

   Mr. Cameron Cook stated the property is owned by the City Redevelopment Agency and the desire of the
   tenants is to provide storage and help with cleaning of the area while limiting the use of pedestrians. Chair
   Graf stated the Design Guidelines for the Junction project indicate walkability is an important feature. Mr.
   Cook responded the walkway is not being used as much as was anticipated when the project was designed.
   Commissioner Sandau asked if there is a concern with safety with the walkway being eliminated. Ms.
   Corbridge stated both Fire and Building Services have not yet provided their recommendations which will be
   applicable, and may or may not address safety. Commissioner Boykin stated while safety may be a concern, it
   is not a reason for action by the Planning Commission.

   MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to deny the installation of the wall and gate as
           proposed based on the findings they are not compatible with the surrounding development and it is
           important to retain the pedestrian walkway. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Safsten and
           passed 7-2 with Commissioners Blaisdell, Safsten, Sandau, Schade, Southwick, Stoker and Graf
           voting aye and Commissioners Boykin and Garner voting no. Commissioner Garner stated he felt it
           would be an improvement to enclose the area, as it would provide better security. Commissioner
           Boykin stated he often walked in this area and was not aware this was a pedestrian walkway due to
           its narrowness, and felt it would better serve the area as storage.

8. Request to Expand Nonconforming Use, for shed at 2102 Monroe Boulevard.

   Mr. Robert Tassone stated he is requesting approval of a storage shed, stating this home was built in1886
   and is on a corner lot, and there is little room for storage. He stated the existing shed and lean-to were built
   by the tenant and was less than 200 square feet so it had been understood no building permits were required.
   While the original construction was of two sheds, at some point they were connected to provide a single
   storage space.

   Ms. Olson stated this is a nonconforming duplex in the R-1-5 zone and the original nonconforming use
   certificate and site plan did not identify a shed being on the site at the time it was issued. Permits were not
   obtained for the accessory buildings and this request is to allow the expansion of the existing nonconforming
   use to allow the addition of the shed. She stated the Commission is to consider whether the expansion is
   compatible with its surroundings and whether any impact to surrounding properties is created by the
   expansion. Consideration also must be given to the safety of the building in relation to other properties.
   Because of its proximity to the side yard property line, a fire-rated wall is required. The eaves are constructed
   so that storm water drains to the neighboring property which is not allowed. The construction material also is
   not allowed within the East Central Community, and the shed is visible from the street. Staff recommendation
   is for denial of the expansion, which would cause the existing shed to be removed or revised to meet the site
   development standards.

   Commissioner Southwick asked if the size of the shed could be revised so that it could be compatible, and if
   there is any designated off-street parking for the property. Mr. Tassone responded the shed could not be built
   in the rear yard due to the limited space available on the corner lot. Commissioner Sandau stated there are
   concerns with its draining onto the neighboring property as well as the materials. Discussion continued as to
   whether the overhanging eaves might be removed, the building materials upgraded and a fire wall installed in
   order to make the shed compatible.

   MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to deny the expansion of the nonconforming use
           based in its impacts to surrounding properties and it is not compatible with the surrounding area.
           Motion was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously, with Commissioners
