1. Discussion on Fiber Optic Cable Options for Millcreek; Jeff Christensen, EntryPoint
Networks
Mayor Silvestrini said Jeff Christensen contacted him before the City was incorporated. He
and Christensen have met 3 times to discuss having a municipal broadband fiber network.
The Mayor has also received over 100 emails from Millcreek residents asking for fiber. The
Council will be exploring the idea and no decisions have been made.

Jeff Christensen said the point of the presentation is to introduce some concepts. He said the
world thinks about infrastructure and services as tightly coupled but Christensen said they
are divided. He said the question for the City is, “Is this infrastructure essential?” The goal
is not to disrupt Comcast or CenturyLink, but to disrupt their model. He quoted Kevin
Kelly, “Communication is not just a sector of the economy. Communication is the
economy.” He said he frames municipal broadband under the umbrella of a smart city.
Robust internet access for all residents is an important element in a smart city, but so is
having the infrastructure to do whatever it is the City needs to do, preferably without having
to ask for permission.

Christensen said fundamentally the current model is broken because the networks were
designed to serve their interests. He said these networks are too fundamental now to just
make it about a return on investment. People and businesses all pay for the networks but do
not have control over what they offer. Any city should care about how the network enables
health care, education, and public safety. The fiber optics should be the utility and should be
open, so anyone can access it. He said rule #1 is whoever controls the infrastructure has
control. Christensen argued that the control should be for the common good. The unique
value proposition of municipal networks is to create local control and ownership of
infrastructure. The private sector should provide services running over that infrastructure.
He argued for an open virtualized broadband network and said 5G wireless is telecoms’
answer to cable. Wireless works best the faster it gets back to fiber. He illustrated how to
change the ISP as a subscriber with EntryPoint. His last point was on how to finance the
network. The funding should come from the property owners who voluntarily sign up which
makes subscribers owners and not renters. There would be three categories from subscribers
for a total monthly cost; infrastructure expense, monthly maintenance and operations, and
ISP Internet Services. He said once the subscriber has paid for the infrastructure, that part of
the equation goes away. As a case example, he illustrated EntryPoint’s model that was
adopted by Ammon, Idaho. The model reinforces the complete separation of infrastructure
and services, lit fiber network infrastructure is organized as a utility, services are not part of
the utility, and it is a much lower risk for the municipality.

Mayor Silvestrini asked why the connection speed in Ammon is limited to 100 Mbps.
Christensen said the ISP is the way to access the internet and whatever the City picks as
their package will control their access to the internet. The pipe that gets installed is a
gigabyte. Council Member Marchant asked what the infrastructure would look like.
Christensen said there would be an analysis of buried lines or attachments on poles. Council
Member Catten asked about subsidized connections for low income families. Christensen
said there are local and state programs for subsidized services that the City could participate
in. Council Member Catten asked about safety with subscribers being about to change the
ISP so quickly. He said people will leave a digital footprint. Mayor Silvestrini asked how
many people would have to subscribe to make this viable. Christensen said he would work

2. Unified Police Department Update by Mayor Silvestrini
Mayor Silvestrini said the Board of the Unified Police Department (UPD) has done some

There are currently 47 officers working in Millcreek and they are looking to hire up to 58.

4. First Reading Calendar
a. Town Center Overlay Zone

Mayor Silvestrini said the Planning Commission approved the Town Center Overlay
Zone. Francis Lilly said staff has drafted an RFP and will be sending it to a consultant
pool in a week to gather a team to help put together a master plan for the Millcreek
City Center. Mayor Silvestrini said the plan is being paid for by a grant from Wasatch
Front Regional Council. He said the City has done work on establishing a community
reinvestment area and commissioned 3 different groups to do various studies which is
prerequisite work. Staff is still working on negotiating a series of boundary line
adjustments with Salt Lake City. Staff has also developed maps and have held various
stakeholder meetings. Mayor Silvestrini said 2 projects have been approved and the
developers are waiting to see what the City does so they can be consistent with the city
center. Lilly said staff will soon have a small area meeting with residents who live in
the Mountair Subdivision.

The overlay zone sets minimum standards for building design, the creation of public
space, and appropriate uses. It also limits height and imposes height transitions that are
aggressive which are intended to protect surrounding residential uses and give staff
time to develop a plan that responds to residents’ wishes for a town center. He said the
current zoning already allows significant density and height. He said the ordinance is
simple; the overlay zone acts as supplemental standards to the base zone. The City’s
interest is to concentrate uses in the right place and exposure to create the city center.
Lilly went over the permitted and conditional uses and the design standards. Lilly said
the plaza language came from the South Salt Lake City street car corridor. Mayor
Silvestrini encouraged the Council to walk along the street car corridor in South Salt
Lake. He said there is a great deal of urban renewal there.

Mayor Silvestrini cautioned staff about getting into the nonconforming use discussion
like what was had with the manufacturing zone. Lilly read from the zone text
19.62.070(3) General Standards of Applicability, “Any new development, exterior
remodeling, and building expansions exceeding 15% of the gross square footage of the
existing building, shall conform to the Town Center Overlay Zone. Conformity shall
not be required for maintenance or interior remodeling of a building. New signs or
sign replacements shall also comply with the TCOZ.” Lilly said there needs to be a
comfortable nonconforming use policy that makes sense to the existing property
owners and a provision that promotes adaptive reuse of existing buildings.

Lilly said the next move on town center public engagement is for the City to have a
large meeting when the consultants are hired and a small neighborhood outreach to the
Mountair Subdivision in the coming weeks.
