5. Conditional Use Permit, to allow truck driving course at 3651 Wall Avenue.
   Mr. Simpson stated Mountain West Driving School is requesting to use existing parking space in the
   back of Newgate Mall to train drivers to back trailers. Their proposal is to use 2.75 acres which would
   eliminate up to 336 parking spaces. While the use of space inside the mall for an office is permitted,
   the driving school use is a conditional use which requires Commission approval. He stated the driving
   school would operate between the hours of 8am until 6pm which is also typical hours of business for
   the mall. He reviewed the conditional use standards and stated the use for semi-truck driving appears
   to be more industrial in nature than the commercial uses of the mall and surrounding uses. He stated

                                               Page 2 of 8
   the existing parking lot was designed for smaller vehicles and the use as truck driving may interfere
   with parking and circulation patterns for users of the mall. The City also is trying to encourage
   reinvestment into the mall and attract new anchor stores, and if parking is eliminated, stores may not
   be able to lease space within the mall. He stated when the theaters were developed during the 90’s, a
   parking analysis was completed, finding there is no excess parking for the mall based on the floor area,
   even when allowing for overlapping hours of use. Conditional use standards include consideration of
   both compatibility with surrounding land uses and compliance with existing zoning regulations.
   Because the proposed use would eliminate needed parking space for the use of the mall and violate
   the zoning code, Staff recommends the permit be denied.

   Commissioner Safsten asked if the mall management had been contacted. He also asked if the
   proposed use would require improvements or signage to the existing lot so users of the mall would be
   aware of the use. Mr. Simpson responded he has had no contact with the mall management, and is
   not aware of any required improvements if the use were to occur. Staff feels the use cannot be
   considered due to the parking needs of the mall as well as compliance with the zoning code.

   MOTION:     A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to deny the conditional use permit based
               on the findings it would create detrimental impacts that cannot be mitigated and is not
               compatible with the main use of the retail mall itself. Motion was seconded by
               Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously with Commissioners Blaisdell, Garner,
               Safsten, Schade, Southwick and Graf voting aye.

6. Amended CBD Site Plan/Encroachment Permit, for mixed use building at 2501 Adams Avenue.

   Mr. Ray Nettleton stated the owner is anxious for this project to be approved and is willing to satisfy
   concerns of both the Staff and the neighbors. He stated since it was last reviewed by the Commission,
   minor changes haven been made, with a previous recreational deck being changed to penthouse
   apartments, adding four units to the project. The original intent was a public walkway around the
   building to showcase the art on the building, but due to concerns of potential use by vagrants, this has
   now been changed to a gated, private walkway. This application also includes encroachment of
   balconies over the right-of-way, but the stained glass art has been relocated to be within the property.

   Commissioner Southwick asked if the units are to be owner or rentals, and asked about the fencing
   plan for the walkway area. Mr. Nettleton responded the units are to be rentals, and there will be a hard
   wall on the southwest corner with a wrought iron fence extending to the south.

   Mr. Montgomery stated the Commission had granted preliminary approval for this project in October,
   but since that time the previous patio area has become residential units. He stated the applicant has
   also made some revisions in response to his recommendations. A studio apartment has been
   removed and replaced with a laundry facility. Parking is reduced to 28 spaces to allow for mechanical
   equipment and elevator space. Four electric charging stations are provided for residents.

   The architectural lines of the building have been changed, with windows now being dominated by
   balconies. Building materials are the same and a more defined cap to the building is provided. The
   front door to the office and retail space has been relocated to the corner with the entrance on Adams
   Avenue. Some changes have been made to the landscaping as a larger transformer for electrical
   service is required. He stated there is concern of neighbors about the creation of blind areas which
   attract illegal activities, so the space is now proposed to be secured with a metal fence and an art wall
   and will be private space for the tenants with 10’ between the building and the adjacent home. The
   dumpster will be located within the building and rolled out to be serviced. The applicant is requesting a
   reduction of parking requirements, indicating the previous action allowed a reduction from 46 to 30

spaces which provides one stall per unit but is now requesting that be reduced to 26 spaces, providing
.8 spaces per unit. Guest parking would be available along the street, but there is no additional parking
available for the use. The applicant feels their clientele may not have cars and due to the number of
studio apartments, fewer parking is warranted. In order to grant a parking reduction, the Commission
is to consider whether the reduction creates a shortage of parking in the general area. While there are
existing parking lots in the area, they are privately owned and not available for use and resident parking
would spill into 25th Street. Because of the location of the BRT stop, many spaces along 25 th Street
will be limited.

Staff recommends approval of the encroachment permit for the balconies and art work as it is typical
that utilities extend into the right-of-way, stating other conditions of the October have been satisfied. If
the Commission finds reducing parking makes the area more walkable due to its proximity to
downtown and bus service, and does not create a shortage for other uses in the area, a parking
reduction can be granted. Staff recommendation is for approval, subject to the following conditions:
1. Remove decorative wall on south side and replace with fence;
2. Remove walkway along south and west sides of building and replace with landscaping with no
    public use;
3. New building elevations be approved;
4. Reduction of parking to .9 stalls per unit (27 total stalls)
5. Departmental requirements and other conditions of the previous approval being satisfied; and
6. Approval of 4’ encroachment permit for balcony and art glass work with a minimum of 9’ clearance
     from the sidewalk.

7. Public Hearing, Petition to rezone from NC-2 to MU, property between 24th Street and 25th Street and
   Monroe to Quincy.

   Mr. Brandon Cooper started the Redevelopment Agency has been assembling properties within this
   block with the desire to create a neighborhood commercial in accordance with the East Central
   Community Plan. He stated while the property has been commercial use for decades, the desire is to
   create a new mixed use project including both residential and commercial uses. He felt the standards
   of the MU zone are more appropriate and compatible with the community plan as it would allow
   maximum flexibility and introduce additional households which would support neighborhood
   commercial uses. The plan is to add townhomes, stacked flat apartments, condominiums and
   mansion homes, feeling a variety of housing types with different prices would create both market rate
   and affordable housing options for the community. Discussion continued relating to affordable
   housing and attracting a variety of tenants.

   Mr. Brierley stated this is a request to rezone the entire block to MU which also entails completion of a
   development agreement and concept development plan. The determination of the Commission when
   considering a zoning change is whether it is consistent with the General Plan as well as the East
   Central Community Plan and will be a benefit to the overall community. He reviewed the goals of the
   East Central Plan, stating it identified this block as a neighborhood center, stating the concept
   proposed with traditional architecture, walkability and landscaping appear to be in line with the intent
   of the plan. Since the East Central Plan was adopted, the commercial buildings have been removed, a
   redevelopment district has been developed, and additional properties within the block have been
   acquired by the Redevelopment Agency. The bus rapid transit route also has been identified to have a
   stop at this location which creates the need for transit-oriented uses. The proposed concept master
   plan creates pedestrian circulation within the block with an east-west corridor which could be
   considered an extension of Capitol Street. With a plaza as a common recreational area within the


                                                Page 5 of 8
block. The block also is to be bisected by a north-south walkway to connect interior uses with the BRT
route. Two development options are proposed, with the first to include a grocery store. While both
plans show a variety of housing and commercial types, the first option includes a grocery store. The
second option eliminates the grocery store in favor of mansion townhomes, but does include ground
floor commercial uses in many of the buildings, which might include grocery, daycare or other service
uses. Staff suggests the parking lot entrances be designed so as to line up with existing intersections,
stating one parking space is required per residential unit as well as one space per 300 square feet of
commercial space. He stated while the grocery store could be larger, other commercial uses should be
limited to 10,000 square feet. While outdoor dining is allowed, it is to be limited to food service
businesses, and drive-up windows for restaurants are not appropriate. The development agreement
identifies uses allowed, setbacks, parking standards and live/work standards. As parts of the project
are developed, the Commission would review each proposal to determine whether it is consistent with
the development agreement. Staff recommendation is for approval of the requested zone change from
NC-2 to MU, approval of the concept development plans as well as modifications to the revised
descriptions, to adopt the MU Development Standards for Capitol Square with requested amendments
and to specify particular items identified for future development agreements.

Commissioner Safsten asked about ongoing care of common green space and plaza area. Mr. Brierley
responded the intent is that these areas would be privately owned. Mr. Stratford added the City may
request a public easement be recorded to allow public access. Commissioner Schade noted the East
Central Plan indicates a priority for a neighborhood commercial use at this location, and it appears the
primary use is multi-family residential, with limited retail or commercial space. Mr. Brierley responded
the list of uses identified are those that would serve the surrounding community, and while the climate
for grocery store is currently a challenge in this area, both options include commercial space.
Commissioner Schade expressed concern with the ratio of commercial space to residential,
suggesting it may be better to rezone it to C-3 where there are development standards for residential
uses but also may better attract larger commercial uses. Mr. Brierley stated the City’s intent for the
MU zoning is to regulate the details of potential development, indicating some C-3 uses are not
appropriate for this area due to the surrounding residential neighborhood.

Commissioner Garner expressed support of a grocery store, and while the plan shows good pedestrian
connections, the retail use also is important. He also expressed concern about the mansion concepts
proposed as these appear much like many existing residential buildings in the area, but the City has put
much effort into reducing units in these buildings, and to create more seems counterproductive.
Commissioner Southwick agreed a grocery store is desirable, and suggested a Trader Joe’s would do
well in this location. He also felt a public easement throughout the project is desirable to assure the
plaza and green space is not eliminated. Commissioner Safsten indicated there is some concern with
public area being used by homeless people, and indicated the proposed high density residential uses
may be enough to persuade a retail grocery store to locate in this area. Chair Graf felt a quality
development will radiate into the surrounding neighborhood and be an impetus for redevelopment and
revitalization so long as it is done correctly. He asked who was responsible for the proposed plans
being presented. Mr. Brierley responded the Redevelopment Agency has been working with potential
buyers and developers and with ideas from several sources, developed the two options proposed. Mr.
Cooper responded there has been much study concerning potential uses on this block for the past
several years, stating the City had worked with Neighborhood Walmart in 2017, and their corporate
offices denied the location due to the demographics of the area, indicating there is a need for more
residential uses to support such a facility. The variety of housing types is proposed to attract a variety
of tenants and owners based on current market conditions. Discussion continued relating to uses and
amenities to support residential development, indicating each phase of the development will be

   Mr. Brierley addressed questions, stating the zoning on the east side of Quincy is primarily PI which

Review of Meeting
Commission expressed appreciation of residents relating to the Capital block rezone and felt their
concerns and questions were valid. Mr. Stratford indicated the Planning Commission is often a better
venue to address these concerns and felt the Commission and Staff addressed their concerns.

Mr. Stratford indicated rezoning to MU can be confusing as if it is a Redevelopment area, less details are
need as the City Council is involved in the design and review process and less details are required at the
time of rezoning. A private request for MU zoning requires complete detail before the rezoning is
approved as if changes are made, the Council loses its legislative ability.

Mr. Montgomery announced the discussion for the work session will be incorporating standards of the
Make Ogden Plan into Ordinance language as well as a discussion on waterwise landscaping.
