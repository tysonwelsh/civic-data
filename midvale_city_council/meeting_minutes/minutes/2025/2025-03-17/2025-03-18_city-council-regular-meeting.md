       C.     Unified Police Department Report
Chief April Morse reviewed the statistics report for February which included 2018 calls
for service resulting in 650 cases generated and 25 booking arrests. The Drug
Enforcement Unit was working non-stop, they prepared and obtained 5 different search
warrants in one month, which is above average. Everything they do is evaluated on a
threat matrix and due to these search warrants being high on the threat matrix they
used the UPD Swat Team to serve 4 of the 5 warrants. Out of the warrants served the
unit recovered 17 firearms, 4 pounds of Fentanyl, 12 ounces of Meth, 164 grams of
Heroin, 27 grams of Cocaine, and 15 grams of Crack Cocaine: all in one month’s work.
They also responded to a bomb threat and a structure fire.

       D.     Arts Council Annual Update
Wade Walker, Chairman and President of Arts Council, introduced other board
members that were in attendance: Stephanie Johnson, Lauren Richards, Bryant Brown,
and Nate Rockwood. He expressed his appreciation to Midvale City for their support of
the arts. The Midvale Arts Council was created in 1985 and became a 501-C3 non-profit
in 2012, which was officially recognized as Midvale’s official arts organization.

Mr. Walker presented some of the 2024 highlights of the Midvale Arts Council Concerts
and events. He said they held nine concerts with 124 artists, and over 2800 people
attending the free events. They always presented three theater productions during the
year. In addition, this past year they introduced a new event, free movie month, which
they had over 120 people attend. They also put on Mary Poppins Jr. and a Sondheim
Tribute Revue that included 50 performers with over 750 attendees.

Mr. Walker introduced the Hall of Honors recipient, Hal Hale. Hal was Hillcrest High
Schools first Student Body President and played professional basketball. Hal still lives in
his childhood home in Midvale.

The Arts Council also hosts a Visual and Literary Art Contest, in 2024 the Visual Art
Contest had 25 local artists participate, with 68 entries, and over 150 attendees. The
Literary Art Show is in its 4th year with 17 participants and growing interest every year.

The Arts Council also participates in the Main Street Business Alliance, one of the
highlights early this year was getting new carpet. He thanked Midvale City and the

       C. CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 2025-R-11 ADOPTING A STREET TREE
           SELECTION GUIDE FOR MIDVALE CITY.
Adam Olsen said throughout Title 17 “Zoning”, reference is often made within various
landscape requirements for street trees to be selected from the City’s Street Tree
Selection Guide. However, no such guide exists. Staff commissioned MHTN Architects
to assist with the creation of a guide for reference when homeowners, builders, and
developers provide street trees.

The selection guide provides general tree guidelines for best design practices, tree
establishment and care, as well as a recommended species list. The recommended
species list provides lists of trees recommended for planting within small park strips (3-
5’), typical park strips (5-8’) and large park strips (8’+). Pictures and information such as
mature height and width are provided for each tree identified in the lists.

The adoption of the guide will provide reference when called out in Title 17 and be a
resource for homeowners and developers for street tree selection.

Cody Hill said on June 7, 2025, the Redevelopment Agency of Midvale City is
organizing the fourth annual Los Muros on Main Midvale City Mural Festival, aiming to
install ten new murals within the Main Street CDA. The Redevelopment Agency of

recommend Eddie Chaffer be the artist to paint the mural. His work illustrates an
incredibly high level of talent, an intricate and unique color theory, and subject matter
appropriate for a mural on public property.

The Agency now asks the city council to consider permitting a mural to occupy that
space, painted by Eddie Chaffer, the recommended artist.

FISCAL IMPACT: The cost of the mural, not to exceed $7,000, will come from the
Agency’s Main Street CDA public art fund.

VIII.   DISCUSSION ITEM
        A. DISCUSSION REGARDING MIDVALE’S BIANNUAL BULKY CURBSIDE
           WASTE PROGRAM.
Glen Kennedy said the City offers curbside bulky waste collection twice a year through
its contracted waste hauler, with one service in April and the other traditionally in late
October. Last year, the October service was moved to late November to better align with
the community’s leaf removal needs and the City’s leaf bag program, which is managed
by the Engineering Division as part of the stormwater management plan. However, City
staff received significant negative feedback from residents via social media and direct
calls regarding the November dates.
