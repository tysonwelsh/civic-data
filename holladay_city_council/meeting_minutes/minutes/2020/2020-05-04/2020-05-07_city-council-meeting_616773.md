IV.    Public Hearing on Proposed Rezone for Property Located at 2417 East Butternut Road
       from R-1-10 to R-1-8 for Approximately .66 Acres.
Jonathan Teerlink, City Planner presented the staff report and reported that staff was
recommending a rezone of two properties from R-1-10 to R-1-8. The applicant was looking to
demolish two existing homes, reorganize the lot lines, dedicate space, and add a new home. The
R-1-8 zone would allow this to occur. An outline was provided of the proposed home locations.
The Planning Commission unanimously recommended approval.

Council Member Gibbons asked for a summary of the public comments made at the Planning
Commission Meeting. Mr. Teerlink reported that there were two comments from neighboring
property owners – one opposing and one in support. The Planning Commission felt the project
had direct compatibility with the surrounding homes. Council Member Durham raised concerns
with the right-of-way. Council Member Petersen stated that the project was previously denied
by the Planning Commission and the City Council. Mr. Teerlink reported that this is a new
applicant and the proposal is somewhat different.

Nick Mingo – applicant 2815 E 3900 S and stated that there are two homes. One is functioning
as a duplex with three families living in it. All three homes will face west. The previous
application was for a very different configuration. He believed the prior sentiment opposing the
rezone focused on the placement of the homes, not the fact three homes are being proposed.

VI.     Presentation on 2020-21 Tentative Budgets – Manager Chamness.
Gina Chamness, City Manager presented the Tentative Budget through mid to late June 2021.
She explained that the budget reflects prioritization in contractual obligations for police, public
works, and fire services with other priorities as allowed. Additionally, holiday presence has been
a priority so the budget sets aside $15,000 for that to occur. The budget also provides $100,000
for a skate park to be developed. In keeping with the Council’s desire to develop a Financial
Sustainability Plan in the form of Holladay @20, a plan was to be provided to the Council but
had not been incorporated into the current Tentative Budget.

Ms. Chamness reported that this is the first year sales taxes have been a major source of the
budget, however, next year sales and property taxes will be more in line with each other and
there will be growth in the use of fund balance.

Concerning the expense budget, it was reported that currently about 69% of the General Fund
budget is contracted services. In 2021, this was expected to increase to 73%. Salaries and wages
were expected to decrease slightly. Sne anticipated a 10% decrease in sales and gas tax revenue
and assumed that building permits will remain flat due to uncertainty in market conditions.
Given the current conditions, she also reduced the City Hall and Parks Rental Income and
Revenue from the City traffic school. She kept revenue from the Justice Court as flat.

Sales tax projections are based on a formula of what is sold in Holladay and with 50% based on a
population formula for distribution. The City of Holladay generally benefits with about a 25 to
45 percent sales tax. She was implementing a 10% decrease but it was difficult to know where it
will end up. The City of Holladay will continue to acquire additional data to provide better
information. The City’s sales tax comes from a growing portion of on-line sales of 10 to 20%.
Groceries have also been a stable source of sales tax revenue.

Council Member Fotheringham asked why a 10% decrease was chosen as opposed to 15%.
Ms. Chamness reported that based on current information that is the best prediction but it may
change with additional data.

On the expense side, the City’s largest category is the Unified Police Department (“UPD”) at
approximately $276,000. Mayor Dahle state that there will be 2.76% market and 2% merit
increases. The UFA increase will be approximately $1,500. The City’s Public Works contract
will increase by approximately $87,000 with the excess coming from the General Fund.
Negotiations with other cities resulted in the removal of a slurry contract that will now be paid
for independently by cities. Finally, she reduced the transfer to the Capital Projects Fund by
about $400,000.

Ms. Chamness noted that new ongoing expenses have not been captured, which may include the
cleaning of park restrooms on an hourly basis as well as the Justice Court cleaning process

Council Member Fotheringham asked about recommended cushions for reserves. Ms. Chamness
responded that there is a minimum of 5% and a maximum of 25%. Based on current forecasts
she expected to be around 18%.

The Arts Fund included a recommendation for two art shows. The budget includes cancellation
of the Blue Moon Festival in August but assumes the summer concerts will continue in some
form. .

The Capital Projects Fund includes a match of $50,000 for the Arts Fund. She recommended
setting aside $250,000 for maintenance which is low but reflects current conditions. Council
Member Fotheringham commented on the cushion and that the City will have to be careful with
discretionary spending for the time being.

Mayor Dahle reported that there would not need to be much discussion tonight but simply a
review of the Tentative Budget. A public hearing was set for June 4. He commented this is a
minimal budget and there are few discretionary decisions to be made. The Council can discuss
the budget with Ms. Chamness between now and when the vote takes place in June. He
reminded the Council Members to take a two-year look at the budget and consider that there will

IX.     Consideration of Resolution 2020-19 Adopting the 2019 Salt Lake County Hazard
        Mitigation Plan.
Julie Harvey, Municipal Emergency Management Planner introduced the resolution and reported
that the purpose is to adopt the Salt Lake County Hazard Mitigation Plan to promote sound
public policy to protect and reduce the vulnerability of citizens, facilities, infrastructure, and
private property within the natural environment of Salt Lake County and the City of Holladay.
The resolution reflects the work of multiple jurisdictions and citizens and has been reviewed by
the State and the Federal Emergency Management Agency (“FEMA”). She explained that there
are no identified expenditures but there may be items for Council consideration in the future as
projects arise.

Ms. Chamness reported that Hazard Mitigation Plans are an essential step in guaranteeing the
City’s eligibility for FEMA funds in the event of a disaster.

Council Member Fotheringham moved to approve Resolution 2020-19 Adopting the 2019 Salt
Lake County Hazard Mitigation Plan. Council Member Petersen seconded the motion. The
Council roll call vote was as follows: Councilmembers Gibbons, Fotheringham, Quinn, Durham,
Petersen and Mayor Dahle in favor. Resolution 2020-19 was approved by a unanimous vote.

XII. City Manager Report – Manager Chamness.
Ms. Chamness provided an update on COVID-19 related items. To address the needs of City
Hall, the public was being encouraged to access City services online. There have also been
physical changes for safety reasons within City facilities. Employees were encouraged to
telecommute in addition to coming into the office. Staff was planning for the next stage of
reopening that would include additional physical changes to the City facilities.

A question was raised about unemployment impacts on the City of Holladay. Ms. Chamness did
not have that data but would provide information at the next meeting.

On a positive note, Ms. Chamness informed the Council that the melt in the creek is manageable
this year because of a slow thaw and the City should only have minimum exposure to possible
flooding.

The 3900 South Project continued to move forward. The City was aggressively acquiring
temporary construction easements. The Highland Drive Project was also expected to go out to
bid in the next four weeks.

Ms. Chamness informed the Council that the Gunderson Safe Sidewalks project, on 2000 East
and Gunderson Lane has received additional funding from the Utah Department of
Transportation (“UDOT”) and, therefore, the City will be able to award a contract and start the
work.
