1.      Promise          Program          Annual           Report;           Ashley       Cleveland,              Promise            Program             Manager
Ashley      Cleveland               thanked       the Council              for creating          the Millcreek               Promise          Program         and its
mission       statement.             She said the program                    was announced                 on January               10, 2018       and she started
working       in it in May              of that year.          She said Millcreek                  Promise           will     work      to change           discriminating
and inequitable               community               conditions           to address         barriers     to education,               health      and safety,               and
economic          wellbeing.            The      initiative        will     be a catalyst          for youth          success,         creating          opportunities,
and mitigating               issues     facing        many        of the City's          young         people        and their         families.         There         is list of
mutual      community                 goals,     which        are education,             safety,       health,       and economic                wellbeing,            and 4
subcommittees,                2 support          committees,               and many           community           partners           working         towards           those
goals.     She reviewed                the committees               and their          2019      attendance           statistics,        what      they      were         doing,
and what          they      planned       to do. She went                  through       the process          for developing                  action      plans;        identify
major      drivers,         narrow       and prioritize             drivers,       identify        interventions              based      on drivers,            and narrow
and prioritize             interventions.             She reviewed             the Promise              Program          funding         since     inception            which
included       a number              of grants,        donations,           and fundraising.               Cleveland            described          the Phase            II
improvements                 for Sunnyvale              Park      and the pandemic                 influenced            changes         to the Saturday                  Farmers
Market.       She said she had increased                           the Promise            Program          funding           by 400%           in the last year               and a
half.


Cleveland          highlighted            program          achievements                over     the last year in each of the community                                       goal
areas.     In education,              the Program             received         a grant        to start after         school         programming                 at Mill       Creek
Elementary,            launched          a volunteer           reading         with      kids    program,           and installed              ten little       libraries
throughout          the community.                    In safety,      the Program               received         a grant       for improvements                   to
Sunnyvale           Park,      and the subcommittee                        was focused            on domestic               violence          and mental           health
resources         for minoritized               residents.         In health,          there     was a suicide               prevention          task force            working
with     the schools,           gardening             boxes      had been          set up in Sunnyvale                      Park,    and there           were     food        drives
in the schools.              In economic              wellbeing,           there    had been work                done        on digital        inclusion          with
subsidized          and free internet                 programs.           The long-term             growth         for the Program                 included            work        on
Millcreek         City      Council       Meeting         Minutes                                                27 July       2020        Page 3 of 11

     a.      ZM-20-006,             Request         to Rezone         from     R-l-10         & R-1-21          to R-1-21       at 3646 Viewcrest
     Circle;       Erin      O'Kelley,          Planner
     Erin      O'Kelley        said the applicant             was proposing                to rezone property          located       at 3646 Viewcrest
     Circle      originally         platted     as two separate residential                    lots in the Mount            Olympus          Hills
     subdivision           plats. The property              was currently            zoned      R-1-10.        The vacant       area behind            the
     house was zoned R-1-21.                        The applicant        would         like to put a guesthouse                on the property              which
     was permitted             in the R-1-21            zone. By definition,                a guesthouse         must be located             on the same
     lot of the main              dwelling.        Therefore,      a lot consolidation                was required.         However,          Millcreek
     code requires            that the consolidation               of two previously                 subdivided      lots must be re-platted                  to
     reflect     the property          boundary          changes.       The proposed             plat and consolidation               would          result in a
     split zone lot where               one zone allows               guesthouses           and one zone does not. The City                          required
     the applicant          to rezone          the property        in addition         to the subdivision            application.           The rezone
     would       be in harmony              with     the General        Plan. The neighborhood                     had concerns            about
     construction,          utility     maintenance,            use of the guesthouse                 as a short-term          rental,      and
     obstruction           of view      and privacy           but most were in support                   of the rezone         and proposal.            The Mt.
     Olympus           Community              Council     and Planning              Commission           recommended             approval         of the
     rezone      with      the zoning          conditions       that short-term             rentals      would     be prohibited,           and animals
     would       be limited         to household          pets.


     Mayor       Silvestrini          said the Council            did not entertain            public     comment           on first reading           items
     but would          when       it came back to the Council                      for action.       Mayor      Silvestrini        asked for public
     comment.          There       was none.
