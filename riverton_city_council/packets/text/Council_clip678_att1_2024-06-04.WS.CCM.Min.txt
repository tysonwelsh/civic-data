37          Budget Discussion
38
39   Councilmember Pierucci raised a question regarding the budget for the Justice Court,
40   specifically noting an increase in the cost for bailiffs, which doubled from $17,000 to $34,000.
41   He sought clarification on the need for two bailiffs and whether the additional bailiff is a part-time
42   position, given that it is not reflected in the total number of City employees. In response, Ryan
43   Carter, City Attorney, said the second bailiff was required by the Administrative Office of the
44   Courts (AOC). The AOC rules require a bailiff to operate within the courtroom, adhering to strict
45   protocols, and also to manage the magnetometer outside the courtroom entrance. Since these
46   responsibilities cannot be managed by one person simultaneously, it necessitates having two
47   security personnel: one inside the courtroom as a bailiff and another outside operating the
48   magnetometer and managing entrance security.
49
50   Councilmember Pierucci asked if the Riverton Police Department could fulfill this requirement.
51   Mr. Hicks said that the police department could indeed fulfill this role with additional training and
     Page | 2 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     June 4, 2024




 1   support. However, he noted that this would likely need to be managed as an overtime
 2   opportunity for existing officers, unless the police department were expanded or other services
 3   were cut. Financially, it makes more sense to continue the contract with the constable due to the
 4   additional costs associated with state retirement system benefits and other related expenses for
 5   police officers.
 6
 7   Councilmember Buroker highlighted that a significant majority of residents in her area (70%)
 8   expressed a preference to keep recycling, with 30% preferring not to continue it. She stressed
 9   the importance of respecting this feedback from the residents. However, the goal remains to
10   ensure that the sanitation fund operates independently as an enterprise fund without subsidies
11   from the general fund.
12
13   For clarification, Mr. Hicks noted that making the sanitation fund fully self-sustaining while
14   maintaining recycling services would require an increase in household fees. Staff estimates
15   indicated that this would involve a $3 per household increase for the first garbage and recycling
16   totes, and an additional $3 for those with extra recycling totes. This pricing structure was
17   projected to cover costs for one year, with a potential 50-cent increase in subsequent years
18   under the current contract with Waste Management.
19
20   Mr. Hicks advised against separating the fees for garbage and recycling, as it could lead to
21   confusion and resistance from residents who might then opt out of recycling, which the current
22   contract does not support. For now, the focus should be on ensuring the combined fee is clear
23   and transparent to residents without causing contractual issues or operational complications.
24
25   Councilmember Pierucci asked how many Planned Unit Developments (PUDs) do not pay the
26   City for sanitation services. Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, said there are
27   approximately 3,400 units or households in Riverton that fall into this category.
28
29   Councilmember Pierucci discussed the need for the sanitation enterprise fund to be self-
30   sustaining. He mentioned his initial support for eliminating recycling to avoid fee increases but
31   changed his stance after engaging with residents. A survey showed that 70% of residents in his
32   district wanted to keep recycling despite potential fee increases. He emphasized the importance
33   of resident feedback and the value of engaging the public in discussions that impact their daily
34   lives. Based on this feedback, he supported keeping recycling and adjusting fees to ensure the
35   sanitation fund is self-sustaining.
36
37   Councilmember McDougal reiterated the importance of the enterprise fund being independent
38   and highlighted the misconception among residents about landfills. He explained that landfills
39   like Trans-Jordan convert waste into methane, which is then used to generate power, thereby
40   contributing to recycling efforts. He emphasized that this process is environmentally beneficial
41   and different from the notion that landfilling is purely detrimental.
42
43   Based on the data and feedback from residents, Councilmember McDougal expressed support
44   for eliminating recycling to maintain a self-sustaining enterprise fund, arguing that the landfill's
45   recycling process is sufficient.
46
47   Councilmember Haymond expressed strong support for maintaining curbside recycling,
48   reflecting the preferences of his constituents. He also advocated for the sanitation fund to be
49   self-sustaining.

16   Councilmember McCay raised questions about the impact fees listed in item 144, specifically
17   pointing out the fee for daycare centers, which is $31,981 per 1,000 square feet. She noted that
18   this fee is significantly higher compared to other facilities such as elementary schools ($3,000
19   per 1,000 square feet), hospitals ($2,700 per 1,000 square feet), and even animal hospitals

21   might contribute to the rising costs of childcare, making it difficult for families, particularly
22   working mothers, to afford it.
23

26   based on data regarding how different types of facilities impact roadways, including trip
27   generation numbers, which indicate the volume of traffic generated by a particular use. While it
28   was acknowledged that the specific details of the study were not immediately available, it was
29   noted that the high fee for daycare centers likely reflects their higher trip generation numbers
30   compared to other uses. He assured the Council that the impact fees would be reviewed and
31   updated soon, and the issue of daycare fees would be carefully examined during that process.
