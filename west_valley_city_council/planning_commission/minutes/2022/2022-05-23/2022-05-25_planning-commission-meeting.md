ZT-3-2022
West Valley City
An ordinance change to prohibit private streets, allow parking spaces for townhomes and
condominiums attached to the side of each other to back onto a street, and create two new
street classifications for use in planned unit developments.
This application was continued by the Planning Commission during the public hearing on May
11.

At the request of the City Council, City staff is proposing an ordinance amendment to:
    1. prohibit private streets,
    2. allow parking spaces for townhomes and condominiums attached to the side of each other
        to back onto a street, and
    3. create two new street classifications for use in planned unit developments.

For the first change, the City Council has expressed concerns about the financial burden private
streets place on the residents of planned unit developments (PUDs) with private streets. The
proposed ordinance would prohibit the creation of new private streets. Streets internal to a
development under one ownership, such as an apartment development where no subdivision is
needed, would still be allowed.

For the second change, the current ordinance includes requirements that prohibit the driveways
for attached housing like townhomes to access public streets. Section 7-9-106 prohibits vehicles
in parking spaces for all development except single and two unit dwellings from backing onto
streets. Section 7-9-107 establishes minimum spacing between driveways for all development
except single and two unit dwellings. The proposed ordinance adds townhomes and condos that
are attached to the side of each other to the list of uses that are excluded from these two
provisions.

For the third change, two new street classifications are proposed for use in planned unit
developments with attached housing. The current standard for residential streets is a 54’ wide
right-of-way composed of 29’ of pavement, 2.5’ of curb and gutter on both sides, 5’ parkstrip on
both sides, and 5’ sidewalk on both sides. The proposed new street classifications are:
     a PUD Primary Street that is a 44’ wide right-of-way composed of 29’ of pavement, 2.5’
        of curb and gutter on both sides, 5’ parkstrip on one side, and 5’ sidewalk on one side and
     a PUD Secondary Street that is a 34’ wide right-of-way composed of 29’ of pavement
        and 2.5’ of curb and gutter on both sides.

There are several reasons for creating two new street classifications for use in PUDs with
attached housing. These reasons are outlined below:

      By ordinance definition, PUDs are intended to allow flexibility and initiative in site and
       building design and location. Having only one residential street classification limits
       flexibility and initiative in design.
      Many PUDs have sidewalks that are internal to the development. For example, the fronts
       of rear-loaded townhomes often face each other with the front door in the front of the
       unit. Requiring sidewalks on all streets would result in sidewalks on the sides of
       buildings with no doors.
      With roughly 92% of the City developed, new development often occurs on small, infill
       parcels or developed parcels that are redeveloped. Such parcels are often narrow, have an
       awkward shape, require connecting to adjoining streets, or exhibit other characteristics
       that make development more challenging. Having only one residential street
       classification would make development on such parcels difficult if not impossible in
       some cases.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. The ordinance should be approved as proposed by staff.

   2. Continuance.

   3. Denial.

 C-12-2022
 KeyBank West Valley
 2807 S 5600 W
 C-2 Zone (1.12 acres)


The applicant, Ben Gingrich, is requesting conditional use approval for a new bank, which
will be located at 2807 S 5600 W. The property is zoned C-2, General Commercial, and the
West Valley City General Plan designates this area as General Commercial. The subject
property is also located in the 5600 West Overlay Zone. The surrounding zoning is C-2.
Adjacent land uses include a credit union to the north, a restaurant to the east, and multi-
tenant commercial buildings to the south and west.

This application was continued from the May 11, 2022 Public Hearing. The Planning
Commission requested that some issues raised during the meeting regarding parking and the
building footprint be addressed. The applicant is proposing to construct a 3,442 sq. ft.
KeyBank branch. Access to the site will be from existing shared access points on Grantham
Lane, which is a private street, Highbury Parkway, and High Commons Way. There are two
adjacent parcels with existing buildings on them: a restaurant to the east and a multi-tenant
commercial building to the south. The subject parcel has an existing pad in the north-west
corner that was placed there for a future building. There have been multiple proposals to
develop the pad in the past, but no one has followed through. The proposal for the bank does
not utilize the existing pad. That area will be incorporated into a portion of the new building
footprint and into expanding the parking lot.

The bank will be oriented to the north and will have drive-thru lanes on the east side of the
building. Some of the existing parking lot and landscape islands will be reconfigured.
There are currently 187 stalls on the three parcels that share parking, circulation and access.
The proposed building and parking lot modifications would result in 154 stalls remaining on
the site. Based on the existing and proposed uses, 150 stalls are required when calculated
using the shared parking table in 7-9-105.

During the Public Hearing there was also a concern raised about the size of the proposed
building and drive-thru canopy. The subdivision plat does not regulate locations for building
pads or dictate maximum building footprint sizes. The City does not get involved in CC&R
regulations, if there are any. The only City Code that regulates building size in a C-2 zone is
the provision that buildings cannot occupy more than 50% of the lot area.
Allowable building size can also be affected by required setbacks, parking and landscaping
provisions. The proposed building size meets all zoning regulations.

The existing 5600 West streetscape landscaping will remain west of the sidewalk. The rest of
the landscaping on the site needs to meet the other general site and parking lot landscaping
requirements of Title 7. The applicant is proposing to remove the lawn east of the sidewalk
and landscape with trees, shrubs, perennials, and ornamental grasses. All landscaped areas
need at least 50% coverage of live plant material.

The building shall meet all the Commercial Design Standards outlined in 7-11-300P. Since
the building is also located in the 5600 West Overlay Zone, there are also additional building
requirements for the exterior. One-hundred percent masonry materials are required on the
street-facing façade, which is the west elevation, and the building needs to be designed so
that there is equal emphasis on all facades.

There will be wall signs on the building. The applicant has not requested a new monument sign
at this time, but there is enough street frontage for one additional monument sign in the future.
All signage on the property shall comply with the regulations in Title 11.

Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any concerns raised at the public hearing as well as
the following:

           1. The site shall be constructed per the approved plan.

           3. Landscaping shall meet all applicable Title 7 requirements, which includes at
              least 50% coverage of live plant material in all landscaped areas.
           4. The building elevations shall meet all the Commercial Design Standards
              outlined in 7-11-300P, as well as the 5600 West Overlay requirements found in
              7-14-403.
           5. Signage shall comply with Title 11 of the West Valley City Code. One

BACKGROUND:

Ted Didas, representing the Valley Fair Mall, is requesting preliminary approval for 2 individual
subdivision applications. The purpose for the applications is to divide the remaining outbuildings
on the west side of the mall into individual lots. Because these applications are essentially the
same, staff will prepare one report that addresses each subdivision.

ISSUES:

The proposed application is being submitted to create 2 individual subdivision lots. According to
Brian Moss, Partner with Coventry Real Estate Advisors, creating new lots will provide the owner
an option to sell stabilized properties separately from the larger mall asset that continues to evolve
and requires redevelopment. He further states that the proceeds from potential sales of the lots
will be utilized to pay debt and allow Coventry to continue to invest in the future development of
the mall site. One such example of this, is the revitalized plaza space located between these two
lots. The mall recently completed improvements to the plaza to make it more accessible, open and
inviting for a multitude of activities.

As we did with the first subdivision applications, staff inquired about parking and cross access and
was informed that the newly created lots will be created with shared parking and access
agreements. The larger mall asset will stay with Coventry so that no restrictions on the
redevelopment of the mall property would be subject to approval from new property owners.

According to Brian’s letter (attached as part of the packet) he states that, “retail’s general
headwinds and the pandemic, specifically relating to interior malls have required owners of
properties to spend meaningful time, think creatively and invest additional capital to ensure the
mall components succeed.” He lists a number of ways the mall ownership has done this in his
letter.

Each lot will comply with provisions of the C-2 Zone. Specifically, each lot will meet or exceed
20,000 square feet which is the base minimum of the C-2 Zone. During the review of the original
outparcel subdivisions, the Planning Commission inquired about Granger Hunter Improvement
services to the mall property and the creation of individual lots. A master easement agreement is
in place to manage services provided by GHID. In addition, since the mall property is private, a
master storm water easement is also in place. Staff recommends to mall ownership that any new
tenants within these lots be made aware of the requirements and regulations regarding water
leaving the individual building site.

All of the existing buildings, landscaping etc., have been reviewed by Staff in previous
applications. At this point, no changes to those buildings or sites are expected. The application
simply allows the mall owner a future opportunity to sell these newly created lots and reinvest the
proceeds back into the mall.

Castlewood Development is requesting conditional use approval to redevelop 3.02 acres at 2940

unit mix for the project is 42 studio units, 193 one-bedroom units, and 95 two-bedroom units.
The majority of the property (2.94 acres) is zoned CC (City Center) and the balance (0.08 acres)
is zoned R-2-8 (Two Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square feet). The entire
building is within the CC zone. The property is designated as very high density residential (over
20 units/acre) or office for most of the property and promenade/plaza for the remaining north
portion in the Fairbourne Station Vision, which is part of the City’s General Plan. The property is
currently vacant as the office development previously on the property was demolished in 2014.

During the study meeting there were questions raised about parking and possible future
improvements along 3650 South. While there are some locations on 3650 South where on-street
parking can occur now, the frontage of the subject property is not one of those locations.
According to the Public Works Department, 3650 South will be improved between 3200 West
and 2700 West in 2024 or 2025. Planned improvements include consistent curb, gutter, and
sidewalk along both sides of the street as well as a left turn lane in the middle of the street. Once
these improvements are made, no on-street parking will be allowed.

The Commission also inquired about visitor parking since the parking structure is secured. The
applicant is working on solutions to this issue.

Project Review

      Create a recognizable center or downtown for West Valley City. At 7 stories, this

      Encourage and direct development that supports transit. High density residential
       development is one of the best land uses for supporting transit.
      Encourage infill and redevelopment near the future transit station by City Hall. The

      Reinforce the use of public transportation by locating higher-intensity development,

      Encourage mixed-use development to reduce automobile dependency and roadway

Amenities

ordinance with at least three of the five being major amenities. This project has six major
