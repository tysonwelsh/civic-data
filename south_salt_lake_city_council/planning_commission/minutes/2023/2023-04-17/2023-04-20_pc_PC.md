1.     Public Hearing – A Petition for Preliminary Plat Approval to Subdivide Eight Existing
       Parcels into a 61-Lot Subdivision Plat.

Action Item

Applicant:     C.W. Urban, Ian Cahoon, Representative
Address:       547-617 East 3900 South

Ms. Ungricht presented the Staff Report and stated that the application before the Planning
Commission was Preliminary Plat approval of The Monroe Townhomes. It was a public hearing
and the application was to subdivide eight existing parcels into a 61-lot subdivision plat at 547-617
East 3900 South. There were two steps in the process, which included the Preliminary Plat approval
and a recommendation to the City Council to amend the zoning to include the Townhome Overlay
(“TO”). A map of the project was shared. In total, the property was just under four acres. She
reiterated that there are currently eight parcels. The applicant wanted to subdivide that into a 61-lot
townhome subdivision. The property is currently in the Mixed-Use Land District. To the north, it
is R-1 and to the south, there is a mixed-use designation in Millcreek. To the west, is a school zone.

Images of the existing conditions were shared with the Planning Commission. Ms. Ungricht reported
that it is currently vacant land. There used to be a surgical center there but it was demolished in
2016. There was parking for the old surgical center on the site. The Site Plan was shown. It would
be comprised of 56 townhome units. As for the most southern units, there were 19 proposed
live/work units, which would allow the tenant of the unit to have an office or retail space on the
ground floor. The rest of the units would be traditional townhomes. There were nine townhome
buildings proposed with multiple amenities. For instance, outdoor areas and pickleball courts. The
Plat was looked at by the Community Development, Engineering, and Fire Departments, and all
requirements were met. The proposed renderings were reviewed. Ms. Ungricht identified the
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Meeting                                                  3

proposed live/work units. In those instances, the ground floor has more glass and more of a
commercial front compared to the other traditional townhome units.

The Preliminary Plat was shared. A few easements run through the plat but none would be built
over. Ms. Ungricht reported that an irrigation ditch runs through the property. She reiterated that
there would not be any building over that easement. As part of this application, the neighboring
property was being platted because currently, the parcel for their drive approach on the east side
does not have ownership tied to the parcel. The applicant would plat that as well so there is a cross-
access easement on the drive approach. She explained that the plat was still being reviewed and
would come before the Planning Commission at a later date.

Commissioner Carter asked about the work/live units that will face 3900 South. He wanted to know
how the City would ensure that those units were being used for their intended purpose.
Mr. Weidenhamer noted that there was no condition that the units be used for commercial. It was
simply an option that was available to the owner since the unit was designed to a higher standard.
He anticipated that the owners would utilize them in that way since it was a better option for the
community and the neighborhood. However, there was nothing in the Code forcing the owners to
use the live/work units for commercial. Ms. Ungricht clarified that the tenant who lives in the unit
will also own the workspace. It is slightly different from other live/work units in the City.
Commissioner Carter wondered if the owner of the live/work unit will have the option of subletting
the workspace. Mr. Weidenhamer did not believe that was the intention but noted that the applicant
was present and would be better able to address that concern.

Ms. Ungricht shared additional information about the application. The development was determined
to meet all of the parking requirements. She explained that 112 stalls were required in garages for
the 56 units, which was met. Additionally, nine stalls were required for the ground floor commercial
live/work units as well as 28 guest stalls. 41 stalls would be provided, which was more than required
by the Code. One of the Conditions of Approval related to signage since the development is
surrounded by other parking lots. This would ensure there is no confusion and visitors park
appropriately.

The application meets multiple General Plan considerations, which included the following:

   •   Community Values Goal 2: support neighborhood livability by creating pedestrian, bike, and
       play environments appropriate to the surrounding housing types;
   •   Community Values Goal 3: take advantage of opportunities relating to the City’s location at
       the center of the regional transportation, transit, trails, open space, library, recreation, and
       school systems to serve residents, businesses, and visitors;
   •   Land Use and Neighborhoods Goal 1: continue to welcome new residents and businesses
       into South Salt Lake;
   •   Housing Goals Strategy 3: encourage the development of housing that ranges in size and
       scale to accommodate the needs of all residents; and
   •   Economic Development Strategy 3: encourage the development and preservation of
       neighborhood-based shops and services in strategic locations.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission hold a public hearing and approve a Preliminary
Subdivision Plat to subdivide eight existing parcels, located at 547-617 East 3900 South, into a 61-
lot plat. The Staff recommendation was based on the analysis and findings in the Staff Report.

Ms. Ungricht noted that the applicant was present to answer any additional questions. Commissioner
Bellina asked about the sidewalk that will be added. Ms. Ungricht explained that the remaining
infrastructure will remain in place. There will be a park strip, a sidewalk, and a landscape buffer.
There will be interior sidewalks within the development to connect the units. The 15-foot landscape
buffer was because of the easements as nothing can happen in that area. Typically, on plats, there
is a requirement to place power lines underground but the ones there are large and expensive. As a
result, they were allowed to remain in place. No building over the easement was permitted.
Commissioner Bellina asked about the guest parking spaces and asked where those interested in
visiting the live/work units will park. Ms. Ungricht explained that visitors will need to park at the
back of the community and walk to 3900 South.

C.W. Urban representative, Ian Cahoon, introduced himself to the Planning Commission. He
appreciated all of the time that had been devoted to the application and felt that a great product had
been proposed. It was well-designed and will offer a lot of amenities. He was excited about the
live/work units. Since the property is located within the Mixed-Use area, it made sense to provide
that mixture to the community. Mr. Cahoon felt that the Townhome Overlay and proposed
subdivision would be a notable addition to South Salt Lake. He addressed the concerns raised by
Commissioner Carter earlier in the meeting. It was noted that the live/work units were designed to
fulfill that multi-purpose use. There are designated locations within the units for work, but there are
stairs that go up into the residential. Theoretically, it would be possible to have someone else work
in the main area, but there was a connection to the upstairs unit. As a result, it was anticipated that
the owner of the unit would utilize both the commercial and residential space.

Commissioner Carter wondered if there was a desire for the live/work units to be utilized as
designed. Mr. Cahoon felt that owners should have flexibility. The live/work units would be built
with the intention of there being commercial and residential use, but at the end of the day, owners
can do what they want with their units. Commissioner Carter noted that there are a few buildings in
the community where the first floor is commercial. The businesses rotate in and out as often as
renters. His concern was that the commercial portion of the townhome would eventually become an
extension of the living space due to the rotation of businesses. That being said, if the owner is forced
to maintain a business on the first floor, it might be difficult to sell the units. Mr. Cahoon explained
that it would be difficult to limit the townhomes to only business owners. There would not be
anything in a Purchase Agreement that would tell the owner how to utilize the space.

Commissioner Southey asked if the live/work units will be more expensive to purchase. Mr. Cahoon
explained that it is early in the development process and pricing still needs to be finalized. However,
he did not see the prices being that different from the other units. Chair Pechmann wondered if all
units will be for individual sale within the project. Mr. Cahoon reported that the plan for the project
was to be a for-sale community.

The public hearing was opened.

James Smith gave his address as 3774 South 1645 East and stated that he does not live far from the
development. He pointed out that the pain clinic and the cancer clinic that was located adjacent to
the property will lose 25 parking spaces as a result of the development. The loss of those parking
spaces was something to be considered even though accommodations for parking within the
development had been taken care of. Mr. Smith expressed concerns about crime in the nearby area.

There were no further comments. The public hearing was closed at 7:26 p.m.

There was discussion about crime in the area. Commissioner Ewell wondered if the area in question
is part of the parking lot for the commercial businesses that abut 700 East. Ms. Ungricht explained
that the application was presented to both the Police Chief and Fire Chief and there were not a lot of
concerns because once people are living in the townhomes, there will be more eyes on the area than
there are currently. In addition, there will be internal lighting to address crime-related fears. The
Commission reviewed the site layout. Chair Pechmann wondered if there were windows on the
sides of the units that faced the parking lot to the north. Ms. Ungricht confirmed this. Commissioner
Bellina asked if there would be a fence along the property line. Ms. Ungricht stated that there will
be a fence and shared site details.

Commissioner Southey agreed that having more eyes on the street would be beneficial in terms of
crime prevention. Chair Pechmann noted that it is currently an open lot. He believed the concern
expressed by Mr. Smith was that constructing the fence would obstruct the view and wall off the
area. This was confirmed. Mr. Smith noted that it would be a difficult area to police. Commissioner
Carter discussed the Center Square Subdivision. That lot will be difficult to develop moving forward
because it is almost landlocked. He wondered if that had been considered. Walker Wood, another
of the applicant’s representatives, stated that there had been a lot of discussion with the owner of the
site. It was an oddly shaped lot and a lot of time had been spent making it as clean as possible. Due
to the parking requirements for the square footage of the building that was currently platted, it had
not been possible to rearrange the parcel lines, but there had been a lot of coordination.

Chair Pechmann asked if the Homeowners Association (“HOA”) will have a security plan.
Mr. Wood confirmed that they would and explained that the security for the site will be managed by
the HOA. Chair Pechmann asked if there are security cameras in the current building plans.
Mr. Wood explained that they will not be placed on individual units but cameras could be installed
in the common areas. While there was not a current plan for cameras to be on site, he acknowledged
that cameras could be added to the development, if desired.

2.      Public Hearing – A Petition to the Planning Commission for a Recommendation to the
        City Council to Amend the Zoning for Properties Located at 547-617 East 3900 South,
        to include the Townhome Overlay (“TO”).

Action Item

Applicant:     C.W. Urban, Ian Cahoon, Representative
Address:       547-617 East 3900 South

Ms. Ungricht reported that the application before the Planning Commission pertains to a
recommendation to the City Council to amend the zoning for properties located at 547-617 East
3900 South to include the TO. She noted that this is the second public hearing connected to the
property. She shared information related to the TO. It overlaid the existing base zone, which in this
instance is the Mixed-Use Land Use District. It allows townhomes to be developed. Townhomes
are not permitted to be built without the application of the overlay zone. She reported that Staff
conducted a technical review and all of the TO requirements were met. As a result, Staff was
comfortable with the application and recommended that the Planning Commission forward a
recommendation of approval to the City Council to amend the land use map to include the TO. The
Staff recommendation was based on the analysis and findings included in the Staff Report.

The public hearing was opened. There were no comments. The hearing was closed at 7:36 p.m.
