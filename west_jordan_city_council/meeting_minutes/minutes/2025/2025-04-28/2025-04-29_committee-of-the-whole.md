 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
       a. Presentation of proposed updates to the 2024 Sanitary Sewer Master Plan
          Public Utilities Director Greg Davenport introduced proposed updates to the Sanitary
          Sewer Master Plan. Mr. Davenport explained that the Sanitary Sewer Master Plan, which
          examined current and future demands, was part of the City Master Plan. He asked the
          Council to consider and provide feedback regarding maximum density to plan for in
          Overlay Zones. Responding to a question from Council Member Bloom, Mr. Davenport
          said he was not aware of any area in the West Jordan sewer system in need of immediate
          upgrade.

          Council Member Green wanted the Council to be able to tell developers that proposed
          developments would or would not work with the City plan. Mr. Davenport answered
          questions from Council Member Bloom regarding inflow and ground water, grant
          opportunities, and communication with stakeholders. He anticipated the proposed
          updates would be presented to the Council for adoption in four or five months. Council
          Member Bloom expressed a desire for environmental sustainability and future-proofing

       b. Discussion of the Visionary Square development - proposed amendment to the Future
          Land Use Map and ensuing rezone request
          Associate Planner Mark Forsythe introduced a petition from Joel Frost requesting a
          Future Land Use Map (FLUM) amendment for three parcels from Low Density Residential
          and Neighborhood Commercial to Professional Office designation, and a rezone of two
          parcels from SC-1 Zone (Neighborhood Shopping Center) to P-O (Professional Office). The
          three parcels, located on the northwest corner of Bangerter Highway and 9000 South,
          were surrounded by residential neighborhoods, Copper Ridge Medical Center, Jordan
          Valley Hospital, and Salt Lake Community College campus.

          Mr. Forsythe showed a concept plan, which incorporated suggestions made by staff, and
          described improvements to storm drain lines that would be needed. He said the proposed
          small incubator offices would all be single-story. Council Member Bloom asked if
          residents in the area had expressed concern with changing the zone away from
          residential. Vice Chair Whitelock said the main concerns expressed at a recent town hall
          meeting had to do with the stub street. She said the developer agreed to put up a fence
          that would prohibit pedestrian traffic between existing residential and the planned office
          development.

          Council Member Bloom asked how the City could support economic growth in the area
          and maintain the desired aesthetic and character. She said she liked the proposed plan
          and wanted the businesses to be successful, and wanted to make sure a support system
          was provided. Mr. Forsythe said the developer had been creative in trying to make the
          plan economically viable. He said the proposed access had not yet been approved by
          UDOT.

          Council Member Shelton felt the proposed plan was a dream come true for the City and
          thanked everyone involved. Council Member Green believed the proposed plan was the
          best possible concept for the developer and the neighborhood. He believed the most
          difficult aspect was the traffic.

          Chair Lamb suggested the matter could be brought back to a Council meeting for action.

       c. Discussion of proposed Title 13 amendments, specifically 13-2-3: Definitions, and 13-
          8-14: Storage of Commercial Vehicles in Residential Zone

          Assistant Planner Megan Jensen introduced the proposed amendments to Title 13 which
          were drafted using feedback previously provided by the Council. Ms. Jensen reviewed
          drafted amendments with the Council, and answered questions.

          Council Member Jacob felt it appeared the proposed language would prohibit a truck with
          two sets of rear tires (dually truck) from being parked in front of a house. Ms. Jensen said
          the language was drafted with that prohibition, but could be changed if the Council
          desired. Council Members Bloom, Shelton, and Jacob expressed a desire for property
          owners to be able to park dually trucks in front of homes. Vice Chair Whitelock suggested
          differentiating between personal-use and commercial-use dually vehicles. Council
          Member Green pointed out some vehicles could be used for both personal and
          commercial, and said he believed differentiating would be difficult. Council Member
          Shelton suggested classifying a box truck separate from a dually truck.

          Council Member Bloom wanted to make sure the proposed storage limits were not too
          restrictive. Vice Chair Whitelock believed the biggest investment for most individuals was
          their home, and the appearance of a neighborhood affected property value. She believed
          protecting resident investment in property was part of the government’s responsibility.
          She did not want residential neighborhoods to look like commercial property. Vice Chair
          Whitelock pointed out that residential roads were not built for heavy commercial vehicle
          weights. Council Member Shelton felt bad for residents who drove semi-trucks for a living
          and would now not be able to park them at home.

          Council Member Green suggested adding “completely enclosed in a garage or shed” as
          permitted for Class 5 vehicles. Council Member Green commented that many service
          providers took their work vehicles (tow trucks, plumbing vehicles, etc.) home because
          they were on call overnight, and he would be comfortable allowing most Class 5 vehicles
          to be parked in a driveway as long as they did not hang over into the sidewalk. Council
          Member Bloom agreed. Council Member Shelton would prefer to not allow box trucks in
          front yards. Council Member Green suggested adding a limit of one Class 5 vehicle in a
          front yard. The Council and staff discussed enforcement of parking regulations.

          Council Member Jacob viewed the whole topic as a freedom infringement problem. He
          said any regulations the Council put in place would have consequences beyond fixing what
          they perceived as the problem, and said every solution that had been discussed would
          overregulate the problem. Council Member Green said the ordinance attempted to set
          reasonable regulation that could be communicated to residents to both allow business to
          be conducted to a certain extent, and allow neighbors to know what to expect. Mr.
          Langford commented that he liked the simplicity of the proposed Code, which would be
          easy to explain to residents.

          Council Member Shelton agreed with Council Member Jacob about infringing freedoms,
          and also agreed with Vice Chair Whitelock that some property owners would abuse the
          situation and abuse their neighbors if allowed. Council Member Shelton believed the Code
          was getting to where it should be, and was pleased with what had been done.

          changing the term to “front driveway” or a different term that would not imply vehicles

       d. Discussion on Council Priorities from Legislative/Executive Retreat in January 2025
          Policy Analyst & Public Liaison Warren Hallmark showed a list of common themes taken
          from thoughts about a vision statement submitted by members of the Council, thoughts
          from the Mayor, and common themes or guiding principles from the General Plan. He
          showed six potential vision statements drafted using information provided. The Council
          discussed the statements.

              •   A vibrant, inclusive city where community, innovation, and natural beauty
                  converge – building a sustainable future where residents live, work, and thrive.
              •   West Jordan: a welcoming and forward-thinking community that celebrates its
                  rich history, fosters diverse neighborhoods, and leads in growth, innovation, and
                  quality of life for generations to come.
              •   A connected city with heart and spirit – where residents and visitors enjoy vibrant
                  neighborhoods, preserved landscapes, and opportunities to grow, innovate, and
                  belong.
              •   West Jordan as a regional hub of culture, commerce, and community – balancing
                  sustainable development with natural preservation, and inspiring pride in our
                  past, present, and future.

              •   West Jordan aspires to be a vibrant, sustainable city that honors its past while
                  embracing future opportunities – building a community where families flourish,
                  businesses thrive, and natural resources are preserved for future generations.
              •   A city with purpose and pride – building a livable, innovative, and resilient
                  community where every resident feels at home and connected to a thriving

          Council Member Bloom suggested: “West Jordan is a vibrant, welcoming, sustainable
          city…”. A majority of the Council appeared to like the fifth statement, replacing “aspires”

          Council Member Bloom suggested that the vision statement be adopted with an official
          resolution. Council Member Jacob desired to see something done with the vision
          statement. Vice Chair Whitelock believed the statement should be a guiding principle for
          budgeting. Council Member Bloom wanted the statement to be included in any update of
          the General Plan.

          Council Member Bedore liked the vision statement but felt it was very broad. Council
          Member Shelton felt that anyone who felt strongly about anything could say their pet
          project fit into the vision. Council Member Green believed the vision statement
          represented the “dream”, and the next step was figuring out how to get there.

          The Council discussed the list of priorities generated using Council survey responses (in
          order from highest to lowest priority):

              •   Economic Development and Planning
              •   City Identity
              •   Civic Events and Celebrations
              •   Infrastructure and Public Works
              •   Public Art and Aesthetics
              •   Communication (Administrative or “internal”)
              •   Communication (External, promotional)
              •   Transportation and Active Mobility
              •   Code and Policy Improvement
              •   Environmental Sustainability
              •   Public Facilities and Amenities
              •   Diversity and Community Engagement

          Council Member Green spoke of checking potential projects against the list of priorities.
          Council Member Bedore suggested expanding lists underneath each of the priorities to
          build the vision. Mr. Hallmark said staff could add in subtopics with information provided
          at the 2025 Council Retreat.
