2. Mayor's Budget Comments
Mr. Darrin Casper , Deputy Mayor of Finance and Administration, stated the budget this 
year was tough and took a lot of work.  The budget is structurally balanced, and the 
structural balance will be helped by the fact that short-term interest rates have risen.
The theme of the budget has been trying to do more for County employees through health 
insurance benefits, pay increases, etc.  This was accomplished through the denial of new 
requests and through budget cuts.  Going forward, the bar for new requests will likely be 
quite high.  The revenue committee will carefully monitor revenues.  
Mayor Jennifer Wilson  stated this was not an easy budget year.  She appreciated the 
engagement from the Council and staff, particularly on compensation issues.  Contra 
accounts on personnel and operations are not the norm and will create challenges, but she 
thought the budget was good and she hoped for an affirmative vote tonight.   
3. Open Public Hearing - Public Comment
A motion was made by Council Member Bradshaw, seconded by Council Member Harrison,

Council Member Alvord read a prepared statement:
As a fiscal hawk, our office created a list of cuts from which I proposed 
and made many motions. Had those motions been supported by the 
majority, they would have trimmed over $ 32,000,000.00 in permanent 
cuts from the mayor ’s proposed budget. These cuts included a 
permanent 2% operations cut, eliminating dozens of vacant positions, 
cutting “equity” pay which was meant to give raises to those in 
protected classes - eroding morale among other county employees. The 
cut list also included the Salt Lake County Co -op (a social program ), a 
large contribution to Sundance Films, The Kem Gardner Policy 
Institute, a WFRC grant, as well as expensive county lobbyist contracts . 
I believe these cuts would not have resulted in any change to the level of 
service for residents. 
Sadly, all those cuts were not supported by the majority. However, I am 
grateful to Councilman Sheldon Stewart, and Councilwoman Dea 
Theodore for supporting most of my motions. On the bright side, the 
Republican Caucus was united in cutting $ 9,500,000 - included in 
these cuts was the WISE program (social engineering) and the office of 
diversity and inclusion. I also applaud the one -million dollar cut to 
travel which was an item my office had help to identify. It’s worth 
noting the Councilwoman Laurie Stringham had some cuts of her own, 
which I supported, which sadly, did not receive a majority vote. 
Since not all of my motions passed, I believe that this budget did not cut 
deep enough - and unless we see growth projections improve 
significantly, I fear next year’s budget will be in the red.  
This budget is not as lean as I would have preferred. With that said, I 
will vote to support it as it does an adequate job in being balanced 
thanks to our cuts, did not increase taxes, and represents many of the 
priorities my constituents value.

structurally sound.
Mr. Darrin Casper , Deputy Mayor of Finance and Administration, stated 
there are many assumptions built into this year ’s budget and time will tell if 
they come into fruition.  He promised that he and fiscal staff would have a 
keen eye toward structural balance as they always did.  There are always 
pressures against structural balance because revenues and property taxes are 
not allowed to grow.  The State Tax Commission sets a rate that ignores new 
growth.  In inflationary times, that pressure can come to bear on the 
structural balance. 
Council Member Harrison  stated compared to the budget passed in 2023, 
there was a savings of over $ 25 million in General Fund dollars and related 
tax funds.  She was glad to be able to improve employee compensation and 
prioritize needed services for Salt Lake County residents.
Council Member Stewart  stated he was disappointed in the decision to 
keep contra accounts intact, but he would still be voting in favor of the 
budget.
