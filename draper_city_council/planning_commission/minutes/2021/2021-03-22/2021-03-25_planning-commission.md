   4. Public Hearing: City Initiated 2021 Hedgerow Text Amendment (Legislative Item)

Chairperson Adams stated that, on the request of Draper City, there has been a Text Amendment
to amend Section 9-23-110 of Draper City Municipal Code. This application is known as the
City initiated Hedgerow Text Amendment. Application: TEXTMAP-0029-2021. Staff contact is
Todd Draper, (801)576-6335, todd.draper@draperutah.gov.

Mr. Draper stated, this amendment is in response to comments from the Planning Commission
and the City Council about the use of a hedgerow in place of an eight-foot fence. This ordinance
removes that as a potential consideration by the planning commission when approving deviations
to landscape buffer requirements. Additional clarifications are provided that any approved
reduction in land use buffer width does not include any reduction the quantity or quality of
landscaping associated with the buffer area.

Mr. Draper asked if there were any questions.

There were none.

   6. Annual Ethics Training
      Training by Mike Barker

Mr. DuShane, Attorney, showed a video about conflict of interest and ethics violations. Mr.
DuShane then stated that, according to the ethics code, no city officer or employee shall grant
any special consideration, treatment, or advantage to any citizen beyond that which is available
to every other citizen. By recusal or disclosure, one must avoid a conflict of interest. ‘Interest’ is
defined as direct or indirect pecuniary or material benefit accruing to a city officer or employee
as a result of an official act or action by, or with the city, with the exception of similar benefits
made available to other Draper citizens similarly situated. This extends to immediate and
extended family, people of a contractual relationship, businesses for which one has a significant
leadership role, businesses for which one has an ownership stake of more than 5%.

Whereas a ‘gift’ is defined as anything that is given within an official capacity with the exception
of items or services with a monetary value of less than $50, items given only occasionally, a
publicly presented award, and a campaign contribution. One must disclose interests that could be
seen as a conflict of interest, including compensation received for assistance in transaction with
the city, interest in a business entity regulated by the city, interest in a business entity doing
business with the city, and personal interest or investment creating a conflict of interest.

When in doubt, disclose. Recusal is another good tool. When a person recuses, they announce
that they’re recusing and leave the room. Not every situation requires a recusal; however, full
disclosure will help to recognize situations where recusal is required. In addition to disclosure,
recusal in official situations for which a conflict of interest is had, helps to keep people in
alignment with ethics laws. Do not accept gifts in the form of goods, services, or employment
opportunities in exchange for favorable treatments. Acceptance of small tokens of appreciation,
campaign donations, and publicly presented awards are permitted. When in doubt, ask the city
attorney. Disclosure is always a good idea. If still unsure, recuse. Any questions?
