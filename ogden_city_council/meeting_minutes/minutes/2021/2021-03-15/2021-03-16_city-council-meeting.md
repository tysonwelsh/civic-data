Proposed Ordinance 2021-10 amending the Ogden Municipal Code to
address changes to utility fees and to revise water utility rate and fee
provisions
         A memo from the Management Services Department regarding proposed utility billing fee amendments came before the
Council for consideration. The memo stated in conjunction with the City's continued implementation of the Tyler Enterprise
Resource Planning (ERP) Solutions system, the City's administration is proposing changes to billing and operational fees for the
water, sewer and refuse utilities. These changes are not related to utility fees such as water or sewer rates; rather, the changes are
generally related to updating fees and standards for associated services and account maintenance issues. Council members have
expressed interest in establishing a grace period after a utility bill due date prior to the assessment of the proposed late fee.

Proposed Ordinance 2021-12 to amend Project AR091 for road
connections, taxiways, and all types of needs for airport expansion at the
Ogden Hinckley Airport: AND
Proposed Ordinance 2021-11 to amend the budget in the amount of
$6,000,000 for infrastructure support improvements at the Ogden Hinckley
Airport
            A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed amendment to the Fiscal
Year (FY) 2021-2025 Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposed
amendment modifies the scope of work for project AR091 to allow the completion of additional infrastructure work. The original
CIP brief contemplated planning for future growth. Revenue recently received from the State of Utah will now allow site
preparation to begin to accommodate future development on the west side.
             An additional memo from the Management Services Department regarding a proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2021 budget
amendment came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposed budget amendment recognizes and
appropriates $6 million from the State of Utah. Funds will be used for infrastructure improvements at the Airport, including
utilities, roadways, access, and direct flight support. This action would result in a net adjustment of$6 million to the Airport Fund
in the FY2021 Ogden City Budget.
            Senior Analyst Sorensen summarized the memos and expounded on the adjustment to the scope of the CIP project, which
is divided into four distinct components as follows:
            1.       Tie into, Improve, and extend 4000 South onto Airport property, turning North until just past the two concrete
            water tanks where the road will terminate in a cul-de-sac with a sufficient diameter to allow semi-trucks with heavy
            earth-moving equipment to access the development site.

         Minutes of Regular Meeting of Council of Ogden City, Utah, March 16, 2021 Page 97


          2.       The design and construction of a partial parallel taxiway west of the approach to Runway 17. This taxiway will
          fully meet all FAA requirements and will allow aircraft using either OGD Airport runway, to exit the runways and gain
          access to the new west side development.
          3.       The clearing, grading and drainage of the new OGD Airport west side development area. This includes the
          removal of the concrete and construction debris from the dumping that has occurred at the OGD Airport over the past 30-
          40 years, including the old Ogden Mall. This will require all basic stormwater elements, the pulverization of old taxiway
          and runway surfaces, and preparing the grade for proper drainage and maintenance.
          4.       The strategic stubbing out and placement of the utilities so that the developer(s) can access these utilities at a
          reasonable and known expense.
          Council member Hyer asked if the money being considered in this budget amendment is just the first phase of the grant
award and if the City should expect to receive additional funding next year. Mr. Sorensen answered yes; Administration hopes to
receive an additional $4 million next year. Council member Hyer reported he participated in a site visit with Airport Manager
Garrett to become familiar with the conditions of the site where this CIP project will be performed. It is clear to him that this area
of the Airport needs attention, and he supports these amendments.

Proposed Ordinance 2021-13 to amend the budget in the amount of
$285,900 for increasing the salary ranges for building inspectors and plan
reviewers and recognizing revenue from the sale of fleet assets
          A memo from the Management Services Department regarding a proposed amendment recognizes revenue from the sale
of fleet assets and transfers that revenue to the CIP for use in the Fleet Replacement Program. The funds would be spread across
the departments to address fleet replacement needs. The amendment also transfers funds from the payroll contingency account to
the Building Services payroll account to cover additional payroll expenditures that will result from the proposed reclassifications
of three job categories (eight positions).

          Management Services Director Brown summarized the memo and stated the proposed budget amendment includes
adjustments to the FY21 Salary Schedule to increase the salary ranges for building inspectors and plan reviewers. It also transfers
funding from the salary contingency fund to cover the additional FY21 payroll costs of $8,050. The budget amendment also
recognizes revenue from the sale of fleet assets ($286,900) and appropriates the funds in the Capital hnprovement Plan for use in
the fleet replacement program.
          Council member Hyer referenced a bill passed in the 2021 Legislative Session that would allow developers to hire private
building inspectors to perform building inspections. He asked if the implications of that legislation are factored into this
adjustment. Ms. Brown stated it is her understanding that developers can only hire a private building inspector if the City does not
respond to the request for an inspection in a timely manner; Ogden City has always been responsive to inspections requests and
she does not think the new legislation will present an issue for the City.

Public Comments
          Heath Satow stated it has been over a month and he still has not received answers to the questions he asked about the real
estate transaction regarding the Brown Ice Cream building. This week he would like to ask which Council members have followed
up on his questions with City Administration and if they have any answers tonight. The only Council member to contact him
about this issue had no answers to share with him. He is only asking for accountability and honest answers should come quickly;
the length oftime it has taken to answer these simple questions is only making him more suspicious that something is very wrong.
He asked why it would take more than a month to come up with the truth and he indicated he will ask his questions for the third or
fourth time now: where are the listings that a broker would have created if he was actually working for the City and not the buyer;
why did Community and Economic Development Deputy Director Cooper tell the Council the broker was listing the building in
all the usual places; was Mr. Cooper misled or was he misleading the Council; do any of the Council members truly believe the
broker, whose professional career is evaluating, appraising, buying, and selling property botched the size of the building by over
50 percent and that Mr. Cooper did not know the real size of the building he was working to redevelop. He stated it is Mr.
Cooper's job to understand and evaluate commercial properties so that the Council does not have to. He asked how the
professional broker and Mr. Cooper got the valuation of the building so wrong and if there is any accountability for this failure.
He stated these questions matter as this action resulted in hundreds of thousands of taxpayer dollars being thrown away on this
project, and potentially millions more on other projects where selection processes are just as opaque. He asked why there is a
competitive bidding process for smaller projects, but not for a multi-million development; the system is wide open for corruption
and he is not getting any answers that make him feel more comfortable about it. There is one question the entire Council should be
able to answer right now; based on false data Mr. Cooper gave the Council regarding the value of the building, he asked the
Council if their decision to sell the building was influenced by the valuation. He stated that not one member of the Council has
answered this question and he asked if that is because they are embarrassed, they were misled; the residents are left with the
appearance that no one really cares that they were lied to and he refused to believe that is true. The citizens need the Council to
have the courage to seek the truth, even if it makes them uncomfortable. He understands that some property discussions are
private, but the Council needs to acknowledge that they have heard his questions and that they share his concerns. At the very
least the Council needs to tell him they are working on the issue and when they will have answers for him; the Council needs to
demand these answers from City Administration before any more movement is made on developing the property.
