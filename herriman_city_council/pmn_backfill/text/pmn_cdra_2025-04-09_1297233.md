     3. Discussion and Action Items
         3.1. Consideration of an amendment to the Tax Increment Participation Agreement
              with Elevated Acquisitions, LLC for The Commons at Herriman Towne Center
              project – Blake Thomas, Community Development Director
     Community Development Director Blake Thomas presented the proposed first amendment
     to the participation agreement with Elevated Acquisitions. He explained that the project
     location was at the corner near CVS on 13400 South and Mountain View Corridor. The
     original agreement was approved on December 11, 2024 as part of an incentive package for
     the Commons at Herriman Town Center project, including sales tax, impact fees, and tax
     increment financing from the CDA.

     Director Thomas noted that the amendment was requested by the developer to finalize their
     financing. The financial group they were working with wanted to guarantee that they would
     be able to receive the payment from the master developer with no issues in assigning the
     agreement over to them. He emphasized that it was a simple change but necessary for the
     project to move forward, with groundbreaking planned for mid-next month.

     Director Jared Henderson asked City Attorney Todd Sheeran if the City was protected in a
     worst-case scenario. Attorney Sheeran confirmed that this was a requirement from the
     developer's side and he didn't see any issues affecting the City or creating liability.

     Director Steve Shields expressed philosophical concerns about giving taxpayer money
     directly to the developer, noting that this approach wasn't taken for the auto mall project. He
     questioned whether there was any requirement that the assignment had to be to a tax-
     generating entity. Director Shields worried about CDA funds being directed or assigned to
     an entity that wasn't generating sales tax revenue for the City.

     Director Henderson acknowledged the concerns and noted the difficulty in renegotiating
     something that was already agreed upon. He explained that the agreement was set up to
     incentivize entities to come and produce sales tax, but developers could use the funds for
     various purposes, including infrastructure that doesn't directly produce sales tax.

     Attorney Sheeran clarified that the developer would remain the developer for the project,
     and the language was clear that it was just about how the reimbursements would be assigned.
