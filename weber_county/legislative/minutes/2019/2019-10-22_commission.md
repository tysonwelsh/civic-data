    3. PRESENTATION AND DISCUSSION OF THE INTENT TO LOWER THE COUNTY’S GENERAL OPERATIONS TAX
       LEVY BY $500,000 AND INCREASE THE UNINCORPORATED SERVICES FUND TAX LEVY BY $364,000.
       a. The purpose of the shift is to eliminate the financial subsidy currently being provided to the unincorporated
          residents from the County’s General Fund.
       b. With this shift, approximately 94% of county residents will see a tax decrease and 6% will see a tax increase.
       c. The unincorporated services fund tax on a $403,000 residence would increase from $29.26 to $58.52 per
          year, which represents a 100% increase in that tax line

         Scott Parke, County Comptroller, presented the proposed shift in the tax rate explaining this item in great
         detail. The county proposes to lower its general obligation tax rate by $500,000 and also increase the
         unincorporated services fund tax rate by $364,000. Mr. Parke said that 100% of county residents will
         receive a decrease in their Weber County General Fund tax line, and those who live in the unincorporated
         areas, which is about 6% of the population, will see a tax increase on their unincorporated services fund
         line. This is to eliminate a subsidy that is being provided to the unincorporated residents by the residents
         of all of the cities through the county’s General Fund and to correct this inequity. State code requires
         that the entire cost of providing services to unincorporated areas of the county are to be paid for by taxes
         or fees generated from the unincorporated areas. Mr. Parke showed all the cities tax rates in the county.
         In the proposal, every resident in the county (including unincorporated areas) will see a decrease in the
         Weber County tax line in their 2020 tax bill, 6% of the county residents (in unincorporated areas) will
         see an increase in their unincorporated services line—as an example for the latter, a $403,000 house will
         see a tax increase from $29.26 to $58.52, which is still lower than the lowest city in the county. The
         unincorporated residents are still paying an extremely low tax rate for the services they receive. To help
         address the earlier public comments, he and Ricky Hatch, County Clerk/Auditor, explained in some detail
         how taxes work in relation to property value increases, etc. Commissioner Harvey said that Visit Ogden
         reported yesterday that Airbnb collects taxes and remits taxes to the county and that VRBO will start on
         11/1/2019. John Ulibarri, County Assessor, outlined the process his office follows to value 95,000
         taxable properties. Chair Jenkins noted that property owners have the opportunity to appeal their property
         values annually. He said that the law went into effect in 2014 and the county started the Municipal
         Services Fund account, transferring money into it to make it whole. The county has kept track of services
         provided. The fund balance has decreased, and this year it will go broke because there is not enough
         money. He said that the tax rate should increase a little more than double but they could not see increasing
         it more than that at this time and will continue to monitor it. He invited questions but none were offered.
         There will be open houses on this item and a public hearing on the 2020 tentative budget in November.
