       A.     CONDITIONAL USE HOME OCCUPATION – “CRAWFORD SHORT-TERM
              RENTAL” LOCATED AT 4627 WEST SEMINOLE CIRCLE. APPLICANT,
              CALEB CRAWFORD

Chair Rushton opened the public hearing.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich, presented the staff report and stated that the request is for a
home occupation that is unique from similar requests. This short-term rental has its own
criteria in the ordinance. He explained that a short-term rental is similar to an Airbnb or
VRBO in that they are limited to a rental of up to 29 days in duration. The subject property
is located on the west side of the City in a cul-de-sac on Seminole Circle. The home has a
three-car garage and an extra-wide driveway that will accommodate four cars wide and two
cars deep. Mr. Prestwich commented that there appears to be adequate parking.

The property is in the Western Spring Subdivision which is zoned SPR-6 zone, which allows
up to six units per acre. The lot was estimated to be 7,000 square feet in size or .16-acre.
It was noted that short-term rentals are allowed conditional uses in all residential zones. The
criteria to be met by a short-term rental were reviewed. Mr. Prestwich described items to be
completed before a Conditional Use Permit can be issued. Staff recommended approval
with the conditions set forth in the staff report.

The public hearing was noticed to neighbors within 300 feet. Staff received no feedback.

Timing issues were discussed. City Attorney, Ryan Carter, stated that certain items are
pending at the Conditional Use phase that can be reasonably be fulfilled and completed
within a certain amount of time. It is reasonable to recommend approval, which can be

In response to a question raised by Commissioner Brown, Mr. Prestwich stated that the
applicant can choose to rent a portion of or the entire home. The applicant is not required

In response to a question raised, Mr. Carter stated that Code Section 18.190.051 specifies
that a short-term rental property must be owner-occupied and the business owner’s primary
residence. The applicant must show that this is his primary residence. It is not, however,
necessary for the homeowner to reside in the home simultaneously while someone else is
renting it.

Commissioner Rushton described the differences between an Accessory Dwelling Unit
(“ADU”) and a Short-Term Rental. He noted that anything other than the two would be a
traditional rental. His understanding was that either would require the owner to be present.
It was confirmed by staff that with an ADU the owner is required to be present.

Commissioner Lefgren commented that there is a difference between someone who simply
rents their home out and someone who lives on the property and rents out a portion of it.
Mr. Carter confirmed that the Code specifies that the City shall only permit an ADU when an
owner occupant lives on the property within in either the principal or accessory dwelling unit.
Exceptions were reviewed. Contact numbers must be provided to neighbors within 300 feet.

Chair Rushton felt that based on the applicant’s statement, this is not their primary
residence.

Commissioner Lefgren moved to recommend APPROVAL of PLZ-21-2031 “Crawford
short-term rental” located at 4627 West Seminole Circle with the following conditions:

       1.     The site, structures, and use shall remain in compliance with any and all
              applicable Riverton City standards and ordinances, specifically the City
              Home Occupation Ordinance (18.190) and applicable Building and Fire
              Codes.

       2.     Short-term renal must be operated within the rule and regulations for

       B.    CONDITIONAL USE PRIVATE LANE – “SMART PRIVATE LANE”
             LOCATED AT 1602 WEST 13595 SOUTH. APPLICANT, DAVID SMART.

Chair Rushton opened the public hearing.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the above request is for a
Conditional Use for the “Smart Private Lane” located at 1602 West 13595 South with access
granted off of Redwood Road. The proposal will not increase any lots and will be completed
with existing parcels. The request was noticed to the neighbors and no comments or
feedback were received. A property rendering was displayed. Mr. Prestwich reported that
the proposed parcel will be accomplished through a parcel line adjustment and the
applicants are requesting permission from the Planning Commission to provide access
through the new private lane. Private lanes are allowed in single-family zones by Conditional
Use when they meet two of the following:

      The property is a lot of record that is pre-existing and has no frontage or adequate
       property to construct a public street or it can be demonstrated the property cannot be
       physically subdivided with a public street.
      The development on the private lane will not impede the extension of any adjacent
       public right of way now or in the foreseeable future.
      The development does not impede the necessary access from the adjoining
       properties as required by the Master Transportation Plan.

Mr. Prestwich stated that if the Planning Commission approves the private lane through
Conditional Use, the applicant will be required to work through the parcel line adjustment
and site plan with Staff. The matter was noticed and staff recommended approval with
conditions set forth in the staff report.

Access issues were next discussed. Mr. Carter stated that if there is a desire to further
explore access, he recommended it be tabled to a later meeting to allow staff to present a
more thorough report. He confirmed that there is more than one owner that shares the
private lane requiring the applicant to seek approval. Mr. Prestwich confirmed that the
property is owned by two owners. Conditions of approval were reviewed.

The applicant, David Smart, stated that the existing private lane already contains the
maximum five lots per the City requirements. The parcel of land where the private lane is
located measures 25 feet wide, five feet wider than the required 20 feet. He confirmed that
he is the owner of both parcels and is requesting the adjustment to allow both to be usable.
Approval is required by the City prior to requesting access from the Utah Department of
Transportation (“UDOT”).

Commissioner Cannon moved to recommend APPROVAL of PLZ-21-2028 “Smart

                    non-conforming condition on the property and meets the

the property rights are already firmly established in the surrounding properties. He did not
see another way of routing a private lane that does not present the same problem explored
previously. The Conditional Use approval does not add or take away pressure from UDOT
as to whether they grant access.

Commissioner Cannon believed the worst-case scenario would be for UDOT to grant a
single access moving the existing driveway to the south.

Mr. Smart stated that the adjacent parcel is owned by Shawn Larsen who made an offer to
purchase the property. He originally desired a lot line adjustment to expand the property.
Mr. Larsen prefers his parcel not be split separating it from the adjacent parcel. An aerial
view of the property was displayed.

Mr. Carter stated that UDOT would be wise to recognize that granting access when the
zoning is at the current density was a better way to proceed.

3.       DECISION ITEMS

         A.     FINDINGS OF FACT – MCDOUGAL CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT.

Mr. Carter reported that as Riverton City Attorney he got involved mid-stream on the
McDougal Conditional Use Permit application. He received a Notice of Appeal from
Mr. McDougal that is pending before the Board of Adjustment. He presented a document
detailing the reasoning behind the denial of the Conditional Use Permit. The record includes
a transcript of what was said by each individual at the meeting. After reading it, he believed
the applicant will always bear the burden of persuasion on deciding whether the criteria for
a private lane have been met. There has not been enough information before the Planning
Commission to justify the private lane under the circumstances of this case. He explained
that when private lanes are considered, the three criteria include the following:

        The property is a lot of record that is pre-existing and has no frontage or adequate
         property to construct a public street or it can be demonstrated that the property cannot
         be physically subdivided with the public street.

        The development on the private lane would not impede the extension of any adjacent
         public right-of-way now or in the foreseeable future.

        The development does not impede the necessary access from the adjoining
         properties as required by the Master Transportation Plan.

Mr. Carter stated that the evidence shows that the road in question where the applicant was
looking to tie in has plenty of frontage to put in a private or public roadway. He believed the
third criteria is not an issue that goes against the developer as the surrounding property has
been developed. In Subsection B, he believed the applicant needs to return and
demonstrate that the property cannot be physically subdivided with public streets and that
development on a private lane will not impede the extension of any adjacent public right of
way either now or in the foreseeable future. The question was not if the Planning
Commission got it right but if the Planning Commission had enough information to get it
right, which falls back to the developer.

When Chair Rushton mentioned Subsection B, the applicant was not directly responsive to
the question. Instead of addressing whether it can be demonstrated that the property cannot
be subdivided with public streets, he reminded the Planning Commission that there is
preamble language in the first part of Subsection 1. It was argued that due to the
impracticality of the property, they should be entitled to a private lane. He believed the
applicant used that practicality as an answer to Chair Rushton’s question. He explained that
the point he intends to make before the Board of Adjustment is that by stating that there is
an impracticality that does not exempt the applicant from the requirement of providing
information proving that he meets the criteria. The argument was that not all of the
information has been provided. As a result, the Planning Commission was not comfortable
making a decision.

Mr. Carter emphasized the need for transparency and an argument based on the public
record. The purpose was to place the Findings of Fact before the Planning Commission to
allow them to make a determination and assist the Board of Adjustment in narrowing down
the issues for review. He stated that the Findings of Fact document is not necessary in
requiring approval of the Planning Commission. It is not explicitly required in the Code but
he believed it will be helpful when making a presentation to the Board of Adjustment.

Chair Rushton was not comfortable signing the document and believed the Commission did
a thorough job of vetting the issues and asking questions of the applicant. Their decision
was documented as part of the public hearing and he understood that the record stands on
own merits. If access to Redwood Road is possible, he suggested exploring that option
further. Mr. Carter questioned UDOT’s willingness to grant an additional access when there
is one in such close proximity. He suggested crafting a Development Agreement.

Commissioner Breinholt revisited the meeting where the issue was discussed and was
asked by the Council to reopen a motion previously made without having the full Commission
present. He felt that their actions should have been based on the City’s policy and
recommendation as opposed to documented policy. He asked if the Commission can be
held accountable individually in civil court for any decision they make either individually or
as a Planning Commission body that is contrary to what is documented by City policy.
Mr. Carter agreed to discuss those issues in a Closed Session. He suggested reviewing the
audio from the meeting as the minutes are intended to serve as a summary of the meeting
rather than a transcript. He reiterated that the Board of Adjustment’s decision will be based
solely on the effort presented and the determination of whether that effort is sufficient.
