 Time. The purpose of the Public Comment Time is to allow citizen’s access to the Council. Citizens requesting to
 address the Council will be asked to complete a written request form and present it to the City Recorder. In general,
 the Chairman will allow an individual three minutes to address the Council. A spokesman, recognized as
 representing a group in attendance, may be allowed up to five minutes. Comments which cannot be made within
 these time limits should be submitted in writing to the City Recorder prior to noon the day before the meeting so
 they can be copied and distributed to the Council. At the conclusion of the Citizen Comment time, the Chairman

                                    RESOLUTION NO. 2026-03

                  A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF HOLLADAY
                      AMENDING THE COUNCIL’S INTERNAL POLICIES


       WHEREAS, the City Council of the City of Holladay pursuant to the requirements
of Utah State law, has previously adopted Internal Policies for the Council to govern the
conduct of its meetings; and

       WHEREAS, the City Council of the City of Holladay finds that it is in the public interest
and will promote the efficient functioning of the Council to revise the Council’s Internal Policies;

       NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the City Council of the City of Holladay as
follows:

       Section 1. Adoption. Section 4.1 of the Internal Policies for the City Council of the City
of Holladay is hereby amended to read as follows:

       Section 4.1. Members of the Council may elect to use their salary to participate
       in benefits offered to City employees, such as medical, dental, insurance and “Section
       125 Plan”, subject to the rules and regulations thereof.

       Section 2. Adoption. Section 4.3 of the Internal Policies for the City Council of the City
of Holladay is hereby amended to read as follows:

       Section 4.3. Council Members may also participate in the City’s non-contributory
       retirement plan, an elective 401(K)/457 retirement plan on the same basis as City
       employees. City Council members who join the Council after July 1, 2011, and who have
       not previously been vested as Tier 1 employees in the URS system are not eligible for
       participation in the URS system. City Council Members who join the Council after July
       1, 2011, and who are vested in Tier 1 system are also entitled to system contributions on
       their behalf by the City on the basis set forth in state law and URS Rules and Regulations


       Section 3. Severability. If any section, part or provision of this Resolution is held invalid
or unenforceable, such invalidity or unenforceability shall not affect any other portion of this
Resolution, and all sections, parts and provisions of this Resolution shall be severable.

       Section 4. Effective Date. This Resolution shall become effective immediately upon its
approval by the City Council.

MEETING DATE: January 22, 2026
SUBJECT: Bike Signage and Marking Policy
SUBMITTED BY: Jared Bunch, P.E., Public Services Director



The city’s traffic engineering consultant, Joe Perrin, PhD, PE, has counseled the city to establish a
bike signage and marking guidance policy to reduce liability in the event of an accident. The
objective of the policy is to establish consistency throughout the city and document it. Dr. Perrin
was asked to prepare the guidance policy. His deliverable is enclosed. The first four pages of the
guidance policy provides a written description of the types of signs and markings and placement
locations. Pages 5-24 are figures of how they apply to our streets, focusing on signalized
intersections. The guidance policy will also assist city staff with responding to citizen requests for
additional signs and markings.
November 30, 2025




                                                             TRANSPORTATION ENGINEERING


RE: Bike Route Policy - Holladay, UT


Possible Code Language / Guidance:

Bike Lane and Bike Route Guidance

Definition:

Shared Bike Route is a designated roadway where the bicyclists DO NOT have a specific designated area
for their travel and therefore used a shared lane with vehicles or in the shoulder area where parking may or
may not be allowed.

Bike Lanes are defined as have a separate striped area specifically designated for bike usage and protected
from parked vehicles by “No Parking Signs” The minimum width for a bike lane is generally 4 feet (1.2 m)
on roads without a curb and 5 feet (1.5 m) on roads with a curb or adjacent parking.


Signage

Shared Bike Route Signage
     The recommended spacing for bike route sign (D11-1) is within 50 feet of the start of the bike
        route or immediately after a signalized intersection.
     The D11-1 signage should be placed every 2 to 3 blocks on-streets when no turns are necessary to
        reassure cyclists they are on the correct path.
     If a Bike Route changes direction, then a direction Bike Route sign needs to indicate the turn
        required on the approach street and a Bike Route Sign on the acceptance street.
     Shared Bike Routes should include the R9-20 sign “Allowed to Use Full Lane” and placed within
        50 feet after a signalized intersection and then spaced along the running sections of the road no
        more than 500 feet.

       Signs should be visible and legible from the previous sign. Therefore, signs should be placed no

       The first signs should be placed no further than 50 feet downstream of a signalized intersection
        where there is an existing bike lane.



Bike Striping

Shared Bike Routes
The typical striping for Shared Bike Routes, a Shared Lane Marking (SLM) is centered in the travel lane to
ensure the driver knows that the bike may use the entire lane.




Shared Bike Route Striping Spacing
     The first shared-lane marking downstream from an intersection should be placed no more than 50
        feet from the intersection.

       Place Shared Lane Markings no more than 300 feet apart
       The Shared Lane Marking should not be placed on roadways that have a speed limit above 35
        mph.
       Shared-lane markings shall not be used in shoulders.
       Green-colored pavement shall not be applied as a background to shared-lane markings



Bike Lane Striping
The Bike Lane Striping is to provide drivers and bicyclist with a clear indication of where the bicyclists
should be located on the asphalt which is NOT within the travel lane as on a shared bike route.




       The first Bike Lane marking downstream from an intersection should be placed no more than 50
        feet from the intersection centered within the bike lane. If there is a separate bike lane throughout
        the approach lane then that should be located within 50 feet of the approach stop bar.
       Bike lane markings should be placed after major driveways and intersections, and at least every
        500 feet (150 m) along the lane
       At signalized intersection, green pavement marking under the Bike Lane Marking should be
        applied on the approach, transition and departure but this does replace the white striping
        requirements of the auxiliary lane lines.

Green Background Paint
At signalized intersections, the use of green pavement marking is reserved to highlight and emphasize the
conflict points and be more visible than simple white striping. The use of green pavement marking is
optional but should be applied consistently throughout the City at signalized intersections.

Signalized Intersection Treatment
Each intersection must be evaluated for the lane geometry, the route direction, speed limit, etc. The typical
application will include highlighting the bike lane symbol on the approach, through any transition where
the right turn movement and bicycle conflicts will occur and an emphasis of the acceptance lane bike lane
location.




The 16 signalized intersection located within Holladay City were reviewed for their characteristics and a
recommendation is provided for the striping emphasis using the green paint.

Once the policy is adopted, the City should include an inventory and review of the running sections of each
Bike Lane and Shared Bike Route for a signage and striping review to determine what changes are needed
to provide consistent striping and signage throughout the City.
