OPENING:
     Mayor Randall called the meeting to order and welcomed all in attendance. The

DISCUSSION REGARDING THE FISCAL YEAR 2021-2022 BUDGET:
     City Manager Adam Lenhard explained staff approached this budget as fiscally
     conservative as possible. This is a catch-up year, meaning a number of the projects
     cut out of the current year’s budget are proposed in the fiscal year 2021-2022
     budget. He outlined the proposed changes to the City’s organization chart included
     in the preliminary budget.

      Budget and Financial Planning Manager Deanna Brklacich provided a handout and
      outlined the City Manager recommended new personnel and promotion requests.
      After being asked by Councilmember Curtis, Ms. Brklacich explained that the City’s
      cost for healthcare benefits for employees has increased 4% and 5.1%, depending
      on the type of plan after which she explained the types of plans the City offers.
      She stated that the total budget is approximately $415 million, up by approximately
      $110 million, a 36% increase from last year. The main reason for the increase is
      due to the following: projects that were put on hold due to Covid are now being
      funded; $2 million for the purchase of additional cemetery property (site to be
      determined); $16 million in ARP Covid money; $12 million for the Wastewater
      Treatment Plan facility; $11 million in capital works project funds; $1.25 million for
      the facility services building; and $1.3 million for the clubhouse at St. George Golf.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard noted a few years ago, there were some citizens who
      were vocal regarding the budget. Ms. Brklacich met with these residents and
      outlined the budget, staff has not heard from them since.

      Councilmember Hughes pointed out that those that have complained and compared
      the City’s budget with other municipalities, they do not realize that St. George offers
      more services than other cities the same size.
City Manager Adam Lenhard noted when comparing taxes, St. George’s are fairly low
compared to other cities similar in size.

Ms. Brklacich spoke about sales tax; things are going well. The hope is that the
trend continues.

Fire Chief Robert Stoker outlined the proposed budget for the Fire Department
including personnel, equipment needs, bids for Station 9, and additional PPE for fire
personnel.

Police Chief Kyle Whitehead outlined the proposed budget for the Police Department
including capital outlays and equipment needs, personnel, and taser replacements.
He then spoke about the request for a community services officer to respond to
citizens walking into the office and additional full-time evidence and property
employee. With regards to training of officers, he believes there are additional needs
as he does not feel the training has been to the level he would like.

Mayor Randall asked for a list of training that has been approved and taken out of
the requested budget. She stated that she would like to limit officers responding to
minor thefts. Instead, she would like to see those types of calls be handled
differently so that it does not take an officer away from patrol or more serious calls.

Chief Whitehead explained they have an online reporting system; but it needs
improvements.

Water Services Director Scott Taylor outlined the proposed budgets for Water
Distribution, Water Services Administration, Irrigation, Wastewater Collections, and
Wastewater Treatment Plant including projects, secondary irrigation, culinary water
projects, proposed pass increases, personnel, and Phase 2 expansion of the
treatment plant.

Mayor Randall called for a five-minute recess.

Administrative Services Director Trevor Coombs outlined the proposed Administrative
Services budget including personnel, workload, update on 311 service, and
technology.

Support Services Director Marc Mortensen noted Golf revenues are up approximately
$1.2 million. He provided a handout and outlined the proposed increase to fees for
the Golf Division as well as the improvements and expenses the proposed increase
will help fund. He spoke about why they do not charge resident versus non-resident
fees. He then outlined the proposed budgets for Technology Services, and Facilities
Maintenance including cyber security, personnel, projects, and equipment needs. He
provided updates on the Facilities Maintenance building, SunBowl improvements, and
the demolition of the St. George Inn.

City Attorney Shawn Guzman outlined the proposed budget for the Legal Department
including personnel.
      The Mayor and Council moved to the 2450 East Park for the remainder of the
      meeting.
