        The purpose of the joint work session was to review the agendas for the Special City Council, Redevelopment Agency
(RDA), and Municipal Building Authority (MBA) meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.; discuss the proposed Fiscal Year
(FY) 2025-2026 City budget, including a general discussion of the proposed budget and Schedule A-1, the Council’s budget
message, and proposed goals and guidelines; proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 year end budget amendments for the City and
RDA; discuss Council/Board/Trustee business; and hear Council/Board/Trustee comments.

MBA meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

          Proposed Budget Message: Communications Director Garside explained the proposed budget message aims to simplify
the complexities of Ogden City’s budget, making it more comprehensible. The FY26 Budget Message draft is subject to change
based on the final adopted budget. The final draft will ultimately be hosted on ClearGov.com alongside all documentation
pertaining to the FY26 Final Budget in a user-friendly format and will contain more data. He provided an overview of the draft
document.
          Proposed Goals and Guidelines: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith facilitated Council’s review of the proposed
changes to the Ogden City budget guidelines for FY 2025-2026. Amendments included a statement indicating the City is in the
process of updating its Strategic Plan, but the 2019 version of the Plan will be used until the update is complete; technical changes
to update the budget year; a statement that audited information will be reported with the Annual Comprehensive Financial Report
for the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) budget; and shifts in budget priorities identified by the Council for each department of the
City.
          Council discussion regarding the budget goals and guidelines centered on making further adjustments to the section
dealing with community safety responsive to the public survey conducted regarding the topic; trail improvement projects included
in the FY 2025-2026 budget; moving the development of a comprehensive financial sustainability model from the ‘additional
budget goals’ section to a concrete budget priority; and prioritizing the creation of a ‘dashboard’ tool that the Council can access
to get information about the status of different projects or initiatives;

Proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Year End Budget Amendments
         Finance Director Sorensen explained the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 budget amendments will increase the
General Fund to fund several programs and events, recognize revenue in the Capital Improvement Fund and Utility Funds for
grant funding from various state, federal, and RAMP grants, and adjust Internal Service Funds to recognize charges to other city
departments for Fleet/Facilities and IT services. The total budget amendment increases the Fiscal Year 2024- 2025 budget by
$4,689,300. Mr. Sorensen used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the source and use of funds involved in the
budget amendment; for a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Throughout his presentation, Mr. Sorensen engaged in discussion with the Council regarding allowable uses of the
various grant funds identified in the budget amendment and the lack of public engagement for some of the projects scheduled to
be funded with grant funds.

Redevelopment Agency:
Proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Year End Budget Amendments
          Finance Director Sorensen explained the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 RDA budget amendment recognizes
revenue to the Adams Community Reinvestment Area (CRA) from bond proceeds ($3,358,000). The bond proceeds will be used
to fund issuance costs ($118,000) and the remaining amount due on the purchase of the Forest Service Building ($3,240,000). A
prior budget amendment authorized a reimbursement to the city of $360,000—the down payment-- from bond proceeds. The full
amount of bond proceeds totaled $3,718,000—the purchase price of $3,600,000 plus the issuance costs ($118,000).

Council/Board/Trustee Business
         Council Calendar: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reviewed the Council calendar for the summer months.
         Vice Chair Hyer asked that Council include information on the calendar regarding the tour events offered by the Weber
Basin Water Conservancy District (WBWCD). This led to a brief discussion regarding different programs offered by the District.
         Business Licensing Process and Small Business Loan Programs: Council member Choberka stated she participated in a
meeting a few weeks ago with some small business owners to listen to their concerns about the City’s licensing process and small
business loan programs. She communicated those concerns to the Council, including lack of communication regarding the number
of steps a small business owner must take and the different entities they need to secure approval from in order to open their
business and minimal information about landscape requirements or infrastructure improvements that must occur as part of the
licensure process. The group suggested the City offer a business infrastructure loan with a lower interest rate to help businesses
tackle costs that are not often built into a business plan. She thought that was a good idea and asked the Council to consider it.
Small business owners also feel some tension from the Ogden Downtown Alliance (ODA) and that the alliance is not necessarily
advocating for them through their partnership with the City. The Council discussed the ideas raised by the group of small business
owners and indicated they felt the input was valuable and they would take the recommendations under advisement. They also
received input from Chief Administrative Officer Brown about efforts to streamline and improve processes overseen by the
Business Information Center (BIC).

Council/Board/Trustee Comments
