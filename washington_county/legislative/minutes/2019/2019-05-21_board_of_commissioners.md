PRESENTATION: DIXIE REGIONAL MEDICAL CENTER GIFT TO THE
COMMUNITY PRESENTATION

Brian Chadaz, Chairman of the Dixie Regional Medical Center Board, explained that the
Dixie Regional Medical Center is a not-for-profit hospital, with the mission statement of
‘helping people live the healthiest life possible.’ The hospital brought in approximately
$500 million dollars to Washington County in 2018, and is the largest employer in the
County.

The property tax the hospital would have paid on its exempt property in 2018, were it not
exempt, is estimated to be approximately $4.9 million. The total community gift includes
programs and services that improve health, increased access to care for the uninsured,
eliminate barriers, and relieve government burden. The total gift to the community is
valued at $331,463,445.

Chairman Cox questioned if anything is being done in the hospital to reduce the impact of
the opioid epidemic.

Mitch Cloward, Hospital Administrator mentioned that the hospital prescribed 30% less
opioids in 2018. Mr. Cloward also mentioned that healthcare clinic hubs are being
constructed throughout the county to provide healthcare closer to where people live.

REPORT ON PROTEST COUNT AND CONSIDERATION OF A RESOLUTION
APPROVING THE HARMONY HEIGHTS PAVING SPECIAL ASSESSMENT
AREA

Washington County Clerk/Auditor Kim Hafen explained that multiple letters were received
from property owners within the Harmony Heights Subdivision both in favor and in
opposition of the paving project. A total of nineteen (19) letters of opposition were
received, which accounted for 42.22% of the residents. According to State Code, if a
threshold of forty percent (40%) in opposition is met, then the project cannot move
forward.

Don Driscoll, a resident in Harmony Heights mentioned that he was for the paving project
and questioned how the County verified the authenticity of the letters of opposition that
were received.

Mr. Hafen explained that he had all of the letters and they could be obtained from him
through a GRAMA request.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Almquist Not to Move Forward with the
Harmony Heights Paving Special Assessment Area, Due to the
Opposition Demonstrated. Motion seconded by Commissioner Iverson
and carried by unanimous vote, with Commissioners Cox, Iverson, and

Almquist voting aye.

CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE _ NO. _2019-1137-O, AN ORDINANCE
REZONING _A__ PORTION OF VEYO FROM _ THE C-3_ (GENERAL
COMMERCIAL) ZONE TO THE RA-1 (RESIDENTIAL AGRICULTURAL 1

ACRE MINIMUM LOT SIZE) ZONE

Community Development Director Scott Messel explained that a parcel of property has
been purchased with the intent to build a home which is located within a commercially
zoned area in Veyo. A ten (10) foot buffer must be in place in order to separate residential

from commercial.

In order to build a home on the property, the zone must be changed from the C-3 zone to
the RA-1 zone. The Planning Commission voted to deny the zone change.

Rachel Henderson, representing the applicant explained that the applicants were given the
setbacks before making an offer on the property and were under the impression that they
would be able to build a home as well as keep horses.
