ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 21-41, AMEND SECTIONS 7-1-103 AND 7-6-
201 AND ENACT SECTION 7-7-115.5 OF THE WEST VALLEY CITY
MUNICIPAL CODE TO AMEND CERTAIN REQUIREMENTS
APPLICABLE TO ACCESSORY DWELLING UNITS
Steve Pastorik, CED Director, discussed proposed Ordinance 21-41 that would
amend Sections 7-1-103 and 7-6-201 and enact Section 7-7-115.5 of the West
Valley City Municipal Code to amend certain requirements applicable to accessory
dwelling units.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
information as follows:

       City staff is proposing an ordinance amendment to add regulations for
       internal accessory dwelling units. HB 82 was passed by the Utah
       Legislature in the 2021 General Session of the Utah Legislature and later
       signed by the governor. A copy of this bill is included with this issue
       paper. Among other changes, the bill “requires municipalities and counties
       to classify certain accessory dwelling units as a permitted land use” and
       “prohibits municipalities and counties from establishing restrictions or
       requirements for certain accessory dwelling units with limited
       exceptions”. In summary, cities must now allow internal accessory
       dwelling units within single family dwellings and the State now limits the
       extent of local regulation of internal accessory dwelling units.

       The proposed ordinance incorporates all the internal accessory dwelling
       unit regulations allowed under HB 82. If this proposed ordinance or one
       like it is not adopted, the City would be required to allow internal
       accessory dwellings units without regulations on things like lot size or
       parking.

Councilmember Lang asked if the tax rate is adjusted in rental units. Wayne replied
that he believes the exemption would still apply since the home must be owner
occupied. Councilmember Lang stated that some Cities turn in rental units so they
can be taxed higher. Wayne replied that he would need to see examples but noted
the County makes the final determination, not the City. Councilmember Buhler
asked if this bill would give the City any more teeth in regulating illegal ADU’s or
rentals. Steve replied that enforcement is difficult since the City needs to have
access to the home or proof that illegal separation or renting is occurring. Steve
stated that the bill also changes building code standards. The Mayor and Council
discussed Airbnb’s, rental of rooms, and renting an entire home. Steve stated the
home must be occupied by the owner in this scenario. Councilmember Lang stated
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JUNE 22, 2021
                          -7-


     under this bill, residents can separate the basement and upstairs of a single-family
     home legally. Steve replied yes. Mayor Bigelow stated that this would be like
     turning a home into a duplex. Steve replied yes and noted that the City cannot
     regulate that any longer. Councilmember Fitisemanu asked if noticing is done for
     the change. Steve replied individual notices to the public are not provided but a
     notice is posted to the Public Notice Website and on the City’s website. Mayor
     Bigelow noted that converting a home to an ADU is very expensive and to recover
     the cost through rent would take years. He indicated that he feels the changes
     implemented by this Legislative Bill will impact a fairly low number. Steve replied
     that there are some City’s that currently allow ADU’s and were not seeing a large
     number of permits.

     Proposed Ordinance 21-42 related to the proposal to be considered by the City

     ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 21-42, AMEND CHAPTER 7-11 AND
     SECTIONS 7-2-126, 7-6-204, 7-6-401, AND 7-14-701 OF THE WEST
     VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO AMEND CERTAIN
     REQUIREMENTS APPLICABLE TO RESIDENTIAL BUILDING DESIGN
     Steve Pastorik, CED Director, discussed proposed Ordinance 21-42 that would
     amend Chapter 7-11 and sections 7-2-126, 7-6-204, 7-6-401, and 7-14-701 of the
     West Valley City Municipal Code to amend certain requirements applicable to
     residential building design.

     Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
     information as follows:
MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JUNE 22, 2021
                     -8-


   HB 1003 was passed by the Utah Legislature in the 2021 1st Special Session of
   the Utah Legislature and later signed by the governor. A copy of this bill is
   included with this issue paper. Among other changes, the bill prohibits cities
   from imposing building design elements on a one to two family dwelling.
   Exceptions to this prohibition include:

          Building design elements agreed to under a development agreement
          A land use regulation, including a planned unit development or
           overlay zone, that a property owner requests:
               o the municipality to apply to the owner’s property and
               o in exchange for an increase in density or other benefit not
                  otherwise available as a permitted use in the zoning area or
                  district.

   The vast majority of remaining vacant ground planned for single unit dwellings
   either already has a development agreement or would need to be rezoned and
   would therefore become subject to a development agreement. The City can still
   require building design elements in these instances through a development
   agreement.

   The following is a list of the building design elements from HB 1003 that are
   prohibited: exterior color; type or style of exterior cladding material; style,
   dimensions, or materials of a roof structure, roof pitch, or porch; exterior
   nonstructural architectural ornamentation; location, design, placement, or
   architectural styling of a window or door; location, design, placement or
   architectural styling of a garage door, not including a rear-loading garage door;
   number or type of rooms; interior layout of a room; minimum square footage
   over 1,000 square feet, not including a garage; rear yard landscaping
   requirements; minimum building dimensions; and a requirement to install front
   yard fencing.

   The proposed ordinance change removes from the City’s Zoning Ordinance any
   reference to building design elements as defined by State law.

Councilmember Buhler verified that existing Development Agreements must be
met and new zone changes can have a Development Agreement linked as well but
homes with existing residential zoning in place can disregard City standards. Steve
replied that the City would have very limited options in these types of scenarios
under this new bill. Councilmember Buhler stated that that if someone wants to
build under existing zoning, such as A, they can build to these lesser standards that
the City has no way to regulate. Steve replied yes.

     RESOLUTION 21-07: APPROVE AMENDMENT NO. 4 TO THE
     AGREEMENT FOR DISPOSITION OF LAND FOR PRIVATE
     DEVELOPMENT WITH HEXCEL CORPORATION
     Jeff Jackson, CED, presented proposed Resolution 21-07 that would approve
     Amendment No. 4 to the Agreement for Disposition of Land for Private Development
     with Hexcel Corporation.

     Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
     information as follows:

            In October of 2020, the RDA became aware that Hexcel was considering
            moving its HQ & R&D facility from California to another location. During
            a quick evaluation of Hexcel’s WVC location, the RDA determined the site
            has enough additional acreage to accommodate this new facility. The RDA
            saw an opportunity to attract the new project which consists of $25M of
            capital investment along with 115-150 jobs with salaries between $130-
            180,000 per year.

            Hexcel currently has a tax increment finance (TIF) agreement in place to
            capture up to $18.7M of over $350M of capital investment. The RDA
            proposes to simply raise the ceiling from $18.7M to $20M and give Hexcel
            the opportunity to try and capture an additional $1.3M of post-performance
      MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JUNE 22, 2021
                           -13-


              tax increment. Up to date, Hexcel has captured $10M of its potential
              $18.7M max.

              Additional TIF captured by Hexcel will serve to offset additional
              underground infrastructure costs needed for the research & development
              portion of its facility along with interior access improvements to the site in
              order accommodate the structure to be built. This is an important detail to
              make sure the West Valley City site is competitive with the other proposed
              sites.

              The opportunity for Hexcel to capture an additional $1.3M represents less
              than a 50% pass through to Hexcel.
