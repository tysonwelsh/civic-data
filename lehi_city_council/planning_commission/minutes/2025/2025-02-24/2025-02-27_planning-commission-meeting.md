3.2) Public hearing and recommendation of Lydon Dangerfield’s request for review of the
KAZ Design Build Zone Change from RA-1 (residential/agriculture) to R-1-Flex
(residential) on 0.99 acres located at 850 North 1200 East.

Jacob Curtis presented the item. The change is consistent with the general plan. The applicant
plans to make a flag lot in the future.

Commissioner Jackson joined the meeting at approximately 7:15. He asked if there was
sufficient frontage for a flag lot. Curtis explained that if the zone change goes through, the
reduced frontage should be sufficient.

Commissioner Kunze asked about the structure that was currently on the property. A citizen in
attendence clarified that it was a workshop.

The applicant, Lydon Dangerfield, was present. He explained his plans to remove the garage on
the current home to provide sufficient setback for a driveway on the flag lot. He plans on tearing
down the shop when construction on the second home begins.

Commissioner Everett opened public comment.

Stacey Bailey lives near the lot. She is against the creation of a flag lot due to increased traffic on
her street. She worries that she will lose her right to agricultural zoning if a precedent is set and
wants to maintain the right to have animals on her property. She mentioned that increased
development has resulted in wild animals being pushed out of their homes and onto her property,
leading to deaths in her flock of chickens.

Mike Bailey, Stacey’s husband, spoke next. He mentioned that multiple lots around their
property have already been turned into flag lots. He shares concerns about animals being forced
out of their habitats. He is also concerned about the possibility of more than one home being
added to the lot. He represents an irrigation company that owns the ditch running through the
property. He asked that it be piped as there are no water rights on the lot.

Jason Adamson spoke for himself and on behalf of his parents who are also neighbors of the lot.
He doesn’t like that the new zoning would sandwich the lot between two agricultural properties.
He mentioned that the existing fencing requirements for lots adjacent to agricultural properties
can’t stand up against larger animals like cows. He also shared his concerns about additional
traffic. He feels as though the city’s traffic engineers don’t come at peak traffic times, and that
their estimates don’t represent the reality of the roads.

Lane Adamson, Jason’s brother, is a Lehi resident. He stated that the purpose of the R-1-Flex
zone in the code is to create diversity in the community. He feels like the neighborhood has
enough small lots already without the addition of a flag lot.

Keith lives directly north of the property and has been there for thirty years. He worries that he
will lose privacy if additional houses are built on the lot.

Linda Peterson has parents who built a house in the area. She shared complaints about noise and
privacy if the area continues to be developed.

Colleen, Keith’s wife, has family ties to the area. She is concerned about traffic issues. She
frequently sees people run red lights and knows, personally, at least one person who has been the
victim of an accident in the area.

Jason Adamson came back up to the stand briefly to mention irrigation rights on the property and
share his worries about potential floods that may come from regrading the lot.

Commissioner Everett closed public comment.

Jacob Curtis addressed concerns about subdividing the property further by explaining that there
is not the space to add more than one house. Commissioner Everett asked about the possibility of
ADUs being added to the property. Curtis admitted that the addition of at least one ADU would
be technically possible for each house on the lot, but that there would be a lot of factors, and it
would be a long process. Commissioner Kunze asked about a pair of nearby lots that have three
houses on what looks to be about the same lot size. Kim Struthers confirmed that several flag lots
have been approved in the area. He went on to explain the concept of ADUs to the gathered
citizens and explained that they are available to everyone in the neighborhood.

Brad Kenison addressed traffic concerns. He explained that the roads are designed to handle
about 16,000 cars a day, and that the current count is slightly below 10,000 a day. He admits that
there is a lot of traffic, but the maximum threshold is not being reached. Kenison also addressed
irrigation concerns. Jason Adamson, in attendence, stated that the ditch had been previously
worked on. Kenison explained that other properties are required to take care of their own piping.
Agricultural fencing would also be required. He mentioned that this is the first time he’s heard a
complaint about the city’s standards for agricultural fencing.

Lydon Dangerfield returned to the stand to address concerns. He explained that as his lot is less
than an acre, there is no way with the current city code to subdivide it beyond the flag lot he is
intending to make. He has no desire to build any additional units. He expressed his willingness to
pipe the ditch and fence the property. He asked if the fences currently up would meet city
standards. Brad Kenison let him know that it is unlikely. Dangerfield also mentioned that as he
only intends to put in one single-family home, it won’t impact local traffic much.

Commissioner Roberts agreed with the present citizenry that traffic is bad in the area but agrees
with Dangerfield that one more house won’t add much. He also sympathizes with concerns over
animals being disturbed by construction. Commissioner Jones mentioned that he sympathizes
with both the neighbors and the applicant. He stated that since the zone change is in compliance
with the general code, the commission ought to give a positive recommendation, especially
considering the ruling on the previous item. Commissioner Kunze pointed out that the other flag
lots in the area also set a precedent for approval of the lot.

Jason Adamson asked about the sizes of the previous lots. Commissioner Everett explained that
usually they don’t field questions from citizens during deliberation. Commissioner Jackson and
Jacob Curtis explained that that kind of analysis would take time. Commissioner Eyre explained
that the Planning Commission deals in the black and white of the municipal code and suggested
that concerned citizens continue to talk about and follow the issue, especially to the City Council
and other deciding bodies that are allowed to take more nuance into account.

                Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to offer a positive recommendation to the
                Lehi City Council based on the findings that the proposed zone change is
                consistent with the general plan designation of low density residential, the
                proposed zone change will allow for a flag lot in the area, and the suitability of
                the properties for the use request matches the suitability for the existing uses
                identified by the general plan. He included all DRC comments. Commissioner
                Jones seconded.

                Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner Eyre, yes. Commissioner
                Kunze, yes. Commissioner Roberts, yes. Commissioner Jones, yes.

                Motion passed unanimously.

Commissioner Everett once gain encouraged citizens to continue following the issue. He then
ceded leadership duties to Commission Chair Jackson. Commissioner Jones left the stand.

Holbrook Farms Area Plan Amendment and concept plan to add 7.15 acres of property to
the area plan at approximately 2100 North Miller Campus Drive.

Jacob Curtis presented the item. The applicant worked with the planning division to address
DRC comments.

The applicant, Spencer Mothem, was present. His company is interested in developing retail in
the area already, and they want to keep their options open for residential development in the
future. They do not currently have any concrete plans.

Commissioner Jackson opened public comment.

Jason Turner commented online that he is in favor of the change and likes the idea of more
housing near him.

Jordan Davis commented online that he feels there is too much housing in the area and would
prefer a grocery store.

Commissioner Jackson closed public comment.

Commissioner Roberts asked for clarification on the plans for the lot. Spencer Mothem explained

long stretches of time while the city waits for interested businesses.

Commissioner Everett mentioned that this might be a good place to try designing a walkable

Commissioner Eyre asked for clarification on one of the DRC comments.

Commissioner Eyre asked if the concept plan dies in Planning Commission. Kim Struthers

3.5) Public hearing and recommendation of ProSteel Customs request for review of a
Development Code Amendment, amending Table 05.030-B to allow auto sales and rental
on parcels larger than an acre in the Commercial zone.

Jacob Curtis presented the item. The applicant would like to continue renting U-Haul trucks in
addition to his repair business. He would like to adjust the code to bring this into compliance.

Commissioner Everett clarified that the applicant would be brought into compliance by changing
the code, not by changing their behaviors.

Lyssa Mang was present representing ProSteel Customs. She is the office manager. She stated
that having U-Hauls in the community has been a huge benefit, and she has received positive
feedback from her neighbors.

Commissioner Jackson opened public comment.

Brad Lang works for U-Haul. He emphasized that the presence of the trucks is a positive in the
community. He specifically mentioned how U-Haul has been able to help Lehi with the rapid
growth it’s been experiencing.
Commissioner Jackson closed public comment.

Commissioner Jackson asked how long the applicant has been in violation, and how long they’ve
been knowingly in violation. Jacob Curtis said that it has been about six months since the initial
report of the code violation. Commissioner Jackson asked if ProSteel Customs has been ‘playing
nice’. Curtis confirmed that they had. Commissioner Jackson mentioned that he doesn’t like
rewarding code violations with compliance from the city. Commissioner Kunze asked if it was
an outside complaint. Kim Struthers confirmed that there was only one report, and it came from
outside the organization.

Commissioner Eyre shared that he thinks changing the code is a terrible idea. He clarified that
his isn’t opposed to the business, but he doesn’t want to open the door to other businesses that
could use it as precedent on their own one acre lots. Commissioners Everett and Jackson agreed
with him. Commissioner Everett expressed worry over unforeseen consequences of adding a
permitted use to the table. Commissioner Jackson suggested waiting until there were several
instances before making an official code change.

Kim Struthers suggested that the commission give a negative recommendation to the city
council, but that they should encourage a development agreement so that the business could
continue.

Brad Lang asked if a conditional use would be a possibility. The commission was decidedly
against the idea.

Brad Kenison suggested a general plan amendment that would only affect the specific property.
The commission felt that a development agreement would be a better and more specific solution
to the problem.

                Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to offer a negative recommendation to the

Commissioner Jackson asked for stronger language in support of the business to be added to the
motion.

                Amended Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to offer a negative
                recommendation to the Lehi City Council on the findings that the proposed
                development code amendment is not in conformance with the purposes, intent,
                and provisions of the general plan and it’s various elements. He included all DRC

3.6) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for review of a
Development Code amendment to Chapter 12-B, removing the requirement that property
owners cannot purchase property from the City.

Jacob Curtis presented the item. The purpose of the amendment is to keep options open to the
city for property that has been encroached upon. Currently the city is not allowed to sell property
which limits its options when working with citizens who have encroached on city property.
Curtis mentioned that the City Council is in favor of the change.

Commissioner Jackson said that as the council is already in favor, he feels as though this is more
of an academic exercise than anything else. He thinks that it is shortsighted for the city to prevent
itself from selling property and that making this change would create a freer market.

Commissioner Jackson opened and closed public comment.

Commissioner Everett expressed that he liked the idea of the city having the ability to sell land
but worries that it’s being put into place as a result of specific instances of encroachment in the
city rather than as a proactive measure.

Commissioner Eyre asked about the intent behind the stricken clause. Kim Struthers that it was
added about a year ago in response to development in Traverse Mountain. In his opinion, the city
council want to have the option as it could be beneficial for the city.

Commissioners Eyre and Jackson discussed wording and suggested rewriting the clause to
explicitly add the option for the city. Craig Chambers explained that it’s not necessary to write
specifically that the city can sell property.

Commissioner Everett was troubled and felt that extreme actions taken by citizens wouldn’t be
treated seriously by the city if the option was made available. Craig Chambers assured him that
citizens that encroach are not getting away with anything. He knows this because of his work on
the current cases.

Commissioner Everett suggested bringing cases like these to auction.

Jacob Curtis emphasized to the commission that this is mainly an amendment in the grading
chapter of the city code.

                Motion: Commissioner Kunze moved to give a positive recommendation to the
                Lehi City Council, finding that the proposed development code amendment is in
                conformance with the purposes, intent, and provisions of the general plan and its
                various elements. Commissioner Everett seconds.

Craig Chambers requests that Commissioner Kunze add a recommendation for the City Council
to look at the language of the clause. Commissioner Everett agrees. He expresses that he doesn’t
like the way the change has come about. He fears losing open space as a public amenity.

                Amended Motion: Commissioner Kunze moved to give a positive
                recommendation to the Lehi City Council, finding that the proposed development
                code amendment is in conformance with the purposes, intent, and provisions of
                the general plan and its various elements. She also recommends that City Council
                look at the language of the clause and adjust it for clarity and intent.
                Commissioner Everett withdrew his second. Commissioner Eyre seconds.

                Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes. Commissioner Everett, no. Commissioner
                Eyre, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes. Commissioner Roberts, yes.

                Motion passes four in favor, one against.

3.7) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for review of a
Development Code amendment to Chapter 12 to update buffer and screening requirement
for non-residential zones adjacent to residential zones.

Jacob Curtis presented the item. This amendment was reviewed at the January work session. The
intent behind the amendment is to beef up the buffer and screening requirements. The larger
changes focus on including fencing specifications for use and zones next to residential zones, as
well as adjustments to landscaping clauses.

Commissioner Everett asked if drive isles are for planting boxes or for ingress/egress. He asked
for clarifying language to be added to the clause. Kim Struthers and Jacob Curtis agreed to look
at it.

Commissioner Eyre asked about potential exceptions in the landscaping clauses. He wants to
make as few exceptions as possible.

Commissioner Jackson opened public comment.

Matt Berg commented online and came to speak in person as well. He shared his concerns over
the compatibility section being deleted. He lives against a large commercial development and has
been able to use the clause in the past to argue against it and attain something more desirable for
his neighborhood. He admits that it is a highly subjective clause. He asks that the city clarify it
rather than delete it entirely.

John Madsen, Matt Berg’s neighbor, was also present. He has been before the city council many
times. He also advocates for the maintenance of the compatibility assessment clause. He
mentioned that in the past he has missed opportunities to provide feedback to the city on certain
decisions and has ended up with fencing issues on his property due to it. He appreciates the
clarified fencing amendments for this reason. He stated that he understands that making the
compatibility clause concrete will likely take a number of public hearings and meetings, but he
thinks it would be worthwhile. He would like to avoid taking all of it out in one go.

Commissioner Jackson closed public comment.

The commission thanked Berg and Madsen for their concise and applicable comments.

Commissioner Eyre asked the planning division why the compatibility clause is being removed.
Jacob Curtis explained that it’s a very surface level clause that defines nothing and is nearly
impossible to enforce. Craig Chambers further elaborated that as it stands it mainly opens up the
city for claims, as it’s too open of a clause. He suggested sending a recommendation to the city
council to look into it further. Commissioner Everett asked if there could be an intent statement
in the code that addresses compatibility. Chambers said that the best way to do it would be
through specific buffering guidelines in the code. Curtis suggested putting the intent statement
into the following section of the code that discusses buffering. Commissioner Eyre clarified that
the suggestion from the city’s legal team is to remove it. Chambers confirms that that is correct,
but that the next sections should still be investigated so that the intent can be enforced, rather
than existing as fluff in the code.

                Motion: Commissioner Roberts moves to recommend approval of the proposed
                amendments to the Lehi City Code, given that the language is clarified and
                defined so that section 12.070 can be included and defined in the following
                paragraphs. The proposed development code amendment provides different
                buffering and screening requirements based on intensity of use, it protects
                residential property owners more fully, it is in conformance with the purposes,
                intent, and provisions of the general plan and its various elements. He included
                the comments from the public. Commissioner Eyre seconds.

Commissioner Eyre would like to amend the motion to charge the city to rework and reword
12.070 in a way that is applicable, appropriate, and can give teeth, where possible.

                Amended Motion: Commissioner Roberts moves to recommend approval of the
                proposed amendments to the Lehi City Code, given that section 12.070 be
                reworked and reworded in such a way that it is applicable, appropriate, and
                enforceable, where possible. The proposed development code amendment
                provides different buffering and screening requirements based on intensity of use,
                it protects residential property owners more fully, it is in conformance with the
                purposes, intent, and provisions of the general plan and its various elements. He
                included the comments from the public. Commissioner Eyre seconds.

                Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes. Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner
                Eyre, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes. Commissioner Roberts, yes.

                Motion passes unanimously
