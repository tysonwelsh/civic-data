Hinduism, gave the invocation, and Councilmember Flake led the Pledge of Allegiance.

OPEN SESSION – Citizens’ Comments

Mayor Fullmer opened the public session.

Pete Evans with Flagship Homes reported that this week Flagship had acquired all of the
property from Anderson Geneva and its associated entities. He stated that Flagship Homes was
looking forward to working with the city council and staff on the remaining parcels on the
Geneva site to develop it to its potential for the community. He offered to answer any questions.

Mr. Church asked about the Eastlake at Geneva industrial subdivision in north Vineyard. Mr.
Evans replied that yes, Flagship acquired whatever interest Anderson Geneva still had there.
Councilmember Riley asked if it included all reimbursement funds. Mr. Evans responded with a
qualified yes, and further explained that in the purchase agreement, there were some previous
reimbursement requests and Anderson Geneva retained the right to continue to seek those
reimbursement requests. Other than those, all approved RDA reimbursements would go to
Flagship Homes. Mr. McHargue sought clarification on existing reimbursement agreements
such as those with Utah Valley University (UVU) and the Megaplex. Mr. Evans confirmed that
Flagship had acquired all approved reimbursement agreements.

Councilmember Flake gave an update on the current session of the Utah State Legislature. One
bill he highlighted was House Bill 228 Towing Revisions, which was before legislature this
evening. Mayor Fullmer explained that this bill took the authority to regulate towing away from
the city and gave it to the state. She said that she had written to urge them to keep the authority
with the cities, and that others had also been working on this, such as Mayor Kaufusi of Provo
and the Utah League of Cities and Towns. The discussion continued. Mayor Fullmer also
mentioned Senate Bill 268 Transportation Infrastructure Bond Amendments and how that might
affect the city.

STAFF, COMMISSION, AND COMMITTEE REPORTS
City Manager/Finance Director – Jacob McHargue- Mr. McHargue reported that staff had been
meeting with Orem City and Central Utah Project (CUP) about the water tank and that he would
update the council as that continued to progress. He also reported that the city had a meeting

City Recorder – Pamela Spencer – Ms. Spencer was excused.

Building Official – George Reid – Mr. Reid had no new items to report.

Water/Parks Manager Sullivan Love – Mr. Love had no new items to report.

Clean Air Task Force – Resident Josh Gilman – Committee Member – Mr. Gilman reported on
the recent meeting of the Utah Chamber of Commerce Clean Air Task Force, where they
discussed the Utah Transit Authority’s (UTA) UVX line, which ran between UVU and BYU.
Monthly riders had doubled in the past six months, and the Task Force felt it was a good product
that people were starting to use. He was in contact with the Heritage Commission about a booth
to raise awareness about air quality at the upcoming Vineyard Heritage Celebration. He then
addressed the state’s Commercial Property Assessed Clean Energy (C-PACE) program.
Councilmember Flake asked where the closest air quality monitor was located, and what were
the options for funding to get an air quality monitor in the city. Mr. Gilman replied that he
would find out and report back to the council.

8.2 DISCUSSION AND ACTION – Adopting Rules of Procedure – Resolution 2019-03
    As per Utah Code 10 -3-606 (2)(a), a municipal legislative body shall adopt rules of order
    and procedure. City Attorney David Church will present recommended rules of procedure for
    public meetings. The City Council will possibly act to adopt by resolution, the rules of
    procedure for conducting a public meeting of the legislative body.

Mayor Fullmer turned the time over to David Church.

Mr. Church explained that City Recorder Pamela Spencer had not been able to find Vineyard’s
officially adopted rules of parliamentary procedure. State law required that city councils adopt
rules of procedure and conduct and have a copy of it available for public review. He gave a brief
summary of the Rules of Procedure and Order that would govern the meetings of the Vineyard
City Council. He added that the rules may be changed or modified in the future to become more
or less formal, by resolution. The discussion continued.

Mayor Fullmer called for a motion.

Motion: COUNCILMEMBER FLAKE MOVED TO ADOPT THE RULES OF PROCEDURE
RESOLUTION 2019-03. COUNCILMEMBER RILEY SECONDED THE MOTION. ROLL
CALL WENT AS FOLLOWS: MAYOR FULLMER, COUNCILMEMBERS FLAKE, JUDD,
AND RILEY VOTED AYE. COUNCILMEMBER EARNEST WAS ABSENT. MOTION
CARRIED WITH ONE ABSENT.

8.3 DISCUSSION AND ACTION – Comcast Franchise Agreement Ordinance 2019-03
    Staff will present a Franchise Agreement between the City of Vineyard and Comcast of
    California/Massachusetts/Michigan/Utah, LLC. The mayor and City Council will act to
    approve (or deny) this agreement by Ordinance.

Mr. McHargue explained that this agreement had expired and they were simply renewing it. Mr.
Church explained a franchise agreement was defined as a right that a city gave to private
businesses to use public property for their business purposes.

Councilmember Riley asked about exclusivity. Mr. Church confirmed that franchise agreements
could not be exclusive.

Mayor Fullmer called for a motion.

VOTED AYE. COUNCILMEMBER EARNEST WAS ABSENT. MOTION CARRIED WITH
ONE ABSENT.
