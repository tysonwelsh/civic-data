2. Presentations and Reports

2.1 Discussion of minimum lot size requirements for detached accessory dwelling units.
Kim Struthers, Community Development Director, presented this to the Council. They discussed
potentially lowering the minimum lot size requirement for detached accessory dwelling units
(ADUs) from 15,000 to 14,520 square feet and bringing it close to the size of a true 1/3 acre lot.
Council members generally supported the change, seeing it as a minor adjustment that maintains
the spirit of existing zoning and could help meet state housing goals. While some expressed
caution about setting precedents, the overall sentiment was that the modest reduction would
provide more flexibility for homeowners without significantly impacting the city's zoning
regulations.

7. Consideration of Reallocating Community Development Block Grants (CDBG) funds
   due to budget constraints.

    Claudia Saldana, with Mountainland Association of Governments (MAG) presented the
    updated CDBG funding allocation. The actual amount received was $332,739, down from
    the anticipated $343,852. Under the proposed reallocation, the Curtis Center would receive
    only $2,739 instead of the originally planned $13,852.

    Staff explained that CDBG funds cannot be used for improvements on city-owned buildings,
    so the Curtis Center would need to use funds for salary expenses. Staff initially
    recommended allocating the small remainder to sidewalk contingency rather than the Curtis
    Center.

    Councilor Albrecht opposed the staff recommendation, arguing that CDBG funds have
    traditionally focused on downtown sidewalks while widespread community needs exist. She
    emphasized that the Curtis Center serves a unique, underserved population throughout the
    entire community and should receive full funding.

    Councilor Albrecht shared her personal experience with special needs services, explaining
    that the Curtis Center is critical for families on the Division of Services for People with
    Disabilities waiting list, which can exceed a decade. Without the Curtis Center, special needs
    adults would have no day programs available unless self-funded.

    Lorin Powell, City Engineer, clarified that the $290,000 sidewalk allocation would not
    complete the entire project, but the city would supplement with additional tax funds as done
    in the past.

    Councilor Stallings expressed concern about using CDBG funds indirectly to maintain city-
    owned buildings, suggesting the city should directly pay for the Curtis Center's lock repairs.

    Councilor Condie supported broader distribution of CDBG funds beyond just sidewalks,
    noting other community needs exist.

    After discussion about administrative burden and funding mechanisms, council members
    agreed that both projects would ultimately be completed regardless of funding source.

    Motion:     Councilor Condie moved to allocate Community Development Block Grant funds
                to fully fund the Curtis Center at $31,864, subtract that amount from the $290,000
                for sidewalks, with these two amounts plus the $40,000 administration fee
                comprising the total CDBG funding allocation for the year. Councilor Hancock
                seconded the motion.

9. Consideration of Resolution #2025-30 adopting the Lehi City Fiscal Year 2026 Budget
   and Consolidated Fee Schedule
   Council members discussed potential budget cuts and reallocation, with significant debate
   around the $800,000 ice rink purchase, staffing needs across various departments, and long-
   term capital project investments. The conversation primarily focused on future infrastructure
   planning, areas like park maintenance, police staffing, code enforcement, and Phragmites
   management.

    Councilor Stallings advocated prioritizing police staffing, arguing that Lehi is understaffed
    compared to Orem. She proposed cutting 1.5 park staff positions out of the proposed budget to
    fund additional police officers, emphasizing public safety and officer retention. Steve
    Marchbanks, Parks and Facilities Manager, provided context about park maintenance
    challenges, noting they're managing 200 acres with limited staff and struggling to maintain
    existing parks. Mayor Johnson warned of the impact that pulling park employees would have
    on maintaining park standards.

    The ice-skating rink was discussed. Steve Marchbanks emphasized its popularity and potential
    for community engagement. He also suggested the possibility of relocating it in the future to a
    more ideal location at Family Park. There was a discussion about how it provides a unique
    recreational opportunity. Councilor Stallings and Councilor Condie questioned the financial
    vitality, pointing out the rink's limited seasonal use and the existence of other ice rinks within
    a 30-minute drive. Councilor Albrecht suggested that the city pursue grant opportunities to
    help with funding.

    Councilor Condie wanted to address Phragmites management due to long-standing fire hazard
    and liability issues that needed immediate attention. He also advocated for providing more
    funding for the Hutchings Museum and code enforcement.

    Councilor Stallings advocated for the code enforcement position, arguing that the current
    planning staff are overwhelmed and only reactive in addressing community code violations.
    Councilor Condie proposed funding the position using $150,000 saved from removing the ice
    rink funding. Councilor Newall expressed caution, questioning whether planning should
    handle code enforcement and emphasizing the need for more comprehensive information
    before making a staffing decision. She was concerned about funding a new position without
    fully understanding its impact. The need for an internal auditor position was also discussed.

    The Council also discussed the purchase of a curbing machine for the bike lanes. Councilor
    Hancock suggested that it be considered as an anticipated future need.

    After extensive discussion, the council ultimately voted 4-1 to maintain most of the original
    budget recommendations, with a key modification of removing the ice rink purchase. They
    agreed to keep the Phragmites mower in the budget and left open the possibility of future
    budget amendments. The debate highlighted the complex challenge of balancing limited
    municipal resources across competing priorities, with council members expressing varying
    perspectives on staffing, public services, and strategic investments.

    Motion:     Councilor Hancock moved to approve Resolution #2025-30 adopting the Lehi City
                Fiscal Year 2026 Budget and Consolidated Fee Schedule as it was originally
                proposed, including the purchase of a Phragmites mowing machine; for now do not
                purchase the ice rink and do not use the funding or the money that would be said to
                fund any particular position at this point, because they have not fully vetted what
                of all the many positions that are not funded; it doesn’t mean that the Council can’t
                come back with a future budget amendment with that $150,000 that would have
                been ongoing cost for the skate rink can now be used somewhere else and that can
                be reviewed at a future date. Councilor Albrecht seconded the motion.

    Councilor Hancock clarified his motion, stating that the only thing being pulled from the
    original budget is the ice rink. Everything else stays, because that was discussed fully across
    staff. Everyone was fully vetted by everyone. The $150,000 left should not be reallocated at
    the moment because while the ice rink is not funded this year, maybe next year it is, and maybe
    that’s the right spot for it. Just because it’s there doesn’t mean we have to spend it. Maybe it is
    for another police officer or code enforcement, but the Council has time to make that decision
    because it does not have to be made tonight. It was also clarified that the phragmites mower
    will be included in this budget.

    Councilor Newall stated that she would like to see data and reports on police calls and response
    times, and hear from Chief Paul before deciding to add an additional police position in this
    budget, and vet any other added positions between all the staffing requests.

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Yes; Councilor Hancock, Yes;
    Councilor Newall, Yes; and Councilor Stallings, No. The motion passed with 4 in favor and 1
    opposed.
