Item 3. Presentations

3.1 Pam Roberts, Wasatch Front Waste & Recycling District, Presentation Regarding the District’s
Proposed Fee Increase and Information Regarding the Background

Ms. Roberts, Wasatch Front Waste & Recycling District, presented the Council with a report
presenting what the Board is proposing for the District’s 2018 budget. The Board is proposing a fee
increase for the number of services they provide.

Ms. Roberts referred to her graph, which outlined the proposed fee increases. She noted the current
rate for per month is $14.75 and that included the administrative services they took on in 2013. She
pointed out the actual cost is $19.58. She stated they are not proposing that fee; the Board is trying
to sustain a cash balance. She explained how the District would be able to do that. The District will
reduce their liability on post-employment benefits that retire after July 2019, which will be a
reduction from 2 million to 1.3 million. She went on to explain they are proposing to reduce the
number of truck they replace each year and setting the cap each year they go forward.

Ms. Roberts said the Board is proposing an increase of a majority of the entities in the service area.
The graph represents Millcreek’s rate would go from $14.75 per month to $17.00 per month. In
addition, the Board is proposing the second can go from $15.00 to $17.00 a can. She explain the
reason is to get people to shift from two cans and recycle more if they use their second can for
green waste.

Ms. Roberts noted the District actually receives a minimal revenue to have recycling in the valley.
She stated the District does not have a lot of control over what they do; the maximum rate they pay
is $20-25 a ton.

Mayor Silvestrini stated that is pretty low for a capital investment. Ms. Roberts pointed out the
District is also pushing their replacement years; they used to replace garbage trucks every three-
year. Now, they are replacing between every four to five-years, and they are going to go to six-
years. However, that will increase their maintenance costs.

Ms. Roberts said the District restructured quite a bit as of late; the most recent change is Millcreek’s
collection day. She added after she sent her presentation to the City, the District received word the
landfill is also increasing their fee to $1.5 per ton and green waste is increasing be a $1 per ton.

Council Member Jackson inquired why it cost more to have a green can than for a blue can. Ms.
Roberts explained the blue can is a part of the District’s service; the green can would be a 6 million
dollar start-up cost. In addition, it would be a 7 million dollar expenditure to run the program

3

annually. Council Member Jackson said she has had requests to extend the green waste collection.
Ms. Roberts indicated she could have her team set down and give the City an analysis; it costs $9.50
a month.

Mayor Silvestrini pointed out the City just had their area clean up and several residents contacted
him. He said some said the dumpsters were too far from their homes; however, the District handled
the residents gracefully. He went on to say, under the current rate structure, the cost is $3.50 a
week (that is a truck coming to a house and taking it to a recycling or solid waste location). He
thought that was an incredible deal; it is a service that goes on in the background. The Mayor
added, in perspective, the rates are being proposed to be raised .55 cents per week and the landfill
is having an increase. He noted the cost of vehicles and garbage trucks and fuel are going up. It
could be worse. He said no one likes to see increases, but he thought most people are willing to pay
$4.05 a week to have their garbage picked up and taken to a facility.

Council Member Jackson questioned why the District billed quarterly. Ms. Roberts explained the
billing/administrative cost would cost more if they billed monthly, for things such as, postage, staff
time, paper, etc. Council Member Uipi indicated she paid it automatically through her bank or there
is the option to split the payment into two.

Council Member Marchant thanked Ms. Roberts for everything she did: He went on to say the Board
has been very conservative, and he congratulated her on everything she did. He noted one of the
ways the District helps the valley’s air quality, is having natural gas vehicles/garbage trucks instead
of fuel for saving our ecology.

Council Member Marchant wanted to point out Millcreek residents do pay more per truck. He
explained why the residents pay more is because a lot of the cities surrounding Millcreek subsidize
the waste collection within their cities because they have ownership in the landfills. Millcreek does
not receive dividends. In addition, Millcreek provides more services to its residents than any of the
other entities. He stated his personal opinion, we pay for what we get; it is a conservative cost for
what we get.

Council Member Uipi indicated she wanted to take the City back to where they were; recycle every
2-weeks. She noted the change in collection couldn’t be worse; the residents think the City is doing
it to them. She stated she wanted to acknowledge the timing of collections and the fee increase is
not the City; it’s the services and what the City wanted to maintain.

Mayor Silvestrini stated the Council would consider a Resolution to consider the District’s fee
increase during the Council’s Regular meeting on October 16, 2017.

9.1 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution No. 17-__, a Resolution of Millcreek Adopting a
Cooperative Agreement Between the Utah Department of Transportation and Millcreek for New
Sidewalk Construction

Mayor Silvestrini explained the Council needed to adopt a Resolution between UDOT and Millcreek
that would provide for sidewalks on 3300 South between 2725 East to 2769 East. He noted it was a
matching grant; Millcreek’s portion is 25 percent, which is a matching total of $12,500.; DOT’s
allocated amount is a maximum of $37,500. The total amount of the grant is $50,000 for three
residential properties. He added the City will have another grant for the other side of 3300 South for
next spring.

Mayor Silvestrini noted the grant was in process before Millcreek was incorporated. The Council
needs to approve the match and the contract and pay the $50,000 upfront. Once the City
demonstrates the project is completed; UDOT will reimburse the City. In addition, the City received
a report from the City Engineer that there will not be any additional engineering costs.

Motion: Council Member Jackson moved to adopt Resolution No. 2017-76, a Resolution of the
Millcreek Council Approving a Cooperative Agreement Between UDOT and Millcreek with Respect to
New Sidewalk Construction on SR-171 (3300 South) from 2725 East to 2769 East. Council Member
Uipi made the second. Members Voting Aye: Mayor Silvestrini, Council Members Catten, Marchant,
Jackson, and Uipi. The motion passed unanimously.

Discussion with John Miller, Salt Lake County, Millcreek Engineer, Engineering Updates and Discussion
Regarding a Cooperative Agreement Between UDOT and Millcreek for New Sidewalks

A%

Mr. John Miller, Millcreek Engineer, updated the Council on the proposed resolution with UDOT for safe
sidewalks on 3300 South from 2725 East to 2769 East. He explained it is a matching grant, and the City’s
portion is 25 percent, which is a matching total of $12,500.; DOT’s allocated amount is a maximum of
$37,500. He noted it chosen because of its top priority.

Mayor Silvestrini pointed the agreement also talks about some of the engineering costs. He inquired if
Mr. Miller anticipates any engineering costs that would exceed $50,000. Mr. Miller said he did not. He
added that the engineering costs can go toward the cost of the match; in addition; if the project goes
under budget, it can go toward the match too. He explained, UDOT will track all cost.

Discussion followed regarding another project associated where the sidewalk is missing on 3300 South
connected with a wall/fence. Mr. Miller indicated they have been in touch with Ms. Lund regarding the
situation. He advised the Council, that the Engineering Department will have to document the situation
and do an environmental study. The wall is over 50-years old, and they feel it may be historical. He
explained there are vines intertwined with the fence. When the wall is replaced, it might destroy the
fence; if that happens, they will have to reimburse the property owner for the vines.

Mayor Silvestrini explained they talked at their County meeting regarding the CATNIP project. He went
on to say, they did experimental striping on Juniper Street. Council Member Jackson noted she had
complaint about the striping because they thought it was narrowed down to one lane.

Mayor Silvestrini pointed out if the residents slowed down, they would not have a problem. He said he
spoke to David Baird, Mt. Olympus Community Council, regarding the issue. Mr. Miller added they
talked about getting more funding from the CATNIP project for the area to make it better; however, this
is the lowest, cost experiment for traffic calming. Council Member Uipi commented it was her opinion
that the violators are the individuals that live in the neighborhood.

Mr. Miller addressed the CATNIP project. He said it is for area-wide bicycle type improvements and to
encourage multiple uses. The uses range from dedicated bike lanes to way-finding. He explained in the
application, they took the County-wide engineering to give priorities to Millcreek. He noted the top
priorities they recommended to the Mayor were: Jupiter Drive Loop, Melbourne and Honeycutt, with
the addition of a bike lane, Upland — Wasatch Drive to 2700 South, with the addition of sidewalk next
spring and summer, and Siggard Drive, which will tie into Melbourne and Honeycutt. Mr. Miller stated
there are many more projects, but they think those four projects are the best for now.

Mayor Silvestrini stated if the City can show they are building on other projects and show active
transportation and walkability in the City, they will have a better opportunity of getting grants. Mr.
Miller indicated most of the projects are minor requests — being between $20,000 and $50,000. Mayor
Silvestrini said the letter are due, and the City needed to indicate they want the grant. As a City, they
just need to put a placeholder in for the funding. All the City Council Members concurred with
supporting the UDOT Cooperative agreement for safe sidewalks.

Mr. Miller noted the Wasatch Front Regional Council is requiring a letter of intent for Trans-
portation Alternatives Program (TAP), Transportation and Land Use Connection (TLC), and
Congestion Mitigation/Air Quality (CMAQ) to be submitted by October 30, 2017, and his department
is preparing the letter now. He went on to say the engineering team will prepare a final list that
