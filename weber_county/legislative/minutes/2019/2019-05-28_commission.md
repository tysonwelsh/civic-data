      1. WEBER COUNTY OPIOID COST RECOVERY PUBLIC NUISANCE RESOLUTION – RESOLUTION 28-2019.
            Matt McCune, outside legal counsel representing the county in this litigation, noted that defendants in the opioid
            lawsuits across the United States have been trying to get certain claims dismissed because the counties or cities
            did not adopt a resolution declaring opioids a public nuisance, necessitating this resolution in order not to have
            claims dismissed based on that argument. Of the 16 counties that his law firm and two others that represent in
            Utah, about half thus far have entered into these resolutions and they are working with the remaining to get
            them entered. It is these firms’ opinions that counties should be seeking their own recovery and last year they
            filed a suit on behalf of Weber County against certain opioid companies for damages it incurred due to the
            opioid epidemic and to try avoiding what happened in big tobacco settlements where all the settlement money
            went to the states, which built roads but did not fight tobacco addiction, cancer, etc. Mr. McCune addressed
            the commissioners’ questions.
         In order to expedite discovery, the firms have worked cooperatively with the defendants and are moving all the
         cases into Summit County’s case (which filed the first case in Utah). Almost 2/3 of the counties in the State
         are represented by their legal group. The State Attorney General is pursuing the State’s damages—there are
         different/distinct damages borne by counties, the State, and health departments. Already in Oklahoma a couple
         of the defendants have settled for several million dollars with counties and the State. Should they try the case,
         they would like the ability to do so here in Weber County in front of jurors that are members of this community.
         If the attorneys are not successful in this lawsuit, there is absolutely no cost to the county, and if they are, the
         recovery first reimburses the law firms that have fronted the money and a 20% contingency fee for the attorney
         time invested, with the remaining going to Weber County to help fight this epidemic, try to get remediation in
         place, establish addiction treatments, etc. He said that these companies knew of the addictive nature of opioids
         that they had been marketing for quite some time for treatment of acute pain, and overtime they noticed that
         their market share was decreasing, they then pushed to market it for long term chronic pain, and misinformed
         doctors and the public. This has led to heroin addiction, people buying pills on the street, etc.
         Commissioner Froerer moved to adopt Resolution 28-2019, Weber County Opioid Cost Recovery Public
         Nuisance Resolution; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Froerer – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye
