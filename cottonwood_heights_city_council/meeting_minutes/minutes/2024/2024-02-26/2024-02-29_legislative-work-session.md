MINUTES OF THE COTTONWOOD HEIGHTS CITY COUNCIL LEGISLATIVE
WORK SESSION HELD THURSDAY, FEBRUARY 29, 2024, AT 9:00 AM VIA ZOOM
WITH AN ANCHOR LOCATION AT THE COTTONWOOD HEIGHTS CITY COUNCIL
WORK ROOM LOCATED AT 2277 EAST BENGAL BOULEVARD, COTTONWOOD
HEIGHTS, UTAH

Members Present:         Mayor Mike Weichers, Council Member Suzanne Hyland, Council Member
                         Shawn Newell, Council Member Ellen Birrell

Staff Present:           City Manager Tim Tingey, Records Culture and Human Resources Director
                         Paula Melgar, Administrative and Financial Services Director Scott Jurges,
                         Community and Economic Development Director Michael Johnson

City Lobbyists:          Brian Allen, Chantel Nate, Greg Curtis

Excused:                 Council Member Matt Holton; Police Chief, Robby Russo, IT Manager,
                         Matt Ervin; Public Works Director/City Engineer, Matt Shipp

1.       Welcome – Mayor Weichers.

Mayor Mike Weichers called the meeting to order at 9:02 AM and welcomed those present.

2.       Legislative Update and Discussion – City Manager, Tim Tingey and City Lobbyists Greg
         Curtis, Brian Allen, and Chantel Nate.

City Lobbyist, Greg Curtis, presented the Legislative Session update. The following Bills were
discussed:

     •   HB-488 – Transportation Funding Modifications Bill. It was reported that $1.5 million of
         funding was designated for the Fort Union Boulevard and 2300 East area. The proposed
         language allowed the Utah Department of Transportation (“UDOT”) to use 2% of the
         Cottonwood Canyons Fund for Public Safety in the Canyons. The bill creates an
         Infrastructure Loan Fund in counties of the first class. Upon repayment of the funds, 20%
         will go toward a Transit Mobility Hub on property north of Big Cottonwood Canyon. The
         Bill passed the House in a completely different form and went to the Senate Committee
         earlier in the week. There was a substitute bill with a second substitute bill that included
         technical changes. It must pass the Senate and return to the House for concurrence.

     •   Council Member Birrell asked if the Salt Lake County Council was supportive of the
         Transit Mobility Hub at the mouth of Big Cottonwood Canyon. Mr. Curtis reviewed the
         bill with Mayor Wilson with one portion causing concern. He spoke with the Lobbyist for
         the Canyons who stated that it takes the cap off of the $20 million Cottonwood Canyons
         Fund allowing for sales tax growth. He emphasized that sales tax growth could potentially
         be utilized to fund the Phase I Mobility Center. If it can be demonstrated that Phase I is
         successful, he questioned why funding for a $1 billion gondola would need to be
         considered. He noted that Mayor Wilson was agreeable to working with those who are in

    •   Mr. Curtis reported all Gravel Pit bills and their machinations are dead. He noted that
        Senator Cullimore played a significant role in following those through. City Lobbyist,
        Brian Allen reported that Senator Cullimore was extremely helpful in killing the Gravel Pit
        Bills and the Study Bill was the only one remaining. All other gravel pit items have been
        shelved.

    •   SB-185 – Inspection Bill, Senator Evan Vickers. Mr. Allen understood there were no
        House Members who supported the bill in its current form. The House Sponsor was
        uncomfortable with the bill himself and wanted to negotiate to a position where the cities
        agree to the terms. They hope to provide language that would specify that after three days,
        the contractor would be required to refer to a random list of Building Inspectors who are
        paid for by the cities as opposed to selecting their own. Doing so would eliminate any
        conflict of interest. Negotiations would continue.

        City Manager, Tim Tingey, reported that there was pressure from the Utah League of Cities
        and Towns (“ULCT”) to all members who were in opposition to SB-185. He thanked Mr.
        Allen for working on the issue. Mr. Allen reported that SB-185 appears to be the one bill
        remaining that may be problematic. All other bills that were of concern have died or been
        fixed. He would continue to monitor the bills and potential substitutions over the last two
        days of the Legislative Session.

Council Member Birrell asked for updates with regard to HB-449, HB-345, and HB-201.
Mr. Allen provided the following updates:

    •   HB-449 – Bicycle Safety considered in street design. It passed out of the Senate Committee
        and is currently in the Senate Rules Committee. The bill would require prioritization by
        the House. Mr. Allen did not foresee any controversy in its passing.

    •   HB-345 – Increasing the fine for speeding in construction and school zones, has passed.

    •   HB-201 – Speed cameras being used in intersections. There has been some controversy
        with this bill. His understanding was that it would not get out of the House.

    •   The Annexation Bill had a substitute. The communities of Granite and White City were
        concerned with how the bill was initially written, which would require them to be annexed.
        There was an effort to create a substitute bill to allow them to self-determine. The
        unannexed communities would be given until 2027 to determine if there was a feasible way
        to self-determine. The bill was currently in the Senate Rules Committee.

    •   The Bill that would require a separate hearing for Officer Compensation Amendment
        Increases was at a tolerable place and had been circled on the House calendar. It had yet
        to be voted on.
