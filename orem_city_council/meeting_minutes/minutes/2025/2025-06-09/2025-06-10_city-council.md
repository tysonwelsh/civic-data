Utah Risk Management Agency (URM) Presentation Presenter: David Peterson, URMA

Executive Director

Mr. Peterson, Executive Director of the Utah Risk Management Agency (URMA),
presented to the Orem City Council about the benefits and operations of the organization. As one
of the four founding members in 1985, Orem is part of a non-profit, governmental entity
designed to provide a self-insured alternative to expensive traditional insurance. Mr. Peterson
highlighted that URMA's model is unique because it's actuarially based, which means member
cities are shielded from massive losses in other states, such as those caused by wildfires or
hurricanes. This differs from a traditional insurance model where all policyholders share in the
cost of losses regardless of location.

A key feature of URMA is its emphasis on risk reduction over simple claims
management. The agency uses a data-driven approach, powered by its premier software system,
Origami, to analyze risk factors rather than relying on speculation. This data helps them advise
cities on best practices, such as how to handle Taser training to avoid worker's compensation
claims. Mr. Peterson also noted the significant cost savings, stating that member cities would pay

Furthermore, the URMA model gives member cities a high degree of control and

Utah Valley HOME Consortium Resolution Discussion Presenter: Heather Cox, Management
Analyst

During the city council meeting, Ms. Cox presented a resolution for the renewal of the
Utah Valley HOME Consortium Interlocal Agreement, which must be renewed every three years.
The HOME Consortium allows cities like Orem to pool resources and qualify for federal housing
grants from the Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), as individual cities do
not meet the minimum population or demographic thresholds to receive the funds on their own.
The HOME program specifically targets housing opportunities for low-income residents and has
been used to fund homeless and domestic violence shelters, among other initiatives.

Council members raised questions about the types of projects that receive funding.
Councilmember Millett asked if the consortium could prioritize down payment assistance or
neighborhood revitalization, expressing a desire to see more of these funds directly benefit Orem
residents in purchasing homes. Ms. Cox confirmed that down payment assistance programs do
exist and noted she would speak with the program administrator in Provo to explore these
possibilities. She agreed to bring the topic of down payment assistance and neighborhood
revitalization to the forefront of future consortium discussions. The resolution to renew the
interlocal agreement was placed on the consent agenda for the council's vote.

PUBLIC HEARING - Enterprise Fund Transfers - FY 2025-2026 Presenters: Brandon C.
Nelson, CPA, Finance Director

Mr. Nelson presented a report on the required transfer of funds from Orem’s enterprise
funds to its general fund. This practice stems from a state auditor's requirement for cities to show
revenue for utility services used on city-owned properties, such as parks. To avoid raising utility
rates, the city’s general fund pays the enterprise funds (water, sewer, stormwater, and street
lighting) for these services, and then the same amount is immediately transferred back to the
general fund.

This process ensures that the financial accounts accurately reflect the services provided
without impacting the city’s budget or utility rates. The presentation detailed the specific
amounts being transferred: $900,000 for water, $16,000 for water reclamation, $141,000 for
stormwater, and $15,000 for street lighting. Mr. Nelson explained that while the state law was
created to prevent some entities from misusing funds, Orem has always maintained transparency
and has never used this process to transfer funds for other purposes. The report also included
detailed breakdowns of revenue sources, expenditure categories, and administrative costs for
each of the enterprise funds involved in these transfers.

PUBLIC HEARING - ORDINANCE - Adopting FY 2025-2026 Budget. Presenters:

Presenters: Brandon C. Nelson, CPA, Finance Director

Mr. Nelson presented the final details of Orem's tentative budget, which is now ready for
adoption after several previous reviews. He highlighted a significant $17 million increase in
overall city revenues for the upcoming fiscal year. A large portion of this new revenue is being
allocated to capital projects, particularly for improvements to roads, water systems, and the water
reclamation facility. The General Fund, which saw a $3 million increase from sources like sales
tax and fire contracts with Lindon and Vineyard, will fund new initiatives such as police
software, a rifle replacement program, and additional personnel for the fire and public works
departments. To address the costs of maintaining and improving utilities, Mr. Nelson detailed
several rate increases. A new "water source fee" will be added to residents' water bills to
specifically cover the costs associated with the Jordan River water supply and a Deer Creek
intake project. Additionally, the Water Reclamation Fund will see an increase in its base rate and
volume charge to prepare for major capital improvements at the water treatment plant. Mr.
Nelson confirmed that the cities of Lindon and Vineyard, which share the facility, will contribute
their portion of these costs. He also noted that while daily admission fees for recreation facilities
will increase by 50 cents, there will be no increase to solid waste rates this year, with the city
using its fund balance to absorb rising vendor costs. Mr. Nelson concluded by showing that
despite the changes, Orem's overall utility rates remain favorable compared to other cities in the
area.

PUBLIC HEARING - RESOLUTION - Redevelopment Agency of the City of Orem (RDA)

Fiscal Year 2025-2026 Budget Adoption
Mr. Nelson explained that Orem's Redevelopment Agency (RDA) is a separate legal

entity from the city, and as such, it requires its own budget. The majority of Orem's RDA areas
have expired, but remaining funds are being used for specific projects. The primary focus of the
current RDA budget is the University Place Community Development Area (CDA). In this area,
an incentive agreement dictates that 90% of property tax revenue goes to Woodbury to help pay
for infrastructure debt. The remaining 10% is split between the city's RDA fund (5%) for future
infrastructure needs and the city's general fund (5%) to cover administrative overhead.

PUBLIC HEARING - RESOLUTION -Special Service Lighting District of the City of
Orem Fiscal Year 2025-2026 Budget Adoption

Mr. Nelson presented the budget for the Special Service Lighting District, a separate legal
entity created in the late 1990s. The district was formed to replace all streetlights previously
managed by Rocky Mountain Power with new poles and energy-efficient LED lights. Now that
the replacement project is nearly complete, the budget's main focus has shifted to maintenance
and repair. The district's revenue comes primarily from street lighting fees charged to residents'
utility bills. The budget includes expenses for operational costs, holiday lighting, and a lease for
the LED lights. To cover these costs, there will be an increase in the street lighting fee.
Businesses pay their annual fee in full upon renewing their business license.
