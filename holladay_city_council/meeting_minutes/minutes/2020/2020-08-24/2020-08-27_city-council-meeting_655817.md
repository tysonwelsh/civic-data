I.     Welcome – Mayor Dahle.
Mayor Dahle called the meeting to order at approximately 6:00 p.m. He read into the record the
Order on Public Meetings of the City Council of the City of Holladay.

II.    Public Comments.
There were no public comments.

III.   Public Hearing on Proposed General Plan Amendment for the Area Located at
       Approximately 2450 East and 4680 South, Currently Designated as MDR-S (Medium-
       Density Residential Stable) to LDR-S (Low-Density Residential Stable).
Mayor Dahle reported that the Council would not be voting on the above matter at this meeting. He
reviewed the information in the packet for the Council’s review and stated that the public hearing will
remain open for at least one additional Council Meeting. The Mayor acknowledged letters received
from Clark Richards, Ron Hilton, and Stephen Doxey, which were made part of the record.

Mayor Dahle then opened the public hearing at 6:08 pm.

Holley Richards - 2470 E Kentucky Ave. She acknowledged the Council’s dedication to the City and
the 14 other applicants she was representing to amend the General Plan. The application was brought
forward because the applicants felt they were at an inappropriate increased risk of development under
the current MDR-S Stable District. She stated that her surrounding neighborhoods are single-family
low-density stable districts that are zoned R-1-10. The purpose of the MDR-S Stable District under
the General Plan is to accommodate traditional multi-family development where increased density
has been previously established or where single-family development is no longer viable, neither of
which apply. Mrs. Richards asked the Council to protect her neighborhood from higher density in
the future.

Steve Doxey - 2430 E Kentucky Ave. He echoed the comments made by Mrs. Richards. He stated
that that the MDR-S designation for future land use is wholly inappropriate with the established
pattern of development and preservation of an existing neighborhood. Mr. Doxey asked the Council

General Plan objectives but remain consistent with the current zoning.

Ryan Steele - 4876 S Colony Dr. He expressed his support for the amendment to the General Plan

IV.    Consideration of Resolution 2020-26 Granting Advice and Consent for the Appointment of
       Robert Falck to the Historical Commission.
City Manager, Gina Chamness reported this is the first appointment for Robert Falck. A
reappointment was also being sought for a second term for Ann Engar.

Council Member Petersen moved to approve Resolution 2020-26. Council Member Gibbons
seconded the motion. The Council roll call vote was as follows: Council Members Gibbons, Petersen,
Durham, Fotheringham, Quinn and Mayor Dahle in favor. Resolution 2020-26 was approved
unanimously.

V.      City Manager Report – Manager Chamness.
Ms. Chamness gave a brief overview of revenue trends received over the past week for the August
sales tax distribution, which is the last piece for the fiscal 2020 June sales. The City of Holladay
exceeded the original budget amount for sales tax and grew by approximately 10% over last year,
which is consistent with the State-wide numbers.

Assistant to the City Manager, Holly Smith was working on the Holladay at 20’ campaign for the
property tax discussion. Several videos were being produced that will be available for use beginning
in January 2021. The video topics will include an overview of the process and the recommendation
of the Citizen Advisory Group and the projected next steps, a video to address the City of Holladay’s
capital and maintenance needs, budget challenges, tools available to the City, and the rationale behind
the recommendations made by the Citizen Advisory Group. Finally, there will be a video pertaining
to property taxes entitled Property Tax 101. The filming will take place on-site this fall. Videotaping
will be done with each individual by appointment regardless of how many videos an individual is
participating in. Ms. Smith asked for the Council’s participation. The Council agreed to help where
needed. There was discussion regarding the details of the information provided in the videos.

VI.   Council Reports and District Issues.
Council Member Petersen was extremely grateful for the service rendered to her recently by UPD.

Council Member Fotheringham reported on an incident involving a large gathering in his area that
UPD responded to and skillfully resolved.

Council Member Durham discussed public works projects underway in his district. He was receiving
positive feedback from the neighbors regarding the work being done.

Council Member Gibbons has been receiving positive feedback on speed limit adjustments on
Holladay Blvd. and thanked the Council for their attention to the matter. He brought up increased
neighborhood crime in his area and the need for additional streetlights. Ms. Chamness reported that
the previous Council decided not to pursue additional streetlights due to budgetary constraints.

Council Member Gibbons also mentioned a discussion he had with neighbors and staff about a portion
of land currently under the City’s control but that could be transferred into a special service district.
He asked the Council to consider holding a Work Session on this subject at a later date. He mentioned
that other jurisdictions have held Town Hall meetings online and wondered if the City of Holladay
would consider doing the same for specific projects. Staff agreed to have further discussions with
Council Member Gibbons regarding conducting future Town Hall meetings online.

Council Member Petersen asked about the speed limit on Holladay Boulevard and the marker for the
speed limit change. It was mentioned that the marker is about one block south of Cottonwood
Elementary.

Council Member Quinn reported that a flyer was sent out regarding parking on the west side of the
street near Wander Lane and she has received significant feedback. As a result, she will be meeting
with staff to carefully identify next steps. Council Member Petersen offered to forward feedback she
received on the issue to Council Member Quinn. There was discussion regarding the City’s
ownership rights on Wander Lane.

Mayor Dahle reported that he met with the Chamber of Commerce and discussed rental assistance for
residents and small businesses. He suggested that the Council remain involved due to the stress it is
placing on small businesses.

Council Member Petersen moved to recess the Council Meeting and reconvene in a Work Meeting.
The motion was seconded by Council Member Durham. The motion passed with the unanimous
consent of the Council.

VII.    Recess City Council to a Work Meeting.
        a.     Discussion on Storm Water Fee – Fred Philpot, Lewis Young.
Fred Philpot from Lewis Young Robertson & Burningham presented a report on the Storm Water
Utility Financial Plan. There was discussion regarding operation and maintenance cost increases
coupled with the City of Holladay’s spending on capital improvements. Ms. Chamness stated that
there could be some savings in the future. Mr. Philpot reported that any savings may be so small that
they are consumed by inflation. Mayor Dahle indicated that the Council will ultimately have to decide
on one of the proposed options at a future meeting. Ms. Smith reported that staff will bring forward
a draft ordinance for the Council’s vote in October.

        b.     Discussion on Previous General Plan Public Hearing.
Mayor Dahle reviewed the current status of the General Plan review and indicated that the public
hearing was still open. The Planning Commission recommended approval and the staff recommended
denial.

City Attorney, Todd Godfrey noted that Ron Hilton originally presented an application to amend the
General Plan and text of the zoning ordinance and then brought forward additional applications that
have confused the process. Mr. Godfrey discussed the history of the applications and the status for
review by the Council.

Mayor Dahle believed the current issue involved the request for the creation of a residential in-fill
zone that was denied by the Planning Commission. The applicant mistakenly believed the request
was active and should come before the Council for consideration. The General Plan amendment is
the ability to review the General Plan and develop a concept to allow for residential infill. Mr.
Godfrey recommended the Council review all of the issues together. It was suggested that the matter
be tabled until all of the issues can be addressed by the Council at the same time. It was noted that
no issues should be broadened after review by the Planning Commission but prior to review by the
City Council.

        c.      Discussion on Proposed Historical Preservation Ordinance.
Ms. Chamness raised an issue regarding the Council’s intent and priorities. She explained that several
years ago, a former Council revised Title 2 including provisions related to the Historical Preservation
Ordinance. The Historic Preservation Commission was interested in expanding the role of the
Commission and seeking role clarification through an ordinance amendment. Ms. Chamness asked
for guidance from the Council.

Council Member Gibbons provided additional insight on the background of the Historical
Preservation Commission and expressed support for amending the ordinance. The Council discussed
next steps and it was recommended that the topic be included on next year’s priority list.

       d.     Discussion of Holladay Crossroads (HCR) Zone – Procedure.
Mayor Dahle introduced the above matter and stated that it needs to be reviewed soon. Council
Member Gibbons reported that the Holladay Crossroads Zone (“HCR”) is a proposal that was
recommended by the Planning Commission and has been before the Council for action but was
delayed due to COVID-19. The issue requires focus from the Council due to the challenge of the
impacted constituents and their ability to participate in public meetings.

Council Member Gibbons stated that the issue will bring several decisions to the Council’s attention
that can improve the lives of those living in the area. He suggested setting dates for an open-air open
house date and a public hearing. Mayor Dahle agreed and mentioned that it may be worth scheduling
a work meeting to review the matter before holding an open house. Discussion ensued regarding the
logistics of future discussions.

       e.     Status of City Council Meetings.
Mayor Dahle raised the issue of future meetings and logistics in a COVID-19 environment and asked
each Council Member for their feedback. Mayor Dahle emphasized that no one will be required to
do anything they feel jeopardizes their safety. Each Council Member went over their concerns and

VIII. Closed Session Pursuant to Utah Code Section 52-4-204 and 205 to Discuss Personnel
       Issues, Potential Litigation, and Property Acquisition and Disposition (if needed).

seconded the motion. The Council roll call vote was as follows: Council Members Durham,
Fotheringham Petersen, Quinn, Gibbons and Mayor Dahle voted in favor. The motion to go out of
closed session at 9:05 p.m. passed with a unanimous vote

IX.     Adjourn.
Council Member Petersen moved to adjourn. Council Member Fotheringham seconded the motion.
The Council voted in the affirmative and the motion carried. The City Council Meeting adjourned to
a closed meeting at approximately 9:06 p.m.
