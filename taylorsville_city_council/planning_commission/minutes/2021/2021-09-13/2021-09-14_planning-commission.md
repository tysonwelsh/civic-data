  4.       File #13Z21. Recommendation to the City Council for a Land Development Code Text Amendment
  to Chapter 13.35; Notice Requirements, to Update the City’s Public Noticing Standards to Align With State
  Law. (Jim Spung/AICP/Senior Planner)


 4.1.    Mr. Spung presented this item.          In the 2021 session, the Utah Legislature passed new law updating
         the public hearing noticing procedures required by municipalities. These changes reflect more modern and
         effective means of reaching individuals. The most notable change is the elimination of the public hearing
         newspaper notice for General Plan, Zoning Map, and Land Use Regulation Amendments. Instead, a notice
         must be published on the Utah Public Notice Website, be mailed to each affected entity (if applicable) and
         posted on the City’s website. The legislation became effective on July 1, 2021; however, the City Code still
         requires the newspaper notice. The proposed text amendment to Chapter 13.35: Notice Requirements of
         the Taylorsville Land Development Code, aligns the City’s noticing procedures with recent legislation passed
         during the 2021 Utah Legislative session affecting Utah Code Ȿ10-9a-203; Ȿ10-9a-204; and Ȿ10-9a-205.
         These sections address requirements for public hearing notice procedures related to General Plan adoption
         and amendments; zoning map amendments; and land use regulation amendments.

         4.1.1     He gave a brief summary of the new legislation to the Commissioners, which included the
                   following language:
                   •        The City must now send a “notice of intent” to prepare a General Plan or comprehensive
                            General Plan amendment;
                   •        A published newspaper notice is no longer required for amendments to a General Plan,
                            Land Use Regulation (e.g. zoning text amendment), or Zoning Map Amendment;
                   •        A “courtesy notice” is now required for Zoning Map Amendments;
                   •        Specifies information and content that must be included for public hearing notices.

         4.1.2     Mr. Spung listed notable changes from the current code being proposed as part of this text
                   amendment:
                   •       Reorganized content for consistency and user-friendliness;
                   •       General Plan adoption and amendments no longer require newspaper notice;
                   •       New subsection regarding “notice of intent” to prepare a General Plan or
                           comprehensive General Plan Amendment;

  5.       File #12Z21. Recommendation to the City Council for a Land Development Code Text Amendment
  to Sections 13.07.020; 131.08.020; 13.09.030; 13.10.020; and 13.11.010, Related to Accessory Dwelling Units
  (ADUs), Extended Living Areas, Guesthouses, and Corresponding Definitions.
  (Jim Spung/AICP/Senior Planner)


5.1           Mr. Spung presented this item. During their 2021 session, the Utah Legislature passed House Bill 82
         (HB82), which preempts local land use authorities to allow internal accessory dwelling units (ADUs) as
         permitted uses in all single-family residential zones. Effective October 1, 2021, municipalities will not be
         able to prohibit accessory dwelling units in single-family areas. The legislation also limits the extent to
         which municipalities can regulate ADUs but provides a list of local regulations that may be imposed.

         5.1.1     Staff recommends the text amendment related to ADUs be adopted on or before October 1, 2021
                   – the date HB82 becomes effective. He advised that HB82 provides that Internal ADUs will be
                   allowed in all single-family residential zones. He went over local regulation that is allowed:
                   Prohibit short-term rentals in ADUs; require owner-occupancy in ADUs; prohibit detached ADUs;
                   prohibit ADUs on lots smaller than 6,000 square feet; prohibit separate utility meters; require

                   licenses/permits, and prohibit the creation of ADUs within a zoning district covering an area that
                   is equivalent to 25% or less of the total area in the City zoned for residential use.

         5.1.2     He provided the following notable changes from current code which are proposed as part of this
                   text amendment:
              •    Updated terminology for consistency with State Code – “accessory apartment” has been changed
                   to “accessory dwelling unit (ADU) internal;”
              •    ADUs no longer require administrative conditional use review – they are “permitted” in all
                   residential zones (except RM and MU) subject to standards;
              •    ADUs are permitted in Mixed-Use (M) zoning district, subject to standards;
              •    “Extended living areas” have been eliminated – these living arrangements will follow the ADU
                   standards moving forward;
              •    ADUs are only allowed on a lot containing 6,000 square feet or more. Current Code does not
                   establish a minimum lot size;
              •    ADUs shall not be used for short-term rentals (i.e., less than 3 consecutive days);
              •    Clarified language related to business licensing requirements;
              •    Identified specific building code standards that will be required for ADU approval;
              •    An affidavit recorded with Salt Lake County is now required to ensure future owners are aware of
                   the standards and conditions associated with ADUs and guesthouses;
              •    New standard requiring evidence of owner occupancy;
              •    Reorganized and reworded standards related to “guesthouses.”
              •    Eliminated standards related to “extended living areas.”
              •    New definition for “accessory dwelling unit (ADU);”
              •    Updated definition for “dwelling”: “dwelling, duplex;” and “dwelling, single-family.”
              •    Eliminated definition of “extended living area.”

         5.1.3     In the August 24th, 2021, Planning Commission meeting, the Planning Commission unanimously
                   forwarded a positive recommendation to the City Council for the proposed text amendment
                   subject to the following changes:
              •    Adding the word “internal” to specify that these regulations apply to internal accessory dwelling
                   units (ADUs), as provided in HB-82;
              •    Update language to clarify that the City (not the applicant) may (not “shall”) record a notice with
                   Salt Lake County to alert future property owners of regulations associated with ADUs;
              •    Remove proposed edits reducing the maximum area allowed for a guesthouse from 75 percent
                   of the footprint area of the home down to 50 percent. Revisions to this standard will be
                   considered at a future date.

         5.2.2  The City is requesting a text amendment to the Taylorsville Municipal Code Sections 13.07.020;

         5.2.4  Additional edits, as outlined in Exhibit Ain the Staff Report are proposed to clarify and update
                standards related to guesthouses and extended living areas.
         5.2.5  The City Council is the final decision-making authority for a text amendment to the Taylorsville
                Municipal Code.
