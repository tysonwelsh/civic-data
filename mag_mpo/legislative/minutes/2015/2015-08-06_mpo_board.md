Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) Modification - Shawn Eliot Orem City has requested that the $442,000 for the State Street/Orem 1200 South Interchange Study be removed from the TIP and the funds transferred to the Orem 800 North/1100 East and expend the scope of the project to address some drainage issues and build a sidewalk on the south side of 800 North. UDOT is doing a study to place a grade separated interchange at State Street and University Parkway, 1200 South is a block away from the Parkway, UDOT is proposing to fund this study as part of the interchange study, which frees up the $442,000. The sidewalk is an addition to the scope of Orem 800 North/1100 East project and would cost about $212,000. This funding change does not impact any other projects and give the Orem 800 North/1100 East the additional funds needed for the sidewalk and the drainage issues. Mayor Richard Brunst moved to have MPO Regional Planning Committee cancel the State Street/Orem 1200 South Study and transfer the $440,000 MPO funding to the Orem 800 North/1100 East Intersection project and add to the scope of work to the project the sidewalk and drainage improvements. Mayor Rick Moore seconded the motion. The motion carried unanimously. FHWA/FTA Planning Certification Review of the MAG MPO - Steve Call, FHWA Every four years FHWA and FTA jointly review and evaluate the MAG MPO, UDOT, UTA transportation planning process. The certification process ensures the federal planning requirements are being satisfactorily implemented. The review process provides advice and guidance to help enhance the planning process and improve the quality of transportation investment decisions. The review committee consisted of Kristen Kenyon, FTA, and from FHWA Steve Call, Lorrie Lau, Eric Pihl, Kelly Lund, and Justin Morgan. MAG, UDOT and UTA activity coordinate the planning issues and activities involved with the development of a well maintained transportation program. The federal certification review committee reported that MAG is fully certificated and a full report will may available at a later date. Few commendations The MPO, UTA and UDOT cooperate and coordinate with each other on all issues. They are out in front of the new regulations and do not wait for the federal clarification Work together to leverage the funds Few recommendations Update the Public Participation Program (MAG is in the process of updating) Take credit for outreach events Outline performance measures Educate and simplify the air quality plan Comments from the mayors Mayor Ward, they do a good and are open with everything they do. Mayor Brunst, they are update and give current details on transportation issues and work well with the legislature and are available when we need them. Steve will have a final report for the committee in the next few months. UTA Local Option Sales Tax Update and UTA Equity Study - Steve Meyer, UTA After the passage of HB-362, UTA transit priorities are looking at the gaps in the system, big picture priorities, baseline transit additions, customer feedback, technical input, and non-bus/rail components. Customers want More early & late service More frequent service Shorter travel time Route sign information Holiday service New service areas Benefits of the new resources: Potential Service Enhancements  Bus frequency and expansion of the core network  50% more routes with 15 minute frequency Mountainland MPO Regional Planning August 6, 2015 Page 5  All routes will run with 30 minute or better frequency all day except in peak hours  Longer service on weekdays and weekend hours  Expand TRAX and Streetcar service  15% more weekday FrontRunner service  Purchase new buses focusing on CNG  More bus stops, shelters and amenities  First and last mile connections  GREEN bike system  Rideshare / Van Pool connections to rail stations  Bike lanes to transit stations, bike repair stands with pumps, bike lockers The Big Picture: UTA will provide more connectivity, more convenience, and more transit options. If we can boost up ridership by 15% and remove 2.3 million cars from the roads every year air quality could improve. Next Steps: Public Involvement, rider survey, city meetings, open houses, an open UTA, online priorities survey, work with Councils of Governments, Transportation Coalition, ULCT, UAC, and city/county councils.
