2.1) Perry Homes Discussion of possible Traverse Mountain Area Plan Amendment, moving
      density from West Canyon to the Perry Planning District.

Matt Swain from Perry Companies presented a proposal to relocate 600 residential units from
West Canyon to lower areas near SR 92, suggesting a potential benefit for the city by reducing
canyon development and traffic near the canyon. The plan involves moving the units to Perry's
remaining land holdings and possibly Riverbend property, creating a mixed-use development
with commercial and residential components. Council members expressed concern about the
proposal, specifically traffic impacts. Swain argued the relocation would create a more
appropriate density near transportation corridors and potentially dedicate the canyon area to open

2.2) Gardner Development Discussion of possible amendment to the Lehi Block Development
      Agreement, adding residential density.

Dave Dennison and his partners from Gardner Group proposed amending the Lehi Block
Development Agreement to increase residential and retail density. Currently approved for 250
residential units and 5,000 square feet of retail, they sought to add 350 more units and expand
retail to 25,000 square feet. The developers argued that more residential units would help
activate the area and attract additional retail, suggesting they needed between 250-350 units to
make the project economically viable. Council members expressed concerns about infrastructure
capacity, particularly transportation and sewer systems. They noted a history of developers
promising retail development but primarily delivering residential units. The discussion centered
on the project's location between two future transit stations, with concerns about utility capacity.
The developers emphasized their desire to create a more mixed-use, walkable environment and
maintain flexibility for potential future office development. While they were not seeking an
immediate decision, they sought council feedback on their conceptual proposal. The council
remained cautious, highlighting the need for comprehensive traffic studies and a clear plan for
balancing residential and commercial development.

2.3) Discussion of Discontinuing the Green Waste Program.

Alyson Alger, City Treasurer, presented a proposal to discontinue the current Greenwaste
program due to Waste Management no longer operating a green waste facility. Currently,
residents pay for green waste collection for nine months of the year, but Waste Management has
been billing the city year-round, and residents can use their cans year-round. Starting in
December, residents will be charged for waste collection for all 12 months, at an additional cost
of $27 per year. Residents will receive a letter in early October informing them of the change and
giving them the option to have their green waste can picked up if they don't want to pay the
additional fee for the extra can. Mayor Johnson inquired about the status of TSSD's green waste
facility, but no definitive information was available. The city hopes that eventually, they might
be able to use a new green waste facility.

9) Consideration of Ordinance #52-2025, approving an amendment to the Lehi City
   Municipal Code Title 7 Chapter 1, eliminating the no parking restriction during the
   winter.

Luke Seegmiller, Traffic Engineer, presented this item to the Council. He explained the proposed
amendment to eliminate winter parking restrictions. Currently, the code prohibits overnight
parking from November 1 to March 30 and restricts parking during snowstorms. The proposed
change would: 1) Remove the overnight parking rule. 2) Strengthen the storm parking rule. 3)
Prohibit parking during snowstorms and up to 48 hours after a snowstorm. 4) Add a restriction
for no parking when there's one inch or more of snow/ice on the roadway.

Luke noted that the current rule restricts parking for 150 nights, but snowplows only operate 15-
30 days per year. Enforcing the current overnight parking rule is burdensome for police. After
reviewing municipal codes in Salt Lake and Utah Counties, Luke found that many cities have
similar or fewer restrictions.

Motion:               Chris Condie moved to approve Ordinance #52-2025, approving an
                      amendment to the Lehi City Municipal Code Title 7 Chapter 1, eliminating
                      the no parking restriction during the winter; and include the Development
                      Review Committee comments. Heather Newall seconded the motion.

Roll Call Vote:       YES: Paige Albrecht, Chris Condie, Paul Hancock, Heather Newall,
                      Michelle Stallings. The motion passed unanimously.


10) Consideration of Ordinance #45-2025 approving the Hammond Zone Change on 0.83
    acres, located at approximately 600 West State Street, changing the zoning from Mixed
    Use to Heavy Commercial.

Justin Hammond proposed rezoning 0.83 acres from mixed use to heavy commercial at 600 West
State Street, with the intent to build an office warehouse. Justin explained his project would
likely be less impactful than other potential mixed-use developments. Council members Michelle
Stallings and Heather Newall expressed concerns about the proximity to residential properties
and the potential for heavy commercial uses. Michelle was particularly worried about the broad
range of uses allowed under heavy commercial zoning, which could include light manufacturing,

recycling centers, and warehouses. Heather noted she preferred the office warehouse concept
over previous drive-through plans for the site.

Mayor Johnson invited the public to make comments.

Rachel Freeman cautioned the Council about proceeding with zone changes that are
incompatible with the surrounding area.

Paul Hancock and Mayor Johnson suggested exploring a development agreement that would lock
in the office warehouse use and potentially include some buffering for neighboring properties.
After extensive discussion, the council decided to table the ordinance, directing staff to work
with Justin on crafting a development agreement. The agreement would specifically allow the
office warehouse use, potentially include buffering requirements, and provide flexibility if the
current plan doesn't work out. The motion to table passed with a 3-2 vote, giving Justin an
opportunity to negotiate terms that would address the council's concerns while allowing him to
develop the property.

14) Consideration of Ordinance #46-2025 approving the Colledge Farms Plat F zone change,
      changing the zoning from TH-5 (transitional holding) to R-1-22 (residential/agriculture)
      on 5.2-acres at approximately 2850 West 1500 North.

Items 14 and 15 were heard together.

Russ Wilson from Symphony Homes presented the proposal. Russ provided background on the
College Farms neighborhood, explaining it was developed in five phases with 98 total lots, an
average lot size of over 18,000 square feet, and included dedicated open space and amenities like
a pool and playground. The proposed development would add nine lots under R-1-22 zoning,
with an average lot size slightly larger than the existing neighborhood. The developer sought to
include this new phase in the existing PRD (Planned Residential Development). Only seven lots
are allowed under the R-1-22 zone. The developer is requesting an additional two lots. Their
justification is that the lot sizes will closely match the existing neighborhood, they are providing
road dedication, and creating landscaping and buffer improvements. They were also one lot short
of their previously approved 98 lots due to an owner combining two lots into one.

The city staff was supportive of adding an additional lot for right-of-way road dedication and
improvement, which would be 8 lots total.

The Council discussed HOA implications, such as developer control, fair HOA management, and
overuse of amenities. They also discussed concerns with setting a precedent by expanding an
existing PRD.

Candace Jones- expressed concern about the precedent set by allowing additional land into the
PRD and the impacts it would have on the area.

The Council discussed the potential effects of using impact fees to pay for road improvements
instead of density in lieu.

The Council discussed and determined that entrance signs and beautification did not justify an
additional lot. They also agreed that one less home in the original PRD (due to combining lots),
does not justify its transfer to another proposal.


Motion:               Chris Condie moved to approve Ordinance #46-2025 the Colledge Farms Plat

17) Consideration of Ordinance #47-2025, approving a Development Code Amendment to
      Chapter 26, Accessory Uses, changing requirements for Detached Accessory Dwelling
      Units.

The proposed change would slightly decrease the minimum lot size from 15,000 to 14,520 square
feet (one-third acre) and limit ADU construction on slopes steeper than 20%. Engineering staff,
including Gary and Brad, argued for more flexibility, believing that with proper engineering,
ADUs could be built on steeper slopes. They viewed slope restrictions as an infringement on
property rights and felt that proper design could address potential challenges. Council members
debated the risks, with Paige Albrecht expressing safety concerns related to the Draper home
sliding incident. They discussed the city's role in ensuring safety versus the property owner's risk.
Michelle Stallings raised questions about geotechnical requirements and soil stability for ADUs.
Gary and Brad suggested that building permit processes would require appropriate engineering
and geotechnical studies.

Motion:               Michelle Stallings moved to approve Ordinance #47-2025, a Development
                      Code Amendment to Chapter 26, Accessory Uses, changing requirements for
                      Detached Accessory Dwelling Units. Paul Hancock seconded the motion.

Roll Call Vote:       YES: Paige Albrecht, Chris Condie, Paul Hancock, Heather Newall,
                      Michelle Stallings. The motion passed unanimously.
