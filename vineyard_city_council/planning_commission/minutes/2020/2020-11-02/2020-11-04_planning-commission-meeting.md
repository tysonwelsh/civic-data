6. PUBLIC HEARINGS
  6.1 ZTA 15.38 Parking Regulations & 15.060.020 Definitions
      Community Development Director, Mr. Brim, introduced ordinance 2020-15. This
      ordinance proposed modified language allowing the Planning Commission the ability to
      reduce onsite parking requirements for specified nonresidential uses. Additionally, this
      ordinance increased front yard parking coverage allowances and amended the method
      used in determining front yard areas.

      Mr. Brim continued to explain that this ordinance would provide an ability for the
      Commission to review applicants for reduced parking at individual sites. Applicants would
      need to submit a parking study to the Commission to be reviewed. He believed that it is
      better for the Commission to approve of these individual cases instead of staff. Mr. Brim
      then read an update to the code: The Planning Commission may use the parking study as
      a basis for reducing the amount of onsite parking required for specific uses defined under
      the following nonresidential categories listed in table 15.38.030(2)(c): Eating & Drinking
      Establishments, Entertainment and Recreational Uses, and Medical Uses.

      Mr. Brim stated that he liked this update because it provides a study from a professional
      with experience in performing parking studies. He then described the process in which an
      applicant would provide a parking study under a site plan to be approved by the City. He
      believed that this would not be a common occurrence. This would create a more
      thoughtful approach to parking instead of using a blanket standard. He then asked the
      Commission for any questions regarding this portion of the ordinance.

      Chair Anthony Jenkins verified with Mr. Brim that these parking reductions would be
      handled at the site plan level of review. Mr. Brim replied that Chair Jenkins was correct
      and added that a building re-use plan would also requirement.

      Commissioner Tim Blackburn asked if there were any other categories of uses that were
      not mentioned in the three (3) categories from the updated code. Mr. Brim stated that
      the three (3) in the code seemed to be the most applicable and other types of businesses
      could apply but would need to get a text amendment to allow it. Commissioner Blackburn
      then asked how the parking study would affect business transitions. Mr. Brim answered
      that the City preforms a zoning check at the business license stage, including checking for
      adequate parking.

      Commissioner Sullivan agreed that it is best for this approval to always go through the
      Commission instead of the staff.

      Mr. Brim mentioned that the other issue is with parking coverage in driveways. He stated
      that there has been demand to have parking on individual’s property instead of on the
      street. Mr. Brim brought up a request for the planning commission to allow for 65%
      hardscape instead of the current 35%/50% hardscape allowance. Mr. Brim liked the
      proposal because it would preserve a considerable amount of yard area while solving the
      issue of on-street parking. This would help older homes become compliant as well as
      create allowance for ADU’s parking requirements. Mr. Brim introduced Brad Gilson with
      Gilson Engineering.

      Mr. Gilson discussed issues with how residents who purchase homes later add on
      concrete pads. This prohibits the builders from doing this to create uniformity in how the

      adding to the ordinance is that it creates uniformity when building. Chair Jenkins
      mentioned that this also prohibits the property owner from pouring hardscape over
      utilities. City Engineer Chris Wilson echoed Chair Jenkins’ concern and agreed that this
      would be beneficial from an engineering standpoint.

 6.2 ZTA 15.34.030 Accessory Buildings, 15.12 Establishment of Districts and Zoning Tables
     and 15.34.150 Home Occupations

      Mr. Brim introduced ordinance 2020-16. This ordinance would allow for accessory
      structures to be used with home occupations. Detached garages would be allowed within
      side yards. It also would clarify setback requirements for residential swimming pools.

                                                                                   November 4, 2020

      PUBLIC HEARING. VICE-CHAIR KNIGHTON SECONDED THE MOTION. ROLL CALL WENT AS

      COMMISSIONER BLACKBURN, AND COMMISSIONER BRADY VOTED AYE. THE MOTION
