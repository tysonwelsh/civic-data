35          Wasatch Front Regional Council
36
37   Ted Knowlton, Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC), presented an overview of its role as a
38   regional governmental organization responsible for long-term transportation planning and funding
39   across multiple counties and cities, including Riverton. WFRC is currently developing a 30-year
40   Regional Transportation Plan through 2055, which serves as the foundation for future roads, transit,
41   and trails projects. For Riverton specifically, three key transit concepts were highlighted: extending
42   the TRAX Red Line toward Mountain View Village and eventually along Bangerter Highway to
43   connect with FrontRunner and the Blue Line; implementing Bus Rapid Transit along Redwood
44   Road; and enhancing bus service along 12600 South. WFRC emphasized that implementation
45   depends on ridership demand, supportive land use, and available funding, and encouraged
46   Riverton to incorporate these concepts into its own transportation master planning to strengthen
47   future funding opportunities and long-term coordination.
48
49   Mr. Knowlton also presented an overview of the Utah Trail Network and the Beehive Bikeway
50   system, which together form a statewide and regional vision for connecting communities through
51   trails for recreation and transportation. These networks integrate existing trails like the Jordan River

 4   studies, and invest local funds are more likely to receive priority.

 8            Budget Kickoff Discussion
 9
10   Nick Geer, Administrative Services Director, presented an overview of the budget process,
11   emphasizing a more flexible and collaborative approach than in past years. He outlined the required
12   timeline, tentative budget by May and final adoption by June, but noted the goal is to complete the
13   budget earlier and improve transparency through better communication. He explained that Council
14   will review revenues, operations, debt, and capital projects in stages, with departments directly
15   presenting their five-year needs during work sessions in late summer and fall. The intent is to give
16   Council clearer insight into priorities, improve long-range planning, and adopt a more conservative,
17   data-driven budgeting philosophy that avoids committing to projects before revenues are secure.
18
19   Mr. Geer highlighted sales tax as Riverton’s primary revenue source, making up about 43% of total
20   tax revenue and funding most general operations, with police and fire property taxes together
21   accounting for most of the rest. Sales tax is economically sensitive and reported with a two-to-three-
22   month delay, making forecasting challenging. While current year collections are slightly below last
23   year, large months like November and December are still pending. There was discussion among
24   Councilmembers and staff about the importance of diversifying the tax base, budgeting
25   conservatively, prioritizing projects based on confirmed cash flow, and preparing for potential
26   legislative changes affecting both sales and property taxes.

29            Timberline Development Discussion
30
31   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, presented a proposed development along 13400
32   South and Redwood Road, where a developer is seeking to rezone the currently commercial
33   property to higher-density residential. Key concerns raised by the Planning Commission and public
34   focused on road connectivity, as two older neighborhood roads currently stub into the site without
35   modern curb, gutter, or sidewalks. The original plan proposed full roadway connections, but this
36   raised issues about traffic, parking spillover, and infrastructure impacts on long-established
37   neighborhoods. In response, a revised concept was shared that would close those road
38   connections, provide only pedestrian and utility links, and instead create a new primary access
39   point from Redwood Road, while still preserving future public access for adjacent undeveloped
40   land.
41
42   The Council generally expressed support for this revised road layout, noting it better protects
43   existing neighborhoods while still allowing connectivity through pedestrian paths. They emphasized
44   the importance of minimizing disruption to 40-year-old communities, reducing traffic conflicts on
45   Redwood Road, and keeping maintenance costs low by favoring simple concrete pathways over
46   landscaped strips.
47
48   Representatives from Fieldstone, the developer, confirmed the project would be an HOA community
49   due to the private road, with the HOA responsible for maintenance and snow removal, and
50   emphasized their desire for Council guidance on closing the two road connections so they can
51   proceed with the revised concept. It was noted that the public has not yet seen the new road-

 2   emergency services and public input will occur before any final decisions.

 4            Sentinel Ridge Median
 5
 6   Mr. Lethbridge explained that increasing development along Sentinel Ridge Boulevard has created
 7   significant traffic and safety issues, leading engineers to require a center median between 13400
 8   South and 13800 South to restrict left-turn movements and reduce conflicts. The median design is
 9   not optional and is based on traffic studies, with the goal of improving safety while still allowing
10   development to proceed. The median will prevent certain direct turns, meaning residents may need
11   to travel up to half a mile to reroute. Emergency services have reviewed the design and confirmed
12   it will not impede response times.
13
14   Council discussion focused on how the median should be treated aesthetically and practically. They
15   strongly opposed landscaped medians due to long-term maintenance and water costs, favoring
16   concrete or low-maintenance designs instead. While some supported incorporating public art or
17   decorative concrete, possibly tied to Mountain Ridge High School, to give the area identity, there
18   was broad agreement that any design must minimize future costs and disruptions. Council
19   emphasized the importance of installing the median quickly so new residents understand the traffic
20   patterns from the start.
