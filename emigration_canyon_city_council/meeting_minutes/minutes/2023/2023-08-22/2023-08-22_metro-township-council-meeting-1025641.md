Reports from other Municipal Providers
Emigration Canyon Community Council (ECCC)
Bill Tobey stated the ECCC currently faced a shortage of members, with only
nine members, and not all of them were actively participating. This shortage was causing delays
in important projects, including the website migration and the fuel break project at Sunnydale.
Mr. Tobey reported on the progress with Salt Lake City to obtain permission for fuel treatment
on municipal watershed property. However, it seemed unlikely that approval would be granted
before the Unified Fire Authority (UFA) fuels crew disbanded for the season.
ECCC was actively pursuing a new funding opportunity through the Forest Service and Utah
Division of Forestry, Fire, and State Lands. They planned to apply for up to $250,000 to support
planning activities and potentially up to $10 million for project implementation. This grant had a
cost-sharing requirement of 10 to 25 percent, and the application deadline was set for October
17, 2023.
Salt Lake County Animal Services held a pet chipping and licensing clinic on July 29, 2023, at
the fire station. While the turnout was not high, those who attended were satisfied with the
service provided.

Parade of Home Show Project
Lance Williams, Producer, stated that the Parade of Homes Show would be
filming in the Pinecrest area. The filming was scheduled to commence in the winter and would
include behind-the-scenes shots and interviews. He expressed the desire to support the canyon
and the community, mentioning collaboration with the Emigration Improvement District (EID) on
a new sewer system. Additionally, he aimed to raise public awareness about Firewise and the
fire season through the show.
He expressed a willingness to work with the metro township to address community issues. To
minimize disruption to the communities, the exact location of the build was intentionally
undisclosed in the show, and viewers would not know which canyon it was filmed in. A piece of
land had been acquired for the project, and a home would be constructed from the ground up.
The show would feature a building segment demonstrating the latest construction techniques.
The Parade of Homes Show was entering its fifth season and boaster a weekly viewership of
approximately 50,000. It aired on CBS channel 2, typically on either Saturday or Sunday at
around 10:00 or 11:00 am. Additionally, three episodes were available for viewing on the
website paradeofhomesshow.com.

Property Purchase and Lease Agreement
Polly McLean stated to purchase property located at 6291 E Emigration Canyon
Road, the Council needs to authorize the Mayor to purchase the property and enter into a lease
agreement. The amount of the purchase is $425,000 with a settlement deadline next week. The
purchase contract was entered into at the end of July. The earnest money is only refundable if
there are issues with the title report. The terms of the sale provide permission to the seller to
bury the power lines on the property. The buyer will lease back the building to the seller for a
two-year term for $1, after which a year-to-year extension will be considered. The seller will pay
for utilities and the buyer will pay insurance. The buyer can work on the restroom and pay
closing costs. If the Council approves the authorization of the purchase, it will go through on
August 30, 2023. The title report and survey were reviewed and look good.

Legislation Converting Townships to Cities and Towns
Polly McLean stated Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns, and Copperton
Metro Township has taken the lead on the legislation. Input had been gathered from all the
metro townships, and efforts were being made to have it presented at the interim committee in
August. However, due to the absence of the legislative attorney, it could not be reviewed at that
time. The hope was to include it in the interim committee in September, and if approved, it

town.

Council Member Brems asked what the distinction is between a city and a

Ms. McLean stated the designation as a city occurs when there are over 1,000
residents, at which point it becomes a city. Once designated as a city, there are some slight
differences, including increased auditing requirements and a larger budget. Instead of having a
town clerk, a city typically has a city recorder. The key advantage of converting to a city is that it
provides more control over finances. If the metro township continued to work with the Greater
Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD) and contracted with them to provide services, it
would also have more autonomy in decision-making.
