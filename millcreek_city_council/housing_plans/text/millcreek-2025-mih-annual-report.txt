3. Describe each action taken between August 1, 2024 and July 31, 2025 to implement the
tasks listed in Question 2. Include how the actions taken support implementation of the
strategy.
A
Millcreek adopted an affordable housing incentives ordinance, that includes an incentive of
waiving commercial requirements for qualifying affordable housing projects.

E
Millcreek Staff has adopted several text amendments to its ADU Ordinance based on barriers
affecting detached ADU's. Proposed amendments include increasing the maximum allowable
size for ADU's to up to 1,000 SF (from 850 SF) and permitting detached ADU's on Flag lots.

                                                                                                509
F
Millcreek Staff have started revising the city's mixed-use corridors, as identified in the Millcreek's
General Plan and is exploring land use and zone amendments to selected mixed-use corridors,
that could tie additional density bonuses to the provision of affordable housing, pursuant to HB
37.

H
Millcreek adopted Affordable Housing Ordinance with zoning incentives that significantly reduce
land use regulation, including reductions on parking requirements for residential development
along transit investment corridors.

U
The Meadowbrook and Millcreek Station Area Plans will work collaboratively with South Salt
Lake and Millcreek to establish a vision and implementation plan for the two TRAX stations,
focusing on maximizing Transit Oriented Development potential, enhancing multimodal transit
connectivity, and supporting diverse and inclusive housing options.

J
Millcreek amended its single-household design standards to encourage flexibility to help entice
owner-occupied developments, particularly on smaller lots. We updated the building envelope
and increased overall height, allowing small lots (5,000 sf or less) to be more buildable.

O
We are partnering with CDCU on two projects that provide housing for households earning 50%
AMI or less. One project, the Howick, is under construction. The other project, the Morrissey, is
awaiting tax credit financing. Combined, these projects will provide over 200 affordable
dwellings. In both cases, we have agreements with the property developer to reimburse the cost
of the building permit using CRA set-asides. For the Morrissey, we sought and obtained a
county grant for pedestrian safety to construct missing sidewalk between the Morrissey and the
Meadowbrook TRAX Station. This grant increased the Morrissey’s overall score in the tax credit
application process.

L
As part of the zoning code update that began in 2023, Millcreek Planning Staff created the
Affordable Housing Incentives Ordinance that includes an incentive of waiving/reducing impact
fees for qualifying affordable housing projects located in the R-4, RM, MD, or C Zones.

G
The adopted Affordable Housing Ordinance allows developers to build more units than typically
permitted in exchange for including qualifiable moderate-income housing units on their projects.
In addition, the proposed ordinance includes an accelerated and streamlined development
review process for moderate -income housing developments.

Millcreek amended its Residential Mixed Zone design standards to encourage flexibility to help
entice owner-occupied developments, particularly on infill lots.

5.      What barriers were encountered by the county or municipality between August 1,
2024 and July 31, 2025 while implementing this strategy? Documenting barriers does
not exempt the community from reporting plan progress The minimum required number
of strategies must advance for compliance, regardless of the barriers faced. If no barriers
were experienced, write "None."
A
Some opposition from residents who believe the city shouldn’t offer or incentivize affordable
housing in general. The opinions from residents who oppose affordable housing in Millcreek are
a small percentage.

E
Staff's ability to further reduce ADU regulations continues to be difficult due to resident
concerns, mostly in terms of neighborhood compatibility. ADUs continue to be expensive to
develop, due to construction and utility costs.

F
Spot zoning, neighborhood compatibility overhaul and fear of decreases in home values by the
residents.Land values are high on these corridors. Staff asked one potential applicant to
consider additional density through a development agreement, subject to an affordability target
of $450,000. The developer declined, citing land costs.

H
Parking continues to be expensive, and an impediment to development. Despite proximity to
transit, bus routes and TRAX provide limited service at certain times of day, and it may not be a
viable option for certain jobs. 2

U
Millcreek is observing a lack of public engagement and interest. Our opportunities for
redevelopment in the Meadowbrook station area is limited. The most significant barrier is a lack
of public open space and walkable connections in the Meadowbrook area, between Big
Cottonwood Creek and 3900 South. Although significant housing is being developed, the
streetscapes in the area are an impediment to walkability.

J
Given the status of the housing market and any real enforcement in place, developers typically
purchase these smaller lots and develop, row-style townhomes that are not affordable. Their
reasoning for having to list them well above 80%-100% AMI is due to the cost of land.Given the
choice, many developers are opting for higher-end housing products on smaller lots

O
The developer is struggling with construction costs and the competitiveness of affordable
housing tax credits. While we have done all we can feasibly do to reduce the bureaucratic
burden on these projects, other constraints have slowed the development of the Morrissey. The
Howick, however, is under construction and should be occupied within a year.

L
Staff has discussed incentives with developers; However, not many developers have taken
advantage of these incentives. The incentives may not be particularly attractive considering land
values.

G
There are many residents that oppose further deregulation for affordable housing. These same
residents are concerned that regulations and incentives for affordable housing are
disproportionate geographically and are aimed at the west side of Millcreek versus the east
side.

V
Not the desired product to sell, according to developers.
