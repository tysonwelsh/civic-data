3. Discuss Brief Overview of Projects and Activities in Emergency Planning and
Preparedness that Benefits White City

        Brian Buckhout, Emergency Planner, said currently the city has the multijurisdictional
hazard mitigation plan that was incorporated across the county. Every city was able to contribute
to this. It captures all White City’s reports, data and historical risk. They also captured the data
of neighboring cities around White City and added it to White City’s report. They captured
changes in priorities and development, previous hazard events, jurisdiction specific
vulnerabilities, and jurisdictional information they will be using to generate annexes. The current
status if the hazard mitigation plan is under review by the Department of Emergency
Management (UDEM) and also FEMA. The plan for the county are expected to be done before
September. The cities will have an opportunity to review the plan and adopt it. Even though it’s



                       White City Council Meeting Minutes – Page 1
not an operation plan, there are benefits from adopting the plan. The recommendations will allow
time for the county and cities to make edits and resubmit or adopt the plan.

        Mr. Buckhout said a hazard mitigation is where they go through a threat assessment,
identify hazards and describe those hazards, identify community assets, analyze those impacts
and summarize the vulnerabilities. That’s what they call a THIRA, which is a Threat, Hazard,
Identification and Risk Assessment. The city will receive a report regarding the top hazards. He
said the consultants they worked with did a lot of public outreaches which went on for about a
month, so they were able to capture a lot of data.

       The Building Resilience Infrastructure and Communities program (BRIC) is a federal
program that has been discontinued. This is a sizeable program that supports state and local
with mitigation grants. They are looking to see what other kinds of mitigation grants would be
available. The MSD and other cities have not used the BRIC grant so he did not think there
would be any impact with the discontinuation of the program.

      The EMPG, Emergency Management Performance, grant funds a lot of emergency
management positions across the country. UFA has two positions that are completely funded
here by this grant. He said they are asking cities and jurisdictions to plan their budget to not have
the EMPG grant in the future. Also, SB97, an emergency shelter bill for animals did not pass.
SB182, regarding Code Red also, did not pass. Code Red is for sheltering individuals from the
extreme heat. They will be looking for other ways to fund this need.

        Evacuation Zones is a hot topic with the recent Palisades Fire in California. They started
off with a zonal approach for every city. The plan is that they will have a county wide evac zone
map. These areas could also be used for sheltering in place, pods, and distribution for logistical
hubs. This allows cities to prioritize the evacuation by those most impacted by the hazard and
limits the need to evacuate larger areas than what’s needed.

       Mr. Buckhout discussed some training opportunities in the near future. He reviewed the
great shakeout event that took place where they include radio ham operators. They are looking
to increase the number of radio ham operators that they can leverage and work with in case of
emergencies. The MSD is looking to procure satellite phones.

3.      UNIFIED FIRE AUTHORITY REPORT
        Chief Ken Aldridge said the UFA budget was discussed at the last Board Meeting. The
member fee increase will pay for two new engine companies being placed into service in Eagle
Mountain and Kearns. Their recruit camp has 33 firefighters and is progressing well. Last month
they have been focusing on wildland firefighting, hazmat operations, technical rescue, and multi-
company live fire rescue scenarios. They finish on May 14 with graduation in Riverton. The next
camp, camp 60, will begin August 4, 2025, and they expect to hire another 20 to 24 firefighters.
He discussed the battalion chief promotion process. Eight candidates made the roster and that
will be a two-year registry for promotions as positions become available.

        Unified’s Wildland Division is actively preparing for wildland fire season and expects it to
be busy. They’ve hired 40 seasonal firefighters to the hand crew and engines where they will be
responding all along the Wasatch Front and out of state. He said they are excited to be in the
final phase of their hotshot crew’s certification process. It’s a 20-member type one team and they
are the real deal firefighters being deployed nationally. It’s been a long process of four years to
get them certified as a type one team.

     Chief Wade Russell was promoted to Assistant Chief over Administration and Planning.
Wade Watkins was promoted to the new Fire Marshall position where he will be working in the

4.    UNIFIED POLICE DEPARTMENT REPORT

       Chief Morse and Detective Josh Smith were present from UPD. Detective Smith said the
numbers look good with a few isolated incidents. Their calls are still in the mid 30’s, but as the
weather warms up, they could increase. As it warms up, people sometimes leave things in their
vehicles that can be stolen. He suggested making sure all items are stowed away and not out in
the open. Property crimes have been increasing, and car prowls are their biggest issue in the
summer. The community knows who should be here and not so please call if you see anything
suspicious.

       Chief Morse said they are having issues with people reporting things to Facebook rather
than the police. She said to please call the police. Outside agencies come into White City for
enforcement and normally will let UPD know. However, there has been a couple over the last
few months that have been more of a task force setup with different agencies working together
and the notification of UPD has been slipping. She has been reminding them to give UPD a
courtesy call to let them know what’s going on. She said they are in the middle of the budget
and are still working on it and maintaining a 5% proposed budget increase minimum.

       Paul Ashton said as the townships became cities, the makeup of the MSD Board of
Trustees had to be adjusted because it was based on the old metro township model. That statute
automatically appointed the mayor as the member of the MSD Board and the mayor pro-tem as
the alternate member to act in the mayor’s place as needed. When the legislature appealed the
metro township statute, they forgot to put in place the alternate. So as part of the clean-up bill
this year the Professional Special District Association asked the legislature to pass a bill that
allows for an alternate MSD Board of Trustees members to be appointed.

      6.4    CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 2025-05-03 ADOPTING THE WHITE CITY
             ENGINEERING STANDARDS & SPECIFICATIONS

        Chad Anderson said HB368 was passed this year requiring with provisions that require
inspection standards to be adopted by each municipality. With engineering under the County
until recently, White City has not had these standards. He said he presented the proposed
engineering standards and specifications to the Council at the last meeting. Since then, they
have presented it to the Planning Commission, where they received their approval. He was now
bringing it back to the Council for final approval.

       Mr. Anderson reviewed the changes made after the discussion at the last meeting to
include the city engineer having the ability to approve exceptions to the standards. There was
also some discussion on standard provision 255. In the County standards enforced previously,
there was a provision that required anyone cutting into roads to provide a mill and overlay wider
than just the trench if the cut was longer than a couple hundred feet long. He said APWA
standards have been commonly adopted across the state and other cities do provide for
exceptions. He said his recommendation based on Council discussion at the last meeting would
be a mill and overlay would never be required where there’s an upcoming city project that will
mill and overlay or reconstruct the road within two years. Otherwise, the mill and overlay will be
required when the pigment is less than three years old or the street cut is longer than 300 feet
and the pavement is 3-7 years old and not required if the pavement is greater than seven years
old.

      Paul Ashton said on behalf of the Water District, he appreciates the exceptions.

        Mr. Anderson said there was a lot of discussion on the lights. He said he had not yet
received the light details, so he removed the light details from the standards. Once he receives
the light detail from the vendor, he will add it to the standards.

      Council Member Cardenaz, seconded by Mayor Flint, motioned to approve
Resolution No. 2025-05-03 Adopting the White City Engineering Standards &
Specifications. The motion passed by unanimous vote.

        Jeff Miller, Planner, said he was covering for Brian Tucker. He discussed the proposed

replaced its subdivision Title 18 as well as the zoning ordinance, which was Title 19. Staff have

infrastructure and public improvements to be installed by a developer was inadvertently left out
of the adopted Title 19 in 2023. These improvement installation requirements are subject to the
limitations on exactions in the Utah Code. He said this ordinance was presented to the Planning
Commission, which was approved.
