2 Administrative Reports
            2.1    Discussion regarding The Christmas Box Angel Monument for Herriman Cemetery
                   - Anthony Teuscher, Deputy Director of Parks, Recreation and Events
Deputy Director of Parks, Recreation and Events Anthony Teuscher reported a project suggested by resident
Christa Benward. The Christmas Box Angel monument is associated with the ceremony in the Richard Paul
Evans novel, The Christmas Box. This monument was meant to be a place where parents who have lost a child
can come to grieve and mourn their loss. There is a nationwide candle light vigil, observed on December 6th of
each year, where the public may gather for a brief program and healing ceremony. He reported there are over
130 angel monuments nationwide.

The price of the Angel statue is $15,500 and would be acquired through donations and fundraising by the
community. The base and associated landscaping was estimated to cost $10,000-$15,000 and would be provided
by the City.

Resident Christa Benward relayed her desire why she would like to bring the Christmas Box Angel monument.
The Council expressed their appreciation to Ms. Benward for bringing this idea forward and offered their
condolences to the families who have lost a child.

           2.2      South Hills Master Development Agreement Discussion - Blake Thomas, Community
                    Development Director
Community Development Director Blake Thomas offered a follow-up discussion to offer input on several items
regarding the South Hills Master Development Agreement. City Attorney Chase Andrizzi interjected he had
reviewed the agreement and determined the City did not have an obligation to grant the density provision
request. Community Development Director Thomas informed the Council the applicant had recently decided
not to pursue the request for additional density for the land donated by the City.

Director Thomas explained property acquired from Rosecrest Communities known as pod 35 and 39 have been
requested to be incorporated into the master development agreement boundaries. The pods are zone as

            2.3     Discussion of a text change to the M-1 (Manufacturing) Zone to add Personal
                    Instruction Services as a permitted use (File No. Z2020-048) - Michael Maloy, City
                    Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy explained the land uses currently allowed in the M-1 Zone. He noted the personal
instruction service would be established primarily to provide informational, instructional, personal improvement,
or other similar services. He anticipated the uses to include art and music schools, driving and computer
instruction, gymnastic and dance studios or handicraft or hobby instruction, and martial arts training.

            2.4     Discussion pertaining to bonding options for Water infrastructure - Alan Rae,
                    Finance Director
Finance Director Alan Rae explained the team had been looking into options for funding for water infrastructure
priorities. Public Works Director Justun Edwards offered an overview of the projects which would need funding
to complete including the North Herriman Water Improvement line, existing zone 4 pump station upgrade,
zone 5 VFD pump station, east Herriman water improvements, additional east Herriman water improvements
and the juniper canyon water improvements culinary and secondary water lines.

            2.5      Discussion pertaining to processes for the Standard Operating Procedures, Request
                     for Proposals, and the issuance of checks - Tami Moody, Assistant City Manager and
                     Alan Rae, Finance Director
Assistant City Manager Tami Moody provided an update on internal controls and standard operating procedures
for the issuance of checks. She explained the procedures are a working document, constantly being updating and
reworked as they are influenced by ordinances, state statutes, and other outside influences. Finance Director
Rae emphasized the procedures are based on ordinances and other parameters outlined by the City Council.

Assistant City Manager Moody explained the Request for Proposal process has been outlined with specific
requirement.

6 City Council Board and Committee Reports
Councilmember Clint Smith reported the Conference of Mayor’s meeting was cancelled due to the Utah League
of Cities and Towns conference being held this week so there would not be any conflicting obligations for
members.

Councilmember Smith reported on the Utah Transit Authority (UTA) Advisory Board meeting and noted it was
interesting to see the effects of public transit through the pandemic and how the organization is moving forward.
He explained the services across the board took a dramatic hit as schedules were adjusted and services had been
reduced. Councilmember Smith stated he had a report from the Executive Director he wanted to disseminate
to the Council which indicated the organization implemented all of their normal route schedule in August even
though ridership hasn’t recovered to the pre-pandemic ridership. He suggested remote employment may have
a long term effect of frontrunner ridership.

Councilmember Smith reported on the facility construction for South Valley Sewer and noted there had been
some delays due to the pandemic and it was anticipated the construction would be finalized by the end of January
2021.

Councilmember Smith reported on the Utah Department of Transportation commission meeting which was
held the previous week. He offered public comment on the transit line anticipated to go through the automall
property. He offered appreciation to Community Development Director Blake Thomas for the work conducted.
He informed the Council of the property trade approval received.

Councilmember Smith concluded his report with the announcement that he had been nominated to serve as a
board member for the Utah League of Cities and Townes which offers representation for our community.

Councilmember Ohrn reported on the Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District and noted the area clean-
up was being conducted and was open to Herriman residents. She encouraged residents to go online to reserve
a dumpster to be brought to their home, if needed.

Councilmember Shields reported on the Mosquito Abatement District West Nile testing. Councilmember Ohrn
asked about on site testing. Councilmember Shields explained the process for certification and reported the
district has been approved to conduct onsite testing.

             7.2    Public Hearing of a Fire/EMS Impact Fee Facilities Plan and Fire/EMS Impact Fee
                    Analysis - Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney
City Attorney Andrizzi reported the City had recently contracted with Zions bank to conduct a study for
Fire/EMS Services Impact Fee Analysis and the Impact Fee Facilities Plan. Currently Fire and EMS impact fees
are being collected by the Unified Fire Service Area and the City is looking to begin collecting those fees as we
look to leave the Fire Service District. The impact fee rate changed minimally, which would be paid as new
development comes into the City. Attorney Andrizzi reported there would be two public hearings on this item
tonight. First, would be for the Impact Fee Facilities Plan and the Impact Fee Analysis and the second, would
be for the enactment. The enactment of the fee would go into effect the end of December.

Mayor Pro Tempore Henderson opened the public hearing.

No comments were offered.
