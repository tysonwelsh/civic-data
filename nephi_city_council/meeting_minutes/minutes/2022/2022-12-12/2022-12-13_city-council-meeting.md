                       Nephi City Council Work Session Minutes
                                  December 13, 2022

1.    Mayor Seely and City Administrator Seth reported on a meeting they had attended
     with Mona City about the growth areas of each city and how future gas distribution
     responsibilities would go for future growth.

2. The council discussed the Electric Rate Study prepared by David Berg.


3.    The council discussed that during the 138 kV project, the city may reach the
     maximum load allowed by the current Rocky Mountain Power connection during their
     peak summer season in 2024. City staff had explored options for providing enough
     electricity through this period of time to avoid any blackouts. The option that was
     being recommended was some temporary engines that were fueled by natural gas.

     UMPA has been consulting with city staff on the options being explored. Now that
     the city had been leaning toward the temporary engines, UMPA has offered to
     pursue a longer-term solution that will benefit Nephi City and UMPA. They would like
     to install two engines in a permanent structure (similar to the Provo Plant) that would
     be fueled by natural gas. This would provide the solution to the peaking problem for
     Nephi City but would also help deliver peak capacity for UMPA in future years as well
     as help with black starts. This plant could also serve as an emergency power
     backup for Nephi City in future years. The plant is expected to produce 4.9 MW and
     is expected to cost approximately $9,030,000. The UMPA Technical Board and
     Board of Directors approved the project yesterday.

     To help facilitate this project, staff recommended constructing the gas gate station
     which was to be built anyway for the temporary engines, providing space in
     substation #2 for the interconnection, leasing land for the facility near substation #2
     for near zero rate, provide fiber optic connections for UMPA to control the facility,
     and provide some site clearing services for the building to be placed. Nephi City
     would then sell gas to UMPA when the facility is used. By offering these
     concessions, not only does this help UMPA invest in a $9 million long-term project
     and provide a solution for Nephi City’s potential peaking problem in 2024, but it also
     keeps rates lower for all UMPA members, including Nephi City.

4.    Mr. Atkinson informed the council about the city being approached by a company
     interested in pursuing a solar project to the west of the Nephi Municipal Airport and
     east of the city’s sewer lagoons. The staff spoke about this to the Council a number
     of months ago. They have continued to look for options for their project. Part of their
     project would involve some land owned by the city in the area.
     As staff has evaluated the project there is potential to benefit several different city
     functions. The first would be the city’s electric fund. This project would need to
     construct an electric line from the solar arrays to the city’s future 138 kV line and pay
     an interconnect fee. The interconnect fee would assist the city in funding the 138 kV
     transmission line. The solar project would be for 49 MW and would also include a 49
     MW battery. Both would be a help to the city in providing power backup and
     redundancy if the city transmission lines were to experience an outage. UMPA has
     also expressed interest in possibly purchasing the project after the first 15 years of
     operation. Cost of removal for the solar panels is built into the project. Since the
     project would need to construct a transmission line along Meadow Lane back to the
     city’s 138 kV line and then convey the line to the city, Nephi Power could then hang
     distribution lines and use these lines to bring city-owned power to the airport and the
     sewer lagoons. This solar project transmission line would also help to partially
     complete a transmission loop for the city’s distribution system along the west end of
     the valley.

     The airport is currently served by Rocky Mountain Power and using Nephi Power
     instead will reduce the costs of providing power to the airport facilities. Having power
     to the sewer lagoons would also provide an opportunity to install aerators. Aerators
     help to increase the flow of oxygen in the sewer lagoons which aid in the breakdown
     of solids and lengthen the life of the city’s lagoons. The sewer could also install a
     headworks to prevent larger items from entering the lagoons from the main flow line.
     The lease revenue from the solar project could also assist the sewer fund with their
     future projects. The sewer fund does not have a very healthy fund balance
     compared with the needed projects identified in the sewer master plan.

     One challenge that would arise is that the land that would be used for the solar
     project had also been held in reserve for a new primary cell. Additional land
     acquisition may be necessary if this option is pursued. The city could also
     reconfigure the primary cell to occupy some of the land currently owned by the city
     and still allow for the solar project. This option would need to be discussed in further
     detail with the solar company.


5. Mr. Atkinson reported on a potential airport lease that is wanting a long-term lease
   for financing purposes. The council directed him to move forward.


6.   Mr. Atkinson presented the idea of building a Pump Track at the Jamestown
     Park. Prior to the Jamestown subdivision, the raw property contained a number of
     bike tracks that were undoubtedly used by the local kids in the area. As part of the
     Jamestown subdivision, a portion of land was deeded to the city from UDOT with the
     idea of creating a neighborhood park.
     Recreation Director Craig Oswald has attended a number of conferences where
     pump tracks have been highlighted. Pump tracks are bicycle tracks that have a
     number of small hills and turns and enable the bicycle user to “pump” the bike to gain
     momentum.
     Councilor Memmott expressed his concern that currently we had many irons in the
     fire in the recreation department such as the Greenhalgh property ball fields and the
     commitment to paying for the Hive. Mr. Atkinson said that we do have our first
     payment collected for the first Hive payment and many tournaments are scheduled.
     He stated that last year the city put $11,000 in the Parks Improvement capital
          account. Mr. Atkinson said that Recreation Director thinks that we can get grant
          funds to pay for most of the Jamestown Park.
          The council directed staff to report back to them where all our different sources of
          funds are coming from and where we stand in paying for recreational improvements.

       7. The council discussed the need for sewer impact fees. Public Works Director Kyle
          Marchant said they are currently videoing many of the sewer lines to see where they
          want to direct their attention to in the sewer maintenance and improvement projects.

       8. Councilor Memmott reported on the water rights meeting.

       9. Mayor Seely reported on the Winter Classic wrestling tournament that was held at
          the Hive. The internet services were not adequate. He noted that there were many
          positive remarks about the facility. Councilor Memmott observed that people may
          not be aware of the additional parking available at the west campus parking lot.

       10. Councilor Memmott moved to convene into executive session to discuss the
          character and competence of an individual and real property. Councilor Ostler
          seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote. The meeting
          adjourned at 8:18 p.m
