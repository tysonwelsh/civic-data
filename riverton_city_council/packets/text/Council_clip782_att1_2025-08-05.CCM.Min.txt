21            Code Requiring Cast Walls Along Canals Discussion
22
23   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the fencing ordinance,
24   amended in 2015, requires six-foot-high decorative solid-core precast concrete fences along
25   ditches, canals, or irrigation lines throughout the City, excluding alternatives like hollow foam
26   core or fiberglass mixes. This standard aims to ensure safety and durability, and unlike other
27   fencing requirements, there is no size-based waiver for these waterway fences. While some
28   confusion exists about when this applies, especially between residential and commercial
29   zones or smaller projects, the current ordinance stands firm on this for waterways.
30
31   Mayor Staggs recalled supporting alternative fence types along irrigation canals and similar
32   areas, citing concerns about flexibility and cost differences between traditional precast fencing
33   and alternatives. He noted that while some Councilmembers strongly favored solid fencing in
34   certain zones, he preferred more options for developers in other areas.
35
36   Councilmember McCay expressed support for increasing flexibility in the fencing ordinance.
37   She clarified that the ordinance language covers ditches, canals, and irrigation lines, which the
38   City has also applied to creek channels. She acknowledged the original ordinance’s safety
39   motivation but suggested that responsibility also lies with homeowners. She requested staff to
40   develop more lenient fencing options for future Council consideration, emphasizing potential
41   cost savings and aesthetic benefits.
42
43   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained that historically, canal companies have not had input or
44   control over fencing adjacent to their canals. He noted that Utah law protects canal companies
45   from liability related to drownings in canals as long as easements are not disturbed. The
46   companies do not regulate what occurs immediately outside their canal boundaries and often
47   do not maintain fences or gates along canal roads. He emphasized that canal companies
48   generally show little interest in managing or enforcing restrictions on surrounding properties.
49
50   Councilmember McDougal asked for clarification on how changes to fencing requirements
51   would impact developments already approved. Staff explained that ordinance changes do not
     Page | 4 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   apply retroactively; previously approved projects remain under the standards in place at the
 2   time of approval. Amendments could be requested but would require a new review and
 3   hearing process by the Planning Commission, making such changes unlikely due to time and
 4   cost.
 5
 6   Councilmember McDougal expressed opposition to changing the fencing ordinance, citing
 7   fairness to residents who approved existing developments and concerns about potential "bait
 8   and switch" scenarios. He noted the practical benefits of masonry fences for weed control and
 9   fire safety compared to vinyl, especially as canal trails are paved to create a unified
10   appearance. He was open to considering variations of masonry-style fences but opposed
11   allowing vinyl fencing due to safety and aesthetic concerns.
12
13   Councilmember McDougal emphasized the importance of including clear language in any
14   ordinance change to confirm that previously approved developments would remain subject to
15   the fencing standards in place at the time of their approval, with changes applying only to
16   future developments. Councilmember Haymond requested information on the structural
17   strength differences between solid core and foam core fences, noting concerns about foam
18   core durability based on local examples. Lastly, Councilmember Pierucci expressed interest
19   in understanding how other cities regulate canal fencing and requested approximate cost
20   comparisons per linear foot to assess potential impacts on consumers.

22            Day of Service Discussion
23
24   Sheril Garn, Events and Operations Director, requested guidance from the Council regarding
25   the City's role in organizing events for the annual Day of Service. She suggested possibly
26   directing volunteers to existing projects on the JustServe website and encouraged residents to
27   focus on helping within their own neighborhoods, such as assisting elderly residents with yard
28   work. Additionally, the idea of providing extra dump passes to church groups or organizations
29   for neighborhood clean-up efforts was proposed.
30
31   Councilmembers and staff reflected on past experiences, emphasizing the value of community
32   involvement and volunteer projects that foster a sense of ownership. It was noted that large,
33   City-organized events can be labor-intensive and sometimes inefficient, especially when
34   volunteer turnout is insufficient, requiring staff to complete unfinished work. Instead, several
35   expressed support for more localized, organic service efforts driven by neighborhoods,
36   churches, or community groups with staff providing support and oversight as needed.
37   Suggestions included improving communication through the JustServe website, encouraging
38   residents to identify local needs, providing resources such as dumpster passes, and tracking
39   volunteer participation to recognize community contributions. The overall consensus favored
40   empowering residents to take initiative in their neighborhoods, supported by City coordination
41   and improved publicity, rather than focusing solely on one large annual event.
