1.1 Consideration of ZM-19-012, Rezone from R-1-8 to R-2-6.5 for the Purposes of
Establishing a Planned Unit Development Consisting of 16 Dwellings Location: 1315
East Magda Lane Applicant: Raed Salem Planner: Blaine Gehring
Blaine Gehring said Salem Holdings was requesting the rezone of properties located at 1315
Magda Lane from R-1-8 to R-2-6.5 to facilitate the development of a twin-home PUD on the
property. The subject properties total 1.26 acres with a single-family home and three
duplexes on them. They are adjacent to R-1-8, R-1-10, and R-2-8 zoning with access off of
1300 East. The applicant would like to demolish the existing single-family home, renovate
the existing duplexes, and build four new twin homes and a new single-family home in a
PUD. The existing duplexes are legally non-conforming, but to create the PUD with new
twin homes the R-2-6.5 zone would be required. At their meeting on December 3, 2019, the

with a development agreement. Staff recommended the Planning Commission continue the

additional information would be needed for the development agreement. Gehring said the

1.2 Consideration of ZM-19-018, Rezone from R-1-10 to R-1-6 to Facilitate a Flag lot
Location: 967 E Garden Drive Applicant: Peter Tolman Planner: Erin O’Kelley

Erin O’Kelley said the applicant would like to demolish the existing house on the property
and do a two-lot subdivision for two new single-family homes. In order to qualify for a flag

Millcreek Planning Commission Meeting Minutes 18 December 2019 Page 3 of 10
lot subdivision under the Millcreek code, the applicant must apply for the subdivision under
the R-1-6 Zone to meet setback and area requirements. The existing lot is located on an
interior local neighborhood street surrounded by single-family housing. The lot currently
does not have any public improvements along the street. The existing road width is
approximately 26 feet wide. The existing house was built in 1949 with a remodel in 1996.
There are a lot of mature trees on the property in the back yard. Both driveways on the
property are dirt. Property is currently zoned R-1-10 on an interior neighborhood street.
Other properties along the street have various zoning designations such as R-1-5, R-4-8.5,
and R-2-10.

O’Kelley said the subject property was designated as Neighborhood 1 in the General Plan.
This designation is specifically for single-family neighborhoods with a density
recommendation of 1-6 dwellings per acre. The existing single-family house on a 20,908
square foot lot is a density ratio of 2 units per acre. The rezone to R-1-6 would create a
density ratio of 4 units per acre. Staff recommended a finding that the proposal was
compatible with the Millcreek General Plan and future land use map under the following
considerations: the proposal would be catered to single-family development, would not
exceed the maximum density ratio allowed in Neighborhood 1 (6 units per acre), would be
located near amenities, supported the redevelopment of an underperforming property,
encouraged additional invest in the neighborhood, and stayed within the existing mass, scale,
and height of the immediate neighborhood.

O’Kelley said the applicant held their own neighborhood meeting and general comments
received were about privacy and increase in density concerns, an emphasis on home
ownership, parking, new development would be better than remodeling the existing
structures, and the project should demonstrate investment in the long term vitality of the
neighborhood. Staff had received concerns that day about a flag lot changing the
neighborhood, viability concerns of the new lots, and a general lack of support for a rezone.
O’Kelley said the development agreement for the rezone should address the following:

1. Demolition of the existing house must take place within one year of rezone approval.

2. Enhanced landscaping is required to reduce impact of additional unit of the property and to
promote privacy between properties. Enhanced landscaping must have a minimum of:

e Minimum of 5 trees on each lot are required to create screening and promote privacy. More
trees may be added if privacy is not adequate.

e Trees used for privacy must be a columnar style.

e For each lot, ground landscaping may be up of 50% grass and 50% water efficient
landscaping. May be done in the form of xeriscaping.

e For each lot, two different types of ornamental grasses must be used.

3. Fencing is required along the entire perimeter of both properties except in the front yard of the
base lot and 20 feet along the driveway to the flag lot beginning the Garden Dr. To promote
privacy fencing must be opaque, and a minimum of 7 feet tall. All fences on property must still
meet all other applicable zoning codes.

4. No exterior lighting shall be installed that projects onto neighboring lots. All exterior lighting
must be directed downward.

5. All materials for both houses must be good quality. A maximum of 50% stucco is allowed on
each exterior wall. On the base lot the wall facing Garden Dr. is restricted to 25% stucco.

6. The flag lot is restricted to a building footprint of 2,000 square feet.

7. To be compatible with recent development in the neighborhood, houses on each lot are restricted
to one story with a pitched roof.

O’Kelley said the proposal would meet the minimum requirements for that zone.
Commissioner Reid asked about the large tree in the middle of the property being preserved.
O’Kelley said it could possibly be preserved though she had not seen an official land survey,

Applicant — Peter Tolman, 2564 E. Oakwood Circle, said he was a teacher and not a builder,

certain. He said he purchased the home to remodel it. He offered the three options to the
neighbors for redevelopment; 1) remodel the existing home, 2) build two brand new homes,
and 3) build a duplex. The neighborhood was opposed to the duplex, so they removed that

solutions were a privacy fence, barrier landscaping, and home placement to protect privacy.

proposing due to the setbacks. There would be double car garages and a 20-foot wide
driveway for parking, and no more than 300 feet from the street to the back of the house to
meet the public safety standard. He proposed that the new homes would not be taller than the
neighboring (Soward’s) home and suggested a 6-foot fence on the entire property line. He
said the homes would be craftsman, ranch or industrial/modern in style, would be held to a
2,000 square foot building footprint on a flag lot, and quality building materials would be
used. Tolman said he was okay with O’Kelley’s proposed development agreement
conditions. He said the Community Council favored the project because of aesthetics,

received at the Community Council meeting by a neighbor that the applicant should put a
flag lot in his own neighborhood instead of hers. Tolman said flag lots were in his personal

1.3 Consideration of SD-19-014, Preliminary Plat Approval for a Single-Family
Residential Subdivision Consisting of 5 Lots Location: 3675 South Craig Circle
Applicant: Tony Tippetts Planner: Robert May

Millcreek Planning Commission Meeting Minutes 18 December 2019 Page 6 of 10
Robert May said the 5-lot subdivision proposal was coined as the Millcreek Gully. He said
there was an existing home on the lot. He showed the Commission maps of where the
proposal would be and said the surrounding area was primarily single family residential in
the R-1-8 Zone. He showed the Commission pictures of the property from different
directions that showed standing water and slopes and said there was a spring 500 feet east of
Mountain View Road called Smart Hollow Springs that has two water rights claimed for
irrigation. The current owner has dammed the spring for the excess water. May said the
property had nearly two acres and over 30,000 square feet of it would be slated for a storm
drain and irrigation easement and about 14,000 square feet would be dedicated as a public
road. He showed the Commission an image of the City’s 50-foot local road right-of-way
standard and a plan of the buildable area of each proposed lot. The buildable area would be
subject to the Residential Compatibility Overlay Zone (RCOZ) standards. RCOZ standards at
a minimum would reduce building height to a maximum of 28 feet from natural grade,
reduce the maximum lot coverage of all buildings and structures to a maximum of 33%,
reduce the massing and scale of buildings, and require side yard setbacks to be at least 25%
of the lot width with no side setback less than 8 feet. May said the average of the neighboring
lots width was 80-90 feet wide. The zoning ordinance provided for minimums and there were
factors for surrounding character compatibility. May read from Millcreek Code 1.20.040
Lots; The lot arrangement and design shall be such that lots will provide satisfactory and
desirable sites for buildings and be properly designed according to topography, the
character of surrounding development, and to existing requirements. Staff found that the
following concerns affected the character of the surrounding development and advised the
Planning Commission to consider these concerns in their decision:

1. Millcreek General Plan. Chapter 3.1, Unique Neighborhoods. CHARACTER. GOAL N-1:
Preserve and enhance the physical elements that define each neighborhood’s character. Strategy
1.5: Ensure that new infill development is compatible with existing neighborhoods by regulating
structure sizes and heights; building forms and materials; yard setbacks; streetscape character;
height and bulk transitions; buffering; and other factors.

2. Lot Width: The lot width arrangement does not conform to the neighborhood as most lots in the
immediate area are on average 80-90 feet wide making the placement of the home not only more
desirable but wide enough that a traditional home can be placed as to not impact the neighbors,
respect view sheds, and still meet lot coverages and setbacks. While homes in the area can be big
and bulky but their widths are wider accommodating the size.

3. Topography: Historically, the lot arrangements, according to the area’s topography, have been
placed as such to preserve the ravines, gullies and natural water channels. Development has been
built right up to the edge is some cases, but the majority of the dense tree filled ravines and
gullies have been preserved, which is a feature of the area that makes it unique.

May recommended a continuance for the subdivision until a more suitable and compatible
development was proposed based on the staff findings in the staff report. Vice Chair
Stephens asked about the permanent non-buildable easement areas. May said staff had been
working with the developer for about a year and a half on the sensitive land issues. The goal
was to close off the south end of the property for tree and storm water preservation.
Applicant — Tony Tippets, 4052 W. 5900 S. in Salt Lake City, pointed out the concerns
raised by staff about the proposal and responded to those concerns. He said he had done
engineering of the road and lots, two separate geotechnical studies with positive findings, a
third party review of the geotechnical studies, architectural design of the homes that was
requested by Millcreek though not required and was still in draft phase, and verification of

Millcreek Planning Commission Meeting Minutes 18 December 2019 Page 7 of 10
utilities through will-serve letters. He said staff pointed out lot width concerns but the
ordinance (18.20.040) did not include lot width in the character of the surrounding
development or an average of surrounding lot widths. He ensured that a licensed architect
and engineer would make sure the homes conformed with the neighborhood character. He
reiterated that the project met all the requirements for a subdivision in the R-1-8 Zone under
RCOZ. He pointed out that the lots would front on the new cul-de-sac and there would be no
access allowed on the other street. He also noted that the developers would create as little
impact as possible to the ravine area. The developers have actively tried to engage with the
irrigation company and were willing to comply with their requirements. They will replace a
fence and were working on an agreement with a neighbor. They have also had someone go
out to review the traffic and that person said the proposed development’s traffic would be
nominal.

Commissioner Booth asked about the lot widths. Tippets said the current zoning required a
