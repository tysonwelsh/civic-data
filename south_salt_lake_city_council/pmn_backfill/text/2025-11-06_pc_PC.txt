2.      PUBLIC HEARING
        A Resolution of the City of South Salt Lake Planning Commission to Forward a
        Recommendation to the City Council to Adopt the Water Resource Element Update and
        the On the Move Mobility Plan Update in the South Salt Lake City General Plan.
        ACTION ITEM
        Applicant: South Salt Lake

Mobility Planner, Carl Ostenberg, presented the Mobility Plan Update and stated that no changes
have been made since the item was first presented in a work session. The City’s initial Mobility
Plan was adopted in 2020. For the update, Staff reviewed new plans that were adopted since that
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Regular Meeting                                         7
November 6, 2025

time, including the 2024 Wasatch Front Regional Council ("WFRC") Comprehensive Safety
Action Plan and Utah Department of Transportation ("UDOT") transportation plans. Changes
made in response to those plans were outlined in the update.

Key takeaways from Mobility Plan Update include:

   •    There is strong community support for a safe and landscaped walking loop around South
        Salt Lake City’s downtown. This is a new idea since the original plan.
   •    Improving the quantity of micromobility and pedestrian routes is a higher priority than
        improving the quality of existing routes.
   •    Traffic calming and road safety improvements are very high priorities for everyone.




                                               T
The Mobility Plan is organized as appendices to the General Plan:

   •    Appendix 1 is the original Mobility Plan.
   •    Appendix 2 outlines amendments to the plan.
   •    Appendix 3 includes survey results and other data supporting the choices made in
              AF
        Appendix 2.

Mr. Ostenberg reported that the original Mobility Plan was a standalone document but the updated
plan would be adopted as part of the General Plan.

Chair Carter noted that a moderate-income housing development was recently approved on State
Street and asked if a section of the parcel could be utilized for the green loop around downtown.
Mr. Ostenberg stated that the project was coordinated with the Mobility Plan. One of the travel
lanes will be repurposed into a walkable, landscaped plaza, which Staff believes is a step toward
the goal. However, many property owners will need to participate in order to create a true walkable
loop.
R
Mr. Shum presented the Water Resource Element Update. Staff proposed the amendment to the
South Salt Lake City General Plan to comply with State requirements to develop a water use and
preservation element that is integrated with land use planning and development.

The proposed update was required by SB 110, passed in 2022, and must include the following:
D

   1.   Effect of permitted development on water demand and infrastructure.
   2.   Methods for reducing water demand and per-capita consumption for existing development.
   3.   Methods for reducing water demand and per-capita consumption for future development.
   4.   Opportunities to modify operations to eliminate or reduce conditions that waste water.

Mr. Shum reported that the City has made significant progress in water conservation, decreasing
per-capita water use by over 21%, and was on track to meet the State’s water conservation targets.
The goal of the update was to provide guidance and increase focus for future development and
code amendments to include consideration for water conservation efforts and efficient water usage.
The update also hoped to increase awareness of the connection between water usage and land uses.
The update would raise awareness during future development processes and foster better
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Regular Meeting                                        8
November 6, 2025

coordination between departments in achieving the City and State water conservation goals.
Mr. Shum then reviewed the proposed strategies and implementation elements.

Section 1: Effect of permitted development on water demand and infrastructure.

   •   Status of City water infrastructure and water demand.
   •   Projected water demand from future developments.
   •   Deficit of current water supply and future demand.

Section 2: Methods for reducing water demand and per-capita consumption for existing
development.

   •   Identifies main sources of water waste and inefficient water usage.
   •   Addresses the City’s water conservation goals and water conservation methods
       implemented by the City.
   •   Identifies municipal code sections that address water-efficient irrigation and waterwise

Section 4: Opportunities to modify operations to eliminate or reduce conditions that waste water.

   •   Additional water conservation measures for future implementation.
   •   Explore partnerships between the City and water agencies for further and more efficient
       water conservation efforts. Agencies include the Utah Division of Water and Jordan Valley
       Water Conservancy District.
   •   Improve interdepartmental coordination between water conservation and land use planning

       optimal approaches to water conservation and efficient water usage.
D

Mr. Shum stated that the update did not propose immediate changes to the municipal code but
would provide guidance and increased focus on water conservation efforts for future development
and code amendments. Through the update, the City would continue to explore and adopt new
technologies, best practices, and educational programs that are proven to reduce water
consumption and demand. The update would help ensure that South Salt Lake City remains at the
forefront of water conservation by continually improving its approach to managing water resources
for both current residents and future generations.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission forward a recommendation of approval to the
City Council for both updates. The City Council had reviewed the updated elements during its last
work meeting, and the resolution would be presented for adoption at its next regular meeting.

Chair Carter asked if both near and distant future water use was considered. Mr. Shum reported
that already planned projects were included, as well as estimated water use from future projects.
Ms. Ungricht added that most of the data was obtained from the South Salt Lake City Drinking
Water Master Plan that was approved in August 2023. That plan was created by the City Engineer
and the water engineering firm of Hasen, Allen, and Luce, Inc. and included projections for future
development. The data was reorganized to meet State criteria and formatting requirements.

In response to a question from Commissioner Slifka, Mr. Shum reported that all municipalities in
