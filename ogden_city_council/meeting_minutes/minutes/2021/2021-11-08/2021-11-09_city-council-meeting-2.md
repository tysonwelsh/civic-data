Ogden Police Department UCOPA Accreditation
         Police Chief Young introduced AI Shupe, President of the Utah Chiefs of Police Association (UCOPA). Mr. Shupe
discussed the goal of improving standards and services delivered by the Police Department through accreditation with the
UCOPA. He is responsible for administering the accreditation program for the State of Utah; the Standards set forth by the
association involve thirty -two (32) Chapters containing 160 detailed individual standards which require proof of compliance for
accreditation. The standards include areas such as Law Enforcement Role and Authority, Planning and Research, Fiscal
Management, Complaints, Use of Force, Recruitment, Performance Evaluations, Police Operations, Community Relations and
Traffic Investigations to naine a few. Each standard requires a proof of policy, procedure, or supporting documentation to
establish compliance reflecting professional service delivery. The fmal stage of the accreditation process involves an on-site tour,
personal interviews, and an on-site assessment of the Police Department's policies, procedures, and records by a team of Law
Enforcement Professionals, not associated with the Ogden Police Department. The Assessment Team was assigned to evaluate
Ogden's agency by the Utah Chiefs of Police Association (UCOPA). The fmal assessment was approximately eight hours of
detailed analysis of the Ogden Police Department's compliance with professional standards set by the Utah Chiefs of Police
Association. Mr. Shupe noted he is pleased to present Chief Young and the Police Department with a plaque to commemorate the
Department's accreditation, which has a term of five years.
          Chief Young repmted the process of accreditation began in 2019 under fmmer Chief Watt and there were three different
individuals who worked hard to achieve accreditation for the Department: Troy Burnett, retired Captain; Mike Boon, retired
Lieutenant; and Shawn Hamblin, current Auditor and Assessor for the Police Department as well as a retired Lieutenant. He
thanked them for their dedication and efforts in helping the Department receive accreditation. Finally, he presented a film in which
the Department was featured that was intended to be shown at the 2021 National Chiefs Conference. The Conference was
cancelled due a hurricane in New Orleans, but the film was posted online.

Proposed Ordinance 2021-54 reclassifying property located at 1678 Kiesel

          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a rezone at 1678 Kiesel A venue came
before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the owner of 169 8 Kiesel A venue is proposing to rezone his property from
CP-2 (Community Commercial) to R-1-6 (Single Family Residential). The property used to have a house on it, but it was
demolished, and the property was rezoned to CP-2 to allow for a small parking lot to be built. The property owner would like to
remove the parking lot and build a residence on the property.
          Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
proposed rezone application; he presented a map to orient the Council to the location of the subject property and briefly discussed
the history of the property. The property fronts Kiesel Avenue, which has a single-family character. It is also a local street, which
supports R-1-6 zoning. Staff found that the proposed zone change, and subsequent project, will strengthen the core of the single-
family neighborhood. He concluded the Planning Commission voted to approve this item based on the following fmdings:
               4. The request is consistent with the policies of the general plan.

               6. The zoning reflects the character of Kiesel Avenue.

and it was noted that most people who visit the business along 17th Street park on the road, so the commercial parking on this
property may not be necessmy. One commissioner voted against the rezone, the reason being that he felt the commercial on-site
parking should not be removed.

location for a single-family home.

Proposed Ordinance 2021-45 reclassifying property located at 550 East 900
North as Multiple-Family Residential Zone (R-3)
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the rezone of property at 550 East 900
North came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated a developer has petitioned to rezone a vacant church property
from R-1-6 (single-family residential) to R-3 (multi-family residential). The petitioner is requesting to rezone the property so he
can build a townhome development. The Planning Commission determined that this rezone request does comply with the General
Plan, and that townhomes would create an effective transition from higher density housing at Washington Boulevard to single-
family housing to the east of the property in question. It was also noted that a new development would be preferable to a vacant
church lot, which could fall into disrepair and negatively impact the neighborhood. The Planning Commission unanimously
recommended rezoning the property from R-1-6 to R-3. This item was considered during a Council Meeting on October 5, 2021,
and the Council decided to extend the item so further discussion could take place. There was a question as to whether this petition
followed the community plan, which states that all new development east of Adams Avenue should be of the single-family
variety. Further discussion took place on October 19th, but a general consensus was not reached by the Council.

Proposed Ordinance 2021-55 amending the Ogden Municipal Code to
revise total renumeration provisions related to bonuses and stipends for city
employees
         A memo from Council staff regarding adjustments to the Ogden City Municipal Code regarding employee stipends came
before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Council will consider proposed amendments to Ogden Municipal Code
§ 2-6-9(G) relating to employee bonuses or stipends. The proposed amendments reflect direction given to staff during the
September 2021 strategy session. The proposed amendments limit the bonus amount an employee may receive in any year to 10%
of the employee's salary range. A repmt of fiscal year bonus pay-outs will be provided to the Council with the Mayor's proposed
budget each April or May.
         Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and stated that per the Council's direction, staff is
proposing an amendmentto Municipal Code §2-6-8(G) to limitthe bonus amount an employee may receive in any given year as
follows:
                   "The total of all perfonnance-based bonuses or stipends received by an employee shall not exceed the
                   employee's salary range authorized by the pay plan by more than ten percent (10%) in any single fiscal year."
         The staff proposal also includes removing the word "annual" from Section (G) to allow a bonus to be awarded any time
during the year. A report for the current fiscal year listing the number of employees who have or will be receiving annual bonuses
in each department and division, and the amount of and purpose for each respective bonus will be provided as part of the annual
budget process in April or May of each year.

                   "An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Subsection 2-6-9 .G to
