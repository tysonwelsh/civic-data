DESCRIPTION OF REQUEST
The applicant, Mr. Adam Nash, of Adam Nash Land Development LLC, representing property owners Nordic Alliance, LLC; Nupetco 106, LLC; and The Trustees of The IGP Trust, is requesting the Planning Commission to review a site plan for a new commercial retail building, located at 984 E. 10600 S. in Sandy City.  The existing zoning is Special Development-Carnation (SD-Carnation).  The proposed single-story commercial building will contain 8,742 square feet of gross building area, with approximately one-half of that space to be occupied by the Jolley Pharmacy chain as a compounding pharmacy and retail store, which is a permitted use in the existing zone.  The Planning Commission is the land use approval body for this action, as designated in the Sandy City Development Code.

BACKGROUND
This 2.17-acre site has been assembled from seven individual property parcels beginning approximately 1999, including property obtained from Sandy City and Salt Lake County from the vacation of a portion of 1000 East.  1000 East was relocated to the east, by Salt Lake County,

The first building and site plan review was approved in September 2004 and included a conceptual site plan for the entire 2.17-acre property and a Phase 1 approval for the Big 5 Sports retail building.  Only the western portion (Phase 1) of the site was developed with the existing retail building and a portion of the parking lot to the north of the building and some additional parking to the northeast of the building, fronting along 10600 South.

Existing zoning of the property is SD-Carnation.  Zoning to the west and south is Open Space and has been developed by Salt Lake County as the Dimple Dell Recreation Center and outdoor sports play fields with parking lots.  Property to the east, across 1000 East is zoned residential R-1-8 in Sandy City. The applicant has purchased the eastern five of the seven existing property parcels from the original commercial property owner, and has applied for both this site plan review and a related, three-lot commercial subdivision, representing three property ownership entities.  Unfortunately, this project got off to a difficult start by a commercial contractor, hired by Mr. Nash, presenting building construction plans to the Building and Safety Division, without applying for and going through site plan or subdivision review.  Mr. Nash first contacted the City Planning Division in response to the contractor being told that the City could not process the building permit application prior to the project receiving site plan and subdivision approval from the Planning Division.  This was approximately a year ago— fall of 2018.

Since that time, staff and the developer's design team have been working through the many issues of this project, resulting in many changes to the proposed project and proposed building sizes, locations and site configurations.  These protracted efforts have resulted in the plan that is now before the Planning Commission for consideration.

NEIGHBORHOOD MEETING
A neighborhood/community meeting was determined to not be required for this application.

ANALYSIS

PROPOSED SITE PLAN AND BUILDING.

Master Development and Phasing Plan.  The site plan approved in 2004 included a concept plan for the entire 2.17-acre site and a Phase 1 site plan review for the Big 5 Sports building, which was built substantially consistent with the approved plan.  The Big 5 Sports building was approved with parking at approximately 4.25 stalls per gross square footage of the Big 5 Sports building, instead of the 5.0 stalls per 1000 square feet of gross building required for retail commercial. (This appears to have been a staff error at that time.)  However, the site plan was redlined by staff to clarify that the 2004 approval was only for the Phase 1 area, and that any future development of the Phase 2 area would require a new site plan approval, which would meet all the requirements of the Sandy City Development Code.

Required parking numbers became a major source of disagreement between Mr. Nash and the planning staff, because Mr. Nash wanted to build two new buildings of such size that the site could not support the required number of new parking stalls at the 5.0 stalls per 1000 square feet ratio.  Staff finally conceded to an argument that Mr. Nash put forth, that the parking provided for the Phase 1 building (Big 5 Sports) was "grandfathered" at 43 stalls for that building (the number of parking stalls built in the Phase 1 project).  The new Phase 2 development is subject to the current code requirement of 5.0 stalls per 1000 square feet of gross building area.

The Phase 2 area consists of two of the proposed lots in the related subdivision and the currently proposed 8,742 square foot retail building for Jolley Pharmacy and some additional retail space in this building.  The site plan also shows a future building pad area of undetermined size on the proposed lot 3, depending on the parking requirement for that future use.  All of the potential additional on-site parking spaces and all of the required landscape areas and site improvements will be installed with this Phase 2 construction, which will finish the currently vacant portion of the site.

Vehicle Access and On-Site Circulation.  Current vehicle access to the site consists of a full access driveway on 10600 South, which enters just west of the center of the property along the east/west property frontage on 10600 South.  City ordinance and proper site circulation requires a second point of access to the expanded development.  The applicant has pursued reciprocal driveway access with Salt Lake County to connect with a major access driveway abutting the property on the south, which serves the Dimple Dell Center.  Although all parties hope that reciprocal access to the county property will be a possibility, the Salt Lake County Property Manager has indicated that such connection will take a substantial amount of time--much longer than the applicant is willing to wait.

To solve this issue of a second access, staff and the applicant considered the possibility of connecting the east most parking lot isle to what is the remnant of the old 1000 East right-of-way, which is not a part of the Dimple Dell Center property.  This was refined and is feasible to provide the second point of vehicle access to the site, which was a major breakthrough in the resolution of site issues.  The City Engineer and City Transportation Engineers now support the site plan, site circulation and vehicle access elements of the proposed plan.  Access and egress will be sufficient for the phased expansion.

Building Siting and Massing.  Although different from the concept master development plan considered with the Phase 1 project, building siting and building massing is proper, meets the development code requirements, and meets the Architectural Design Standards of Sandy City as now proposed. (Please see the attached 2004 Phase 1 site plan.)

Parking.  With the Phase 1 site plan and overall site concept master development plan, the owners have always intended to provide reciprocal access between all the parking across the entire site.  This is also the intent of this current proposal.  As explained in the background portion of this report, Phase 1 as built does not meet the current parking requirements for retail at 5.0 stalls per 1000 square feet of gross building area.  Staff has agreed to recognize that the owners have a legitimate claim to a "grandfathering" argument relative to the Phase 1 development providing only 43 stalls.

Vesting the Big 5 Sports building with 43 stalls, the new building for Jolley Pharmacy requires 44 parking stall at the 5.0 stalls per 1000 square feet ratio, totaling 87 stalls for the two buildings.  The site plan being considered yields 96 stalls; this leaves approximately 9 parking stalls for a future building on the proposed lot 3 portion of the site.  Nine parking stalls would allow 1,800 square feet of retail space for lot 3 or for consideration against a building use change for the Big 5 Sports building, such as to a medical use.  Required vehicle parking stalls meet the requirements for the first two building and allow for a possible third building, subject to proposed land uses in all two or three buildings on the larger site.

Landscaping.  All of the remaining vacant property on the site will be improved with required site improvements and landscaping, including the potential building area on lot 3, as a part of this Phase 2 site plan review.  Landscaping will meet City requirements.

Trails Master Plan.  The adopted Sandy City Trails Master Plan designates a ten-foot wide multi-use trail along the south side of 10600 South Street.  The existing sidewalk and landscaped area are being re-constructed to meet this requirement.

Street Right-of-Way Dedication.  Street right-of-way dedication is required on both 10600 South and on 1000 East.  The current seven property parcels extend into these roadways now and the subdivision plat will dedicate these areas to Sandy City as a condition of subdivision approval.  A total of 17,945 square feet will be dedicated along 10600 S. 1000 East right-of-way dedication will allow for the currently required 5-foot wide sidewalk and 5-foot landscaped parking strip, totaling 540 Square feet.

City Department Review.  All other City Departments and Divisions have reviewed the Phase 2 project plans and support the preliminary site plan review approval by the Planning Commission, subject to their usual and standard comments and conditions, to be perfected with the final site plan review by staff.

Building Architectural Design, Materials & Colors.  The building architectural design, materials and colors meet the requirements of the Sandy City Architectural Design Standards, and are recommended by staff to be approved by the Planning Commission as proposed.
