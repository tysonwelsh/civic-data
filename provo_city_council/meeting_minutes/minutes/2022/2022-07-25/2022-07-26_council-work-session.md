1.       A resolution approving an Interlocal Agreement for the use of surplus water. (22-
         060) (0:13:19)

Dave Decker, Public Works Director, presented. This interlocal agreement is scheduled for
approval at the Council Meeting this evening. For some time, the City has allotted a small
amount of water to be used by the South Utah Valley Solid Waste District-contracted landfill
west of Utah Lake. Mr. Decker provided additional context about the arrangement and what the
water is used for. The landfill ownership recently changed, so a new contract is required.

Councilor Rachel Whipple asked some clarifying questions—Mr. Decker explained that the
arrangement is essentially the same, but updated to reflect the new ownership and formalize
continuation/renewal of the agreement with them for the next 25 years. Ms. Whipple asked if the
City decided in the future that the water is no longer surplus, whether a change application would
be required. Mr. Decker indicated it was a small quantity of water, 1 cfs or 100 acre-feet. The
City’s annual water consumption is 28,000 cfs. Presentation only. This item was already
scheduled for the Council Meeting on July 26, 2022.

3.      An ordinance amending the zone map classification of approximately 32.6 acres of
        real property, located at 3701 W Center Street, from Agricultural (A1.10) to
        Airport Industrial (AI) and authorizing a related development agreement.
        (PLRZ20210263) (0:26:06)

Dustin Wright, Planner, presented. This item has an associated development agreement which
was proffered by the applicant. The applicant’s concept plan is to develop this property for three
light industrial buildings. The tenants are not yet lined up. Public Works had some concerns
about the concept plan, without knowing about future street improvements or utility
improvements; the development agreement is intended to address those concerns as the project
plan approval moves forward later in the development process.

Councilor Travis Hoban noted some feedback from the neighborhood chair, who suggested
diverting large truck traffic off of Center Street to Lakeview Parkway. Mr. Wright said that
David Day, Public Works’ Engineering Development Coordinator, would be present at the
Council Meeting that evening and could speak to this question. Councilor Katrice MacKay asked
if this was something that could be included in a development agreement; she noted that children
cross the street to attend school, which made semi-truck traffic a concern. Mr. Wright noted that
at the concept plan level, they did not know all the impacts of traffic on the development. The
development agreement will require that there are specific plans put in place when a project plan
proposal comes forward in the future. Brian Jones, City Attorney, addressed this concern and
said the development agreement probably could not require that and the agreement terminates
upon issuance of the certificate of occupancy and wasn’t really the appropriate tool to resolve
this problem. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth thought it was important to be business-friendly and
there was a large need for industrial uses. Councilor Rachel Whipple asked if the applicant
would be completing a traffic study as part of their project plan approval. Mr. Wright noted the
applicant could better address that question at the meeting that evening. Presentation only. This
item was already scheduled for the Council Meeting on July 26, 2022.

5.      A presentation from the Administration regarding the possible allocation of
        unrestricted use funds from the American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA). (22-017) (0:40:07)

Wayne Parker, CAO, presented. He reviewed the past discussion on restricted use ARPA funds
from the last Work Meeting; he noted that his presentation today would focus on the use of
unrestricted funds. He noted Fire Chief Jim Miguel was also present to respond to questions. The
Administration is working on programming the discretionary funds from the American Rescue
Plan Act. Mr. Parker shared details about use of the old City Center building and the process of
selecting a new partner for the redevelopment. They are still determining the costs for
redevelopment, demolition, and parking, as well as for the Fire Station.

They have estimated the cost of Fire Station 1 to be about $5 million. They had previously
allocated about $3.5 million in Legacy CIP funds. With increasing costs to construction and
materials, they have proposed to allocate $1.5 million in discretionary ARPA funds, which
would get them to the $5 million in expected costs to complete the Fire Station 1 project. They
intend to discuss the use of the remaining discretionary funds in a future meeting, but some of
the proposals they have submitted to the Council’s ARPA Committee relate to constructing a
parking lot at the old City Center site and advancing the development of the regional sports park
on an accelerated timeline. If the Council is comfortable with the $1.5 million for the Fire Station
1, it would be appropriate to place an appropriation on the agenda of a future Council Meeting.

Councilor Shannon Ellsworth expressed that she did not know if there was consensus among
Council members regarding the regional sports park timeline acceleration. Mr. Parker indicated
they had anticipated to advance these projects one at a time, as the Council is considering and
balancing many needs. Ms. Ellsworth also asked if there was a budget outline for Fire Station 1.
Chief Jim Miguel explained that they were working with VCBO Architecture, who had prepared
some preliminary plans a couple years ago. They are waiting on the engineers’ estimate for the
final cost. They had initially provided the architect with an estimated budget of $5 million.

Councilor George Handley asked some clarifying questions; Chief Miguel confirmed that this
requested $1.5 million is just for Fire Station 1, and they have already previously appropriated $3

6.      A presentation regarding the Statistical Survey done in support of the General Plan
        Update. (22-011) (1:29:55)

Brandon Larsen, Planner, presented. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked if the survey results
were broken out using the current Council districts or newly adopted districts. Mr. Larsen
confirmed it was broken out by current districts. Mr. Larsen highlighted some of staff’s
takeaways from the survey results, including:
     Focus on congestion and parking in the survey responses
     Focus on having a mix of housing types, including single-family homes
     Residents want to preserve outdoor recreation opportunities
     Air quality, recycling, and the Provo River are key environmental concerns
     Air quality is important to all demographics

Some of the minor repeating themes included:
    Having a place for children and family to live
    Maintain low taxes (rated important but not a top aspect to address)
    Grow local small businesses and the startup community
    Accessibility was rated as important but not a top priority (accessibility in housing,
      diversity, employment, etc.)

Mr. Larsen reviewed a further breakdown of the data for several types of survey questions by
district, highlighting some of the trends observed in the results. Ms. Ellsworth asked about one of
the questions about shopping centers; Mr. Larsen clarified that these were answers ranked by the
respondents. Councilor Rachel Whipple commented that in her area, there is some shopping and
some townhomes, so residents responding to the survey in her area may have answered these
were priorities but not in their area since there was existing stock in those types of land uses.

Mr. Larsen indicated that three things seemed to be priorities related to transportation, across all
districts: traffic and congestion, air quality, and parking. Mr. Larsen then compared some of the
results of the surveys conducted by Y2 Analytics and Design Workshop. Mr. Larsen highlighted
some specific suggestions for the General Plan:

under the proposal.

Councilor Katrice MacKay asked what projects this would apply to and what percentage. Ms.
Dayley explained it would apply to all City projects and the rate was 1%. There are several
aspects of the proposal that the committee was seeking feedback about, including whether some
projects would not apply and whether there was a floor or ceiling to the percentage. Councilor

projects presented an important opportunity for the City to be able to engage with art installed as
a result. She noted that being able to put money into the fund was an important aspect since all
the art may not be installed in conjunction with a particular project.

Arts Council. The Council or a Council committee would also have some input as to the use of
the funds. Ms. Dayley highlighted the last three sections of the policy, noting that they worked
closely with the Legal Department to draft the provisions. Title and ownership of art described
who names the art and who owns it once it is installed. The next section addressed maintenance
and security of art, including a designation of funds towards maintenance. The section “rights of
the artist” incorporated elements utilized at the State and in several Utah cities regarding artists’
rights. Ms. Dayley asked for any other input, questions, or concerns from the Council.

Ms. MacKay asked if staff could email the policy to the Council. Ms. Dayley indicated she
would email it out to the Council. Ms. Whipple noted that rights of the artist was an important
consideration and an issue that came up when they redid the mural in Maeser Park. Being able to
respect the rights of the artist and to have clear boundaries with ownership was very helpful in
protecting the legal rights of those involved. Ms. Ellsworth asked if there was a floor or ceiling
amount for specific art projects; Ms. Dayley said they could review this with the committee. Ms.
Ellsworth welcomed a variety of unique types of art. Ms. Dayley indicated that the committee
would bring this item back to a Work Meeting in August. Presentation only.

Melia Dayley, Policy Analyst, presented. She reviewed in brief the information that they had
presented at the previous Work Meeting, including information about the three pathways for
residents to apply for ADUs. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth wondered why they would prohibit

Jones noted that while the Council may not be able to prohibit spot zoning, the policy could
certainly signal that spot zoning was discouraged.

Mr. Fillmore asked if that was consistent with Development Services’ intentions. Bill Peperone,
Development Services Director, clarified that small rezones were not the same as spot zoning.
He thought if the Council was uncomfortable with that approach, they should indicate that
through their policies and promote the avenues they did encourage; this would not prohibit that
type of application but would encourage applicants in other directions. Ms. Ellsworth asked what
the fees would be to initiate that type of application. Mr. Peperone said the rezone application fee
was about $1100. Ms. Ellsworth thought the Council should keep that in mind. Ms. Ellsworth
also asked about whether the committee had explored a process similar to that which is used for
major or minor home occupation approvals. The committee did not contemplate that type of
approach. Ms. Dayley then reviewed some of the questions regarding the three pathways.

Councilor Travis Hoban had previously asked about what is defined as adjacent neighbors. He
wanted to keep options open but wanted to encourage residents to follow the process with the
three pathways. He also supported opening up ADUs on the west side. Councilor Katrice

Ellsworth asked about the annual fees for having an ADU; Ms. Dayley said they just needed to
have a rental dwelling license. Ms. Ellsworth thought these pathways were too high of a hurdle.

9.      A discussion of the Neighborhood Program Review Committee regarding the
        committee's recommendations for the Neighborhood Program. (22-055) (3:07:28)

Council Chair Travis Hoban introduced the item and noted the intent was for the Council to have
some public discussion. He thanked the public and neighborhood chairs for their feedback on
this proposal. He reiterated that the Council values this program and that it provides a sounding
board for decisions that the Council makes. He also expressed appreciation for the services
rendered by neighborhood chairs, vice-chairs, and community members to their community. Mr.
Hoban noted the Council did not want to eliminate this program or render it ineffective; they
hoped to improve the function and intent of the program and the role that it plays.

Karen Tapahe, Community Relations Coordinator, presented. She highlighted recommendations
in the current draft of the proposal, including public input, representation of less-engaged
neighborhoods, staggering term length on the executive board, executive board term limits, and
allowing two vice-chairs. She noted additional comments on items contained in the proposal,
including training, communications, and neighborhood identities. Other discussion items include
incentives for volunteering, frequency of meetings, and district size. The proposal is tentatively
scheduled for the August 9 meeting. Councilor George Handley shared feedback about meeting
frequency and indicated he favored monthly standing meetings.

Mr. Hoban invited Council feedback on each discussion point. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth
shared her feedback. She did not like having incentives for volunteering; the incentive is the
experience of fulfilling one’s civic duty. She supported having meetings every two months in
order to be more efficient. Regarding district size, she thought 6-8 neighborhoods per district
worked—she supported whatever was most inclusive and efficient for staff. Mr. Handley
commented on the other discussion points. Ms. Ellsworth suggested they take a yes or no vote on
incentives. Mr. Handley asked what the Council had in mind; he appreciated Ms. Ellsworth’s
comments but he also wanted to make sure people felt appreciated. He did not know what form
this should take. Mr. Handley thought the roles should be clearly articulated and a nomination
process should be put in place. Mr. Hoban shared comments on the idea behind this question.
Mr. Fillmore agreed it was good to show appreciation to volunteers. Ms. Ellsworth suggested
being cognizant of what the City is doing with other boards and commissions. Councilor Katrice
MacKay agreed with Ms. Ellsworth on incentives and thought it was a can of worms. She
thought showing respect and helping neighborhood participants feel empowered was incentive
