A.   ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING APPLICATION SV-1-2023,
     FILED BY WEST VALLEY CITY, REQUESTING A STREET VACATION
     OF 5720 WEST AT 4535 SOUTH
     Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council
     Meeting scheduled February 20, 2024, in order for the City Council to hear and
     consider public comments regarding Application SV-1-2023, Filed by West Valley
     City, Requesting a Street Vacation of 5720 West at 4535 South

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:


              The West Valley City Engineering Division is requesting a street vacation
              for all of 5720 West at 4535 South.
MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING FEBRUARY 20, 2024
                        -3-

           The street to be vacated was platted as part of the Cape Cod Estates Phase
           3 Subdivision. This subdivision was recorded in 1994. The stub street was
           platted to provide access to the vacant property to the south which was
           anticipated to develop with residential housing.

           A short time ago, staff received an application for a Charter School to be
           constructed on property immediately south of the stub street in question.
           While the Charter School will have its main access at 5600 West, a proposed
           second access was indicated at 5720 West.

           After reviewing the site plan, and in light of traffic problems the City has
           experienced with other Charter Schools, a decision was made to vacate the
           right-of-way.

           Charter Schools in general draw few children from adjacent or nearby
           neighborhoods. The majority of students are dropped off and picked up by
           parents. The volume of vehicles differs greatly from a traditional school,
           thus creating traffic problems both on major streets and through
           subdivisions.

           In an attempt to mitigate vehicular traffic from negatively affecting the
           neighborhood to the north, the proposed vacation was initiated by the City
           Engineering Division. Staff anticipates that traffic will already be
           problematic on 5600 West and will potentially extend north and possibly
           west on 4535 South. Eliminating an additional point of conflict at 5720
           West was seen as a viable and reasonable solution to help lessen the traffic
           congestion the Charter School will bring.

          As part of the request, the City will be removing the existing improvements
          and will install a new drive approach and driveway for the two residents to
          share. Landscaping will be installed and restored where necessary.

   Councilmember Harmon asked why the issue paper doesn’t list the fiscal impact
   for the removal of the improvements. Dan Johnson, Public Works, replied that the
   street vacation does not have a financial obligation, it simply vacates the street
   itself.

   BLOOD PLASMA CENTERS WITH FRONTAGE ALONG REDWOOD
   ROAD AND ON 3300/3500 SOUTH BETWEEN 2700 WEST AND THE
   JORDAN RIVER
   Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council
   Meeting scheduled February 20, 2024, in order for the City Council to hear and
   consider public comments regarding application ZT-8-2023, Filed by West Valley
   City, Requesting a Zone Text Change to Amend Section 7-6-301 and add Section 7-7-
   104.5 to the West Valley City Municipal Code to Prohibit Blood Plasma Centers with
   Frontage Along Redwood Road and on 3300/3500 South Between 2700 West and the
   Jordan River.

   Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
   information as follows:

          City staff is proposing an ordinance change to amend Section 7-6-301 and
          add Section 7-7-104.5 to include use-specific regulations that would
          prohibit blood plasma centers on all properties with frontage along
          Redwood Road and on 3300/3500 South between 2700 West and the
          Jordan River. For reference, a blood plasma center is defined in the
          Zoning Ordinance as “an establishment where patrons receive
          compensation for donating blood plasma”.

          By way of background, incidents involving homeless persons increased
          significantly in the City when the homeless resource center in South Salt
          Lake at 3380 South 1000 West opened. The City has been tracking these
          homeless incidents, which include crime, for several years now. The
          highest concentration of these incidents has consistently been the area
          surrounding the intersection of 3500 South and Redwood Road. This
          concentration of homeless incidents has only increased with the opening
          of the State’s temporary homeless shelter operating at 3381 South
          Redwood Road.

          Staff recently received an inquiry about a blood plasma center moving into
          a location within close proximity of the temporary homeless shelter
          operating at 3381 South Redwood Road. Given the already high
          concentration of homeless incidents in this area, staff is concerned that a
          blood plasma center will only make the situation worse.

   Mayor Lang opened the Public Hearing. There being no one to speak in favor or
   opposition, Mayor Lang closed the Public Hearing.

ORDINANCE 24-08: AMEND SECTION 16-6-105 OF THE WEST VALLEY CITY
MUNICIPAL CODE TO AMEND CERTAIN REQUIREMENTS CONCERNING
FIRE ACCESS
John Flores, Interim Assistant City Manager, presented proposed Ordinance 24-08 that
would amend Section 16-6-105 of the West Valley City Municipal Code to Amend Certain
Requirements Concerning Fire Access.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Title 16 of the West Valley City Municipal Code adopts several international codes,
       including the International Fire Code. The International Fire Code permits
       municipalities to adopt requirements to permit fire department access in the event
       of an emergency. This ordinance updates existing requirements concerning
       acceptable access methods. The ordinance also provides that establishments with
       a hazardous materials permit are required to provide fire department access.
       Finally, the ordinance requires that multi-family dwellings provide access to
       common and public facilities. Provisions for fire department access enable faster
       response to emergencies and prevent unnecessary damage to private property to
       gain access in the event of a false alarm.

RESOLUTION 24-24: APPROVE AN AMENDED STATE OF UTAH GRANT
AGREEMENT
John Flores, Interim Assistant City Manager, presented proposed Resolution 24-24 that
would approve an Amended State of Utah Grant Agreement.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The proposed amendment allows use of ARPA funds for Phase 1 of the project and
       extends the completion date to December 2024. This will permit West Valley City
       to use grant funding to finish the park design, begin infrastructure installation, and
       construct a portion of the trail areas. Phase One is estimated to cost $4.35 million
       dollars and will be paid using funds previously approved; $2 million from ARPA
       and $1.6 million through Public Works stormwater funds. The Parks and
       Recreation Department secured an additional $750 thousand dollars through a Utah
       Outdoor Recreation Grant to complete this phase.

       West Valley City owns 71.52 acres of land located at approximately 6598 W.
       Parkway Blvd., which contains wetland areas and is currently used as storm water
       storage. The Park Maintenance Facility is also located on this property. ARPA
       awarded the Parks and Recreation Department $2 million dollars for the
       development of this property as a Wetland Park. The issue paper to accept the grant
       with matching Public Works funding was approved by the City Council and signed
       in 2021. The Parks and Recreation department petitioned the grant commission to
       permit use of the funds for Phase 1 completion by December 2024 and reduced the
       match amount from $12 million to $2.75 million. This parcel of land is ideal for a
       wetland/natural park for the community and is something which the residents have
       expressed a great interest in having in West Valley City. The Parks and Recreation
       Department, working with the Public Works department, has a completed design
       and is prepared for bidding the first phase of the project.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and she called for a motion.

       Councilmember Fitisemanu moved to approve Resolution 24-24.
