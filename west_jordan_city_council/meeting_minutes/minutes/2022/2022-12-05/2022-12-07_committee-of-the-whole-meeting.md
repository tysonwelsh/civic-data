 1. CALL TO ORDER
Chair Whitelock called the work session to order at 5:30 pm and noted that Council Members
Worthen and Jacob were excused.

 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
         a. Discussion regarding Proposed General Plan Goal Organization and
            Implementation
            Policy Analyst Cassidy Hansen reported the General Plan had been forwarded from the
            General Plan Committee to the Planning Commission. The Planning Commission would
            forward a recommendation to the City Council. Ms. Hansen spoke of ongoing versus one-
            time goals and projects, assignment of responsibilities, and timing of financial allocation
            to projects. She asked for feedback from the Council regarding an implementation plan.

            A majority of Council Members indicated they believed Council Staff should work with
            Administrative Staff to assign departments to goals in the General Plan. A majority of the
            Council indicated interest in brainstorming with Administration regarding when goals
            should receive fiscal support. Council Member McConnehey suggested such
            brainstorming should be integrated into the budget process. City Administrator Korban
            Lee emphasized that a healthy prioritization process was needed to be able to effectively
            allocate the City’s limited resources.

         b. Discussion regarding Amendments to Title 13-13-10(G) and 13-18-10(E)(2) to
            Adjust the Tree Clearance Height for Clearance and Visibility

            Council Member Bloom explained a request from some citizens to adjust the tree
            clearance height from 15 feet to 14 feet to better match actual vehicle height and protect

          existing trees. Council Office Director Alan Anderson presented a summary of damage to
          City vehicles caused by trees since fiscal year 2018. He pointed out that two measuring
          points were mentioned in City Code – one from the curb strip or park lane, and one from
          the travel lane. Mr. Anderson asked if the Council wanted to establish a consistent
          measuring point. He commented that tree branches tended to hang lower when weighted
          down by spring blossoms and winter snow.

          Vice Chair Green believed the current Code was extremely subjective, which was difficult.
          He agreed the City needed a “stick” to ensure trees were trimmed but did not believe the
          punishment needed to be the same level as that of a Class B Misdemeanor. Vice Chair
          Green felt an objective standard was needed.

          Council Member McConnehey said UDOT allowed up to 14 feet for commercial vehicle
          height, and he was comfortable with the 15-foot restriction to accommodate more than
          just City vehicles. He suggested a little more leeway be given for young trees along the
          park strip.

          Chair Whitelock had not received complaints from residents prior to the change in Code
          from 9 feet to 15 feet. She was troubled by the amount charged for branch overhang and
          expressed the opinion that the measuring point for tree height needed to be consistent.
          Chair Whitelock noted that she would be in favor of lowering the tree clearance height to
          14 feet as long as the language included where the height would be measured from, and
          how the measurement would be consistent.

          Arborist Ty Nielsen spoke of the importance of planting the right tree in the right place.
          The current ordinance included a list of trees recommended for park strips. He reported
          that it was best to trim and train a tree when it was young. Mr. Nielsen said measurements
          were done from where curb and asphalt met. The current limit was set at 15 feet to
          provide a buffer for branches that hung lower with blossoms and snow.

          Chair Whitelock said she was not able to easily find a recommended list of trees for park
          strips on the City website. She asked Mr. Nielsen how the City could make 14 feet work.
          Mr. Nielsen responded that pruning was necessary to make an existing tree compliant.
          However, pruning would stimulate growth, which would make the branches grow and get
          more in the way. He stated a 14-foot tree clearance height would require a lot more
          maintenance, and would remove the buffer for branches that seasonally hung lower. Mr.
          Nielsen said it was not just City vehicles that sustained damage from tree branches.
          Private campers and trailers also sustained damage.

          Chair Whitelock asked why residents were fined for not trimming their trees if the park
          strips were City property, and the City Arborist was responsible for ensuring trees were
          trimmed. Mr. Nielsen responded that adjacent property owners were required by
          ordinance to maintain and care for trees in park strips. He emphasized that currently, a
          majority of park strip trees in the City had not been maintained.

          Council Member Bloom expressed frustration that developers were planting the wrong
          trees and everyone else was having to pay for it. Mr. Nielsen responded that new

       c. Discussion Regarding Facility Use Policy Framework
          Mr. Anderson said the Legal Department was working on a facility use policy and fee
          waiver guidelines. He said the framework of space (public space, contract partner space,
          or employee space), and the framework of city speech (what could be displayed in public
          space), needed to be defined. Responding to a question from Chair Whitelock, Mr.
          Anderson said the City Museum was a contract partner space. Staff answered questions
          regarding specific City properties. During open City park hours, anyone would be allowed
          to come and go. Assistant City Attorney Dave Quealy listed sidewalks and parks as
          examples of where individuals could engage in unrestricted speech. He explained that
          within a single structure, every kind of free speech, space, or forum could be allowed.

          Chair Whitelock said she would prefer for Administration to present their
          recommendations to the Council for Council consideration and response. Council Member
          McConnehey said he would want to be able to limit the types of activities at Pioneer Hall
          given the age of the building and the desire to avoid damage. Mr. Quealy said the type of
          activity could be restricted if the rule applied to all events at the location.

          Vice Chair Green said he believed parking lots needed to be included as public facilities
          and needed to be included in rental guidelines. He suggested food trucks at City parks
          needed to be addressed, and said he thought there were some buildings that should be
          declared non-public space.

          Mr. Lee summarized that the Council wanted Administration to bring back a list of
          facilities recommended for certain types of uses and certain types of speech. Vice Chair
          Green mentioned the Field of Flags, and suggested staff look into the ability to limit the
          types of flags that could be used.

       d. Discussion of Landlord / Tenant Agreements for City Utilities
          Council Member McConnehey said he did not feel there was sufficient time remaining in
          the meeting to fully present and discuss this agenda item. Chair Whitelock suggested
          putting the item on the December 21, 2022 work session agenda.

 3. ADJOURN
Vice Chair Green moved to adjourn the Committee of the Whole. Council Member Bloom
seconded the motion, which passed by unanimous vote (5-0).

The meeting adjourned at 6:44 pm
