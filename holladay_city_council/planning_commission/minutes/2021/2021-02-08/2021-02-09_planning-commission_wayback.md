PUBLIC HEARING
1.      Ordinance Amendment – Holladay Village, Development Clarifications (13.14.071).
        Consideration and Recommendation of an Amendment to Existing Ordinances
        Clarifying and Updating Design Requirements and Procedures.
Planning Manager, Jonathan Teerlink, presented the staff report and stated that the proposed
ordinance amendment is a joint request between staff, the Planning Commission, and the City
Council. The Holladay Village has experienced a tremendous amount of interest, and a great deal
of development has occurred over the past 14 years. They are now at the point that updates need
to occur specifically with respect to procedure and elements that staff and applicants have
requested in an effort to make the process more fluid. The issues were narrowed down to points
in the ordinance where terminology needs to be clarified.

Mr. Teerlink reported that the draft text does the following:

   •   Condenses statements of intent, purposes, and goals that were considered repetitive
       previously;
   •   Updates terminology and figures, specifically photos of the Village;
   •   Clarifies the City’s position on various design themes; and
   •   Clarifies standards in the required use of acceptable materials, lighting, building
       articulation, and pedestrian orientation.

2.      Ordinance Amendment – Development Near Waterways – Supplementary
        Regulations (13.76.400). Consideration and Recommendation of an Amendment to
        Existing Ordinances Establishing Process and Development Standards for New and
        Existing Development Near Natural and Human-Made Waterways.
Mr. Teerlink reported that currently with any development if a home is redeveloped on a lot that
is within 100 feet of a waterway or 20 feet from a canal, even though the setbacks may allow it,
the Planning Commission is required to approve it as an exception. It is essentially a Conditional
Use Permit. The Planning Commission’s role is to approve a conditional use for development
within the proposed distances from waterways. This has been the course of action for the Planning
Commission since the City was incorporated. Mr. Teerlink explained that there has always been
an issue of conservation over types of development. Over time it has created a situation that
confuses the standards of what they (The City) want to protect. The drafted standards, as proposed
have been created by the Planning Commission in the form of findings as each project has been
reviewed. The standards were compiled over time and a document was created that provides a
standardized set of regulations for development near a waterway or canal.

Chair Bradshaw suggested the Commission review the main sections. Appreciation was expressed
to City Engineer, Jared Bunch, Mr. Teerlink, and staff for their time and effort.

Chair Bradshaw opened the public hearing.

Mr. Teerlink identified the language of the original exception, which was eliminated. Specific
language was provided later in the document in terms of distances and activities that are allowed.
Mr. Bunch stated that the waterways were also named. He explained that some streams flow only
in the spring and do not require the same treatment.

Mr. Teerlink explained that some perennial waterways and drainages such as Hughes Canyon are
classified as ephemeral. With Hughes Canyon, they are finding that there is frequently water in
the stream bed, almost near round. Most of it could be due to storm water or other types of
drainages from water tables. Canals were kept separate.

The Purpose Statement was presented, which gives a general purpose and reasoning for the
ordinance. The General Provisions specify that no one may engage in any ground disturbing
activity, development, or demolition within 100 feet of a natural waterway or 20 feet of a canal or
water parcel that will remove, dredge, clear, grub, destroy, armor, terrace, or otherwise impact the
waterway through manipulation of soils except by provisions allowed in the section, site plan
approval from the Planning Commission, or anything that is pre-reviewed by the Army Corps of
Engineers, Salt Lake County Flood Control, Utah State Engineer, or any other agency with
jurisdiction over the waterway.

Mr. Teerlink reported that the Community and Economic Development Director has the authority
to approve the concept of a Waterway Permit, which is a new permit being created to enforce the
section. The Technical Review Committee (“TRC”) is the recommending body to the Community
and Economic Development Director. They review the application first for compliance and then
forward a recommendation for approval and appeal. The various elements of a complete site plan
were listed in the document.

Another concept was to establish a high water level. Mr. Teerlink explained that it did not exist
in the ordinance previously and is extremely important in terms of distances from waterways. If
the high watermark cannot be found, the City Engineer may find and delineate it. Waterway
protection areas were next addressed.

Mr. Bunch stated that they recognize that it would be difficult to impose some of the requirements
on properties that have already been developed and are being redeveloped. It is easier to impose
them on a vacant property that has never been developed. The requirements are more conservative
for undeveloped properties. Slope and distance issues were discussed. It was noted that additional
refinements were to be made before being presented to the City Council.

Mr. Teerlink reported that once the area has been established for an activity, the activity needs to
be specified. Charts were provided that would apply to undeveloped or developed ground. Lists
of activities were provided with an indication of whether an activity would be permitted. Specific
scenarios were described. Bank restoration issues were discussed.

City Attorney, Todd Godfrey suggested that the Commission evaluate the matter from a planning
perspective since planning issues often arise with rebuilds and they can relate to the bank. When
the issue gets to the Council level he will be looking at it as an exaction in determining whether
there is enough of a link and whether the amount is proportionate to be legally sustainable. He
indicated that he had not yet conducted that analysis. The Council will evaluate the legality of the
amount proposed and the general principle.

Alyssa Lloyd liked it in concept but believed that waterways are a finite and very valuable resource
to society at large. She suggested that they be more proactive in protecting them. She considered
this to be the first step.

Ann Mackin commented that the City of Holladay has a number of waterways that run through
neighborhoods. She agreed that they should be proactive about how they draft the ordinances.

Marianne Ricks agreed and recommended that they do as much as they can legally.

Martin Banks was uneasy with the idea of imposing a 5% cost on a new homeowner in another
area of the river that may not have anything to do with his property. He did not want to put the
burden on new renovations. It was clarified that the 5% limitation would apply to existing
accessory structures. That being the case, Commissioner Banks had no concerns.

Mr. Teerlink agreed that it is a concept that could be overbearing, especially when the scale of the
project is a home. They could focus it in light of what was mentioned by legal counsel. On second
thought, Mr. Bunch suggested that any expansion be discouraged in Area A. The Commission
Members agreed. Area A allows for 25 feet while Area B allows for 50 feet. Mr. Teerlink
proposed that they include the caveat that as a tradeoff for allowing expansion in Area A, the
restoration project cost of 5% must be applied to the stream. He explained that for anything that
is on developed land, the use must be shown in the table or it is explicitly prohibited. The intent
was for the charts to show all of the activity that is allowed.

Mr. Teerlink explained that any use that is found to be in violation of the provisions shall be legal
non-conforming and authorized to continue. The use chart sets forth provisions for public works
and utilities. There are canal companies that maintain their waterways, which might be contrary
to the uses in the chart. If there is an emergency situation, gain access. Bank restoration will
require a retroactive protection permit. Mr. Teerlink stated that the standards included in the
document are similar to those that are already regulated.

There were no public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Commissioner Ricks moved to forward a positive recommendation to the City Council with the
corrections added by Jared Bunch and Jonathan Teerlink for the Ordinance Amendment
regarding Development Near Waterways – Supplementary Regulations (13.76.400) and
additional clarifications. Commissioner Mackin seconded the motion. Vote on motion:
Marianne Ricks-Aye, Ann Mackin-Aye, Alyssa Lloyd-Aye, Martin Banks-Aye, Chair Jan
Bradshaw-Aye. The motion passed unanimously.
