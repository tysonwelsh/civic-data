BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This request is for the approval of Ordinance 1483, public right-of-way vacation of a portion of
Wildrye Field Way. Wildrye Field Way was dedicated with the Big Willow Creek Phases 1A and
1B Subdivisions and recorded October 17, 2018. At the time, the Big Willow Creek Subdivision
did not include several parcels that were later added to the overall project area in the first and
second amendments to the development agreement, executed April 16, 2019, and May 5, 2020,
respectively. Since that time, Ivory has been working on a comprehensive development plan
for the additional property to be integrated into the Big Willow Creek Subdivision. This effort
included a desire to relocate the subdivision access from 11400 South to a more central part of
the subdivision.

Ivory has proposed to vacate a portion of Wildrye Field Way, in Phase 1A and a portion of 1B,
see Exhibit A. The proposed north terminus of Wildrye Field Way will be modified to a cul-de-
sac within Phase 1B, by amending the plat. Access for the subdivision to 11400 South will be
relocated to Engelmann Drive (470 West), at the same connection point at 11400 South.
Engelmann Drive (470 West) extends from Big Willow Creek Phase 2 through Phase 6 Minor
Subdivision. Both of these phases are going through the review and approval process.

Ordinance 1483, vacating a portion of Wildrye Field Way, will not become effective until Big
Willow Creek Phase 6 Minor Subdivision is approved and recorded with Salt Lake County, at
which time the vacation will be recorded as well. If Big Willow Creek Phase 6 Minor Subdivision
is not recorded by April 6, 2022, Ordinance 1483 will be void. The new public right-of-way will
be constructed by Ivory prior to the recording of Phase 6. There will be a paved public right-of-

ANALYSIS

The plan that Ivory Development has will provide the development with a public right-of-way
entrance to the subdivision centrally located within the community. The current subdivision
access, on Wildrye Field Way, is located along the western boundary of the subdivision.

The process to amend the street design consists of the following; having the City Council vacate
of a portion of Wildrye Field Way stipulated on the approval, construction, and recording of
Phase 6 Minor Subdivison. Phase 6 will include the new public right-of-way access from 11400
South to the development. Once Phase 6 is completed and the vacation recorded, then the
remaining portion of Wildrye Field Way will be modified to a cul-de-sac through the second
amendment of Phase 1B.

There is a single residence on the portion of Wildrye Field Way, located at 527 West 11400
South. This residence is not a part of the Big Willow Creek development. Their access will be
protected by an easement granted by Ivory Development and connected to the public-right-of-
way created in Phase 6.

The existing lots that will be impacted by the creation of the cul-de-sac are owned by Ivory
Development and have not yet had any residential construction on them. All other lots within
Big Willow Creek Subdivision will not have their access to a public street modified.

This process addresses the changes to the streets within the subdivision and will not entitle any
new lots. The interests of the existing utilities will be protected, including preservation or
creation of new easements with the second amendment of Phase 1B.

The interests of the public are preserved, no adjacent property owners will be materially
harmed, and there is good cause for the vacation. Public access to the subdivision will be
maintained through the subdivision approval, construction, and occupation of homes within the
subdivision.

Criteria For Approval. The criteria for review and potential approval of Ordinance 1483 is found
in Section 17-9-020(D) of the DCMC and requires vacating a public right-of-way comply with
state code. Utah State Code 10-9a-609.5 requires approval by the city and are as follows:

         (4). The city council finds:
            a. good cause exists for the vacation; and
            b. neither the public interest nor any person will be materially injured by the
               vacation.

REVIEWS

Engineering and Public Works Divisions Review. The Draper City Engineering and Public Works
Divisions have completed their reviews of the vacation request and find that the public right-of-
way may be vacated and relocated without impacting the traffic and utility needs of the public.
