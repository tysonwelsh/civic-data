2.      Public Hearing – S-21-009 – A Preliminary Plat Amendment to Consolidate Seven
        Existing Parcels into a 46-Lot Subdivision Plat.

Action Item

Applicant:
Address:
              AF
               300 Lofts, LLC
               3363 South 300 East and 354 East 3360 South

Deputy Development Director Eliza Ungricht presented the next two agenda items together as they
were both related to the same project. Two separate recommendations would be made to the City
Council with two separate public hearings.

The first application was for a townhome development located at 3383 South 300 East. Preliminary
Plat Approval and a recommendation to the City Council were sought for the townhome overlay. If
the City Council grants approval, the application will come back before the Planning Commission
for Final Plat approval.
R
Ms. Ungricht reported that the existing building is the former Salt Lake County Parks and Recreation
facility and is currently vacant. The applicant has purchased a single-family home and will
incorporate the property into the project. The street will connect from 300 East to 3360 South for
fire access.
D

There are 45 proposed units in the project. The applicant meets all requirements for the overlay and
amenities. Ms. Ungricht presented renderings and stated that Staff reviewed the proposal and found
that the request meets all requirements. There is a dedication of 8.5 feet along 3360 South and a
dedication of 31.5 feet along 400 East. Staff recommended approval of the Preliminary Plat to
consolidate seven existing parcels into a 46-lot subdivision based on the Findings of Fact and the
Conditions of Approval set forth in the staff report.

Commissioner Southey asked if just one single-family home was proposed to be demolished.
Ms. Ungricht confirmed that was the case and stated that the home is located directly west of the
Shenandoah Apartments on 3360 South.
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Meeting                                                   5

Vice-Chair Pechmann asked about the current zoning for the area. Ms. Ungricht stated that it is
Residential Multiple, which allows for multi-family development. The City’s two residential zones
are R-1, which is single-family, and Residential Multiple. The Townhome Overlay is slightly
different due to the new ordinance, which was passed last year. For any townhome development,
the overlay is applied to the existing zoning.

Vice-Chair Pechmann opened the public hearing at 7:17 p.m.

Kathy Ivie gave her address as 3385 South 300 East and stated that she has lived there for 26 years.
Her concern was with multi-family rentals due to the increase in crime in the area. She preferred to
see single-family, owner-occupied units. She stated that they have spoken to the Mayor about their




                                                T
concerns in the past. Another concern was with parking. She lives in a complex with 60 units and
there is not adequate parking. If units have three bedrooms, there is a need for more than one parking
stall per unit.

Vice-Chair Pechmann stated that he would defer to Staff on this question as he was unsure how
              AF
much jurisdiction the Planning Commission has to dictate owner-occupied versus rental units.
Deputy City Attorney, Brieanne Brass, stated that as this is just a public hearing, residents can reach
out to Community Development Staff for answers to specific questions.

Callie Quong gave her address as 357 East Newsome Park Lane, which borders this project to the
south. Many of her concerns were the same as the previous resident with respect to parking and
renters, as well traffic. She asked for clarification on the setback of the units as she was told that
they will be about five feet from the perimeter fence. Because the proposed development will be
three stories in height and her development is only two stories, she was concerned that people could
throw debris and trash down onto their roofs.

Melanie Allgood gave her address as 357 East Newsome Park Lane. She expressed concerns similar
R
to the previous two residents. She is the Homeowners Association (“HOA”) President in her
complex and has been for 19 years. She was concerned with additional traffic, speeding, parking,
and the loss of her view.

Femi Collaku gave his address as 363 East Newsome Park Lane and stated that in addition to the
concerns already raised was the issue of privacy. They all have very small rear yards and with the
D

addition of new units next door that will be able to look into their yard, they will no longer feel safe
using their yard.

Vice-Chair Pechmann asked if the site plan showed a wall between the two properties. Ms. Ungricht
stated that a six-foot pre-cast concrete wall is required.

Ms. Allgood stated that the Parks and Recreation Department originally installed a chain link fence.
When the property she resides in was developed, the developer put up a vinyl fence to block the
view of the Parks and Recreation property. There is property between the fences that belongs to
people in her development. She asked if the property line would be re-evaluated. Ms. Brass referred
her to the Community Development Department.

Ms. Ivie stated that the fence needs to be higher than six feet as it is easy for many people to jump
over it at that height.

Amanda Galloway gave her address as 3061 East Newsome Park Lane. Her concerns were similar
to those previously stated.

Laura Nava gave her address as 3439 South and asked if the units will be renter or owner-occupied.

7:34 p.m.

John Thomas from Next Level Homes stated that they realize that change is difficult and no one
likes having new neighbors move in. They have taken steps to help make it a nice place to live. In
the past, there has been a lot of crime, homelessness, and drug abuse in the area. On this particular
              AF
property, there are many places for people to hide and do nefarious things. This project will actually
help decrease crime in the area. He was unsure if the units will be for rent or for sale since it will
depend on the market. The units were expected to sell for approximately $600,000. If rented, they
will cost approximately $2,700 per unit per month. The value of the homes will draw responsible
citizens with good jobs like those who have spoken during the public hearing.

Many of the new building standards, which will cost the developer more, will beautify the
community and raise the bar for the surrounding area. There will be two-car garages for each unit
and an additional 22 parking spaces throughout the project. Traffic studies were conducted and 300
East will be able to accommodate the additional traffic. Mr. Thomas stated that their goal is to
improve the community and continue to add value to the area. He gave his email address and invited
the residents to reach out to him with any questions.
R
Vice-Chair Pechmann asked if Mr. Thomas would explain some elements of the design that comply
with the Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design (“CPTED”) to help alleviate concerns
identified by the neighbors. Mr. Thomas stated that there will be cameras throughout the project, a
Community Watch Program, and outdoor space to allow for more outdoor living. There will be
other elements as well but he would have to review the parameters of the program. He stated that
D

he could make more money with an apartment complex but townhomes would be more conducive
to alleviating crime in the area.

Commissioner Carter asked if the individual lots shown on the plat will be consolidated.
Ms. Ungricht stated that they would all be consolidated. He expressed concern with the way the
project adjoins the neighboring properties. He did not like the location of the project as it seems to
be pieced together and almost forced in this location. Ms. Ungricht stated that all of the parcels with
the exception of the one single-family home were all previously owned by the County and operated
as one parcel. The homes on 3360 South are all multi-family.
