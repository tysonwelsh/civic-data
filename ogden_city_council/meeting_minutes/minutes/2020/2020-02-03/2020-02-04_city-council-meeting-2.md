Ogden High School Marching Band
          A memo from Council staff regarding recognition of the Ogden High School Marching Band for their successful season,
including winning the State Championship, came before the Council for consideration. Council member Lopez read the certificate
ofrecognition issued by the Ogden City Council and Mayor Caldwell and presented Band Director Stan Renda and members of
the band with the certificate.
          Mr. Renda thanked the Mayor and Council for recognizing him and his team; he stated it is an honor to have his
marching band recognized by Ogden City. Ogden High's Marching Band has a rich history that he wanted to be part of. The year
before he took over as the Band Director, the band had 23 members and he felt strongly about increasing the participation and
quality of the band. He did not think that could be accomplished in just three years, but it was. He asked his senior band members
to stand and be recognized, noting these seniors began participating in marching band in ninth grade. At that time the band was
participating in the IA school classification and placed last in the State. Beyond increasing in State ranking, he wanted his band
members to have a good experience and improve their skills. He also wanted them to trust that he would work hard and help them
improve and after three years of hard work, the band won the State Championship. The fact that his band members stuck with
their craft makes him very proud. It has been an absolute pleasure to be their Band Director; he feels at home and will serve as the.
Director for as long as he is allowed to. The success of the band would not be possible without the community support it receives;
Ogden is a unique community and very tight knit. The population is diverse and that diversity is represented on the band, but they
all come together to reach a common goal. He thanked his staff and his family for their support and declared that the band will
only get better from here.

      Proposed Ordinance 2020-3 amending the budget for Fiscal Year July 1,
      2019 to June 30, 2020 for water infrastructure projects and the replacement
      of five city refuse trucks
                A memo from the City Comptroller regarding proposed budget adjustments came before the Council for consideration.
      The memo stated City Administration has proposed budget amendments that will result in a net increase of the Fiscal Year (FY)
      2020 City budget revenues by $5,636,250.
                Treasurer Johnson smmnarized the memo and stated this proposal includes three central budget amendments, including
      designating $4,000,000 for water utility infrastructure improvements and purchasing ten new Compressed Natural Gas (CNG)
      refuse trucks. This proposed budget opening includes funding for one FY2020-2024 Capital Improvement Plan project: Water
      WUO 15 - Distribution, Fire Flow & Pressure Improvements. This project addresses the various needs of Ogden's culinary water
      distribution system. She concluded this infonnation was initially presented to the Council during a work session meeting in
      January of 2020.
                The Chair then called for a public hearing on proposed Ordinance 2020-3, entitled:
                         "An ordinance of Ogden City amending the budget for the Fiscal Year July 1, 2019 to June 30, 2020 by
                         increasing the anticipated revenues for a gross increase of$5,636,250 from sources as detailed in the body of
                         this ordinance; and increasing the appropriations for a gross increase of$5,636,250 as detailed in the body of
                         this ordinance; and providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final
                         passage."
                The Deputy City Recorder presented written notice of said hearing and affidavit of the publication in the Standard-
      Examiner on the 26th day of January 2020 specifying the time and place of this meeting as the time and place when and where the
      proposed ordinance would be given a public hearing and be considered for final passage. The proof of publication was accepted
      and filed.
                There being no persons appearing to be heard, COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED THE PUBLIC HEARING BE
      CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY VICE CHAIR BLAIR, ALL VOTING AYE.
                Council member White stated she understands the reasons that City Administration is pursuing the use of natural gas

      Proposed Ordinance 2020-5 reclassifying 133 West Binford Street from
      Manufacturing and Industrial Zone (M-2} to Central Business District Zone
      (CBD)
                A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed rezone of 133 West
      Binford Street came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated a petition has been filed by the Department to rezone
      properties located generally at 13 3 West Binford Avenue. The properties subject to the petition include several vacant properties,
      including the site of the former St. Anne's building, several single-family homes, and several cmmnercial properties. The
      proposed rezone would reclassify the properties from M-2 (Manufacturing and Industrial) to CBD (Central Business District). The
      proposal is meant to further the development of the downtown area. The Ogden Redevelopment Agency authorized the Agency's
      Executive Director to purchase the St. Anne's property and the adjacent Western Ice House property in September of 2019.
                Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the
      proposed rezone of property at 133 West Binford Avenue from M-2 to CBD. The proposed zone change will allow for future
      expansion of plans for downtown Ogden. The area to be rezoned is comprised of the former St. Anne's Site (26.6 percent),
      railroad property (49 .5 percent), and six other private properties (23 .8 percent). The City has worked with Utah State University
      (USU) to plan for potential development of the railyard area and has developed plans for expansion of downtown development

      with the character of the area, which includes M-2, CBD, C-2, and R-4 zoning; will correct split parcel zoning; allows for future
      development as planned; and allows for more density in the downtown area as proposed by the General Plan. The Planning

Public Comments
          Angel Castillo addressed the Council regarding certain policy issues that they have direct control over. As the City's
budget process begins, she would like for the Council to consider what it will cost to provide the Planning Division the resources
they need to update the City's General Plan and development code. The Council has talked about adjusting the code to allow for
smaller lot sizes and smaller home footprints and she is supportive of these adjustments to allow developers to build homes that
are more affordable for residents of the community. She encouraged the Council to reach out to the Planning Division to gain a
better understanding of the support and resources they need to complete tasks assigned to them. The City has not updated its
General Plan in over 20 years and there is the potential for the City to be negatively impacted by the outdated plan. She would like
for the City to be proactive rather than reactive in its planning efforts. She then noted she would also like the Council to consider
regulations for predatory loan operations; there are 25 loan businesses listed in Ogden and one of those offers a 299 percent annual
percentage rate (APR). Utah Law indicates the maximum APR a lender can charge without a contract is 10 percent, but predatory
lenders have gotten around that by requiring borrowers to sign contracts allowing them to charge these astronomical rates. If an
individual borrows $1,500 from this lender, their monthly payment would be $400. The City should take a stance against this
practice and place a sunset date on this type of business operation in the City. If the Council truly cares about its residents,
particularly those that live at or below the poverty level and that are preyed on by these lenders, they should take a stand and not
allow them to take advantage of citizens.
