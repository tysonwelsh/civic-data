2.    DISCUSSION ITEMS
        a. Tentative Budget for Fiscal Year 2023
Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck explained changes made to the proposed FY 2023
Tentative Budget since the last Council meeting. She presented an option available to the Council to
adopt separate ordinances for the General Fund, utilities, CIP, and special revenue funds so that not
all fund budgets would remain open for discussion if the Council were to proceed with the truth in
taxation process. Council Member Jacob commented that employee compensation was one of the
primary reasons for considering a property tax increase and pointed out that employee salary
decisions impacted all fund budgets. Ms. Steck agreed.

Ms. Steck showed proposed property tax increases included in fiscal year 2023 tentative budgets
for other cities in the area. She showed that, with the proposed property tax increase, property tax
revenue would support approximately 75% of police expenses, and sales tax revenue would
support a majority of services in West Jordan, including approximately 25% of police expenses. Ms.
Steck showed that West Jordan had the lowest employee to population ratio compared to other
cities in the area. She commented that both skilled and unskilled labor was increasingly hard to find,
and emphasized the importance of retaining productive employees.

Ms. Steck presented proposed budget expansion for existing personnel compensation. Public
Services Director Isaac Astill explained the request to add a new Parks Project Manager position to
oversee the design, development, and construction of parks, and review land dedicated to the City
for open space, and two new Parks Maintenance Workers to maintain the growing inventory of park
acreage (added 50 acres of park and 45 acres of open space since 2020). Mr. Astill emphasized the
difficulty for parks employees trying to care for and maintain far more land/projects with the same
number of staff the Parks Department had 10 years ago. He explained the request for compensation
increases for the Cemetery Department, as recommended by the recent Compensation Study. Mr.
Astill commented that from his perspective, all employees in the City were spread thin, with more

Manager position was made clear to him on a ride-along visit to park properties.

The Council and staff discussed a roundabout project on 7800 South included on the Capital Projects
Fund project list, and whether or not to accept the UDOT grant and proceed with the project. Vice
Chair Green argued that a roundabout would not contribute to making the City more walkable.
Council Member Jacob said the roundabouts around the Highlands Commercial Area were a
decision made by the Council several years ago. Council Member Bloom said she would want 7800
South to be completed before putting in a roundabout. Tauni Barker commented that the project
was on UDOT’s STIP (Statewide Transportation Improvement Program) list at that point, and a
delay or cancellation may have political ramifications that could delay funding and future
transportation designations from the State. Vice Chair Green expressed the opinion that the City
continued to create separation between neighborhoods by installing roundabouts and not
considering and promoting walkability. Council Member Jacob said he thought roundabouts slowed
traffic and made the City more walkable.

Responding to a question from Chair Whitelock, Public Works Director Brian Clegg said concrete
projects were currently scheduled a year-and-a-half out. He said the two new Streets Maintenance
Worker positions would allow the concrete team to split into two teams that summer to work on
the more than 300 work orders currently in the queue. Mr. Clegg reported the environmental study
for the 7800 South project was completed, and said that construction would probably not begin
until 2024.

Council Member Worthen asked about funds included in the budget for the planned Community
Arts Center. Vice Chair Green stated he would not vote in favor of including $7.6 million dollars for
a Community Arts Center on the Capital Projects List knowing that the City Hall Remodel project
would exceed the $9 million most recently anticipated, and would perhaps total $12 million by the
end of the project. Council Member Worthen said she would not want to start construction on a
Community Arts Center without knowing the City had enough money to complete the project. Chair
Whitelock suggested keeping the funds for the Community Arts Center in a reserve fund until the
project could continue. Council Member Jacob commented that construction costs were only going
to go up. He said if the City was going to build the Community Arts Center, funds should be included
in the budget and construction should begin. If the City was not going to build the Community Arts
Center, he said he could think of many other uses for the $7.6 million. Council Member Jacob
suggested the Council think outside the box for a Community Arts Center. The Council discussed
scheduling a Work Session to talk about alternatives. Chair Whitelock summarized that a majority
of the Council wanted to remove the Community Arts Center from the FY 2023 Capital Project List
and put the funds in a reserve account. Vice Chair Green emphasized that a Community Arts Center
could be included back in the budget with a budget amendment after further discussion of
alternatives. Mayor Burton said he was looking into alternative funding options for the balance of
the Community Arts Center project.

Ms. Steck explained current efforts to reorganize divisions and existing staff to create the desired
Public Utilities Department (previously Capital Projects). Vice Chair Green commented that the
Council approved an Alternate Transportation Master Plan several years ago that included several
projects, some of which were quite small. He said he had not seen any of those projects completed.
Vice Chair Green commented on the increase in the City’s reserve fund in the last few years, and
stated the tax payers were not seeing anything for the money they were paying. He emphasized the
need for progress with the Alternate Transportation Master Plan. Mr. Astill spoke of projects that

Council Members to show progress with projects. Vice Chair Green said he was thinking specifically
about small projects to connect neighborhoods. Mr. Lee commented that two of the new staff
positions requested were in direct response to the concern expressed by Vice Chair Green.

Council Member Jacob referred to the Compensation Study, and said he knew he would not receive
an inflation adjusted wage increase in the private sector. He said he agreed with the need to take
care of staff, liked the idea of a market study, agreed with merit increases and cost of living
adjustments (COLA), but said he felt an average increase of 9.7% in one year was big. Council
Member Jacob suggested adding together the $1,187.114 total increase suggested by the market
study for FY 2023 and the anticipated $25,000 for FY 2024, dividing by two, and implementing the
average to spread out the impact and reduce the property tax increase necessary in FY 2023.
Responding to a question from Chair Whitelock, Ms. Steck stated the Council was informed in August
2021 that the police compensation increase approved at that time would require a 10% property
tax increase in the FY 2023 budget. She spoke of the risk of losing employees in all departments to
other organizations if an annual compensation study were not implemented and followed to help
with retention. Chair Whitelock expressed support for following through with the property tax
increase necessary to adequately fund the Police Department and keep the City safe. Council
Member Jacob repeated his suggestion to average and phase the market study increase over FY
2023 and FY 2024.

Council Member Jacob left the meeting at 7:03 pm, and returned at 7:08 pm

Council Member Worthen commented that the Series 2015 Sales Tax Revenue Bond would be paid
of in 2025. As an option for reducing the amount of property tax increase needed, Council Member
Worthen suggested using money from reserves for the next two fiscal years until the funds obligated
for the bond were available. Ms. Steck said the needed property tax increase would reduce to 5%.
Council Member Bloom said she thought it was a good idea. Ms. Steck said the long-term impact
would be a reduction in reserves. Vice Chair Green commented that at some point the Police and
Fire Departments would need to expand to service the growing community, and reserves may be
needed for that purpose. Mr. Lee said he understood both points of view, and summarized that the
possible 10% increase now would be reduced to 5%, with perhaps a 5% increase in a few years
instead of a reduction when the debt was paid off. Council Office Director Alan Anderson said the
Council would be taking the proposed $1.8 million property tax increase and replacing it with $1.8
million sales tax revenue. He commented that sales tax revenue was the more volatile of the revenue
sources. Council Member Worthen said her idea was to use $700,000 rather than the full $1.8
million.

Ms. Steck said to implement the idea she would reduce the property tax revenue in the budget by
$713,000, and show use of General Fund reserves to balance the budget. Chair Whitelock said the
hope would be that sales tax revenue would continue as anticipated or better. If the economy tanked
and people stopped spending money, a budget amendment would be necessary to draw more from
reserves.

Council Member Jacob said he supported Council Member Worthen’s idea but would still want to
phase-in the market study compensation increase over two years. Vice Chair Green and Chair
Whitelock mentioned the possibility of losing employees to other organizations if the Council chose
not to implement the full market study increase recommended for FY 2023. Ms. Steck pointed out
the City would be at the average mark with the recommended market study increase, not at the top

of the market. She expressed the opinion that what staff proposed was fair and reasonable with the
market. Responding to a question from Chair Whitelock, Ms. Steck said she was not aware of any
cities spreading an increase over two years. A majority of the Council indicated they were not
interested in phasing the market study increase. Mayor Burton said he knew other cities contacted
West Jordan Department Heads on a regular basis with job offers involving more money.

Council Member Bloom said she was in favor of the proposed personnel increases. Vice Chair Green
said he would like to see a forward plan, expressed support for the proposed increases in personnel,
and said he would rather see the number of positions higher for the Police Department. He
emphasized the need to plan appropriately for growth in the City. Chair Whitelock said she agreed.
Deputy Police Chief Jeremy Robertson said the goal in the Police Department that year was to be
fully staffed. One allocated community officer position was not currently filled. He spoke of plans to
be more efficient, which would involve additional community officers. Ms. Steck stated an additional
community officer would cost around $79,000 per year.

Chair Whitelock asked if the Council would support adding a full or half community officer position
to the FY 2023 budget. Deputy Police Chief Robertson said he believed the Department could afford
to wait a year to add positions. Vice Chair Green and Chair Whitelock said they did not want to wait
a year. Chair Whitelock said she wanted the Police Department to be able to respond quickly when
citizens called. Council Member Worthen said she was inclined to follow the recommendation of
Deputy Police Chief Robertson. Deputy Police Chief Robertson repeated his recommendation to wait
a year to add a position. Mr. Lee said the Council action in August 2021 made a big difference in the
ability to fill positions in the Police Department.

Council Member Bloom referred to senior property tax exemption legislation that may impact the
City in the future. Chair Whitelock said it was her understanding the Legislature was looking at it
more as a deferral than a full exemption. Council Member Bloom said a property tax increase would
hit those on a fixed income the hardest, and suggested the Council consider such an exemption.

Council Member Worthen left the meeting at 7:31 pm, and returned at 7:32 pm
