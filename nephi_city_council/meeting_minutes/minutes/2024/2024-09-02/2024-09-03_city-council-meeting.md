RED CLIFF STORAGE ANNEXATION REQUEST:

   City Administrator Seth Atkinson reviewed with the council an annexation request on the south end
of the city. Councilor Travis Worwood excused himself during the discussion of the annexation due to
conflict of interest. The property is west of the Juab County Jail and east of the I-15 freeway. It would
be contiguous with the current boundaries of the city. Many improvements have already been
completed on the property in preparation for the storage business. The property owner would like to
receive power from Nephi City and a power purchase agreement to this effect was considered by the
Council at the last meeting.
  Mr. Atkinson explained that the petition had been submitted but the area is currently designated as
Level 3 in the annexation area map. A change to a Level 2 and analysis would be required prior to the
property being made a Level 1 according to the Annexation Area Policy Plan.
   Councilor Skip Worwood moved to designate the Red Cliffs Storage parcel to a level 2 and to add the
smaller triangular shaped parcel also included in the annexation request to the annexation area map
and make it a level 2 also. Councilor Parady seconded the motion. The motion carried on a unanimous
vote.

Councilor Travis Worwood rejoined the meeting.

UTAH ARMY NATIONAL GUARD (UTARNG) MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING APPROVED:

  Mr. Atkinson presented a memorandum of understanding (MOU) with the UTARNG. The Utah Army
National Guard (UTARNG) Readiness Center west of the city’s airport is close to being completed. This
facility will contain military equipment that will be housed in a locked armory. The facility will have
numerous safeguards in place to avoid having this equipment falling into the wrong hands, but federal
requirements mandate that the Readiness Center receive a response from local law enforcement within
a certain time frame.
  This request has led to a request from UTARNG for a MOU to have the city’s law enforcement respond
within a certain time frame. After review from the city’s staff and attorney, language was proposed to
show that the city would make best efforts to meet the time requirements without obligating the city to
do so.
   Councilor Travis Worwood moved to authorize the Chief of Police to sign the MOU with the UTARNG.
Councilor Shari Cowan seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

FINANCING AGREEMENT FOR THE DRUG TASK FORCE AND POLICE VEHICLE DISCUSSED:

  Mr. Atkinson said that the police department and the drug task force are working out an agreement
with Unified Fleet Services for the financing and leasing of three vehicles for the task force and one
vehicle for the police department. The staff has wondered if it would be better for the city to do an
interfund loan rather than going out to another financial institution for the financing.
  Mr. Atkinson said that he would bring back a recommendation at the next meeting for financing these
police vehicles. The council seemed open to the idea of interfund loan financing.

CIVIL SCIENCE APPROVED AS THE CRIMSON FLATS/RED CLIFFS PARK DESIGN FIRM:

  Mr. Atkinson reported that a selection process to find a design firm for the Crimson Flats Ballpark and
the Red Cliffs Park had been completed by the city staff. The city received responses from four firms
and held interviews with two firms. The recommendation from a committee, made up of members of
the public and staff, is to use a firm called Civil Science for the design of both the Crimson Flats Ballpark
and the Red Cliffs Park. Funds from the city’s prior fiscal year surplus have been budgeted in the Capital
Projects Fund for the design costs for both projects.
   Mr. Atkinson added that having a design in place could allow the city to prepare for financing options
by providing a detailed estimate of costs. It will also involve the public so that amenities recommended
by residents can be used. A design will also provide a starting point that can be used to leverage grant
opportunities. Design and construction costs are increasing and waiting to complete a design could
potentially result in higher costs later.
   Councilor Travis Worwood asked about the progress of the completion of 1000 South that would
provide access to the Red Cliffs Park. Mr. Atkinson answered that the design of the park will help with
the completion of the road and infrastructure needed.
   Councilor Skip Worwood moved to name Civil Science as the firm to design Crimson Flats Ballpark and
the Red Cliffs Park. Councilor JD Parady seconded the motion, The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

Council.

1.   Mr. Atkinson reported that he would add a Hive tab in the financial reports so that the council will
     know the financial position of the Hive.

2. The council discussed whether to help fund The Greater Nephi Chamber of Commerce. Councilor
Parady said that there is precedent in the state that communities help fund their chambers of
commerce. From his experience, he has seen a lot of progress in the services that the Chamber offers.
The previous funding that the council had provided had been well spent, memberships have gone from
5 to 125 members. Councilor Parady felt that the council should set some expectations for the funding
contributions and added that Snow College is very supportive of our chamber, and they are putting
more facilities in our community. Parady suggested $15,000 being contributed to the Chamber.
   Councilor Travis Worwood said he is not in favor of an ongoing funding source. He is in favor of
getting other cities and the county involved in helping contribute but would support helping this next
budget cycle.
Councilor Skip Worwood said that the goal of the Chamber should be to become self-sufficient.
Councilor Callaway said he does not want to see perpetual funding and that the city should step their
contribution down in the future.
Mr. Atkinson stated that if the city contributes the $15,000 then their platinum membership dues of
$750.00 should be included in the contribution. Executive Director of the Chamber Jaque Thorn said that
would be the case and expressed her appreciation to the council for their support.

3. Mr. Atkinson reported that a local developer is currently working through the process with Juab
County to approve a 7-lot residential development. The parcel is west of the current Nephi City limits
and near, but not contiguous with, the island annexation containing the meat processing facility. The
developer has expressed an interest in receiving city utilities including electric, water, and sewer. The
developer also understands that they would be required to pay the costs associated with the utility
extensions and are willing to pay them. There is also an understanding that if electric service were to be
provided, and the parcel is not located within the city limits, then Rocky Mountain Power would need to
grant permission for Nephi City to service the development.

  The development is currently being proposed as 7 residential lots of 1-acre or larger. This is a
development that has been desired by the city but has never been proposed so far away from the
current city boundaries. The developer has shared the information that the costs of purchasing land
near or in the city limits of Nephi make it infeasible to do a development of this type but that cheaper
land outside of the city limits allow for development of this type Mr. Atkinson gave a list of pros and
cons for allowing the developer to annex into the city and pros and cons for providing city services
without annexing into the city. He recommended denying the request for city services.
  The council decided to take more time to make a decision on this issue. Mr. Atkinson said that he
would come back with some recommendations on how to encourage developers to develop less dense
subdivisions. Councilor Travis Worwood said that he has an issue of staying unbiased on this issue and
would excuse himself from decisions on this issue.
