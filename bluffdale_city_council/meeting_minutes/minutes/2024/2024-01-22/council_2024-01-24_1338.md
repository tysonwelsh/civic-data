       4.2     Presentation of the 2022-203 Fiscal Year Audit Report - Hansen Bradshaw
               Malmrose & Erickson (Prgyg/iter: JeffMilesX [Joint Presentation of Audit: City
               CouncO. Local Building Authority, and Redevelopment Agency Board.]

Jeff Miles from Hansen Bradshaw Malmrose & Erickson presented the Audit Report. He commended
the City's Management Team for their work with the assistance of Accountant, Dave Sanderson. Mr.
Miles reported that they were on site in the City for approximately four days, which is a small amount
of time. Everything was well organized, which helped move the audit progress along seamlessly.
The first page ofthe document included the Independent Auditor's Report of a clean opinion on the
City's financial statements.


Mr. Miles referred to page four ofthe document, which is the Management Discussion Analysis that
was prepared by Administrative Services Director, Bruce Kartchner and Dave Sanderson. It provides
a summary ofthe financial information. The assets and deferred outflows exceeded the liabilities and
deferred inflows of the City by $211 million at the end of fiscal year 2023. The net position of the
City increased by $23 million in 2023 with much being attributed to major infrastructure additions
that have been ongoing for the last several years. The Unassigned Fund Balance ofthe General Fund
is available to the City Council and has the most direct influence on their decisions. It was $3.7
million, which is approximately 27% of General Fund expenditures. The State metric is a percentage
of revenues of which the City is not allowed to keep more than 35%. The City's outstanding long-
term liabilities decreased by nearly $1 million and the City continues to service its outstanding bonds.

Mr. Miles reported that as part of every audit they update their understanding of the City's internal
controls. Page 56 of the report addresses internal controls. No significant deficiencies or material
weaknesses within the internal controls over the financial reporting ofthe City were found. The last
report on page 58 deals with the State Compliance Audit Guide and ensures that the City is in
compliance. The items that were looked at in fiscal year 2023 were budgetary compliance,the Justice
Court,the fraud Risk Assessment, Enterprise Fund services, fund balance, restricted taxes and related
revenues,governmental fees, and the Open and Public Meetings Act. No instances ofnon-compliance

Chief Burton reported on special events the department is involved in. He referenced transparency
and use offorce and stated that the department has an extremely low number of cases involving the
use of force. With over 11,000 calls for service in the City of Bluffdale, there are only 17 cases
involving the use offorce and nine involving show offorce. That speaks to the level of training and
professionalism ofthe officers. ChiefBurton described the difference between use offorce and show
of force. Use of force involves hands-on and/or the use of a taser. A show of force involves the

showing ofa firearm or a taser without using it.


A video was shown that highlights the capabilities of the Police Department. Chief Burton
commented on staffing and stated that his main consideration is officer safety. He added the category
of Mental Wellness, which was going very well. He pointed out that people are most important in
their organization. He reported that an officer's time is divided between what he is obligated to do
and what he has time left over to do. Obligated time involves answering calls. Administrative time
involves training or is administrative in nature. Sometimes there are community support duties. The
time that remains is when officers have to do patrols and perform traffic enforcement. The goal is to
find a balance. The goal is for officers to have enough unobligated time that they are free to do extra
neighborhood patrols and school zone speed enforcement.

For years they have tracked calls for service. ChiefBurton reported that there was a decrease in calls
for service this year. The last 10 years averaged 8%. He arrives at that number by applying that
average to what he expects will be the increase in 2024. From 2022 to 2023,the change dropped with
695 cases where fewer cases were officer initiated. By taking the 695 cases and applying that number
to the 2022 figure, they have a 2% increase rather than a decrease. It shows that officers are so busy
that their unobligated time is not as available as they would like it to be.


ChiefBurton reported that when they hit 1,000 calls for service per officer, per year they start feeling
the crunch in staffing and worry about officer safety. If they go with the 2024 projection, they will
