         The purpose of the Joint Work Session was to review the agendas for the Redevelopment Agency and Municipal
Building Authority meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.; review the proposed Municipal Building Authority Fiscal Year
2024-2025 budget; discuss the proposed FY 2024-2025 City budget; discuss proposed FY 2024-2025 proposed fee amendments;
discuss Council/Board/Trustee business; and hear Council/Board/Trustee comments.

Agenda Review
       Chair Richey and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the Redevelopment Agency
and Municipal Building Authority meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Proposed Municipal Building Authority Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget
         Comptroller Sorensen explained that the proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Municipal Building Authority budget totals
$3,678,500. The budget maintains a balance of $5,000 for any expenses related to financial reporting and the remainder is debt
service for WonderBlock parking infrastructure. He noted the bond for the WonderBlock parking structure was issued by the
Municipal Building Authority and, therefore, debt service for the bond must be programmed into the Municipal Building
Authority budget.
         There was discussion among the Board and staff regarding other infrastructure and redevelopment projects planned in
other areas of the City, including the Marshall White Center and Union Station, and where the funding for those projects will be
allocated. Management Services Director Stout stated that funding for other major projects is planned for the City budget and
Redevelopment Agency budget.
         Chair Richey asked if the revenues that will be generated for the downtown Ogden paid parking system will be held in
the Municipal Building Authority budget and used for debt service, to which Mr. Sorensen answered yes; an enterprise fund will
be created to collect and appropriate the revenues.

Proposed Ogden City Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget
         Public Safety Employee Group Representatives: Alex Sanders, Ogden Local 552 Fire Fighter’s Union, stated the Union
represents about 95 percent of the Ogden Fire Department employees. He thanked the Mayor and Council for their continued
support of Department employees via wage increases. He noted, however, that other agencies also continue to increase their
wages, which makes it more difficult each year to remain competitive. The Ogden Fire Department would like to remain
competitive with agencies similar to itself, though the Ogden Fire Department is the busiest Department in the State of Utah when
considering calls/case load per Fire Fighter. For example, Salt Lake City responds to 30,000 calls per year, and they have over 300
Fire Fighters in 15 stations. The Ogden Fire Department responds to 22,000 calls from five stations and with just over 100 Fire
Fighters. Ogden has one-third of the work force of the Salt Lake City Department, but three-quarters of the call volume. He stated
that a wage study was conducted recently by the Provo Union; they evaluated 16 different Fire Departments in the State and
Ogden’s wages were below average for each position. Some cities bolster their wage data by creating very broad pay ranges;
Ogden’s pay range is good with seven steps in it that are attainable. However, the Union is respectfully asking for better pay
within those ranges. He noted that Chief Mathieu is constantly seeking grant funding that could help to increase the staffing levels
and wages in the Department, but it is the Union’s current position that the Fire Department needs to create an additional 15 Fire
Fighter positions in order to ensure safety and provide adequate staffing on each shift. This led to discussion regarding
professional standards relating to the appropriate number of Fire Fighters per shift and the acceptable call volume per employee.
          Ann Stellmon, Ogden Police Benefits Association President, stated that the past support of the Mayor and City Council
has been felt by the Police Department; however, there is a now a perception in the Department that the City has lost its
motivation and momentum to be competitive with other agencies in the State. Recruitment and retention remain a struggle based
upon hourly wages, and this has caused frustration for managers and supervisors of the Department. The Police Officers love the
Ogden community, but when they see job advertisements for other agencies with significantly higher pay, it is difficult to stay
dedicated to Ogden.
          John Thomas, Fraternal Order of Police President, echoed the sentiments shared by Ms. Stellmon; he has been with
Ogden City for almost 24 years and each year the Police Department must come before the Council to communicate how hard
they work and how their pay is not competitive with other agencies. The Ogden Police Department is the busiest Department in
the State of Utah, but each year it must ‘beg for scraps’ and the Officers are unhappy. Many employees are looking for
employment elsewhere and other agencies will hire them in a heartbeat because Ogden officers are well trained and competent. He
stated he is not sure the cause of the disconnect related to equitable wages for Ogden officers. A system must be developed to
ensure appropriate and equitable pay for the men and women of the Ogden Police Department so that it is not necessary for
employee group representatives to appear before the Council year after year to plead for wage increases.
          Council member Myers stated that he is grateful for the Public Safety employees of the City; one of the first things he did
after he was elected to his position was to participate in a ride-along with an Ogden Police Officer and it was eye opening to him.
The job they do is extraordinary and the risk they take is commendable. He stated the budget is very complex and he does not
have any answers for the employee group representatives but to say that he has heard them and is on their side.
          The group engaged in discussion about current staffing levels and desired staffing levels and how pay increases will help
the Department achieve the goal of being fully staffed.
          General Fund Revenues: Comptroller Sorensen reported in the Mayor’s proposed budget, the amount of revenue to be
received is estimated. Revenues are projected based on historical data and other variables that could impact revenue sources such
as the economy and consumer confidence. Revenues are tracked through the year as they become available and are reported to the
Council in quarterly financial reports. If appropriate, the budget can be amended to increase or decrease the budgeted revenues.
Five significant revenue sources account for a majority of the revenue in the General Fund. These sources are property tax, sales
tax, franchise tax (taxes on utilities), fees in lieu of taxes (privilege taxes), and allocations to municipal operations (transfers from
Enterprise Funds to the General Fund). Other than the five large revenue generators, other General Fund revenue comes from fees,
fines, permits, licenses, miscellaneous other taxes, grants, and some intergovernmental funds such as Class B&C Road Funds and
Active Transportation (Prop 1) Funds. Mr. Sorensen used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the estimated
General Fund revenues for Fiscal Year 2024-2025, totaling $90,106,375. General Fund revenue includes property tax revenue of
$58,889,625, which is a 2.05 percent increase when compared to the Fiscal Year 2024 adopted budget. For a copy of the
presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          The Council participates in a discussion with Mr. Sorensen and Chief Administrative officer Brown regarding
Administration’s property tax philosophy. Ms. Brown noted Administration will recommend an annual review of property tax
rates and, at a minimum, maintaining the certified tax rate from year to year in order to avoid a situation where the City is required
to approve a significant property tax increase as a result of failing to maintain the tax rate for a several year period. The Council
communicated support for an annual examination of the certified tax rate in order to consider whether to maintain or increase the
rate or accept the County Assessor’s recommended adjustment to the rate.
          There was also discussion about the transfers from Enterprise Funds to the General Fund, which is classified as a
revenue; Council member Myers asked Mr. Sorensen to explain the purpose of the transfers in a way that can be clearly
communicated to residents. He asked if the transfers are intended to cover the costs associated with administrative
services/support to the Enterprise Funds. Ms. Stout stated that the total transfers are 17 percent of the total fund; five percent is
intended to cover administrative costs, but the remaining 12 percent is a simple transfer with no exchange of value. The City has
used the justification that the transfer is equivalent to franchise fees and property taxes the Enterprise Funds would otherwise pay
to the City. Council member Myers stated that other utility providers generate a profit for providing services; he asked if Ogden
City is generating a profit for utility services provided. Ms. Stout stated that the City cannot levy a tax on its own utility funds, so
the transfer was considered a fee in lieu of taxes; this was a policy decision made several years ago and the City has an established
transfer that is completed each year: six percent for property tax and six percent for franchise tax. The transfer helps the City to
keep its property tax rate lower than would otherwise be necessary. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith noted the City’s
financial advisor has been asked to study the transfer and he has issued a draft report of his findings for the Council to consider.
This information will be presented to the Council in the near future and the Council can pass that information on to their
constituents.

Water and Refuse Discount Amendments
           Financial Analyst Johns explained that currently, Weber County administers a property tax relief program and provides
the list of qualified participants to the City. The City applies a 50% water base rate discount and a $2.50 refuse discount to each
utility bill. The proposal is to expand the program to individuals who are blind, to disabled veterans, and to deployed members of
the military and would apply a set monthly discount rate of $15.50 to the billing account. The discount would increase with the
blended inflation adjustment.
           Discussion among the Council and Mr. Johns centered on qualifications someone must meet in order to receive a
discount; they also explored the option of creating a program that would give other customers the opportunity to make a donation
or charitable contribution to the City’s utility billing office to be used to pay for another customer’s bill if they meet certain
criteria.

Water Rate Amendments (related to 36” Canyon Waterline Replacement)
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith explained the proposed water rates amendments are related to the debt service for
the 36-inch Ogden Canyon Waterline Project. The proposed amendments would increase water rates for all users by 4% per year
for the next five years. This would be a set increase over and above the blended inflation adjustment. The Council has received
presentations on the proposed funding for the canyon waterline and the Council adopted a parameters resolution for approximately
$77 million in federal and state loans. The rate increases are needed to pay the debt service on the loans.
          Fred Philpot of Lewis, Robertson, Burningham (LRB), the City’s Financial Advisor, used the aid of a Power Point
presentation to discuss optional water utility rate changes for the Council to consider; his presentation expounded on the long-term
impact of different rate structure models and the projects and goals that can be achieved based upon certain increases. There was
discussion among the Council regarding the most appropriate action to take relative to utility rate increases.
