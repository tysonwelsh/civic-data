 3. PUBLIC COMMENT
      Chair Jacob opened the public comment period at 6:02 pm.

      Comments:
      Chris McConnehey, West Jordan resident, voiced excitement to be the first member of the
      public to speak from the podium in the newly renovated Council Chambers. Mr. McConnehey
      highlighted an issue regarding the fence between his property and neighboring property. He
      was advised by the County to have a survey done, which cost him $2,200. He listed the various
      fees involved in a lot line adjustment and suggested a fee similar to the $65 miscellaneous
      permit fee, plus an hourly administrative staff fee, would be more reasonable. He asked the
      Council to review the fees for lot line adjustments.

      Amy Motta, resident of Salt Lake City, said she was a volunteer with Bailing Out Benji. She
      spoke of a puppy mill in Nebraska that lost its license stating that many of the dogs were
      accepted by the Utah Best Friends Animal Society. Ms. Motta shared a list of findings
      associated with the Nebraska puppy mill.

       c. Resolution No. 24-007 requesting the resignation of Natalie Cline from the Utah
          State Board of Education

           MOTION:     Chair Jacob moved to APPROVE Resolution No. 24-007 requesting the
                       resignation of Natalie Cline from the Utah State Board of Education.
                       Council Member Bloom seconded the motion.

           Council Member Whitelock read aloud the following statement:
             “Tonight, we are being asked to join a growing contingent that are asking for a
             resignation of a member of the education community. While what this person did was
             wrong on many levels, and I do not condone her actions, I’ve struggled with is this an
             appropriate action for this body to take and more important what my role should be.

             So many thoughts have been occupying my mind, and my sleep or lack thereof. From a
             great teacher we are taught ‘He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone’
             from Marcus Aurelius we hear ‘Be tolerant with others and strict with yourself’ and
             more importantly from the City of West Jordan Council Rules: SECTION 104
             CEREMONIAL RESOLUTIONS (2) The Council ... discourages resolutions that distract
             from the non-partisan, local control of West Jordan governance by the City Council. (3)
             Resolutions considered to be extremely controversial (such as a censure of an
             individual) or outside the political oversight of the City Council may require support of
             four members of the council to be considered for addition to an agenda.

             Clearly there must be 4 members that are in favor of this resolution, otherwise it would
             not be before us tonight.

             Here is where my struggle is, I agree the actions of the person are not ok and we should
             not condone them but is it my role to ask for the resignation when it feels outside of my
             lane as a Council Member. This resolution deals with a member of the State School
             Board, the State School Board is a political subdivision of the State, so is it their role to
             decide what action should and can be taken. Should the other members of the State
             School Board work to censure inappropriate behaviors of their members. Or should it

             be left to the voters. When people reach out about school issues, we point them to the
             School Board and District. This resolution seems a better fit for the education
             community. I understand the desire to do something when bad things occur to try and
             help make it better. After all my background is nursing and that is what we do try and
             make it better while staying in our lane to ensure the outcome is the best possible. I’m
             confident no one in this room wants me to perform brain surgery on them, but I could
             do a terrific job in dressing changes if they suffered a severe burn. Staying in our lane
             is a mantra we hear often.

             Additionally, I believe we should speak out about behavior but be careful to not become
             the bully to another human being. I appreciate the statement by Utah PTA it reads: ‘We
             are deeply disappointed to learn that an elected official would subject a child to ridicule
             and harm on their social media account. We encourage all elected officials to meet a
             higher standard in protecting children by engaging the public in united and equitable
             efforts to secure the physical, mental, emotional, spiritual, and social well-being of all
             children and youth. It is imperative that adults protect and defend children and decry
             any attempt to ridicule them.’

             Back to the resolution before us, I wish this resolution was more about speaking out
             about the actions and less about the person. Here are the lines of the resolution I can
             support:
             ‘WHEREAS, the West Jordan community vehemently opposes any form of bullying
             targeting our children, particularly when perpetrated by an adult, and especially so
             when that adult occupies an elected office and is thus held to a higher standard of
             conduct; and

             WHEREAS, West Jordan is committed to fostering a safe environment within our city
             for all residents, including children;’

             So, as I ponder what is the appropriate action for me as a member of the city council, I
             want to again stress that our words and actions matter. Using any form to harm a child
             is never ok. Putting things on social media platforms have consequences and can
             literally put others' lives in danger. There are appropriate ways to convey messages of
             what you feel are right and wrong, using someone else's child in your message is wrong
             and can cause damage to them their family and community. This is indeed a hard
             decision and one I do not take lightly.”

           Council Member Bedore believed he was elected to be a voice for the people and reported
           that his own child had been extremely upset by the situation. He viewed what happened
           as an adult who knew what they were doing, and a situation that hurt people. Council
           Member Bedore said his voice for the people would be to say that West Jordan did not
           condone the behavior and had zero tolerance for such behavior. He expressed support for
           the proposed resolution.

           Vice Chair Lamb reported that many people had reached out to him in support of the
           proposed resolution. He researched what had taken place with Ms. Cline over the past few
           years, and fully supported Resolution No. 24-007.

           Council Member Bloom read aloud the following statement:
             “This resolution underscores a critical moment of reflection for our community and for
             all who hold positions of influence and trust. At the heart of this deliberation is the
             acknowledgment that words and actions, especially from those in elected offices, carry
             profound weight. The incident involving State School Board Member Natalie Cline has
             brought to light not just the immediate harm inflicted upon a young athlete and her
             community but also broader concerns about the responsibilities incumbent upon our
             public servants.

             An apology, in its most sincere form, is not merely an expression of regret but a
             commitment to understanding, restitution, and change. It is a recognition of harm done
             and an overt pledge to mitigate such harm in the future. Unfortunately, Ms. Cline's
             subsequent statements, rather than embodying this spirit, appeared to perpetuate
             narratives that further marginalized vulnerable populations and justified actions that
             the community at large found unacceptable. Such responses do not fulfill the reparative
             function of a genuine apology; instead, they contribute to the perpetuation of division
             and discomfort.

             The essence of Ms. Cline's actions and her response to the ensuing criticism reveals a
             troubling willingness to engage in discourse that not only fails to uphold the dignity of
             all individuals but actively undermines it. The specific targeting of a high school athlete,
             under the guise of commenting on broader social issues, is particularly disconcerting.
             It demonstrates a disregard for the individual rights and well-being of students, the
             very demographic Ms. Cline was elected to serve and protect.

             This resolution today, while a response to a specific incident, speaks to a larger, more
             universal principle: the imperative of nurturing a community where every individual,
             especially the youngest among us, feels safe, valued, and supported. This document is
             not just a call for accountability; it is a reaffirmation of our collective commitment to
             fostering an environment of respect, empathy, and understanding.

             In light of these events, it becomes clear that the actions and rhetoric we choose to
             employ in our public and private lives have lasting impacts. As members of a
             community, it is incumbent upon us to advocate for a culture that prioritizes the well-
             being and dignity of all its members, particularly when confronting issues of such
             sensitivity and importance. Let this moment be a catalyst for a renewed dedication to
             these principles, ensuring that our community remains a bastion of safety, inclusivity,
             and mutual respect for all its residents.”

           Council Member Green wanted the Council to be careful not to go down a “slippery slope”
           of doing this on a regular basis. He quoted former-Congressman Ben McAdams as saying,
           “There is a time to campaign, and there is a time to govern,” and asked the Council to keep
           the statement in mind. Council Member Green said as representatives of West Jordan the
           Council governed the entire City and did not work for any particular special interests. He
           expressed a desire to govern correctly and emphasize unity in the community.

           Council Member Shelton felt the decision was difficult for all. He had done research and
           did not condone the actions of Ms. Cline. He felt for the student who was the subject of
           bullying. Council Member Shelton said the State Board of Education had met on the
           subject, which was where he believed the conversation belonged. He felt the proposed
           resolution was outside the realm of what the Council should get into.

           Chair Jacob said a past situation with the West Jordan City Council reminded him of what
           the School Board was dealing with. At the time he would have appreciated a letter or
           resolution from a neighboring city saying they backed up the West Jordan Council’s
           decision. Chair Jacob said the State Board of Education had made a unanimous decision,
           and he wanted them to know the West Jordan Council had their backs.

           Council Member Bedore hoped the Council never had to consider anything like that again.
           He believed there was a time to speak up so things did not continue. Council Member
           Whitelock did not condone the actions and if the resolution only called out that such
           behavior was not welcome in West Jordan, she could support the resolution. She believed
           based on Council Rules, the proposed resolution was inappropriate.

           Chair Jacob reiterated that the proposed resolution was an expression of an opinion, not
           a call for legal action.
