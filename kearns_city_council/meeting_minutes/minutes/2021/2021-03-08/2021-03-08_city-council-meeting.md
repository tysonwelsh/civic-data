Order of Business

Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District Transition to a Local District Resolution

                   Pam Roberts, Director, Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District (WFWRD),
stated when WFWRD was established it was as a special service district because that was the
only option available. Since that time, Legislation has been passed that allows the creation of
local service districts. The difference between the two types of districts are that a special service
district is still under the creating entity as far as levying taxes, issuing debt, holding elections,
change of boundaries, and composition of its board. A local service district is completely
independent of any other entity. The resolution before the Council gives consent from Kearns to
transition WFWRD to a local service district. Resolutions consenting to this transition will have to
approve by all entities that participate in WFWRD. The goal is to allow the WFWRD Board to
have total governing authority.

               Council Member Snow asked if any services would change.

               Ms. Roberts stated services would have nothing to do with this process. WFWRD
Board is the one that determines what services are available.

RESOLUTION NO. 21-03-01                                                          Date: March 8, 2021

       A RESOLUTION OF THE KEARNS METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL
       CONSENTING TO THE REORGANIZATION OF THE WASATCH FRONT
       WASTE AND RECYCLING DISTRICT AS A LOCAL DISTRICT

                                               Recitals

         WHEREAS, the County Commission of Salt Lake County on January 19, 1977 established
a special service district known as Salt Lake County Special Service District No. 1 (the “Sanitation
District”) for the provision of garbage collection services in the unincorporated area of Salt Lake
County; and

       WHEREAS, much of the original area of the Sanitation District was subsequently
incorporated into or annexed by municipalities, while remaining within and continuing to receive
services from the Sanitation District; and

       WHEREAS, the Salt Lake County Council established an Administrative Control Board
(the “ACB”) to govern the Sanitation District and appoint the members representing both Salt Lake
county and the municipalities served by the Sanitation District; and

      WHEREAS, until January 1, 2013, the Sanitation District was considered a division or
agency of Salt Lake County government, with the Sanitation District’s employees being
employees of Salt Lake County and administrative and support services being provided by Salt
Lake County agencies; and

         WHEREAS, by its Resolution No. 4670 (the “Governing Resolution”), as of January 1,
2013, the Salt Lake County Council, pursuant to the rules set forth in the Special Services District
Act, Title 17D of the Utah Code, delegated to the ACB full governance of the functions and
activities of the Sanitation District and since that time, the Sanitation District has employed its own
personnel and maintained sole responsibility for the operations and administration of the
Sanitation District; and




                                                  2
DATE           MONDAY                           MARCH                                        8, 2021

         WHEREAS, the Governing Resolution renamed the Sanitation District as the Wasatch
Front Waste and Recycling District (“WFWRD”), and WFWRD has exercised and been subject to
all the rights, powers, duties, governance, and responsibilities of a special service district under
the provisions of the Special Service District Act, Title 17D of the Utah Code; and

         WHEREAS, the Governing Resolution stated that the Salt Lake County Council had found
that it was in the best interests of the citizens of Salt Lake County, the partner municipalities which
were included within the Sanitation District, and the property owners receiving services within the
Sanitation District for the Sanitation District to become independent from Salt Lake County,
however a special service district is by definition a hybrid entity that is still subject to Salt Lake
County oversight and control in several regards; and

        WHEREAS, for WFWRD to become fully independent, as the Salt Lake County Council
desired, it must be converted into a local district governed under the Local District Act, Title 17B
of the Utah Code and the ability to reorganize a special service district into a local district was not
enacted until 2013, under Section 17D-1-604 of the Utah Code (the “Reorganization Statute”);
and

       WHEREAS, the Reorganization Statute authorizes Salt Lake County to reorganize
WFWRD into a completely independent local district and requires that the reorganization may not
occur unless each municipality that is included within WFWRD must consent to the
reorganization; and

        WHEREAS, the Kearns Metro Township is a member municipality of WFWRD and has
determined that it is in the best interests of WFWRD and of Kearns Metro Township for WFWRD
to be reorganized as a local district.

       NOW, THEREFORE, it is resolved by the Kearns Metro Township Council as follows:

           1. The Kearns Metro Township Council hereby consents to Salt Lake County Council
              reorganizing WFWRD as a local district under Section 17D-1-604 under
              substantially the following terms:

               a. The WFWRD name will remain the same.
               b. The current WFWRD boundaries will remain the same.
               c. The services authorized to be provided by WFWRD, namely waste and
                  recycling collection services, will remain the same.
               d. The governing board appointment type, to the maximum extent possible, will
                  remain the same.

           2. This Resolution shall take effect immediately upon its passage.

           3. In the event of any conflict between this Resolution and any other enactment of
              the Kearns Metro Township Council, this Resolution shall control.

ADOPTED this 8th day of March, 2021.

Legislative Update

               Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, reviewed the following
bills:

     SB 58 – Metro Township Amendments

This bill allows metro townships to impose the Municipal Energy and Telecommunication Sale
and Use Tax. The bill gives the authority to the townships, but the townships still need to
determine if it makes sense to impose the taxes. There is a specific process the township would
need to go through to notify citizens of the desire to impose these taxes.

     HB 244 – First Class County Highway Funds Amendments

                Mr. Bracken stated this bill appropriates $300,000 yearly to Kearns for the next
15 years. This is not new funding. It is funds that Salt Lake County receives from a special
transportation tax.

     HB 98 - Local Government Building Regulation Amendment

                 Mr. Bracken stated this bill that would allow developers to hire their own inspector
to do building inspections, plan reviews, and certificates of occupancy if the municipality does not
meet a specific time requirement. There are a lot of concerns with this bill including the fact that
it takes away the ability of land use authorities to require certain design elements such as
minimum building size and how a building looks. The bill limits the number of plan reviews to one
time, which could cause the need to issue stop orders during the building process. Another issue
with the bill relates to damages caused by disastrous. The bill allows a building that was damage
due to a disaster to be rebuilt to its previous state without meeting current codes.

    HB 82 - Single Family Housing Modifications

               Mr. Bracken stated the bill requires accessory dwelling units (ADU) be a permitted
use in any single-family zone. Kearns already has an ADU ordinance that will need some minor
tweaks to comply with the law.

    SB 189 – Tobacco Retailer Amendments

              Mr. Bracken stated this bill adds additional requirements to tobacco shops. The
Kearns Metro Township ordinance will need to be updated to account for those new requirements.

    HB 364 – Utah Lake Authority

                Mr. Bracken stated this bill did not past, but he wanted the Council to be aware of
it. The bill would have allowed the sale of land in Utah Lake and using the proceeds to clean up
the lake. It prompted a lot of discussions from the water communities especially the Jordan River
Conversancy District, which relies on the water from Utah Lake. There will be ongoing
discussions on this bill over the interim.

    Other Bills

               Mr. Bracken stated other bills that did not past related to billboards, time limits for
issuing permits for business licenses, and a gambling bill.
