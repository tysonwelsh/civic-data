                        SPECIAL CITY COUNCIL
                         MEETING MINUTES

                           Wednesday, August 03, 2022
                            Approved August 24, 2022

The following are the minutes of the special City Council meeting of the Herriman City
Council. The meeting was held on Wednesday, August 3, 2022, at 6:30 p.m. in the
Herriman City Hall Fort Herriman Conference Room, 5355 West Herriman Main Street,
Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was posted in the City
Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members of the Council, media, and interested
citizens.

Presiding: Mayor Lorin Palmer

Councilmembers Present: Jared Henderson, Teddy Hodges, Sherrie Ohrn and Steven
Shields (participated electronically)

Staff Present: City Manager Nathan Cherpeski, Assistant City Manager Tami Moody,
Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas, Community Development Director Blake Thomas,
Records Specialist Ashley Glines, Finance Director Kyle Maurer, City Attorney Todd Sheeran,
Communications Manager Jonathan LaFollette, City Planner Michael Maloy.

Conference Room)

2. Administrative Reports
   2.1. Discuss and Negotiate MDA/PID with auto mall developer – Nathan Cherpeski,
        City Manager, Tami Moody, Assistant City Manager, and Blake Thomas, Community
        Development Director
Mayor Lorin Palmer emphasized the mutual goal trying to be accomplished with the auto mall
project. He turned the time over to City Manager Nathan Cherpeski.

City Manager Cherpeski explained the discussion was intended to be a chance for the Council
to talk with the developers, similarly to what had been conducted for the Olympia
development. He stated there were four Master Development Agreements (MDA), and all
had requested a variation from the zones the property was located. He further elaborated
this was a work meeting, so a decision would not be rendered during the meeting, as it was
simply a chance to discuss the issue with the developers. He said the Council had both the
MDAs and the public infrastructure district documentation, which would fit together to
create the overall project. He also shared a map of the project areas and oriented the Council
as to the locations for different aspects of the development. He added the Planning
Commission had held a brief work session about the development and suggested the
Commission might have some input for Council.

Mayor Palmer asked the developer to talk about the proposed changes made to each area.
Developer Larry Mylar offered a brief overview of the changes and stated the City as well as
the tenants wanted assurances the zoning designations would stay for a reasonable amount
of time. He discussed tenants wanted the Public Infrastructure Districts to be locked in and
create the MDA. Developer Mylar explained between those two options, it was less expensive
to have an MDA in place as it was recognized at the state level. He further explained the areas
needed to be split into four different agreement areas to make it cleaner rather than having
one lengthy MDA.

Mayor Palmer asked the Council for feedback on the presented MDAs, and the Council
agreed it would be better to proceed with the development areas being separated rather than
having one agreement to cover the entire development. Councilmember Jared Henderson said
it was logical; however, wanted to ensure it was legally the correct path to follow. City
Attorney Todd Sheeran confirmed. Councilmember Steve Shields asked about the timeline
for the MDAs to be put in place. Developer Mylar responded the anticipated timeline was 15
years due to the size of the auto mall. He acknowledged they were not locked into the
timeframe currently. City Manager Cherpeski added the 15-year timeframe would match the
Public Infrastructure District (PID) documents. Councilmember Shields was fine with the
timeline; however, wanted to see language incorporated into the agreement to allow parties
the opportunity to review performance targets. He noted he would not support the timeframe
if there was no flexibility to follow-up. Developer Mylar explained the difficulty to have
performance target measures placed on commercial property as it was hard to determine
when commercial developments would commence. He suggested it would be easier to have
those performance target measures placed on the mixed-use developments. City Manager

Cherpeski opined the PID was enough of an incentive. Councilmember Shields still wanted
to have more of a discussion about the timeline, since the economy, and particularly, the way
consumers purchased cars, could change greatly in the next 15 years. Developer Mylar
acknowledged agreed, and reminded the Council there was a check-in review at the four-
year mark. Mayor Palmer thought a check in at four years would be sufficient

Developer Mylar presented the auto mall design concept and relayed the proposition to add
a service center for the complex. It was reported It was reported a Mitsubishi dealership was
interested in having a service center with an attached sales room. Developer Mylar explained
there had been another buyer which was going to buy nearby land for a similar use, but these
plans had fallen through, and his development team had been approached to purchase the
property. He relayed his desire to consolidate the auto mall property, and noted they were
under contract to purchase the property. He said the Council would determine if he could
purchase the property or not and asked the Council to consider incorporated the property
into the Auto Mall Special District. Councilmember Teddy Hodges asked if a stand-alone
service center met the zoning requirements for the Auto Mall. The developer confirmed the
permitted use.

Mayor Palmer asked if there were any concerns about the language in the MDA. The Council
concluded additional time was necessary to go through the agreement. Mayor Palmer thought
it could be helpful to show what the developer was excluding to give the Council a better idea
of what changes were proposed. Community Development Director Blake Thomas
overviewed the agreement and offered an overview of the major changes. He explained the
desire to not limit only major franchises or dealerships in the agreement. The Council
indicated some areas in the document needed to by modified. Councilmember Shields opined
to have the language broad enough to not overly restrict the type of businesses which could
be located in the area. Staff agreed the language should be more inclusive and it was also
clarified the MDA would modify the zoning of the auto mall development. The Council elected
to create an MDA rather than a text amendment to have the desired changes in place in a
more permanent manner.

Director Thomas indicated changes which had been made regarding the ten-foot screening
walls which would be placed around auto storage areas. He relayed the concern of the
concrete walls being plain and requested to the developer to make them more aesthetically
pleasing in some fashion. Director Thomas reported language had been added to the
agreement which required the walls to have architectural features which would add an
element of design. He then indicated there was a large section modified regarding signage.
The sign plan was being revised through the overlay ordinance which had been presented
during the last Council meeting. Some of the proposed signage did not comply with the
ordinance and the developer agreed to modify language to resolve the discrepancy.
Developer Mylar commented signage was also being planned along Auto Mall Road which
would be in compliance.

Director Thomas summarized the largest change was to alter the language to permit used car
dealerships, whereas they were prohibited prior. He said change had been allowed in the
AMSD previously, but now a specific sign plan which was more inclusive and dialed in the
details of permitted signs. The developer commented the AMSD MDA laid the framework for
the following agreements as well. He summarized the main issue was to decide on what uses
should be permitted and then determine if the PID could be utilized for the other MDA areas.

Director Thomas said it was acceptable to use the PID for the auto mall special district;
however, it would need to be determined if it would be legal to use the PID in the other
development areas. Developer Mylar asked what Council would be comfortable with in terms
of the PID, and specifically, the viability to attract a hotel to the area since the mill levy would
be higher. There was a brief discussion about what would be considered an infrastructure
improvement for the public. City Attorney Sheeran noted public ownership was the main
determinant in what would qualify for the PID, and generally, things like roads and sewer
lines were clearly for public ownership. He explained it needed to go through State analysis,
as well as tax-exempt analysis in order to ensure the PID was legal. Developer Mylar clarified
an additional mill levy would be removed, and there would be no direct harm to the City if the
PID was to move forward. City Attorney Sheeran confirmed and explained the process of
how the mill levy amount was determined. He added the main risk to the City was there could
be a possibility to miss out on a possible tenant who did not want to pay the additional tax.
He said the advantage of the PID was the costs to develop the infrastructure in the area would
be available up front.

Councilmember Shields noted the PID was neither an incentive or disincentive, but merely a
method used to develop infrastructure. He said the need was to prioritize infrastructure,
since it would not be sufficient to fund all of the infrastructure. Councilmember Henderson
added details could be outlined in the governing documents. Mayor Palmer relayed his
support to have PIDs in all four areas. Councilmember Ohrn requested more time to
thoroughly read through the documents before rendering a decision. City Manager Cherpeski
said he would add a section to the documents which went over prioritization and would send
the documents out to the Council for review prior to consideration.

Councilmember Henderson pointed out impact fees could not be collected in the PID areas
and questioned how the rest of the money for infrastructure would be raised. City Manager
Cherpeski clarified impact fees could still be collected for infrastructure not covered by the
PID. He explained the rule was to not “double dip” and use impact fees to fund developments
covered by the PID. However, impact fees could be used in some cases, and offered an
example if a park was added to the area, impact fees could be utilized to help cover those
improvements. City Manager Cherpeski clarified about 80-percent of the infrastructure
developments could be funded by the PID, and the remainder of the infrastructure would
have to be funded by the developer.

Mayor Palmer moved the conversation on permitted uses to determine what to allow. He
suggested discussing the area north of Miller Crossing first, continuing on with the crescent
piece and then moving to the garden plots. Director Thomas noted some exceptions in the
MDA. He said larger developments typically required a central focal point, such as a park or
a playground, and noted this requirement was waived. It was also highlighted bike parking
and trails adjacent to the neighborhood would not be required. Stone architectural elements
and brick would not be required either. Director Tomas read the permitted uses, which
included office professional, restaurant, vehicle storage yard, equipment rental, wireless
telecommunications facilities, flex space, office warehouse, and wholesale. He asked if there
were any concerns with the outlined permitted uses. Councilmember Shields asked for a
definition of “vehicle storage yards,” and it was explained this referred to the storage space
dealerships needed to hold inventory. Councilmember Shields clarified this did not refer to
a vehicle repair yard or service center, and he thought it made more sense to make this an
accessory use to a dealership, rather than a standalone use. Director Thomas confirmed. The
Council also discussed “equipment rental” use, and agreed to permit a rental business, such
as U-Haul in the area, if there was a commercial sales tax. It was confirmed agencies such
as U-Haul and other truck and car rental companies were taxable. Developer Mylar
commented it would not be difficult to tweak the language in the document.

Councilmember Ohrn confirmed “wireless telecommunication facilities” referred to the
actual tower, and not a brick-and-mortar store for a carrier such as Verizon. The Council
discussed there were ways to camouflage cell towers to make them more aesthetically
pleasing, and Developer Mylar clarified no one had expressed interest in installing a tower.

Councilmember Henderson clarified the permitted uses outlined in the MDA circumvented
zoning, and the definitions needed to be tied down to ensure clarity in permitted uses.
Currently the area had a C-2 zoning designation; however, governing documents would
supersede the designated zone. He expressed the importance to have the definitions of
permitted uses clarified.

Mayor Palmer moved on to the garden plot. Developer Mylar observed the removal of some
uses in the C-2 zone as requested by Council and added hotel as a permitted use. He felt it
was a zoning agreement which would work for both parties. Required standards had been
outlined for the hotel in the zone. City Manager Cherpeski offered his assistance to the
developer in coming up with incentives to bring a hotel to the City. Developer Mylar further
stated the need to have an overflow space in the crescent piece in case a tenant desired a
showroom. Councilmember Henderson said he was not opposed to a service center or
showroom. Developer Mylar noted there were no candidates for a hotel but thought the use
would be great for the tax base of the City. He stated there would not be homes or apartments
permitted in the zone. Councilmember Hodges asked how tall a hotel building could be, and
Council discussed a limit of 45-feet did not seem sufficient and recommended 60-feet.
Developer Mylar commented on how hotels in downtown Salt Lake City were zoned and said
it would be easy to clone the language in Salt Lake City’s Code to apply it for their own uses.

      He thought it would be best to go with a 65-foot height limit for hotels. He said he could work
      with Community Development Director Thomas to draft the code.

      City Manager Cherpeski stated private garages were explicitly prohibited in Herriman’s Code
      but were listed as a use in the garden plot MDA. Developer Mylar replied he would take it
      out of the document. He added he would also remove language which permitted climate-
      controlled storage units. Councilmember Ohrn noted private garages were also permitted in
      crescent and clarified this was only for RV and boat companies to store inventory, but not
      for private citizens to store recreational vehicles. The Council determined the language
      would need to be modified for clarity. Director Thomas explained the RV and boat company
      wanted to store boats for people after they had purchased them, since many people did not
      have storage space for a boat, so they would need to be careful with the language to ensure
      the use remained legal, while still not allowing private storage spaces.

      City Manager Cherpeski said he would send a strikethrough document to the Council, which
      would give a side-by-side comparison of the old and new versions of the MDA and highlight
      all changes. City Planner Michael Maloy said there was a proposed public hearing on August
      18th for the Planning Commission and asked if it should still move forward. Council consensus
      determined to move forward with the public hearing.

      3. Adjournment
 Councilmember Ohrn moved to adjourn the Herriman City Council meeting at 8:09 p.m. Councilmember Henderson seconded
 the motion, and all voted aye.
