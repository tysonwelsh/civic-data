    4. CONTRACT WITH THE BLUE STAKES OF UTAH TO ALLOW WEBER COUNTY SURVEYOR’S OFFICE TO
       BECOME AN ASSOCIATE MEMBER OF BLUE STAKES.

         This item allows the County Surveyor’s Office to receive and monitor Blue Stake ticket notifications that
         come into conflict with the county’s survey monuments.                Devron Andersen, of the County
         Recorder/Surveyor’s Office, noted that over the past 150 years the Surveyor’s Office has taken care of
         monuments, totally relying on employees and relationships with surveyors at large to monitor those
         monuments. Staff has done all they can to find out when there are construction activities and those that
         would disturb monuments. But over the past 30 or so years it has been difficult to keep up with those
         activities. More than half of the monuments are missing in some county areas. Previously, Blue Stakes had
         not been able to specialize the county’s map for the circumstances that it needed. They have now allowed
         the county to customize its map. Blue Stakes retroactively looked at the 2018 numbers and based on the
         map that the county provided, they said that if the county had been an active member in 2018 it would have
         received 4,010 tickets, which means that many times there was construction close enough that could have
         damaged/destroyed the county’s monuments. Only 8 permits were submitted last year for 8 monument
         locations in the entire Weber County, that is not even ¼ of 1% of all construction activities and has been an
         extremely inadequate process. He asked for the commissioners’ support of SB 104 that would allow county
         surveyors to require permits even for the instances within municipal boundaries.
         Commissioner Froerer moved to approve the contract with Blue Stakes of Utah to allow the Weber County
         Surveyor’s Office to become an Associate Member of Blue Stakes; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Froerer – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye

    2. PUBLIC HEARING FOR CONSIDERATION AND/OR ACTION ON PROPOSED ORDINANCE TO ALLOW LARGE SOLAR
       ENERGY FARMS IN THE A-3 ZONE, TO CREATE A SOLAR ENERGY OVERLAY ZONE (SOZ), TO MODIFY SOLAR
       ENERGY REGULATIONS IN THE M-3 ZONE, & TO CREATE STANDARDS A PROCESSES GOVERNING THE SAME.

         Charles Ewert, of the County Planning Division, stated that an application was received to create a zoning
         tool to allow the creation of large scale solar farms on the western side of unincorporated Weber County in
         the A-3 Zone, where the applicant has its sights on a particular piece of property and which currently does
         not allow solar energy farms. They are allowed, however, in the M-3 Zone by conditional use permit. With
         the Solar Energy System Overlay Zone (SOZ), the County Commission will have full authority to
         approve/deny an overlay. This proposal changes the code to allow this new SOZ to be put in place only in
         the A-3 or M-3 Zones at the discretion of the County Commission after a recommendation from the Planning
         Commission. The Planning Commission felt that this is a better option rather than just allowing it as a
         conditional use permit in the A-3 Zone because there may be areas where it would not be appropriate and
         detrimental effects may not be mitigatable. He noted that one thing that is specifically prohibited in the SOZ
         is the use of reflective devices. The Planning Commission and the County Planning Division recommended
         approval of this item. He addressed the commissioners’ questions including that the minimum contiguous
         site area shall be 100 acres, and that this ordinance sets up the ability for solar energy systems to be an
         allowed use with the overlay zone, then the overlay zone can be applied to a piece of property, which will
         enable the use as a permitted use on that property.
    3. Public Comments:
       --Ada Almanza, of Ogden, supports solar energy. She was referred to speak with the Chief Deputy of the
       County Clerk’s Office after this meeting regarding translation of voter registration forms into Spanish.
       --Doug Larsen, representing the applicant, Strata Solar Corporation of North Carolina said that Strata is one
       of the top five solar farm producers and leads the country in solar projects installed. He is excited that the
       county is embracing renewable energy projects and being a leader. The original agreement on the subject
       land would have consumed almost 300 acres to produce 36 megawatts of power but recent technology has
       narrowed it down to about 230 acres. Strata recommends defining a utility scale solar farm as a solar project
       that consumes at least 100 acres and produces at least 10 megawatts of power. They hope to start construction
       in 2020 and finish by the end of that year.
