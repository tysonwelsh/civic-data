            AN ORDINANCE OF THE COPPERTON METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL
                        ADOPTING THE 2021 FEE SCHEDULE

                                          RECITALS

       WHEREAS, Utah Code §10-2a-414(3) provides that a Salt Lake County ordinance will
remain in effect as an ordinance of the Copperton Metro Township (“Copperton”) “until the metro
township council amends or repeals the ordinance;” and

      WHEREAS, Copperton contracts with the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District
(“MSD”) to provide planning and development services; and

       WHEREAS, in addition to Copperton, the MSD provides planning and development
services to the metro townships of Copperton, Emigration Canyon, Magna, and White City as well
as unincorporated Salt Lake County; and

       WHEREAS, Section 3.42.040(A)(1) of the Copperton Metro Code stated that all “fees to
be charged by [a Copperton] office or agency shall be proposed by the agency each year as part
ofthe annual budget processes;” and

      WHEREAS, Section 3.42.040(A)(3) states that “[u]pon review and approval of fees by
the…council, the fee schedule shall be placed on file with the…clerk;” and

       WHEREAS, the MSD board approved the attached 2021 fee schedule for the planning and
development services the MSD provides; and

        WHEREAS, the Council desires to adopt the attached 2021 fee schedule, which accounts
for the requirements of Chapter 5.02 of the Copperton Code, which does not charge a fee for
business license applications.

   NOW, THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED BY THE COPPERTON METRO TOWNSHIP
COUNCIL that effectively immediately:

      1.     The attached 2021 fee schedule is adopted pursuant to Section 3.42.040 of the
Copperton Metro Township Code; and

       2.    Staff are instructed to file this resolution and the attached fee schedule with the
Copperton Metro Township Clerk; and

       3.     Staff are instructed to post this resolution and the attached 2021 fee schedule to
the Copperton Metro Township website and to the “Municipal Resolutions Book” within the
Copperton’ Municode page.

Amazon Letter Concerning Utility Pole

                Council Member Stitzer stated she has expressed her concern about the utility
pole at the intersection near the Amazon facility. She sent a draft letter to Mayor Clayton and
Nathan Bracken. Nathan Bracken also visited the site and was able to add some pictures. The
letter then went to all Council Members for review. If approved, Mayor Clayton will sign the letter
and Mr. Bracken will send it to both South Jordan and West Jordan.

                Nathan Bracken stated there is also a second pole at the site to the west. They
take up all the median. There is no way a pedestrian or bicyclist can walk by the pole without
going into the road.

He noted his law firm works for South Jordan City and he does water work for them. He and his
partner have never worked on anything like this.

              Council Member Stitzer stated they have not had communication with West
Jordan or South Jordan about this situation. The purpose of the letter is to express the Council’s
concerns, see if the city has plans to revive that area, and work with them to get this resolved.

               Council Member Olsen, seconded by Council Member Bailey, moved to approve
the letter. The motion passed unanimously.

               Council Member Stitzer asked that the letter be amended to include emergency
vehicles.

               Mr. Bracken amended the letter.

               Council Member Bailey, seconded by Council Member Sorensen, moved to repeal
the last motion, and approve the amended letter. The motion passed unanimously.

Legislative Response Resolution

               Nathan Bracken proposed a resolution that would allow him and Mayor Clayton
to respond to legislation during the current state legislative session. He and Mayor Clayton are
occasionally asked to respond to legislation with very little turn-around time and before he can
get the Council’s position. He gave examples of this problem.

Overall, he is looking for a process to guide his input to the legislature. He feels Copperton would
oppose any bill that diminishes local control or that threatens public safety.

              Council Member Stitzer stated Copperton is a unique community whereby the
Mayor does not have the same authority as that position holds in other municipalities. They have
a diverse Council, and she is not comfortable with others making decisions for them.

               Mr. Bracken reviewed the following bills to get the Council’s position:

SB 58 Metro Township Amendments

               Mr. Bracken stated the Council has already agreed to support this bill.

HB 244 First Class County Highway Road Fund Amendments

               Mr. Bracken stated this bill would allocate road funds to municipalities in Salt Lake
County. He prefers that the metro townships not be treated differently. However, he was told the
bill cannot be changed very much because it would cause a lot of rigmarole. He suggested
Copperton let the bill ride.

               Council Members agreed.

HB 98 Local Government Building Regulation Amendments

              Mr. Bracken stated there is talk of making a legal challenge to this bill. Copperton
would not be impacted very much but he worries about the precedent of someone other than the
government issuing occupancy permits for homes. He would like to work with the MSD staff to
make any changes they can.

               Council Member Bailey stated the UFA is completely on board with shutting down
this bill.

                Mr. Bracken stated the legislators pushing this bill are the leaders of both houses
of the legislature.

                                                 5
DATE            WEDNESDAY                             FEBRUARY                            17, 2021


             Council Member Stitzer stated she strongly opposes this bill. She prefers the
community having more control than the developer.

            Council Member Stitzer, seconded by Council Member Olsen, moved to oppose
HB 98. The motion passed unanimously.

              Mr. Bracken stated the League of Cities and Towns will likely be neutral on this
because they want to spend their political capital on other upcoming bills. He asked if someone
asked him or Mayor Clayton to sign a letter to oppose this bill, would the Council allow them to do
that.

               Council Members agreed.

SB 61 Outdoor Advertising Amendments
SB 144 Billboard Restrictions Amendments

               Mr. Bracken stated these are both billboard bills.

               Council Member Stitzer stated she is neutral on it because it has little impact on
Copperton.

               Mr. Bracken recommended the Council monitor the bills with concerns.

               Council Members agreed.

HB 82 Single Family Housing Modifications

                Mr. Bracken stated the is the alternative dwelling units bill. The League has
worked to make it more tolerable. It now allows municipalities to regulate these dwellings
regarding fire, health and building codes. In Copperton, it will allow people to build basement
apartments.

                Council Member Stitzer stated she is okay with the bill if the dwelling units follow
the usual city laws.

               Council Members agreed they had no objection to the bill.

             Mr. Bracken stated he will make no objection to the bill and make sure the
necessary changes are included in the bill.

Future Guidance

               Council Member Stitzer stated WFWRD reappointed their board chair.
