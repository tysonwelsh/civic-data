 1   The Lease Adjustment will not be created yet but is required for bond issuance to ensure that the lease
 2   payments are covered by the City's General Fund and Water Fund.
 3
 4   Matt Dugdale from Stifel, Nicolaus & Company was asked to describe the bond process. He thanked
 5   Mayor Hall and the City Council for going through the additional steps that were made necessary by
 6   recent legislative changes. He noted that, with a Lease Revenue Bond, the City creates collateral for
 7   investors. The City Council is limited in what it can bind future Councils to. With General Obligation
 8   Bonds, the City Council agrees that taxes will be levied to pay the debt service for the term of the
 9   bond. With Lease Revenue Bonds, the City Council commits to appropriating funds each year for a
10   lease payment to the LBA to make payment on the bonds. Collateralizing the assets, in this case, City
11   Hall and the Public Works facility, helps investors get comfortable with that format.
12
13   With regard to the process, an additional resolution for $16 million is required. A calendar has been
14   established and additional members will be selected for the finance team. Dates are set to sell the
15   bonds to the public, and they expect the bonds to close by mid-August. The current interest rate
16   estimate is approximately 4%. The City has an excellent bond rating, so the lease revenue bonds will
17   likely come out with an Aa2 rating.
18
19   Mayor Hall thanked Mr. Dugdale for attending. Council Member Lord reported that he read through
20   the agreements and was glad they know what they are doing because a lot of work goes into ensuring
21   compliance. Mr. Dugdale agreed but noted that the State's statute for Lease Revenue Bonds is solid
22   and investors are aware of that. City Staff has spent a lot of time understanding the process and the
23   new requirements. He also noted that Gilmore Bell, a highly qualified bond counsel firm, drafted the
24   documents.

                                RESOLUTION NO. 2024-57

 A RESOLUTION APPROVING A REDUCTION OF THE KROGER TRANSPORTATION IMPACT
    FEE AND AUTHORIZING THE EXECUTION OF AN AGREEMENT MEMORIALIZING THE
                       UNDERSTANDING OF THE PARTIES

WHEREAS, on August 29, 2017, the City of Bluffdale (“City”) and Bluffdale Marketplace, LC
(“Woodbury”) entered into a Roadway Agreement (“Reimbursement Agreement”) related to
the development of certain real property in the City of Bluffdale near Bangerter Highway and
Redwood Road (“Property”); and

WHEREAS, under the terms of the agreement Woodbury agreed to construct certain
identified road improvements (14000 South) and the City agreed to reimburse Woodbury
through impact fees;

WHEREAS, Woodbury constructed the 14000 South road improvements and the City has been
remitting transportation impact fees collected within The Ranch development (“project”) to
Woodbury to reimburse for the improvements;

WHEREAS, The Kroger Co., an Ohio corporation, (“Kroger”) now desires to develop a part of
the Property for commercial purposes and has proposed a 100,252 square foot grocery store;

WHEREAS, Kroger is required to pay transportation impact fees related to the development
of part of the Property;

WHEREAS, on March 29, 2023, the City provided Kroger with an estimate of the anticipated
impact fees for its proposed development;

WHEREAS, the March 29, 2023, estimate included a transportation impact fee of $509,392.80
for Kroger’s proposed development;

WHEREAS, on June 28, 2023 the City adopted an amended its Impact Fees Facility Plan
(“IFFP”) as Ordinance 2023-13;

WHEREAS, the estimated transportation impact fee owed by Kroger under the new IFFP has
increased to $1,098,876.74;

WHEREAS, the City is willing to reduce the transportation impact fee owed to the City by
Kroger to $849,000 due to the unusual circumstances and lengthy time frame involved with
the City’s grocery store recruitment efforts;
WHEREAS, Woodbury agrees to this impact fee reduction and acknowledges and
understands that this reduction may result in a delay of reimbursement contained in the
Reimbursement Agreement and that the reduction may result in an overall reduction in the
reimbursement depending on the impact fees subsequently collected by the City within the
remainder of the project.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE BLUFFDALE CITY COUNCIL AS FOLLOWS:

       Section 1. Approval of Reduction of Transportation Impact Fee for Kroger. The
City agrees to reduce the transportation impact fee for Kroger to $849,000 with the
understanding that Woodbury has approved of this action.

       Section 2. Authorization to Execute Agreement. The City Council authorizes the
City Manager to enter into an agreement memorializing the Kroger impact fee reduction on
terms to be negotiated by and agreed upon by the parties to the agreement. A draft
agreement is attached hereto as Exhibit A, but it is understood that this should not be
considered the final agreement.

      Section 3. Effective Date. This resolution shall be effective immediately upon
passage.

PASSED, ADOPTED AND APPROVED: July 16, 2024.

RE:    Smith’s #212 Grocery Store – Impact Fee City Participation Request


To the Honorable Mayor and City Council,

During our due diligence period prior to purchasing the property, Smith’s development team
began due diligence research to establish budget funding for our proposed 100,252 square foot
grocery store. City staff was gracious enough to generate a cost estimate for anticipated impact
fees on March 29, 2023 which resulted in the following budget planning:
       Culinary Water (3” Meter)                        $67,268.00
       Storm Drain                                      $63,982.50
       Parks & Recreation                               $0
       Public Safety                                    $4,571.00
       Transportation                                   $509,392.801
               2023 Total Estimated:                    $645,214.30
       1) Transportation Impact Fees estimated were reduced 40% per Resolution 2021-31.
In August of 2023, Smith’s application for site plan approval was accepted by the City of
Bluffdale staff which commenced our first step in the entitlement process to permit planned
construction. In January 2024, while updating transportation trip generation data, Smith’s
became aware of the financial impacts resulting from the City’s adoption of the Impact Fee
Facility Plan Impact Fee Analysis in June of 2023. The resulting impact fees after the change are
now estimated as follows:

       Culinary Water (2” Meter)                        $33,472.00
       Storm Drain                                  $79,798.47
       ((372,761 sf impervious area / 2,700 sf impervious area per ERU)*$578 per ERU)
       Parks & Recreation                               $0
       Public Safety                               $ 50,772.07
       ((101,252 sf building / 9,000 Commercial sf per ERU)*$4,558 per ERU)
       Transportation                                   $1,098,876.74
       (2,169 daily trips*$405.34 per daily trip)
               2024 Total Estimated:                  $1,262,919.28

This has resulted in Smith’s Food & Drug Center, Inc. experiencing a budget shortage with
respect to the payment of City impact fees in the amount of $617,704.98.
As you are aware, the development of this property will increase both sales and property tax
revenue within the community. We respectfully request the City Council consider participation
by paying a portion of additional impact fees now resulting. The opportunity to strategically
partner with the City in this regard is the intent of our writing.
The shortage appears to be directly related to changes to impact fees adopted by the City Council
in June 2023. One element in the realized transportation fee increase is the change in the type of
transportation trip generation data used to determine the fee. Changing the basis of the impact
fee from PM Entering Trips being estimated as 126 trips to Average Daily Trips being estimated
as 2,169 trips has a significant financial impact for commercial development. While the fee per
trip was reduced overall, the significance of the difference in the vehicle trip number utilized for
the calculation results in a significantly higher assessed value. The second, more obvious,
change is attributed to the City Council repealing Resolution 2021-31 eliminating the 40%
transportation impact fee reduction previously provided.
We appreciate your consideration regarding this participation request. If there are any questions
or needed clarifications, please do not hesitate to call 206-516-9075 or email
mitch.mauer@sfdc.com.
