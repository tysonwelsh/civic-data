 97   3.3) Public Hearing and Recommendation of AWA’s Request for Review of a Development
 98   Code Amendment to Chapter 37, Adding an Exception to the Connectivity Standards for
 99   Vehicular Safety.
100
101   Jacob Curtis presented the item. The amendment would allow an exception to the connectivity
102   standards between residential and non-residential zones if it creates an incompatible traffic
103   pattern, such as a loading dock or a drive aisle. The amendment would still require bike and
104   pedestrian connections.
105
106   Commissioner Lockhart asked if the applicant had any other options. Kim Struthers said they
107   would need to find a different site, as there is currently no way to apply for an exception. Jacob
108   Curtis clarified that if this amendment is made, the applicant will still need to apply for the
109   exception. Kim said that there will likely be similar situations that would use the amendment in
110   the future. He also clarified for Commissioners Lockhart and Everett that DRC wants to keep the
111   amendment as restricted as possible to ensure as many connections as possible.
112
113   Rick Magnus was present on behalf of the applicant, AWA Engineering. He stated that the goal
114   of proposing the amendment was to allow a grocery store to be built on a specific lot. The
115   company was unable to find a way to situate the store differently on the lot so that it complied
116   with the current code.
117
118   Commissioner Eyre brought up an example of a similar situation in Pleasant Grove. He asked if
119   any exceptions could be made for grading purposes. Kim explained that the grading of the lot
120   isn’t steep enough to qualify.
121
122   Acting Chair Everett opened and closed public comment.
123
124   Acting Chair Everett stated that he would like to keep the exception narrow, to avoid mass
125   exceptions. He also stated that he would like to remove the word “however” from the
126   amendment. He said that he does not like changing code for one instance, and that a larger
127   overwrite of the clause in the future may need to occur. Commissioner Eyre stated that he would
128   be uncomfortable if the clause allowed the removal of all connectivity, but as it stands, he has no
129   issue with the amendment. Commissioner Lockhart mentioned that she would like to see it more
130   open ended in the future.
131
132                   Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to offer a positive recommendation to the
133                   City Council with the findings that the proposed development code amendment is
134                   in conformance with the purposes, intent, and provisions of the general plan and
135                   its various elements. He included all DRC comments. Commissioner Roberts
136                   seconded.
137
138                   Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner Lockhart, no. Commissioner
139                   Eyre, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes. Commissioner Roberts, yes. Motion passed
140                   4-1.

146
147   Jacob Curtis presented the item. The applicant is requesting a setback longer than the allotted 50

181   3.5) Public Hearing and Consideration of Dot Architecture’s Request for Approval of the
182   Valley Vet Clinic Concept Plan Located at 3055 North 1200 West in the Neighborhood
183   Commercial Zone with an Exception to Have the Door Face Away From 1200 West.
184
185   Jacob Curtis presented the item. He explained that the main concern was over animal safety. A
186   door facing the street would allow animals to run out into 1200 West, which is a major safety
187   hazard for pets, pet owners, and drivers.
188
      Lehi City Planning Commission                    4                                November 14, 2024
189   The applicants, Alex Park and John Lee, were present. Alex currently owns Valley Vet in
190   Draper, and John is with Dot Architecture. Alex clarified that an animal escaping is not a matter
191   of if, but a matter of when. He wants to minimize the safety risks for all parties involved, by
192   funneling escaped animals into a fenced area with few or no moving vehicles. Acting Chair
193   Everett asked if a vestibule would work. John explained that with how the setbacks work
194   currently on the lot, the addition of a vestibule would result in the loss of multiple parking spaces
195   and/or exam rooms. There would also be a potential traffic jam problem if the door is placed
196   closer to the street. Commissioner Eyre asked about pedestrian access and the egress door facing
197   the street. The applicants explained that the egress door is primarily there for fire escape
198   purposes. A more pedestrian-friendly walk-up is not possible if they want to maintain the design
199   that funnels and confines escaped pets. Commissioner Eyre also asked if it was possible to make
200   this use specific. Kim Struthers let him know that it isn’t. Everett asked for a clarification of the
201   services that would be provided. The applicant explained that it will solely be used for veterinary
202   services. Occasionally a patient may need to stay overnight, but it will not be used for kenneling.
203
204   Acting Chair Everett opened public hearing.
205
206   Troy Sheffield lives directly east of the property. He is concerned about the height of the
207   building. He is also concerned about the height of the fence based on the different grading
208   between the two properties. He is also concerned about the potential of euthanasia and cremation
209   happening near his home, and the potential smells that would cause. His biggest concern is about
210   parking lot lighting and lighting on any potential signs facing his home.
211
212   Tyler Andersen lives down the street to the east from the site. He is also concerned about
213   fencing. He is worried about noise from dogs staying overnight. He wants to know if the
214   proposed dog run counts as part of the landscaping buffer required by code.
215
216   Shane and Jenna Rowley commented online. They are concerned about signage, minimizing
217   lighting, and ensuring that the landscape buffer is in place. They clarified that they see both
218   positives and negatives about the project.
219
220   Acting Chair Everett closed public hearing.
221
222   Acting Chair Everett asked staff what the proposed dog run would be classified as. Kim told him
223   that since the space is being utilized as part of the business, it would be counted as part of the
224   clinic. The applicants clarified that the dog run would primarily be used to allow pets to relieve
225   themselves before appointments or procedures and would be cleaned up after every usage. They
226   also stated that they would have more specific answers to all questions in later presentations, but
227   that they would do their best to answer them now. They intend to provide their own fencing.
228   They are very open to public suggestions about lighting. They only intend to have lighted signs
229   on the west side of the building facing the street. While euthanasia will happen on site, cremation
230   is taken care of by a third party. Euthanized animals will be picked up from a discreet, fenced
231   off, outdoor freezer two or three times a week. They also clarified that the building is entirely
232   soundproofed, and any areas where pets would be staying overnight are in the center of the
233   building where they will benefit from extra layers of soundproofing.
234
235   Staff were able to answer other questions. Jacob explained that the height of the building is 31
236   feet, four feet below the maximum allowed height. As it stands, the building would need an
