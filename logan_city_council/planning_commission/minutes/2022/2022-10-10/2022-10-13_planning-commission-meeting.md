   ▪   PC 22-055 William A Burnard Warming Center [Conditional Use Permit] Nicole
       Burnard/Corp of Episcopal Church in Utah, Jason Samuel, authorized agent/owner are
       requesting a Conditional Use Permit to operate a warming center during the winter months
       to serve any person in need of temporary overnight refuge from freezing temperatures,
       located at 85 East 100 N in the Town Center One (TC-1) zone; TIN 06-018-0037 (Adams
       Neighborhood).

       STAFF: Tanya Rice, City Planner reviewed the request for a Conditional Use Permit to use
       a portion of the St. John’s Episcopal Church for a Warming Center that will provide temporary
       overnight shelter from 7:00 PM to 8:00 AM for anyone in need of refuge during the winter
       months. St. Johns Episcopal Church is located at 85 East 100 North in the Town Center
       Zone. The Land Development Code (LDC) allows uses of a charitable nature serving people


       of the community in the TC-1 zoning district as a conditional use. T. Rice reviewed the
       neighboring businesses and uses adjacent to the proposed project, and presented the
       Warming Center’s floor plan, located in the north portion of the church, proposed to include
       a check-in area, kitchen, bathrooms, nursery, outdoor patio area, a grass area for pets, and
       the main resting area that includes 15 cots. The applicant hopes to increase up to 25 cots in
       the future.

       The Planning Commission will need to review the impact of the Warming Center on the
       surrounding community which may include compatibility, parking, and access. The impact of
       the Warming Center will be minimal if managed as proposed. The Warming Center Board
       Members are in the process of establishing policies and procedures to govern the Center and
       prevent negative impacts on the community. The applicant has provided a Guest Occupancy
       Agreement written by the Warming Center Board Members to be signed by each guest upon
       arrival at the Center. This agreement addresses consideration for the surrounding
       community, property, guests, and on-site expectations. This agreement pertains to
       neighborhood compatibility, loitering around the building or parking areas before or after
       hours, no leaving the Warming Center after checking in, and parking stalls must be vacated
       by 8 AM. Warming Center guest check-in (7-10 PM) occurs after most businesses are closed
       and check out (by 8:00 AM) occurs before most of the businesses reopen. Quiet hours are
       designated between 10 PM - 6:30 AM. Per the applicant, there will be at least two (2) on-site
       volunteers and staff members during open hours to provide support and active supervision.

       The LDC does not identify a specific parking requirement for a temporary overnight charitable
       cause. Similar overnight uses in the LDC require one stall per 2-4 people. The applicant is
       uncertain of the exact parking need. The church has three parking stalls and there have
       been 10 additional parking stalls made available by Engle & Volkers at 112 East 100 North,
       which are located approximately 300 feet from the church.

       PROPONENT: Nicole Burnard, Founder, and Chairperson for the Warming Center said that
       this project started after completing the homeless street outreach with Bear River Association
       of Governments (BRAG) during the winter of 2022, where within the first two hours, they
       found eight people living in vehicles. The warming center is an emergency crisis response
       for those who are not able to get housing assistance to get out of the cold. The center will
       offer basic shelter to keep people safe where their basic needs can be met. Safety is the
       number one priority, and this space will allow constant monitoring. One volunteer will be
       stationed at the entrance table, where they will be able to see outside the entrance, into the
       nursery, into the big room with the cots, and anyone going in and out of the bathrooms. The
       second volunteer will be stationed in the big room by the exit to the patio and pet area. This
       plan has been closely modeled after a similar plan done in a town in Montana, whose center
       started in the basement of a church four years ago and now has its own space. They are
       willing to respond immediately if any issues arise, and work with the community to make sure
       that safety remains the priority. The Warming Center has a board of directors as well as an
       advisory board consisting of human service agency leaders, faith leaders, and community
       advocates who have experienced homelessness. The board will meet quarterly and can be
       consulted on any needs that come up with the Warming Center. They will also be conducting
       volunteer training.

       PUBLIC: Herm Olsen, said he and his wife Norma spent a tenure as co-chairs of the BRAG
       homeless committee. During that course, they discovered that homelessness is a complex
       issue that embraces mental health, PTSD, abuse, and multiple other factors. People need a
       place to go in the cold winter months and this is the best idea that has been presented in a
       long time.


       Dr. Gail B. Yost said she volunteers on the homeless council and talked about when she
       worked for BRAG in the 80s and the study on homelessness that was conducted.
       Homelessness is an issue in our community, and this is the first step in helping.

       Jo Leary talked about her uncle that was homeless in cache valley for over a decade, who
       often survived by staying near businesses where he could feel the warmth coming from
       inside.

       Helen Hillegass a member at St. Johns Church asked the committee to approve this warming
       center. Homelessness happens to good people, people that work hard. She spoke about
       her experience living in her car to stay in school. Having the warming center would have
       been a god sent to her at that time.

       Krystina James serves on the Warming Centers board of directors spoke about her
       experience with homelessness with her three children. The Warming Center is an absolute
       need. A Warming Center would have made her situation much better.

       Cecile Gilmer member of St. Johns Church and on the board for Family Place Board said
       that this will benefits people that need somewhere to go in the middle of the night when they
       need to escape a situation before it gets to a critical point.

       Jane Nicholson asked that the committee be realistic with the possible issues that could come
       up.

       Jimmy Burbon, United Way of Cache Valley Director spoke about messages he received
       from homeless individuals in the community needing help. This service will add value to our
       community and help those in need.

       COMMISSION: Commissioner Croshaw asked about condition number 10 and if the fire
       department has completed their inspection to determine the max occupancy. T. Rice said
       that the inspection has been done and the max occupancy is 45.

       Commissioner Lucero asked Tanya to explain the re-evaluation process and timeline. T.
       Rice said that after the Warming Center concludes in the spring, City staff will discuss the
       operations of the Warming Center with the applicant to determine whether there were any
       issues or concerns regarding the operation during the winter of 2022/2023 and whether the
       CUP would need to be remanded back to the Planning Commission for further consideration.

       Commissioner Guth asked if the Conditional Use Permit could be reviewed as needed
       throughout the first year as issues or complaints arise. The Commission discussed whether
       there was a need to remand back to the Commission at any time if problems arose and
       whether the CUP could be brought back.
       Commissioner Lucero asked about the required parking. T. Rice said that there is not a
       parking requirement for this type of use, but staff has estimated that with the proposed 25
       guests, counting two people per car, the proposed 13 parking stalls put the project at 104%
       on parking. As soon as the occupancy requires more parking, the applicant will need to
       submit an alternate parking plan.

       Commissioner Heare asked how parking is supposed to be conditioned when there is not a
       parking requirement established for this use.


       Mohamed Abdullahi, Assistant City Attorney said that the biggest issue seems to be the
       parking. Just like any other land use, the only way the city would know about an over-
       occupancy issue is if there were complaints made about the number of cars. Staff should be
       able to work with the applicant on any parking issues that arise. If there is a substantial
       change in the number of parking stalls needed, greater than 10%, the applicant would then
       come back to the Planning Commission.

       The Commission discussed adding additional parking requirements to the conditions of
       approval, and if the requirement should include an occupancy limit or number of parking stalls
       that would trigger the need for an alternate parking plan.

       It was determined that the conditions should be updated to state that if additional parking is
       needed over the proposed 13 stalls, an alternate parking plan will be submitted to Community
       Development for approval.

       MOTION: Commissioner Heare moved to conditionally approve a Conditional Use Permit
       for PC 22-055 with the amended conditions of approval and findings for approval as listed
       below. Commissioner Peterson seconded the motion.

       CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL
       1. All standard conditions of approval will be recorded with the Conditional Use Permit and
           are available in the Community Development Department.
       2. Warming Center hours of operation are 7:00 PM to 8:00 AM.
       3. Quiet times shall comply with the City noise ordinance of 10 PM to 8 AM
       4. Maximum occupancy for the Warming Center shall comply with fire and building codes.
       5. The Boards Policies and Procedures shall be completed prior to opening.
       6. The Guest Occupancy Agreement shall be available prior to opening the Warming
           Center.
       7. All parking related to the Warming Center including staff, volunteers and guests shall be
           off the street. If occupancy increases beyond the estimated 25 occupants and the 13
           parking stalls, then the applicant shall submit an alternative parking plan to Community
           Development for approval.
       8. A re-evaluation of the operations impacts and conditions shall be made by Community
           Development and the applicant in the spring of 2023. If significant issues have been
           raised with the operational standards, neighborhood impacts, or the applicant desires to
           expand or change the operation, the matter will be remanded back to the Planning
           Commission for further consideration.
       9. No signs are approved with this Conditional Use Permit. All signage shall be approved
           and permitted by staff in accordance with the Land Development Code.
       10. A Logan City building/fire inspection is required to determine maximum occupancy prior
           to operation.
       11. A Logan City business license is required prior to operating.
       12. The Warming Center shall comply with local and state Building, Health, Fire, Safety,
           Maintenance and Nuisance Codes prior to operating.
       13. Prior to issuance of Business License, the Director of Community Development shall
           receive a written memorandum from each of the following departments or agencies
           indicating that their requirements have been satisfied:

              a. Fire —contact 716-9515
                 • This church building is equipped with a fire alarm and fire sprinkler system in
                     the areas of application. This is a change of use. The building department
                     will need to approve the change of use and establish the occupancy


                    limitations, and determine time of day and number of days to allow this
                    temporary use. The historic chapel does not have fire alarm or fire sprinklers
                    and will not be allowed to be used for this type of use.
              b. Engineering —contact 716-9153
              c. Water/Cross Connection—contact 716-9627
                 • Any facility that is used that has Logan City water should have a hazard
                    assessment inspection to help ensure the safety of the water for occupants
                    and city water source and system.
              d. Environmental —contact 716-9761
                 • Applicant can continue existing service.

       FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL
       1. A Warming Center is compatible with surrounding land uses and zoning designations
          and, as conditioned, will not interfere with the use and enjoyment of adjoining properties.
       2. The streets providing access and other infrastructure to the subject property have
          adequate capacities and level of service for the conditional use.
       3. The Conditional Use Permit conforms to the requirements of Title 17 of the Logan
          Municipal Code as an identified conditional use.
       4. The project was noticed as required by State and City regulations and proper procedure
          was followed in processing this request.

   ▪   PC 22-060 Logan City 2022 Moderate Housing Plan [Plan Amendment] Mike
       DeSimone/Logan City are requesting to amend the Logan General Plan to comply with
       UCA 10-9A-401 & 10-9A-403 by adopting the Logan City 2022 Moderate Income Housing
       Plan.

       STAFF: Russ Holley, Senior Planner reviewed the 2022 Draft Moderate Income Housing
       Plan (MIHP). Logan City adopted the current MIHP in August 2019. In 2022 the State
       Legislature passed HB 462 modifying the States moderate-income housing strategies as well
       as providing clearer expectations for local municipalities in meeting the requirement to
       provide for moderate-income housing in their communities. municipalities are required to
       implement strategies from the specific list of 26 strategies, prepare implementation plans for
       the chosen strategies, and report annually on the progress made. Failure to do so results in
       potentially jeopardizing State transportation funding. The City will need to include in the MIHP
       at least 5 strategies from the State’s list to be in “priority consideration” for State
       transportation funding. HB 462 also specifically requires an implementation plan be included
       in the 2022 MIHP identifying a timeline for the implementation of each strategy along with
       specific measures and benchmarks that will be used to determine success.

       R. Holley reviewed three options for the City to amend the current MIHP:
       Option one is to reaffirm three MIH strategies and adopt and implement a plan and timeline
       for each. This meets the base requirement of HB 462.


       Option two is to incorporate at least two strategies beyond the base requirements and adopt
       and implement a timeline for each. This option qualifies us for priority consideration for State
       funding (transportation – TIF, TTIF).
       Option three is to do nothing. This option would disqualify us from transportation funding.

       The role of the Planning Commission tonight is to identify eight strategies. Staff will then
       prepare implementation plans to be reviewed at the 10/27/22 meeting, and ranked in order
       of importance, timing, and feasibility. The Commission will then send a recommendation to
       the Municipal Council.

       R. Holley reviewed the 26 moderate-income housing strategies from UCA 10-9a-403(2)(b)(iii)

       The Commission agreed on the following eight strategies for implementation plans:
          C Demonstrate investment in the rehabilitation of existing uninhabitable housing stock
             into moderate income housing;
          E Create or allow for, and reduce regulations related to, internal or detached accessory
             dwelling units in residential zones;
          F Zone or rezone for higher density or moderate income residential development in
             commercial or mixed-use zones near major transit investment corridors, commercial
             centers, or employment centers;
           I Amend land use regulations to allow for single room occupancy developments;
          J Implement zoning incentives for moderate income units in new developments;
          K Preserve existing and new moderate income housing and subsidized units by utilizing
             a landlord incentive program, providing for deed restricted units through a grant
             program, or notwithstanding Section 10-9a-535, establishing a housing loss
             mitigation fund;
          R Eliminate impact fees for any accessory dwelling unit that is not an internal accessory
             dwelling unit as defined in Section 10-9a-530;
          U Develop a moderate income housing project for residents who are disabled or 55
             years old or older.

       R. Holley stated that at the next Commission hearing on the Moderate Income Housing Plan,
       staff would provide an implementation plan for these eight identified strategies.
