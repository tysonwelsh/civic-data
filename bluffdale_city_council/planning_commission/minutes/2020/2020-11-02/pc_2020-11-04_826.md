4.     PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND RECOMMENDATION on a Zoning
       Map Amendment Application for 2.05 Acres from 1-1 Light Industrial Zone to Heavy
       Commercial Zone (HC) located at Approximately 955 West 14730 South, Sean Shah,
       Applicant. (Application 2020-53). Staff Presenter, Jennifer Robison.

Senior City Planner, Jennifer Robison, presented the staff repo1i and identified the location of the
subject property. It is in an older part of the community, has been considered industrial in nature, and
has not undergone substantial development. The applicant was seeking to have the subject property
rezoned from (I-1) Light Industrial to (HC) Heavy Commercial. Adjacent parcels are currently zoned
HC. The Land Use General Plan shows that a wide variety of businesses are envisioned for the area.
The Zoning Map shows a differentiation of the uses from the Land Use General Plan, including Heavy
Commercial and Light Industrial. The subject property, which is currently zoned 1-1, includes some
I-1 property to the west. Farther west is land that is zoned Mixed Use. There is a substantial amount
of property zoned HC to the north and east. To the south is a new SD-R zone that was created for
Independence Village. The topography creates a buffer between the residential and light
industrial/heavy commercial properties.

The applicants were seeking to have the subject property rezoned to be consistent with the
surrounding Heavy Commercial property. They anticipate potential development of flex office space,
incubator spaces, warehousing, and employment opportunities for businesses in the area. The
difference between the 1-1 and HC zones is not substantial; however, there are enough differences to
warrant the applicants' request to have the property rezoned to HC. The recently approved change in
setback requirements for HC zones has been a factor in the request to have the subject prope1iy
rezoned.

The front setbacks were changed from 30 feet to 15 feet. The side setbacks and the landscaping
requirements remained the same. The proposed zone change meets the General Plan because it allows
for a continuation of the development pattern that has been established with the adjacent HC property.
The proposed rezone would not be harmful to the businesses that currently exist near the subject
property because the uses are similar and compatible with each other. Mrs. Robison indicated that
the subject property is in an area the City Council has been looking at to enhance commercial
development. It was noted that there would be public access to the area. Mrs. Robison indicated that
the HC zone allows for greater flexibility in c01mnercial development than the 1-1 zone, so the zone
change would allow for greater potential development on the property.

The applicant, Sean Shah gave his address as 118 East 12675 South in Draper. He and his team own
other properties in the area and plan to bring great businesses to Bluffdale. If the proposed rezone is
approved, they anticipate bringing in about 40,000 square feet of industrial warehouses and flex office
space. He noted that several businesses are interested in building on the subject property.

In response to a question raised, Mr. Shah stated that the uses will primarily be industrial warehouses
and office space.

7.     Discussion of Home Occupation Text Amendment, Staff Presenter, Todd Sheeran.

Mr. Sheeran reported that staff had been working on text amendments to the Home Occupation
Ordinance. It had been a difficult ordinance to draft because there were many issues with the current
Code. The City does not want to tell people what they can and cannot do, but they also want to protect
the neighbors from undue encroaclunent from the impacts of home occupations. The proposal makes
a distinction between Minor and Major Home Occupations. One of the weaknesses of the existing
Code is the lack of clarity of the definitions for the terms used.

The difference between a Minor Home Occupation and Major Home Occupation is the impact of the
business. A Minor Home Occupation has virtually no visible impact. Another challenge is to
detennine the threshold at which a Major Home Occupation becomes Commercial.

The proposed text amendments establish clear lines so that it is very clear which category of home
occupation the applicant falls into. A Minor Home Occupation would allow six trips per day. Parking
will also have to occur on the property and not on the street with some exceptions. A Minor Home
Occupation does not allow for employees while a Major Home Occupation allows up to one
employee. Clientele using the home occupation has also been addressed. Some home occupations
