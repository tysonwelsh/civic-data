   2.1. Discussion regarding the Planning Commission alternative appointments – Michael Maloy,
   City Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy indicated he spoke with current alternate Planning Commissioner Joy Kaseke, who
notified him she would be moving this summer and would not be able to seek reappointment. He stated this
would mean there are three alternate positions available on Planning Commission. Planning Commissioner
Heather Garcia has currently been serving as an alternate member. Two names have been submitted to be
considered as alternates. The first is Darrel Fenn, an engineer and Forrest Sickles who is an architect. Initially,
Planner Maloy was waiting to see what the state was going to do with design review limitation, and noted the
City could benefit with an architect on Planning Commission. He noted there would be some limitations;
however, there is a negotiated development process where the Planning Commission could participate in design
standards and offer a review.
Councilmember Smith questioned what process was gone through and reviewed for these new candidates and
if there were additional names submitted. Planner Maloy responded additional names were submitted which
could be forwarded to the Council for consideration. Councilmember Smith expressed his desire to see all
names considered along with staff recommendation.
The time was then turned over to Heather Garcia by Councilmember Smith. Planning Commissioner Garcia
indicated there were three alternates during her service the first year, and about nine months into the term of
service, on alternate Commissioner moved leaving a vacancy. She noted frustrations among developers because
one alternate would vote on something one night and another would vote on something the next, this happened
a few times, delaying the processes due to differing opinions.
Councilmember Smith asked if this had unduly relayed the process. Planner Maloy agreed with Commissioner
Garcia on the possibility when an alternate is present and participating one meeting and brings up an issue, then
the next meeting a new alternate is present, new issues could arise. He explained alternates are free to participate
in a work meeting, but they have not allowed alternates to participate in the general meeting unless their regular

   2.2. Herriman Pavement Preservation – Bryce Terry, Staff Engineer
Staff Engineer Bryce Terry introduced a presentation to discuss where roads in the City are located, condition-
wise, according to an updated study and the plan to keep the roads in good condition moving forward. He
explained the presentation will be about condition of the road surface. He then offered an overview of the
presentation, then moved to an illustration showing the amount of pavement/roadway installed for new
development annually.
Engineer Terry explained to get a better understanding of the condition of the road a consulting firm (IMS) was
hired to drive around the city to scan roads and pick up crack information, the raveling or rutting in the road or
even how rough it is. They can also test the road to determine how tough the asphalt currently is. Each roadway
was ranked based off a pavement conditional index grading system for roadways (PCI). Engineer Terry then
presented some illustrations to show road condition based on the rating each received and a map with rating
results. He then went over the Pavement life Cycle showing how roads are designed to last 20-25 years and what
contributed to the asphalt deteriorating. He concluded this is the reason to shift focus to keep roads in best
condition and how it will be a better investment overtime.
Councilmember Smith asked if there was data to show the curve change with appropriate maintenance. Engineer
Terry then offered a visual aid to help understand the life extension due to preventative maintenance.
Public Works Director Justun Edwards offered an overview of the budget allocations. He described how the
City is using more cost-effective treatments. Finance Director Alan Rae clarified the road fund does not cover
the entire road maintenance program. Director Edwards added funds are all being used, but they are not
covering everything needed. He offered an overview of the goals for the road conditions.
Councilmember Clint Smith acknowledged the point of maintaining the roads in good condition and pointed
out roads which needed attention and are constantly getting worse.

3. Budget Report
   3.1. Fiscal Year 2021-2022 Herriman City Budget Review – Alan Rae, Finance Director
   Councilmember Jared Henderson asked for clarification regarding problems outlined in January and where
   they are being addressed in the current budget.

    Finance Director Alan Rae reported meeting with Councilmember Henderson to talk about the problems
    with the budget. He explained the problem was not that there was not enough budget but the planning of
    fiber optic, public safety, public works building, and so forth. Finance Director Rae made a point they could
    operate on the current budget, but not do the additional things being considered. He then stated the average
    coming out of the general fund at the end of each year is 1.8 million dollars. After working with the managers
    on this budget, they came out with $3.4 million. Discussions ensued with manager about these concerns
    and ultimately the excess was allocated into capital projects. Manager had made adjustments in their
    respective budgets to help come up with answers to these concerns. He observed the low balance to cover
    the fire service district for this current budget year as the City would have to cover the cost of the service
May 12, 2021 – City Council Minutes                                                     Page 5 of 10

   until the debt could be repaid by taxes collected in November. Those funds would be replenished later in
   the budget year.

   Councilmember Smith asked of the 3.4 million how much of the increase from this year to next year is
   through an increase in taxes verses cost saving. Finance Director Rae responded it was mostly through
   growth, but in the past the growth was replaced by added expenditures to operations. This budget
   anticipated an added person in the general fund, five police additions, and five water personnel additions.
   He stated the City is growing more with revenue and has managed to maintain the natural growth with
   expenditures.

   Councilmember Smith asked Police Chief Troy Carr for a summary about the plan for the mental health
   need internally and externally. Chief Carr responded they have learned one need would not necessarily fit
   the other. There are many opportunities available for internal mental health individuals. He noted a
   proposed coordinated effort with South Jordan and Riverton in July. Those funds would become available
   by the federal government. The federal government just released the documents for them, and it is moving
   quickly. Chief Carr predicted the City would see amount grow throughout the nation as the federal
   government puts more money into mental health and law enforcement allocations. Chief Carr commented
   further regarding this internal need, stating larger agencies he referred to previously have both caseworkers
   and mental health professionals. He stated the City does need to address the issue of these mental health
   flash point calls. He recognized officers would not go on mental health calls initially; however, the goal
   would be to not have a second call to be able to get people the resources needed. Councilmember Smith
   questioned whether they would hire someone with this skill set or train them. Chief Carr responded they
   would hire someone with this mental health skill set since this is very new for everyone. Councilmember
   Shields inquired if this is anticipated being a joint effort on these flashpoint calls, with law enforcement and
   mental health professionals going together. Chief Carr responded law enforcement or fire would still
   respond to the scene but then could foresees them calling additional resources out.

   3.2. Future Bonding Discussion – Alan Rae, Finance Director
   Finance Director Rae reported during the recent legislative session the legislature promised $800,000 a year
   for 15-years. This could mean the City could bond for $10,500,000 using the allocated $800,000 as payment.
   This provided an opportunity to provide funding for roadway improvements needed.

   He further explained the need to rebuild fire station #103. Finance Director Rae noted different cost
   estimates had been received, and recommended if the Council chose to bond for this purpose it should be
   considered to raise the bond amount off to repay the bond obligation with Unified Fire Service Area
   (UFSA). The current UFSA bond obligation is $5,874,000 over the next 15-years. If the City chose to pursue
   this route, UFSA would have to agree to the repayment. The average payment would be $780,000. This
   bond would be funded by fire service property tax.

   Councilmember Henderson brought up the need for an additional fire station within the next five-years.
   Finance Director Rae added there would be additional operation and maintenance costs to consider. He
   proposed implementing a Municipal Building Authority for the bond security. If property taxes are utilized
   to secure the bond, an election would have to be conducted.

   Director Rae showed some rough numbers for the Public Works Facility Bond to start discussion on the
   topic. He reflected on the last meeting about the discussion regarding police utilizing space in the public
   works building. He indicated the originally proposed building in phase 1 was $24M and noted there was still
   an outstanding balance of $3M on the land. Interest rates and costs for a multi-year bond were highlighted.
   Councilmember Henderson expressed concern with committing millions for 15-years when there is a five-
   year window prior to having additional troubles. He recommended he would feel more comfortable
   reviewing the plan and having additional dialogue when the new City Manager has started.

7. City Council Board and Committee Reports
Councilmember Shields reported on the Mosquito Abatement District. He recounted a discussion relating to
questions raised by physicians regarding safety of the methods utilized by the department. They are primarily
focused on trying to kill mosquitos before they become adults, by spraying ponds and such. He reported they
do not typically do this unless there are cases of disease associated with a particular group of adult mosquitos.
He stated the guidelines followed by the EPA is adhered to by strict rules and it is done in a safe way. He pointed
out this does not happen often and is not a big problem in our community. Councilmember Shields referred
the public to the Mosquito Abatement Website for any further questions on the subject.
Councilmember Shields recounted planning for development being brought up with the Association of
Municipal Councils. Former Director of Salt Lake County Development raised questions about if it were too
early to plan for autonomous vehicles. Councilmember Shields said it was an interesting question as it would
change the way cities were planned. He then gave the example of going to a facility, such as Rio Tinto as there
would be a lot of asphalt for parking around the stadium causing a lot of congestion. This was suggesting, should
it be something that gets developed in the future, there would not be a need for onsite parking. More facilities
would be closer together and use less land. However, there would need to be a place to park those vehicles
when not in use. This would allow for flexibility and change on-street parking.
Councilmember Shields relayed a question from a presentation offered and asked what is the solution to provide
low to moderate income housing. He said the conclusion when he heard this question was there had to be some
way to subsidize or incentivize the cost of housing. He disclosed there was a report conducted by the state
annually regarding affordable housing. The report highlighted two primary elements which were leading to the
lack of inventory from low to moderate housing. The first factor was wages as they have not increased at the
same rate as housing has increased. Secondly, there are 76,000 units which would otherwise be available to low
to moderate income households which are currently occupied by people who can afford more expensive
housing. The reason for them not to purchase more expensive homes is due to the lack of availability which
drives up the price of traditional suburban housing.
Councilmember Shields offered an update on the biweekly Salt Lake County COVID meeting the county hosts.
Recently there has been a slight uptick of cases in the area which could be due to outbreaks in local schools.
The vaccine has now been approved for children ages 12-15. The goal would be to keep kids in school and have
a successful year starting this fall.
