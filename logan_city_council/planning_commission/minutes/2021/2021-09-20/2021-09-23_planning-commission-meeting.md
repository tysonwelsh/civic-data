 PC 21-056 Logan Library [Design Review Permit] Kent Craven/Logan City, authorized
 agent/owner request a design review permit to build a 3 story, 35,000 SF public library
 building and construct new parking area. The project is located at 255 North Main in the
 Town Center One (TC-1) zone; TIN 06-016-0032; -0033. (Ellis Neighborhood)

STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a new 3 story, 35,000 SF public Library building at the
corner of 300 North and Main Street in the TC-1 zone. The building has unique fenestration
patterns that allow for better viewing from inside and out. There are two outdoor terraces, one on
the south side, second floor and one on the north side third floor. The proposed site plan includes
a plaza and a reconfigured parking area that will bring the total number of parking stalls to 254
which is 40 more stalls than there is currently. This building meets the minimum elevation, mass,
and fenestration requirements.

PROPONENT: Kent Craven said they are excited about this design, how it connects main street to
the plaza with the windows letting you see through. The floor plan is very functional and allows
library staff to monitor all entrances to see who is entering and exiting the library.

Commissioner Goodlander asked if the brick is real and if there was a concern with controlling the
temperature inside the building having such large windows. K. Craven said they will be installing
electric floorboards under each window that will help keep the heat in.

Commissioner Guth said that the grey color is very trendy right now and the color chosen should
stand the test of time and fit in with the rest of historic downtown.

Mayor Daines said that they have looked at three colors, red, buff, and grey. All three represent
the area and fit in with historic downtown. More people seem to prefer the gray brick.

PUBLIC: Karen Clark, library director said that they have been working on this plan for the past
year, paying close attention to detail to make sure it is what the patrons want and what works best
for the community.


Commissioner Goodlander asked where the books will be stored during construction. K. Clark said
some of the books we be stored in the City Hall basement and some at the service center. They
will follow the same process as when they were closed due to Covid. They will also be getting
more digital content to offer during this time.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Dickinson said she is still concerned with the parking and children
having to walk through the parking lot to get to the library. R. Holley said this is a smaller scale
parking lot, with the short rows of stalls it does not justify sidewalks within the parking lot. This new
parking configuration improves vehicular circulation.

 PC 21-051 Land Development Code Amendment-Chapter 17.08, 17.11, 17.37, 17.62
 [Code Amendment] Mike DeSimone/Logan City, request to amend chapter 17.08
 “Neighborhood Residential Uses”; Chapter 17.11 “District and Corridor Zone Uses”; Chapter
 17.37 “Additional Development Standards”; and Chapter 17.62 “Definitions” To include
 standards for community agricultural uses, urban agricultural uses, and the keeping of
 domestic fowl, bees, and livestock in urban areas.

STAFF: M. DeSimone reviewed the background and updates of the code amendment on chapter
17.37: Additional Development Standards Domestic Fowl, Beekeeping, Livestock, Urban
Agriculture. M. DeSimone went over the changes in the proposed draft from the previous meeting
based on input from the Planning Commission and residents and went through a comparison of
how other jurisdictions in both Cache Valley and Statewide regulate chickens/fowl.

Currently agriculture uses are only permitted in the NR-2, NR-4, RC, GW, CS, IP, and AP zones.
Agricultural uses are prohibited in the NR-6, MR, and all other commercial zones not listed above.
Due to the amount of inquiries the city receives on raising chickens, the complaints about
neighboring animals (nuisance – odors, noise), and lack of operating standards (numbers,
locations, setbacks, containment), Logan City needs some minimum standards for regulating
animals in residential neighborhoods as well as separating out larger, industrial/commercial
agricultural uses from smaller, residential scaled agricultural uses.

The purpose of this code amendment is to:
   • Establish minimum standards for keeping domestic fowl (chickens), bees, & livestock in
      residential areas

   •   Clarify the distinction in types of “Agriculture” that are appropriate for Logan – community
       gardens, community supported gardens, market gardens, commercial/industrial agriculture

   •   Minimize negative impacts of keeping fowl, bees, & livestock on adjoining properties

   •   Attempt to balance the “rights & desires” of all landowners – those owning animals and
       those living next door

Proposed 17.37.080 – Chickens (updated)
   • Urban Agriculture – food production within an urban area on a scale that is for personal
      use/consumption and compatible with neighborhood

   •   Number of chicken’s correlate to lot size:
         • < 6,000 sf – 5 chickens
         • 6,001 – 10,000 sf – 6 chickens


           •   10,001 – 12,000 sf – 8 chickens
           •   12,001 – 20,000 sf – 10 chickens
           •   20,001 sf + – 15 chickens

   •   Location/Enclosure: fenced side or rear yard with a coop (contained)
   •   Coop Setback: 25’ from dwellings on adjoining lots (odors/noise)
   •   Sanitation: keep sanitary & clean (odors)
   •   No roosters, geese, turkeys, peacocks (noise)
   •   No Outdoor Slaughtering (noise, odors)

Proposed 17.37.090 – Bees (updated)
   • Reached out to Beekeepers who commented on early draft
   • Beekeeping conducted in conjunction with SFR
   • Registration with Utah Dept. of Agriculture & Food
   • Numbers of beehives based on minimum lot size w/caveat
   • Location – fenced side or rear yard w/caveat (fence requirement waived if beehives at least
      50’ from adjoining dwellings)
   • Beehive setback: 25’ from dwellings on adjoining lots
   • Condition & Sanitation Standards
   • Convenient Source of Water
   • Flyaway Barrier – direct bees on flight path & elevation (language from Brigham City’s
      Ordinance)

Proposed 17.37.100 – Miniature Livestock
   • Miniature livestock = small goats, sheep, and pigs
   • Numbers tied to minimum lot size – 10,000 sf minimum
   • Only permitted in a fenced side or rear yard
   • Minimum shelter/pen area per animal
   • Minimum 50’ setback from adjoining dwellings
   • Health and Sanitation Standards – odors, rodents, insects, vermin, pests, diseases, fresh
      water
   • No outdoor slaughtering
   • Only permitted with a detached SFR in the NR-2, NR-4, NR-6, or RC

Proposed 17.37.110 – Livestock
   • Standard sized livestock
   • Numbers of livestock tied to minimum lot size – 20,000 sf minimum
   • Only permitted in a fenced rear yard
   • Minimum 50’ setback from adjoining dwellings
   • Minimum shelter/pen area per animal
   • Health and Sanitation Standards – odors, rodents, insects, vermin, pests, diseases, fresh
      water
   • No outdoor slaughtering
   • Only permitted with a detached SFR in the NR-2, NR-4, NR-6, or RC

M. DeSimone went over the added community agriculture definitions proposed in chapter 17.62
and the proposed added uses to the charts in chapters 17.08 and 17.11.

for 4-H projects. Sheep are not loud, and they are not a nuisance. There are many homes that
have a side yard that is used as a pasture.

K. Housley stated that those properties that have historically been used for agriculture can apply
with the city and would possibly qualify as a legally existing non-conforming use. M. DeSimone
said there is also a right to farm section in the code that states that if there is an existing agriculture
use that is ongoing, it can continue.

Kim Johansen said that there should be evidence and scientific reasoning behind the required
space per animal. How much space does each animal need? She currently owns two miniature
goats that would not be allowed on her .21-acre property. She asked about the slaughtering of
animals being prohibited and what about the processing of wild game.

Jodi Lundburg said that she is concerned with side yard pastures being prohibited. She has a
pasture on the side of her home and would like side yards to be added as a permitted location for
livestock.

Dianna Shaffer spoke about the proposed flyaway barriers on the beehives. Typically, the barriers
are only in front of the hive and extend out to the sides. The bees need airflow to help control the
internal temperature of the hive, so it does not get too hot in the summer, or wet and moldy in the
winter.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Heare suggested changing the wording on the beehive fly away
barrier from surrounding the hive to in front of the hive. The commissioners all agreed on this
change.

Commissioner Goodlander said that there should be specific land size required per animal.

K. Housley said that this is a zoning issue, the planning commission would not determine the
animal’s wellbeing. Zoning is a balance of interests; we are trying to balance this ordinance
between those who love their agricultural animals and those that want to live free from the impact
of those animals. The commissioners will determine the number they believe is the best balance
between all interests.

M. DeSimone said there is a wide range of context in each neighborhood, and not everyone wants
to have agricultural animals living next to them. We are trying to provide a standard that people can
live with.

Commissioner Dickinson asked how many years the animals have had to be on a property to be
determined a legally existing non-conforming use. K. Housley said that animals were not regulated
until 2016-2018, if a residence has had animals established since then and it is an ongoing use
they could be grandfathered.
