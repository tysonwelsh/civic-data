1.         21-321                 USU Utah Wellbeing Project presenting the Sandy Wellbeing Survey 
                                  Findings
                   Attachments:   Link to Sandy Wellbeing Survey Findings
                                  Wellbeing Survey Presentation.pdf

                                  Council Member Houseman introduced  Dr. Courtney Flint with Utah State University who 
                                  provided an overview and highlights from the Wellbeing Survey sent out to residents in 
                                  February 2021. The purpose of the survey was to assess the wellbeing of city residents 
                                  and to provide valuable information to city leaders to help in their general planning 
                                  process. 1,159 resident surveys were recorded. Survey results rated overall personal and 
                                  community wellbeing in Sandy City as above average among the 29 cities participating in 
                                  the survey. Dr. Flint reviewed the highest rated, the most important and the red zone 
                                  domains as well as provided an overview of the demographic characteristics and top 
                                  concerns of respondents. Dr. Flint discussed the importance of building community 
                                  connections which is influenced by  a community's design, activities and animal 
                                  relationships with pets. Overall, the results were very positive and the Council thanked Dr. 
                                  Flint for including Sandy City in the Wellbeing survey.

                                  Council moved to Item 4 on the Agenda: Discussion of new Police Department pay plan 
                                  proposal.

2.         21-320                 Sandy City Fire Department providing the Council with an update on 
                                  fireworks for 2021.
                   Attachments:   2021 Council Firework Memo.pdf
                                  2018 Firework Restrictions Survey (PDF).pdf
                                  2019_Fireworks_Display.pdf
                                  2020_Fireworks_Display.pdf
                                  2021_Fireworks_Display.pdf

                                  Fire Marshall Robert DeKorver provided a summary of the department's  2021 fireworks 
                                  response. He discussed with the Council five areas and provided specific details:
                                     1. Number of firework calls
                                     2. Information, education, and enforcement of fireworks in Sandy
                                     3. Cost of firework code enforcement, information and education.
                                     4. Future of firework legislation
                                     5. Lesson learned from this firework season.

                                  He provided a map detailing the areas where Fire was dispatched to fireworks calls for the 
                                  current year and prior years. He discussed the calls received by neighboring cities and 
                                  also discussed the costs associated with fireworks calls and enforcement, signage, and 
                                  education. Fire Marshall DeKorver encouraged the community to reach out to their state 
                                  legislators to express their concerns with fireworks. He mentioned the need to add an 
                                  area by Alta View hospital to the restrictions map for next year and he discussed the 
                                  need to review the budget to address costs associated with call backs, education and 
                                  signage. He recommended the Council consider changes to the sign ordinance to allow 
                                  for the installation of permanent fireworks restriction signage. He stated that overall, this 
                                  was a successful year for fireworks. A survey will be going out to the residents in the 
                                  upcoming months to gather community feedback.

                                  Council questions and comments followed. Chief Cline and Fire Marshall DeKorver 
                                  responded to Council questions. Council thanked the Fire Department staff.

3.         21-313                 Effects & Options from State Amendments - Internal Accessory Dwelling 
                                  Units (I-ADUs)
                   Attachments:   082421 - Discussion on HB 82

                                  Mike Wilcox with the Community Development Department, presented on the effects and 
                                  options from state amendments related to Internal Accessory Dwelling Units (I-ADU's). 
                                  He reviewed highlights from HB 82 and provided an overview of the requirements imposed 
                                  on municipalities with respect to I-ADU's and addressed the "shall" items and "may" 
                                  items permitted by municipalities under the state legislation. Mr. Wilcox reviewed the 
                                  current city regulations and the proposed changes to the City code and the 
                                  recommendations from staff. Council questions and discussion followed. Council provided 
                                  feedback to staff regarding direction going forward. Staff will prepare a draft ordinance, 
                                  forward to the Planning Commission for review and then bring before the Council for 
                                  consideration and vote. Council must act prior to October 1st or the minimum standards 
                                  set forth in the state statute will govern.

4.         21-324                 Discussion of new Police Department pay plan proposal.

                   Attachments:   Police Pay Plan Proposal

                                  Council Chair Stroud read a statement regarding the Council process for this evening's 
                                  presentation on Item 4 which is listed as an information item. With Council support, this 
                                  agenda item can be moved forward to the next Business meeting on September 7th at 
                                  which time the Council can hold a public hearing and vote on the proposed compensation 
                                  plan for the Police Department.

                                  Police Chief Severson presented on this item and spoke about the current state of the 
                                  Sandy City police force and the general state of police forces around the country. He 
                                  spoke about the difficulties associated with recruiting and retaining experienced officers 
                                  and gave a timeline of events that have led to the current shortage of police officers. He 
                                  reviewed the current officer pay scale for Sandy City and neighboring cities. Many police 
                                  agencies around the valley have moved to raise officer salaries. Sandy now falls at the 
                                  lower end of the pay scale for officer salaries.

                                  Katrina Frederick, Human Resource Director, reviewed the proposed pay plan for the 
                                  police officers. She reviewed the philosophy behind the pay plan, the impact of staying 
                                  competitive with officer salaries, and recent announcements by neighboring cities  
                                  increasing officer pay scales. She also reviewed additional components included in the 
                                  plan proposal and the budgetary impact. 

                                  Following the presentation, Council Member Brook Christensen made a motion that was 
                                  later withdrawn.

                                  Council discussion, comments and questions followed the presentation. The City 
                                  Attorney, Council Attorney and Executive Director provided insight and guidance 
                                  regarding the process for moving this item forward to a public hearing and Council vote. 
                                  The Council was very supportive of the proposed compensation plan and agreed to move 
                                  the item forward to the September 7th Council Business meeting for a public hearing and 
                                  Council consideration and vote.

                                  Council moved to Time Certain Items on the Agenda and heard General Citizen 
                                  Comments.

5.         21-322                 General Citizen Comments

                   Attachments:   Click here to eComment on this item

                                  Council Chair Stroud invited the public to participate in General Citizen Comments.

                                  Mr. Jim Edwards spoke about his youth and the dream of many little boys back then who 
                                  wanted to become a police officer. He wondered if kids still have that same dream today. 
                                  He spoke about duty and honor in serving one's community. He spoke about the 
                                  expectation of our first responders to receive a salary that allows them to support their 
                                  families. He expressed support for the pay increase for officers. He also thinks the City 
                                  and Council should look for other ways to support our police officers, like providing 
                                  affordable housing opportunities for officers to live in the city they serve.  

                                  Council Member Monica Zoltanski read the following two general citizen comments, 
                                  which she receive via email, into the record (they were unable to leave an ecomment on 
                                  the Legistar system):

                                  Lisa Angelos and Jane Farrow - both are 100% in support of increasing officer pay. If you 
                                  don't pay the officers well, you (the police department) will loose them. Law enforcement 
                                  is a hard and difficult job. If you pay them more they will work even harder because they 
                                  will feel valued. 

                                  Karri Jensen, from District 1, has lived in Sandy for thirty years and is a big supporter of 
                                  the police. She has been a crossing guard in Sandy for ten years. The crossing guards 
                                  are a division of the Sandy police department and she was hoping the crossing guards 
                                  could also be considered for a pay increase. Sandy City has a hard time filling crossing 
                                  guard positions and she reminded the Council of the value of crossing guards and 
                                  encouraged residents to apply for these positions.

                                  Public Comment closed.
