GENERAL PLAN/ZONE TEXT APPLICATIONS
GPZ-2-2022 Petition by CHRISTOPHER CLIFFORD requesting a General Plan change
           from Mixed Use and Large Lot Residential (2 to 3 units/acre) to Small Lot
           Residential (4 to 7 units/acre) and a zone change from A (Agriculture,
           minimum lot size ½ acre) to RS (Residential Sustainability). The property is
           located at 5143 W 3500 S on 5.9 acres. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-
           3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov)

Discussion:       Steve Pastorik explained that the subject property involves four parcels
                  surrounded mainly by single-family detached homes with a few other
                  multifamily type uses. Steve stated that the applicant is requesting the RS
                  (Residential Sustainability) zone, which includes numerous energy efficiency
                  standards along with a PUD. He presented the concept plan and expressed
                  that this plan is very conceptual. Steve explained that the developer is
                  currently working with Public Works on access and offsets for this
                  development. Therefore, Steve stated, the layout will most likely change due
                  to feedback from UDOT and the Public Works Department. He noted that the
concept plan gives the Commission an idea of the number and size of the
proposed homes.

Steve said that he had discussed the pros and cons of having amenities in a
central location instead of smaller areas throughout the development. He
stated that the applicant was proposing at least two different styles of homes
and presented the renderings to the Commission. Steve noted that the
Planning Commission should consider if this is the right location for the RS
zone, if the proposed density is suitable for this location, and if the
combination of amenities and open space offset the density.

Chair Meaders asked Steve to clarify if the stub street ends in a cul-de-sac or
goes through the development. Steve stated that the cul-de-sac would be
public, and the connection leading into the subject property would be private.
He explained there are differing opinions on whether to keep this connection
or not. Steve received a call from a resident who lives on Kiowa Way, the
street that dead-ends on the east side of the subject property, and she feels this
connection will increase traffic on her road. The Public Works Department
would like to see public access from Kiowa Way through the subject property.

Commissioner Fuller said that connection doesn’t go west, does it? Steve said
that residents might use the private street, which has more direct access to go
west. Steve Lehman had also mentioned that fire code allows up to thirty
single-family detached homes on one point of access so, if there is going to be
more than thirty units, they will need a second access.

Commissioner Winters asked if they could remove the cul-de-sac and have the
road go straight through the subject property. Steve said that is an option, as
long as the road doesn’t dead-end. Commissioner Winters feels the RS zone
will be a good fit in this location.

Commissioner Lovato asked Steve if there had been a discussion about the
City widening 3500 South and the impact on this development. Steve said that
if this proposal is approved, the development agreement will require right-of-
way dedication, allowing UDOT to widen the road.

Commissioner Lovato stated that one issue would prevent him from living in a
development such as the one proposed. The City does not pick up trash on a
private street. Therefore, residents will need to hall their waste to a public
road for pickup. Steve noted that there will be a discussion at the Council
level about the City continuing to allow private streets and if the City would
enable streets narrower than the current standard to be public streets.
Commissioner Winters is looking forward to that discussion.

Commissioner Winters said that a development near his residence uses
dumpsters instead of trash cans and suggested that as a possibility for future
                  projects.

                  Commissioner Lovato asked if there had been a discussion regarding street
                  lighting. Steve stated that existing developments have the addition of street
                  lighting as part of the development agreement. He said he would speak with
                  the developer about doing the same with this development.

                  Commissioner Winters stated that he was reviewing the housing standards,
                  and it says that basements are allowed unless a physical issue prevents the
                  addition. He then asked Steve if there is high groundwater on the subject
                  property preventing the addition of basements. Steve explained that if the
                  zoning is approved, a soils study is required to evaluate the water levels on the
                  subject property.

                  Commissioner Fuller feels the most significant issue will be the mixture of
                  public and private roads. Steve said that he had discussed this with the
                  applicant and explained that the street network could be addressed as part of
                  the zone change; however, typically, it is assessed as part of the subdivision.

                  Chair Meaders asked if this parcel could be developed without a PUD. He
                  said that a PUD is necessary for the applicant to develop the property
                  according to his plans. Steve explained that if the parcel were developed
                  without a PUD, the lots would need to be at least 8,000 square feet.


CONDITIONAL USE APPLICATIONS
C-1-2022    Petition by J5 INFRASTRUCTURE PARTNERS requesting a conditional use
            for roof mounted antennas on the Harmon Building. The property is located at
            3540 South 4000 West on 1.94 acres and is zoned C2 (General
            Commercial). (Staff – Jody Knapp at 801-963-3497 or jody.knapp@wvc-
            ut.gov)

Discussion:       Jody Knapp explained that this application is for roof-mounted cell towers on
                  top of the Harmon Building. She said there is more than one carrier in this
                  location. However, Verizon is the carrier wanting to consolidate and relocate
                  its antennas. Jody stated that the applicant is proposing to have all their
                  antennas in a central location on top of the penthouse, where they will also
                  meet the maximum permitted height. She said that Verizon is requesting not
                  to screen their antennas. Jody stated that the question for the Commission is if
                  they are okay with the antennas being left unscreened and painted brown to
                  match the roof color or if they would prefer screening. Chair Meaders asked
                  the Commission if they had any concerns regarding the screening of the
                  antennas. The Commissioners did not have any at this time.
