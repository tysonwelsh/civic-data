 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
       a. Discussion regarding a petition from Susan Lund requesting a Rezone and Master
          Development Agreement to add the Senior Housing Overlay Zone (SHO) to
          approximately 1.77 acres of property for a conceptual development, Serenity Senior
          Housing, located at the northeast corner of the intersection of Redwood Road and
          8660 South.
          Senior Planner Tayler Jensen presented a proposal for the property at 8600 S Redwood
          Road, currently zoned for professional office use, seeking approval for a Senior Housing
          Overlay (SHO). The concept plan included a three-story building with podium parking
          and 58 dwelling units. Council discussions revolved around whether to support the SHO,
          the appropriateness of the density, and concerns about potential future changes to the
          development.

          Council Member Green expressed skepticism about the success of SHO developments in
          the area and raised concerns about the possibility of future requests to convert the
          housing into regular apartments if the SHO concept failed.

          Chair Jacob emphasized the importance of trusting the proposed plan and stated that
          any future changes would require going through a rezoning process.

          Council Member Whitelock sought clarification on the feasibility of the project,
          particularly in terms of market demand and long-term viability. She also inquired about
          studies showing the project's distinction from previous SHO proposals.

       b. Discussion regarding a petition by The Boyer Company & Peterson Development
          requesting a Future Land Use Map Amendment, Rezone, and a Master Development
          Plan/Agreement. Changing the Future Land Use designation for approximately

          community needs, emphasizing the project's adherence to regulations.

       c. Discussion on the Utah Community Renewable Energy Act (CREA)
          Chair Jacob introduced the Community Renewable Energy Act disclosing that O2 Utah, a
          company related to the topic was a client of his. Despite this connection, however, he
          does not receive compensation based on whether cities choose to participate in the
          program or not.

          Chair Jacob introduced Christopher Thomas who works for Salt Lake City in their
          Sustainability Department and Member of the CREA Board along with Mayor Silvestrini
          from Millcreek.

          Christopher Thomas outlined that the Utah Renewable Communities initiative is a
          coalition of 18 communities partnering with Rocky Mountain Power (RMP) to
          implement a new clean energy program. He emphasized the program's goal of achieving
          net-100 clean energy consumption by 2030, supporting clean energy development, and
          reducing air pollution. Despite challenges and recent setbacks with RMP's renewable
          energy plans, the program intends to move forward with acquisitions of utility-scale
          clean energy and integration into PacifiCorp's transmission resources.

          Mayor Silvestrini clarified that participation would involve opting into purchasing clean
          energy credits, with residents having the option to opt out of the program if desired. He
          highlighted that Millcreek had already committed financially to support initial legal and
          consulting costs, emphasizing community support and environmental benefits as driving
          factors.

          Council members raised concerns about the financial implications and the opt-out
          nature of the program, with Council Member Green particularly wary of committing
          taxpayer funds to an initiative with uncertain costs to residents. Other members, such as
          Council Member Bloom, expressed support for the program's environmental benefits
          and the opportunity for community involvement in sustainable practices.

       d. Fiscal Year 2025 Tentative Budget Discussion
          Chair Jacob noted this discussion was the final opportunity, reminding council members
          if they did not adopt the tentative budget by June 30th, the current tentative budget as
          proposed would automatically become the adopted budget.

          Budget & Management Analyst Rebecca Condie provided updates from the County
          regarding the certified tax rate. She outlined the past five years rates, with the 2024 rate
          set at .001331. If the council chooses to add a 3.5% increase, the rate would be .001378.
          Ms. Condie presented a chart showing associated dollars: the city would receive
          $19,283,277, with new growth revenue almost doubling the planned amount at
          $817,285. Without an increase, revenue would be $20,855,652, while with the 3.5%
          increase, it would be $21,559,172. Council Member Lamb referenced his own tax bill,
          explaining that he might save money even with a tax increase, suggesting that many
          residents might pay less than they did last year. Council Member Green noted that the
          impact of tax changes would vary, with some residents paying more and others less. Ms.
          Condie confirmed this variability and provided numbers for average home values,
          emphasizing that property taxes are assessed at 55% of home value.

          Council Member Green was of the opinion that city government had expanded beyond
          new growth, hiring more staff than the increase in new revenue would typically support.
          Chair Jacob contended that rising costs necessitate these hires, while Council Members
          Lamb and Bloom highlighted the benefits of reducing burnout and turnover among staff.
          The discussion then turned to whether the city should continue expanding its workforce
          at the current rate.

          City Administrator Korban Lee highlighted that the Council and Mayor prioritize
          community aesthetics, addressing issues that, while appearing to be on public property,
          are often on private property. Examples included the entryways to subdivisions and
          subdivision walls. There was a consensus on the need to hire a code enforcement officer,
          with Council Members Jacob and Bloom strongly supporting the addition of a new hire
          to address these aesthetic issues.

          The Council briefly reviewed a list of requested budget changes, including items such as
          interpreter costs for courts, fire turnout gear, and IT equipment for new employees.
          Chair Jacob felt the list was comprehensive but wanted further discussion on the fee
          schedule.

          The Dumpster Program was discussed, and costs were outlined. The annual cost of the
          program is $40,000, serving 3,350 vehicles and collecting 104 dumpsters, including 15
          dumpsters of green waste. With landfill costs at $24 per ton and an average of 2.52 tons,
          costs were $60.48. Overhead costs were $40.61. Consequently, the overall cost for
          dumpster rental this year was $243.66.

          Council Member Whitelock raised concerns regarding the impact of corporate
          ownership of homes on the housing market, proposing a tiered business license fee to
          discourage entities from purchasing multiple homes. Council Members Lamb and Bloom
          supported studying the issue further, though Green and Bedore cautioned that any
          additional fees might be passed on to renters. Council members agreed that the issue
          was concerning and proposed collaborating with experts to find a solution.

          Council Member Whitelock questioned the disparity between park and facility rental
          fees, feeling that park rentals seemed disproportionately high. Public Services Director
          Cory Fralick explained that fees for larger groups accounted for increased cleanup and
          damage costs. He also noted that staff was working on a way for adults to reserve fields
          in the city. Council Member Whitelock questioned the fee for colored maps and council
          consensus was to review and possibly update fees to reflect current costs.

          The Council discussed lot line adjustments and the associated fees, prompted by a
          specific case where former Council Member McConnehey encountered issues. Council
          Member Green emphasized the need for a fair and consistent process for fee waivers,
          while Chair Jacob suggested addressing the broader neighborhood issues related to lot
          lines.

          Council Member Bloom left at 10:08 pm

          Chair Jacob proposed introducing a Transportation Utility Fee to address the city's
          significant unfunded liability for road maintenance. City Administrator Korban Lee
          explained that while the need is evident, implementing such a fee requires a
          comprehensive study to justify the cost. Council Member Green and others stressed the
          importance of a reasonable basis for the fee to withstand legal challenges. Chair Jacob
          suggested starting with a small fee, similar to the streetlight fee, to fund the necessary
          studies and begin now to address the issue. Chair Whitelock was willing to collect fees
          for a year while they figured out the correct amount.
