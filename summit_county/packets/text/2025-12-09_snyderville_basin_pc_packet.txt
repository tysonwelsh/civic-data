Background 
On September 4, the Community Development Department received a plat amendment 
application requesting approval for a lot combination within the Timberline 1 Subdivision. The 
proposal seeks to combine Lot 54 and 55, both owned by Toni Hampshire . Lot 54 contains 
approximately 0.48 acres while Lot 55 contains approximately 0.56 acres. The combined lot will 
be 1.04 acres in size and will resolve an existing nonconformity where a portion of the existing 
structure on Lot 55 encroaches into Lot 54. 
 
The Timberline 1 Subdivision was legally established through a Summit County land division 
process recorded under Entry No. 98292. Lot 55 is developed with a primary residence . 
However, a portion of the existing building and deck encroach onto Lot 54. Lot 54 remains 
undeveloped and does not contain a standalone structure. The proposed lot combination will 
not alter property access or impact any public or private roads. 
 
Under Section 10 -3-18 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code, the Snyderville Basin 
Planning Commission serves as the Land Use Authority for any amendment to a recorded 
subdivision plat involving a lot combination. Community Development staff, in coordination 
with applicable service providers, has reviewed the application and determined that the project 
complies with all relevant requirements of the Snyderville Basin Development Code. 
 
Analysis and Findings 
Chapter 3 – Amendment to Recorded Subdivision Plat (10-3-18) 
Standard: Section 10 -3-18(B) of the Snyderville Basin Development Code  states that plat 
amendments that result in the combination of lots, adjustments to building pads, changes to 
subdivision titles, or alterations to plat notes, and that do not affect public or private roads, 
shall be considered by the Planning Commission. 
 Analysis: The Snyderville Basin Planning Commission is the designated land use 
authority for this type of plat amendment. The proposed combination of Lots 54 and 55 does 
not affect any public or private roads. COMPLIES

approve, approve with conditions, or deny the plat amendment.

PROPOSAL 
The proposed amendments to the Development Code would correct unintended 
issues/problems and simplify the rules for Accessory Buildings in the Snyderville Basin.  
 
BACKGROUND 
On September 9, 2021, the Summit County Council adopted comprehensive amendments 
to the accessory building regulations in the Development Code. The amendments created 
regulations limiting the size and number of accessory buildings that a property owner 
could have on a lot.  
 
Highlights included: 
 
• Creation of Minor Accessory Buildings 
o Allowed in all zones 
o Size of building = 75% of principal building footprint up to 2,000 
square feet (2,667 square foot principal building = 2,000 square foot 
footprint of the Minor Accessory Building). 
o Secondary structure allowed up to 400 square feet footprint (this 
structure would be for a garden shed or smaller storage shed). 
o Combined Minor Accessory Building, Secondary structure cannot 
exceed 90% of principal structure footprint 2,667 square foot 
principal building = 2,000 square foot Minor Accessory Building + 400 
square foot secondary structure).  
o Maximum height = 26' 
• Creation of Major Accessory Buildings 
o Conditional Use Permit required in all residential zones.  
o Lots smaller than one acre not eligible. 
o Maximum size depends on size of the lot, with max size allowed 12,500 
square-foot footprint.  
45
2 
 
o Only one allowed per lot or parcel 
o Maximum height = 26' 
 
Since the ordinance was adopted, staff have encountered a few issues/unintended 
consequences of the ordinance. The purpose of this amendment is to correct those 
issues.  
 
Issue #1  
Current accessory building regulations limit the number of accessory structures permitted 
to 1 Major, 1 Minor and 400 square feet of smaller accessory buildings. This rule has 
created challenges for property owners. In some cases, applicants are required to remove 
an existing accessory building to construct a new one, even when the overall size and use 
of the structures would remain compatible with the property and surrounding area.  
The proposed language establishes a standard based on maximum lot coverage, 10% 
coverage up to a maximum of 14,500 square feet. Under the new regulations, property 
owners may construct multiple accessory buildings if the combined footprint does not 
exceed 10% of the lot area, providing flexibility while maintaining an appropriate scale of 
development. 
 
Issue #2 
The definition of a Major Accessory building requires that the building be at least 2,000 
square feet or more in size. This means that if a property owner has a 1,500 square foot 
minor accessory building on the lot and would like to build another approximately the 
same size, she can’t do it because the ordinance states only one minor accessory building 
is permitted. Assuming the property owner does not already have a major accessory 
building, her only option would be to increase the proposed footprint of the second 
structure to at least 2,000 square feet and apply for a major accessory building. 
To fix the problem, staff is proposing that the Major/Minor provision be eliminated and 
replaced with a requirement that any Accessory Building with a footprint greater than 2,000 
square feet be reviewed as a Conditional Use. This would give property owners more 
options without requiring that they increase their building footprint to meet the minimum 
requirement to be a major accessory building and still give the Planning 
Commission/public an opportunity to review/comment on larger buildings.  
 
Issue #3 
The size of the buildings is based on the footprint. Currently there is no definition of 
footprint in the Development Code. Generally, the footprint has been calculated by 
measuring the perimeter of the cement foundation of a structure. However, many 
accessory buildings have cantilevers that are supported by posts but not a foundation and 
are therefore not included in the calculation of a footprint. This can have a significant 
impact on the mass and scale of the building. To clarify, staff is proposing the following 
definition:  
 
46
3 
“BUILDING FOOTPRINT. The total area of the plan view projected to grade, of a 
Building or structure, measured from the outside edge of all exterior walls and 
supporting columns. Cantilevered portions of roofs, up to a maximum projection of 
36 inches from the wall or supporting beam line are not included. Uncovered; 
exterior stairs, ramps, patios, and decks, less than 30 inches above grade are not 
included.” 
I
ssue #4 
Staff have also identified situations where property owners circumvent existing accessory 
building limits by attaching new structures to the primary building or by constructing 
accessory buildings directly adjacent to one another. These practices create larger 
building masses than intended and are contrary to the intent of the rules. The proposed 
amendments address this issue by defining what constitutes an attached structure and by 
establishing a minimum five-foot separation between all buildings on a lot.  
S
UMMARY 
The proposed accessory Building amendments do the following: 
• R
equire a CUP for any accessory building with a footprint of 2,000 square
feet or larger.
• Prohibit construction of an accessory building before the principal building
or use exists.
• Limits total footprint of Accessory Buildings to 10% of the lot area (max of
14,000 square feet).
• Gives Planning Commission ability to modify setbacks during CUP process.
• Maintains max height of 26 feet rule.
• Requires detached accessory structures to be at least 5 feet apart.
• Defines when an accessory structure is considered “attached”
• Prohibits steel shipping containers.
• Creates definitions and modifies the Use table.
R
ECOMMENDATION 
Staff recommend the Planning Commission review the proposed language and provide 
staff with direction.

A. Purpose: To establish clear, consistent regulations for Accessory Buildings, to 
ensure they remain subordinate to Principal Uses, are compatible with surrounding 
Development and do not circumvent requirements applicable to Principal Uses.  
 
B. Standards for Accessory Buildings  
1. Conditional Use Permit (CUP): Any single Accessory Building with a Footprint 
2,000 sq ft or greater requires a CUP , even if the Lot allows a larger combined 
Footprint. 
 
2. Timing of Construction: An Accessory Building or Structure shall not be 
constructed or placed on a Lot prior to the establishment of a Principal Building 
or Principal Use. Construction of an Accessory Building may begin once a valid 
Building Permit has been issued for the Principal Building.  
 
3. Maximum Combined Footprint: The total combined Footprint of all Accessory 
Buildings may not exceed ten percent (10%) of the Lot area, up to 14,000 square 
feet. All Accessory Building Footprints count toward this maximum.   
 
4. Setbacks: Accessory Buildings must meet all required Setbacks. The Planning 
Commission may require greater Setbacks to protect Adjacent residential uses 
or maintain neighborhood character. 
 
5. Height: Accessory Buildings may not exceed twenty-six feet (26’) in Height. 
 
6. Separation: Accessory Buildings shall be detached and located a minimum of 
ﬁve (5) feet from any other Building on the same Lot, as measured between the 
nearest exterior walls. A greater separation distance may be required by the 
adopted Building or Fire Code, whichever is more restrictive.  
 
C. Attachment Standard. For the purposes of this Section, an Accessory Building is 
considered attached when it is physically connected to the Principal Building by 
either: 
 
1. A shared wall of at least ten (10) feet in length; or 
2. An enclosed and conditioned connector that is at least ten (10) feet wide and 
results in no more than twenty (20) feet of separation between foundations.  
 
If neither of these conditions are met, the Accessory Building is considered 
detached. 
 
D. Steel Shipping Containers 
Steel Shipping Containers are prohibited, except for use during active construction 
with a valid Building Permit.
