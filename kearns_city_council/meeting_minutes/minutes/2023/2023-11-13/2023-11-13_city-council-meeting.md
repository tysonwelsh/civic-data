Citizen Public Input

               Craig Stinson stated he was a long-time resident of Kearns and had been
having issues with Code Enforcement over a vehicle parked in the dirt on the side of his
house. It had never been an issue before. He brought a letter he had received from Code
Enforcement.

               Nathan Bracken said most of the code enforcement provisions had been
inherited from Salt Lake County.

               Mayor Kelly Bush explained that the location of the car was a violation
because chemicals could leach into the soil. Even though the issue had not been brought
up before, it was still a code violation.

              Nathan Bracken noted all the violation codes in the letter were under Code
19, which was in the process of being revised.

               Mayor Bush gave Mr. Stinson six months to comply with the Code.
She asked Mr. Bracken to send an email to Code Enforcement that she was going to
rescind the notice for case number CEZ232887.

Special Guest – Jordan Valley Conservancy District

                Jacob Young, General Manager, Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District
(JVWCD), reviewed a handout entitled Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District: Jordan Valley
Regional Water Treatment Plant Drought, Wildfire, & Earthquake Resiliency Project. JVWCD has
a partnership with Kearns Improvement District (KID) to provide water to Kearns Metro Township.
The project to improve the facility will cost approximately $70,000. JVWCD is trying to mitigate
the cost to residents by pursuing a FEMA grant in the amount of $46,000. It would be a Building
Resilient Infrastructure in Communities (BRICK) grant. He provided a QR code leading to a
comment form on JVWCD’s website. Congressman Burgess Owens has provided a letter of
support to go with the grant application.

              Mayor Kelly Bush stated she would put the QR code on the Township’s website
as well.

Unified Police Department (UPD)

               Chief Levi Hughes, Unified Police Department, reviewed the Patrol Activity
Report for October of 2023. It is on track for call volume, but assaults and burglary alarms are
up. Damaged property decreased, which is notable because it has resulted from some of the
arrests made by Detective Omar Flores.

Patrol officers are making stops on 6200 South and 5600 West. Two drug dealers were pulled
over and UPD was able to recover narcotics, cash, and guns.

Jason Mazuran has been selected as Chief of UPD going forward. There is going to be a
reshaping of UPD and shared services. The shared services model is important to Kearns. When
the Sheriff leaves, there will be a surplus of around 48 officers. UPD needs to start “right-sizing”
now. There are some positions in Kearns that could go to shared services.

Officer Omar Flores accepted a job in violent crimes in Robbery and Homicide. Officer Travis
Clark has moved to the K9 Unit.

                Chief Jason Mazuran, Unified Police Department, explained about how UPD
updates and how the updates come about. There is a reorganization committee which is a work
group consisting of a wide range of people across the organization. There is also a command
team that reorganizes and tests what the organization committee provides. The organization
chart is currently changing frequently.

Planning

Title 12 Updates

               Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated he has been
working on updating Title 12 with Municipal Services District (MSD) staff. Title 12 is basically the
procedural aspect of code enforcement. The updates will be more specific to a municipality’s
authority. There will also be a new abatement process where a resident will be given 30 days to
clean up a violation before any fees are imposed. Fees will be minimal compared to what they
were before. Like any code, it will need to be updated from time to time.

Legal Updates/Information

Approval of Community Renewable Energy Program Agreement

               Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated Kearns Metro
Township has agreed to participate in the renewable energy program. A Board was created to
manage this, and Council Member Schaeffer sits on that board representing Kearns. The Board
approved the utility agreement on October 2nd. The next procedural step is for the Council to
approve the utility agreement. This agreement would not finalize Kearns’ participation. That can
only be done as a final step. It will, however, determine the costs of participation. After the
Council approves the agreement, the Board will need to apply to the Utah Public Service
Commission. Once the application is approved, it will become more clear what the costs will be.

The agreement addresses four elements: It commits to pay for a consultant; reimburses Rocky
Mountain Power for costs associated with opt-out notices; it addresses termination fees, and
identifies any thermal power plants that will not serve program customers.

                 Council Member Snow, seconded by Council Member Peterson, moved to approve
the utility agreement. The motion passed unanimously.

HB 374 Implementation Update

               Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, explained an interlocal
agreement is being written and that should go into effect around July 2024. Sheriff Rosie Rivera
has requested additional funding for Southwest regional parks. The Board had questions and
wonders whether the current interlocal agreement with UPD requires the County to fund all
recreation areas out of the General Fund. The Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Agency
(SLVLESA) cannot pay for recreation areas without violating the terms of the interlocal
agreement. Mr. Bracken has prepared a draft letter asking for time for the SLVLESA members
to meet with the County mayor to better understand the request and discuss increased demands
for law enforcement.

     A RESOLUTION ADOPTING THE KEARNS METRO TOWNSHIP’S 2024 TENTATIVE
     BUDGET

WHEREAS, Kearns Metro Township (“Kearns”) is a municipality and political subdivision of the
state of Utah; and

WHEREAS, Kearns is required to adopt a tentative budget for the upcoming fiscal year pursuant
to Utah Code § 10-6-1111; and

WHEREAS, Kearns has prepared a budget for 2024 and desires to adopt it as its tentative 2024
Budget.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED effective immediately:

1.      The attached budget is hereby adopted as Kearns’ tentative budget for the 2024 fiscal
year.

2.    The Kearns Council will hold a public hearing on December 6, 2023, at 6:00 p.m. at the
Kearns Library located at 4275 W. 5345 S. to receive public comments on the tentative 2023
Kearns budget.

3.      Notice of the public hearing shall be provided pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 10-6-112.

4.      A copy of this tentative budget shall be made available for public inspection for a period
of at least ten days prior to the public hearing, pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 10-6-112.

5.      This resolution shall take effect upon its adoption.
