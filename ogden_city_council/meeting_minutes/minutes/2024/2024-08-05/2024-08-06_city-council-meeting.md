Proposed Compensation Increase for Executive Municipal Officers
         A memo from Council staff regarding proposed compensation increases for Executive Municipal Officers came before
the Council for consideration. The proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 Budget includes a proposed minimum four percent
salary increase for all city employees, including executive officers (but not elected officials). Action on the proposed
compensation increases will occur with adoption of the Final FY 2024-2025 Budget.
         Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound
on the proposal. Senate Bill (SB) 91 was adopted by the Utah Legislature in 2024 and requires a legislative body proposing a
compensation increase for specified officers to notice and hold a public hearing on the proposed increase; the hearing must be held
separately from any other hearing, but action on the proposed increases can occur with the Council’s action on the final budget
adoption. Municipal Executive Officers include:
              • Chief Administrative Officer (CAO) and Assistant CAO’s
              • City Attorney and Deputy City Attorney(s)
              • Department Head and Deputy Department Head(s)
              • Division Manager and Deputy Division Manager
              • Chief and Deputy/Assistant Chief(s)
         Ms. Eller-Smith emphasized that Municipal Executive Officers does not include elected officials. She concluded her
presentation by providing an overview of the McGrath Compensation Study, which was the basis for the proposed compensation
increases throughout the City included in the FY 2024-2025 budget. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the
information packet for the meeting.
          Chair Richey then opened the public hearing.
          Linda Sanchez asked if there are salary caps for Municipal Officers. She noted that the private sector has caps for
employee salaries.
          Robert Hunter stated he is the former City Manager of Ogden City and was the last City Manager who served before the
City’s form of government was changed to include a full-time Mayor position. During that time, he spent a great deal of time with
City employees and even hired a police and fire chief during his tenure. It was very easy for him to see how hard the employees
worked and how much they cared about doing the right things for the City. He stated that employees deserve to be compensated
fairly and this may require a tax increase, which is a very difficult decision for the Council to make. However, the Council must
also consider the citizens of the community; he is retired and on a fixed income, but he feels that it is important for local
government to be funded appropriately. He trusts the judgment of the City Council in making this important decision for the
community.
          Richard Bergner stated he is opposed to any tax increase because the City is already overtaxing him by $50,000. It is
disappointing to see the streets that are being improved in the community, while others are left to deteriorate with no maintenance
or repair. He would rather see more firefighters and police officers hired than pay increases for the City Council.
          Heath Satow stated he believes the City’s employees need to be paid competitively, especially since a majority of
residents do not live in Ogden and may live in other communities with a higher cost of living. However, he is concerned about
people on fixed incomes being forced to pay higher taxes. There must be a point at which the City deems it appropriate to consider
other sources of revenue for paying higher salaries. Or, perhaps, too many people are employed by the City and some efficiencies
could be explored. He has not heard much discussion of opportunities for reducing costs and saving money throughout the process
to develop the FY 2024-2025 budget.
          Teresa Bramwell stated she is not opposed to pay increases that will bring compensation to a point where employees’
wages are competitive with other cities. However, rather than a tax increase, it would be more effective to make good use of land
in the City. There is a finite amount of land left undeveloped, but a lot that is not being used for its highest and best use. There are
opportunities for converting land uses to bring more tax revenue to the City.
          There being no further persons appearing to be heard, COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED THE PUBLIC
HEARING BE CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA, ALL VOTING AYE.

Departments across the City are appropriate.
          Council member Choberka invited Mr. Bergner to report any street maintenance issue to any member of the Council or to
the City Administration to bring attention to the issue.

          A memo from Council staff regarding the final Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 budget came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated the items being presented to the Council this evening as part of action on the final budget are the
Goals and Guidelines for the FY2024-2025 budget; FY2024-2025 arts grants awards; FY2024-2025 salary schedules; and the
certified tax rate for FY2024-2025.
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
summarize the FY2024-2025 Goals and Guidelines; the document is intended to communicate the Council’s expectations for
implementation of the City’s budget, which is the primary policy document of the City. In approving the budget, the Council
strives to ensure that the policies supported by the budget reflect both the operating needs of the City and the desires of its
residents as outlined in the Five-Year Strategic Plan. The budget guidelines generally remain consistent from year to year unless
policy changes are implemented. The budget goals change as projects are completed and focus moves to other projects or
priorities. Changes to the document for FY2024-2025 include a reference to the Comprehensive Financial Sustainability Model
(CFSM), guidance regarding reappropriation of unexpended salary and benefit funding, and changing evaluation of the certified
tax rate from a biannual review to an annual review. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for
the meeting.
          Arts Administrator Buckley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the recommendation regarding
FY2024-2025 arts grants awards. Each year the Ogden City Council appropriates grant funding to support various local arts
organizations and programs. The Ogden Arts Advisory Committee and Administration review each application and provide
recommendations to the Council. The FY2025 budget for arts grants is expected to be $150,000. This year Ogden City Arts
received 31 applications requesting a total of $248,500 in funding. Of this, 11 applications applied for a total of $130,000 in
general operating support; 20 applicants applied for a total of $118,500 in project support. She summarized the criteria used by the
Arts Advisory Committee to evaluate and score each arts grant application, provided a two-year comparison of applications
received and funded, and listed the projects and programs for which the Advisory Committee has recommended funding. For a
copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Council Deputy Director Symes then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the Council’s review of the
Mayor’s proposed FY2024-2025 budget and salary schedules; he also presented year-over-year comparisons of the FY2024
adopted budget and the FY2025 tentative budget, noting the budget is increasing by $12.4 million, or 4.89 percent. Administration
has recommended the Council consider an increase to the City’s certified tax rate; based upon Utah Code 59-2-924(1)(g):
               • Certified tax rate allows taxing entities to receive the same property tax revenue from the previous year plus
                    new growth
               • As the City’s total taxable value increases, the certified tax rate decreases
               • If the City’s total taxable value decreases, the certified tax rate increases, e.g. 2004-2005, 2008-2012
               • No inflationary adjustment built into the calculation
          The Council has considered the following methodologies for calculating the certified tax rate:
               • Specific Dollar Amount – Mayor’s proposal of an additional$183,000 (0.94 percent rate increase)
               • CPI Adjustment – three percent increase in revenues
                        o Additional $591,400 in revenues
               • Maintaining the Rate – Setting the rate at the 2023 level of 0.002239 (5.36 percent rate increase)
                        o Additional $1,053,425 in revenues
          Mr. Symes noted that Ogden City has increased property taxes just four times since 1985 when the Truth in Taxation law
was enacted. He identified property tax relief programs administered by the Weber County Assessor and Clerk and indicated
property owners can appeal their property valuations and taxes through those programs.
          Mr. Symes then discussed the salary schedules and proposed implementation of the McGrath Compensation Study,
which includes:
               • Recommended salary ranges and pay grades for Fiscal Year 2024-2025
               • If below, bring employees to the recommended McGrath compensation study pay grade minimum (merit) or
                    closest step (public safety)
               • Merit increase of up to 4% for general employees who did not receive at least a 4% increase with bring to
                    minimum implementation
               • Step increases on anniversary date for sworn fire and police who are eligible
               • Recommended job title changes
          Mr. Symes concluded his presentation by presenting the proposed changes to the budget included in the Schedule A and
Schedule A-1 documents. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.

           Teresa Bramwell also expressed her support for arts in the community; she attended a recent concert that was supported
by an arts entity in the community, and she appreciates the manner in which arts contribute to the quality of life for residents. She
echoed Mr. Hunter’s recommendations that the City increase communication regarding the tax relief programs available to
property owners. She advised property owners to find a realtor that can assist them in obtaining information regarding comparable

           Linda Sanchez stated that she recently spoke to a property assessor who told her that ‘the people are being taken
advantage of’; according to State law, when property values go up, the tax levy must go down. She stated that everyone has seen
their taxes skyrocket and she asked if the levy has actually gone down. Cities and counties can hold a truth in taxation meeting and
the public must agree to being taxed. She may be misunderstanding the process, but she does not believe that the City is doing
things correctly because residents would not agree to being taxed more or to pay more for the same benefit. She asked that the
Council help to protect the residents; she must live within her means, and she advised that the Council do the same on behalf of
the City.
           Michelle Tanner stated she is representing the Ogden Friends of Acoustic Music (OFOAM) and thanked the Mayor and
Council for their support of arts in the Ogden community. She has heard from people who are impressed by the arts scene in

for first responders to deal with the trauma they experience on a daily basis.

identify the manner in which the City’s tax rate has actually been adjusted downwards over a 14-year period when it was adjusted
based upon increased property values. He reiterated the City has only increased taxes four times since 1985.
           Council member Choberka also reviewed the notice she received from the Weber County Assessor’s office, which
identifies the various taxing entities in the County and indicates that her tax bill would actually decrease if Ogden City chose to
accept the certified tax rate rather than increase taxes. Chair Richey stated it is important for residents to understand that their total
tax bill is impacted by many taxing entities and only 18 percent of the total tax paid is diverted to Ogden City. The five-percent
rate increase is only for the portion of the taxes paid to Ogden City, not the overall tax bill.
           Council members then spoke to their role of ensuring the City is appropriately funded in order to provide quality services
to residents; it is necessary to fairly compensate City employees to provide stability in the community and to ensure adequate

projects in the community.

Public Comments
          Linda Sanchez stated that given the Council’s action to increase taxes and adopt a budget for Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-
2025, she does believe that the men and women of the Fire Department do need some help. In the Police Department, she would
recommend improved de-escalation training for employees to make residents more comfortable calling for emergency support
from a police officer. She recited the following quote: ‘Hard times create strong men. Strong men create good times. Good times
create weak men. Weak men create hard times.’ She reminded everyone to learn from their own history to avoid being in hard
times on a regular basis. She then stated she identifies as an over-taxed, under-represented, non-woke, pissed off American, but
she has been impressed by the efforts of the City Council to help to represent all residents of the community. She stated the
American flag is her flag and she will not apologize for that; it does not stand for skin color, religion, or sexual orientation, but it
does stand for freedom. She referenced the Council’s conversation in tonight’s work session regarding diversity and indicated she
hopes they will be careful not to divide people in the community. She then noted that the City’s approval of high-density housing
projects in recent years were intended to reduce the tax burden of the community, but she has not seen that come to fruition.
          Pieder Beeli referenced the Council’s prioritization of Ogden City employees in the annual budget. He challenged that
priority and indicated government does not generate wealth, but it extracts wealth from its citizens and the citizens and their
pocketbooks should be the top priority for the City Council. It may be possible to parcel out some services performed by City
employees to the private sector and see if prices can be reduced. He stated if City employees are unsatisfied, they can go to the
private sector; he does not understand why the City performs so many functions and services that could be provided by a private
company. Government is parasitic by nature, and he is displeased to hear about the Council’s prioritization of taking care of the
parasitic personnel. Wealth generation is performed by service to others and self-sacrifice and government does not do either of
those things.
          Steve Jones thanked the Mayor and Council for the hard work they have dedicated to developing a budget; he plans to be
in attendance at future meetings on a regular basis to resume his dialogue with the Administration and Council regarding the
Union Station. He referenced the “Save the Union Station” Facebook page, which includes a great deal of information regarding
the Station and opportunities for improving the campus. He stated that Mayor Nadolski has spent a great deal of time examining
the recommendations of those interested in the project and he feels it is time to proceed with action.
          Emily Beeli relayed a story about a family that purchased a home made of ‘hodge-podged’ materials; they sought to
renovate the exterior of the home and learned that their options are extensively restricted based upon zoning of the home. All
options available were very expensive and they were concerned about going into debt in order to proceed. She asked that the
Council reconsider these zoning codes to increase options available to homeowners interested in improving the appearance and
increasing the value of their home.

Mayor Comments
           Mayor Nadolski thanked Ms. Sanchez for her comments and stated he feels the same about the American flag and he felt
a sense of unity when driving under the large flag that was hung over Washington Boulevard during the Pioneer Days celebration.
He pushed back against Mr. Beeli’s comments about City employees being a ‘parasitic cartel’; it is his perspective that staff
should be a priority for the City because they are a reflection of the community. He would love to recognize the selfless service
and sacrifice they provide each day, even though they earn a paycheck for their work. This year’s budget process was very
difficult, but he believes the best things come through struggle; however, he appreciates the collaboration of the City Council and
Administration to develop a great budget with many meaningful projects and programs. Ogden City is rebuilding, and it is very
hard and very expensive; this is very burdensome on City staff, and he is dedicated to providing the resources they need to
complete their jobs.

Councilmember Comments
           Council member Blair stated he also disagrees with Mr. Beeli’s comments about City employees; he argued that City
employees generate value and revenue and if they are not treated with respect or made to feel they are easily replaceable, the City
will spend a lot of money associated with employee turnover. His highest priority is to take care of City employees and he has
always appreciated their efforts to help the City Council members fulfill their campaign promises. He also acknowledged the
value added to the community by the many arts programs that are supported via annual arts grants. He noted he visited the
Marshall White Center (MWC) project site and looks forward to a beautiful facility, which also includes arts installations that will
tell the story of the building. He concluded he attended an event a few weeks ago where someone told him they had met with
Chief Administrative Officer Brown and that their experience with her was very positive and productive. He stated he appreciates
the way Ms. Brown represents the City.
           Vice Chair White stated the budget process for Fiscal Year (FY) 2025-2026 starts in less than six months.
           Mayor Nadolski thanked the Council for cancelling their meeting scheduled for August 13, 2024, to give everyone the
ability to attend the National Night Out Against Crime event hosted by the Police Department.
           Chair Richey thanked City employees for their service; public service is a thankless job and Ogden City has some of the
most dedicated public servants in the State of Utah.
           Council member Hyer led the Council and audience members in a round of applause for City employees.
