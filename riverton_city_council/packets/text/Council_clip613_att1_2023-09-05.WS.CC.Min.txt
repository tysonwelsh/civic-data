 3            Town Days Discussion Continued
 4
 5   Ms. Garn led a discussion regarding the city's parade route for Town Days. The current parade
 6   route, starting at 1930 West and ending at 12600 South (Riverton High School), was outlined. It
 7   was explained that this route had been in use for over 20 years and was efficient for unloading
 8   parade participants. However, concerns were raised about the suitability of this route for a
 9   growing city, and the need for a longer location for unloading and offloading parade participants.
10   The proposal was to start the parade at CR Hamilton Sports Park, which had a larger parking lot
11   for unloading and getting participants in place. This route would increase the parade distance by
12   three-quarters of a mile, providing more space for spectators.
13
14   Ms. Garn acknowledged some challenges in this new parade route, such as the increased
15   distance for marching participants, especially children and marching bands. The proposal was
16   said to address traffic issues, with adequate public parking areas. One specific concern was the
17   roundabout on the proposed route and whether it could accommodate larger floats. Ms. Garn
18   mentioned using a drone to assess the float's maneuverability around the roundabout, which
19   appeared to work well.
20
21   Ms. Garn said the main point of contention was the additional cost for police protection due to
22   the need to close some neighborhoods along the route. The decision on whether to change the
23   parade route was left to the elected officials.
24
25   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained that the current parade route had issues related to
26   accessibility and created disadvantages for both parade participants and spectators. He said the
27   new parade route was considered more accessible from all angles.
28
29   Mr. Carter acknowledged there were some disadvantages of the new route, such as the
30   additional distance, but it was argued that the benefits in terms of accessibility and safety
31   outweighed this drawback. He emphasized that the proposed route was a result of careful
32   consideration and planning for public safety.
33
34   Ms. Garn discussed safety concerns related to the city's parade. Photos were shown to illustrate
35   the issues that have arisen during previous parades, emphasizing the need to address safety
36   concerns. The following points were highlighted:
37
38            Crowd Encroachment: Photos showed spectators standing very close to the parade
39            route, sometimes even crossing the white line meant to separate them from the parade.
40
41            Swag Distribution: Concerns were raised about the distribution of swag (parade
42            giveaways) to spectators. It was noted that spectators often rush to collect swag from
43            parade participants rather than letting it be thrown to them, which can create safety risks.
44
45            Spotter Challenges: Some parade participants, particularly those with large floats, face
46            difficulties in ensuring the safety of spectators, especially children, who come too close
47            to their vehicles despite having designated spotters.
48
49            Safety Measures: Several potential safety measures were discussed, including the
50            possibility of eliminating candy and swag throwing altogether. However, it was
51            recognized that this could diminish the hometown feel of the parade.
     Page | 3 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1
 2            Fencing: The idea of using portable fencing to create a barrier between the parade route
 3            and spectators was mentioned. This measure has been implemented in other parades to
 4            prevent encroachment.
 5
 6   Ms. Garn suggested that a more in-depth discussion on parade safety and potential measures
 7   be scheduled for the upcoming strategic planning session in January. This would allow for a
 8   thorough evaluation of safety concerns and potential solutions.
 9
10   Councilmember Wells acknowledged that stopping the distribution of candy and swag would
11   likely lead to dissatisfaction among parade-goers. He also pointed out that despite efforts to
12   enhance safety, it may never be perfect due to the spontaneous nature of spectator behavior
13   during parades. He appreciated the ongoing efforts to address safety concerns and suggested
14   waiting until January for further discussion on the matter.
15
16   Councilmember Pierucci expressed his interest in exploring multiple parade route options rather
17   than settling for a single alternative. He suggested having several choices to consider during the
18   January discussion. He requested input from Chief Barfuss regarding safety concerns and
19   alternative solutions.
20
21   Chief Barfuss explained that implementing the new route would require a significant number of
22   officers, crossing guards, barricades, and other resources to ensure safety and security. He
23   also noted that the current route could still be improved by increasing the presence of officers
24   on bicycles, enhancing motor patrols, better messaging to the public, and stationing personnel
25   at regular intervals.
26
27   Councilmember Buroker shared her perspective on the candy-throwing tradition during parades,
28   recalling a past attempt to eliminate it, which proved unpopular and short-lived. She suggested
29   conducting a survey to gauge residents' preferences regarding candy throwing, increased
30   barricades, and other safety measures. She expressed support for waiting until January to
31   decide on the parade route.
32
33   Councilmember McCay acknowledged the tradition of candy-throwing during parades and
34   suggested that limiting candy might not be well-received by the community. She supported the
35   idea of extending the parade route along 13400 South, as it provides more space and could
36   help keep people back from the parade route. She mentioned the ongoing construction and
37   how it could affect the parade route, regardless of the chosen option. She expressed her
38   support for implementing the longer route in the upcoming parade if possible.
39
40   Mr. Carter explained that there have been recent discussions about the possibility of
41   constructing a bridge over 13800 South to improve accessibility. While this is within the realm of
42   possibilities, there are ongoing negotiations regarding cost allocation and other factors. The goal
43   is to open 13800 South in time for the next school year, which would significantly impact traffic
44   circulation in the area.
45
46   Councilmember McCay asked about the bike routes on 13400 South. Mr. Necaise mentioned
47   that they've secured funding for the project and presented two options for widening the road.
48   The first option involves widening both sides of the road by about four feet each, while the
49   second option is to widen one side by approximately six feet and narrow the lanes to create a
50   bike lane.

18   escalate into a negative situation quickly. To address this, it was suggested that a pre-recorded
19   general announcement could be played at various points along the parade route to remind
20   attendees without personal confrontation. This approach would depersonalize the message and
21   reduce the likelihood of adverse reactions from the crowd.

20
21   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the agreement was a revised
22   version of an agreement that had previously been seen in June. A few other cities involved in
23   the agreement had made some modifications to their respective portions of it. These changes
24   did not impact Riverton City's part of the agreement. However, because the modifications
25   resulted in a new version of the agreement, it was brought back to the Council for an updated
26   signature.
27
28   Mr. Carter explained that Herriman withdrew entirely from the agreement, opting to establish a
29   direct connection with HUD at the federal level to potentially secure CDB-type grant funding.
30   This shift in approach does not exempt them from adhering to the policies outlined in the
31   agreement. This agreement essentially operates on a "pay to play" basis, historically benefiting
32   Riverton City with CDBG funding. However, it's worth noting that such funding opportunities do
33   not arise every year, and periodic agreement renewals are necessary to demonstrate eligibility
34   for potential funding.
35
36   Mayor Pro Tem McDougal clarified that this agreement was already approved in June. It
37   essentially establishes a gateway for the City to access certain funds if they ever decide to
38   pursue a project that aligns with the requirements. Right now, there are no specific projects in
39   mind.
40

31            Resolution No. 23-72 – Authorizing the Mayor to execute an agreement with Big
32            Bear Electric for the completion of the City Hall Back Plaza Project
33
34   Craig Calvert, Purchasing Manager, provided an update on the City Hall Back Plaza Project.
35   The project had an initial budget of $1.2 million, but the bid came in at $1,375,000. This cost did
36   not include a restroom or pavilion, which would add an additional $150,000 to the project. He
37   mentioned that preliminary work had been done, including addressing tunnels and an
38   abandoned wellhead on the site. During construction, it was observed that the stairway on the
39   project had cracked due to water damage, and it would require replacement at an additional
40   cost of $80,000. He expressed hope that this cost could be included in the Community Center's
41   lower-level project using TRCC funding. Depending on the Council's decision, additional costs
42   for the project could be either $375,000 or $455,000. He sought guidance from the Council on
43   how to proceed with the project.
44
45   Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, said he hoped that TRCC would be willing to
46   keep it at $1.2 million as they initially committed but cover some of the additional expenses,
47   including the stairs and other costs. This way, the City could potentially avoid a significant
48   budget amendment.
49
50   Councilmember McCay said that in situations where other projects experienced cost overruns,
51   TRCC has typically considered it the responsibility of the City to cover those additional costs.
     Page | 9 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     September 5, 2023




 1   However, if the City were to approach TRCC about expanding the project to address safety
 2   concerns with the stairs, it would likely need to be voted on by the TRCC board. This would not
 3   be a general practice of automatically covering cost overruns, but rather a specific consideration
 4   for the circumstances.
 5
 6   Councilmember Buroker asked if the stairs were required and Mr. Calvert said they were
 7   required.
 8
 9   Councilmember Wells asked if TRCC doesn't agree to cover the additional costs and the stairs
10   are deemed a safety issue that must be addressed, where the funding to cover these costs
11   would come from within the City's budget.
12
13   Mr. Hicks said if TRCC does not provide additional funding and the City needs to cover the
14   $455,000 budget increase for the project, one option could be to allocate the necessary funds
15   from the City's fund balance and the 45 fund. However, this would require a budget amendment
16   and approval by the Council.
17
18   Councilmember Pierucci asked if the cost increase was from materials, labor or something else.
19   In response, Mr. Calvert said that the cost increases for the project are primarily due to the
20   rising costs of materials and labor. He discussed the process of bidding the project twice and
21   the challenges faced by general contractors, including time constraints and difficulty in securing
22   subcontractors, especially concrete contractors. The completion date was extended for the
23   second round of bidding, and two responses were received.
24
25   Mayor Pro Tem McDougal expressed his opinion on the project, highlighting the changes in the
26   market and the City's fiscal responsibility. He emphasized the importance of moving forward
27   with a budget amendment to cover the cost overrun, given the current positive sales tax growth
28   and the anticipation of rising construction costs in the future. He suggested that delaying the
29   project might lead to even higher costs down the road and recommended investing in it now.
30
31   Councilmember McCay expressed agreement with moving forward on the project but raised the
32   question of the project's timeline, particularly regarding the pavilion and restrooms. She
33   suggested the possibility of waiting for two weeks or until the next council meeting for
34   clarification or potential funding options.
35
36   Mr. Calvert responded by confirming that it would be possible to proceed with the contract for
37   Big Bear without immediately including the restroom and pavilion. He suggested the option of
38   revisiting the approval for the restroom and pavilion in the future. Mr. Hicks suggested that the
39   County might be open to including the restroom and pavilion as part of the project within the
40   original $1.2 million budget, highlighting the importance of exploring this possibility.
41
