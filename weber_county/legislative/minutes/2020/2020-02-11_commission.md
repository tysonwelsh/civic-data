 E. PUBLIC COMMENTS:
    --Lynne Carter, thanked the Commission for their support of the Ogden Botanical Gardens on behalf of the Utah
    State University (USU) Weber Extension and the Ogden Botanical Gardens. Commissioner Jenkins asked for
    the yearly allocation by the county to the Gardens and Naomi Brower, Weber Extension Director, said that it is
    $35,000/year for the salary of Helen Muntz, Horticulture Agent. Commissioner Jenkins has no problem that the
    county gave some money to get the park started but it is an Ogden City park, and the Gardens have been completed
    (about 26 years), thus, he does not know why the county continues to pay into it. Ms. Muntz’ stated that it is a
    City park, that USU presently owns the education building and does all the mowing, gardening maintenance and
    the City maintains the parkway. Jerry Goodspeed, Ogden Botanical Gardens/Utah Botanical Center Director,
    would be a good resource for answers and Commissioner Harvey would like to get a report directly from him
    (who has been there about two decades). Ms. Brower said that the building was donated to USU by someone in
    the community but without funding for maintenance, that it was given on public property for the City but it has
    been a question from the beginning as to who should maintain it—it makes sense to her that it would be the City.
    Chair Froerer said that it is a true gem and draw for the community and needs to continue. Sean Wilkinson,
    County Community Development Director, has that 1995 agreement and will distribute it to everyone involved.
    --Jill Hipwell, of West Weber, asked if the PRUD chapter had been rewritten. She read from code that if the
    county desires it to remain a legislative decision than the chapter should be rewritten with more objective criteria
    to remove the potential of the arbitrary decision making. She said that the two planning commissions approved
    this overlay zone in 2018 and asked when it was tabled after that. Rick Grover, County Planning Director,
    responded that it had not been rewritten and that the previous Commission gave some direction to look at making
    it an overlay zone but it was put on hold. Currently it is an administrative review—it comes before the
    Commission because it is a conditional use—and the legislative portion was put on hold because the Commission
    at that time felt that it takes away some development rights making it more restrictive/difficult for development
    to occur. She had heard that there are some loopholes, and one portion she feels is a necessity for all subdivision
    sections relates to streets—that there should always be at least two access points into development if it contains
    more than 30 residences, regardless of the exception if they have fire suppression systems because the occurring
    emergency may not be a fire. Chair Froerer suggested that Ms. Hipwell start with the Planning Commission.
    --Ralph Price, of Ogden, implored the Commission to use their influence to not close the Lantern House, which
    serves three counties, on March 1 because it is key to help people get their lives back together and has been
    successful because they started with the overflow style. Two days ago there were 90 people there, 30 of them
    children, and what a tragedy to throw them out in the middle of winter. Chair Froerer said that the Lantern House
    Board is aware of this and are looking to negotiate with the North Ogden City Fire Department regarding the fire
    issue.

         Commissioner Jenkins said that it is difficult to have oversight of ACH payments because they go on staff’s

         Commission Office, gave an example of the Purchasing Agent calling her about whether she had issued a bid
         for a purchase, and she affirmed that PCards are being monitored. Mr. Hatch will speak with the Comptroller
         and Purchasing Agent and report back.

    5. CONTRACT WITH DICIO FOR A PROFESSIONAL SERVICES AGREEMENT FOR A PUBLIC RELATIONS,
       MARKETING AND COMMUNICATIONS CONSULTANT FOR THE SHERIFF’S OFFICE.
         Christopher Crockett, of the County Sheriff's Office, stated that the county has a county-wide contract with
         Dicio. The Sheriff’s Office oversees a lot of things for which the public wants to be informed and the Sheriff
         has identified a need for additional public relations in order to increase transparency and foster public trust.
         Commissioner Jenkins stated that this contract is up to $120,000, which is a very hefty amount for these
         services. He has a difficult time supporting this open-ended contract. Chair Froerer noted that when this was
         originally discussed the amount was $50,000-$60,000 and that the county needs to verify the expected
         deliverables. Mr. Crockett said that contract requirements include that Dicio is to develop a benchmark
         measuring plan to assess and prioritize needs of the Sheriff’s Office, which have to be done within the first
         six months of the agreement, and Dicio shall submit invoices on a monthly basis that specifically describe
         the performed services. This is an at will contract. Commissioner Harvey would like a report within 90 days
         with measurables. Chair Froerer had asked for metrics within 30 days. Ralph Price, of Ogden, spoke in favor
         of this contract.
         Commissioner Harvey moved to approve the contract with Dicio for a professional services agreement for a
         public relations, marketing and communications consultant for the Sheriff’s Office with the caveat to review
         the contract in 60 days and to have the contract measurables in a formal presentation no later than May 1,
         2020; Chair Froerer seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – nay; Chair Froerer – aye

    7. CONTRACT WITH DR. ROBERT S. GROW FOR EMT PHYSICIAN ADVISOR REQUIRED BY UTAH
       ADMINISTRATION CODE R426-3-700.

         Christopher Crockett, Deputy County Attorney, noted that the county has three interlocal agreements (with
         Ogden City Fire, Roy City Fire and Weber Fire) to provide paramedic services throughout the county, which
         currently is with Dr. Grow. The contract needs to be renewed. State statute requires a physician advisor for
         each emergency medical services program. It is an increase in compensation from $45,000 to $55,000. Mr.
         Crockett outlined the extensive list of requirements and said that Dr. Grow’s qualifications are quite extensive.
         Chair Froerer’s understanding is that $55,000 is a very good deal for Weber County. Mr. Crockett addressed
         Commissioner Jenkins’ question stating that these contracts used to be housed with Weber Human Services
         and will now fall under the Clerk/Auditor through County Purchasing, and the contract request came from
         the Purchasing Agent. He said that a lot of the negotiations happened through the County Attorney’s Office.
         The completed draft was sent to the three districts for review. Ricky Hatch, County Clerk/Auditor, stated
         that his understanding was that Jason Horne, County Purchasing Agent, spent a lot of time with the doctor,
         the Fire Chiefs and those that work with the services, that there had not been an increase to this contract
         amount for about 10 years, and that there had been a lot of negotiation. During Commissioner Harvey’s
         research on this item, he had spoken with public safety personnel and based on what Dr. Grow has done
         regarding paramedic services, the responses came with resounding recommendations.
         Commissioner Harvey moved to approve the contract renewal with Dr. Robert S. Grow for an EMT Physician
         Advisor as required by Utah Administration Code R426-3-700; Commissioner Jenkins seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye
