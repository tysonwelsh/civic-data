3. CBD Site Plan, to reduce parking requirements for 166 25th Street. (Attachment E)

   Mr. Jared Allen indicated this building has been vacant for nearly a decade, and it is his desire to create eight
   short-term residential units. He stated on-site parking is important in attracting people to stay in the area, but
   the site does not have space to accommodate the required eight parking spaces. This request is to reduce
   required parking to four on-site parking spaces.


                                                                                                     Page 3 of 13
Ms. Corbridge stated the request is to reduce required parking from eight stalls to four stalls in order to allow
residential use of the building. In addition to parking, the site also must accommodate a dumpster and
landscaping. She stated the proposed lot is adjacent to a City-owned (RDA) lot, but no approval has been
given for designated parking for private use of buildings along Historic 25 th Street. She also identified the
location of an access easement extending from the RDA lot to the private property, and indicated the site plan
has been reviewed and approved by the Landmarks Commission subject to sidewalk being provided between
the parking and the building, with on-site landscaping shown at 13%.

In considering a reduction of parking space, the Commission is to determine the peak need is at different
times of day, will not cause a shortage of parking for other uses located in the vicinity; and will contribute to
the vitality and overall well-being of the downtown, as well as whether off-site parking may be available for the
use. The Ordinance requirement is for one space per dwelling unit, and the request is for four spaces. She
indicated peak usage of parking spaces in this area is during evening hours, and parking may or may not
always be available in the RDA lot. She stated the Building Code requires at least one ADA stall, and the
dimension available for a parking lot limits parking to three spaces. She also indicated the location of the
dumpster limits the availability of one space which is shown on the applicant’s plan. As it currently exists, the
site could accommodate only two parking spaces which would line up with the existing access easement
owned by the RDA. Staff suggests the applicant could work with the RDA to widen the access easement so
the site could accommodate three parking spaces. Staff recommendation is for approval of reducing the
required parking spaces, with the following options:
A) allow three stalls and direct the applicant to work with the RDA (Redevelopment Agency) to obtain
     approval of amending the plat to widen the access easement aligned with the stalls, or
B) allow two stalls and work with staff to meet parking, site, and building requirements
In addition, the site plan approval is subject to the following:
  • Work with staff on the final landscaping design with pedestrian access paths, plants, and trees.
  • Meet the Landmark conditions of approval, including using brick as the main material for the dumpster
      enclosure, and to match the brick of the existing historic building.
  • Meet all department staff review comments, including meeting the ADA parking stall requirements.

Commissioner Safsten expressed concern about reducing parking spaces as these are valuable to both
tenants and customers of the area. Ms. Corbridge responded the Ordinance allows for shared parking in the
CBD and it is anticipated the adjacent lot would likely have spaces available for parking for the businesses
along Historic 25th Street.

4. Consideration of Variation of number of materials and colors, for proposed Senior Housing apartments at
   2955 Washington Boulevard. (Attachment F)

  Mr. Bill Knowlton stated he is requesting a variation in colors and materials as allowed in the Ordinance
  regulating multi-family structures in commercial zones. He stated the architectural theme of the building is
  compatible with its location and other existing buildings in the area. He stated the main entrance is defined by
  white columns on Washington Boulevard and is inviting to both tenants and guests. He stated the proposed
  colors and materials create a theme for the building and fits with surrounding buildings.

  Mr. Montgomery stated the Commission and Council had recently adopted ordinances governing multi-family
  uses in commercial zones, and this is the first test case since its implementation. The Ordinance allows two
  colors and two materials but allows the Planning Commission to vary that amount if it finds the request is
  consistent with the architectural theme of the building, is compatible with the context of the building location
  and surrounding buildings, and the colors and materials do not create a visual conflict to the visual qualities of
  the street. This request is to allow up to two additional colors and materials, with the use of brick, stenciled
  stucco, vertical metal siding and two types of cement siding. Colors proposed are eggshell white for the flat
  finish, gray metal siding, natural cedar for lap fiberboard and gray brick and stenciled stucco. If the gray colors
  are the same tone, the request would add only one color, but if the tones vary as is shown on the materials
  board, the request would be for two additional color. He expressed concern that the diagram shows some
  materials extending to the ground while the Ordinance requires the ground floor material to be brick or stone.
  He also questioned the use of metal siding on the ends of the building and asked about its purpose, stating it
  does not seem to have a function or fit with the rhythm of the building when it is on the ends only. He asked
  Mr. Knowlton to address these two questions: why the metal is used on the ends, and whether material,
  particularly stucco, extends to the ground floor of the building.

  Mr. Hooper Knowlton stated he is not opposed to brick on the ground level as it does produce better durability
  over time. He stated the metal adds variety and texture to the design and is compatible with materials of the
  adjacent service station. Mr. Bill Knowlton stated the intent is to provide affordable housing for seniors, and
  the increased cost of architectural elements, particularly brick, would necessitate an increase in rent prices.

  Mr. John Shirley, architect for the building, stated the building is surrounded by landscaping, and stucco would
  not be in areas where there are walkways where it would become damaged. He stated the use of metal was
  intended to provide a separation in the massing of the building and to better identify the building as a
  wayfinding measure.

  Ms. Angel Castillo expressed support of the variation of materials stating affordable housing is important to
  the community. She stated many buildings in the community have more than two colors, stating color and
  texture adds to its character.

  Commissioner Southwick felt the building is well designed and fits well with its surroundings. He stated likes
  the colors and materials and feels it creates a sense of ownership and identity. He felt brick should be
  required on the ground level as it is more durable and does not become damaged from moisture or use.

                                                                                                   Page 5 of 13
  Commissioner Schade felt the metal should be a lighter shade of gray so that it matches the brick and stucco
  colors. Commissioner Safsten stated there is a long-term benefit for solid materials on the ground floor,
  particularly in high traffic areas. While not opposed to the metal extending to the ground, he felt stucco
  should not be allowed on the ground level. Commissioner Garner expressed concern with the use of metal on
  the south side of the building as it would attract heat.

  Mr. Shirley stated the metal is in a recessed area and well shaded, indicating there is no concern about heat or
  reflection. Mr. Bill Knowlton asked if the Commission were considering the historic use of stucco or the new
  stucco material containing polymers. He also stated he is not opposed to the lighter metal color to match the
  brick color. Mr. Hooper Knowlton stated he is not opposed to brick being extended to the ground all around
  the building as it improves durability and extends the life of the project.

  Mr. Montgomery stated the use of metal is appropriate to define the stair towers on the building.

   MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Southwick to approve the request for additional building
           materials and colors subject to brick being extended to the ground level on all sides of the building
           except for where metal is used which can be used on the ground level. Stucco is not allowed at the
           first level of the building. The applicant is to work with Staff to address the color of the metal to
           match the brick, with the colors and materials otherwise as proposed by the applicant. Motion was
           seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously, with Commissioners Blaisdell,
           Boykin, Garner, Safsten, Schade, Southwick, Stoker and Sandau voting aye.
