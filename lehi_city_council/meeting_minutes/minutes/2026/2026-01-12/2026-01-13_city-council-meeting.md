2.3) Open Public Meetings Act (OPMA) Training- City Attorney
      Ryan Wood presented the training to the Council and provided an overview of the Open
      and Public Meetings Act (OPMA), emphasizing that public business must be conducted
      openly so the public can see both decisions and deliberations. He explained the
      differences between a public meeting and a public hearing, and defin ed key terms like
      public body, quorum, and what constitutes a “meeting”. Using hypotheticals, he clarified
      when casual or social gatherings are not meetings and when they can effectively become
      meetings if council business is discussed. He also outlined when and how the council
      may go into a closed session (e.g., personnel, litigation, real estate), the rules for
      electronic communication between members, notice and agenda requirements, and the
      penalties and risks for violations.

2.4) Presentation of the Transportation Impact Fee Facilities Plan and Analysis- Bowen Collins
      and Associates
      Rachel, with Bowen, Collins, and Associates, presented on the transportation impact fee
      update. The transportation impact fee update uses the Transportation Master Plan to set
      how much new development should pay toward road projects needed in the next 10
      years. Based on how much each project benefits existing users versus new growth, the
      consultant recommended a per-trip fee of $146.87, implemented as a schedule that raises
      today’s fee by about 10% in 2026 (to roughly $134 per trip) and about 20% by 2035. The
      council discussed the need to update the fee regularly (about every two years) to ref lect
      inflation and changing project costs.

2.7) Discussion on establishing the Lehi Small Business Council
      Councilor Harrison proposed creating a Lehi Small Business Council—an unpaid
      advisory group of Lehi small business owners, including home‑based businesses. Its role
      would be to meet regularly, gather input from small businesses about red tape, licensing,
      zoning, and other city processes, and then report to the City Council (e.g., twice a year)
      with concrete recommendations on how the city can better support them.

       Council members generally supported the idea and added that the small business council
       should represent multiple industries (retail, food, manufacturing, etc.). They discussed
       modeling it after other advisory boards, having a council liaison, and involving staff
       (likely Economic Development / Community Development). Jason Walker said staff
       would research how other cities structure similar councils, examine successful models
       and bylaws, and return with a recommended framework for the council to consider and
       formally establish later.

2.8) City Council and Planning Commission Procedures for Resignation/Vacancy

Lehi City Council Meeting                       5                               January 13, 2026
       The council spent time reviewing how resignations and vacancies are handled for both
       the City Council and the Planning Commission, using the recent appointments as context.

       For City Council vacancies, City Attorney Ryan explained that the process is controlled
       by state law, not city code. A resigning council member should submit a written notice to
       the legislative body (the full council), the city must give at least 14 days’ public notice of
       the vacancy, and then the council fills the vacancy in an open meeting. Ryan
       acknowledged that in December, some steps weren’t executed perfectly (e.g., the
       resignation letter first going to the mayor and then to the council, but characte rized these
       as technical (“harmless”) errors rather than defects that would invalidate the appointment.
       The purpose of the law, council notice, and an open appointment was still met.

       For the Planning Commission, Ryan noted that its bylaws require members intending to
       resign to give written notice to the City Council, the Planning Commission, and the
       Mayor, with a resignation date that allows time to appoint a replacement. There was
       concern that members said they did not see a resignation notice until the night of the
       appointment, and only later realized the bylaws required broader notice. Ryan again
       treated this as a procedural miss, not something that would undo the commission
       appointment.

       Several council members stressed that process matters and that they want future
       vacancies handled strictly according to statute and bylaws. Councilor Stallings raised
       concern that, during the December council appointment, the mayor stopped the vote once
       a majority was reached, preventing her and another member from formally voting; Ryan
       accepted responsibility, saying his advice about ending “rounds” was misunde rstood by
       the Mayor as ending the entire vote. Both Councilor Harrison and Councilor Freeman
       emphasized that even under a perfect process, they still would have supported appointing
       Councilor Lockhart, and multiple members publicly affirmed that her seat is legitimate
       and that the intent of this discussion was to clarify and improve procedures going
       forward, not to challenge her appointment.

2.9) Discussion on Request to the State for Equitable Transportation Investment and
      Local Flexibility in Housing Implementation
      The council discussed a draft resolution aimed at (1) seeking more equitable state
      transportation investment in northwestern Utah County and (2) pushing back on state
      mandates around local housing and zoning.

       Councilor Harrison had drafted a resolution pointing out that Utah County’s high growth
       and economic contribution are not matched by transportation funding, especially when
       compared with Weber and Davis Counties. He stressed that Lehi and neighboring cities
       (Eagle Mountain, Saratoga Springs, American Fork) urgently need major road
       investments—like 1900 South and better I‑15/Redwood connections—and that, at the
       same time, the Legislature is increasingly pre‑empting cities’ land use and zoning
       authority with new housing mandates. There was discussion to possibly split the draft
       into two resolutions- one on transportation, one on housing/zoning).
