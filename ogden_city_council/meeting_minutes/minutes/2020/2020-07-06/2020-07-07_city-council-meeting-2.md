Proposed Ordinance 2020-28 designating the Stone Farmhouse, located at
301 West 2nd Street, as an historic resource
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a historical designation action came
before the Council for consideration. The memo stated petitioners Anna and Michael Keogh are proposing the home and property
located at 301 West 2nd Street, known as the Stone Farm, be listed as an historic resource on the Ogden City Register of Historic
Places. The designation would require any significant changes proposed for the building, including demolition, to be approved by
the Landmarks Commission through the approval of a Certificate of Historic Appropriateness. The designation would help ensure
the preservation of the property and would help to ensure that any changes proposed would maintain the character and historicity
of the home and property. The City's Landmarks Commission has reviewed the proposed designation and has forwarded a
recommendation of approval finding that the building meets both the historical and architectural criteria listed in the City's code
for local designation.
          Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
nomination to include the subject property on the register of historic places. He used the aid of an aerial photograph to identify the
location of the property, which is part of the historic Stone Farm. The home on the property was constructed in 1925 on a unique
fort remains foundation. There are four accessory structures, including a garage, chicken coop, three-sided tractor shed, and
workshop. The homeowner has approved the nomination. When considering the nomination, there is a focus on historic
significance of the property and below are the fmdings of historic significance:
              •    The five Stone Farm buildings were built in the 20's and 30's and tell a story of the property being used as a
                   diversified farm.
              • The property was a first-rate dairy farm, transitioning to diversified farming of sugar beets, hay, and wheat.
              •    Further transitions included from horses to tractors.
              •    The Stone farmhouse was a modern, new bungalow.
          The architectural significance of the primary dwelling unit was also considered; the Landmarks Commission found it is a
one-story bungalow constructed with a bungalow roof; it has two gables on the north fac;:ade and gabled ends include arts and
crafts elements with half-timbered design of wood and plaster. There are original doors and windows on the main level and an
original enclosed frame porch altered in the 80s with new windows and siding. The Landmarks Commission voted unanimously to
recommend approval of the property at 301 West 2 nd Street to be in the local historic register based on the property's historic and
architectural significance.
          Council member Stephens asked who is responsible to maintain the building and property in the future if historical
designation is granted. Mr. Montgomery stated that the property is private and should be maintained by the owner.

Proposed Ordinance 2020-29 amending the Zoning Map of Ogden City
(Code 15-3-3) to reclassify property generally located at 4400 South
Harrison Boulevard as Community Commercial Zone (C-2)
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed rezone of 4400 South
Harrison Boulevard came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated a petition has been filed to rezone just under
three acres of property located to the southeast of the 4400 South Harrison Boulevard intersection. The proposed rezone would
reclassify the property from R-3 (Multi-family Residential) to C-2 (Community Commercial). The petitionerrequesting the rezone
has indicated that the intent is to develop a hotel on the site. The petitioner has been working with Weber State University (WSU)
to develop the property. The rezone to C-2 is consistent with the proposed Southeast Ogden Community Plan, scheduled for
Council consideration on July 14, 2020.
          Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
purpose of the petition; if the zone change is approved, the petitioner would like to proceed with development of a 100-room hotel
adjacent to WSU. The area to be rezoned is 2.9 acres and contains five parcels; the rezone would require re-platting of the
properties. The applicant is working with WSU to provide hotel amenities close to the campus similar to the type of layouts
present on several college campuses throughout the State and the nation. He presented an aerial photograph of the property to
illustrate its proximity to the Dee Events Center, the future Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) route, and stonn water infrastructure. The
Planning Commission considered compliance with the City's General Plan and zoning policies; they examined whether the rezone
would strengthen the cmmnercial edge between Harrison Boulevard and the University. They found the area includes several
adjacent commercial (C-2) properties and the rezone will result in split zoning on parcels, which is why the new plat would be
required to correct incongruent zoning boundaries. They also considered whether the petition is consistent with the Southeast
Ogden Community Plan, which has been updated, but not yet officially adopted by the Council. The proposed rezone is suppmted
in the recent update of the Plan, which calls for C-2/R-3 and transit-oriented development (TOD) zoning options on the property.
The Planning Commission voted seven to two to recommend approval of the rezone from R-3 to C-2 with the following condition:
the applicant will re-plat the property to address split zoning. The two Commissioners who voted in opposition to the
recommendation communicated that they feel the prope1ty should be student housing rather than a hotel use and they were also
concerned about tree removal on the property.
           Council member Hyer asked ifthere is any use allowed in the C-2 zone that would not be appropriate for this location.
Mr. Montgomery stated that in this case, it is important to keep in mind that the sale of the land is contingent upon WSU having
the authority to approve the proposed use before the sale is completed.

Proposed Resolution 2020-19 approving the form of the lease/purchase
agreement with Zion Bancorporation and authorizing execution and delivery
          A memo from the Management Services Department came before the Council to consider a resolution regarding a fleet
lease program. The memo stated Administration is proposing that the City enter a lease purchase agreement, or capital lease, with
Zions Bank to fund the purchase of heavy equipment to support various City services and functions.
          Treasurer Johnson summarized the memo and stated the City has leased several key pieces of heavy equipment from
Wheeler Machinery for the past four years. This agreement has been an operating lease or a rental agreement where the equipment
is returned to Wheeler at the end of the term of the agreement. As the City was reviewing the benefit of continuing this practice
and renewing a new operating lease or rental agreement for this equipment, the City also evaluated the cost and benefit of
purchasing the equipment. With the help of the City's financial advisor, Lewis Young Robertson and Burningham (L YRB), the
City issued a request for interest rate proposal to fund the purchase of equipment. The proposal asked respondents to provide a six
year and eight-year amortization. The City received four competitive proposals and it was detennined Zion's Bankcorp offered the
best terms. Upon the approval of the resolution, the City will enter into a lease in the amount of$1,003,000 to fund the purchase
of$983,900 in equipment outlined below and the fees associated with the debt instrument, this will be a six-year capital lease with
an annual interest rate of 1.99 percent. At the end of the lease, the City estimates the equipment will still have a useful life of
approximately four years. The annual payments under these te1ms will be approximately$ 190,000, with the exception of year one,
which will be approximately $90,000. The lease payments with the costs ofan annual maintenance agreement for this equipment
will increase the cost to the City by approximately $16,000 annually over the operating lease. The City will absorb these costs
through the current replacement budget in fleet. By entering into a capital lease, the City will build equity that can be used to help
fund the replacement of this equipment in future years.
          Laura Lewis of L YRB reviewed the lease financing sources and uses; she summarized the debt payment schedule
highlighting the amount of principal and interest paid in each fiscal year from 2021 to 2026; the total interest paid in that time
frame is $76,322, or 2.06 percent. Financing fees for the lease total $10,000.
          Council member Lopez asked for more infonnation regarding the timing of the equipment purchases and the procurement
process the City will follow. Ms. Johnson stated that the equipment will be purchased from Wheeler Equipment, which is a
company listed on the State Contract for purchasing; they will have some of the equipment available for purchase over the next
several months.
