         The purpose of the work session was to review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.;
discuss the proposed Fiscal year (FY) 2025-2026 budget specific to enterprise fund transfers, proposed Schedule A-1, and the
Capital Improvement Plan (CIP); discuss the FY 2024-2025 Third Quarter Financial Report and CIP Report; discuss Council
business; and hear Council comments.

Agenda Review
        Chair White and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Proposed Ogden City Fiscal Year 2025-2026 Budget
          Enterprise Fund Transfers: Finance Director Sorensen explained Utah Code Annotated §10-6-135.5 requires that
notice be sent to all users when a City is proposing to transfer funds from an enterprise fund to another fund for purposes “not
directly related to the goods or services provided by the enterprise for which the enterprise fund was created.” Ogden City’s notice
is posted on the state’s Public Notice Website and included with the City’s utility bills. The City’s utility billing system works on
a four-week rotation with one quarter of the users receiving their bill each week. Noticing began in early May and will conclude in
early June. This ensures that all utility users received notice at least one week prior to the hearing scheduled for June 17, 2025.
The proposed budget includes transfers of $3,475,925 from water, $1,835,650 from sanitary sewer, $799,425 from storm sewer,
and $935,700 from refuse. The transfers are equivalent to franchise and property taxes paid by non-governmental utilities.
          Council member Richey inquired as to how long the City has been transferring funds from the Enterprise Funds to the
General Fund. Mr. Soresnen indicated he has been with the City for 10 years and the transfers have been occurring that entire
time; he is not sure how long they were occurring before then. The State only started requiring this type of notice 11 years ago.
Council member Richey asked if the rates used to calculate the transfer amounts have been the same for that time. Mr. Sorensen
answered yes. He then facilitated a review of the public notice provided to all utility customers regarding the proposed transfers.
          Chair White stated that each year the Council is asked why the money is not simply left in the enterprise funds. Mr.
Sorensen stated leaving the money in the enterprise funds would support the City’s utilities operations, but it would leave the
General Fund in a deficit. The City commissioned a study from Lewis, Robertson, Burningham (LRB) regarding the transfers;
they were asked to determine whether the five percent of utility revenues that are transferred to the General Fund annually is
reasonably related to the actual cost of the administrative services (HR, accounting, legal, etc.) provided to the water, sewer, storm
and refuse utility operations. LRB was also asked to analyze the impact on the General Fund and utility funds if the utility
transfers were discontinued. LRBs analysis showed that the five percent administrative fee transferred to the General Fund was
justified by the services provided to the utilities. Their analysis also showed that a significant increase in property taxes would be
required to replace the General Fund revenue lost from the utility transfers. Vice Chair Hyer added that another benefit of the
transfer is that all utility customers are required to pay into the system; if the burden were shifted to those paying property taxes,
non-profit entities or others that are exempt from paying property taxes would not be charged. Mr. Sorensen stated that is correct.
           The Council engaged in discussion regarding the implications of future rate increases in terms of the potential for the
transfer amount to increase as well; there was also a focus on the administrative services that are partially funded by the enterprise
fund transfers.
           General Discussion – Proposed Budget, Proposed Schedule A-1: Council Assistant Executive Director Symes used the
aid of a PowerPoint presentation to present possible changes to the budget via Schedule A-1:
                • Increase merit increase from 1.5% to 2%
                • Non-sworn public safety employee mental health services
                • Increase to ongoing arts maintenance funding
                • Trails position, partnerships with trails orgs
                • Schedule A-1 changes from Administration
           Mr. Symes facilitated Council discussion of each of the items listed above to determine if there is support among the
Council for the change. The Council determined more discussion is needed on the item related to merit increases; most supported
the increase of the merit amount from 1.5 to two percent; they asked for more information and financial data on bullet items two
and three on the list and, regarding bullet item two, briefly discussed the fact that the City already provides some mental health
programs to general employees; for bullet item four, they favored partnering with trails organizations rather than creating a new
employment position to oversee trails maintenance.
           Finance Director Sorensen then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize Administration’s proposed A-1
changes to the budget. The total adjustments in the General Fund and Major Grant Fund are $179,675 and $71,750, respectively,
resulting in a total Ogden City Budget of $266,969,850. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet
for the meeting.
           General Discussion – Capital Improvement Plan: Council Senior Policy Analyst Burton stated the Council has been
revieing Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) proposals over the course of the past several weeks as part of the review and discussion
of each Department budget. The Council has expressed their CIP funding priorities for FY2025 to City Administration and the
intent of tonight’s agenda item is to hear any final input before the CIP proposal is finalized as part of the final budget proposal for
FY 2025-2026.
           Vice Chair Hyer stated he does not have any concerns about the projects included in the FY26 budget, but one project
was taken out of the CIP because it did not meet certain criteria for being included in the Plan; he would like to know what needs
to be done to add that project back in, which is a traffic signal on Monroe Boulevard. Council staff will work with Administration
to propose an amendment to the CIP to get that project added back into the document. Council member Myers stated he thought
that traffic signal was part of EN017. Vice Chair Hyer stated it was, but the Mt. Lewis Community Plan calls for an evaluation of
a roundabout at the intersection, rather than a traffic signal and if a traffic signal is preferred, the General Plan and Community
Plan need to be amended. Public Services Executive Director Anderson indicated he needs to follow-up with the Utah Department
of Transportation (UDOT) to learn of their final plans for traffic improvements in that area; any signalization of intersections
along Washington Boulevard will trigger the need for signals along Monroe Boulevard as well.
           There was brief discussion about CIP projects that include parks, restroom, and pavilion improvements in the coming
FY; Mr. Anderson stated that Administration prioritizes park improvements projects each year and he is happy to provide a full
report of project plans and the redirection of unused CIP parks funds to the Council.

Council Business
          Council member Myers stated he was approached by a resident who is interested in purchasing City owned property in
the Ogden Canyon and he inquired as to who to direct the resident to. Public Services Executive Director Anderson stated the City
has purchased several properties in the Canyon in an effort to protect critical infrastructure and provide for access to the properties
during construction projects. The City plans to retain ownership of these properties until all work is complete in the Canyon. This
led to discussion of the party that owns the property that is used to access the old railroad line through the Canyon.
