         2.1. 13800 South Property Ownership Options - Blake Thomas, Community Development
              Director
Community Development Director Blake Thomas explained Herriman City was deeded property along the
existing portion of 13800 South located in Riverton and Bluffdale, which was expected to extend west and
connect with the Mountain View Corridor Northbound, bisecting the Mountain Ridge development between
Riverton and Herriman. Herriman City was unaware of receiving this property by deed until it was brought to
their attention after the deed was recorded. The parcel owned by Herriman City included the north half of 13800
South from 4000 West to the Welby-Jacobs Canal, a portion of unpaved roadway in Bluffdale, and approximately
1120 sq. ft. of private property. He presented a map to orient the Council of the location of property. Riverton
City had requested Herriman City deed the property over to them so they could install improvements across the
canal and into the Mountain Ridge Development.

Mayor Watts asked how they acquired ownership of the property and Director Thomas explained that South
Farm LLC was acquired by Rosecrest Communities, and a deed was recorded on October 20, 2011. On February
1, 2018, Rosecrest deeded the property to the City. Mayor Watts asked why it was deeded to them instead of
Riverton, and Director Thomas replied that he wasn’t sure of the reason. Since it was not a special warranty
deed, the City did not have to sign for it. Since it was deeded through a deed, the City would have to go through
the surplus process.
December 9, 2020 – City Council Minutes                                                              Page 3 of 14

Director Thomas briefly reviewed the surplus process. According to the City ordinance, if the value of the
property was less than $250,000, where they were required to send public notice 14 days in advance of the
surplus. At the end of that timeframe, the Council would make a formal vote on the surplus.

Councilmember Smith asked who had been paying the cost of maintenance on that parcel, and Director Thomas
said Riverton City had paved it and they had paid for paving treatments.

Director Thomas stated there were three options for the Council to consider. First, Herriman City could
maintain ownership of the property. Second, the City could deed the property to Riverton and allow them to
move forward with road construction, and surplus what was not needed to either Bluffdale or an adjacent
property owner. The last option was to do a combination of the previous options. Director Thomas said it was
up to the Council to determine the highest and best use of the property.

Councilmember Ohrn asked what was available for trade. Director Thomas replied the only property that
Riverton owned in Herriman that he was aware of was the property by the tanks on Rosecrest Plat X, but they
were waiting for a UDOT right of way assessment.

Councilmember Smith asked City Attorney Chase Andrizzi for his opinion on the gain for both cities and how
to deal with the issue. Attorney Andrizzi replied they needed an appraisal of the roadway to determine the fair
market value and assess the maintenance costs. It might be easier to get rid of the property to alleviate the City
from maintenance liability. Regardless of what the Council decided to do, the guidelines of the SOP and
ordinance still needed to be followed. Councilmember Shields commented they did not want to take any steps
that would prevent the road from being completed, because they needed that road to connect. Mayor Watts
suggested deeding the land to Riverton with a road easement. Director Thomas stressed the need to preserve
the easement so that the road could be completed.

Councilmember Smith asked how this process differed from the Bluffdale property. Director Thomas explained
Bluffdale had insisted on paying for the property so that they truly owned it, and there were no “favors” involved.
Councilmember Ohrn said they shouldn’t assume they got the subject property for nothing, and suggested they
take a deeper look into it.

Director Thomas reiterated the ordinance was clear on the surplus process, once the value was determined. Staff
would have the property appraised and get that information back to the Council.

        9.1. Discussion and consideration for the adoption of a resolution authorizing the issuance and
              sale of not more than $25,000,000 aggregate principal amount of water revenue and
              refunding bonds, Series 2021; and related matters - Alan Rae, Finance Director
Finance Director Alan Rae reported the Water Department had proposed a number of projects in a parameter’s
resolution, including the following:

             North Herriman Water Improvements $ 1,575,000
             East Herriman Water Improvements 12,000,000
             Additional East Herriman Water Improvement 2,500,000
             Juniper Canyon Water Improvements 2,400,000
             Zone 4 Pump Station Upgrades 550,000
             Zone 5 VFD Pump Station 1,100,000

The total bonding amount would be $25,000,000 with interest not to exceed 3-percent, and partially used to
refund two existing Water Revenue Bonds from 2015 and 2017. The proposed term of the bond would not
exceed 21 years. The total payment on the new bonds was estimated to be $1,800,000 annually for the first
eleven years and approximately $1,250,000 for the remaining years. The City did not want to extend the life of
the two refunding bonds, so they would be amortized separately. Refunding Water Revenue Bonds 2015 and
2017 would result in a net present value savings of an estimated 6.761-percent net present value. These bonds
would be water revenue bonds, meaning that the revenue from the water fund would be the security for the
repayment. Director Rae explained the process prior to the issuance of the bond.

Councilmember Shields noted there would not be an increase to water fees to cover this bond.

Councilmember Ohrn added she was uneasy about bonding for $25 Million, but there were critical infrastructure
needs within the City. If they funded projects as they brought in money, they would do so at more of an expense.
The bond rates were low, so they could save money in the long run by bonding now.

Councilmember Shields relayed a desire from the residents to build infrastructure ahead of growth and
development, not after. He thought there was more they could have bonded for and he supported the bond.

Councilmember Ohrn emphasized this was not anticipated revenue from anticipated growth.

        9.5. Discussion and consideration of a resolution confirming that Herriman City shall not
               provide the services proposed to be provided by the Herriman City Fire Service Area -
               Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi explained a resolution was created in connection with the local district act when a
new local district was created. It required the creating entity to pass a resolution outlining their intent to provide
the service or not. There has been discussion of creating the City’s own fire district but it was equally as
beneficial, if not more-so, to combine fire service within the HCFSA local district, and different services which
would be permitted under the local district act. The City would collect tax under the City’s own service district
that USFA was currently using for collection. Residents would not see an increase. This proposal served to
formalize that the City was not providing those services and would continue to contract with UFA to provide
the services by collecting taxes through the HSCEA.

services, and Attorney Andrizzi answered negatively. Councilmember Shields asked what the circumstances

Councilmember Ohrn clarified that on resident’s bill they would see an increase in the Herriman City safety and

police fees. Budget-wise, the City was very cognoscente that these were two very different pockets. Attorney
Andrizzi commented that the current benefit was that the rate they had with UFA was established by contract,
so they would know exactly what fund they would need to satisfy that obligation, which made budgeting easier.
