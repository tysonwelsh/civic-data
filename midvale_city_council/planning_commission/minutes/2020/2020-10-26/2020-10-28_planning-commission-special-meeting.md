Property Rights: In General
What are property rights?
Property rights are legally protected entitlements for real property:
• Right to possession;
• Right to control;
• Right to use and quiet enjoyment;
• Right to allow others the right to use (licenses and leases)
• Right to privacy and to exclude others
• Right to disposition or transfer the property to someone else
• Right to use property as collateral

Whose rights are protected?
Whoever owns them.

Planning Commission: Midvale
Midvale Municipal Code 17-4-3. Powers and duties.
The planning commission shall:
A. General Plan. Prepare and recommend a general plan and amendments to the general plan to
the city council;
B. Zoning. Recommend zoning ordinances and maps, and amendments to zoning ordinances and
maps, to the city council;
C. Subdivision Regulation. Recommend subdivision regulations and amendments to those
regulations to the city council;
D. Subdivision Applications. Recommend approval or denial of subdivision applications as provided
in this chapter;
E. Conditional Use Permits. Approve or deny conditional use permit applications; and
F. Other Duties. The planning commission shall have all of the powers and duties explicitly or
impliedly given planning commissions by the laws of the state.

Decisions: Hypothetical
A developer owns several single-family lots
adjacent to the City Park at the corner of 7500 S
and Jefferson. The developer has proposed
tearing down the existing homes and building a
20-story apartment in their place. In order to
complete this project, the developer has
proposed a new zone. The neighbors are
concerned about the impact on the
neighborhood. Do you have to approve the
developer’s proposed zone and project?

• No. This is a legislative decision. You have the
  discretion to deny the request.
Decisions: Hypothetical
A developer owns several single-family lots at
the corner of 7200 S and 300 E. The developer
has proposed tearing down the existing homes
and building a 20-story apartment in their place.
This type of development is permitted as a
conditional use. The neighbors are concerned
about the impact on the neighborhood. Can you
deny the developer’s proposal?

• Probably not. You can only deny the proposal
  if the developer cannot adequately mitigate
  the impacts of any objective conditional use
  requirements.
Decisions: Hypothetical
A developer has submitted a subdivision
application for approval. The layout is
atrocious, but it meets all the standards of
Midvale’s subdivision ordinance. You think
that some tweaks to the layout would be a
significant improvement for the future
residents. Can you condition your approval
on compliance with your suggestions?

• No. If the subdivision meets all of the
  objective requirements, you must
  approve it. However, you can still make
  your suggestions to the applicant.
Decisions: Hypothetical
A developer has submitted two applications for the corner of
Nicoletti Drive and Ramanee Drive: a rezone request and a large-
scale master plan. The developer is requesting that the 7200 South
Overlay be extended to his single-family properties. The developer
wants to create a mixed-use development. The large-scale master
plan complies with the ordinance. You like the concept but don’t
think this is the right location. Do you have to approve the rezone
because the large-scale master plan complies with the ordinance?

• No. These are two separate decisions. The rezone application is
  a legislative decision. The LSMP is an administrative decision.
  You must only approve the LSMP if you approve the rezone.
