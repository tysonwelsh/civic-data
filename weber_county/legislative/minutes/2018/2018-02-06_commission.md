    7. CREDIT AGREEMENT BY/AMONG UTAH SMALL BUSINESS GROWTH INITIATIVE, LLC, DBA, BUSINESS
       LOANS OF UTAH AS BORROWER, UTAH SMALL BUSINESS GROWTH INITIATIVE, LLC, DBA BUSINESS
       LOANS OF UTAH AS ADMINISTRATIVE AGENT, UTAH SMALL BUSINESS GROWTH INITIATIVE LLC,
       DBA BUSINESS LOANS OF UTAH AS CLASS A LENDER, CITY OF OREM, OGDEN CITY CORPORATION,
       PROVO CITY, SPANISH FORK CITY, WEBER COUNTY AS CLASS B LENDERS & UTAH SMALL BUSINESS
       GROWTH INITIATIVE LLC, DBA BUSINESS LOANS OF UTAH AS A CLASS C LENDER.
         Holin Wilbanks, noted that a few weeks ago the Commission adopted a resolution that paved the way to
         participation in the Small Business program. This is the ensuing credit agreement. Michael Plaizier, Utah
         Center for Neighborhood Stabilization Executive Director, outlined the program stating that the U.S.
         government signed the Small Business Jobs Act of 2010 after the recession and gave States money for small
         business lending. His organization was a successful bidder and did loans, guarantees, etc. The program
         ended in 2016. He said that their board saw the program’s success and the need of small businesses to obtain
         financing in amounts between $50,000-$250,000. They work with cities and counties giving them a way to
         lend to small businesses without having to staff the program. For every $50,000 a city or county puts in
         they get access to $350,000 to loan to small businesses. The county will have a say whether the business is
         a good fit and then authorize for underwriting. He outlined the voting and veto powers of the committee
         members.
         Commissioner Ebert moved to approve the Credit agreement by and among Utah Small Business Growth
         Initiative, LLC, DBA, Business Loans of Utah as Borrower, Utah Small Business Growth Initiative, LLC,
         DBA Business Loans of Utah as Administrative Agent, Utah Small Business Growth Initiative LLC, DBA
         Business Loans of Utah as Class A Lender, City of Orem, Ogden City Corporation, Provo City, Spanish
         Fork City, Weber County as Class B Lenders and Utah Small Business Growth Initiative LLC, DBA
         Business Loans of Utah as a Class C Lender; Commissioner Gibson seconded.
         Commissioner Ebert – aye; Commissioner Gibson – aye

    2. PUBLIC HEARING REGARDING A PROPOSAL TO AMEND THE FOLLOWING WEBER COUNTY CODE SECTIONS:
       DEFINITIONS (§101-1-7), ADDITIONS AND ENLARGEMENTS (§108-12-3), SUBDIVISION PLAT REQUIREMENTS
       FOR NONCONFORMING LOTS; EXEMPTIONS (§108-12-11), AND ENLARGING NONCONFORMING LOTS (§108-12-
       12). THE PROPOSED AMENDMENT WILL ALLOW PARCELS CREATED PRIOR TO JULY 1, 1992 CONTAINING A
       PERMITTED SINGLE FAMILY DWELLING TO BE CONSIDERED A LOT OF RECORD.

         Charles Ewert, of the County Planning Division, noted that there is a small amount of lots in western Weber
         County and in the Ogden Valley that have existing single family dwellings that have been permitted but the
         property boundaries have changed making them potentially illegally-created nonconforming lots. Today’s
         action designates those lots prior to 1992, when the State code changed to require subdivisions for all
         changes of this nature, as non-conforming lots. Commissioner Ebert wanted to ensure that in making them

    3. PUBLIC HEARING REGARDING A PROPOSAL TO AMEND THE FOLLOWING COUNTY CODE SECTIONS:
       DEFINITIONS (§101-1-7), CONDITIONAL USES (§104-22-3, §104-23-3, §104-24-3, §104-25-3) TO DEFINE
       OUTDOOR STORAGE & TO INCLUDE IT AS A CONDITIONAL USE IN THE M-1, MV-1, M-2, & M-3 ZONES.
         Charles Ewert, of the County Planning Division, explained that this is to allow outdoor storage in the
         manufacturing zones. Currently, the county does not have zones that allow outdoor storage—contractor
         storage yards, RV yard storage, etc.—for commercial gain.
    4. Public comments: Commissioner Gibson invited public comments on the public hearings. Brent Hipwell,
       of West Weber, expressed concern with nonconforming lots if those properties or adjacent properties would
       be involved in rezoning, subdivision or clustering. He said that this should be addressed to make them
       conforming lots. He also expressed concern with outdoor storage stating that farm equipment could fall
       under that category. Mr. Ewert stated that the agriculture zones allow storage of agriculture equipment
       onsite; the above item is for commercial/manufacturing types of outdoor storage. Mr. Hipwell asked who
       makes the determination whether it is construction or farming. Mr. Ewert spoke of regulations and said that
       if someone in an agriculture area stored unrelated farm equipment that would be outdoor storage. He further
       addressed the commissioners’ questions regarding nonconforming lots and outdoor storage.
