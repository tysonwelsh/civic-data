Ogden/Weber Convention and Visitors Bureau 2020 Annual Report
           The City Council has requested to receive information from local foundations and nonprofit organizations that have a
contractual agreement with Ogden City. These presentations help the Council to become more familiar with the objectives and
efforts ofthe organizations.
           Sara Toliver, President/Chief Executive Officer of Visit Ogden, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to describe the
Convention and Visitors Bureau's (CVB) organizational mission and goals, successes and challenges over the past year, and
future plans for the organization. Early in 2020, there was great momentum in the community surrounding tourism and special
events; in January 2020, Ogden was in the media listed among the 50 best cities in America for starting a business in 2020. The
New York Times wrote an article about visiting northern Utah to ski at Snowbasin and Powder Mountain ski resorts. The CVB
team grew in March of2020 as it welcomed a Director of Marketing and Director of Sales, but shortly thereafter the COVID-19
pandemic began to impact everything. The ski season ended early, marketing was halted, events were cancelled, and there was
little to no tourism activity in the community. CVB marketing materials used the term 'unprecedented circumstances' for the first
time in relation to adjusting programming responsive to COVID-19. The CVB began to pivot to respond to travel trends
influenced by CO VID-19; one-in-five American travelers reported taking a stay cation last summer and this number was twice as
high as in prior years. Ms. Toliver displayed a chart illustrating perceptions of travel and leisure activities as unsafe from March
13, 2020 to February 14, 2021; concerns seem to have decreased recently and many are resuming travel plans. In April of2020 the

        CVB shifted their focus to things like providing partner resources and helping businesses communicate with their customers
        during the pandemic; unfortunately, nearly half the CVB team was furloughed at this time. The CVB created user guides and a
        webinar series aimed at helping businesses navigate the pandemic and as CARES grant resources became available, the Bureau
        assisted partners in making applications for those funds. Collaboration efforts included partnering with Weber/Morgan Health
        Department to facilitate weekly meetings; working with GOAL, Ogden Arts, Culture, and Events, Ogden Downtown Alliance,
        Weber County, CP&R, and Weber State University (WSU); participating in weekly Utah Office ofTourism Marketing Cmmnittee

        Industry Association. In June, some visitor spending returned and the CVB worked with partners to launch the in-State campaign
        for summer travel and convention activities. The summer 2020 strategy was guided by trends in drive market travel and general
        consumer confidence. The CVB introduced new partners, dedicating 15 percent of the summer budget to a local market while
        having a presence in drive distance markets. The CVB created new marketing materials that were utilized to encourage visitors to
        be safe, but to get out and enjoy Ogden. In July 2020 the CVB secured a grant that was utilized to create a year-round leisure
        campaign and the City was recognized in two different media pieces as one of the best places to travel in September 2020 and also
        one of 12 small towns with beautiful architecture. Ms. Toliver then noted that in September of20 19 the CVB received a $20,000
        grant from the State Office of Tourism to create a Tourism Master Plan; work on this project began in August with a
        brainstorming process to identify the vision, position, and areas of focus for the Plan. The sales team began a campaign targeting
        100 meeting planners per month regarding how the community could help them meet safely and responsibly while meeting their
        goals. The CVB continued to take advantage of available grants and secured a $182,000 award, which was used to pay payroll and
        rent costs, which allowed the Bureau to bring back their furloughed team members. She also worked to secure a Meet IN Utah
        grant of$112,800, which was used to help incentive meeting planners to book their events before the end of the year. The CVB
        has learned that cost is the main factor that meeting planners consider, but they are also concerned about safety and this is one of
        the slowest sectors of the convention and tourism industry to return to pre-pandemic trends. The CVB typically launches their
        winter/ski campaign on October 1 of each year, but due to uncertainty, the fall campaign was utilized in 2020 and it focused
        primarily on in-state travel and tourism. Ms. Toliver than reported on perfonnance of other tourism markets in the United States;
        while people and corporations are not travelling as much as in the past, planning was still a big focus and the CVB was working
        hard to engage potential clients through search engine marketing. In October, CVB staff members attending their first tradeshow
        since the COVID-19 pandemic began and that yielded 63 contacts with meeting planners over three days; the target was to meet
        with planners that plan in the western United States and hold meetings that match Ogden's capabilities in terms of space and
        accommodations. There is a lot of interest in this community because people are looking for smaller destinations, but event
        planning has been slow to come back to normal. The CVB applied for and received a Weber CARES grant ahead of the winter of
        2020 and that was utilized to sustain operations. After delaying the winter/ski marketing campaign a number of times, it was
        fmally launched in November, but was constantly evolving; it ultimately became a much more regionally focused campaign when
        compared to past campaigns that targeted a national audience. The regional market has been most successful because many people
        were still concerned about flying to other destinations for a ski trip. The CVB has worked with ski resorts to effectively
        communicate their operating policies and procedures to potential visitors. She then concluded her presentation by reporting on
        year end numbers for tourism tax revenues and lodging data; revenues were down for most of 2020 when compared to 2018 and
        2019. Tourism was Utah's most devastated economy responsive to the COVID-19 pandemic, but the industry is predicting that
        activities will begin to rebound in 2022, but increased revenues will likely not be realized until 2023 or later. However, CVB
        looks towards the future with optimism and will begin working to implement their 10-year Tourism Master Plan; the pillars of the
        Plan are economic impact, community, placemaking, and advocacy. While there are challenges that lie ahead, the CVB is grateful
        to be part of this community that has great grit and detennination to respond to unprecedented circumstances. She knows that
        Ogden will come out better and stronger on the other side of the pandemic and she thanked her team for their leadership and
        dedication. She also thanked Ogden City Administration and the City Council for their support and partnership.

Utility Billing Fee Amendments
          Council Deputy Director Symes explained that in conjunction with the City's continued implementation of the Tyler
Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) Solutions system, the City's administration is proposing changes to billing and operational
fees for the water, sewer and refuse utilities. These changes are not related to utility fees such as water or sewer rates; rather, the

              Minutes of Joint Session of Council of Ogden City, Utah, March 2. 2021 Page 79


     changes are generally related to updating fees and standards for associated services and account maintenance issues. At this work
     session, Council members will continue the discussion regarding the proposed late fees and the service trip fees included in the
     proposal. Council members have expressed interest in establishing a grace period after a utility bill due date prior to the
     assessment of the proposed late fee. In addition, Council members have expressed interest in receiving additional information on
     the proposed service trip fee.
                Senior Accountant Johns started the discussion by summarizing how the City's utility billing cycle functions; it is a two-
     week cycle wherein meters are read and re-read during the first ws::ek and billing data is loaded and printed in the second week.
     Bills are typically sent on a Wednesday and become due the following Saturday; a full week after the bill due date, the next billing
     cycle starts and it the previous bill is not paid by the end of the next billing cycle, a door hanger is placed at the physical property
     address. Water shutoff does not occur until after nonpayment of two consecutive bills. There is currently no true late fee and just a
     $15 door hanger fee assessed after two bills are past due. According to the feedback receiving from the Council, the current
     proposal is to move the bill due date from 17 days to 23 days and the late fee would not be assessed until after the 23 days. This
     would essentially provide customers an additional week to pay. Relative to trip charges, the current practice is to use trip charges
     to reimburse the City for stafftime and vehicle costs and the conservative estimate is $30 per trip; fees range from $10 to $20
     depending on the reason for the trip. The proposal is to move to a single trip charge rate of $40 that would be assessed only if a
     property owner requests a service. The City will utilize marketing tools to inform utility customers of the proposed changes if they
     are approved; this includes a bill insert in Spanish and English for two months, notification via the At Your Service newsletter,
     advertising on the City's website, and posting in the quarterly Ogden Connection publication.
               Discussion among the Council and staff centered on current meter reading practices, and billing policies for rental
     properties.
                Council member Choberka thanked Administration for taking into consideration the Council's feedback regarding a
     grace policy for utility bills; she appreciates the adjustments that have been made to the proposal and supports proceeding with
     scheduling a public hearing on the matter. Vice Chair White echoed Council member Choberka's comments.
               Mr. Symes indicated ifthere is no objection, Council staff will work with Administration to include an action item on an
     upcoming meeting agenda to set a public hearing.

     Capital Square/Rite Aid Block- Acquisition of property at 856 25th Street
     (Real Estate Purchase Contract)
                Community and Economic Development Department Deputy Director Cooper presented a proposal from City
     Administration to accept the assignment of a real estate purchase contract (REPC) for purchase of property located at 856 25th
     Street, the 4-C Laundromat property. Mr. Cooper discussed the history of the project to redevelop and revitalize the Rite Aid
     block dating back to 2015. The Redevelopment Agency (RDA) has purchased several properties on the block for the purpose of
     revitalizing the entire area in partnership with a private developer. He presented a rendering of a concept plan that has been
     created for the redevelopment project; it is a difficult site to design, and it is important to include desired amenities that will attract
     people to live and work in the space. At this time, Administration is recommending the acquisition of subject property in order to
     expand the footprint of the project area; the purchase price is $460,000 and the developer has already paid $30,000 in earnest
     money. He reviewed additional terms of the purchase agreement, noting that some environmental work will need to be done at the
     site and possession of the property is subject to a six-month post occupancy license agreement. The developer is to purchase the
     property and take out the D.L. Evans loan subject to an approved development agreement. The seller is not willing to extend the
     expiration date and the lender for the developer is unwilling to loan the full amount until the mitigation is completed. That is why
     the developer has asked the RDA to step in; once remediation is completed, the developer will repurchase the property from the
     RDA. The terms of this type of arrangement would be stipulated in a land transfer and development agreement (L TDA), which
     will be presented to the Board in the near future. Mr. Cooper concluded Administration is recommending the use of the $600,000
     set aside in FY20 (Business Depot Ogden Lease Revenue) for acquisition and pre-development activities at the Rite-Aid block as
     the funding source for the purchase.
                Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reported that ifthere are no objections, Council staff will place action items on
     both the City Council and Redevelopment Agency Meeting agendas for March 9, 2021 to set a public hearing regarding the
     required budget amendments; action on the REPC could also be scheduled for March 9, 2021. Council member Choberka asked if
     it is problematic to approve the REPC in advance of acting on the budget amendment. Ms. Eller-Smith stated action on the REPC
     does not require a public hearing, while the budget amendment does; however, Mr. Cooper has indicated that action on the REPC
     could be delayed to the same meeting when the Council and RDA will act on the budget amendments if that would make the
     Council more comfortable. Council member Choberka stated that she would feel more comfortable with that timeline.
