Administrative Services Director, Bruce Kartchner, reported that staff and the City Council have
been trying to set the stage for a new Public Works Building. The way the City would be able to
finance it is through a bond at an estimated cost of $12 to $13 million. As a result of conversations
with the City Council, it was determined that the City may want to put it in a location other than
the property that is already owned by the Ci ty. Mr. Kartchner explained that they have
incorporated additional money in the resolution. The bonds will only be issued for the amount that
is needed with the maximum being $16 million.

Mr. Kartchner introduced Matt Dugdale from Stifel Finance. Mr. Dugdale explained that the
current Parameters Resolution is similar to the Water Revenue Bonds that were issued the prior
year and they were successful in financing a water tank. As a result of the COVID-19 pandemic,
interest rates are low, and the City was doing well. Mr. Dugdale explained that the cuITent
resolution creates a box of acceptability and the bonds will be issued at maximum par amount,
interest rate, and term. He explained that adopting the resolution does not require the City to issue
the bonds. Mr. Dugdale explained how the Building Authority's purpose is to issue bonds for
capital. He described the benefits of this method. He also explained that there is a resolution for
the City and the Building Authority Board.

scheduled for the June 23 City Council Meeting. Once that takes place, it is a matter of structuring
the bonds, finding a purchaser, and locking in an interest rate. They expect the process to be
completed by the middle or end of July but indicated that there is some flexibility.

Mr. Reid explained that once they have the property, a cost bid, and have decided how much to
put down, the C ity will come up with a bond amount. Mr. Dugdale explained that with a
Parameters Resolution there is no timeframe required on the issuance of the bonds.
