 PC 20-044 Center Ave Subdivision [Subdivision Permit] Todd Horman/Two Brothers Inc.,
 authorized agent/owner request a 7-lot subdivision on 1.5 acres located at 541 Center Avenue
 in the Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) zone; TIN 02-058-0005.

STAFF: Mr. Holley reviewed the request for a 7-lot single-family subdivision located on a 1.50-
acre parcel that recently contained a single-family home. A canal runs east to west bisecting the
property in half. The property is generally flat with trees near the canal and along Center Avenue.
Both streets bordering the property (Center Avenue & 500 South) do not currently have curb, gutter
or sidewalk. Railroad tracks run parallel to Center Avenue along the east side and the Logan City
Aquatic Center sits on the corner across from the site.

PROPONENT: Todd Horman said basements are not planned due to the water table in the area.
The corner lot will be designed to work within the parameters. Commissioner Dickinson asked
about the plans for the canal. Mr. Horman said those decisions would have to be made by the
canal company; they will adhere to the required setbacks. Mr. Holley noted that there are
restrictions along the canal and the canal company requires an easement for maintenance.

PUBLIC: Two emails were received and distributed to the Commission prior to the meeting and
have been included in the project file.


Travis Dunn said the canal will require an easement. Mr. Holley said this is addressed within the
conditions of approval. There is a 33’ corridor (depending on where the top of the canal is
measured from). Homeowners will have to work with the canal company in reference to fencing.

COMMISSION: Mr. Holley confirmed for Commissioner Dickinson that as house permits are
applied for the water levels will be determined and reviewed by the Engineering Department.

Mr. Holley explained that corner lots are unique, with smaller building envelopes. The setbacks
are 20’ on the corner and 25’ on the front. A building plan will have to fit within the lot.

Chairman Nielson asked about the canal setback. Mr. Holley explained that the 15’ setback is
from the top of the bank and the 8’ required setback will overlap (they are not added together).
He noted that the active irrigation ditch is addressed in the Engineering conditions of approval.

 PC 20-045 East Center Building [Design Review Permit] Michael Stettler, authorized
 agent/owner, requests a 3,000 SF two-story building for a small professional services firm. The
 building includes a large open lobby, five offices, three client rooms and surface parking in the
 rear located on .18 acres at 72 East Center Street in the Town Center (TC-1) zone; TIN 06-
 029-0019.

STAFF: Mr. Holley reviewed the request for a new two-story 3,000 SF brick/masonry office
building, a rear asphalt 8-stall surface parking lot, streetscape and landscaping improvements. The
dark brick front facade is shown with a small decretive pre-cast concrete cornice, large windows
spanning both floors and a metal canopy over the main front entryway. A concrete bulkhead is
proposed with raised planters and landscaping along Center Street. The changing brick patterns
form typical signage areas above the windows near the bottom of the cornice.

The 8-stall parking lot is located in the rear of the property and accessed from a shared
easement/right-of-way connecting to 100 East.


Because this building will be located within the Logan City Historic District, the Historic
Preservation Committee (HPC) reviewed and approved the facade at the September 8, 2020
meeting. The Planning Commission will act as the review authority for the proposed parking and
site improvements.

Staff recognizes that downtown Logan is unique. Balancing parking requirements with the historic
character and dense core that makes downtown is challenging. This business has a low demand
for employee parking and lower customer traffic numbers. The request for a 20% parking reduction
is a reasonable request for this area of the City. Street parking is available on Center Street and
several public parking lots are within a 1-½ block radius. Staff recommends that the Commission
accept the Alternative Parking Plan and the 20% reduction.

PROPONENT: None

PUBLIC: None

COMMISSION: Mr. Holley confirmed for Commissioner Dickinson that the Alternative Parking Plan
remains with the development, not the business. If the business changes, the new business will be
required to provide adequate parking for the use.

Chairman Nielson said the parking permit letter indicates that the building will be just under 2,500
SF. Mr. Holley said the net square footage is 3,000 SF due to the atrium in the front portion of the
building. The offices will be in the back of the building.

Chairman Nielson asked about the right-of-way. Mr. Holley said there is an existing 15’ right-of-
way that several properties share rights to (access out to 100 East). Chairman Nielson asked what
would happen if employees or overflow parking began using Allen-Hall Mortuary’s lot. Mr. Holley
said the mortuary could stop it if it became a problem. Chairman Nielson said the only access
through there seems to be the right-of-way. Mr. Holley said the businesses that have been there
for decades have used it without problems. Fire and emergency access will be via Center Street.

Mr. Holley answered for Commissioner Croshaw that the minimum requirement for bicycle parking
will be met. The requirement is based on a proportional standard.

Commissioner Ortiz likes the project, especially for downtown. Due to limited space, he feels the
proposed parking plan is reasonable and justified. Commissioner Lucero agreed that the
requested 20% parking reduction seems fine due to the location and street parking. She
questioned the accessible parking in the area. Mr. Holley said the number of ADA parking stalls is
based on use/size of the building, in this case there is one (1) stall required. There are some ADA
spots along the street that are not tied to any particular buildings.

Commissioner Goodlander is concerned with snow plowing and where the snow will be kept. Mr.
Holley said that is a common concern in all surface parking lots during the winter.

Chairman Nielson said his only concern with the project is the ownership of right-of-way. If it is ever
blocked, then there will be no access to parking in the back of the building. He would feel better if
there was a written agreement in place.

 PC 20-046 3rd Gen Machine [Design Review Permit] Brett Jensen/ABCW, LLC, authorized
 agent/owner, request the development of a 40,000 SF industrial building as Phase 1 of a future,
 larger development on 12.07 acres located at 600 West 2200 North in the Industrial Park (IP)
 zone; TIN 04-062-0302;-0303;-0304.

STAFF: Ms. Rice reviewed the request for one (1) new 40,000 SF industrial building as Phase I of
a future larger development. The building on three sides is 34’ tall concrete tilt-up style with the
west wall consisting of architectural metal panels. The building is positioned along 2200 North, at
the corner of 600 West with the parking lot on the east and loading docks on the north. The design
and arrangement of the future phases of the development are unplanned. The total development
site, including the future phases, is approximately 12 acres in size and configured in three (3)
parcels. This proposal includes a boundary line adjustment to align with phase lines. The phase
one area is approximately 3.18 acres.

PROPONENT: Brett Jensen said they have been working with the City for months trying to come
up with a design that will work in the zone. Moving the building with the amount of foot and truck
traffic that is anticipated, is not feasible. This is Phase 1 of the development, if parking were
located all the way to the back, employees and customers would have a long walk to the building.
They will be using faux windows on the south elevation to meet the requirement for the front
facade. Another challenge with this location is room for truck access/turnaround/loading areas
without being able to move the building into the wetland area. This proposal is a good compromise.
The north side of the building will be the machining area where the environment will need to be
controlled. They have tried hard to balance form and function.

Commissioner Lucero asked about the single door on the south side of the building (2200 North).
Justin Campbell, the project architect, said the single door will be an actual entrance into the
building. The east portion of the building will be two stories of office, engineering and quality
control. There will also be a door on the north side for symmetry purposes, in terms of use they do
not expect much foot traffic to use the door due to the interior layout of the building.

Commissioner Ortiz asked about mitigating measures related to transparency. Mr. Campbell said
higher ceilings and LED lighting help provide a comfortable quality inside the building. More
windows could affect the temperature needed to run the machines.

Commissioner Dickinson asked about the landscaping on 2200 North, which will be considered the
front. Mr. Campbell said they will meet all the required conditions listed for landscaping. The goal
is to create a nice “corner” view by dressing up more than just one facade.

Mr. Jensen pointed out that this is only developing three (3) of the total 12 acres. With the
architectural features proposed, this will be the nicest looking building along 600 West. They can
incorporate good landscaping and a berm to help screen the parking. They are committed to make
this a nice development.

PUBLIC: Blake Parker, the listing agent for this property, in reference to a precedent being set,
the property to the north has been approved for storage sheds and the other property will remain
vacant for airport clearance. This is the last parcel in that area that will be developed and will be,
by far, the nicest looking building and the last building along 600 West.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Lucero asked which frontage would be considered the front, 600
West or 2200 West. Mr. Holley said the applicant can decide and then it would have to meet the
applicable Code requirements.


Commissioner Goodlander asked if the north elevation met the 4-sided architecture requirement.
Ms. Rice said it does by including elements to break up the facade.

Ms. Rice confirmed for Commissioner Ortiz that the location of the building, with relation to the
parking, has been discussed. The proponent has proposed this plan due to the use of the interior
space of the building and the challenge (lack of space) due to wetlands.

Commissioner Goodlander said this is setting a precedent for the area and is not meeting the
Code. The front entrance of the building seems to clearly be 600 West, which would allow parking
in the front of the building. Even with the mitigating factors, this is a concern.

Commissioner Lucero said it would be helpful for the Commission to have a better sense of
interplay between City and County property and future planning perspective. In the development
standards, it mentions this as a gateway and the importance of first impression and design quality.
She agrees with Commissioner Goodlander that 2200 North is the “pretend” front of the building so
the parking lot can be located where it is. She understands the mitigating factors of the property,
but the parking lot is quite large. She would like to see more architectural interest on the 2200
North side as well as increased landscaping (like the recently approved USU Credit Union).

Mr. Holley confirmed for Chairman Nielson that doors do require a pedestrian covering.

Commissioner Dickinson agreed with the idea of increased landscaping and a berm to help screen
the parking.

Bill Young, the City Engineer, said the area west of 2200 North is in the future annexation plan.
The platted area, going across the railroad tracks to the east, is the last, undeveloped potential
east-west crossing, which could possibly provide a corridor in the future.

Chairman Nielson said this is a nice building and he is not as concerned with the parking lot
placement because it is next to an industrial park. Recently a car dealership, wanting to display
vehicles in the front, also with a wetland issue, was approved. This plan seems to make sense for
the use of the building, and he commended the applicant on the proposal.

Commissioner Croshaw would like to see the main entrance better emphasized.

 PC 20-040 A-OK RV Park – continued from August 27, 2020 [Zone Change & Design Review &
 Conditional Use Permit] Danny MacFarlane/Stan Checketts Properties LLC, authorized
 agent/owner, request an RV park with 86 sites, 10 cabins and a 3,500 SF office/administrative
 building, and to rezone the 7.87-acre property located at 800 West 2000 South from
 Commercial (COM) to Recreation (REC); TIN 03-006-0006.

STAFF: Mr. Holley reviewed the request. The applicant does not want to pursue the Commercial
Services (CS) zone as discussed at the last meeting. A Code Amendment addressing long-term
RV occupancy has been submitted and will be heard at the October 8, 2020 meeting.

PROPONENT: None

PUBLIC: Blake Parker is familiar with the property and use. As a real estate broker, one of the
most common requests he receives is why there are not more RV Parks. There is a value and
need for it and the location is excellent as it relates to coming into Cache Valley. There is a huge
demand and Logan is short on this type of amenity for tourists who come to the area.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Lucero asked if the future Code Amendment has any bearing on
this request. Mr. Holley has talked with the applicant, who has indicated that if the occupancy rules
are not changed, this may not be a viable option. This request can move forward independently of
that application.

Mr. Holley confirmed for Commissioner Croshaw that currently only an occupancy of 30 days or
less is allowed, however, this is very difficult to enforce. The applicant thinks allowing longer
lengths of stays would be appealing to students, contractors and tourists who would like to stay
through a season. With rising home prices, this may offer some housing affordability options.

Mr. Holley clarified for Commissioner Goodlander that the office would be the ancillary use to the
primary use of an RV park and would be allowed.

Commissioner Dickinson said RV parks are very popular now, especially noted during a recent
vacation, and this could be a wonderful business opportunity.

Mr. Holley answered for Commissioner Ortiz that the Code allows for an RV Park in the Recreation
(REC), Commercial Services (CS) and Industrial Park (IP) zones. There could be an argument
that this type of use should not be in prime commercial or downtown areas. This use also requires
quite a bit of space.
