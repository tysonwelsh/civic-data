       1.c     Public Meeting: City-Powers I-ADU Deviation Request (Administrative
               Action).
               On the request of Gary Powers representing The Powers Revocable Trust, a request
               for approval of a deviation from the prohibition against the creation of an Internal
               Accessory Dwelling (I-ADU) within the Primary Dwelling on the subject property,
               0.97 acres, located at 14653 S. Pristine View Cove Known as application 2024-
               0287-VAR.       Staff     Contact:    Nick      Whittaker,     (801)      576-6522,
               nick.whittaker@draperutah.gov.

City Planner, Nick Whittaker, presented the Staff Report and stated that the request is for an
Internal Accessory Dwelling Unit (“I-ADU”) Deviation Request. Vicinity and Aerial Maps were
displayed. The land use designation for the property is Residential Medium-Density and the
zoning is R-3. The applicant would like approval of an I-ADU. He explained that there are areas
in the City where they are prohibited. A GIS map was displayed which highlighted some of those
prohibited areas. Mr. Whittaker explained that it is a basement unit, and no changes are needed to
the existing elevations. The floor plan to the basement unit was displayed. It will have its own
garage and space to park two additional vehicles. Mr. Whittaker reported that it is possible for an
I-ADU to be allowed in a prohibited area by adhering to deviation standards set forth in Draper
City Code title 9-31-055. After review by staff, it was determined that Mr. Powers’ proposal meets
the standards for deviation.

Commissioner Fowler asked staff what disqualifies a certain area from having ADUs. Planning
Manager, Todd Draper reported that when the I-ADU ordinance was adopted, the City was allowed
to restrict up to 25% of the land area zoned as single-family residential from having an I-ADU. A
map was created with the help of the GIS Division that specified just over 20%. The main criteria
that went into determining what would be on the map was anything located or accessed from a
private or narrow street where on-street parking would likely not be possible. With a typical I-
ADU, State law specifies that the City can only require one parking stall. By allowing the
deviation, they can ask for a second parking stall to ensure that there is enough parking without it
overflowing into the street. Mr. Draper commented that the City Council felt that the 20,000-
square-foot lot sizes could contain the additional parking.

The applicant, Gary Powers stated that the home is about 9,000 square feet in size. The lower
level is a daylight basement with four bedrooms, two bathrooms, a full kitchen, and an enclosed
garage. Access to the unit comes around the house with additional parking behind that would
only be used for the I-ADU. Mr. Powers stated that he serves on the HOA board and there is
nothing prohibiting what is proposed. Mr. Powers stated that the space will be rented.

       1.h    Public Hearing: City Initiated 2024 Moderate-Income Housing Plan.

              On the request of Draper City, a request for a General Plan Amendment to replace
              a Moderate-Income Housing Plan strategy. Known as application 2024-0303-TA.
              Staff Contact: Todd Taylor, (801) 576-6510, todd.taylor@draperutah.gov.

City Planner, Todd Taylor, reported that last month staff presented a summary of the 2024
Moderate-Income Housing Report to the Planning Commission. It was subsequently presented to
the City Council the following week. At that meeting, they discussed Strategy L, which is to
reduce, waive, or eliminate impact fees related to moderate-income housing. Staff had noted that
problems had been identified with proceeding with the strategy and there was a desire was to
replace it. Optional replacement Strategies E and X were also discussed. Strategy X generally
involves reducing regulations related to multi-family dwellings that are compatible in scale and
form with single-family dwellings. When the optional strategies were discussed with the City
Council, they were supportive of both but recommended proceeding with Strategy E because it is

       1.i     Public Hearing: City Initiated 2024 Legislative Zoning Text Amendment

               On the request of Draper City, a request for a Zoning Text Amendment to sections
               9-5-200 and 9-27-140 of the Draper City Municipal Code in order to address recent
               changes to Utah State Code related to development agreements and residential rear
               yard setbacks. Known as application 2024-0304-TA. Staff Contact: Todd Taylor,
               801-576-6510, todd.taylor@draperutah.gov.

Mr. Taylor reported that this amendment is in response to changes to State Code, specifically
House Bill 476 during the 2024 Legislative Session. It added language regarding Development
Agreements and building elements that encroach into residential rear yard setbacks. The
Development Agreement language went into effect on May 1 and the residential rear yard setback
provisions go into effect on November 1. A few other changes were also made for clarity. The
proposed text to Development Agreements is in Section 9-5-200 and brings in text from Utah State
Code. It deals with the types of projects that can request a Development Agreement, when they
can and cannot be required, and specifies procedures for recording with the County. The proposed
changes to residential rear yard setbacks are in Section 9-27-140 of the Code. It addresses lots and
yards and the types of structures that can be located within setbacks. This section has also been
modified slightly for additional clarity.

It was noted that State Code addresses walk-out porches and exterior landings, balconies, and
stairways that are uncovered and comply with the Fire and Building Code. They can be located in
rear or side yards, can be no more than 32 square feet in size, connect to and provide ingress and
egress from the dwelling, and project no more than five feet. In response to a question from Chair
Adams about why walkout porches and landings were separated, Mr. Taylor noted that the code
currently allows uncovered decks in the rear setback, but they must be less than three feet in height.
State Code specifies that a landing is an uncovered, above-ground platform with or without stairs

uncovered platform that is on the ground. For that reason, the two were separated.
