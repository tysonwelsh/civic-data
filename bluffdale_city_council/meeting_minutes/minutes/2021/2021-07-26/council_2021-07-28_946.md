4.     PUBLIC HEARING - Update and Report Regarding a Proposed Property Tax
       Increase by Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District for Water Service Purposes,
       Presenter, Sherrie L. Ohrn, Board of Trustees, Jordan Valley Water Conservancy
       District.

Ms. Ohm was present with Matthew Olsen and presented the proposed 9.4% property tax rate
increase for 2021 from the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District ("JVWCD"). She explained
that for a $465,000 home, the increase would equate to $8.1 8 per year. The increase will be used
to pay a $180 million bond over the next 15 years. The water treatment faci lity needs improvement
at a cost of $64 million and reservoirs need to be expanded due to growth in the community.

A question was raised as to why developers are not paying for more of the cost in the form of
impact fees. If the majority of the costs are due to growth there was some question as to why the
burden is being put on existing residents. Ms. Olu·n explained that water generates revenue through
(1) property taxes; (2) fees ; and (3) impact fees . The reason they can tax is because there is a huge
public benefit from water. Reservoirs, for example, do not exist solely for recreation but serve as
water storage areas. Another purpose is flood control since reservoirs help mitigate flooding. They
also assist in firefighting effo1ts. User and impact fees are used to pay for certain things but other
aspects require water storage. As a result, property tax funds are used. Ms. Ohm explained that
the $1.8 million in revenue exceeds the growth requirements, which are paid for using impact fees.

Traci Crockett noted that property values in Utah have risen dramatically so, based on the tax
increase, she will pay approximately $200 more per year in taxes with no tax increases simply due
to the valuation increase. As a result, the budget should be growing substantially w ithout a tax
increase. She asked how much of the increase in property value is accounted for in the budget.

Administrative Services Manager, Brnce Kartchner, explained that the increase in home values
does not change the amount of taxes paid. The State of Utah backs into the percentage each year
so the dollar amount for a given group of property tax assets remains t he same. As prope1ty values
increase, the percentage decreases. The C ity and JVWCD do not benefit from any increase in
value.

Mr. Olsen clarified that ifthere was no growth in an area and the property tax rate was not adj usted,
they would sti ll generate the same revenue as the previous year. As a result, the property tax rate
is adjusted down because prope1ty values increased. Mr. Kartchner explained that inflation
impacts purchasing power. As a result, in order for taxing entities to remain even, they should be
increasing their taxes by at least the rate of inflation occasionally. Mr. Olsen stated that the

7.     Consideration and Vote on Resolution No. 2021-43 Notice of Pending Ordinance to
       Amend the Residential Yard Sign Section of City Code, Staff Presenter, Todd
       Sheeran.

Mr. Sheeran reported that the above issue was presented at the last Council Meeting and staff
received counsel asking for an additional discussion. He reviewed the first amendment in general,
signs in the current sign ordinance, and potential changes. It was noted that signs carry first
amendment protections. Unlike speech, signs can obstruct views, distract motori sts, and create
other problems. For that reason, cities generally regulate signs. Specific cases were identified.
Mr. Sheeran reported that signs must comply with the commercial sign regulations but can contain
any content.

Mr. Sheeran reported that there are specific laws in the Utah Code addressing political signs. It
allows people with authorization to remove them. It also specifies that if a city plans to allow one
person to post a sign, they cannot restrict others from doing so. In 2019, Senator Todd Wheeler
proposed SB-90, which dealt with park strips. The bill did not pass the House. It was an interesting
topic in that Senator Wheeler was arguing that park strips that are not owned by the City should
allow for signs to be posted there.

The former City Code that was enacted in 20 10, specifies that campaign signs do not require a
permit or to follow regulations of the City Code if they are announcing candidacy for City public
office. Based on another case from Gilbert, Arizona, this provision of City Code would likely be
unconstitutional. The current City Code specifies that signs can be located on private property
with the owner' s permission. They can be located on public prope1ty and can be in the clear view
triangle area.

Mr. Sheeran sh1died regulations from other cities and found that none allow signs on public
property or in public rights-of-way. A ll restricted regulations on private property. He spoke to a
representative from the Utah Department of Transportation ("UDOT") about a Betterment
