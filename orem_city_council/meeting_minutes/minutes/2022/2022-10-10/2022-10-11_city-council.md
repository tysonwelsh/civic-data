PRESENTATION - North Point Solid Waste Special Service District (NPSWSSD)

Mr. Bybee introduced Mr. Schwendiman and listed what his job entails as well as some of his
accomplishments.

Mr. Schwendiman started by giving some of the history of the transportation and location of
North Point Solid Waste Special Service District. He continued to discuss rail and semi
transportation, services, operation and where the waste currently resides took place. He then
explained some of the services and operations of the company. Clarification of “clean” waste
took place. He showed a pie chart that displayed curbside waste, public self-haul, city vehicles,
coupon customers, and direct green waste customers of Orem in 2021. He then spoke about
curbside tons, curbside green waste tons, and history of the coupons being used.

General discussion ensued regarding the purpose of dump passes in Orem and how they have
been used in the past as well as the curbside fee history.

Mr. Schwendiman concluded by reviewing what the future may hold and the master plan
findings. He then showed some images of what the transfer station could look like. He explained
how they are working to improve the current space within the year to the next few years.

Mr. Bybee stated that because of Mr. Schwendiman and his creative skills they are able to keep
the current location of the building.

General discussion of the current board members, the height of the stock wall, the wildlife that
resides on the land, appreciation of cost effectiveness, relationship with waste management, and
wait time for dumping.

PRESENTATION - Deer Creek Intake Project (45 min)

Mr. Denos explained what the intake water project was and some of the reservoirs associated
with it. He stated that Deer Creek Reservoir services 88% of the drinking water to Utah’s
population. Mr. Denos discussed some of the areas that are in need of repair and renovation.

Mr. Jorgenson stated the “why” of the project and what the driving force

is. - Aging Infrastructure
o Construction in the wet doesn’t impact existing operators, fish, etc.
o Isolating existing intake allows for maintenance, inspections, etc.
o Restore aging guard gate(s) operators
o Redundant intakes/pipe that are designed for seismic

* Invasive species threat

- Innovative construction “in the wet”

He then explained 3 solutions to aging infrastructure and the schedule that has been going since

PUBLIC HEARING/RESOLUTION-Amending portions of Section 2-1-5 Board of
Building and Fire Code Appeals pertaining to number and length of terms a Board
member can serve.

Mr. Bench stated the Board of Building and Fire Code Appeals is a body of local citizens made
up of contractors, engineers, architects and other professionals. Knowledge of general
construction principles and practices is an essential qualification for a Board member.
Membership is also restricted to Orem residents. Because of the qualifications there is a limited
number of eligible applicants. The Board also meets infrequently on an as-needed basis, often
going months or years without a meeting. The request made by Development Services would
allow Board members to serve more than two terms, which will help with preventing vacancies
on the Board.

Mr. Lambson asked what their term would consist of. Mr. Bench replied it would be a full
3-year term.

Mayor Young opened the public hearing at 6:13pm. Hearing no comments, Mayor Young
closed the public hearing and brought the item back to the Council for further discussion.

Mr. Macdonald moved by Resolution to Amend portions of Section 2-1-5 Board of Building

and Fire Code Appeals pertaining to the number and length of terms a Board member can serve.
Mrs. Lauret seconded the motion. Those voting aye: David A. Young, David Spencer, Debby
Lauret, Tom Macdonald, Jeff Lambson, Terry Peterson and LaNae Millett. The motion passed.

PUBLIC HEARING/ ORDINANCE- Amending Section 22-6-9(F)(8) Home Day Care
Services-Child & Adult (SLU Code 6263) pertaining to outdoor play areas; and enacting
Section 22-6-9(G)(14) Commercial Day Care Services (SLU Codes 6261 and 6262)

pertaining to outdoor play areas.


Mr. Bench stated that Development Services requests the City Council amend the Home
DayCare requirements pertaining to play areas. The proposed language would require play areas
to be fully enclosed and require a self-closing and self-latching gate. It would also require
outdoor play areas to have a soft-surface area for children to play on. (See Section 22-6-
9(F)).Development Services also proposes a new ordinance regarding Commercial Day Care
Services. It would require commercial daycares to have a play area that is fenced with self-
closing and self-latching gates. It would not allow play areas and play structures to be located in
parking lots or on top of other hard surfaces. (See Section 22-6-9(G)).

General discussion ensued regarding past playground areas, past issues, potential issues, what a
soft surface classifies as, and new licensing requirements.

Mayor Young opened the public hearing tat 6:16 p.m.

A person who did not state their name asked for clarification on who would need to apply to
these changes to their businesses. Mr. Bench replied stating that all day cares in Orem would
need to follow the guidelines.

Eliza Penniger the director of Learning Adventures Child Care Center stated that she opened up
her child care center last year and explained her reasoning for starting her business. She
explained that she recently sold the business and she would need to get a new business license
for this because Orem City does not allow new licenses to be transferred to new owners once the
business is sold. She stated that getting the new license and complying with the new business
regulations would cost her a significant amount of money. She explained that the last line of the
Ordinance is unclear and she mentioned using turf over concrete as a solution.

General discussion ensued regarding the history of Ms. Pennigers business, current licensing
versus expired licenses, benefits in the play area being a soft surface versus a hard asphalt
surface play area, temperature regulations, shade required, verbiage/language in the new
ordinance.

Mayor Young suggested the Ordinance define what a soft surface play area is.

Ms. Penniger proposed they be able to proceed with licensing. Discussion in regards to moving
forward and what surfaces should be allowed for Ms. Pennigers Day Care.

Mayor Young closed the public hearing at 6:44 p.m. and brought the item back to the council
for further discussion.

Mr. Lambson moved by Ordinance to Amend Section 22-6-9(F)(8) Home Day Care Services-
Child & Adult (SLU Code 6263) pertaining to outdoor play areas; and enacting Section 22-6-
9(G)(14) Commercial Day Care Services (SLU Codes 6261 and 6262) pertaining to outdoor play
areas minus the last sentence in the ordinance. Mr. Macdonald seconded. Those voting aye:
David A. Young, David Spencer, Debby Lauret and Jeff Lambson, Tom Macdonald and LaNae
Millett. Those voting nay: Terry Peterson. The motion passed.

PUBLIC HEARING / ORDINANCE - Enacting Section 22-6-9(M) Residential

Preschool (SLU Code 6265) and Standard Land Use Code 6265 Residential

Preschool.
Mr. Bench stated it has been requested a new land use code be enacted for residential
preschools. Currently, residential preschools are being regulated and enforced under Home Day
Care Services (SLU Code 6263). (See Section 22-6-9(F)). Enacting a new land use code will
allow for better regulation and enforcement of residential preschools because different
regulations apply to them.

Mayor Young opened the public hearing at 6:46 p.m. Hearing no comments Mayor Young
closed the public hearing and brought the item back to the Council for further discussion. +

Mr. Spencer moved by Ordinance to Enact Section 22-6-9(M) Residential Preschool (SLU
Code 6265) and Standard Land Use Code 6265 Residential Preschool. Mrs. Lauret seconded.
Those voting aye: David A. Young, David Spencer, Debby Lauret, Tom Macdonald, Jeff
Lambson, Terry Peterson and LaNae Millett. The motion passed.

ORDINANCE -— Amending Section 14-1-4 Sign Zones and the Sign Zone map of the
Orem City Code extending Sign Zone E to include a portion of the CenterPoint Church
property located at 1550 S Sandhill Road.


Mr. Bench stated Centerpointe Church would like to install a double sided, pole mounted digital
sign on their property. This sign would replace the historic use of hanging 10’ x 10’ banners on
the building to advertise for events, such as a Christmas Eve Service. The pole sign would be
installed near the southwest corner of the building oriented towards the freeway. 1550 S Sandhill
Road does not currently have a sign zone, so it is considered to be in Sign Zone “D”. Sign Zone
“D” allows free standing monument signs, however it does not allow any free standing pole signs
(Section 14-3-3). The applicant is requesting that sign zone “E,” which allows pole signs, be
extended to include 1480 S Sandhill Road and a portion of 1550 S Sandhill Road property.

Mr. Bench stated that they are still working with the applicant to get the agreement signed.
They have signed it. They are just looking for authorization that this individual has the ability to

sign on behalf of the church. He concluded that it had not been accomplished by the time the
meeting started and asked this item be continued for two weeks.

Mr. Macdonald moved to continue the item amending Section 14-1-4 Sign Zones and the Sign
Zone map of the Orem City Code extending Sign Zone E to include a portion of the CenterPoint
Church property located at 1550 S Sandhill Road until the October 25" City Council Meeting.
Mr. Spencer seconded. Those voting aye: David A. Young, David Spencer, Debby Lauret,
Tom Macdonald, Jeff Lambson, Terry Peterson and LaNae Millett. The motion passed.

PERSONAL APPEARANCES

Margaret Black, an Orem resident and former City Council member voiced some concerns that
she had with the City Council ethics, feasibility study, and proposition 2. She mentioned
meetings that she has attended and what she witnessed at those meetings. She is concerned with
the adversarial nature that has been presented regarding Alpine School District. She concluded
by stating she would like to see the Council work with Alpine School District in a positive way
in the future.

Linda Campbell Stated she feels as though there are council members going around in the
community with information that encourages residents to vote yes to the School District split.
She voiced her concerns with the ethics of the City Council members. She believes that there is a
lack of civility and respect in the current city council meetings. She would like to know if the PR
person hired to do campaigning for proposition 2 has used any of Orem’s tax payer money. She

concluded by expressing her frustrations with the city council's behavior and the live stream
video not taking place.

Jody Sumberg, an Orem resident, told a story about two teenage boys who had taken signs from
somebody in regards to Proposition 2. She spoke about her own elections and how signs have
been handled in the past. She explained some of the current rules and regulations in regards to
sign postage. Ms. Sumberg talked about her 20-year experience on an elected board and the
importance of following the law especially during an open meeting. She explained that she has
been disheartened over the last several months with the Mayor and three council members who
have chosen to disregard the law. Holding a quorum outside of official closed meetings regarding
creating resolutions and other policies while not including three of the City Council members and
changing agendas without the knowledge of three of the council members. She expressed her
concern with recent displays of attack during city council meetings of certain council members
when they are in opposition of views. She concluded by encouraging the council to have in depth
training on ethics and open meeting laws and seek to restore trust by Orem citizens.

Stephanie Bismont voiced her feeling of the importance of a city council being neutral with
topics on the ballot. She stated she does not believe that means that they cannot have an opinion,
but rather when they come together as a council, they remain neutral. She feels as though citizens
should feel comfortable in contacting anyone on the council with questions and concerns and not
be swayed one way or another. She concluded by expressing that she is not a fan of the resolution
but she does support the city council through the very important issues in Orem City.

Tim Stay shared his involvement in the community and explained his point of view on the
academic wellbeing of Orem. He is concerned with the impact of education being their own
district and how that would affect Orem children. He explained frustration with more money
possibly going to the administrators and less going to the students. He gave examples of other
cities and their economic state due to similar situations. He encouraged the city council to stay
neutral and for Orem citizens to vote. He concluded by stating his frustration of being in another
city and them being able to live stream their meeting and Orem City not live streaming their
citizen comments.

Marilyn Larsen thanked the council for considering a smaller school district. She spoke about the
feasibility study and others points of view on the feasibility study. She believes that smaller
classroom and smaller city governments are phenomenal. She related a conversation she had with

an Alpine School District board representative and how it has impacted her.

Donna Boyd spoke about her involvement in education and the community. She stated that some
of the redirect has been confusing in regards to tax payer increases with Orem becoming their
own school district. She spoke about tax increase, debt, fixed income, the school district bond,
and seismic upgrades in Orem schools. She prefers that her tax dollars stay in Orem to create a
new school district. She listed off some of the benefits of having an Orem only school district.
She concluded by stating she believes that those who oppose the school district split have been
misinformed and she voiced her approval of the credibility of the people who conducted the
feasibility study.

Samuel Fisher spoke about election signage and different emotions associated with election
signs. He expressed frustration dishonestly with those who are opposing the Orem only school
district. He read a statement by the Utah Taxpayers Association and explained his point of view.
He hopes that going forward everybody is able to have open and honest conversations. He stated
it seems like those talking, if they can’t lie, then they attack the person who’s talking and if they
are proved wrong they then move to the next stage and make up new statistics. He concluded that
he believes by passing the resolution it will help clear the air.

Karen Turner expressed appreciation towards the council for giving the citizens of Orem the
opportunity to vote for the future of Orem Schools. She has enjoyed having discussions with
friends and neighbors in regards to the proposition on the ballot. She explained some of the
benefits of an Orem only school district. She believes that this is possible without putting the
citizens of Orem in deep financial bondage. She stated that Orem has the financial resources to
make this happen. She concluded by encouraging the citizens of Orem to do their research on
both sides of the proposition before they vote.

Karen Adamson stated she believes that Alpine School District is putting Orem in a position
where they will be unable to help themselves. She expressed some of her concerns with Orem
staying in the Alpine School District including debt, having options, and helping the children.
She believes that Orem has the resources to make its own school district. She concluded by
thanking the Mayor and City Council for giving Orem the chance to make this happen.

Jason Jolly expressed his confusion with the live streaming not being on. He stated he views the

split as an ideological battle, not about the seismic fit of Orem buildings or about a lot of
different issues that have been brought up. He said that he has seen animosity brought on both
sides of this opposition. He thinks this has made it very difficult for undecided voters to come to
a conclusion. He concluded by commenting about doing better as individuals, the stakeholders
being affected by the proposition, and current programs that the Alpine School District offers.
Michelle Lee stated there is a group that is opposing the proposition that she felt was founded on
false numbers and inaccurate data. She expressed frustration with board members of Alpine
School District indicating she felt they were not remaining neutral and using their district email
accounts to form a group against the feasibility study. She asked if residents were supposed to
believe every single independent organization and government agency and individual that has
strongly supported proposition 2 is lying and those on the opposite side is the only group in
Orem that has the truth. She expressed frustration with the Alpine School District using
employees and resources to fight against the School District split. She concluded by stating that
one thing that she believes Orem should do would be to branch away from Alpine School
District.

RESOLUTION - A Resolution of the City Council of the City of Orem Outlining its

Support for a new Orem School District

Mayor Young read the resolution. He then asked the council members for their comments.

Mr. Lambson stated that tonight they are not voting whether to split or not to split, they are
voting on a resolution that the City Council is making a statement about a particular stand on an
issue that is on the ballot that he does not feel good about. He expressed his concern not that this
issue is on the ballot. He believes that the citizens of Orem will vote for what they believe is best
for the City of Orem. His concern is that as an elected body, it may appear they are trying to
influence the voters. He stated they are elected to represent all of the citizens of Orem. He had
spoken with some people in the education community who were frustrated and disappointed that
the city council would make a resolution supporting one way or another on the ballot. He said
that the resolution feels like the city council is trying to influence the people to do the will of the
city council. He stated that until after the election they will not know what the people want and
until then he does not feel good about an elected body trying to influence the vote. Mr. Lambson
stated that he would like to represent all of the citizens of Orem and make sure they all are heard
by getting to listen to their votes in the future election. Mr. Lambson concluded that he would
like to amend the wording of the resolution. Mr. Earl advised that it may be best to consider that
after everyone has commented.

Mr. Peterson stated he is speaking tonight in favor of an Orem only school district. He explained
his perspective with his past experience being on the Alpine School District Board and where he
stands on an Orem only school district. Mr. Peterson then used an analogy stating what cities are
currently in the Alpine School District. He spoke about potential debt the Orem could get in if

they stay with Alpine School District. He then stated some concerns with seismic safety,
resources, and locations of schools and complaints that were made. He stated Alpine school
district has known about seismic unsafe Orem schools for 6 years and they have not done
anything about it. He gave examples of schools in Alpine School District that have received
resources and funding when Orem has not. He then spoke about the feasibility study and some
factors that played into it. He talked about the benefits that some of the smaller school districts
have had and about future projections in an Orem only school district. Mr. Peterson stated that
Orem does not have a diminishing population of children, Alpine School District has a
diminishing enrollment of children in Orem. He stated his point is that one in five children in
Orem do not attend Alpine School District. He believes that Orem citizens are going to other
schools because their test scores are higher, and smaller class sizes. He then stated how Orem
taxpayers’ money is going to be spent going to the Orem School District. He explained that the
feasibility study was the fourth study that was conducted and this process is not being rushed
because of the $600 million dollars that is involved. He discussed the men who conducted the
feasibility study and their credibility, how Orem has the funding for an Orem only School
district, the perspectives of the opposing side, and how he will be voting.

Mrs. Millett stated her frustration with comments on social media and newspapers being
incorrect about proposition 2. She said that this resolution informs the citizens of Orem of the
facts. She shared her point of view having been involved and done a lot for different schools. She
stated she believes to be true about the future of Orem schools if they were to stay with Alpine
School District. She explained that her intentions are always in the best interest of Orem children.
She feels it is important to have a small neighborhood school culture in Orem and what it means
to her. She read a letter form a retired school teacher and explained her opinion on what teachers
input means to her. She believes that in a smaller district individuals would have more of a voice,
teachers would have the same benefits, retention, and retirement plan. She stated that as shown in
the feasibility study Orem school district teachers would be compensated the same as they are
now for at least 10 years. She encouraged Orem residents to re-watch the August 2, 2022 city
council meeting to hear the research that has been done in determining if Orem should

have its own school district. She concluded by reviewing some of her own research she had done
in regards to growth and funding for the programs.

Mr. Spencer reviewed the timeline and the research that had gone into creating an Orem School
District. He quoted statements from the Utah Taxpayer Association and things that were
discovered in the feasibility study. He stated it is up to the voters in Orem to vote based on facts
and not fear. He believes there have been some false statements made and distortions that have
been said. He concluded by explaining if Orem decides to create its own school district, taxes
will not increase due to not being a part of the Alpine School District Bond.

Mrs. Lauret stated she was concerned during previous meetings and believes council members
should be neutral, although technically within the bounds of the state statues, she believes that it
violates the spirit of the law. She continued to state she would like to trust the process of the
citizens of Orem voting to how they feel things should be, there are many opportunities on both
sides for citizens to be educated. She sees the pros and the cons on both sides of the arguments
and believes there are benefits to both. She stated she is concerned about the startup costs of an
Orem school district running into the millions that the city would have to bear. She concluded to
state she has had conversations with both Lindon and Vineyard and those cities are watching and
waiting but have made no commitments to joining Orem.

Mr. Macdonald stated he does not think that long speeches from the city council will change
anybody’s opinion. He told a story about his neighbor putting up vote yes signs shortly after he
put a vote no sign and how they are able to still be friendly with one another despite their
differences. He agrees with those who have stated they would like to see civility from both sides
on this issue. He concluded by stating he would prefer to stay clear of any hint or perception of
impropriety or circumvention of established best practices.

Mayor Young stated the reason for putting the resolution on the agenda was because he would
like to clear up any misinformation given in the past. He would like the citizens of Orem to make
their voting decisions based on the truth. He reviewed the frequently asked questions about the
Alpine School District Bond and the responses to those questions He stated he feels Alpine
School District loyalists put out a hit piece on his father that was designed to change the
conversation from what should be discussed into an effort to demonize his father. He explained
his father’s current circumstances and his frustration with people twisting his father’s prayer into
something negative about teachers. He expressed his father’s point of view on the situation and
why he said what he said in his prayer. He stated he respects all sides of the arguments in this
election, and would like Orem residents to make decisions based on facts and not be misled.
Mayor Young concluded by reviewing past tax fluctuation, projected future taxpayer
fluctuation, timing of the school district split, and current situations in Orem schools.

Mr. Peterson moved by Resolution the City Council of the City of Orem Outlining its
Support for a new Orem School District Mrs. Millett seconded. Those voting aye: David A.
Young, David Spencer, Terry Peterson and LaNae Millett. Those voting nay: Tom Macdonald,
Jeff Lambson, and Debby Lauret. The motion passed.
