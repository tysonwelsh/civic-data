1. 24-337 Council Special Recognition and Presentation of the 2024 Noal Bateman
Award to Patrick Casaday
The Noal Bateman Award is a program that is sponsored and run by the Sandy City
Council. This award, first given in 1986, is named after its inaugural recipient, Noal
Cushing Bateman. Noal Bateman, widely known as “Mr. Sandy” devoted over 70 years of
his life to community service. The Noal Bateman Lifetime Service Award recognizes
individuals whose contributions profoundly enhance the quality of life in the community.
This honor serves as a lasting tribute to those remarkable persons whose constant efforts
leave an indelible imprint on the community and leave a positive legacy for future
generations. The Noal Bateman Chair, John Winder and Vice Chair, Tracy Cowdell
presented the 2024 Noal Bateman Award to Mr. Patrick Casaday in recognition of his
lifetime of service to the community. Mr. Casaday's list of accomplishments and service
is extensive. He is involved with the Sandy Rotary Club, CDBG Committee, Public
Utilities Advisory Board, Honorary Colonels, Healthy Sandy, The Sandy Club, Planning
Commission, Utah League of Cities and Towns, and many more organizations. Mr.
Casaday has also served as a Mission President, Stake President and Bishop for the
Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. He has also served as a senior civil attorney
in our legal department and as Deputy Mayor of Sandy City. He has a distinguished
career in the United State Air Force as a Command Pilot and served for over 20 years.
We thank Mr. Casaday for his service to our community and to our country. On behalf of
the Sandy City Council and the Noal Bateman Committee, we are honored to present this
year's award to Patrick Casaday.

2. 24-341 Public Works presenting a update on the Bulk Waste Program
Attachments: Bulk Waste Update 10124v2

Ryan Kump and Blaine Botkin with the Public Works Department, provided an update to
the Council on the Bulk Waste program. The Spring 2024 program schedule ran 13
weeks from March 11th to June 7th and was prioritized to cover the west side of the city,
moving across the city to the east side. The Fall 2024 program goes from September 9th
to November 15th and will be prioritized to cover the east side of the city and moving
across the city to the west side. When the bulk waste schedule is completed, the crews
then move into our summer/winter work activities. On the average, our crews make 2,500
trips to the Trans-Jordan landfill in the spring and 1,800 trips in the fall. The city also
offers a year-round dumpster program which is available to our residents. Our annual
communications and educational campaign includes post card mailing, website updates,
SandyNow! text messaging, and social media outreach across all city social channels.
Council questions followed. Mr. Kump and Mr. Botkin responded to questions regarding
the new transfer station located in Sandy City. The proximity of the transfer station has
cut our crews drive time in half providing significant savings on our crews' schedules, but
the fees at the transfer station are higher. There is no separation of green waste. Street
sweepers go throughout the city following each bulk waste program completion. The
Council expressed their appreciation to the Public Works staff for their efforts running
such a successful bi-annual bulk waste program.
Council moved to General Citizen Comment.

3. 24-342 First Reading: Council Members D'Sousa, Stroud and Dekeyzer
presenting an amendment to permit tattoo shops.

Attachments: Memorandum Tattoo Zoning Final

First Reading: Council Members Stroud, Dekeyzer, and D'Sousa presented an
amendment to the Land Development Code to permit tattoo shops in commercial zones.
Currently, tattoo shops are only allowed in industrial zones. They asked for feedback from
the Council to move forward with working with city staff to pursue possible amendments
related to this request. This request was brought to the attention of the Council by a local
business owner who currently rents space to a business that provides permanent
makeup. They would like to expand their business to include tattoos, but are limited by
the zoning restrictions currently in place. Council was supportive of gathering additional
information such as how other cities zone for tattoo businesses and the impact on the
neighboring area. The Council was supportive of moving forward with the process to
gather and evaluate additional information regarding a possible code amendment.

The Council enjoyed selecting the winners of the car show. Last week there was a
multi-agency law enforcement operation on the south lawn. There were 100 officers from

Youth Council the Sandy Youth Council for their participation and help at the Heritage

city website for residents in unincorporated areas who are looking to annex into the city.
They can now compare their current property tax with that of Cottonwood Heights and

as they consider which city to annex into. She provided an update from the Salt Lake
Convention Advisory Board. The Mountain America Expo Center is in the black by one
million dollars. The Salt Palace is in the red by a corresponding amount. The Salt Palace

breakdown in revenues generated by rent and food at each of the facilities. She attended

focused on the housing crisis, The event speaker was Chuck Marohn, President and
Founder of Strong Towns. The event was well attended. Mr. Marohn feels the answer to
the housing crisis is not to give out money but to build higher density. He also believes
that cities should eliminate parking mandates. She spoke about a friend that lives in a
studio apartment in Salt Lake City that offers no available parking and she shared with
the Council the impact that has on the apartment residents, from parking three or four
blocks away from his complex to avoiding using the car so he doesn't lose his parking
spot.
