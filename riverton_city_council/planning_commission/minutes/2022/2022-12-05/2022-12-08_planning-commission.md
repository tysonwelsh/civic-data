              CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT FOR A HOME REMODEL/REPAIR
              BUSINESS LOCATED AT 2918 WEST 13400 SOUTH TO PARK WORK
              VEHICLES IN THE DRIVEWAY. APPLICANT – NICK BERRETT.

City Planner, Lisa Halversen, reported that the applicant, Nick Berrett, applied for a Home
Occupation Permit for his home remodel and repair business, Golden, Inc. located at 2918
West 13400 South. The house faces 13400 South and has an additional driveway and
detached garage on 2900 West. The application stated that a Conditional Use Permit was
desired so that Mr. Berrett could come into compliance and park work vehicles on his
property. Mr. Berrett has a one-half-acre lot. No customers, deliveries, or employees will
come to the home; however, there was a desire to park a truck and trailer on the property.
Ms. Halversen reported that a mailer was sent to the surrounding neighbors. No email
responses were received but one neighbor contacted the City and was concerned about
screening.

be parked. She explained that the application is unique because Mr. Berrett’s wife has a
Conditional Use Permit for a Home Occupation as well as a home daycare. Vehicles drop
off and pick up children from 13400 South. Mr. Berrett plans to park his business vehicles
on 2900 West in the garage. The proposal was to use the space behind the fence for
business parking. Additional images were shared with the Planning Commission for
reference. Ms. Halverson identified the area where the neighbor expressed concerns about
screening. The tarp had come loose and was not fully covering what is currently in the

The proposed Conditions of Approval were read. In response to a question raised,

public street does not include chain link with slats. Commissioner Matheson wondered if
the existing fence would need to be replaced or moved. Ms. Halverson stated that the fence

Mr. Berrett explained that he is trying to do home remodeling and repair work. He purchased

       D.     COMMERCIAL SITE PLAN – MY STORAGE RIVERTON, PLZ-22-8021, A
              COMMERCIAL SITE PLAN AND AMENDED SUBDIVISION TO BE
              LOCATED AT 13487 SOUTH 5600 WEST. APPLICANT – JEFFREY
              BERNSON.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the applicant, Jeffrey Bernson, had submitted a Commercial Site
Plan and Amended Subdivision for 2.1 acres on the west side of the City. The property was
located at 13487 South 5600 West. It was located in the C-G (Gateway Commercial) Zone.
Storage units were a Conditional Use in the C-G Zone and a Conditional Use Permit was
approved in January 2020 and in January 2022. After the Conditional Use was received in
2020, there had been meetings with Staff, but the applicant did not follow up with a Site Plan
application. Approximately a year ago, there were additional meetings and the Conditional
Use Permit was granted again in January 2022. Plans had been exchanged with Staff since.

The Amended Subdivision portion of the application was minimal. Mr. Prestwich reported
that the property line will be adjusted between two lots. A water line runs down the north
property line in an easement and the line was bent slightly. As a result, the property line
needed to be adjusted to keep the easement on the north side of what would be the fence
or the backside of the storage units. An area zoning map was shared. The corner was C-
G and to the east was Parks and Open Space. The City has jurisdiction over the sidewalk
on 5600 West. Beyond that, it was in Herriman’s jurisdiction. The south property line has
an existing eight-foot masonry wall. Along the fence, there is a six-foot existing wall. The
slope of the property is fairly steep and there was a large elevation change from 5600 West.

The proposed Site Plan was shared. Mr. Prestwich noted that it features three buildings,
however, four were listed because the office was attached to the storage units. The layout
was based on setbacks and fire access. He identified the main entrance. Visitors to the site
will pull in, make the turn, visit the storage units, and come back out. Mr. Prestwich reviewed
the setback requirements for the site. The Lighting Plan was shared and showed lights
along the south side of the building that will be pointed downward. The direct light was zero
at the property line, as required by ordinance. There was also a Condition of Approval to
state that the lights need to point downward and be shielded from neighboring properties.

Mr. Prestwich discussed the architecture. He referenced the main entrance from the
northwest corner to the southeast. The building will have accent panels and access on the
inside. The main building in the center is the tallest. The office has some height to it but the
units behind are shorter and run along the property line. The architecture will feature brick
with vertical and horizontal accents. There will also be metal awnings. Additional sample
images were shared with the Commission.

When the Conditional Use Permit came to the Planning Commission for consideration, some
of the discussions related to the building height in relation to the neighborhood to the south
and the park to the east. Based on those conversations, changes were made to the design,
and additional materials were submitted. Mr. Prestwich shared renderings to illustrate the
visual looking north and northeast. He noted that there was a grade difference between
Rosewater Drive and the property. The applicant had done as much as possible to bring
the site down. It was quite a bit higher at the road, so it was cut into to lower the site on the
west side and continue to the east. He reported that the proposal was in compliance with
the 35-foot maximum height.

Mr. Prestwich further reviewed the renderings and noted the windows shown. The buildings
have interior access and there will be hallways with lights on inside. He presented images
of the east and west elevations and shared the Landscaping Plan. Mr. Prestwich explained
that storage units had unique features in the Code. For instance, storage units can have a
greater density of lot coverage than typical commercial structures. For a commercial site,
only 40% lot coverage is permitted. There is a lower parking requirement with a storage
unit, so 60% lot coverage was permitted. Only 20% landscaping of the front area is required.
The application exceeded that with the proposed landscaping. There was a landscape
buffer along the south property line of seven feet and five feet along the park area. Trees
on the inside would be maintained by the developer and the trees on the outside would be
maintained by the City.

One of the Conditions of Approval was that there be screening between the neighborhood
and the storage units and between the park and the storage units. Six feet of landscaping
was proposed. No trees were proposed within the strip because there was a 17-foot utility
easement with a City waterline running through. After speaking with City Staff, there was
no firm response about the waterline but there may be room inside the six-foot strip to plant
trees. The Conditions of Approval were modified to accommodate that possibility, with
Condition of Approval #12. Condition of Approval #12 was not included in the packet as it
was added late in the day. It stated:

      Developers shall plant seven trees along the east property line, subject to City Staff
       identifying an acceptable species and planting location.

Staff reviewed the plans three times. The City Engineer, Public Works Department, Planning
Department, and Fire Department also reviewed the materials. As a result, Staff
recommended approval of the Commercial Site Plan and Amended Subdivision. The item
was noticed as a public hearing but no public comments were received.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the proposal included earth tones on the brick façade. The

that the amendments would add clarity to the ordinance. The Table of Commercial Uses

section of the Code. This created confusion so the desire was to combine those.

list. Some uses had also become outdated and ceased to be meaningful in the code. There

changes. There was also a sample Code with the corrections.

denied this. There were no substantial changes. However, the Planning Commission could
recommend additional changes to the City Council. Commissioner Breinholt asked if the
maps would change. Mr. Prestwich reported that the maps would stay the same. It was
only the table that would be altered so everything was in one place.

permitted. Mr. Prestwich was not certain why that change had been made. It may have
