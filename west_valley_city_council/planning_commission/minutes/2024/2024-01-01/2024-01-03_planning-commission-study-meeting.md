ZONE TEXT CHANGE APPLICATIONS
ZT-8-2024  Petition by WEST VALLEY CITY requesting an amendment to Section 7-6-301
               and the addition of Section 7-7-104.5 to add use-specific regulations that would
               prohibit blood plasma centers on all properties with frontage along Redwood
               Road and on 3300/3500 South between 2700 West and the Jordan River. (Staff –
               Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov)

 Discussion:   Steve Pastorik explained that when the homeless shelter opened in South Salt
               Lake, the homeless population significantly increased, as did the demand for more
               shelters throughout the City.
               Steve noted that Staff believe Blood Plasma Centers would attract the homeless
               population because they can donate their plasma as a source of income and mill
              around that location. He noted that this could hurt the surrounding businesses and
              residential areas. Steve stated that this proposal would prohibit blood plasma
              centers from being placed in areas with an existing transient issue.
              Commissioner McEwen doesn’t feel the plasma center would increase the
              homeless population and does not favor this proposal.
              Commissioner Winters asked about the history of an increase in the homeless
              population near blood plasma centers. Steve said substance abuse issues are often
              seen within that population. He will look for more data on that.
              Fuller asked if he was saying that more homeless are attracted to areas with blood
              plasma centers.

SUBDIVISION APPLICATIONS
SMI-6-2024 Petition by FIRST BAPTIST CHURCH OF GRANGER is requesting minor
              subdivision approval for the Mountain High Subdivision and amending lot 74
              Wright Subdivision No. 10. The subdivision will consist of 2 lots on 2.9 acres, is
              zoned C-2 (General Commercial) and is located at 3831 South 2700 West. (Staff
              – Steve Lehman at 801-963-3311 or steve.lehman@wvc-ut.gov)

Discussion:   Steve Lehman explained that the request is to subdivide the subject property into
              two lots. He stated that a Baptist Church currently occupies the property, and they
              want to downsize. Steve said the property owner to the north is interested in
              developing the recently rezoned commercial property.
              Steve mentioned that there is an existing development agreement as part of the
              rezoning. As part of that agreement, the buildings are limited to single-story, a
              masonry wall will need to replace the existing fence separating the subject
              property from the residential property, and street trees will need to be added along
              2700 West, matching the spacing of those across the street.
              Steve noted a specific use planned for the north lot containing a drive-thru. He
              pointed out that the City Council has stated they want patrons to avoid crossing
              the drive aisle to enter the establishment, and the concept plan has been modified
              to meet their request.


DISCUSSION ITEMS
    Review staff presentation regarding the General Plan update
    Review staff presentation on trends that impact land use
    Discuss development/redevelopment possibilities along Redwood Road between 3500
     South and 4100 South

Discussion:   Steve Pastorik explained that the City is working on a comprehensive update to
              our General Plan. He noted that the last update was in 2015. Steve said with a
grant from the Wasatch Front Regional Council, we hired a consultant to look at
three different things:
   1. Evaluate the development or redevelopment potential of properties within five
      areas of the City.
   2. Identify the most likely options for development or redevelopment.
   3. Compare the revenue to the City from the different options identified.

Steve stated that the consultant presented two options for each property. Option A
is lighter on residential and heavier on commercial, and Option B has more of a
residential use than commercial. He explained the differences between the two
options.
Steve reviewed trends and projections that impact land development and use. He
stated that over the next twenty-five years, an increase of 1.5 million jobs is
projected. The county's population is going to increase to approximately 484,000
by 2060.
He noted that West Valley City has higher household sizes than Salt Lake County.
Steve pointed out that the median age for West Valley City is three years younger
than the county's. He explained that both Salt Lake County and West Valley City
have had more attached product constructed than detached product. Steve
reviewed income vs. housing prices and noted that one needs to make $150,000
per year to afford a median-priced home.
Steve said remote work is one of the most impactful land use factors. He stated
that before the pandemic, six percent of the workforce worked from home; at the
end of last year, it had increased to between twenty-eight and twenty-nine percent.
Steve noted that some people believe the percentage of work-from-home
employees will stay above twenty percent, directly affecting office use.
Steve presented data showing that there are fewer families with kids and more
with seniors.
Steve presented a map depicting the areas that the consultant determined were
likely to be developed or redeveloped in the short term (> five years) and long
term (over five years). He explained that the consultant then provided two options
for development or redevelopment. Steve noted that he would like the Planning
Commission to discuss what option would work best for each property. He
explained that he will share the feedback from the Commission with the Council
when he has this discussion with them.
Steve presented the first property located at 3995 S. Redwood Road. He reviewed
option A, which is for more residential and less commercial, and option B, which
is for more commercial and less residential. Steve noted that a proposal for a
townhome development had already been denied. Therefore, option A would be
more likely for single-family homes than option B for 26 townhomes.
Commissioner Winters said building a townhome development on the southern
portion of the property would be possible. Steve said that is a possibility, but the
townhomes would be sandwiched between two commercial properties.
Commissioner McEwen asked if this property is owned by Smiths. Steve pointed
out the portion of the property that Smiths owns and said the remainder has a
different owner.
The Commission agreed that option A would be the best option.
The next property discussed is located at 3899 S. Redwood Road.
Commissioner Winters asked if this property would meet the criteria for higher-
density housing. Steve said it would as far as the zoning; however, that stretch of
road already has the highest concentration of apartments.
Commissioner Winters is leaning toward option B because he doesn't feel a
single-family development would work here.
Commissioner McEwen wants to see more commercial development in this area.
Chair Fuller agreed with Commissioner McEwen that option A would be the
better option, but he would like to see the entire property used for commercial
purposes.
Commissioner Winters said that he appreciates the feedback from Commissioner
McEwen and Fuller because they live near this property.
The next property discussed is 3910 S. Redwood Road. Steve Pastorik explained
its current uses.
Commissioner McEwen would like to see this property have higher commercial
use so that he would choose option B. He said the Redevelopment Department
could look into a large retail use. Steve stated that there has been demand for pad
site developments, but it has been a while since we have seen a larger use, such as
Walmart or Target. The Commission feels option B would be the better option but
would prefer the entire parcel to be developed commercially.
Chair Fuller mentioned Lamberts owns most of the property, and he doesn't
believe they would sell.
Commissioner Winters said it would be helpful to know what businesses are
needed in certain areas of the City. He asked if there is a way to attain a map
showing how much residential vs. commercial property is in the area and, if it is
commercial, what type. Steve said yes, but it would be hard to read due to the
large amount of data. He explained that the colors would blend if we used a color-
coded map, making it almost impossible to see the individual colors. However,
Steve mentioned that if there are more detailed maps the Commission would like
for these discussions, we can look into that.
The first property discussed is located at 3777 S Redwood Rd. Steve feels option
A makes sense because of the limited frontage.
Commissioner Porter said that Steve's data indicates the need for more residential
development. However, the problem is that the residents want to avoid adding
more high-density residential to their neighborhood, and you will unlikely find
anyone who wants to develop a single-family product here.
Steve pointed out that most recent townhome developments just south of Paris
Furniture started out with units priced around $400,000 per unit, and toward the
end of the development, they were selling for half a million dollars. He stated that
they cost more than the single-family homes next to them. Steve said that staff felt
that adding a for-sale townhome development would increase housing but not
rental product.
Chair Fuller asked if there is any data on the number of police calls for townhome
vs. condo developments. Steve said that he does have data on the frequency of
police calls and criminal behavior that he will provide to the Commission. He
noted that single-family detached rentals seemed to have the most criminal
activity. Steve stated that, when looking at a number of police calls and criminal
behavior for larger complexes the management is the biggest factor. How
thorough their screening process is, and how effectively they handle problem
tenants.
Commissioner McEwen feels there needs to be a balance throughout the City.
Currently, the east side has more high-density housing than the rest of West
Valley City. Steve said that he understands Commissioner McEwen's concern.
However, this particular parcel does not front onto Redwood Road, making it
difficult for a commercial development to succeed.
The next property discussed is located at 3750 S. Redwood Rd.
Steve explained that the residents' two biggest objections to redeveloping this
property are removing the drive-in and losing the nostalgia and moving the swap
meet.
Chair Fuller believes most of the objections regarding moving the swap meet
were from vendors operating there. Steve said yes, and so did their family
members. He pointed out that the rent is quite low, and it would be hard to find
another location that would compare.
Commissioner McEwen liked the previous proposal for this property, which
included a mix of single-family and town homes. He feels this would be a great
location for a residential development, so he would choose option A.
Commissioner Winters agreed.
The next property discussed is located at 3725 S Redwood Rd.
Commissioner Porter said a commercial development, option A, would be the
best option here, as it has frontage on Redwood Road. He doesn't believe this lot
is large enough for a townhome development.
Commissioner Winters agreed. He noted that smaller townhome developments
don't have strong HOAs.
               The next property discussed is located at 3660 S Redwood Road. Steve explained
               that this property is similar to the one discussed previously. He said the
               Commission might also feel option A is the best option here.
               The Commission agrees that option A would be the better option.
               The next property discussed is located at 3565 S. Redwood Rd.
               Steve felt the consultant looked at higher density here because the site has a large
               warehouse building, and that would be the most cost-effective option for
               redevelopment.
               Commissioner McEwen feels a commercial development would work best here,
               off 3500 S. and close to I-15.
               Chair Fuller asked Steve how the consultant determined a property would develop
               or redevelop in the next five years. Steve said he looked at the County Assessor
               data to find the cost of the land. For example, the drive-in property is relatively
               low because there is little development. However, there is a large warehouse on
               this property, which holds value.
               Commissioner Lovato said he struggles with adding a residential development on
               Redwood Road because he would not want to live there. However, his son has
               mentioned not needing a yard, so a townhome development on a main street could
               work for him. He understands there are many commercial uses along Redwood
               Road, and adding a residential development in this location would be beneficial.
               Steve pointed out that the consultant looks to add high density for the property to
               be profitable.
               Commissioner Winters said this property would work well with a mix of uses. He
               pointed out that all of the properties we looked at during this meeting are within a
               one-mile stretch of Redwood Road. He feels that looking at this section as a
               whole would create a more cohesive stretch of Redwood Road. Steve said that he
               thinks the consultant was trying to do something like that by presenting two
               options for each property, one having a commercial focus and the other
               residential.
               Chair Fuller mentioned that this area has a sizeable transient demographic, so he
               is curious to know who would feel comfortable living there.
               Commissioner Winters said if he had a better feel for what we're missing, what
               we might be missing regarding general types of businesses. That could help me
               understand the ratio of commercial to residential. Steve said he would work on a
               reference map for the Commission.
