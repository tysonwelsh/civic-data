ADDISON VISTA SUBDIVISION – 5700 South 800 West – Project #22-054 & 22-055
Susan Nixon presented the request for a residential infill project, which is conditional use, as
well as preliminary and final subdivision review. The property is currently in the R-1.8 zone. The
zoning was changed last December from A-1 to the R-1.8 with the intention of subdividing the
property; the property is 1.62 acres. Years ago, Murray realized they were losing larger parcels
of property in the valley, so they adopted this residential infill option in Section 17.58 in the
Zoning Code that allowed for smaller parcels less than two acres to be developed. This gave
the Land Use Authority/Planning Commission the authority to vary right-of-way improvements to
limit public right of ways and other improvements. She showed the subdivision plat from the
meeting attachments. The applicant is requesting the residential infill only for the rear setback of
20 feet, they want to maintain the R-1.8 setback in the front of 25 feet. This is being requested
in order to have the proper width at the setback as each lot must have at least 80 feet at the
front setback for an interior lot, and 90 feet on a corner. She showed a chart with the lots and
future addresses, all the lots have at least 8000 square feet and range from 8000 square feet to

just over 13,000 square feet. All the lots meet the front setback of 25 feet and 20 feet in the rear,
along with all the side yard, interior and corner setbacks. The plan shows two streetlights,
shown as #6 on the plan, along with all the driveways for each; the city engineer has reviewed
and approved those. This proposal includes a 42-foot right-of-way, including 25 feet of hard
surface, six feet of sidewalk and 2.5 feet of curb and gutter. There will be no park strip. There is
also planned a six-foot vinyl fence at the northwest and south sides of the property, which will
need to meet the fence regulations of six feet maximum and reduced along 800 West to a three
to four foot maximum height. When properties of at least one acre are developed, state law and
Murray City Code requires they meet Low Impact Development (LID) for smaller developments.
This plan creatively incorporated green space to have water drain, and then go into the city
system through lot 6 to the north. Based on the application, City Code Section 17.100 and
review of plans, staff is recommending approval of the conditional use for residential infill, as
well preliminary and final subdivision, based on the 10 conditions shown in the meeting packet.
She noted that notices were sent out, no public comment was received.

5700 South 800 West, subject to conditions 1-10. Seconded by Ms. Patterson.

THE WYATT – 4926, 4938 & 4940 South Center Street – Project #22-078
Mr. Smallwood presented the application for design review and preliminary condo subdivision
approval. The property address is 4930 and 4938 South Center Street. This is in the MCCD
Zone, which allows for multi-family residential. The two combined properties together are 0.33
Planning Commission Meeting

acres, and they are proposing a 50,000 square foot building with 26 individual units. The
applicant is proposing for those units to be owner occupied, hence the condo subdivision
preliminary approval request. Mr. Smallwood showed what was presented before the MCCD
Design Review Committee. They had some concerns with the blank frontages on the south
side, and there were a number of different materials being used; overall, they asked for it to be
simplified. He then showed the updated plan from the meeting attachments, everything in the
maroon color is brick, which came from working with some of the nearby residents. The
applicant held a neighborhood meeting on his own and discussed this with some of the local
residents. He also simplified some of the color schemes so it was a little more uniform and
cleaner. Mr. Smallwood presented more images from the meeting attachments including the
front and noted that he was impressed with the changes made to the south side, incorporating
different materials to help see something other than white stucco. The front will be a mix of
stucco, metal accents on top and brick encompassing the majority of the building. This building
is generally lower than the Center Court Apartments; it is a 3.5 story building that includes a
subfloor with units under the upper three stories. Regarding the site design, the MCCD requires
a seven-foot sidewalk and five-foot park strip, the applicant has shown that on the plans
submitted and included in the meeting attachments. Staff believes the proposed bike rack and
bench for the proposed frontage is adequate. Open amenity space is required at 15% or 2156
square feet, the applicant has provided almost 3900 square feet which is mixed between the
actual open space on the building and mixed in with the amenity space inside building. The
landscape plan is not very detailed, but he is putting in the required amount of trees and will
have to conform with the required number of bushes and other landscaping aspects per the
landscaping ordinance. The parking garage will have mechanically stacked parking spaces, as
well as two ADA spaces. At subgrade there are four 2 bed/2 bath units, the second floor has
four additional units on south side with accesses from both the south and east. Regarding the
stacked parking, he showed some pictures from the meeting attachments and discussed how
the stacked parking will look and function. It was noted that he was told a Suburban would fit
into one of the parking stalls easily, but he will let the applicant discuss that further. He reviewed
the brochure for the combo lift stacked parking, which is available in the meeting attachments.
He showed some YouTube videos with examples of the mechanical lifts, and the links for those
and others are also in the meeting attachments. Above the parking areas, five units will be on
the next floor directly on top, with four more on the other side and a fitness areas and deck. The
fourth floor has five more units on the north side, with a flex space and deck, and four others on
the other side. There is a rooftop amenity that faces away from the existing single family
residential, it includes a rooftop deck facing west and a social area facing the same way with no
balcony. The MCCD Design Review Committee has already been through this and based on the
analysis and preliminary subdivision plans the staff feels this meets all the requirements and is
in harmony with the zone and purpose and intent of the general plan and design guidelines.
Staff is recommending the planning commission grant the design review approval and
preliminary subdivision approval, subject to the nine conditions.

Ms. Patterson asked about the comments regarding housing prices going down with too much
housing. Her understanding is that’s not the situation the valley is currently in.
Mr. Smallwood said no, we are not in that predicament at all, and he doesn’t see that changing
for the next 10-15 years minimum if they are aggressive in providing a lot of housing. That is
part of what happens, it’s basic supply and demand, when you oversupply your demand shrinks,
and when you don’t have enough supply your demand increases. If the market gets flooded with
a lot of housing, it doesn’t matter what type of housing, if it is built and people have places to
live they will not spend as much money to buy new places to lives.
Ms. Milkavich said she finds that discussion a little ironic, as yesterday she had the same
discussion with a builder on her street, as she was a little concerned that we are so excited to
meet the need for housing that maybe we are moving too fast. He said that his projections say
we won’t meet the need at the current rate. Who knows whose projections are correct, but she
also heard on the radio another discussion where they were saying that the building rates have
drastically slowed and we are way behind on the need.
Mr. Smallwood wants to be careful discussing politics and said that with the raising interest
rates it is making is harder for people to purchase even starter homes. The demand is still there,
especially in this valley, but now it’s even more expensive to try and get into a house when we
already have inflated housing prices. It’s a balancing act, demand will probably continue to rise
for quite some time. He hasn’t seen recent stats, but the last ones he saw show we are at a
2000 home shortage statewide every year, and that could exacerbate in the more developed
areas where people want to live.
Mr. Lowry noted that no one has a crystal ball to project what will happen. There have been
