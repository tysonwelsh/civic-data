1.     PUBLIC MEETING – A Petition to Approve a Design Review Application for an
       Expansion of Mark Miller Subaru’s Showroom located at 3535 South State Street.

ACTION ITEM

Applicant:     Mark Miller Subaru, represented by Josh Goldsmith
Address:       3535 South State Street

Ms. Ungricht reported that above the request is for a Design Review Application for Mark Miller
Subaru located at 3535 South State Street. Planning Commission approval is required for the
applicant’s recently approved Conditional Use Permit (“CUP”) because the dealership is adjacent
to a Residential district. To the north is R-1 and Residential Multiple zoning and R-1 Single-Family
to the south. The applicant is proposing to do an 11,000-square-foot addition to the front of the
dealership. The location of the proposed addition was identified on a rendering displayed. It will
be attached to the building and is not a new structure. As part of the addition, about 8,000 square
feet will be for a new showroom and just over 3,000 square feet for new office space.

Currently, the entire building is on one level. The materials will remain the same with Aluminum
Composite Material (“ACM”) with a slate tower and blue trim along the top. The newly proposed
elevation will be 28 feet, which meets the City's requirements. Buildings in the area are required
to be 26 to 36 feet tall. The current building does not meet that standard, but the new addition will
meet the height requirement. All of the building materials meet City standards and will remain the
same as what exists. Staff reviewed the proposal and renderings, which were determined to meet
all Design Standards and criteria. The proposed Site Plan shows State Street on the left-hand side
and Winslow to the north. The applicants are removing a few lanes of customer parking in front
but have adequate parking on site for the stalls to be relocated elsewhere. The new showroom will
have a curve to provide articulation to the building.

The General Plan consideration is that the request meets the Economic Development statements
and goals of keeping businesses in the City and allowing them to expand their operations. Staff
recommended that the Planning Commission review and approve the Design Review Application
for the Mark Miller Subaru Auto Dealership located at 3535 South State Street based on the
information submitted by the applicant, the General Plan considerations, and ordinance analysis

Commissioner Bellina asked when the CUP was approved. Ms. Ungricht reported that it was

Plan and the continued buffer that is required. They are considering an eight-foot concrete fence
around the perimeter of the property that will buffer much of the noise and visual impacts. They
also had a fairly detailed discussion and agreed to meet subsequently on a Landscaping Plan that
can further mitigate and enhance the buffer, which could be taller than the wall. Staff felt good
about the discussions and the applicant took to heart the Planning Commission's intent.

owner. He has not yet visited the site at night to verify its success.

off. Mr. Weidenhamer agreed to follow up. Staff’s understanding was that the lighting is now
consistent with what was submitted and meets the City’s valuation for foot-candles trespassing
from the site.

2.      PUBLIC MEETING – A Petition for Final Plat Approval for a Two-Lot Plat, to
        Construct and Operate Flex Buildings located at 800 West 3300 South.

ACTION ITEM

Applicant:     Dakota Pacific, Represented by Will Clark
Address:       800 West 3300 South

Ms. Ungricht presented the Staff Report and stated that the application was previously before the
Planning Commission on February 15 at which time the Preliminary Plat was approved. This is a
two-step process. The first was the Zoning Map Amendment, which the City Council approved on
February 8, 2023, and the zoning was changed from Commercial Corridor to Flex. The applicant
is now ready to replat the parcels. The existing conditions are the Golf Around golf course. This
plat differs from other plats in that there are parcels on the plat and lots. Typically, a plat includes
only a lot that is required to have the right-of-way improvements installed and the parcels are not
platted. If the applicants come back in, they will need a plat to convert to a lot. It was noted that
the applicants are in the process of entering into a Ground Lease with Central Valley Water
Reclamation Facility (“CVWRF”). The phasing of the project is for the two parcels to the west,
which are outlined in blue. Currently, those parcels expand into the Central Valley Plat. Due to the
complexity of the easements on the CVWRF property, they will have parcels and lots, but they
can only develop on the lots that will be approved.

The two lots that will be created from the subdivision application were identified as Lots 1 and 2,
which have access from 900 West and 3300 South. The area to the east was identified as Parcel A.
The applicants will need a plat amendment to develop a lot and put in the infrastructure
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Meeting                                                 7
April 18, 2024


improvements at that time. The ALTA survey was presented with the red area being roughly,
where Phases 1 and 2 will be. The yellow line is a 30-foot sewer easement, which will remain. It
serves the sewer treatment plant so they will build around that easement. The two buildings on the
left-hand side were identified as the first phase and will be developed if the plat is approved. The
future buildings to the east will require another subdivision plat.

The proposed renderings and elevations for the first building were presented. It was determined to
meet all City Design Standards and has the required window articulation and building materials.
Building B is the second building in and also meets all Design Standards. The development will
benefit the area as it meets several General Plan considerations. It is a great location for the
proposed use and will have higher-density employment-based uses, especially due to its proximity
to the freeway. The site is not ideal for housing, however, because it is a Ground Lease. If CVWRF
ever needs to expand its operations, they do not want to have residential use and require residents
to relocate. They are taking advantage of the location. 3300 South is targeted as a corridor for
growing businesses, and the subject property will add economic interest to the area with the square
footage that it will provide. The first building is about 400,000 square feet in size and the second
is roughly 200,000 square feet. As part of the plat and the rezone, there will be a wide sidewalk
along 3300 South, which will be an upgrade from what currently exists. It will also help with
drought mitigation because they will go from a golf course that uses a lot of water to an industrial
space that meets the City’s landscaping requirements but is limited compared to the existing use.

Staff recommended approval based upon the information submitted by the applicant, the General
Plan considerations, and ordinance analysis and subject to the Findings of Fact and Conditions
enumerated in the Staff Report.

Commissioner Ewell asked about the upgraded sidewalk system and if it will be completed when
the other phases are underway. Mr. Weidenhamer reported that the sidewalk on 3300 South will
be required on the front end at which time they will complete the infrastructure and utility work.
It will be required as a public improvement. The second phase will include the upgraded sidewalk.
A question was raised as to whether the sidewalk is being considered as a recreational opportunity
for the Parks and Open Space Goal #2. Ms. Ungricht reported that it helps connect to the Jordan
Parkway and other parks. There is also a new park that is having its official grand opening within
the next month. It will help connect people to parks by providing a safer walking route than
currently exists.

Commissioner Pechmann commented that the constituents he has spoken to about the loss of the
golf course bemoan the loss of wildlife. He wondered what kind of landscaping this project will
provide and if there is any thought to maintaining biodiversity. Because there is an emphasis in
the area with the Tracy Aviary, he would like to see a partnership. Ms. Ungricht stated that the
Landscaping Plan that was submitted meets all of the City’s landscape standards and their
approved plant list. She offered to see what plants would be recommended for preserving the
wildlife habitat. She was not sure, however, how viable it would be due to the proximity to 3300
South.

Commissioner Bellina asked if there is a tree inventory or if they plan to replace the trees, they
remove. Ms. Ungricht stated that they may require a specific number of trees and spacing, but they
meet the number of trees that are required per Code.

Commissioner Pechmann clarified that this is just zoning and asked if the applicant will come back
for Design Review before breaking ground. Ms. Ungricht indicated that in this instance they will
not need a Design Review Application as it is purely looking at the subdivision lot to ensure that
it meets all legal requirements. Staff will conduct an internal review and look at the Building Plans
when the Building Permit is issued that will include review of the Landscaping Plan.

The applicant, Will Clark, was present on behalf of Dakota Pacific. He reported that this is an
industrial project that has been in the works for about five years. They have worked diligently with
the City and Central Valley Water Reclamation on a Ground Lease to develop a new industrial
park. The impetus of the project is that they are a large landowner in South Salt Lake and other
areas where they redevelop industrial sites. They came to the City to ask if there was an opportunity
for redevelopment. This opportunity came up and they were excited to be moving forward to Final
Plat approval. They hope to move the project forward in the near future. There are numerous
benefits for local stakeholders such as the groundwater that will be saved from being sent to the
Great Salt Lake, a property tax increase, and economic interest in 3300 South.

Commissioner Pechmann asked about their plans for native plants. Mr. Clark stated that their
landscaping plans were designed in accordance with City Code. They had not necessarily looked
at the number of trees being removed versus the number to be planted. All of the landscaping has
been designed to conform to the natural environment. Commissioner Pechmann suggested that at
some point they potentially include a butterfly garden or similar since the goodwill from the public
will be enormous.

Mr. Weidenhamer commented that in terms of economics, the tax base cannot be understated as it
currently generates about $5,000 in property taxes. They expect as much as $600,000 per year
once the property is developed. The move for Central Valley has to protect its constituents in the
long term. The second phase of the project has not been producing for them, but they need to keep
it for the next generation in the event they need to use the facility. The Ground Lease is a great
way to produce revenue and protect shareholders and residents from significant rate increases.
That has been appealing and a drive for the board. With regard to water use, the golf course uses
150 to 180 million gallons of water per year. The proposed project is estimated to reduce that by
approximately 140 million gallons per year. While the developer needs to do a great job with the
landscaping, it is a significant value add to the broader valley in terms of water consumption.

Commissioner Bellina asked about the access. Mr. Clark stated that the access will be fully
installed as well as the 3300 South expansion. They are coordinating with the Utah Department of
Transportation (“UDOT”) as well. Loading will take place on the west side.
