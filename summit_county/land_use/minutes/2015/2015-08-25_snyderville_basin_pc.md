the primary use of the Home Savings Bank building to office uses; John Kucera, 
applicant – Sean Lewis, County Planner 
 
County Planner Sean Lewis presented the staff report and provided a history of the Home 
Savings Bank building, noting that it has been vacant for several years.  He recalled that a 
different applicant came to the County Council in February of 2015 to request a Special 
Exception, because the original development agreement, which has expired, specifically

prohibited uses other than commercial banking and financial.  They requested office uses 
but had no specific tenants at that time.  The County Council included a condition of 
approval that a future tenant would have to come to the Planning Commission for a 
Conditional Use Permit (CUP) for whatever use they wanted in the building.  The 
building has since been sold, and the new owner is requesting a general CUP for any of 
the uses the Council anticipated might be allowed in the building because he does not 
want the CUP to be a stumbling block for potential tenants.  After applying for this CUP, 
the applicant entered into a potential lease agreement with a law firm with under 10 
employees and only one or two client visits per week.  It is anticipated that the upper 
floor of the building would be used for executive offices.  Staff felt that General Offices 
was the best definition in the Code for the type of use going into this building.  Staff 
recommended that the Planning Commission approve a CUP for the potential law office 
and other potential office uses. 
 
John Kucera, the applicant, stated that it was his understanding that the CUP would be for 
the entire building for all of the office-type uses, such as professional office space.  He 
believed it would be too intensive to have each business owner come to the Planning 
Commission for a CUP and requested approval for professional office space for the 
whole building, regardless of the tenant. 
 
Planner Lewis noted that the County Council approved a 10% expansion of the building 
to enclose the drive-through area, and the applicant does not propose that as part of the 
conditional use. 
 
Commissioner Klingenstein reported that he asked Planner Lewis about General Office 
versus Moderate Office and noted that traffic and parking were discussed by the County 
Council.  He questioned whether the General Office use would open the door to a broader 
commercial use in the future and whether General Office is the correct category.  Planner 
Lewis stated that the concern he heard from the neighbors at the County Council meeting 
was that they did not want a use that would overwhelm the parking area and filter into the

Commissioner Lawson asked if the 15 parking spaces would limit what could be 
approved as office space.  Planner Lewis replied that the development agreement only 
addresses the number of trips, and most of the bank customers would have driven

use.  Any use will have to go through a traffic impact fee analysis, which will allow Staff 
to determine whether a use generates too much traffic.  Commissioner Lawson asked if 
they could add a condition that future office uses must be compatible with the number of 
available parking spaces.  Planner Lewis replied that would not change anything, because 
Staff would look at that anyway. 
 
Commissioner Peck asked what control the County would have if a business were to 
eventually end up with 20 employees and only 15 parking spaces.  She asked if anything 
in the Code would prevent someone from parking on the street.  Planner Lewis replied 
that nothing in the Code would prohibit someone from parking on a public street except 
between November and April.  The Council’s intent was to have whatever use would go 
into this building to have a public hearing through the CUP process to be sure the public

Deputy County Attorney Jami Brackin asked if it was the County Council’s intent that

Commissioner DeFord stated that he would not be willing to approve anything other

Commissioner Peck made a motion to approve a Conditional Use Permit to 
allow the proposed use of the Home Savings Bank SPA as Office, Moderate, 
to accommodate the potential tenant of a law office based upon the following 
Findings of Fact and Conclusions of Law as shown in the staff report: 
Findings of Fact: 
1. SUNI, LC, is the listed fee title owner of Parcel HSBSPA-A. 
2. Parcel HSBSPA-A is 0.83 acre in size. 
3. Parcel HSBSPA-A is located at 4580 North Silver Springs Drive. 
4. According to Summit County assessment records, a commercial 
building was erected on Parcel HSBSPA-A in 2002 in compliance with 
the Development Agreement in effect at that time. 
5. Parcel HSBSPA-A was rezoned to Specially Planned Area in 2002 via 
Summit County Ordinance 428. 
6. The structure on Parcel HSBSPA-A is considered to be “legal non-
conforming,” as it was developed as part of a SPA process, and the 
accompanying development agreement that sets forth use and bulk 
regulations has now expired, per the five-year sunset clause written 
into the agreement. 
7. The adjacent uses are commercial, office, and institutional in nature. 
8. The Summit County Council granted a Special Exception to expand 
the list of permitted uses available via the Home Savings Bank 
Development Agreement to include General Offices, Moderate 
Offices, Intensive Offices, and Medical/Dental Offices on January 7, 
2015. 
9. The proposed uses are office in nature.

10. Staff does not anticipate any non-mitigatable impacts related to 
traffic, parking, lighting, or noise as a result of the proposal. 
11. The property has been used for commercial and office uses for the

Conclusions of Law:
