2. Summit Veterinary Urgent Care; 1227 West 9000 South; Conditional Use Permit for
Veterinary Services; SC-2 Zone; Summit Veterinary Urgent Care/Cassie Czerny &
Taylor Howard (applicant) [#32346; parcel 27-02-301-093]
Taylor Howard and Cassie Czerny, applicants, are seeking to open an urgent care facility for animals.
Ms. Czerny currently owns Hillside Veterinary Hospital in Cottonwood Heights, and they have been
struggling to keep up with the need for urgent care, which falls between general practice and
emergency care. The facility would be open Monday through Friday from 8:00 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. This
facility would be fed with animals from her hospital that need to be seen without having such a long
wait. If the situation warrants, they will send the animal to an ER for treatment or for overnight care.
Dr. Howard stated that he also operates a mobile vet service, so they have a full range of service types.
Ms. Czerny clarified that the business is privately owned, and there would not be any overnight stays.
Megan Jensen gave an overview of the application, zoning, and site plan. Business hours are listed as
7:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m. Monday through Friday. There will be five to seven employees on site at a time,
and three to five clients per hour. She reviewed the conditions of approval.
Staff recommended that the Planning Commission approve the Conditional Use Permit for veterinary
services (small animals only), located at 1227 West 9000 S in an SC-2 zone, with the conditions of
approval listed in this staff report.

3. Redwood Housing; 9222 South Redwood Road; Future Land Use Map Amendment for
0.56 acres from Community Commercial and Parks and Open Lands to Professional
Office and Rezone 0.56 acres from P-O (Professional Office) Zone to P-O (SHO)
(Professional Office — Senior Housing Overlay) Zone, and Development Plan; JZW
Architects/Gary Knapp (applicant) [#27152, #27744, #27745; parcel 27-03-376-007]

Waleska Iglesias, property owner, reviewed the proposed rendering for a 33-unit, for rent, active 55+

apartment complex. They started this process 2 2 years ago. The building has 4 floors with parking

underneath and around the facility. The units are one-bedroom or studio. The facility includes a

courtyard, exercise room, common areas, lobby, and offices on first floor. She showed some statistics

from Census.gov that show the older population in the U.S. is growing at its fastest rate since 1880 to

1990. So, there is a need for the active 55+ community. A chart showing Mortgage Payment to Rent

Ratio indicates that rent is more affordable and there aren’t additional HOA fees like a condominium

or home might have. The 55+ tenant is stable, respectful, safety-conscious, responsible, and tends to

look out for their neighbors. An apartment is a good way to downsize and to have maintenance-free
living and to create social opportunities with neighbors who have similar interests. The product is not
assisted living.

Scott Dilley, property owner, reviewed how multi-family housing increases revenues with more people

spending their dollars locally and it reduces fiscal burdens by saving costs that are incurred by critical

physical infrastructure, such as new roads, and water lines. This product can help to bridge the housing
gap that Governor Cox has reported on. He said they have the experience and a good track record with
other owner-operated apartments that they have renovated.

Waleska Iglesias stated that the property currently has an old house that is next to a 6-lane road and a

credit union. It would be difficult to sell the home in that situation. David Schmidt, the property owner

to the north and owner of Willow Cove apartments, sent a letter of support for the project.

Gary Knapp, JZW Architects, agreed with the comments that it will be a great project, especially
compared to what is on the property now.

Ammon Allen asked for clarification on the number of parking spaces for the residents and visitors.
Gary Knapp stated there are 21 spaces on the main level and the bottom level has 28 spaces. Thirty of
the units are 1-bedroom and three are studios. The number of stalls provided allows for at least one per
unit and he thought that having five or six visitor stalls would be adequate.

Ammon Allen said they go by what is required by code, but he asked them to consider providing at
least two stalls per unit for an active 55+, especially if there are two working adults where both would
need transportation. He referred to a development that recently went away from senior housing
because they were not able to fill the units. He asked what their contingency plan is.

Scott Dilley said that this is an apartment complex and not a condominium, so there is more
affordability than having a mortgage. They believe that there is a need for 55+ apartments that would
attract singles and empty nesters, and they would not be requesting to remove the overlay.

Waleska Iglesias said that type of request usually comes when it is a single-family purchase product
versus an apartment. There is a bigger percentage of people who want apartments rather than
purchasing, because of HOA fees, and yard and home maintenance, etc. whereas an apartment does not
have those fees and rent usually includes most utility costs and building repairs.

Ammon Allen understood her to state that in their experience filling a rental unit is easier than a
purchased product.

Ms. Iglesias said that is correct, she had no concerns with filling the units.

Lisa Elgin provided an overview of the project. The property owner to the north is not opposed to the
development. To the west is an apartment complex buffered by a canal. To the south is a credit union,
and to the east is a business park. Staff did not see any compatibility issues with this use in the area.
The rezoning will add the Senior Housing Overlay to the property that is already zoned P-O. The land
use map would change to professional office, which matches the properties to the east. She reviewed
the many services that are found within a quarter mile of the property. The staff recommendation was
to deny the application, but she clarified that the planning staff supports the application as a good
project in a good location. The reason for the recommendation to deny was that the adequate public
facilities study was not completed, which was a miscommunication on staff's part. Staff asked that the
public hearing be opened for comment and closed with a motion to postpone the decision until after
engineering completes the public facilities report. It may be a month or two before the information will
be provided.

Based on the analysis contained in the report, staff recommended that the Planning Commission make
a recommendation of denial (negative recommendation) to the City Council for the Future Land Use
Map Amendment and Rezone. Staff also recommended denial (negative recommendation) to the City
Council of the Development Plan with modifications as suggested by staff. The negative
recommendation is due to the Adequate Public Facilities Memo. The applicant and the Public Utilities
Department are working on this issue and anticipate bringing the item back to Planning Commission at

Requirements of Approval:

1. Development Plan - The plan calls for a 15’ rear setback, however, the P-O Zone requires a 20’

Suggestions:

Map Amendment and Zoning Map Amendment.

Trish Hatch asked what advantage the senior housing overlay gives the project over a multifamily

product.

Lisa Elgin said the overlay allows for senior housing in the P-O zone with the density being

determined by the city council. Also, senior housing is exempt from the limits in the balanced housing

ordinance. She explained that the applications include a general land use map amendment, a rezoning,
and the development plan. Staff is recommending a condition for the development plan that the rear
setback be adjusted and the suggestion to the city council that the minimum number of type A units
would be 25% or eight units, which makes it more favorable for seniors to age in place.

Trish Hatch said she was not a fan of the requested height increase. She said it felt like we are trying to

make something happen that does not fit here. She said that the property definitely needs to be

developed, but she did not know if this was the right place for this product.

Jay Thomas asked about the parking requirement per the ordinance.

Lisa Elgin said the senior housing overlay allows for a reduced number of parking spaces, which has

been met.

John Roberts asked what the city can do to prevent being backdoored if the owners decide to remove it

from the senior housing overlay, which would cause a non-conforming situation as to the multifamily

use and parking requirement.

Lisa Elgin said that would be up to the City Council to decide. There is an ordinance amendment in

process that would not allow the senior housing overlay to be removed once it is approved.

George Winn said there seemed to be a concern with parking, but his observation was that if someone

had an issue with the parking situation then they could choose not to live there.

Lisa Elgin said she had extensive opportunities to visit the other senior housing apartments in West

Jordan and almost all of the units were occupied by a single person.

Hieu Nguyen, West Jordan resident speaking remotely, stated that he supported the project. Parking is
arbitrary and people will figure out a way to overcome that problem with solutions such as rental bikes
and car sharing. He said the developer should make sure that the ground level is walkable, because the
West Jordan City Center station will be developed in the future and it will connect well with bike
paths, but the pedestrian aspect is often overlooked.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

George Winn said staff has indicated that there were some technical issues with the adequate public
facilities report and is recommending the decision be tabled.

Ammon Allen agreed with Commissioner Hatch, and he did not think that his mind would be changed
by tabling the item. He thought that the apartment complex was fine, but not with the senior housing
overlay. He would be in favor of forwarding a recommendation for denial.

Jay Thomas agreed. The Commission has seen this one too many times where it starts out as one thing.
He understood that there may be a code amendment to stop removal of the overlay, but at this point he
was leaning toward denial.

George Winn spoke in favor of tabling the decision. The applicant and staff have put a lot of work into
designing a clear proposal with clear recommendations.

Ammon Allen said he was not wholly against the option of tabling, and he asked what the adequate
public facilities memo would tell them that would alleviate his concerns with the senior housing
overlay.

Lisa Elgin said that the adequate public facilities memo will not have any bearing on the senior
housing overlay.

Nathan Nelson said the biggest concern is that the current property is a small site with one house and
the proposal is for 33 living units, so there will be a higher need to draw water and sewer capacity.
Staff will go through the analysis to look at the availability, size, and capacity to see what is needed
and then make a determination. It is not in his department, but his opinion was that the city could
probably handle what is needed for this proposal from a facilities standpoint, but the study will verify
that. Staff will also make sure that stormwater will be collected and detained where necessary and that
it has an outlet into the city storm drain.

Ammon Allen asked if the item would wait to go to the city council until after the report came in
should the planning commission recommend denial.

Larry Gardner said that was correct. Regarding their options tonight, the commission could
recommend denial with findings as to why the senior housing overlay is not appropriate, table it to a
future meeting until the adequate public facilities memo is available for the planning commission to
examine, or they could recommend approval. Regardless of the recommendation tonight, the
application will still go to the city council for the final decision.

4. Oneleven at West Jordan; 7141 South U-111 Highway; Future Land Use Map
Amendment for 53.2 acres from Business Research Park to High Density Residential,
Rezone from BR-P (Business Research Park) Zone to IH-D (Integrated Housing
Development) Zone, and Master Development Agreement; DAI Partners LLC/Nate Shipp
(applicant) [#29149, 29151, 29152; parcel 20-27-200-001, 003}
Chase Andrizzi, representing the applicant, provided an overview of the project. They would like to
amend the land use map to medium density, even though the current ordinance says the IH-D zone
requires a high-density designation. They are requesting the new Integrated Housing Development
zone. They have been working with staff on the master development plan and agreement for 1 to 1 4
years. City Council also gave them feedback that they have incorporated. The zone requires several
components. There are 47.7 acres of residential and 5.5 acres of commercial as a supporting use. He
pointed out the seven types of units: front load single-family detached cottage pod, front load detached
village lots, rear load detached single-family, front load duplex, front load townhome, rear load
townhome, and 10 units designated as moderate-income housing. Although the affordable units are
identified on the plan, the location can change as long as there are 10 units. Two access points are from
U-111 and 7000 South at the city boundary. For now, 7400 South going east will terminate at the
boundary, but it will probably be built with future development east of them. There are 897 parking
spaces made up of 101 surface off-street parking that is not a driveway or a garage, 398 driveway
spaces, and 398 garage stalls. They don’t want to plan for street parking, but they know it will happen.

The IH-D zone requires 20% open space, which has been planned out with a dog park, usable
detention pond, tot lot, clubhouse of at least 2,000 square feet and pool, and functional open recreation
space. He reviewed the trails and connectivity throughout the project. Uses within the commercial
component are taken from the SC-1 use table. Buffering along U-111 includes 45-60 feet, that is in the
30% unusable slope and is not counted in the open space requirement. He noted that there will be a
wall, and the residences are set down below the highway.

Answering some of the questions from the commission, Mr. Andrizzi stated that the townhomes will
not have more than six attached units within a building. The moderate-income units will most likely be
townhomes in order to keep them at the moderate price. The architecture for those units will look the
same and could be disbursed anywhere. All amenities and open space will be privately owned and
maintained by the HOA. Railroad crossings will be required for 7400 South and 7000 South once that
property is developed. There are no definite occupants for the commercial property, and they are open
to any recommendations. The phasing plan was shown, and it is estimated that overall buildout would
take three years.

Trish Hatch was concerned with the number of units compared to the number of access points. She
asked the fire marshal to comment.

Paul Brockbank said there is a concern, but the layout meets the code. Eventually 7400 South and 7000
South will connect to the east and then to other roadways.

Chase Andrizzi said they did add one more dead end to the east that will connect eventually.

Tayler Jensen stated that the applicant has been working with staff for more than a year and has
improved the plan and provided everything that staff asked in order to comply with the ordinance. He
clarified that noticing for the land use map indicated high density, because that is what the code
requires for the IH-D zoning district, based on the range set in the 2012 general plan. However,
because the density ranges were updated in the new 2023 general plan, the proposed designation of 6.1
dwelling units per acre falls under the medium density range, and that is the recommendation from
staff. If the Commission felt it is appropriate, they could also pass along a recommendation to City
Council that Section 13-5M-4A be changed to state that the density in the IH-D zone be medium. This
is the first application for the IH-D zoning district in West Jordan. Its purpose is to integrate multi-
family and single-family housing with a flexible arrangement of housing sizes and types. The plan
includes 10 moderate income units, 212 townhomes, 12 twin homes, 100 single-family, and 10.2 acres
of approved open space with a pool and clubhouse. There are at least 3-5 different housing types in
each of the development areas. He pointed out an area that the applicant could have included as open
space but chose not to, which staff appreciated. The amenities are closest to the denser areas. He
showed building and amenity renderings. Total required parking for the full buildout is 648 stalls. Staff
would count the stalls within the garages in the first phase so that makes over 1300 parking stalls or 4
per unit. The applicant has been intentional in making sure the project is parked. A cement fence is
proposed on the west and north, an opaque fence on the east, and a split iron fence on the south. He
explained the roadway designations and widths.

Based on the analysis contained in the report, staff recommended that the Planning Commission make
a recommendation of approval (positive recommendation) to the City Council for the Master
Development Agreement. Staff recommended that the Planning Commission make a recommendation

George Winn asked if there are any other research park zones in West Jordan and what basis there was
that this area is not needed for research park.

Tayler Jensen said none of the surrounding uses are similar to a research/business park or office
development. It is next to a gravel pit and residential uses. The grades are difficult, and staff felt that
residential development does a better job of fitting in with the surrounding uses in this location.

Larry Gardner said there are 30-40 acres designated at Jordan Landing. Also, it is anticipated that the
business research use would be integrated into the Southwest Quadrant zoning district areas. The
business research park designation is more appropriately surrounded by more intense uses.

A small rectangle piece of property was pointed out, which will most likely be absorbed into the
adjoining lot or maintained by the HOA.

Tom Hollingsworth said the issue of soft soils that were found in the Maples subdivision was raised
during the Design Review Committee meeting. He asked if there is a provision to require the builders
to analyze and mitigate any potential impacts to soft soils.

Tayler Jensen said that geotechnical studies and reports are required during the site plan and
subdivision process.

John Roberts said he is familiar with the development to the north outside of the city that is hard to
access because there is a median on U-111. He asked if the access for this development would be any
better.

Nathan Nelson said 7400 South is scheduled to be signalized this summer as part of the Wood Ranch
project. 7000 South is required to be signalized, but it is unclear at this point who will install it and
when it will be installed.

George Winn opened the public hearing.

Hieu Nguyen, West Jordan resident speaking remotely, was a big fan of the new IH-D zoning and
would like to see more of it, and it is nice to see commercial closer to homes. He hoped the
commercial area in this project is not a gas station. He would prefer uses such as a dog groomer,
daycare, café, mini market, etc.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

Trish Hatch loved the project, and she liked that the affordable housing units are integrated into the
project and not segregated.
