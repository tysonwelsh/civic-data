parcels and Country View subdivision, and no additional fencing was required.

and Code Enforcement has been involved. If rezoning were approved, a Commercial
Site Plan would be required, and the issue would be rectified at that time. He noted that
the rezoning was in alignment with the General Plan and neighborhood.

             APPLICATION FOR A GENERAL PLAN AMENDMENT AND A REZONE
             FOR APPROXIMATELY 1.04-ACRES LOCATED AT 11851 SOUTH
             REDWOOD ROAD FROM RR-22 (RESIDENTIAL LOW-DENSITY, .5-
             ACRE MINIMUM LOT SIZE) TO RM-14-SD (RESIDENTIAL HIGH-
             DENSITY, 14 DWELLINGS PER ACRE). APPLICANT IS REQUESTING
             10 UNITS. APPLICANT – RD DEVELOPMENT HOLDING

Mr. Prestwich presented the Staff Report and reported that the subject property is on
South Redwood Road near 11800 South. The developer of Sycamore Glen acquired this
parcel from the Utah Department of Transportation (“UDOT”) and was requesting
rezoning to incorporate it into the project. Two five-plex townhome buildings were
approved for the adjoining property, and the applicant’s stated intention was to build the
same type of units on this property.

Notices were sent to all property owners within 1,000 feet of this site and posted on the
City’s webpage. One comment was received expressing concern about traffic impacts.

Aerial, Zoning, and General Plan maps were reviewed. Mr. Prestwich noted that the
subject parcel is currently zoned RR-22. The adjacent Sycamore Glen subdivision is an
S-D zone, and one special designation was to allow some smaller, single-family lots along
with the two five-plex townhome buildings. The applicant requested rezoning to allow two
more five-plex buildings on the subject parcel. The base zoning on the other parcel was
RM-6, but that zone no longer allows multifamily attached housing, so the request was to
rezone the parcel to RM-14-SD. The General Plan designated the parcel as Professional
Office, so a General Plan Amendment to High-Density Residential was also required.

The approved Sycamore Glen Townhomes Phase 1 Site Plan was reviewed, as well as
the concept for Phase 2. Mr. Prestwich noted that the recommended Conditions of

Approval included a requirement that Phase 2 be consistent with the standards applied
to Phase 1. He then presented a street view of the parcel and noted that water retained
by the current pond will either be redirected into the nearby City retention pond or
captured by upstream systems. City Attorney, Ryan Carter clarified that Riverton City is

In response to a question raised by Commissioner Cluff, Mr. Prestwich reported that when

Commissioner Park expressed concern that the subject property appeared narrower than
the Phase 1 parcel and may not accommodate the proposed townhomes. Mr. Prestwich

narrower on the aerial view.

Commissioner Cannon stated that, in his opinion, the townhomes would be the highest

traffic at the lighted intersections to the north and south and tying this parcel in with the
existing subdivision will keep traffic off of Melba Lane.

townhome parcel, which is one reason they plan to mirror those buildings. The project
was created from three parcels that share access with the dance studio. The traffic study

and they were able to accommodate traffic very well. Their efforts to make the 11800
South intersection adequate and safe included adding a right turn lane that was not
initially required by the traffic study.

Commissioner Cannon stated that a lot of time has been put into the project. Many

to address those concerns. He believes the subdivision has turned out nicely. Mr. Morton

      C.     “EATS & SWEETS LLC,” PLZ-24-2035, AN APPLICATION FOR A
             CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT FOR A HOME OCCUPATION (COTTAGE
             BAKERY) TO BE LOCATED AT 3832 WEST BUMPER CROP CIRCLE.
             APPLICANT – PATTHARASAYA MCGOWAN

City Planner, Lisa Halversen presented the Staff Report and reported that the application
was coming before the Planning Commission due to City Code requirements regarding
home bakeries. The two-story home is located on the corner of Bumper Crop Circle and
Lil Dickens Lane and has a three-car garage and a large driveway. It is 0.26 acres and
zoned R-4. The adjoining properties in the Royal Farms subdivision are similarly zoned.
Orders would be placed online and picked up at a designated public location, so the
business would not generate any traffic and would therefore have minimal impact on the
neighborhood. The Site Plan was reviewed, which indicated that the kitchen and an
upstairs studio space would be used for the Home Occupation.

Notices were sent to property owners within 300 feet of the subject property and posted
on the city’s website. One in-person comment was received expressing support for the
applicant.

Per Riverton City Code, any Home Occupation involving food or drink preparation,
storage, or catering requires a Conditional Use Permit. No more than 25% of the main
or upper floors shall be used for the Home Occupation, and the home Occupation may
include the sale of tangible goods.

The applicant, Pattharasaya McGowan stated that she intends to bake customized cakes
and cookies in her home business. The orders will be delivered to a public location, and
no customers will come to her home. In response to a question from Chair Matheson,
Ms. McGowan clarified that she will meet customers at public locations like a Target or
restaurant parking lot.

Chair Matheson opened the public hearing.

Brent Smart stated that he has been Ms. McGowan’s neighbor for six or seven years and
he is very supportive of her business.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Commissioner Park raised a question regarding proposed Condition 6. The applicant
indicated that she did not anticipate any customers coming to her home and the public
notice stated that orders will be filled by delivery, but proposed Condition 6 allowed for
visitors to the business between the hours of 7:00 a.m. and 7:00 p.m. He believed the
condition should be removed to conform with the public notice.

Ms. Halversen reported that the condition was included to allow some flexibility for
customers to come to the home on rare occasions. Commissioner Park stated that the
public was not informed of that use, and the condition would allow the applicant to have
visitors between those hours. Ms. Halversen clarified that the Planning Commission had
the authority to change any condition, but she did not believe that one or two customers
visiting the home would have a negative impact on the neighborhood.

In response to a question from Commissioner Park, Mr. Carter stated that the evidence
is weighed at a public hearing, and the Planning Commission makes a decision based on
that evidence. The Commission is not restricted by the language of the public notice. If
the Commission does not believe the volume of traffic will be a threat to the neighborhood,
they can approve or soften the recommended conditions. He believes it is reasonable to
expect that even a business with a delivery-only model will occasionally have in-person
customers. However, at the Commission’s direction, a second public hearing could be
held and noticed to indicate that there could occasionally be traffic to the home.

In response to a question from Commissioner Cluff, it was clarified that it is standard to
allow Conditional Home Occupations to have visitors between 7:00 a.m. and 7:00 p.m.
Commissioner Park expressed concern that they were not being transparent with the
public about the potential for visitors to the home.

Commissioner Cannon asked about potential remedies if traffic to the home business
became a problem. Mr. Carter stated that, if the permit were granted with the conditions
as stated, visitors would be allowed during those hours and there would be nothing to
remedy. The Planning Commission could legally grant that condition. However, if they
were not comfortable doing so, the only solution would be to direct Staff to reissue the
notice and hold another public hearing.

Commissioner Rushton stated that Condition 6 is a standard approval, and denying the
application based on it would be penalizing the applicant for self-imposed conditions.
Because she has a different type of business model today, it would restrict her in the

              SUBDIVISION ON APPROXIMATELY 1.56 ACRES LOCATED AT 12191
              SOUTH 3600 WEST. APPLICANT – GARY CANNON.

Mr. Cannon, as a member of the Planning Commission, recused himself from discussion
and action on this matter.

Mr. Prestwich presented the Staff Report and stated that the private lane for this
subdivision was previously approved by the Planning Commission. The aerial map was
reviewed, showing that the parcels to the north and east of the subject property were
rezoned to R-3-SD in approximately 2012, and a six-foot masonry wall was installed along
the boundary at that time. The subject property remained RR-22, and the property
immediately to the south is zoned R-1.

The subdivision plat and site plan were presented. The subdivision will consist of a
private lane, a stormwater retention area, and three lots. New homes will be constructed
on Lots 2 and 3, and the existing house will remain on Lot 1. The parcels meet all City
size, shape, and setback requirements. City Ordinance requires solid fencing but allows
the Planning Commission to make an exception for projects less than two acres in size.
Because the zoning is compatible and the existing fence is of high quality, Staff
recommended allowing the rail fence on the southern border to remain.

Mr. Prestwich reviewed the recommended conditions of approval and clarified that the
City will not maintain the private lane or infrastructure.

Notices were sent to all property owners within 300 feet of the subject property and posted
on the City’s website, and no comments were received.

Commissioner Park stated that he was not in attendance at the previous meeting
regarding this subdivision and asked for clarification on the change. Mr. Prestwich
clarified that the previous approval was for a Conditional Use Permit for a private lane to
serve the two new lots. The item before the Commission was the subdivision plat.

In response to a question raised by Chair Matheson, Mr. Prestwich stated that the
retention areas at the rear of the new lots are shallow as they are only required to retain
stormwater generated by the lots and private lane. The requirement is noted on the plat,

              ATTORNEY

Mr. Prestwich reported that all Planning Commission Members will receive new email
addresses to conduct Commission business. He then reviewed the setup process.

Mr. Carter stated that moving forward, all correspondence involving the Planning
Commission must be routed through the email addresses issued by the City. Private
email addresses can no longer be used. If Riverton City were involved in litigation where
the correspondence between Planning Commission members was in controversy, courts
could issue an order demanding delivery of devices the members used to access their
personal email accounts, including personal computers or phones. By communicating
only through the official email channel, they will avoid any possibility of being required to
hand over their personal equipment.

When a records request is received by the City, Staff must review all records that could
fall within the ambit of the request and hand over what is required. They do not hand over
anything that is considered to be private, protected, or controlled. For example,
responses to correspondence from a developer about whether Commissioners will be at
a meeting are a matter of government record and should be handed over during the
course of a Government Records Access and Management Act (“GRAMA”) request.
Correspondence regarding going for a coffee after the meeting is personal and is not
disclosed.

Mr. Carter clarified that all correspondence received by the Planning Commission is a
government record regardless of whether it is sent to official or private email accounts. If
an unsolicited email is sent to a Commissioner’s private account in their capacity as a
Planning Commissioner, it should be forwarded to their City email account for
preservation. The City can then convincingly defend the position that there is no need for
a discovery order regarding the Commissioners’ personal property.

Commissioner Rushton requested that the City provide a low-cost device for
Commissioners to utilize in their positions on the Planning Commission. Mr. Carter stated
that he would review the request with the City Manager. The email accounts are internet-
based and emails are routed through an archiving tool prior to delivery, so there would be
no danger of personal devices being requested for a discovery order. However, if there
was a concern about wear and tear on personal devices, that was a valid argument. It
was noted that Chair Matheson and other Commissioners were offered iPads when they
