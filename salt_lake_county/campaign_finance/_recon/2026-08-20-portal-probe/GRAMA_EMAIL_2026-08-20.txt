RECORDS REQUESTED

All campaign finance disclosure records filed with the Salt Lake County Clerk by
candidates for and holders of Salt Lake County offices — Mayor, County Council
(Districts 1 through 6 and At-Large A, B and C), Sheriff, District Attorney,
Clerk, Assessor, Recorder, Treasurer, Auditor and Surveyor — and by their
personal campaign committees, for reporting years 2015 through 2021 inclusive.

Specifically, and for each such filing, I request:

  (a) the itemized contributions received (the "Schedule A" detail: date
      received, name of contributor, occupation/employer, and amount);
  (b) the itemized expenditures made (the "Schedule B" detail: date of
      expenditure, name of recipient, purpose, and amount);
  (c) the Summary Page figures (Column A "Total this Period" and Column B
      "Aggregate Total", lines 1 through 7);
  (d) the cover-page information (candidate, office sought, district, party,
      report type, amendment status, signature date, date received); and
  (e) the associated candidate/committee, reporting-year and statement-of-
      organization records for those filers.

These are the reports required of county candidates and officeholders by Utah
Code 17-16-6.5 and by Salt Lake County Code Chapter 2.72A, and they are public
records — the County published every one of them on its own public website.

WHERE THESE RECORDS ARE HELD, AND WHY I AM ASKING

To assist your staff in locating them, these filings were made through two
channels in this period, and it is the electronic one that I most need.

1. ELECTRONICALLY FILED REPORTS — the County's public disclosure application at
   https://disclosure.saltlakecounty.gov (also reached as
   https://disclosure.slco.org). Its public search served one folder per filer at
   /Search/PublicSearch/FolderDetails/{id} and one itemized report per filing at
   /Search/PublicSearch/Report/{id}.

   That application is no longer reachable. As of my testing on August 20, 2026,
   every request to a path served by the application — including
   /Search/PublicSearch itself — is met with a TCP connection reset, over both
   HTTPS and HTTP, from an ordinary web browser as well as from other clients,
   and from more than one network. Requests to other paths on the same host are
   redirected to /Search/PublicSearch, which then resets. The Internet Archive's
   last successful capture of the public search page is dated January 15, 2026,
   and there is none after that. The site's TLS certificate is current, so the
   host itself is still maintained; only the application behind it is not
   responding.

   I am able to identify precisely which records I am asking for, because the
   Internet Archive did capture the folder pages before the application went
   offline. Those pages enumerate 54 County and Metro Township candidate/
   officeholder folders (folder ids 129 through 263) and, within them, 251
   electronically filed reports for reporting years 2015 through 2021, carrying
   report ids in the range 1069 through 2104. By reporting year those 251 reports
   fall as: 2015 — 14; 2016 — 27; 2017 — 6; 2018 — 75; 2019 — 12; 2020 — 98;
   2021 — 19. I am glad to provide the full list of filer names and report ids in
   whatever form is most useful to you, including a spreadsheet.

   The Internet Archive did NOT capture the reports themselves, so the itemized
   contribution and expenditure detail for these 251 filings is currently
   unavailable to any member of the public through any channel.

2. PAPER-FILED REPORTS — scanned and posted by the Clerk at
   saltlakecounty.gov/clerk/elections/financial-disclosures/salt-lake-county-offices/.
   These are still online and I have already retrieved them; I am NOT asking you
   to reproduce them. I mention them only so your staff can tell the two sets
   apart, and to make clear that this request is narrow.

I am likewise NOT requesting the 2006-2015 reports posted at
slco.org/clerk/financialDisclosurePDF/, and NOT requesting the 2022-present
filings available through the County's current EasyVote public filings site. I
already hold all of those.

the electronically filed reports were keyed into a database rather than scanned,

primarily benefits the public rather than me. These are public-classified records
