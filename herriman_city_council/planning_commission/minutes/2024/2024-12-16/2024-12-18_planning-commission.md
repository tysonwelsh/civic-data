1. Commission Business
    1.1. Review of City Council Decisions – Michael Maloy, Planning Director
City Planner Michael Maloy reviewed recent City Council decisions, including the approval
of a Master Development Agreement for 13400 South Commercial District and a series of
Public Infrastructure District annexations for the Wasatch/South Hills area. Commissioner
Rypien raised concerns about Public Infrastructure Districts (PIDs) and the potential for
homeowners to pay more than market value due to PID costs. City Planner Maloy mentioned
differing opinions on PIDs, with one analyst suggesting they should fund improvements

beyond standard services. They also noted that PIDs are often used in commercial projects
but are becoming more common in residential areas. Commissioner Rypien proposed a future
training session on PIDs, with City Planner Maloy suggesting the attorney’s office could lead
a discussion.

     1.2. Review of Agenda Items – Planning Staff
Planner Sheldon Howa touched on the Conditional Use Permit consideration, highlighting that
it included conditions related to engineering standards and a floodplain permit. Due to time
constraints and the need to finalize the matter by the end of the year, the applicant proceeded
with these conditions in the approval. The engineering department was comfortable with this
approach, as they did not anticipate significant impacts to the site if changes were needed.

Planner Howa touched on agenda items 5.1 and 5.2, which involved a General Plan
Amendment and a rezone. He indicated those discussions would be combined into a single
presentation for efficiency. Chair Powell noted receiving three emails with comments on the
agenda items and asked whether everyone had received the same three emails, which the
commission confirmed.

   1.3. Presentation and discussion of Land Use Development regulations, standards,
        policies, procedures, and amendments – Michael Maloy, Planning Director
City Planner Maloy provided an update regarding the single-family zoning project's progress,
which had been temporarily set aside due to the attention required for other substantial
projects. This particular initiative would introduce new zoning tools that would accommodate
smaller, more affordable homes on smaller lots. City Planner Maloy shared the input received
from private-sector consultants on the draft regulations, which proposed several strategic
points for consideration.

City Planner Maloy relayed feedback received from the consultants. The first suggestion
included conducting an analysis of undeveloped properties within the city to determine
where the new zoning tools might be effectively applied. This approach would help gauge
potential impacts, both positive and negative, and ensure the tools could function properly in
areas identified as opportunities rather than stable neighborhoods.

The feedback also highlighted the need to consider additional zoning options beyond the
existing R-1-5 and R-1-8 classifications. One aspect of this was the idea of broadening the
narrow approach requirement to encompass all zones, including R-1-8, which was subject to
further discussion regarding its applicability and impact within the city's context.

The consultants recommended clarifying that applicants should be the designers responsible
for the design decisions, potentially engaging architects, engineers, or urban planners
directly. This was seen as a way to ensure projects met design objectives efficiently and

effectively. City Planner Maloy mentioned that engaging with designers as applicants could
lead to more productive discussions during the approval process. He highlighted another
recommendation to undertake a massing study to assess the impact of the proposed
regulations. This study would involve creating visuals and models that indicate buildable
areas according to the proposed guidelines, providing a clear picture of potential
developments.

Revisiting the 30% lot coverage restriction was another topic of interest. The consultants
expressed opinions that this restriction might be too limiting, especially when considering
the market's feedback on lot functionality. The Commission deliberated on this point,
emphasizing the restriction's role as central to achieving the project's goals for smaller,
affordable residences while raising concerns that relaxing this requirement might result in
larger homes contrary to these objectives.

The Commission discussed reassessing the limitations on a 24' x 24' garage size, and whether
it was sufficient or too small for intended purposes. The subdivision standards associated
with the new zones were suggested to become more detailed, and consultants proposed
establishing bulb-outs as a standard, potentially reducing reliance on the engineering
department's approvals. There was also a debate on how the gross density option served as
a regulatory incentive and whether it provided enough motivation for developers to engage
with the new zoning tools.

City Planner Maloy concluded by noting that progressing with a massing study could be
instrumental in understanding and visualizing the proposed regulations' impact, which would
facilitate more informed dialogue during future commission discussions.

    4.1. Review and consider a Conditional Use Permit to develop a commercial site plan
         and veterinary clinic on Lot 802 in the Anthem Commercial Development located
         at 12121 S Herriman Main Street in the C-2 Commercial Zone.
         Applicant: Russell Sprague (authorized agent)
         Acres: ±1.30
         File No.: C2024-095
Planner Sheldon Howa presented a conditional use request for a veterinary clinic in the
Anthem Commercial Development. The proposed 8,500 square foot building would be
located on the northwest side of Herriman Main Street, just adjacent to Slackwater. The
facility would include a 4,200 square foot veterinary clinic with the remaining space
potentially used for storage or a potential future expansion as the business grows.

Planner Howa outlined that the land use requested for the site was animal services, which
included a component for kennel use specifically for the observation of animals, not for
boarding or daycare. He explained to have met city ordinance requirements for landscaping
and design, featuring finishes such as brick, stucco, stone, and bronze anodized aluminum,
aligning with Anthem Commercial Center's design standards.

Planner Howa relayed staff recommendation to approve with 6 conditions:
   1. Receive and agree to all recommendations from other agencies
   2. No building permits shall be issued until all engineering requirements have been
      satisfied and a full resubmittal of the civil plans signed by a Licensed Professional
      Engineer has been submitted to the City.
   3. Submit a Floodplain Permit as required per Herriman City Engineering.
   4. All landscaping must comply with Herriman City’s Water Efficiency Standards and
      meet the 50% live plant coverage.
   5. Overnight boarding is only permitted for animals receiving medical care and
      observation. All other boarding of animals is not permitted.
   6. No signs are approved with this request.

He noted some of the conditions were already being worked on by the development team to
address engineering constraints.

Mr. Russell Sprague from the development team provided additional details, clarifying further
the intentions and plans. He confirmed they were addressing the 6 conditions, with a
particular focus on requirements from engineering. Commissioner Andrea Bradford inquired
about specific accommodation for overnight animal stays and cremation services. Mr.
Sprague explained that the plan included a synthetic turf area designed for pets, allowing
them a space to relieve themselves, given they may be nervous entering a clinic environment.
He also confirmed that there would be no on-site cremation, while a temporary morgue would
be available.

The Commission covered additional topics including the project's careful planning of
circulation, sidewalks, and the arrangements made for shared parking with adjacent
properties to ensure there was adequate parking availability, which included more spaces
provided than required for anticipated future uses. The veterinary clinic was part of a broader
development, and the addition of sidewalks and shared parking agreements would facilitate
better movement and connectivity in the area. Planner Howa elaborated that the proposed
development was taking significant steps to address future growth, noting that the parking
plan was intentionally designed to be over the required capacity.

Commissioner Rypien moved to approve item 4.1 Review and consider a Conditional Use
Permit to develop a commercial site plan and veterinary clinic on Lot 802 in the Anthem
Commercial Development located at 12121 S Herriman Main Street in the C-2 Commercial
Zone. Commissioner Ferguson seconded the motion.

    5.1. Review and consider a recommendation to amend the Herriman City General Plan
         for property located approximately at 13255 S Rose Canyon Road, and 6228 W and
         6232 W 13400 South from Hillside Agricultural to Neighborhood One. (Public
         Hearing)
         Applicant: Jeff Neal (authorized agent)
         Acres: ±15.8
         File No: G2024-129
Planner Howa presented a request to amend the Herriman City general plan for a 15.8 acre
site located off Rose Canyon Road and 6300 West. The proposal sought to change the future
land use designation from Hillside Agricultural to Neighborhood 1 for about 10 acres to
facilitate residential development with lots ranging from 0.3 to 1 acre. The current zoning of
the site is A-1-43, and the intent of the request is to allow for a potential 27-lot residential
subdivision.

Mr. Jeff Neal from Elevation Homes, the applicant, explained their intention to develop half-
acre lots similar to a previous project called the Reserve at Sky Ranch. He highlighted the
history of Elevation Homes, known for larger, quality custom homes, and stated their
excitement to continue building in Herriman. He noted their approach to incorporating
feedback from neighborhood meetings and addressing residents' concerns.

Chair Powell opened the public hearing.

Jody Eastman inquired about the proposed access roads, whether adjacent properties could
use the new access road for driveways, and the potential for future connectivity to the Myrna
Carter property. Ms. Eastman was concerned about the increased density's impact on traffic
and adherence to the master plan, questioning whether making such changes negated the
plan's purpose. She also expressed the need for careful consideration of the development's
influence on property access and density expectations.

Commissioner Bradford moved to close the public hearing. Commissioner Fenn seconded
the motion, and all voted aye.

Planner Howa provided an overview of the general plan amendment process, detailing how
it required a comprehensive evaluation of the proposal's compatibility with existing
developments, the maintenance of property values, and the assurance of the general welfare
of the community. He clarified that the proposal included a zoning condition that restricts
the density to a maximum of 2 units per acre. This condition aligned with the planning
December 18, 2024 Planning Commission Meeting Minutes                              Page 7 of 9


objectives for the area and its anticipated growth. He emphasized the planning process's
ability to accommodate new information and adapt to changing community needs.

The Planning Commission assessed how the proposal situates itself within the context of
surrounding development, noting the general plan's flexibility, particularly in areas prepared
for a mix of residential types. Mr. Neal reiterated his dedication to maintaining the large lot
aesthetics that were characteristic of their prior developments in the area.

Commissioner Rypien moved to forward a positive recommendation of approval for item 5.1.
Review and consider a recommendation to amend the Herriman City General Plan for
property located approximately at 13255 S Rose Canyon Road, and 6228 W and 6232 W
13400 South from Hillside Agricultural to Neighborhood One. Commissioner Fenn seconded
the motion.

   5.2. Review and consider a recommendation to amend the Herriman City Zoning Map
        for property located approximately at 13255 S Rose Canyon Road, and 6228 W and
        6232 W 13400 South from the A-1-43 to A-1-10 Agricultural Zone. (Public Hearing)
        Applicant: Jeff Neal (authorized agent)
        Acres: ±15.8
        File No: Z2024-130
Planner Howa presented the associated request to rezone the 15.8 acre site to A-1-10 with a
zoning condition of 2 units per acre maximum density. This change would facilitate the
development of lots ranging from 0.3 to 0.5 acres. The intent of the proposal was to allow for
a potential residential subdivision featuring lot sizes that maintain a balance between larger
and more compact settings, enhancing flexibility in designing the layout.

The Commission evaluated how the proposed zoning compares to surrounding areas,
particularly focusing on compatibility with existing developments and the overall community
December 18, 2024 Planning Commission Meeting Minutes                              Page 8 of 9


vision. There was particular emphasis on the quality of past projects by the applicant, as seen
in their previous work in the Herriman area, like the Reserves at Sky Ranch.

The Commission discussed the number of allowable lots under the new zoning and the
potential benefits of the rezoning in creating a community that blends well with its existing
environment. Commissioner Ferguson asked if fencing would be required. Planner Howa
explained that fencing requirements would be discussed during the subdivision part of the
development process.
