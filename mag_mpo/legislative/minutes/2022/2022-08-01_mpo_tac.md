Station Area Plan (SAP) and Technical Assistance to Governments (TAG) – LaNiece Davenport and Dan Wayne, MAG (2:00) Dan Wayne reported that the application process is online, and applications from American Fork, Vineyard, Orem, and Provo have been submitted for the first round. Cities that have multiple stations may combine applications, opposed to submitting them individually. A Policy Committee has been established to provide peer-review and certifications of SAPs and to receive updates on SAP Technical Assistance awards. The committee has met and adopted bylaws. The Committee will meet when there are recommendations to present. The next steps for the SAP technical Assistance Program include: • Work with applicants to finalize scopes and budgets • Procure and contract with consultants from WFRC-MAG Consultant Pool • Future rounds of technical assistance (bi-monthly anticipated) • Work with cities to ensure final deliverables ready for successful certification • Hold SAP Policy Committee meetings to review progress and certify SAPs as they are completed MPO Technical Advisory Committee August 1, 2022 Page 3 The Technical Assistance to Governments (TAG) Program is patterned after the WFRC’s successful Transportation and Land Use Connections (TLC) Program. The new MAG TAG Program will provide technical assistance to cities in Utah, Wasatch, and Summit County for plans and studies that integrate transportation and land use. Projects selected will support implementation of the Wasatch Choice Vision and TransPlan50 Regional Transportation Plan. Funding for the TAG Program is tentatively provided through the Federal Surface Transportation Program, UDOT, UTA, and MAG. MAG’s roll will be to procure from WFRC- MAG Consultant Pool, provide contract management, and act as a technical advisor on city- led projects. The TAG Program is ongoing and can fund a variety of land use connect project. Project awards will support the creation of live-work-play communities; maximize the value of public infrastructure; and increase travel options to optimize mobility. This will be accomplished by filling gaps and addressing obstacles that are unique to each community and project, with deliverables that directly support future capital grant seeking and other implementation activities. Examples of potential project were given. The motion passed unanimously. 2023 Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) Approval – Bob Allen, MAG (25:35) The Metropolitan Planning Organization (MPO) of MAG has the responsibility of preparing and approving a Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) for the area. The TIP is prepared and approved every year and lists transportation projects and programs within MPO Technical Advisory Committee August 1, 2022 Page 4 the MPO for the fiscal years 2023–2027. Conceptual projects are listed through 2030. Municipalities, Utah County, UDOT, UTA, and others are eligible for MPO funds. All regional projects, regardless of the selection agency, must be approved by the MPO committees and be listed in the TIP. The MPO funds projects in the TIP every two years. The Technical Advisory Committee (TAC) and Regional Planning Committee (RPC) created a final prioritized project list on June 2, 2022, following the process established by RPC. That process included: • Reviewing submitted concept reports • MPO staff scoring each project • A field review • Voting to create the ranked priority list of projects RPC must approve the funding scenario for the final prioritized project list and add those projects to the TIP. MPO staff have created a funding scenario. Funds are approximately $117M and will be primarily available in 2025-2026. The scenario includes an additional $20M to be added to the contingency fund due to rising construction costs. Staff recommends TAC and RPC approve the attached funding scenario and approve the TIP presented. Jeff Anderson moved that TAC recommend the final funding scenario and the TIP be approved. Jason Bond seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Safe Streets For All (SS4A) Funding Opportunity Discussion – Jim Price, MAG (55:35) In May 2022, the US Department of Transportation released a Notice of Funding Opportunity for the newly created “Safe Streets For All” (SS4A) program. SS4A provides $1 billion annually, nationwide, for the next five years, for planning and implementation of projects and programs designed to directly address transportation safety issues in local and regional areas. The funds are only available at the MPO and local government level rather than being administered at the state level. Due to the complexities of administering federal grants, MAG staff suggest that the region partners collaborate on the planning and implementation phases that this funding opportunity may provide. Provo City reported that they are doing their own plan and will self-certify to FHWA, confirming that it meets the requirements. MAG will create a regional plan to establish projects to apply for in future years. Complete information may be found at https://www.transportation.gov/grants/SS4A Staff recommends that MAG members collaborate on efforts to obtain and use planning and implementation grants provided by SS4A. MAG would be the lead agency in this effort. Next TAC meeting is scheduled for August 22, 2022. Jason Bond moved to adjourn the meeting at 3:01 p.m. Andy Spencer seconded the motion.
