REGULAR MEETING

Chair Erickson called the Planning & Zoning Commission meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. She
explained how the meeting would proceed. First, the Planning Department would brief the
Commission; then the applicant would speak to the Commission; the item will be opened to the
public for their comments on public hearing agenda items; the Midvale Deputy City Attorney
will then brief the Commission on their role on the given item; and then the Commission would
deliberate and decide.

PUBLIC HEARINGS

   1. Matt McWhirter requests Rezone approval for 7444 S Union Park Ave and a
      portion of 1116 E Chapel Hill Drive. It is proposed that the properties currently
      zoned Single Family Residential (SF-1) be rezoned to Regional Commercial (RC).
      Information regarding the proposed regulations, prohibitions, and permitted uses
      that the property will be subject to if the rezone is adopted can be found in Midvale
      Municipal Code 17-712.

      The current property at 7444 S Union Park Ave was developed under the Neighborhood
      Commercial-2 (NC-II) standards of the Single Family Residential 1 (SF-1) zone which
      limits total parking stalls to eight or less for a business type. Rezoning to Regional
      Commercial (RC) would allow the applicant to construct more parking stalls. The
      applicant has completed a boundary line adjustment with the neighboring property owner
      to allow for the addition to the parking lot. The project will be subject to a Site Plan
      application prior to construction to ensure compliance with the standards of the RC zone
      for the parking lot.

PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION DECEMBER 11, 2024.
       Midvale City Code 17-3-1(E) outlines the criteria necessary for granting a rezone as
       follows:

               17-3-1(E). Zoning Map Amendment Criteria. The city’s zoning is the result of a
               detailed and comprehensive appraisal of the city’s present and future land use
               allocation needs. A zoning map amendment application may only be approved if
               the reviewing body determines, in written findings, that the proposed amendment
               demonstrates one or more of the following:
               1. Proposed rezoning promotes objectives of the general plan;
               2. Existing zoning was either the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact, or
               that it failed to take into account the constraints on development created by the
               natural characteristics of the land, including but not limited to steep slopes, flood
               plain, unstable soils, and inadequate drainage;
               3. Land or its surrounding environs have changed or are changing to such a
               degree that it is in the public interest to encourage redevelopment of the area or to
               recognize the changed character of the area; or
               4. Proposed rezoning promotes the purposes outlined in Utah Code Annotated 10-
               9a-102.

       The proposed rezone request satisfies #1 and #4 listed above. The property type is
       identified as commercial in the General Plan, and rezoning would allow a larger variety
       of business types to operate under the Regional Commercial zone at this location after the
       additional required parking spaces are constructed.

       Public notice has been sent to property owners within five hundred feet of the subject
       area. No written objections have been received as of the writing of this report.

       Staff recommends approval.

APPLICANT:

Matt McWhirter, the applicant, describes that this is an existing commercial building zoned in the
SF-1 Zone. The current business is closing with only eight parking spaces designated for their
business. Mr. McWhirter is purchasing the exiting building along with 1,500 square feet from the
neighboring church. This will provide seven added parking spaces for the retail space.

DISCUSSION:

Commissioner Snow questions if this individual parcel will be the only one affected by the zone
change.

Wendelin Knobloch, Planning Director, clarifies that the existing parcel and the acquired section
of the parking lot will be the only area affected by the zone change.

Garrett Wilcox, City Attorney, states that this is a legislative decision that gives Commissioners
broad discretion regarding the recommendation sent to City Council.

Commissioner Liedtke and Chair Erickson agree that the change will benefit the community and
open possibilities for different businesses to lease the space.

         a. 35 minutes
                  i. Legislative Body versus Municipalities
                        1. It is extremely difficult to win a case against the legislative body
                            unless the argument is clear with solid evidence.
                 ii. Overturning a Legislative Act
                        1. The course of action is debatable, and an argument can be made.
                        2. A legislative act is only in danger of being overturned if the
                            ordinance is discriminatory or clearly against the law. The courts
                            treat legislative acts with a high degree of deference.
                        3. So long as the decision is not biased or discriminatory, there is not
                            much to argue.
               iii. Timing is everything.
                        1. A General Plan may outline a plan that is not necessarily
                            applicable to all projects at the time of proposed development or
                            application.
                        2. Current zoning will always trump the General Plan at the time of
                            this legislative session. This could change in the future if the Utah
                            Legislature changes the state code.
