2.1 Discussion of Potential New Residential Zoning Categories (Staff Presenter,
Grant Crowell and Caitlyn Tubbs)
Community and Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, reported that the Planning
Department frequently receives questions about residential zoning for lots smaller than one acre. The
General Plan contemplates 0.33 and 0.50-acre lots, and small lots were also discussed in previous
work sessions. As it is still a topic of interest, Planning Manager, Caitlyn Tubbs had prepared a Staff
Report with information on the potential zones.

Ms. Tubbs stated that the City’s General Plan is its most important document. State Code requires
that certain things be addressed within the General Plan, including land use development patterns,
housing needs, transportation, and other resources such as water and utilities. The General Plan
outlines the City’s anticipated future growth patterns and goals, and it is the primary tool used to
‘create land use ordinances.
Bluffdale’s General Plan specifies three housing development goals:

1. Retain the character of our existing neighborhoods.

2. Provide a variety of housing choices for current and new residents.

3. Ensure that all housing stock addresses the needs and income restrictions of residents of all

life stages and economic situations.
The Land Use Map was reviewed. The General Plan outlines different land use categories for
residential development patterns, and these categories are used to determine whether a prospective
zone would be appropriate in a specific area of the City. The primary residential land use designations
are Very Low Density Residential (less than one dwelling unit per acre) and Residential (one to four
dwelling units per acre). Smaller areas of the City are designated Mixed-Use or Multi-Family
Residential.
City Manager, Bruce Kartchner, remarked that other cities allow lots as small as 5,000 square feet
and asked if lots of that size would be designated Mult-Family Residential. Ms. Tubbs reported that
the only designation that would accommodate 5,000 to 6,000-square-foot lots would be Mixed-Use.
The minimum lot size currently allowed in Bluffdale is 0.25 acres in the R-1-10 Zone, which
corresponds to the Very Low Density Residential land use designation.
Mr. Crowell stated that a single-family development with eight units per acre could also fit in the
Mult-Family Residential designation. The category was named based on the predominant pattern of
townhomes and apartments. Zoning is not required by law to conform to the General Plan, so there
is room for interpretation. However, if there is a category misalignment, it may be better addressed
with a text amendment.

Ms. Tubbs reported that when the possibility of creating new residential zones was first discussed,
Staff reviewed existing ordinances and created a Zoning Toolbox outlining the various means and
methods to achieve certain development patterns. Standard zones range from A-5 Agricultural to R-
MF Multifamily and the new R-SL Senior Living Zone. Overlay zones are allowed, but they are
limited to certain underlying zones. The City also can create Special Development Zones. Typically,
a property owner will propose a master-planned development that includes a transportation element,
open space, parks, trails, housing, and frequently some commercial uses in key locations.
Development agreements are also allowed and provide a way to tailor a development to a specific
area.
During a City Council Work Session in January 2025, Staff was directed to research other
communities with 0.33- and 0.50-acre residential zones and draft ordinance language for review. A
public Planning Commission hearing was held in April 2025 to review the draft, and public comments
were received both in favor of and opposed to the ordinance. The Planning Commission then tabled
the item. At its January 14, 2025, meeting, the City Council suggested that the proposal be brought
forward for additional discussion.
Research indicated that all surrounding communities have 0.33- and 0.50-acre zones and some allow
lots as small as 5,000 square feet in size.
Ms. Tubbs reported that the ordinance amendment would re-codify the City’s existing residential
zones and add a table with minimum lot sizes and development standards.
Commissioner Swanson asked if there was a benefit to changing the nomenclature to R-1, R-2, R-3,
etc. Ms. Tubbs reported that many surrounding cities name their zones based on the number of units
per acre rather than the minimum lot size. Bluffdale’s zones are titled based on acreage. For example,
the R-1-10 Zone has a minimum lot size of 10,000 square feet. In other cities, it would be the R-4
Zone. There is no requirement to rename the current zones. However, for the sake of simplicity and
continuity with other communities, Staff recommended updating them. Mr. Crowell added that

developments require approval from the Jordan Basin Improvement District. If there is insufficient
