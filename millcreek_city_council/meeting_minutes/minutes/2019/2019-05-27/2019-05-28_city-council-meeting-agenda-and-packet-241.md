a. ZM-19-001, Rezone from R-2-10 to R-1-5 at 4277 S. 500 E.; Erin O’Kelley, Planner
Erin O’Kelley said the applicant would like to rezone the property to create another
buildable lot for a single-family home. The surrounding properties are zoned R-2-10 with a
mix of single-family dwellings and duplexes. The proposed lot widths for this project are
roughly 50 feet which would be in compliance with the R-1-5 zone, but it does not meet the
R-2-10 requirements. O’Kelley showed the Council a site plan of the property. She said the
proposed zone would allow a 25-foot lot width and a 20-foot front yard setback. The
Millcreek Future Land Use map classifies this property as Neighborhood 1; the least dense
classification with a range of density/scale of 1-6 units. O’Kelley went through questions

found in the General Plan pertaining to this project and found that the rezone met all
requirements. At their meeting on February 5, 2019, the Millcreek Community Council
recommended that the proposed rezone be approved in accordance with the exhibits in the
staff memo. Staff received one neighborhood comment from a property south east of the
applicant. The neighbors were not opposed to the project and had no complaints or
concerns to offer staff. The Planning Commission recommended approval of the proposed
rezone. Staff recommended that the rezone not be subject to a development agreement.
Based on the similarities in regulation between the current zone R-2-10 and the proposed
zone R-1-5, staff felt including a development agreement in this rezone would be an
unnecessary burden to the applicant. The goal of the rezone is to allow the new single-
family home to be owner-occupied. The owner can currently build another unit on the
property, but it would not be eligible for owner occupancy. Staff felt that a development
agreement would be too restrictive in this circumstance. Staff did not identify any concerns
with the proposed rezone, recommended the finding that the proposed rezone is compatible
with the Millcreek General Plan, and recommended the finding that the proposed
development with the rezone is compatible with the surrounding neighborhoods.

b. ZM-19-007, Rezone from R-1-8 to R-1-6 at 3125 S. 2300 E.; Erin O’Kelley, Planner
Erin O’Kelley said the property currently has two single family homes, one of which has
fire damage and has not been used since. This property is designated as Neighborhood 1 in
the General Plan. Under the City’s adopted flag lot policy, a property in the R-1-8 Zone is
required to have 20,000 square foot minimum to subdivide and create a flag lot. With this
rezone approval, the applicant would be able to take a nonconforming lot and make it
conform to current City ordinances. At their meeting on May 1, 2019, the Canyon Rim
Citizens Association voted unanimously to recommend approval of the rezone not subject
to a development agreement. At their meeting on May 15, 2019, the Planning Commission
voted to recommend approval of the rezone not subject to a development agreement.

easement to it. Lilly added that he has talked to a lot of people about the property who
wanted to keep the non-conforming use. He said he liked this application because it
conforms with the use.

Public and Quasi-public Uses at 3608 S. Jupiter Drive; Francis Lilly, Community

serves parts of Holladay, Parley’s Canyon, and Millcreek Canyon and it is important for the
City to have a fire station east of I-215 for emergency disaster purposes. He said all the
currently facilities of the station are inferior. The new station will be smaller and less
imposing. UFSA owns the property and the rebuild would be within budget. Mayor

d. ZM-19-010, Rezone from C-2 to R-1-6 at 3244, 3260, 3261, 3273, 3280, and 3290 S.
1000 E.; Blaine Gehring, Planner
Blaine Gehring said there are six single-family homes located north of 3300 South on 1000
East which are zoned C-2. Under the C-2 zoning, these homes are considered legal non-
conforming because the current C-2 Zone does not allow single-family dwellings as a
permitted or conditional use. Millcreek is seeking a rezone of the properties to align their
use with the zone. Adjacent uses include single family homes in the R-1-6 Zone and
commercial uses in the C-2 Zone. The homes were built between 1921 and 1947 according
to the Salt Lake County Assessor’s records. Based on official Salt Lake County zoning
maps and ordinances, these properties were zoned C-2 in 1953 with single-family
dwellings a permitted use. By 1965, the zoning ordinance had changed, and single-family
dwellings were no longer a permitted or conditional use in the C-2 Zone. These homes
have been legal non-conforming uses since at least 1965 because of the change in the
zoning ordinance. This rezone came to the attention of city staff during the General Plan
open houses and hearings. The future land use map in the General Plan calls for these
specific properties to be Neighborhood 1, which is single family residential. At their
meeting on May 7, 2019, the Millcreek Community Council recommended approval of the
zone change by unanimous vote. Staff recommended approval of the zone change. No
development will occur, so it will not be subject to a development agreement. At their
meeting on May 15, 2019, the Planning Commission voted to send a favorable
recommendation to the City Council to approve the rezone request. The public in
attendance at the Planning Commission meeting spoke in favor of the rezone.

Mayor Silvestrini said he spoke in favor of the rezone at the Planning Commission
meeting. He said the rezone was a promise to people who were involved in the community
reinvestment area and with the blight study. He said there is a height transition required
between commercial properties and residential properties, so these properties would not
benefit from the height transition if something were to be redeveloped on 3300 S. while
being zoned C-2. Mayor Silvestrini said he made a commitment to those residents that he
would assist with a city-sponsored rezone. The residents that were affected signed a
petition in agreeance with the rezone.

e. GP-19-001, City Center Master Plan; Francis Lilly, Community Development
Director
Francis Lilly said the proposed city center is between Richmond (1300 E.) and Highland
Drive and Elgin Avenue and 3400 South. He highlighted that the idea of the center in this
location came from the Wasatch Choice 2050 Plan, which is a regional planning effort to
project and anticipate where growth might occur while being economically conservative
and as effective as possible with a transportation investment. The 2050 Plan identified the
proposed city center area as a town center. The current demand for mixed-use, commercial,
and multi-family projects is a product of the success of market absorption. Millcreek has
iconic buildings and uses that go back decades, so people have seen this area as a gathering
place. The planning efforts were an attempt to address existing market forces. Staff wants

Millcreek City Council Meeting Minutes 28 May 2019 Page 4 of 10

to be proactive in transportation choices, complete streets, and a vibrant, active, mixed-use
center. The City Center Master Plan will create a walkable urban space that is iconic. There
will be multiple spaces between 1300 E. and Highland Drive, including a long linear park
(Mill Park) that will be the focus of the City’s investment. The investment will be in the
form of utilizing projected revenues off of tax increment financing to fund a public open
space. That open space will be along the fault line, where building is not allowed, and it
will create an amenity and open gathering space for the whole city. The fault line creates
opportunity for focal point parkway and a link to the McClelland Trail in Salt Lake City.
Lilly showed the Council a building rendering and said elements of a future code will
include active ground floor uses, step backs as necessary to preserve view corridors, and a
store front width maximum.

The City Center Master Plan contemplates civic and public gathering spaces. Lilly
mentioned that movies could be broadcasted on the back of the Villa Theatre. The Plan
should create an engine of population and tax base to help keep taxes low for Millcreek.
The conservative estimate is that the Plan anticipates 1,300 new residential units and 580
potential employees, an additional annual tax revenue of $102,000 specifically to the City,
and 40,000 square feet of retail and 100,000 square feet of office space. The design should
support transportation alternatives. There will be a new 3-lane design for Highland Drive
that promotes pedestrian safety. The Plan will also respect surrounding existing
neighborhoods by promoting height transitions and protecting view corridors.

Lilly said the guiding principles for the City Center Master Plan are: that it should
emphasize walkability, and minimize internal traffic and parking demand; should include
municipal property owned by the City and designated for public use; the design should
represent the City in ways that are distinctive from other cities and unique to Millcreek; and
should emphasize culture, art, ideas, and interaction between citizens. Lilly said the
implementation goals are to adopt a code that impose design standards, a light form-based
code approach, create a temporary gathering place, assemble property for the Mill Park
open space, and complete the Highland Drive/Mountair streetscape.

Mayor Silvestrini asked about there being more public engagement on the Mountair
streetscape. Lilly said the architect consultant will lead the design based on the feedback
previously provided. Mayor Silvestrini said the consultant should have engagement at the
front-end and engagement on the plan they come up with. Lilly said before the consultant
goes through the design development phase, they will solicit more feedback. He said the
scope is small enough to not do an RFP and he would work with Oak City Design who has
done work previously for the City in the area. John Brems said city ordinance does not
require an RFP for professional services. Mayor Silvestrini mentioned that the Gunn
Avenue projects developers bought the Mountair streetscape property and gave it to the
City with an additional $100,000 for maintenance.

Lilly said staff was receiving applications for significant projects in the area and they
wanted to harness that energy to create something of value for the community. The
community councils all recommended approval of the City Center Master Plan, three of
which had unanimous votes, but the Millcreek Community Council had a 10-3 vote. Those
opposed expressed concern that the City was moving too fast on the plan and had concerns
about traffic and density. Lilly said their questions would exist without the Plan and that

the Plan is a proactive attempt to get ahead of those concerns. Staff has asked developers to
wait until the Plan is adopted to develop. Lilly said the Planning Commission’s
recommendation for approval of the Master Plan was unanimous with the following
findings: 1) The Proposed City Center Master Plan creates a framework to implement the
General Plan Goal to establish the City Center as the City’s hub of commerce, governing,
innovation, entertainment, art and culture. 2) The City Center Master Plan establishes
standards for building heights and designs that promote an iconic, walkable community and

Mayor Silvestrini said the Council was considering whether to adopt a plan or not. The
development and density this area currently allows are only affected by the Plan by
reducing the height that would potentially be allowed. He said staff was not coming up
with a plan that increases density but one that potentially reduces it. He said allowing
redevelopment to happen in the area without a plan would be haphazard and the City would
miss out on possible amenities. Mayor Silvestrini rhetorically asked if Millcreek should
have a plan or not and if this plan was a good one. He said Millcreek has engaged with the
public repeatedly on this concept and it has been vetted extensively by the Millcreek
public.

after meeting with the mayors of Taylorsville, Holladay, and Midvale, the cities’ managers,
and finance staff. He said in reviewing the Unified Police Department’s (UPD) budget
proposed by the Sheriff, there were a couple of issues. He said some cities would have
financial distress with how the budget was proposed. The budget contemplated a 2% cost of
living adjustment, a 2.5% merit increase, and a 4% market adjustment. The market study
performed for UPD showed that salaries significantly trail those of other police departments in

the Salt Lake valley; UPD was in the bottom half. They have heard about wage increases
discussed by other departments in the valley. Mayor Silvestrini said UPD would have a fund
