PC 23-078 Old Mann Building Addition [Design Review Permit] -Continued from the December
14" meeting. Tony Johnson/Old Mann Holdings LLC, authorized agent/owner is requesting a design
review permit for a new 3,300 square foot, single-story building located at 185 East Cache Valley
Blvd in the Commercial (COM) zone; TIN 05-014-0077 (Adams Neighborhood).

STAFF: Russ Holley, Planner reviewed the updated site plan showing the new 3,300-square-foot
building relocated near the northeast corner of the property. The new building will create commercial
space for some of the mall tenants to move into. R. Holley reviewed the proposed building design
and site plan.

The east property line appears to run along 200 East so the applicant owns the park strip and has
included it in their required landscaping/open space for the project. Engineering condition number
20 requires the applicant to dedicate the strip of land along 200 East, extending at least 5 feet beyond
the west edge of the sidewalk to plan for a future Arterial Street. Once the land is dedicated to the
city the applicant will no longer meet the landscaping and parking setback requirements.

R. Holley stated that condition number 7 needs to be amended to add pedestrian connectivity from
the buildings and sidewalk along 200 East.

Commissioner Doutre asked about the five-foot street setback in condition number 5 and the 10-foot
public utility easement along the streets in Engineering condition number 4. R. Holley said that
setbacks and easements can overlap.

Commissioner Guth asked if they would have enough parking stalls if the land is dedicated to the
City. R. Holley said they have 139 total parking stalls and are required to have 92 parking stalls.

PROPONENT: Tony Johnson spoke about the land swap that they did with the mall where they
acquired the park strip area along 200 East giving them the extra green space that they needed.


Now the City is asking them to gift approximately 4,350 square feet of land to commensurate with
the dedication required of the mall property to the north. The mall project is a 20-acre, multi-million
dollar project that can absorb a donation of land to the City. Mr. Johnson stated that his site is
approximately 2 acres in size and it would be a hardship to dedicate the additional right of way.

Mr. Johnson spoke about his conversation with the State Ombudsman office and determining
whether their proposed development will cause enough of an impact for the city to take the property
without compensation. Mr. Johnson said that he doesn’t feel that their proposed development is
causing an impact, if anything he feels they are providing a net benefit to the City by reducing the
occupancy greatly from what the building was previously used for to what it is being used for now.

PUBLIC: No public comments were made.

COMMISSION: Mohammed Abdullahi, City Attorney said that the City feels that the amount of land
being requested is proportionate to the impact caused by the development. If there were any
additional land requested above the impact, the City would have to pay for it. The applicant can
initiate mediation by the State Ombudsman's office to see if the amount of requested land is
proportionate.

Commissioner Heare said that conditions can be amended if needed, depending on the outcome of
the mediation with the State Ombudsman’s office.

There was discussion regarding the project meeting the greenspace requirement if or when the road
is widened. Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director said the conditions account for the
future road widening and that the project will be required to meet minimum landscaping and open
space requirements after the road widening is taken into consideration. Condition number 5 requires
a minimum 5-foot setback between the parking area and 200 East.

Commissioner Croshaw asked what portion of 200 East will be widened. Darren Farar, City Engineer
said that the Master Plan calls for 200 East to be widened from 1000 North to 1400 North. M.
DeSimone said it will be some time before the street is widened with all the existing homes and
businesses along 200 East.

PC 24-001 UT1 Woodruff [Conditional Use Permit] Troy Benson-Verizon/Logan City is requesting
a design review permit for a new Verizon Wireless Communication Tower located near the West
Willow Park batting cages at 565 West 700 South in the Recreation (REC) zone; TIN 02-058-0043
(Woodruff Neighborhood).

STAFF: Russ Holley, Planner reviewed the request for a new Verizon Cell Tower next to the batting
cages at Willow Park. The proposed tower is a sage green colored tower that is 45 feet in height
with an antenna height of 50 feet. The Land Development Code allows for a 43-foot total height in
this area. There are Federal Standards that allow cell towers to supersede the City’s allowed height
limits if the applicant provides the radio frequency data that requires additional height or modifications
to achieve sufficient cell coverage, which the applicant has provided.

The proposal includes a chain link fence to secure the tower and generator that will match the
surrounding chain link fences in the area. The Land Development Code requires ground equipment
to be screened from view with a solid screen fence or wall and landscaping. The Planning
Commission will need to determine how to best blend the enclosure into the surrounding area and if
a chain link should be permitted.

R. Holley reviewed the proposed renderings of the tower and the maps Verizon submitted showing
the difference in coverage between a 40-foot and 50-foot tower.

R. Holley said that he spoke to Rex Davis, City Forester regarding a comment received concerning

the health of the trees in the area, and Mr. Davis said he was not aware of any issues between cell
towers and tree health.


PROPONENT: Troy Benson said they proposed the chain link to match the batting cages, but are
open to any type of fencing the commission would like. Verizon said it would not be worth it to build
a 40-foot tower. A 50-foot tower would substantially improve the service in the area.

PUBLIC: Mark Holt who lives near the site spoke about his concern with the proximity of the tower
to the residences to the west across the railroad tracks and asked if it is a conflict of interest for the
Logan City Planning Commission to make a decision on this tower that Logan City will receive rent
for.

Miriam Merrill said this is not the right location for a cell tower.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Lucero asked why this site was chosen. Russ Akina, Parks and
Recreation Director said that originally, they were looking at putting the tower in the sports complex
to the south on one of the existing lights in the baseball field. However, the area was acquired with
a Federal Grant which does not allow cell towers on the specific parcels purchased with those grant
funds. This includes the ballfields and portions of the surrounding parking lots. The proposed
location near the batting cages offers easy access for servicing, is adjacent to an existing structure
and large trees, all of which will help the tower be more secluded. R. Holley said that the Land
Development Code requires a 50-foot cell tower to be setback 100 feet from the street and at least
100 feet from a residential zone. R . Holley also mentioned a comment that was submitted
concerning the health of the trees in the area.

Commissioner Guth said she did not agree with this location and asked if there were other sites
considered.

Commissioner Lucero said she agrees with Commissioner Guth

Commissioner Peterson asked about moving it to the other side of the batting cages, closer to the
railroad tracks.

Russ Akina said that the proposed location allows Verizon to access their equipment monthly for
maintenance with little impact to the park. There is also infrastructure that has to be run underground
so the shortest distance from the parking area is the least destructive.

Commissioner Lucero said a solid enclosure (not chain link) would be less inviting for children to
climb over. Commissioner Guth mentioned a brick enclosure.

The Commission discussed the height difference from the allowed 43 feet to the proposed 50 feet.
Commissioner Heare said if the tower is not tall enough to provide the necessary service what is the
point. Commissioner Doutre said there is not that much of a difference from 43 to 50 feet and asked
about locating it to the north of the property near the existing pump house. The discussion about that
location determined it was not suitable due to setbacks.

Commissioner Guth said Conditional Use Permits have to be compatible with the surrounding land
use and will not interfere with the use and enjoyment of adjoining properties. This cell tower will
interfere with the enjoyment of the property and the adjacent playground.

Mohammed Abdullahi, Gity Attorney said the Commission can only deny a Conditional Use Permit if
they can articulate why the detrimental impacts cannot be mitigated.

Commissioner Lucero said she would like to see some alternate site options and spoke about the
renderings making the cell tower stick out more than it might in reality.


Commissioner Doutre said the renderings are not to scale because several trees in that area are
taller than 50 feet and are not shown in the renderings.

The Commission discussed continuing the project to see a more stealthy tower design, a different
location, and better renderings that show the surrounding trees more accurately.

R. Holley responded to the public comment stating there is not a conflict of interest with the Planning
Commission reviewing a proposed cell tower on City property. R. Holley stated that the Planning
Commission is a quasi-judicial group that is appointed to review projects based on the merits of the
Land Development Code and zoning.

MOTION: Commissioner Lucero made a motion to continue the Conditional Use Permit for PC 24-
001 to the January 25th meeting to see a more stealthy design and possible alternate locations as
discussed. Commissioner Guth seconded the motion.

Moved: J. Lucero Seconded: J. Guth Approved: 7-0

Yea: Heare, Croshaw, Guth, Lucero, Lewis, Doutre, Peterson Nay: Abstain:
