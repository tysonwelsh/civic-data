      A.     GENERAL PLAN AMENDMENT AND REZONE, A REQUEST FOR A
             GENERAL PLAN AMENDMENT AND REZONE OF 5.95-ACRES LOCATED
             AT APPROXIMATELY 12261 SOUTH 4000 WEST. APPLICANT – JOE
             SALISBURY WITH DAI UTAH.

Chair Ruston opened the public hearing.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich, presented the staff report and stated that the request is for a
General Plan Amendment and rezone. It is a legislative recommendation item. Once a
public hearing has been conducted, the Planning Commission should make a
recommendation to the City Council who will make the final decision. The subject property
is located just west of Bangerter Highway and north of 12600 South. The property backs
Bangerter Highway and has frontage on 4000 West. The property was identified on a map
displayed.

The complicated history of the property was described. In 2006 through 2008, a rezone and
subdivision site plan were requested. The subdivision was approved, the plat recorded, and
a bond obtained for the public improvements. Development then got underway. At some
point during the review process, the City required interconnectivity and a plan for future
growth with the expectation that property to the north would be developed as residential.
The property, however, was sold and developed as a church, which cut off the road. The
church was constructed but the housing was not completed due to the 2008 housing crash.
In 2008, assets were bought by banks, traded, and sometimes lumped together into groups
and sold. That was the case with the subject property. There were also lienholders and
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                                   2
October 28, 2021


others who had an interest in the property. It took several years to resolve the situation,
which was the reason it remained vacant.

The 5.95-acre property is located at 12261 South 4000 West. Notice was sent to neighbors
within 1,000 feet. Staff received several phone calls and emails, which were provided to the
Commission. Mr. Prestwich reported that the General Plan and rezone would be considered
together. The General Plan guides future growth in the City and the zoning map shows what
can be done on a particular property. If a property is underutilized or located along a corridor,
there are certain areas the City has designated in the General Plan that differ from the use
of the property with the anticipation of a future use. He explained that the General Plan
guides future land use decisions, and the rezone shows what can be done with the property.
The current General Plan designates the property as low-density residential with a zoning
of R-3, which is single-family residential with 14,000 square-foot lots.

The applicant is requesting a General Plan Amendment to medium-high residential, which
allows for five to eight dwelling units per acre. The zones that correspond to that designation
are RM-6 and RM-8. It was noted that multiple zones could apply to any General Plan
designation. The applicant is requesting RM-6, which is residential with a density of six units
per acre. The zoning of the surrounding properties was described.

Mr. Prestwich reported that when Bangerter Highway was constructed, the City took
proactive steps not knowing where the off-ramps would be located and what type of
development was to take place. The City zoned much of the property Professional Office
and the other properties were zoned and developed as residential. The General Plan for
the properties to the north was R-3 but that had not yet occurred. The R-3 zone allows for
14,000 square-foot lots with traditional setbacks of 15 feet for the home, 25 feet for the
garage, 25 feet for the rear yard, and 10 feet and eight feet for the side yards. The requested
RM-6 zone allows for 5,000 square-foot lots for single-family detached homes with a
minimum frontage of 50 linear feet and setbacks of 15 feet with five on each side. The
current ordinance specifies that if the home is adjacent to incompatible zoning, there must
be one foot in setback for each one foot of building height.

Mr. Prestwich explained that in September, the Planning Commission held a hearing where
they recommended changes to the RM-6 zone to the City Council. The applicant was aware
of those changes. The City Council agenda from the October 5 meeting was where the City
Council began the process of amending the RM-6 zone. The recommendation was to
eliminate the condominium and townhome portion of the RM-6 zone. The City has approved
three instances of RM-6 in the last 18 to 24 months and in each case, the zoning was
restricted to single-family detached housing. The thought was that if that was to be done in
every instance, they may change the ordinance and make everything in the RM-6 zone
single-family detached. If that occurs, because the process of changing the zone was
underway when the applicant made the rezone request, the applicant would not be entitled
to multi-family as a condo or townhome if the Council ultimately removes it from the
ordinance.

The Master Transportation Plan for the area was displayed. The applicant provided a
sample layout concept plan for consideration. Mr. Prestwich clarified that the Planning
Commission would not be granting approval tonight as staff had not yet reviewed it to
determine compliance with the RM-6 zone. To pursue what is requested would require a
special designation in the RM-6 zone.           The Planning Commission can make

Commissioner Lefgren asked what had been done to this point on the property. It was noted
that the plat has been recorded and the bond posted. City Attorney, Ryan Carter, believed
that some surface utility work had also been done. The primary reason for getting into the
history of the property was due to its condition. The original planning objectives were not
met because the traffic circulation was interfered with when the church property was
developed. The primary goal was to determine the best use for the property. The bond on
the property had long since expired and the lending institution that issued the bond failed
during the 2008 crisis. Staff was unable to get anyone to help them resolve the bond issue.
At the time the subdivision was approved, the statutes were written to show that recordation
of a plat and publishing of a bond does not guarantee the existence of a road.

Immediately prior to the recession, the individual who had rights to develop the property
secured lending from two successive lenders neither of which was aware of the existence
of the other. The second lender was the first to record the lending rights of the property. As
a result, the loan to value ratio on the property was 2-to-1 overnight. Litigation took place
over a long period of time before the property made it to a foreclosure auction at which time
it was on the market again. Mr. Carter explained that the history of the property leads them
back to needing to determine the best use for the property.

determined to be medium-density single-family detached homes valued at $550,000 and up.

Commissioner Brown asked if they had looked at what would be possible within the existing
zoning. Mr. Shipp explained that they could develop the 14 lots but would have to deal with
the halfway house on the site and the cost associated with removing it. It will likely remain
if the current plan is not pursued, and much lower quality homes built.

existing neighborhoods on Doves Landing with no new development around it. The home
is set back on the lot with a long, deep driveway. The applicant was identified as Samuel

described as a federal firearms license sale and transfer. Federal Firearms Licenses (“FFL”)
are regulated by the Federal Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, and Firearms (“ATF”). Licensure
with ATF requires background checks and property inspections; however, a license will not
be issued until the City grants zoning or conditional use approval. ATF then proceeds to
issue a license that the City uses to issue the Business License.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the matter was noticed to neighbors within 300 feet. No
comments or phone calls were received. Super Goose has no other employees and

appointment. Guns are shipped and can only be delivered to the licensee of the FFL.
Delivery companies will not leave firearms on the porch or allow anyone else in the home to
sign for them. Mr. Carter will then store the guns in a safe in the home. He would like to
conduct business between 5:00 a.m. and 9:00 p.m. The ordinance specifies 7:00 a.m. to
7:00 p.m. To go beyond that, permission must be granted by the Planning Commission.
