           The purpose of the work session was to discuss the Audit Committee Charter, Apartment Design Standards in
     Commercial Zones, Council business, and hear Council comments.

     Audit Committee Charter
              Council Staff is recommending the creation of a fonnal City Audit Committee to comply with a recommendation from
     the Utah State Auditor's Office. The primary purpose of the Committee will be to assist the Council in its financial oversight
     responsibilities.
              Council Executive Director Eller-Smith presented a proposal to create an Audit Committee. The proposed Committee
     structure includes up to six staff members - four administrative and two from the City Council office - as well as one or two
     representatives from independent auditors, one or more Council members, one financial expert, and citizen representatives. The
     Council discussed their desires for the structure of the Committee and the total number of members to be appointed to the
     Committee. Ms. Eller-Smith suggested that the Committee be fonned by appointing staff and professional representatives first and
     adding citizen representation later. The Council ultimately supported that recommendation.
              Ms. Eller-Smith noted staff recommends the Committee meet a minimum of twice a year and consider compensating the
     financial expert and citizen members. Responsibilities of the Committee would include recommending the appointment of an
     independent auditor for the City, review of financial policies, procedures, internal controls, and use of govermnent assets, and
     review the annual financial audit and make recommendations to the Council based on the findings of an audit.

     Apartment Design Standards in Commercial Zones
               Ogden City Planning is proposing amendments to the regulations for group dwellings and apartments in the City's C-2
     and C-3 Commercial Zones. The proposed amendments are the result of months of study regarding multi-family dwellings in
     commercial zones. A notice of pending ordinance outlining potential changes was issued in April of this year and included the
     option to eliminate multi-family dwellings in the C-2 and C-3 zones. After study and review, the proposal is not to eliminate the
     use but rather introduce :improved design standards and other regulations to help promote higher quality multi-family projects in
     commercial zones.
               Planning Manager Montgomery made a presentation and talked about the proposed amendments. According to State
)    Law, a pending ordinance is a tool to place a 180-day halt on a defined action to evaluate and adjust ordinances to address defined
     possible changes. The pending ordinance regarding this matter was adopted on April 21, 2020 and will expire October 18, 2020.
     The Planning Commission held a public hearing on the subject matter during their September 2, 2020 meeting. The pending
     ordinance halted the acceptance of new applications for permits in the C-2/CP-2 and C-3/CP-3 commercial zones for apartment
     and multi-family (three or more dwelling units in one building) and group dwellings (two or more dwellings in two or more

  buildings). Staff recommended the adoption of the pending ordinance to evaluate the appropriateness of the apartment and group
  dwelling uses in the C-2 and C-3 zones. They also wanted to evaluate possible site design, open space, parking, screening,
  building design, and building materials for the uses. The pending ordinance removed apartments and group dwellings as an
  allowed land use in commercial zones to give staff and the Planning Commission maximum flexibility in determining the results
  of this discussion and ultimate action on the ordinance. He presented a map of the City to orient the Council to the area that the
  pending ordinance applies to which encompasses 6.5 percent of the City. The area the pending ordinance does not apply to is 10.2
  percent of the City where group dwellings and multi-family apartments are allowed. The catalyst for this proposed amendment
  was the dramatic increase in the amount of applications for multi-family uses in the C-2 and C-3 zones from2018-2020. The ratio
  of the number of dwellings per acre has spiked drastically in that time. In the 50 years before 2018, the City had 510 multi-family
  units located in the C-2 and C-3 zones. In the last two years, 893 units have been proposed and there are another 792 units in the
  pre-application phase with applicants waiting for the outcome of this ordinance. He reviewed the surge of the multi-family use in
  the different zones in the City stating 54 percent of the new units have been in the C-2 and C-3 zones and consumed 33 .61 acres of
  commercial land. The average project size is 1.9 acre, and each project has an average number of units of 52. Potential future
  projects would add another 1,033 units City-wide with 694 of them located in the C-2 and C-3 zones. This would consume an
  additional 49 acres of commercial land with the average project size being 6.1 acres. This led staff to wonder if housing is the
  major land use of commercial zones in the City; 52 percent of general fund revenue comes from property tax and sales tax. It is
  important to preserve commercial space for commercial activity for this reason as well as to provide services to the community
, and facilitate job creation. He added that for those residential uses that have been constructed in commercial areas as oflate, there
  has been a lack of site and design consideration and developers are simply building what the ordinance allows. Land is set aside
  by zoning for permitted uses; 51 percent of the City is zoned for residential use, while manufacturing zoned property accounts for
  27 percent of the City's land, open space zoning for 11.6 percent, and commercial zoning for 10.4 percent. The non-residential
  zones also include permitted and nonconfonning residential uses that provide housing opportunities for the community.
  Projections indicate that Ogden's population will grow from 87,773 at present day to over 116,000 in 2050; this would indicate
  280 to 320 new units per year. According to the 2018 American Communities Survey, Ogden has 33,346 dwelling units, which is
  3 6 percent of the total dwelling units in Weber County and 3 .1 percent of all dwellings in Utah. This includes many different
  housing types, such as single-family detached units, single-family attached, single-family manufactured, duplexes, multi-family
  housing units, apartments, and high-rise buildings. Mr. Montgomery presented a chart offering a comparison of design standards
  and density allowances for the various residential zones in the City and noted that standards for residential development in
  commercial areas are minimal while the density is much higher than allowed in typical residential zones. Staff has carefully
  examined the review processes for different types of development applications and found that the process for apartments in
  commercial zones is much simpler and quicker than for other types of projects. Considering these factors, it is easy to see why
  there has been such a push for higher-density residential development in commercial zones. He presented a graph which provided
  an assessment ofrecent projects, noting many of the projects share similar characteristics relating to setbacks, design features, and
  building materials. He discussed parking trends and said parking dictates a project's density and failure to provide adequate
  parking can hinder the owner's ability to lease units. However, developers must strike a balance relative to parking because over-
  parking a project increase the costs of the project. In 2018, parking requirements were adjusted to require 1.5 stalls per housing
  unit. Mr. Montgomery discussed underutilized vacant commercial zoned lands in the City. There are 160 parcels totaling 74.8
  acres -the average size of each parcel is a half-acre. There are 277 single-family zoned commercial parcels for a total of 45.6
  acres with the average size being 7,000 square feet. There are seven parcels over an acre for a total of 40 acres, with the average
  size of 5.7 acres. General observations about property taxation indicate that multi-family uses have higher valuations, but those
  uses are only taxed at 55 percent of their value. He stated if the Council is going to proceed with an ordinance amendment, it is
  important to have a clear understanding of the potential implications. Developers are key in implementing a new plan, yet there
  are two types of developers - short term and long term. Marketability of a project will draw someone to want to live in it and
  attention to detail will ensure that the project is durable. The influx of residential development on commercial property means that
  barriers to securing approval of a project are low and 'the worst thing in the world is not an empty lot but wishing that lot were
  empty'. A long-range perspective is important; housing structures have a much longer life than commercial structures, but they
  have less flexibility to adopt to changes. Additionally, everyone should be able to reside in a safe, livable, and durable dwelling.
  The proposed ordinance has been evaluated for its consistency with the General Plan, which has the following principles:
                 • Higher densities along major transportation corridors.
                 • Utilize design and mixed use along corridors.
                 •    Create site and building design guidelines.
                 • Expand the variety of housing types.
                 • Balance mix of services, retail, and tourism industries.
            Mr. Montgomery said the criteria for the revision include creating common ground between group dwellings and multi-
  unit apartments, ensuring appropriate use of commercial lands and mixes of uses, setting standards that will create an Ogden
  product, simplify processes, and have clarify and flexibility. The first revision relates to use of commercial zones and calls for
  appropriate balance between job creation and services. Both uses compete for major corridors and there are 74 acres ofadditional
  undeveloped or underutilized commercial property. He presented a map that identified future development centers and districts
  throughout the City to ensure appropriate commercial use of commercial property. There is allowance for consideration of multi-

          Mr. Montgome1y said open space requirements could be reduced to 10 percent if the project is located within a quarter-
mile walking distance of a public park. The ordinance also includes architectural standards for multi-family projects, namely for
the entry design, orientation of the project, difference between individual entrances and main entrances of multi-family units, and
the canopy or recess as the defining feature ofa building. He offered photographs of different types of entryways to illustrate the
importance of architectural standards and discussed the proposals relating to building materials for multi-family projects
emphasizing that at least two different building materials will be required. He added that utilities must also be screened from
public view and dumpster enclosure locations and designs will require approval from the City. For group dwellings, the ordinance
includes revisions to ensure that density matches the same density for a zone where multi-family development can take place.
Difficult equations to secure bonus density will be removed and replaced with a simpler percentage-based fonnula. The maximum
density increase in residential zones will be 10 percent, or 20 percent in commercial zones. Building material, architecture
standards, and other design criteria will be imposed on group dwellings similar to multi-family residential development. Parking
requirements for group dwellings will mirror those for commercial zones, with additions for bicycle parking, electric charging
stations, and covered stalls. Landscaping minimums for group dwellings include a 400 square foot plaza with seating and shade as
well as three amenities chosen from a list of optional amenities in the ordinance. Connectivity of sidewalks from parking and
landscape amenities will also be required. The bonus density allows for group dwellings to mirror those recommended for multi-
family projects.
