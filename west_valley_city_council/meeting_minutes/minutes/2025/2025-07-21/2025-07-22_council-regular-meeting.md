PUBLIC COMMENT PERIOD
Kate Rufener, President of the West Valley Arts Foundation Board and a resident of West
Valley City, addressed the Council to express appreciation and request continued
partnership through a financial appropriation. She reflected on the past year since the
Foundation took over the West Valley Performing Arts Center, describing how a
community rescue mission had evolved into a vibrant and impactful organization. She
highlighted three key benefits the theater brought to the City: vibrance, economic
influence, and stability. Kate noted that the theater had hosted sold-out performances,
community events, art exhibits, and youth programs, all of which contributed to civic
connection and engagement. She emphasized the economic impact, stating that
theatergoers supported local businesses and that several grants and private donations had
been secured—including a pair of donors who committed to a double match of any City
appropriation. Kate also underscored the role of the arts in providing community stability
and enhancing quality of life, which in turn made West Valley City more attractive to
potential residents. She concluded by reaffirming the Foundation’s commitment to the City
and its residents, and asked for support to continue their shared progress.

Jana Cox, a resident of District 4 in West Valley City, thanked the Council for its support
of the arts and the opportunity given to the West Valley Arts Foundation to continue its
mission of providing affordable theater to the community. She shared a personal story
about her five-year-old granddaughter’s excitement during a performance of Legally
Blonde, emphasizing the joy and vibrancy the theater brings to families. Jana noted that the
West Valley Performing Arts Center had played an important role in her life and had been
supported by five generations of her family. As a volunteer, she had witnessed the theater's
impact on the community—highlighting stories of local patrons, including families,
teachers, and visitors from outside the city, all drawn by the theater’s inclusive, diverse
productions and affordable ticket prices. She stated that keeping tickets affordable was
possible in part due to City support, and emphasized the cultural, social, and economic
value the theater provided. Jana explained that the theater not only enriched lives through

John Sweeney, interim executive director of the West Valley Arts Foundation, addressed
the Council as both a representative and a passionate advocate for the arts in the
community. He shared his background, having directed more shows at the Hale Centre
Theatre in West Valley than anyone else from 1999 to 2017, and expressed his deep
connection to the city's artistic legacy. John acknowledged the void left when Hale Centre
Theatre moved to Sandy, noting the economic impact it had on the area. He stated that the
West Valley Arts Foundation was positioned to help fill that gap and had already been
engaging with local restaurant and bar owners concerned about declining event activity.
He emphasized that while the Foundation was still in the early stages of becoming a self-
sufficient nonprofit, the progress made in recent months showed real promise for both
survival and long-term growth. John highlighted the Foundation’s efforts to draw
increasingly larger audiences and to introduce new people—including families, students,
seniors, and first-time theatergoers—to West Valley City. He noted the positive economic
ripple effects of theater events, including dining, shopping, and other spending in the city,
and invited attendees to the upcoming production of The Hunchback, opening August 8.
He shared that the organization was building relationships with local businesses and
sponsors but stressed that these take time to fully develop. John concluded by requesting
the Council’s continued partnership and budgetary support during this critical early phase,
framing it as an investment not just in the arts but in the broader future of West Valley
City.

Jim Vesock shared that he recently attended a gang conference and noted that while he
initially expected the conference to focus solely on gang activity, it featured a wide range
of insightful classes. After each session, he asked instructors what they would request from
legislators, expecting to hear calls for more police or funding. Instead, he was surprised to
hear unanimous support for maintaining funding for victim advocate programs, which are
often among the first to be cut. Instructors emphasized that these programs play a critical
role in helping youth—some as young as 8 to 10 years old—who are being recruited into
gangs, and early intervention could lead to significant long-term savings and impact.
Jim also reported on his attendance at the “Chill with the Chief” event. As a member of the
Clean and Beautiful Committee, Jim shared challenges in identifying well-maintained
yards due to a high number of poorly kept properties. He recommended that code
enforcement continue to be supported with the tools and resources needed to perform more
efficiently, including improvements in reporting processes. Jim expressed sadness over the
Utah Grizzlies hockey team leaving West Valley City. He acknowledged the emotional

Morgan Golightly, a former resident of West Valley City and a local artist frequently
employed by West Valley Arts, spoke in support of continued funding for West Valley
Arts. She described the organization as a cornerstone of stability and identity in the

David Madsen, a long-time West Valley City resident and organizer of National Night Out,

kind of foundational asset a city builds around. He emphasized the economic, cultural, and
social value of the theater, stating that it fostered identity, character, and a sense of place
in the community. He contrasted the theater’s unique contribution with the uncertainty
surrounding the Grizzlies’ future, warning that such opportunities couldn’t simply be
replaced or bought. Clark, who is a candidate for Murray City Council, concluded by

RESOLUTION 25-100: APPROVE A FUNDING AMENDMENT TO A
COOPERATIVE AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE CITY AND THE UTAH
DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION
Mayor Lang presented proposed resolution 25-100 that would approve a Funding
Amendment to a Cooperative Agreement Between the City and the Utah Department of
Transportation.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       West Valley City submitted a Safe Sidewalk application in 2024 to construct
       missing segments of sidewalk along the north side of 3500 South between
       Sunshade Drive (5450 West) and approx. 5350 West. The estimate submitted
       with the application to construct the entire length of the missing sidewalk, along
       with the associated right-of-way and improvements, was $500,000.

       The Safe Sidewalk program is administered by UDOT and requires a 25% local
       government. WVC was successful in the 2024 application and executed a
       Cooperative Agreement with UDOT in the amount of $225,334 ($169,000 State /
       $56,334 LG Match). Realizing that $225,334 is not enough to construct the entire
       project limits identified in the application, UDOT allowed flexibility to adjust the
       limits of the project as necessary to maximize the use of the available funds.

       As the design progressed, WVC continued to coordinate with UDOT to identify
       additional funding for the project. UDOT agreed to find additional funding to
       construct the entire segment if WVC acquired the right-of-way needed for the
       project. The City was successful in getting all landowners to donate the property
       and UDOT is therefore providing the additional funding.

       This modification enables access to the additional funds being contributed by the
       state from the Safe Sidewalk Program. The required 25% match for the additional

       funds will be met with a soft match including the design, ROW Acquisition, and
       construction management services.

RESOLUTION 25-101: AWARD A CONTRACT TO MORGAN PAVEMENT
MAINTENANCE FOR THE 2025 ASPHALT PAVEMENT PRESERVATION
TREATMENT PROJECT
Mayor Lang, presented proposed resolution 25-101 that would award a Contract to Morgan
Pavement Maintenance for the 2025 Asphalt Pavement Preservation Treatment Project.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The Public Works Department issued an RFP for venders to propose and apply an
       asphalt pavement preservation treatment product for neighborhood streets. The
       product is a sprayed asphalt emulsion, with fine aggregate, polymer and other
       additives intended to extend pavement life, and prevent oxidative damage from
       moisture and UV rays. The selected product, ONYX, is to be the City’s standard
       product going forward. Contracts for future years will be bid using the specification
       for the selected asphalt pavement preservation treatment product.

       Three proposals were submitted and reviewed, and ONYX Frictional Mastic
       Treatment was selected and will be applied by Morgan Pavement Maintenance in
       2025-26.

RESOLUTION 25-102: AWARD A CONTRACT TO EDA ARCHITECTS INC. TO
PREPARE COST ESTIMATES AND RENDERINGS FOR THE MAVERIK
CENTER
Mayor Lang presented proposed resolution 25-102 that would award a Contract to EDA
Architects Inc. To Prepare Cost Estimates and Renderings for the Maverik Center.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The Maverik center is almost 30 years old and in need of major renovations to keep
       up with the industry as a Sports Arena and Entertainment Center. The Maverick
       Center is also a major venue for the upcoming 2034 Olympic Games. A renovation
       of this magnitude will need a cost estimate and presentation tool to generate funding
       from many partners, such as the State of Utah, and the Olympic Committee. This
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING JULY 22, 2025
                           -9-

       contract will provide a cost estimate for the renovations and presentation tool to
       promote and solicit funding for the Maverik Center’s critically needed renovations.

       The City advertised for the Maverik Center renovations cost estimates Request for
       Proposal (RFP), EDA Architects Inc. submitted the highest scoring proposal.

       The necessary repairs and renovations list will include some of the following: A
       new roof, design and build-out of an “Ultra-Lounge” on 4th floor – combining the
       restaurant and Centennial room, renovation and upgrade of all arena suites and
       furnishings, remodel and update of the arena lavatories and add additional women’s
       lavatories, entire arena seat replacements, and replace Ice Sheet and chiller system.

       The proposal from EDA Architect for these services is $307,000.00.

       The funding for this project is from the Olympic and Paralympic Venues Grant
       Fund in FY 2025 for Olympic venue renovation projects as follows. $500,000 to
       West Valley City for the Maverick Center.
