STUDY SESSION

Discuss Housing Department Work Plan:
Elyse Kats and Jason Glidden presented this item. Kats reviewed projects currently in
progress in the Housing Department. She presented the proposed Lite Deed Restriction
program and stated the program would create more full-time residents. Deed
restrictions would be placed on properties in exchange for a cash payment from the
City. This could provide a down payment for buyers who wouldn't qualify for a home
otherwise. The only restriction on the property would be that the owner was required to
be a resident of Park City.

Council Member Worel asked how the program could work for seniors, who didn't work
30 hours a week. Kats indicated her team discussed replicating the language of the
affordable housing program, which qualified any applicants who worked 30 hours per
week or more in Park City two years prior to retirement. Glidden stated seniors were not
included in this program in Vail and he was open to suggestions on how to make this
work for seniors. Council Member Worel requested the seniors meet with staff to give
suggestions. Council Member Doilney thought two years was too restrictive and

participate. He liked the 30 hours a week as a definition of full-time residency.

Mayor Beerman stated the purpose was to keep these dwellings in the long-term
inventory. He questioned whether the work restriction made sense for this program.
Council Member Gerber stated there was a workforce shortage in Park City now and

this program.

Discuss Park City Soils Management Facility:
Council Member Henney was troubled that $17 million spent on hauling soil to Tooele
could have been spent on City projects. Council Member Joyce stated there was a lot of
misinformation out there and the Council had to decide what to do with the good
information given. Council Member Gerber reviewed the options and was concerned if
OU1 was reopened because it wasn't lined. There was no easy solution, but it was
about who was responsible. Mayor Beerman stated he heard from many concerned
citizens and there was much misinformation. Council and staff needed to continue to
reach new audiences so everyone could understand the fact and fiction behind this soils
facility.

Thacker, Building Official, presented this item. Weidenhamer stated nothing was
prohibiting the City from moving forward in building the soils repository. He reviewed the
proposed site and stated this was designed to fill community needs. He presented
different options the Council could consider, including stopping the project, reducing the
project size, moving forward with the project, or returning the project to the original size.

Weidenhamer reviewed the history of hauling soils to the Richardson Flat repository,
and when that was no longer an option, soils were deposited at the Gordo site. He
stated talks would be ongoing with state and federal officials. Mickelson was hired to
explore soil repository sites and the Gordo property was selected. Jager reviewed the
City outreach efforts associated with this project over the past two months. Council
Member Gerber thanked Jager for the extra work that was put into getting the word out
to residents.

Polly Jessen recapped the risk-related issues regarding mining impact soils. Bevel
waste was not considered hazardous waste. These materials could be put in non-
commercial landfills. The proposed facility was only authorized to accept soils. It would
have a ground water monitoring program, a liner, and inspection and monitoring
protocols. If the facility failed and the toxic minerals were leached into the environment,
the City could be liable for costs of cleanup.

Council Member Gerber asked if there was liability for the soils already at the Gordo
site. Jessen stated that at the time the soils were placed at the Gordo site, the state
regulations were that the soils be placed in a way that it wouldn't spread by wind or
erosion. There was a possibility that the soils could create a liability for parties who
owned the properties and the parties who managed the soils.

Jessens referred to AECOM’s analysis in the packet materials. She acknowledged
some constituents were concerned with the lead and arsenic in the soil. She stated risk
levels were driven by the period of exposure to those materials. The arsenic levels were
low risk and the lead levels were slightly higher than residential exposure. She noted
there would be a double liner and groundwater testing to ensure the toxins would not
pass to the groundwater supply.

Council Member Joyce stated a bike rider on a path that got caught in dust would not be
classified as being exposed, to which Jessen affirmed and noted the incidental
exposure would be higher than exposure from the repository. She stated there would be
procedures in place at the facility that would make exposure highly unlikely. Council
Member Worel asked if staff would be at the site full time monitoring and carrying out
the operating procedures. Twombly stated the operating procedures indicated trained
staff had to be there to administer when any soil was handled or moved to that location.

Council Member Doilney stated regular dirt would be on top of the toxic soils on a
regular basis. Twombly stated if there was a gap between soil deposits, there would be
a cover of soils placed over the toxic soils. Mayor Beerman asked who would approve
the operating plan, to which Jessen stated the Department of Environmental Quality
(DEQ). Weidenhamer added that Thacker would be over any code enforcement

Council Member Gerber stated there was a draft operating plan in the packet and asked
if this would be submitted to the DEQ or were there opportunities to add further
mitigations. Thacker stated the operating plan would come with a grading permit and a
construction mitigation plan associated with the site. Items could be added that were
specific to the site to ensure the City was maintaining safety standards for the
community. The Land Management Code was very specific in soil management.

Mayor Beerman asked for an explanation on soil testing. Weidenhamer stated the
current ordinance was for residential soil testing. The intent of testing was to ensure the
cap was clean. The soil in the Arts and Culture District was bevel exempt, exempt from
testing, and would be permitted to be taken to the landfill. The soil characterization or
testing part would be to look and separate the bevel soil from the mining influenced soil.
If mining influenced soil was discovered, it would have to be taken to Tooele and could
not remain at this repository. David Everitt stated the soil repository was different than a
construction site. Soils at the construction site would go through a characterization
process and then when they arrived at the Gordo site there would be a screening
process to determine that what was said about the soils on a truck was in fact true.

Council Member Doilney stated the discussion had been about what was in place in the
community and where it would be moved to, and was the toxic soil better off in the
community where it sat above the water source, or was it safer to be moved to a
regulated site. Weidenhamer indicated the Water Manager stated there was marginal
difference since it was not seeping into the water at this point. Jessen thought managing
the soils at the repository was safer. Council Member Joyce clarified there would be
continuous projects where the dirt would be disturbed, and the safer issue was to have
the soil in a regulated repository. Council Member Doilney thought the City needed to
move the soils. The action was inevitable, the only question was where to move the
soils.

Council Member Gerber stated the soils would be processed on site, but they would
also be screened at the repository. She asked if the soil could be accessed and
analyzed after it had been deposited if there was a question regarding a log entry.
Mickelson stated at the site the soil would be screened to see if it was bevel. At the
repository it would be screened again. After that, nobody would look at it again. Jessen
noted the purpose of the recordkeeping was to document compliance.

Council Member Worel asked if the operation costs would be part of the project cost.
Twombly stated operation costs would be a separate cost, but since they were all City
projects, staff would know when soils would be deposited. He thought that staff member
might also be over the Arts and Culture District. Mickelson stated the repository

Mayor Beerman asked what the staging timelines were for the soils repository phases.
Everitt indicated the repository would not remain open for the entire construction
process. It might be open at the beginning of one construction season and then
temporarily capped until the next season. Weidenhamer displayed a map of the
proposed soil repository and stated there were two proposed cells. One would be dug
and the existing soils that were left there would be pushed into the hole. Part of the Arts
and Culture District soil could fit in as well and then it would be capped. The second cell
would then be dug to hold 60,000 cubic feet and that would hold the soil from the
remaining Arts and Culture District soil and soil from City affordable housing projects.
Council Member Doilney suggested digging holes for specific projects so it could be
capped sooner.

Council Member Joyce noted Council had discussed allowing residents to use the site
as they had home remodels or other situations where they needed to remove their soils.
He hoped the cells wouldn't be closed too soon so residents would have the benefit of
using the repository. Mickelson stated the state regulation required an intermediate
cover of good soil installed if the site was not in use for 30 days.

Council Member Henney stated the conversation to allow residents to use the repository
was nice, but most people in those residential areas were against the facility because of
their impression of toxic soil.

      Lite Deed Restriction Program
            Recommendation
• Hold a work session to discuss and consider
  next steps to create the Live Park City - Lite
  Deed Restriction program.
                           Summary
• Park City Housing’s Goal is to facilitate a range of affordable,
  quality housing opportunities that meet the life-cycle needs of
  persons at all economic levels
   – This program would help Park City maintain a sense of
     community by incentivizing new and existing residents to
     place deed restrictions on their property in exchange for a
     cash payment from the city
• This program would promote:
   – More full-time residents
   – Encourage long term investments in employee housing
   – Provide down payment assistance for those just out of the market
   – Create an opportunity for current homeowners to ensure their home
     offers the ability for future residents to live and work in Park City in
     perpetuity
 Lite Deed Restrictions Background
• Other communities, such as Breckenridge and Vail have been
  successful with similar programs
• The proposed program would make cash payments available
  to property owners to place a deed restriction on their home
• The deed restriction would restrict properties to “full-time”
  residents, meaning they work 30 hours per week within City
  limits
   – Could also be a long-term renter (greater than 6 months)
   – Or a retiree
• Deed restrictions would exist in perpetuity
          Deed Restriction Purchasing
The amount of money paid in exchange for the deed restrictions being placed
on the property depends on several variables and is market dependent.

Valuations are based on market value and how much the property location
aligns with bolstering the City’s goals.

- In Vail and Breckenridge, compensation for deed restriction ranges from 16-17% of
  the home appraisal value on average
- Staff estimates based on conversations with local realtors that deed restrictions
  would be valued at around 20% of the appraisal value in Park City
- Condition, location, and other factors all contribute to deed restriction purchasing
  price
             Benefits of the Program
As other municipalities have demonstrated, this program can
help to incentivize full-time residency and increase long term
rental opportunities by:
   – Allowing for a mixed approach to deed restrictions in Park City, with this program not
     having income or appreciation caps, will create a more comprehensive deed restriction
     program that helps various socioeconomic classes in Park City.
   – Creating a more vibrant community that has more full-time residents and fewer empty
     units throughout town
   – Lowering traffic levels - more people living in town, less driving
   – Helping with employee recruitment – provides workforce housing options
   – Decreasing worker turnover- when employees are not worried about housing, they are
     more likely to stay in the position longer.
          Users of the Program
• Existing Homeowners- can apply to have a deed
  restriction placed on their property, which could be
  used to remodel or make repairs as a form of equity.
• Future Homeowners- can use the program as a form of
  down payment assistance.
• Property Investors- could use this funding to make
  long term rentals or workforce housing pencil.
• Local Employers- could buy employee housing, which
  would help with recruitment and worker retention.
• Retirees- could use this funding to make
  improvements, and to protect their home and
  community, or to downsize
              Housing Stock Analysis
• Staff has conducted regular meetings with local realtors, including the PC
  Board of Realtors, to better understand what housing stock under 1M in
  Park City may work well with this program
• According to the PC Board of Realtors MLS statistics:
    – There are only 20 active listings under $1 million in Park City limits.
    – There is a much larger pool of almost 1000 potential properties that are not
      currently on the market but could opt into the program.
        • For example, the Prospector area has 509 possible units with an average price of
          $150-300,000, which would be ideal for being included in this program.
        • Beyond this, the Old Town area has about 144 properties that could be targeted,
          and the Woodside area near the resort base has about 325 additional properties
          that would fit well in the program.

Dear Mayor Beerman and Council Members,

The Kimball Art Center is an educational non-profit and our mission is to inspire and connect
through art. IN THE SUMMER OF ’69… A group of local Park City artists launched an
open-air arts festival on historic Main Street. They aimed to imbue Park City – already a
bustling ski resort during the winter – with cultural offerings in the summer months. Inspired
by the artists’ creative energy, local philanthropist Bill Kimball decided to expand upon their
efforts by creating Kimball Art Center, a permanent home for the visual arts in Park City.

Under guidance and management of Kimball Art Center and a longstanding partnership with
Park City Municipal Corporation, the Park City Kimball Arts Festival – true to the spirit of its
founders in the summer of 1969 – has established itself as one of Utah’s leading cultural
events, attracting thousands of visitors annually, and garnering national attention for the
artists whose works are displayed. Now The Park City Kimball Arts Festival is one of the
top-ranked art festivals in the country and an integral part of a Utah Summer.

On behalf of the KAC Board of Directors, we aim to continue this tradition because we
believe art should be accessible to all and that it plays a critical role in the overall success of
the Park City community. We look to extend our relationship with Park City Municipal Corp by
humbly asking to renew our Arts Fest contract for 2022. Our goal is to showcase the work of
local and world-renowned artists during this important summer program as much as we
develop our exhibitions and provide art education in Utah schools to our year-round
residents. Your approval means this program generates income that we allocate back to our
local community, allowing us to host a range of community programs, exhibitions, and events
for our Park City residents.

Thank you for your collaboration. We ask for your support in renewing our partnership.

Sincerely,




Aldy Milliken,
