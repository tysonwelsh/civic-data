27          (“SLEDS”) Ordinance.

29   Chair Allen reported that the discussion item on the Work Meeting Agenda was Project ZTA-19-
30   001 and relates to the Sensitive Lands Evaluation and Development Standards (“SLEDS”)
31   Ordinance. There would be no public hearing or vote on the item during the current meeting. Staff
32   would present a progress report and receive feedback from the Commission about the proposed
33   amendments to the SLEDS Ordinance. Chair Allen noted that a public hearing would be held at a
34   future Planning Commission Meeting. The next meeting was scheduled for March 2, 2022.
35
36   Community and Economic Development Senior Planner, Andrew Hulka explained that the
37   discussion was related to the SLEDS Ordinance, which was the ordinance for any areas of the City
38   that had unusual geologic or geotechnical hazards. For instance, slopes, faults, flood zones, and
39   high groundwater. He explained that one of the major ordinance amendments related to map
40   updates. The map updates will ensure that the latest information is included. Mr. Hulka shared a
41   City map and explained that the pink highlighted area is the current Sensitive Lands District. That
42   boundary identified areas with potential hazards. He explained that some areas had been identified
43   as potential hazards but were not within the pink highlighted area on the map. Those potential
44   hazard areas were still subject to the requirements of the SLEDS Ordinance. He noted that it was
45   important to keep the map as up-to-date as possible to be aware of potential hazards when building
46   permits are submitted.

 9   Mr. Hulka highlighted Slopes Greater Than 30% on the map and explained that because the City
10   was near the canyons, there are a lot of slopes. Commissioner Bob Wilde asked if Staff had an
11   opinion on whether properties would be grandfathered in under the SLEDS Ordinance. Mr. Hulka
12   explained that anything that was existing was grandfathered in, but the current standards would be
13   applied to new additions, new buildings, and anything that meets the definition of development
14   under the Code. There were a lot of homes in the City that were built on or near fault lines or
15   slopes. That made it difficult for someone wanting to build an addition on their home. Extensive
16   studies were required to ensure that whatever is built meets standards.
17
18   Shallow Groundwater was shown on the map. Mr. Hulka explained that the dark blue areas were
19   identified as potential shallow groundwater areas, which was fairly common along the creeks.
20   Studies were required to identify the depth of the groundwater and make sure that there would not
21   be issues related to flooding. Rockfall Potential, Liquefaction Potential, and Landslide Hazards
22   were shown on the map. Community and Economic Development Director, Michael Johnson
23   reported that an Engineer and Geologist would be present at a future Planning Commission
24   Meeting to explain some of the information further. Mr. Hulka showed the Flood Zones and
25   explained that those areas were around the creeks. The Alluvial Fan Flooding and Debris Flow
26   Hazard Zone were also shown. He explained that the latter was an issue that could occur if there
27   was a wildfire and then a storm, where all of the debris, branches, and rocks flow down into the
28   susceptible areas. There were study requirements in place to address that.

 7   Ms. DeSeelhorst shared information related to the Riparian Protection Area. The purpose of a
 8   Riparian Protection Ordinance was to protect and enhance riparian areas, to promote public health,
 9   safety, and welfare. The standards of any Riparian Protection Ordinance aimed to protect habitat
10   and provide a functional buffer between the riparian habitat and developed areas. It would also
11   ensure that the river continued to function as it was meant to and that the river was safe and
12   beneficial to the community. There were also aesthetic and recreational values associated with
13   riparian protection. The benefits of a Riparian Protection Area were overviewed:
14
15      •   Improves bank stability;
16      •   Improves natural river function;
17      •   Reduces flood damage risk;
18      •   Protects native riparian species;
19      •   Enhances water quality;
20      •   Increases habitat and migration routes; and
21      •   Improves aesthetics.
22
23   The riparian section was missing from the SLEDS Ordinance currently. The ordinance language
24   included had a section that stated, “see Appendix 1 for riparian standards”. In that appendix, it
25   stated, “see Chapter 17.73”. Ms. DeSeelhorst reported that Chapter 17.73 does not exist. The
26   standards were referenced but were not there. As a result, Staff had begun to do some research
27   about what potential standards could look like. Several resources were used to create a working
28   draft. Two years ago, the City partnered with the 2019 University of Utah Student Capstone
29   Project. Additionally, existing Riparian Ordinances from neighboring communities had been
30   examined as well as Best Management Practices from various agencies.
31
32   Ms. DeSeelhorst explained that the main concept in the ordinance was a buffer system. It had to
33   do with the creation of three buffer areas, with each buffer area regulating a use differently. For
34   example, there was Area A, which was closest to the annual high-water line, Area B, which moved
35   further away, and Area C, which was furthest away. The idea was that use was regulated and
36   treated differently within the different areas. She shared a draft Table of Uses with the
37   Commission. The Table of Uses will govern how various uses were regulated in the different
38   buffer areas. There would be allowed uses, analysis required uses, not applicable uses, and uses
39   that were not allowed at all. Ms. DeSeelhorst shared some example uses as follows:
40
41      •   Maintenance of any use or structure that was lawfully established prior to ordinance
42          adoption;
43      •   Replacement or expansion of existing buildings;
44      •   Construction of new buildings;
45      •   Maintenance of existing lawn and garden areas;
